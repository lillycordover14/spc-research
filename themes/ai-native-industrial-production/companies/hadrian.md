## What they do
Hadrian builds and runs highly automated factories that make precision metal components for aerospace, defense and now naval shipbuilding. Its in-house software stack, Opus, handles scheduling, tool routing and inspection and is claimed to cut lead times from months to days and to bring a new factory online in under six months [src-20260807-b0dc5b][src-20250820-cfbf02]. It sells parts to Lockheed Martin, RTX and Anduril and sells 'factories-as-a-service' capacity to DoD programs, most visibly a Navy partnership for Virginia- and Columbia-class submarine components [src-20260807-b0dc5b][src-20260320-67ba4c]. Footprint is almost 3M sq ft across four sites (Torrance CA, Mesa AZ, Cherokee AL, plus a planned 400k sq ft HQ/R&D site) with 643 people and a stated target of 2,000 within a year [src-20260807-b0dc5b][src-20250721-d99a2a].

## Economics (what we know)
- Capex per factory: ~$200M for a 270k sq ft leased building (Mesa F3), ~$740/sq ft, ~$570k per job; buildings leased from CRG, so the capex is largely machines and fit-out [src-20250820-cfbf02].
- Maritime program: ~$2.4B for three plants ($900M Navy, >$1.5B Hadrian), implying ~$800M per maritime factory including assembly scope [src-20260320-67ba4c].
- Financing stack: Series C combined equity with a Morgan Stanley loan; Series D equity explicitly excludes factory construction; $360M bank revolver funds machines; Navy co-invests tied to outcomes [src-20250820-cfbf02][src-20260807-b0dc5b][src-20260817-c48f28][src-20260320-67ba4c].
- Lead time: months to days claim via Opus; factory bring-up under six months [src-20260807-b0dc5b][src-20250721-d99a2a].
- Labor: 30-day (Navy plant) to 30-60 day training of non-manufacturing hires; 1,000 jobs at an 80%-automated plant [src-20260320-67ba4c][src-20261022-de170d].
- Revenue: only datapoint is ~$3M in 2024 (Wikipedia citing Breaking Defense); no GM, utilization or 2025-2026 revenue disclosed [src-20261022-de170d].

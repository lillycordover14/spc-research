AI for materials discovery and development is starting to look like a
real category in applied AI.
At Plug and Play Tech Center, we mapped 60+ startups across
Europe and North America building at the intersection of AI,
materials science, chemistry, simulation, and lab automation.
A few things stood out.
First, this matters because materials sit upstream of batteries,
catalysts, semiconductors, and industrial chemistry. Better
materials can reshape cost, resilience, and industrial
competitiveness.
Second, the market has evolved in three broad waves.
1- Software tools: players like Citrine
Informatics, NobleAI, Quantistry, and Polaron built platforms to
improve prediction, simulation, characterization, and formulation
workflows.
2- Hybrid players: companies like CuspAI, Entalpic, and MatNex are
moving from screening known materials to generating bespoke
new candidates and the IP involved.
3- Material owners and closed-loop discovery: players like Lila
Sciences, Radical AI, Periodic Labs, Dunia, and altrove are
connecting AI with synthesis, testing, and iteration to bring more
speed and autonomy to experimentation, to generate validation
data faster, and, in most cases own the resulting IP.
What is most interesting, though, is where the market is heading.
The center of gravity seems to be shifting from enabling discovery
to shaping the candidate, and in some cases toward owning the
material and its IP. In parallel, the field is moving from purely in
silico workflows toward deeper lab integration and, at the frontier,
autonomous closed-loop and robotic systems.
That is also why “AI for materials” is not one bucket. Some startups
work at the atomic or molecular level, others at microstructure,
material, formulation, or process level. Different science, different
business models, different customers, different moats.
The core takeaway: the technology is real, but industrialisation
remains the bottleneck.
More insights on this space are coming soon in a dedicated report.

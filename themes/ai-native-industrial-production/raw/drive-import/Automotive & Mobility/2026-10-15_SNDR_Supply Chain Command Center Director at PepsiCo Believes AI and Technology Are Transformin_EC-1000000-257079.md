Summary

The interview covers decision-making processes for transportation and carrier selection in large CPG supply chains, the impact of technology and AI on network optimization, and considerations around adopting new vehicle technologies.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Over 18 years of experience in consumer packaged goods (CPG) supply chain, including plant operations, warehouse management, supply planning, network strategy, and risk mitigation, with direct involvement in transportation, carrier selection, and network optimization.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Believes that the decision between private fleet and outsourced carriers is highly situational, driven by cost, volume consistency, and network needs.
- Views AI and technology adoption in supply chain as rapidly increasing, with a strong push to implement efficiencies, though CPG is slightly behind other industries.
- Expresses cautious optimism about electric and autonomous vehicles, noting current barriers such as cost, infrastructure, and technology maturity.

TAKEAWAYS

- Carrier selection is based on a mix of cost, service reliability, capacity, and reputation, with large carriers like Schneider, Swift, and J.B. Hunt favored for high-volume needs.
- Private fleets are preferred for consistent, high-volume lanes, while outsourced carriers are used for peaks, new lanes, or when capital constraints exist.
- Network design and optimization are ongoing processes, typically reviewed quarterly or annually, with transportation cost as a major driver.
- AI is increasingly used for exception management, routing optimization, and efficiency improvements, with most large-scale implementations relying on external vendors.
- Adoption of electric vehicles in transportation is limited by cost, infrastructure, and uncertainty, with most companies still evaluating pilot programs rather than full-scale adoption.

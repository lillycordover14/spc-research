Summary

The interview covers accounting and AR practices in a multi-brand residential services platform, discussing the transition to electronic payments, use of AI tools for AR automation, and ongoing challenges with reporting and optimization.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Over 11 years of experience in residential services and PE-backed environments, managing P&Ls from $50 million to $500 million, with deep involvement in accounting tech stack selection, RFPs, implementation, and integration, particularly focused on AR processes.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Believes decentralizing accounting functions and embedding financial partners within brands is critical for operational success.
- Views automation, especially AI-driven tools like Avoca, as essential for optimizing AR and improving cash flow, which is a key compensation driver.
- Sees further opportunity for optimization in dispatch and lead assignment through AI, but notes current limitations in consolidated reporting across multiple business units.

TAKEAWAYS

- Initial centralized accounting structures were ineffective; embedding controllers within each brand improved communication and financial management.
- Billing practices varied widely across acquired companies, with a strategic push toward electronic payments and third-party financing to reduce in-house AR risk.
- AR challenges persist, especially with older customers preferring traditional payment methods and with general contractors in residential construction, who are described as difficult payers.
- AI tools like Avoca have recently been implemented across all brands to automate AR outreach, booking, and dispatch, resulting in efficiency gains and some headcount reductions.
- ServiceTitan is used as the core business management platform, but lacks consolidated AR reporting across multiple brands, which is seen as a limitation.

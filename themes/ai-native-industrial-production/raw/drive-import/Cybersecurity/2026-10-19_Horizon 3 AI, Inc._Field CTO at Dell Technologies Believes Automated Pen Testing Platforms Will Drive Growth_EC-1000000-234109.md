SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The conversation covered the expert's evaluation process for pen testing and attack simulation platforms, including Horizon3, and discussed factors influencing vendor selection, platform stickiness, sales cycles, budget allocation, and the evolving value proposition of automated security validation tools.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Field CTO with extensive experience in enterprise cybersecurity, AI infrastructure, data governance, and technical due diligence, including evaluation and deployment of pen testing and attack simulation platforms across a large, federated organization.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Believes the value proposition of automated pen testing platforms is strong and expects wallet share for these solutions to grow, especially as organizations shift from point-in-time testing to continuous validation.
- Views Horizon3 as having a strong product with unique autonomous features, but notes it currently lacks some enterprise-level capabilities and strategic separation compared to incumbents; expects to reevaluate its fit in future cycles.
- Anticipates significant consolidation in the market, with winning vendors being those that can demonstrate measurable outcomes and integrate deeply into enterprise security operations.

TAKEAWAYS

- The expert's organization regularly evaluates multiple pen testing and attack simulation vendors, including Horizon3, Pentera, Cymulate, Terra, and Picus, with decisions influenced by existing deployments and enterprise readiness.
- Horizon3's NodeZero platform is appreciated for its autonomous attack path discovery and user-friendly reporting, but was not deployed due to some missing enterprise features and overlap with existing tools.
- Switching between platforms is feasible but involves organizational and workflow costs, with stickiness coming from integrations, historical data, and institutional knowledge rather than technical barriers.
- Sales cycles for these platforms in large enterprises typically range from 6-9 months, with faster cycles possible if there is a compelling event, and longer cycles for strategic standardization or global rollouts.
- Expanding from offensive (red team) to defensive (blue team) capabilities could significantly increase the budget pool for vendors like Horizon3, especially if they can prove remediation effectiveness and integrate with broader security operations.

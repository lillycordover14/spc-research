SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covers the expert's use and evaluation of energy transition analytics and data providers, with a focus on Aurora's strengths and limitations across different markets, as well as comparisons to U.S. competitors like Wood Mackenzie, Uplight, and Arcadia.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- The expert is an Aerospace & Renewable Energy Consultant with 33 years of industry experience, including 15 years focused on renewable energy, and has worked globally across conventional and renewable energy sectors, including a former role as VP of Technology at Vestas Wind Systems and 14 years running an independent consulting practice supporting clients in wind, solar, hydrogen, energy storage, and energy analytics.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert views Aurora as a highly capable and valuable provider of energy data and analytics, especially in the U.K. and Western Europe, praising their modeling capabilities and data granularity.
- The expert is critical of Aurora's slower-than-expected expansion and adaptation to the U.S. market, noting missed opportunities and a lack of speed in scaling their offerings outside Europe.
- The expert expresses some dissatisfaction with Aurora's limited data coverage and integration in Eastern and parts of Southern Europe, wishing for more comprehensive and granular data in those regions.

TAKEAWAYS

- Aurora is recognized for its extensive and granular energy market data and advanced modeling tools, particularly in the U.K. and Germany, offering detailed reports and forecasts for power, renewables, hydrogen, and gas markets.
- Aurora's modeling suite includes specialized tools for energy systems, generation equilibrium, global gas markets, electricity networks, and asset dispatch, providing significant analytical advantages over competitors.
- The utility, trader, and operator segments all use Aurora's data for different purposes, such as price forecasting, power purchase agreement negotiations, and grid management.
- Aurora's data and modeling capabilities are less comprehensive and less frequent outside the U.K. and Germany, with notable gaps in Italy, France, and especially Eastern Europe, as well as challenges in replicating their European model in the fragmented U.S. market.
- U.S. competitors like Wood Mackenzie and Uplight offer different focuses, with Wood Mackenzie being the closest analog to Aurora, while Arcadia is more software- and workflow-oriented, particularly in EVs, solar, and billing.

Summary

The interview covered the decision-making process and criteria for selecting nuclear energy suppliers, particularly SMRs, to meet the escalating power requirements of large-scale data centers. The discussion included supplier evaluation, regulatory considerations, and the limitations of renewable energy in supporting future data center expansion.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Former Program Manager II responsible for sourcing and global supply chain sustainability for consumer hardware and data center operations at a major technology company, with direct experience in evaluating and procuring large-scale renewable and nuclear energy solutions for hyperscale data centers.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Strongly believes that nuclear energy, specifically SMRs (Small Modular Reactors), is essential to meet the rapidly growing power demands of AI and GPU-driven data centers, as renewable sources alone are insufficient.
- Views the high upfront cost and unproven nature of SMRs as acceptable trade-offs given the critical need for reliable, scalable, and regulatory-approvable power solutions, and expresses confidence in the long-term necessity and inevitability of nuclear adoption for large-scale data centers.

TAKEAWAYS

- The expert led the evaluation and procurement process for energy sources to support expanding data center needs, focusing on safety, scalability, waste management, and cost competitiveness.
- Kairos Power was selected as a preferred SMR partner due to its advanced safety features, scalability, and waste minimization approach, despite being a newer player without deployed reactors.
- Other evaluated suppliers included Constellation, NuScale, Oklo, Rolls-Royce SMR, and Terrestrial Energy, with European suppliers facing regulatory and logistical challenges for U.S. deployment.
- SMRs were favored over traditional large nuclear reactors due to lower capital costs, faster construction timelines, easier regulatory approval, and flexibility in deployment size and location.
- The expert highlighted the challenges of securing renewable energy due to high costs and limited availability, and noted that major tech companies are increasingly turning to nuclear solutions as renewable options become saturated and insufficient for future growth.

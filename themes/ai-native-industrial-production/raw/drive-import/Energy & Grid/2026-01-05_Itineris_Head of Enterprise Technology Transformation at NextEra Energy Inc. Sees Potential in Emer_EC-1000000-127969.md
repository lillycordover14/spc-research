SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covered the landscape of CIS and CRM solutions in regulated and deregulated utility markets, comparing established and emerging platforms, and discussed the challenges and considerations in selecting and migrating these mission-critical systems.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Head of Enterprise Technology Transformation at a major North American utility and renewable energy provider, responsible for selecting and implementing information technology solutions, including CIS and CRM, across both regulated and deregulated markets.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert is cautiously optimistic about the potential of newer platforms like Kraken and Kaluza for renewables, but emphasizes they are still in pilot phases and not yet proven for large-scale deployment in the U.S. market.
- The expert values established solutions like Itineris for their reliability and flexibility, but sees a need to evolve towards more dynamic, real-time platforms as the business shifts to renewables.

TAKEAWAYS

- The company operates both regulated (vertically integrated) and deregulated businesses, with different CIS/CRM solutions tailored to each market's needs.
- In the deregulated market, multiple CIS/CRM platforms are used due to acquisitions and varying business models; consolidation is planned, with Kraken and Kaluza being piloted for renewables.
- Kraken and Kaluza offer advanced capabilities for dynamic pricing and real-time grid management, but are currently limited by U.S. market data granularity and regulatory structures.
- Traditional platforms like Itineris, powercloud, and Gentrack are well-established for static tariff plans and multi-utility billing, but lack the dynamic, real-time features of newer solutions.
- CIS platforms are highly mission-critical and sticky, making migrations complex and rare, with large utilities favoring SAP/Oracle/Salesforce and smaller ones using Itineris, Gentrack, or powercloud.

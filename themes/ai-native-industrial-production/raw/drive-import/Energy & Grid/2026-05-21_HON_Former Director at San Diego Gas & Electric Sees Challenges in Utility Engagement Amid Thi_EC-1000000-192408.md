SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covered the structure and administration of energy efficiency and customer engagement programs at a major California utility, focusing on the transition to third-party implementers, regulatory requirements, and the challenges of program marketing and measurement. The discussion also addressed the evolving role of utilities in customer engagement and the impact of external factors such as Community Choice Aggregators and state energy codes.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Former Director of Customer Programs and Customer Care at a major California utility, overseeing energy efficiency, demand response, and low-income programs for both residential and commercial customers from 2019 to 2021.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The transition to third-party implementers has fundamentally changed how utilities engage with customers, reducing direct utility involvement in program marketing and administration.
- There are significant challenges in achieving incremental energy efficiency savings due to market saturation and increasingly stringent state codes, making it harder to find new opportunities.
- Automated tools for forecasting and program administration would be valuable, but adoption is constrained by regulatory processes and the unique challenges posed by Community Choice Aggregators.

TAKEAWAYS

- California utilities have shifted from directly managing energy efficiency programs to overseeing third-party implementers, as mandated by the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC), with at least 60% of portfolios managed externally.
- Behavioral programs, such as home energy reports, are implemented by third parties like Bidgely, leveraging advanced metering data to provide personalized recommendations and program marketing to customers.
- Utilities are limited in their ability to market programs directly due to budgetary and regulatory constraints; most outreach is conducted by third-party implementers, with the utility providing indirect support via its website and customer service.
- Program performance, measurement, and verification are highly prescriptive, with extensive audits and CPUC-approved modeling tools used to validate energy savings, and internal tools (historically Excel-based) used for tracking.
- Key pain points include market saturation, the impact of state energy codes, and the challenge of coordinating with Community Choice Aggregators, which serve the majority of customers and offer their own programs, limiting the utility's ability to offer duplicative services.

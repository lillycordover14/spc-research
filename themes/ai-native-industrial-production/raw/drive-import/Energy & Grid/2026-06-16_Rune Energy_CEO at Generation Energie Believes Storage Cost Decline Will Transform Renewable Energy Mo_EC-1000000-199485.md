SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covers the evolution of utility-scale renewable energy procurement, the challenges of basis risk, and the economics of curtailment and storage. The discussion also addresses the future viability of intermediary business models in the context of rapidly advancing storage technology and grid management.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- The expert has over 20 years of experience transitioning from investment fund management to building and operating large-scale energy infrastructure companies, with a focus on storage, solar, and wind projects for major global clients.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert is confident that the cost of storage will decrease significantly, making current business models around curtailment and co-location obsolete within the next five to ten years.
- The expert believes that large technology companies and network operators already have long-term strategies in place for energy procurement and grid management, reducing the need for intermediary companies focused on basis risk.

TAKEAWAYS

- Major corporate energy buyers (e.g., Amazon, Google, Microsoft) have shifted from traditional energy contracts to direct deals with renewable developers, leveraging their scale and expertise to drive down costs.
- Basis risk has become more pronounced as coal and gas baseloads are removed, leading to extreme price volatility during periods of low renewable generation, with companies sometimes paying up to $3/kWh for backup power.
- Curtailment payments to generators are significant, with European markets like Germany paying billions annually, but these are seen as temporary solutions.
- The economics of storage are rapidly improving, and the expert expects storage to become the dominant solution for balancing generation and consumption, displacing current models like co-location and curtailment arbitrage.
- Companies like Rune and Soluna, which capitalize on curtailment and local grid constraints, are unlikely to have sustainable business models as storage costs fall and grid management improves.

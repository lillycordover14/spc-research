SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covered the challenges and economics of utility-scale solar and battery storage development in the U.S., with a focus on interconnection queues, PPA pricing, battery storage economics, and the growing demand from data centers. The discussion also addressed the complexities of behind-the-meter generation for hyperscale data centers and the outlook for solar growth as tax credits phase out.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Head of U.S. development at a utility-scale solar and battery storage project developer with six years of experience overseeing project development, engineering, EPC cost estimating, procurement, and power marketing. Previously worked as a utility-scale solar and battery storage developer in the Northeast U.S. and has experience executing PPAs with large corporate offtakers, including data centers.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Believes only about 20-25% of projects in interconnection queues will reach commercial operation due to grid and capacity constraints, and expects this rate to remain steady.
- Expects utility-scale solar to remain the largest source of new power generation in the U.S. through the 2020s, but anticipates a decline in growth and rising PPA prices as tax credits phase out.
- Views batteries as increasingly essential for data center projects, with two- to four-hour durations generally being economic, but notes that long-duration storage is gaining traction for hyperscale data centers.

TAKEAWAYS

- Only about a quarter of projects in U.S. interconnection queues are likely to reach commercial operation, mainly due to grid capacity and high upgrade costs.
- Utility-scale solar PPA prices currently range from $40/MWh (with tax credits) to $50-$60/MWh in less optimal regions, and are expected to rise as tax credits phase out.
- Battery storage is commonly paired with solar, with two- and four-hour durations being most prevalent; construction costs for batteries are around $200-$220/kWh installed.
- Data center operators are less price-sensitive and are increasingly seeking long-duration storage solutions to meet high uptime requirements, with examples like Google's partnership with Energy Dome.
- Behind-the-meter generation for data centers can bypass interconnection queues but introduces significant financing and operational risks due to the dual stranded asset problem.

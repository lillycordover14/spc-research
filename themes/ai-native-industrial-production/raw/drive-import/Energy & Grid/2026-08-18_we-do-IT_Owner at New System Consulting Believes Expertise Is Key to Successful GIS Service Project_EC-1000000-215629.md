SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covered the selection and differentiation of GIS service providers for electric and gas utilities, the role and value of Esri partnerships, challenges in maintaining ADMS-ready data, and the potential for GIS firms to expand into adjacent technical areas.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- The expert has 24 years of experience at a large northeastern utility, where they started in the mapping (GIS) group and eventually led the department through multiple technology evolutions, including Esri Utility Network deployment, work design, and ADMS integration.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert believes that the success of GIS service projects is highly dependent on the expertise and experience of the individuals involved, rather than just the company or technology stack.
- They see ongoing opportunities for GIS services firms, especially in areas requiring deep subject matter expertise and nuanced understanding of utility data and operations, despite potential expansion of Esri Utility Network capabilities.
- The expert is skeptical about the ability of large IT service firms to effectively enter this niche due to the specialized knowledge required.

TAKEAWAYS

- Selection of GIS service vendors is often based on recommendations, expertise, and prior results, with SSP, RAMTeCH, and Cyient cited as examples used for major projects.
- Esri platinum partnership status is earned through active client work and results, not just payment, and provides partners with better access and responsiveness from Esri, though day-to-day project outcomes still depend on individual expertise.
- Maintaining high-quality, ADMS-ready GIS data is ideally a one-time effort, but in practice, ongoing maintenance is often required due to staffing and governance challenges at utilities.
- Outsourcing large-scale, multi-year managed services for GIS data maintenance is uncommon, with most utilities relying on smaller engagements and struggling with internal data governance and legacy data complexities.
- Expansion of GIS services into areas like pole loading analysis is possible but typically handled by engineering specialists or specific software products, and integrating such tasks into GIS workflows has proven challenging in practice.

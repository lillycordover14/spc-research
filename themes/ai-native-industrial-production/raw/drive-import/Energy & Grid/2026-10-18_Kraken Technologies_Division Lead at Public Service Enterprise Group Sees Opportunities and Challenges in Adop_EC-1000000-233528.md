SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covers the evaluation and adoption of customer management and DER software platforms by a major regulated utility, discussing leading vendors, selection criteria, commercial models, and the challenges faced by new entrants in the U.S. market.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Division Lead and Principal Staff at a large regulated utility in the Northeast U.S., responsible for grid modernization, digital transformation, vendor evaluation, and deployment of advanced software systems (ADMS, DMS, DERMS, CRM, etc.), with direct experience in procurement and integration of platforms from major vendors such as Oracle, Siemens, GE, and emerging players like Gentrack, Kraken, and Kaluza.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert is cautiously optimistic about adopting new software solutions, emphasizing the need for proven track records and regulatory compliance, especially from non-U.S. vendors.
- They believe customer experience is increasingly important for regulated utilities, not just cost to serve, due to growing competition and future market liberalization.
- The expert sees pilots as necessary but slow-moving, with full adoption dependent on successful integration and demonstrated value.

TAKEAWAYS

- Major software providers for utilities include Oracle, Siemens, GE, and Gentrack, with emerging interest in platforms like Kraken and Kaluza, particularly for AI-driven customer operations and DER orchestration.
- Gentrack is currently in a pilot phase at the expert's utility, selected for its strong integration capabilities and analytics, but the pilot is progressing slowly due to integration challenges and internal prioritization.
- Kraken is recognized for its automation and AI-driven customer operations but faces barriers to U.S. adoption due to lack of proven deployments, regulatory complexity, and concerns about non-U.S. headquarters and data sensitivity.
- Utilities evaluate customer management software based on end-to-end customer support, integration with existing systems, digital capabilities, security, compliance, and the ability to leverage AI and analytics for operational efficiency.
- Commercial models for software procurement vary: legacy vendors offer perpetual licenses with annual maintenance, while newer entrants favor SaaS or consumption-based pricing; DER software is typically annual SaaS or per-asset, with costs decreasing at scale.

SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The conversation covered the challenges and economics of power generation and transmission solutions for data centers, including private HVDC lines, behind-the-meter gas, grid power, and the emerging role of solid-state transformers. The discussion also compared market pricing, reliability, and vendor dynamics in the evolving data center energy landscape.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Senior Director on the energy and development team at a leading energy solutions provider, with over a decade of experience in generation asset development and origination (renewables, gas, storage), and recent focus on sourcing and structuring power for AI infrastructure, including grid and behind-the-meter solutions.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Skeptical about the long-term viability and value creation of private HVDC transmission lines for data centers, favoring co-location and grid solutions.
- Believes grid power remains the preferred option for hyperscalers due to reliability, cost, and carbon accounting, but acknowledges behind-the-meter gas as a necessary alternative in constrained markets.
- Optimistic about the future adoption and efficiency gains of solid-state transformers (SSTs), with a preference for innovative upstarts over incumbents in the SST space.

TAKEAWAYS

- Private HVDC transmission lines face significant regulatory, easement, and business challenges, and are generally less attractive than co-location or grid-based solutions for data centers.
- Co-location of data centers with generation assets is typically more efficient and cost-effective, with land and labor constraints rarely justifying long transmission builds.
- Grid power is generally cheaper ($70-$90/MWh) and more reliable than behind-the-meter gas ($110-$130/MWh), and is preferred by hyperscalers for reliability and carbon accounting, though behind-the-meter gas is used in specific constrained scenarios.
- Capacity pricing for private transmission solutions (e.g., $21/kW-month) is above current market rates ($15-$18/kW-month), making them less competitive compared to grid or traditional capacity markets.
- Solid-state transformers (SSTs) promise significant efficiency gains (from ~85% to 98-99%), with upstarts like Heron Power and Enphase seen as leading innovators, while incumbents are slower to adapt due to existing profitable business lines.

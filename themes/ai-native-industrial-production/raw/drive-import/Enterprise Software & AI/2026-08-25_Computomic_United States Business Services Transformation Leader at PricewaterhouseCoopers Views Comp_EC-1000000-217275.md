Summary

The interview covers the selection, engagement, and ongoing relationship with Computomic as a Databricks integration partner, focusing on data modernization, AI enablement, and managed services. It also discusses Computomic's competitive positioning, delivery strengths and weaknesses, and the evolving partner ecosystem for Databricks.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Senior leader with nearly 30 years of consulting experience, specializing in business services transformation, data architecture modernization, and large-scale technology integrations, including cloud migrations and AI enablement for a major professional services firm.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert views Computomic as a highly valuable and responsive partner for Databricks implementations, particularly in data migration and transformation, though acknowledges scalability challenges for very large clients.
- The expert believes Computomic's exclusive focus on Databricks is both a strength (for niche solutions) and a potential risk (due to single-platform dependency), but currently sees them as best-in-breed within their segment.
- Despite initial friction, the expert would choose Computomic again, citing their deep organizational knowledge and improved performance over time.

TAKEAWAYS

- The decision to use Databricks was driven by the need for unified data architecture, improved governance, and significant cost savings compared to on-premises solutions.
- Computomic was introduced by Databricks and selected after a careful vendor evaluation, with their main value being in designing and executing the data migration and transformation strategy.
- Scalability was a concern during implementation due to the size and complexity of the organization, but Computomic adapted and met key milestones after initial delays.
- Current engagement with Computomic has shifted from large-scale implementation to managed services and consultative support, with annual spend now around $175,000-$200,000.
- The expert sees Computomic's main area for improvement in business intelligence and real-time reporting capabilities, suggesting this is more a people/talent issue than a technology gap.

SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview discusses the adoption, usage patterns, and evaluation of an AI-driven due diligence tool (Junior) versus general LLMs within a private equity firm, focusing on workflow integration, compliance, and contract structure.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- AI Lead at a private equity firm, responsible for delivering AI solutions across operations and deal teams, including trialing and rolling out AI-driven SaaS software and building internal bespoke tools for due diligence.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Views the choice between vertical SaaS tools like Junior and general LLMs (e.g., Claude) as dynamic, with user preference and workflow needs influencing adoption.
- Believes that while LLMs are rapidly advancing, vertical tools like Junior currently offer workflow, integration, and compliance advantages, but this could change as technology evolves.
- Sees value in annual contract renewals and regular reevaluation of AI tools due to the fast-moving nature of the AI landscape and fluctuating costs.

TAKEAWAYS

- Junior was adopted over a year ago as an AI-driven due diligence tool, now used by over 60 active users across deal teams, with all expert calls routed through it for compliance and workflow integration.
- Usage of Junior versus general LLMs like Claude depends on call volume and user technical proficiency; high-volume projects benefit more from Junior's workflow, while low-volume users may prefer Claude.
- Junior's main advantages are its integrations with multiple expert networks, compliance features (such as automated MNPI flagging), and a searchable library of past calls, which are difficult to replicate manually at scale.
- The firm maintains one-year contracts for all AI tools, including Junior, to allow for regular reassessment as technology and costs evolve; Junior was recently renewed for a second year.
- Customization with Junior has been more about configuration and co-building features that later become available to all users, rather than bespoke development.

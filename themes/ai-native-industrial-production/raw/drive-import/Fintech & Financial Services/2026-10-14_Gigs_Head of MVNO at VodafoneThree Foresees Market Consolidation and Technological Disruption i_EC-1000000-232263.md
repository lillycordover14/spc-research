Summary

The interview covers the evolution of MVNO, MVNE, and MNO relationships, the commercial and operational drivers behind these partnerships, and the impact of new entrants and business models on the market. It also discusses future trends, including service differentiation, market consolidation, and the potential for new technologies to disrupt the traditional mobile network landscape.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- 20 years of experience in the wholesale and MVNO market, primarily managing MVNO relationships and strategy for major UK mobile network operators, including a period as Chief Commercial Officer at a global MVNO.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- MVNEs and MVNOs are evolving, with software-led approaches like Gigs gaining traction, especially among fintechs, due to ease of deployment and management.
- There is a growing tension between MNOs and MVNOs/MVNEs as MNOs seek to protect their investments and margins, potentially leading to service differentiation and ceilings for MVNO offerings.
- The expert foresees possible market consolidation and a shift in the MVNO/MVNE landscape, with MNOs possibly acquiring MVNEs or building pan-regional capabilities.

TAKEAWAYS

- The MVNO/MVNE/MNO relationship has shifted from simple traffic acquisition to more complex, strategic partnerships, with operational complexity and commercial viability as key considerations.
- MVNEs like Gigs have disrupted the market by offering software-first solutions tailored to non-telecom brands, making it easier for companies like Revolut to launch mobile services.
- Traditional telecom MVNEs (e.g., 1GLOBAL) offer broader international reach and faster multi-market launches, but require more telecom expertise from partners.
- MNOs are increasingly building in-house MVNE-like platforms to streamline MVNO onboarding, but these do not offer the same operational simplicity as third-party MVNEs like Gigs.
- Future market dynamics may see MNOs differentiating their own services with premium features, placing ceilings on MVNO/MVNE offerings, and possibly leading to industry consolidation or new entrants like satellite providers.

What has happened is that the energy cost, that would always have assumed a flat energy cost or a declining energy cost, have doubled, and therefore, probably that cost saving has not been realized.

What you have got in the market as well is that MVNOs tend to be protected from those sorts of costs because they are on three-year or five-year deals at a fixed price, and therefore, they have not seen their costs go up at the same rate as the mobile network operators have seen their margins being squeezed. Therefore, the dynamic you have probably got coming in the market is that the mobile network operator will try and differentiate their service and put a ceiling on the service that is offered to anybody who is not part of their family.

What I mean by that is the investments that they are making will generate faster speeds, better coverage, priority access, more secure connectivity. That probably is going to be the things that the mobile network operators will keep for themselves and sell at a premium price in order to make more money back from the investments they are making. Now, what they would probably do is not wholesale those aspects of that technology to people like MVNEs and MVNOs.

What they will be doing is saying there is a ceiling on what the access to the network you get, and therefore, there is a ceiling on the offerings that you can offer to MVNOs or your customers, things such as speed caps, data thresholds, things such as that. The dynamic I see immediately is that there is probably going to be layers of service in mobile. At the moment, everyone gets access to the same network. The difference in price is determined by the allowances that you buy. I suspect it will split into the difference in price is determined by the speed you want or the access you want to the network.

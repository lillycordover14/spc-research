SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covers the expert's experience implementing Eyrus for construction site attendance tracking, its integration challenges, and its fit relative to other workforce management and site intelligence solutions. The discussion also explores broader trends and unmet needs in construction technology, particularly around data granularity, integration, and AI-driven site intelligence.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Director of Innovation at a large general contractor specializing in multi-family residential and commercial towers, with experience deploying construction site monitoring and intelligence technology, including recent hands-on evaluation and implementation of Eyrus across multiple job sites.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Sees Eyrus as easy to implement and cost-effective for basic headcount and attendance tracking, but not differentiated or compelling enough for broader rollout without further product development.
- Expresses skepticism about Eyrus' claims of advanced integration and AI capabilities, viewing the product as early-stage and lacking in granularity and business intelligence compared to emerging competitors.
- Believes the broader site intelligence and digital twin space is high potential and likely to see significant growth and innovation, but Eyrus currently only addresses a small part of that vision.

TAKEAWAYS

- Eyrus was selected primarily for its perceived integration with the company's existing health and safety system (HammerTech) and ease of deployment, but the integration was less robust than advertised.
- The main use cases for Eyrus are accurate headcount for health and safety reporting, independent verification of subcontractor hours for hourly contracts, and site access control in high-risk areas.
- Adoption barriers are low technically, but operationally there is friction, especially with subcontractors and unions, and compliance can be challenging without strong incentives.
- Eyrus' attendance tracking is seen as basic, lacking directionality and granularity, and does not provide deeper site intelligence or productivity insights; other solutions like Genda (now part of Buildots) are viewed as more advanced.
- Switching costs are low technically, but operational change management makes mid-project replacement undesirable; the expert is unlikely to expand Eyrus use without significant product improvements.

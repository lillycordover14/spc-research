Summary

The interview discusses strategic procurement challenges in aerospace, the evolving role of new precision machining suppliers, and the comparative strengths of legacy and emerging players in the supply chain. Topics include automation, regulatory hurdles, supplier onboarding, and the criteria for supplier selection in both commercial and defense programs.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Senior manager responsible for strategy in the CTO organization at a major aerospace and defense company, focusing on advanced manufacturing, automation, digital engineering, and supplier strategy, with oversight of supplier capability assessment and production readiness for both existing and emerging aircraft programs.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Sees automation and digitalization as key differentiators for new suppliers, but emphasizes that regulatory certification and quality consistency are the primary barriers to adoption.
- Believes that while new entrants like Hadrian have strong potential, legacy suppliers remain critical due to their proven reliability and regulatory acceptance.
- Views cost savings as attractive but secondary to quality and reliability; new suppliers must first meet stringent technical and regulatory requirements before cost becomes a deciding factor.

TAKEAWAYS

- Key procurement challenges include fragmented supply base, unpredictable lead times, skilled labor shortages, and quality consistency issues, especially in precision machining.
- New entrants like Hadrian and Protolabs are currently used as gap fillers for non-critical parts due to lack of full regulatory certification, but have potential for a more structural role in the future if they achieve certification and scalability.
- Automation and process control are seen as major advantages for new suppliers, potentially enabling higher throughput, reduced labor needs, and improved consistency, but scaling with quality is extremely difficult in aerospace.
- Legacy suppliers are preferred for critical parts due to their established processes, regulatory acceptance, and tribal manufacturing knowledge, despite being slower and more expensive.
- Supplier relationships improve over time as new entrants learn complex requirements and processes; performance is not static and is continuously monitored and improved.

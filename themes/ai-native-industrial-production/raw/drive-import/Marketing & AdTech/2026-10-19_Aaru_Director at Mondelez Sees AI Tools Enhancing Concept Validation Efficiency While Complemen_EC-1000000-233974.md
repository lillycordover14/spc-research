SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview discusses the use of AI-based market research tools for concept validation and innovation within a large consumer goods company, comparing their effectiveness, cost, and integration challenges relative to traditional research agencies. The conversation covers adoption hurdles, accuracy, internal stakeholder dynamics, and future budget allocation trends for AI-driven research.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Director overseeing the digital business at a major global snacking company, responsible for digital channels, e-commerce, and innovation, with direct experience piloting and evaluating AI-based market research tools such as Aaru, Simile, and Listen Labs.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert is optimistic about the potential for AI tools like Aaru to improve speed, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness in early-stage concept validation, but remains cautious about fully replacing traditional research methods.
- There is a strong desire for greater transparency and validation from AI tools before increasing spend or replacing legacy research, with internal buy-in from marketing teams still developing.
- The expert expects AI-driven research to materially increase as a share of the research budget in the next 2-3 years, but does not foresee complete replacement of traditional agencies.

TAKEAWAYS

- AI tools like Aaru are currently used for early-stage concept validation, allowing the company to test a much larger number of ideas at a fraction of the cost and time of traditional research.
- Traditional research agencies (e.g., Nielsen, Kantar, Ipsos) are still relied upon for market data, household panels, and complex studies, and are themselves investing in AI and synthetic audiences.
- Aaru and similar AI tools are considered 'black boxes' with limited transparency on methodology, which is a barrier to broader adoption and internal trust, especially among marketing teams.
- The expert estimates Aaru's outputs are about 80% directionally accurate compared to traditional research, and both methods are about 60-70% accurate versus actual market outcomes.
- AI research currently represents a single-digit percent of the research budget, expected to grow to low double digits in the next 2-3 years, driven by productivity gains and enterprise-wide AI initiatives.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert views VR-based safety and field training as increasingly mission-critical, with budgets and adoption expected to grow as the industry recognizes its value for compliance, safety, and operational effectiveness.
- Picsera is seen as a strong strategic partner due to its deep understanding of oil and gas industry needs and its ability to deliver highly customized, compliance-driven training solutions, though the expert notes room for improvement in customer evangelization and support.

TAKEAWAYS

- Budgets for safety and operational training in large oil and gas companies are substantial (typically $20–30 million for safety and $10–20 million for operational training per company), with spending expected to rise as remote and immersive training becomes more central.
- Key purchase criteria for training solutions include technical (especially cybersecurity and scalability), functional (user experience, interoperability), and business (cost, compliance, SLAs) factors, with safety and compliance requirements cutting across all categories.
- The competitive landscape for VR-based safety training is broad, with both direct and complementary competitors such as Worklete, Mursion, XVR Simulation, HSI, iHASCO, Ludus, VR AI, PIXO VR, American Safety Council, and ClickSafety, each with different strengths and industry focuses.
- Picsera is differentiated by its granular, task-based, and highly customizable training modules tailored to specific oil and gas use cases, compliance needs, and regional requirements, making it a preferred partner rather than just a vendor.
- Switching costs are high due to implementation, integration, training, contractual, and reputational factors, making vendor relationships sticky; ROI is measured not just in financial terms but also in value, risk reduction, compliance, and employee retention.

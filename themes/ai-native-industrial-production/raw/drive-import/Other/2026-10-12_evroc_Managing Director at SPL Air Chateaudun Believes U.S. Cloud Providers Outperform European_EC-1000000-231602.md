SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview discusses the trade-offs between European sovereign cloud providers and U.S. cloud giants, focusing on service quality, cost, and regulatory concerns. The conversation covers current cloud usage, provider evaluations, and the disconnect between public sentiment and actual enterprise cloud adoption in Europe.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Managing Director at a Paris-area airport, responsible for IT, software, storage, and database management, with prior experience at a European traffic management simulation company.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Prefers U.S. cloud providers due to superior performance, innovation, and cost, despite public discourse favoring European sovereignty.
- Skeptical about the viability and staying power of European sovereign cloud providers, citing poor service quality and limited portfolios.
- Believes that unless European providers match U.S. offerings in price and capability, adoption will remain minimal and largely symbolic.

TAKEAWAYS

- Currently, only about 5% of workloads are on European sovereign clouds (e.g., evroc, OVHcloud), with the vast majority on Azure due to integration with existing Microsoft services.
- European sovereign cloud providers are seen as inferior in service quality and portfolio compared to U.S. providers; evroc is considered better than OVHcloud but still small and unproven.
- Hybrid or joint ventures between European and U.S. providers (e.g., Bleu, Thales-Google) are viewed as ineffective due to incomplete portfolios and continued U.S. ownership influence (CLOUD Act).
- Cloud spending is increasing 10-20% per year, but the share allocated to European providers is expected to remain small unless regulation or service parity changes.
- There is a significant gap between public statements favoring European sovereignty and actual purchasing behavior, which overwhelmingly favors U.S. providers for business-critical workloads.

SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covers the evaluation, adoption, and usage of various AI coding tools within a large enterprise, focusing on how different platforms are selected and integrated based on business needs, cost, and workflow requirements.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Director of Software Engineering with experience evaluating, onboarding, and integrating AI coding tools (such as Devin, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code, and Codex) into enterprise developer workflows, including direct involvement in decision-making and vendor interactions.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Views Devin as a valuable tool for end-to-end engineering workflows, but notes its higher cost limits its use to specific scenarios.
- Believes flexibility in tool and model choice is important, allowing business units to select platforms based on their needs and budgets.
- Is open to reevaluating tool usage and spend allocation as new platforms (like Claude Code) are tested and as developer feedback is gathered.

TAKEAWAYS

- Devin was evaluated and onboarded early, and is integrated into developer workflows for end-to-end tasks, acting as a 'secondary engineer.'
- Multiple coding tools are used in parallel (GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code, Codex), with each serving different use cases and business units having flexibility in tool selection.
- GitHub Copilot accounts for the majority of spend (55-60%), with Devin at about 15%, Codex at 20%, and the remainder allocated to newer platforms for R&D.
- Devin is significantly more expensive per seat than GitHub Copilot, so its use is reserved for complex, end-to-end workflows rather than routine tasks like unit testing.
- Open source and Chinese LLMs have been evaluated (at a previous employer) but were not adopted for production due to regulatory and trust concerns.

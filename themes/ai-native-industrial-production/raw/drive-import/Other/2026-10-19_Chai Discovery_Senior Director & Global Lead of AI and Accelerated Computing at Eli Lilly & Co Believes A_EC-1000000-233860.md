Summary

The conversation covered the practical use of AI models in drug discovery, the differentiation and business models of leading vendors, and the limitations of current technologies due to data availability and scientific understanding. The expert discussed the company's approach to vendor partnerships, the value derived from AI tools today, and the challenges in benchmarking and advancing model capabilities.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Senior Director & Global Lead of AI and Accelerated Computing at a major pharmaceutical company, with prior experience leading a global team at NVIDIA focused on making AI models practical for drug discovery. Extensive hands-on experience in structure-based drug discovery, biophysics, and deploying AI tools for scientific and molecular innovation.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Cautious about the likelihood of AI models revolutionizing drug discovery in the near term, emphasizing that current models are limited by the available data and fundamental biological understanding.
- Believes that current AI tools are most valuable for diversification and generating novel molecules for known targets, rather than enabling breakthroughs for undruggable or poorly understood targets.
- Expects continued use of multiple vendors due to the hit-or-miss nature of current technologies and the lack of a single model that generalizes well across all targets.

TAKEAWAYS

- The company works with a wide range of AI drug discovery vendors (e.g., Isomorphic, Chai, Boltz, Insilico, insitro, Schrödinger) to de-risk projects, as no single model or vendor is expected to succeed across all targets.
- Isomorphic is used for small molecule drug discovery, while Chai is focused on large molecule (antibody) discovery and optimization; both are considered technologically similar, with differentiation mainly in modality.
- Current AI models are primarily trained on public data, which is unevenly distributed, leading to limited generalizability, especially for targets with little historical data.
- The main value of current AI tools is in generating diverse backup molecules for known targets, supporting patent strategies and providing multiple clinical candidates, rather than enabling new therapeutic breakthroughs.
- Benchmarks for model performance are highly target-specific and not broadly generalizable; downstream data (e.g., toxicity, developability) is largely inaccessible to vendors, limiting model utility beyond binding affinity predictions.

SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The conversation covered the rationale and process for selecting and integrating multiple search and agentic infrastructure vendors, the division of use cases among them, and the evaluation of their strengths, weaknesses, and future risks.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Chief Information Security Officer and acting CIO at an enterprise software company focused on enterprise search, agentic platforms, and workflow automation, with deep involvement in product architecture and security.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert values using best-of-breed solutions, even if it means integrating multiple vendors and absorbing additional complexity.
- They see current vendors like Exa as providing unique value, but acknowledge significant risk from larger foundational model providers potentially entering and dominating the space in the future.
- The expert is generally satisfied with current vendors but sees room for improvement, especially in semantic search and document parsing capabilities.

TAKEAWAYS

- Enterprise search and internet search are fundamentally different, leading to a decision not to build internet search in-house despite core expertise in search.
- The company uses a multi-vendor stack for search and agentic workflows: Google (with Gemini), Brave, Exa, and Firecrawl, each serving distinct roles based on strengths and limitations.
- Brave is primarily used for broad, fresh internet search, while Exa is leveraged for content fetching, parsing, and finding related links, especially in agentic workflows.
- Firecrawl is used for crawling and indexing JavaScript-heavy websites, where Exa's capabilities are limited.
- Spend with each vendor is significant and scales with API call volume, but there are no strong data network effects or high switching costs; integration is relatively straightforward.

SMART SUMMARY

Summary

The interview covers the expert's experience managing global talent mobility, detailing workflows, vendor relationships, and the evaluation of immigration service providers for large multinational technology companies.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Senior leader with 14-15 years of experience managing global mobility, compensation, and people operations at large multinational technology companies, including oversight of end-to-end talent mobility processes, vendor management, and global relocation strategies.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Views current primary immigration vendor as highly capable for large-scale, complex needs, but acknowledges service quality may be influenced by client size and spend.
- Open to switching or splitting volume to other vendors if they can match or exceed current service levels and scale, but would proceed cautiously and require proof of performance.

TAKEAWAYS

- Global mobility at the expert's company primarily involves moving existing employees between countries where the company already has offices, rather than direct international hires.
- The talent mobility workflow includes business initiation, assessment, feasibility and risk analysis, package and cost estimation, approvals, immigration and tax processing, relocation, and onboarding.
- Key vendors used include Altair (relocation), Fragomen (immigration), KPMG (tax/compliance), and soon Topia (integrated mobility platform); Rippling is used at smaller companies for payroll.
- Fragomen is considered the top provider for immigration services at scale, with strong technology, expertise, and responsiveness, though employee satisfaction is affected by government wait times rather than vendor performance.
- Service quality from Fragomen is perceived as higher for large, high-spend clients; smaller companies may not receive the same level of service or responsiveness.

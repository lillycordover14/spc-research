Summary

The interview covers the evolution of drone procurement and deployment in a major Florida law enforcement agency, focusing on the comparative strengths, procurement processes, and future outlook for Skydio and BRINC in the DFR market.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Special Response Team Commander at a large Southeast U.S. law enforcement agency, overseeing drone procurement and deployment since 2018, with direct experience evaluating and operating Skydio, BRINC, DJI, Teledyne FLIR, and Flock Safety drones for both indoor and outdoor law enforcement applications.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Believes both Skydio and BRINC are mission-critical for law enforcement drone operations, with each having unique strengths and rapid product improvement cycles.
- Views the DFR (Drone as First Responder) market as poised for significant growth, expecting widespread adoption across South Florida agencies by 2030.
- Currently rates BRINC slightly higher than Skydio due to the recent release of the BRINC Guardian, but acknowledges that leadership could shift as new products are released.

TAKEAWAYS

- Florida's ban on DJI drones for government use led to rapid adoption of U.S.-made drones, primarily Skydio for outdoor and BRINC for indoor operations.
- Both Skydio and BRINC have improved product quality significantly in recent years, with each company quickly iterating and releasing new, more advanced models.
- Procurement is often facilitated by partnerships (Skydio with Axon, BRINC with Motorola), allowing agencies to add DFR solutions to existing contracts and bypass lengthy RFP processes.
- Switching costs between Skydio and BRINC are low, as infrastructure requirements are similar and both companies facilitate transitions between platforms.
- Current spend is higher on Skydio due to earlier availability of exterior drones, but future spend is expected to be in the millions as DFR programs expand across dozens of stations.

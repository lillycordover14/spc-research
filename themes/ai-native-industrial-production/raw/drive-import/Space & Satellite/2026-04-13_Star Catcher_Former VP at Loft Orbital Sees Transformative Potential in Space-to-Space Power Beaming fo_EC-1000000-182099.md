EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert views space-to-space power beaming as an exciting and potentially transformative capability, particularly for increasing operational flexibility and unlocking higher-value satellite missions.
- They express some uncertainty about the immediate, game-changing impact of power beaming, noting that its value will depend on technical feasibility and specific mission requirements, but anticipate significant upside if technical challenges are addressed.

TAKEAWAYS

- The current managed satellite fleet is under ten, with plans for expansion to a few dozen over the next two years, primarily focused on Earth observation (EO) missions, which make up roughly 70-80% of the pipeline.
- Power is a fundamental constraint for satellite operations; augmenting available power via space-to-space beaming could increase the utilization of sensors, enable more frequent data collection, and support power-hungry applications like AI and inter-satellite links.
- Earth observation satellites are particularly well-suited for power augmentation, as increased power can directly translate to higher duty cycles and more revenue-generating activity, especially for power-intensive payloads like Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR).
- Telecom and IoT payloads could also benefit from additional power, though the value proposition is less clear-cut compared to EO; improvements may be seen in data downlink performance and operational flexibility, especially for smaller CubeSats.
- The expert notes that while the average power consumption for current platforms is in the hundreds of watts, there is a trend toward higher-power missions, and future platforms may require significantly more power, especially if data center applications in space materialize.

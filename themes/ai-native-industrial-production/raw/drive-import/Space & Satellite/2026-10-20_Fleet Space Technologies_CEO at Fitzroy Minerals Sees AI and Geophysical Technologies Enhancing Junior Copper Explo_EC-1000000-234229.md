Summary

The conversation covered the business drivers and challenges of junior mineral exploration, the use of geophysical and AI technologies for drill targeting, and the dynamics of project turnover and service provider business models in the exploration industry.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Chief Executive Officer of a junior mineral exploration company with global experience, particularly in copper exploration in Chile and the Congo, and a track record of leading early-stage exploration projects and managing geophysical and drilling campaigns.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert is cautiously optimistic about the potential of their exploration projects, balancing high risk with the possibility of significant discoveries.
- They express frustration with the current funding environment for junior copper explorers, noting the difficulty in raising capital despite strong demand for copper.
- The expert is open to adopting new technologies, such as AI-driven geophysical data processing, especially where it can improve drill targeting and efficiency.

TAKEAWAYS

- The expert's exploration strategy is driven by maximizing the value of every dollar spent, focusing on the size of the prize, cost of testing, and chance of success.
- Geophysical surveys (MMT, IP, passive seismic) are critical tools in data-poor regions like Chile, with airborne surveys used for broad coverage and ground surveys for higher resolution targeting.
- AI and real-time data processing are seen as valuable, particularly for projects with extensive existing data, to optimize drill targeting and reduce costs, though primary data collection remains essential.
- Project turnover is common in the industry, with juniors often trading or dropping projects as priorities shift, and majors frequently making suboptimal exploration decisions.
- The expert sees potential for service providers to take equity or royalties in promising projects, rather than only acting as contractors, to better align incentives and capture upside.

      OVERVIEW

      Market Need: chemical, semiconductor, and energy companies compete to discover new materials that can improve their product
      and margin performance. These customers invest in identifying new catalysts, polymers, coatings, and electronic materials to drive
      differentiation and profitability within their industries requiring agile R&D. Material discovery through traditional methods often takes
      years of laboratory iterations, and internal R&D teams can test only a limited number of candidates. AI-powered materials discovery
      providers generate and simulate candidate materials computationally, enabling customers to screen more candidates in weeks and
      cut material screening time.

      EXAMPLE USE CASE - Specialty Chemicals Customer Discovering a Catalyst Through a Custom Program

      A specialty chemicals manufacturer with £3bn in revenue wants a new catalyst, a material that speeds up chemical reactions in fuel
      refining, that raises its output by 5% compared to the product it uses today. The VP of R&D owns the budget and hires CuspAI after
      a six-month evaluation of competing providers, and the two organizations agree on what the new material must achieve, the project
      milestones, and a fixed project fee with payments tied to each stage.

      CuspAI's scientists convert the performance requirements into instructions for its AI models, and the models generate thousands of
      candidate materials on a computer. Instead of testing each candidate in a laboratory, which would take years, the team uses physics-
      based simulations to predict how each candidate would behave, and the platform ranks the candidates against the customer's
      requirements. Catalyst researchers at the customer review the shortlists each week and adjust the search, and laboratory
      technicians test the most promising candidates in pilot reactors.

      Within eight months, the program identifies three candidates that outperform the customer's existing catalyst in simulation. CuspAI's
      validation partners produce small physical samples of each candidate, and the customer tests them under real refinery conditions.
      The manufacturer scales the winning candidate into full production and pays CuspAI a royalty on the materials it produces with the
      discovery. The customer's Director of R&D uses the results to justify expanding the program to a second catalyst family.

      COMMENTARY

      Customer Profile: this segment includes chipmakers and electronic and semiconductor equipment producers that engage CuspAI
      to discover materials applied around the chip. Customers use CuspAI’s offerings to discover photoresists, polishing compounds,
      packaging materials, and thermal interface materials to manage high heat loads in high-performance computing and AI hardware.

      Demand Drivers:

      Customers in this segment do not attempt to replace silicon as it is well-understood and a better substitute is less likely to be
      manufactured at scale, limiting CuspAI’s applications to this segment. However, they face sustained competitive pressure to
      innovate quickly as the sector is growing and chip buyers prioritize the most efficient products over the less costly. This makes
      chipmakers invest in R&D efforts around innovative materials to support chip performance required to remain competitive. CuspAI
      covers chip-optimizing applications through its MIRA platform, enabling it to capture this demand.

      Semiconductors & Electronics customers' R&D budgets are growing quickly as increased demand for chips is driving increased
      earnings. This makes customers in this segment less price-sensitive than chemical buyers, enabling CuspAI to charge higher prices.

      ACV Dynamics: semiconductor customers allocated less than 1% of R&D budgets to AI-powered materials discovery in 2025 due to
      strict system integration requirements. Every material is tightly integrated into manufacturing workflows, limiting how many new
      materials each customer can absorb in a year. This drives lower ACVs in this segment as annual testing volume and platform usage
      are limited by integration feasibility.

      Growth: CuspAI is expected to grow revenue at a CAGR of 40% to 60% until 2029 in this segment, driven by increased adoption of
      AI-powered material discovery and new customer acquisition. The share of customers’ R&D spend allocated to providers is
      expected to reach approximately 5% by 2029 as platforms demonstrate initial ROI in specific use cases and customers increase
      spend. Further growth could be limited by the integrated structure of semiconductor manufacturing.

      COMMENTARY

      This product includes access to CuspAI's models and simulation tools through a subscription which is typically bundled with
      consulting services like model guidance and human-led technical support.

      Value Proposition and Demand:

      The MIRA platform enables customers to reduce material screening time by 20% to 25% and trial-and-error laboratory iterations by
      approximately 15%. Faster screening shortens the length of R&D projects and facilitates faster applications of new materials. This
      enables customers to remain competitive in product launches where short time to market is required, driving higher demand for
      CuspAI.

      CuspAI’s model uses physics-based simulations to model structure-property relationships, enabling customers’ R&D teams to
      validate inputs, outputs, and property constraints. The platform also has a high external audit defensibility due to its explainable and
      transparent modeling rather than the probabilistic text generation provided by general AI assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity.
      Customers in regulated industries require validated and explainable models to withstand auditor scrutiny, driving demand for
      specialized discovery platforms and retention.

      Customers who prefer process IP confidentiality often prefer platform access over custom programs. Internal platform operation
      enables customers to maintain process knowledge in-house rather than sharing R&D processes and expertise with external
      consultants. Basic platform access enables CuspAI to serve customers that prefer to retain proprietary research information within
      their own organizations.

      ACV Dynamics: Platform access has contract values more than 50% smaller than custom programs due to lower service levels. This
      also drives lower cost to serve customers in this segment and enables CuspAI to increase its margins.

      Growth: CuspAI is expected to grow at 20% to 30% in 2026 in this segment as new customers must purchase platform access to
      engage in custom programs. It is expected to grow at a CAGR of 10% to 15% in this segment between 2027 and 2028 as it focuses
      on advisory and co-development engagements. The software platform has not reached the maturity level where most customers are
      able to operate it without using consulting services like human-led scientific support given continuous changes in the technology.

      and energy costs rise without corresponding revenue growth, CuspAI’s margins could decline.

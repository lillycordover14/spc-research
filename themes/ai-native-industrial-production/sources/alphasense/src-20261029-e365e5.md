SUMMARY
Howmet Aerospace Inc.

 reported third-quarter revenue of $2.70 billion and increased its full-year 2026 financial guidance across all primary metrics. Management reported record organic revenue growth of 25%, with the gas turbine market growing 45% and commercial aerospace growing 32% year over year. The company increased its share repurchase authorization by $5 billion and raised its 2026 free cash flow outlook to $2 billion. CEO John Plant stated that the company is expanding manufacturing capacity to meet demand for turbine blades that is currently sold out through the early 2030s for certain customers.
Plant reported that the company now expects to double industrial gas turbine sales within three years from a 2025 base, an acceleration from the previous three-to-five-year timeframe.
Regarding the GE partnership, CEO John Plant stated, "We are actually poised to further increase capacity for GE, which will take two years to install, and then there will be a multi-year runway after that."
Management noted that major gas turbine customers have reported being sold out through 2031, with 2032 capacity expected to be full within one or two quarters.
Plant highlighted production increases in the defense sector, stating, "The most important growth vector as a percent of sales of the one sector of defense sales is that of missile parts."
In the Forged Wheels segment, Patrick Winterlich noted that margins reflected the "dilutive effect of sharply higher aluminum cost pass-through."
The company reported that its diluted share count fell to 399.5 million shares, reflecting a reduction of over 100 million shares over the last eight years.
INDUSTRY GLOSSARY
IGT
: Industrial Gas Turbines, which are land-based turbines used for power generation in utilities and data centers.
CAM
: A recent acquisition involving aerospace-grade fastening systems assets.
Brunner
: An acquisition that expanded the company's capabilities in the fastening systems segment.
OE
: Original Equipment, referring to components supplied to manufacturers for new aircraft or equipment production.
MRO
: Maintenance, Repair, and Overhaul, the secondary market for replacement parts and engine services.
LEAP and GTF
: Next-generation engine programs for narrowbody aircraft that drive significant demand for high-temperature turbine components.
Time on Wing
: The duration an aircraft engine can remain in service before requiring a shop visit or maintenance.

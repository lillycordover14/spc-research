 reported third-quarter results that surpassed guidance for revenue and adjusted EBITDA, driven by unexpected shipment growth and the rapid integration of Olympic Steel. Management reported that the company achieved its first-year merger synergy target ahead of schedule, reaching an annualized run rate of $52 million. While the broader industrial economy remained uneven, demand from data center infrastructure and power generation projects provided a significant offset to seasonal softness. The company continues to prioritize capital allocation toward debt reduction and dividend maintenance while executing a strategic shift toward high-margin transactional business.
CEO Lehner reported that the margin differential between a transactional pound and a program pound "has gotten up to about 1,000 basis points," illustrating the current profitability gap between market segments.
Marabito stated that approximately 90% of Olympic Steel's inventory and transaction data is now incorporated into Ryerson's information system, enabling coordinated fulfillment across the network.
The company is progressing on its Connecticut consolidation, which brings together Olympic Milford and Ryerson Specialty Alloys to create a stronger operating platform by the first quarter of 2027.
Management noted that North American shipments in the data center sector increased by approximately 65% in the third quarter compared with the first quarter of 2026.
Lehner identified "tangible evidence of the manufacturing revitalization" as more manufacturers return sourcing and production to the U.S., a trend the company expects to support through its processing network.
Manson indicated that 36 administrative positions were eliminated through attrition by the end of the third quarter, resulting in $9 million in annualized savings.
Greiff noted that hot roll carbon steel supply is expected to remain tight through the first half of 2027, favoring distributors with strong mill relationships.
INDUSTRY GLOSSARY
Adjusted EBITDA
: A non-GAAP financial metric that measures earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, further adjusted for one-time or non-recurring items.
Basis Points
: A unit of measure for percentages where 100 basis points equals 1 percentage point.
Carbon Steel
: A metal alloy where carbon is the main alloying element, commonly used in structural and manufacturing applications.
Cash Conversion Cycle
: The amount of time, in days, it takes a company to convert its investments in inventory and other resources into cash flows from sales.
LIFO (Last-In, First-Out)
: An inventory accounting method where the most recently produced or purchased items are the first ones recorded as sold.
Service Center
: A facility that purchases bulk metal from mills, processes it into specific shapes or sizes, and distributes it to end users.
Transactional Business
: Spot-market sales typically involving smaller volumes and immediate delivery, often carrying higher margins than long-term contracts.

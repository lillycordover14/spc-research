**Company Overview**
- Wire harness manufacturer, founded about three years before the July 2026 profile.
- Rethinks the full workflow, from quoting and procurement to engineering, work instructions, quality control, and production.
- Focus: low-to-medium volume production of highly engineered assemblies.

**Founder and Leadership**
- Jordan Black, CEO, grew up in Southern California. Early jobs repairing rides at the Santa Monica Pier and restaurant work. Graduated from college in Wisconsin. Worked at Ford.
- Joined SpaceX's wire harness organization, supporting internal production and supplier development. Later led R&D for avionics manufacturing (harnesses, PCB assemblies, electromechanical systems, test racks, machining).
- Started Senra after seeing harness build times and quality limit SpaceX's ramp-up. Built the first harnesses on the carpeted floor of his apartment.

**Facilities and Headcount**
- Two manufacturing facilities, about 95,000 sq ft combined (locations not named).
- About 190 employees, with growth to roughly 300 expected by year-end.

**Customers and Markets**
- Target segments: aerospace, defense, industrial. Sweet spot about 100 to 1,000 units per run. Customers not named.
- Growth mainly from existing customers and referrals as engineers, buyers and supplier quality staff moved between companies. No dedicated business development team, historically.
- Selective; does not compete mainly on lowest price; seeks long-term partnerships; positions as a partner in prototype development, not only build-to-print.

**In-House Software: Amp (Automated Manufacturing Platform)**
- Internally developed; touches nearly all operations except finance.
- Quoting: AI reads BOMs in PDF, Excel, PowerPoint, or hand-drawn sketches; imports parts into a library holding engineering and supply chain data for every part quoted or built; an API pulls live pricing, quantities, and lead times from online distributors; supplier quotes compared against distributor data; labor estimates generated automatically; quote turnaround about one day for simple builds with parts in stock, under two weeks when supply chain issues arise; flags engineering conflicts and suggests alternate components.
- Shop floor: generates granular work instructions (wire, cut length, tolerance) on station screens tied to each work order; pushes the correct program to stripping equipment with one click; one machine handles about 100 different stripping operations. Black calls the approach "configuration over automation."
- Quality: acts as the QMS, logging defects, root causes, serial numbers, rework (example: melted insulation during heat-shrink); tracks operation cycle times for industrial engineering; reports first-pass yield of 99%.
- Design changes flow through Amp to engineering, procurement, production planning, machine programming, and quality.
- Repeat builds: copying a work order takes about two seconds once a harness has been built.

**Automation and Equipment**
- Stripping equipment programmed automatically from Amp. No robotics described.

**Culture**
- "Give a damn." Automation frees technicians from paperwork, not replaces them.

**Direct Quotes (Jordan Black, CEO)**
- "We just couldn't get wire harnesses fast enough,"
- "Everything has a digital thread,"
- "I don't want the technician having to even look at the drawing and figure out what the cable length should be,"
- "We come from the OEM industry... We're not just a build-to-print shop, we're a partnership."
- "It takes us two seconds to copy and paste in the work order,"

Contact the press release distributor directly with any enquiries. 1872 Launches Autonomous Steel Fabrication Factory Model in Cincinnati and Closes $15 million Seed Funding Round Business Wire 22 July 2026 6 min read SPCX The O.H.I.O. Fund, investing through its private funds, leads one of Ohio's largest-ever seed rounds to support AI-driven, autonomous manufacturing that meets growing market demand CINCINNATI, July 22, 2026 --( BUSINESS WIRE )--1872, a startup focused on AI-native manufacturing, today announced its opening, introducing what will be an automated steel fabrication factory model in Cincinnati's Camp Washington neighborhood. The company, founded by three former SpaceX engineers, is propelled by a $15 million seed funding round, one of the largest in Ohio history, made through private funds advised by The O.H.I.O. Fund, a private fund investment adviser seeking to generate strong returns for investors while creating economic growth in Ohio. 1872 introduces a unique AI-based factory automation solution to an industry that has struggled to innovate and has been unable to keep up with rising demand for steel products in the modular construction and infrastructure markets. The company's proprietary system integrates AI software that understands the physical world with advanced automation from companies such as Ohio-based Path Robotics. This technology automates heavy fabrication tasks that have traditionally relied on manual labor, now in critically short supply. Early automations are in place at 1872, with the company expecting to achieve its full autonomous vision in 2027. 1872's model plans and orchestrates all downstream welding operations, managing every aspect of the manufacturing process, from purchasing materials and logistics to ingesting 3D designs, controlling robotics and other machines, and moving massive steel components across the shop floor. In an industry that traditionally relies on highly manual and inefficient processes, the company expects to cut lead times for its customers from months or years to weeks for products that typically take months or years to produce. "By converging physical AI with Ohio's history of building things, 1872 represents an incredible opportunity for the type of advanced, tech-first manufacturing that can thrive and expand in our state," said Mark Kvamme, CEO and chief investment officer at The O.H.I.O. Fund. "We're thrilled to partner with and support what the 1872 team is building." The investment also marks a significant milestone for the region's tech ecosystem and its economic landscape. "Camp Washington has been one of Cincinnati's most important industrial districts for more than 150 years, and 1872 proves that its next chapter is being written right here," said Jill Meyer, Founding Partner at The O.H.I.O. Fund. "It signals that Cincinnati means business when it comes to advanced manufacturing investment." "This seed funding from The O.H.I.O. We share that vision with The O.H.I.O. Fund and are excited to share it with the public today." Integrating software, hardware, and physical AI To create its autonomous factory, 1872 will combine AI, software, and robotics, which include: Factory OS : the company's proprietary, AI-based software platform that coordinates the entire product creation process, from materials sourcing and cost estimates to stock availability and production planning, and to the movement of materials, machines, and robots. 1872 agentic AI systems: trained to think like welders, buyers, material planners, logistics professionals, and other domain experts to build, monitor, and maintain Factory OS. Path Robotics, the global leader in physical AI for manufacturing, will provide robotic welding systems, including Obsidian™, Path Robotics' foundational physical AI model that delivers real-time, intelligent, adaptive welding. "The future of manufacturing will be built by companies willing to rethink how critical infrastructure gets made, and 1872 is one of those companies," said Andy Lonsberry, CEO and co-founder of Path Robotics. "Their ambition to modernize heavy manufacturing aligns closely with our mission to bring physical AI to the industries that build the world's most critical infrastructure. Welding remains one of the most difficult manufacturing processes to automate – especially at the scale and level of complexity that 1872 is taking on. We're excited that the 1872 team is embracing Path's solution to meet that challenge and proud to work alongside another Ohio-founded company to strengthen American manufacturing." Rebuilding Ohio's manufacturing heritage Housed in Camp Washington's historic Spring Grove Avenue corridor, 1872's new factory occupies the original 1903 David Hummel Building Company facility. The company was a stone masonry firm that built both Cincinnati's architecturally significant City Hall and Union Terminal. 1872 took its name from the year Andrew Carnegie built the world's first steel mill. The company chose to locate in Ohio for its pipeline of skilled labor and office talent, as well as graduates and robotics experts from top engineering schools such as the University of Cincinnati, The Ohio State University, and nearby Purdue University, Carnegie Mellon University, and others. The state's history of manufacturing, innovation, and heavy industry also drew the team here. Today, 1872 aims to meet the needs of a U.S. market that produces about $350 billion in fabricated metal products annually. Meanwhile, more than 2 million new skilled laborers will be needed to meet surging demand over the next decade, but the American Welding Society expects a shortfall of 320,000 welders by 2030.

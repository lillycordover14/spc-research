- 1872, an AI-native manufacturing startup, announced the opening of an automated steel fabrication factory model in Cincinnati's Camp Washington neighborhood; release calls it one of the first autonomous steel fabrication factories in the US.
- Founded by three former SpaceX engineers. Co-founder and CEO: Dan Summers.
- Funding: closed a $15 million seed round, described as one of the largest in Ohio history, led by private funds advised by The O.H.I.O. Fund (private fund adviser managed by TOF Manager, LLC). O.H.I.O. Fund leadership: Mark Kvamme (CEO/CIO), Jill Meyer and Mike Venerable (founding partners).
- No valuation, headcount, revenue, pricing, margins or customer names disclosed.
- Facility: Camp Washington, Spring Grove Avenue corridor; original 1903 David Hummel Building Company facility (stone masonry firm that built Cincinnati City Hall and Union Terminal).
- Name references 1872, year Andrew Carnegie built the world's first steel mill (per release).
- Ohio chosen for skilled labor pipeline and office talent: University of Cincinnati, Ohio State, Purdue, Carnegie Mellon graduates and robotics experts.
- Timeline: early automations in place now; full autonomous vision expected in 2027; first factory is a prototype for an end-to-end production line for fabricated metal parts; later factories planned and tech to be scaled across manufacturing industries.
- Factory OS (proprietary, in-house): AI software platform coordinating the full production process: materials sourcing and cost estimates; stock availability and production planning; movement of materials, machines and robots.
- 1872 agentic AI systems: designed to think like welders, buyers, material planners and logistics staff; they build, monitor and maintain Factory OS.
- Automated workflows: purchasing, logistics, ingesting 3D designs, controlling robotics and machines, moving steel components across the shop floor.
- Target markets: modular construction and infrastructure.
- Partnership: Path Robotics (Ohio) provides robotic welding systems including its Obsidian physical AI model for real-time adaptive welding.
- Claimed impact: cut customer lead times from months or years to weeks.
- Market context: US fabricated metal products market ~$350B annually; 2M+ new skilled workers needed over next decade; American Welding Society projects shortfall of 320,000 welders by 2030.
- O.H.I.O. Fund: celebrated two years in late June 2026; $647M raised; $219M deployed through March 31, 2026; 33 investments across companies, real estate, infrastructure, funds; focus on advanced manufacturing, biotech, real estate, infrastructure.
- Quotes: Mark Kvamme: "By converging physical AI with Ohio's history of building things, 1872 represents an incredible opportunity". Dan Summers: "Our goal is to create a new model and foundation for American manufacturing". Andy Lonsberry (CEO, Path Robotics): "Welding remains one of the most difficult manufacturing processes to automate". Mike Venerable: "This team helped scale SpaceX through a period of extraordinary technical and commercial growth." Jill Meyer: "It signals that Cincinnati means business when it comes to advanced manufacturing investment."

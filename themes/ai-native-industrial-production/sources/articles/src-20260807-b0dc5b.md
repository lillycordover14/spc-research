- Hadrian raised a $1.37bn Series D valuing the company at $7.87bn (headline rounds to $8bn). Valuation was about $1.6bn in January 2026, nearly 5x in seven months. Bloomberg reported talks in June at a mooted $7.5bn.
- JPMorgan's Strategic Investment Group anchored. Participants: Baillie Gifford, T. Rowe Price accounts, Morgan Stanley Wealth Management, funds run by Apollo; also Founders Fund, Andreessen Horowitz, 1789 Capital (Donald Trump Jr. a partner). Mainstream institutions rather than niche defence funds.
- Founded 2021 per article (other sources say 2020). Founder Chris Power.
- Describes manufacturing as a software problem; runs highly automated plants under a factories-as-a-service model so a defence prime can buy production capacity without building a plant.
- Software system Opus handles scheduling, tool routing and inspection; company says Opus cuts lead times from months to days.
- The equity round will not fund factory construction; Hadrian is arranging a separate line of credit for that and plans to repeat this split.
- Customers: Lockheed Martin, RTX, Anduril. US Navy tapped Hadrian to mass-produce submarine parts in Alabama.
- Footprint: almost three million square feet across four sites.
- Plans to nearly triple workforce to 2,000 within a year; teams in Australia, Asia and Europe by December to make parts for NATO.
- Defence-tech startups raised ~$35bn in H1 2026; Anduril valued >$60bn, Shield AI $12.7bn; Trump plan to spend up to $1.5tn rebuilding the military.
- Power does not plan to test public markets until 2029 at the earliest.
- Quotes: Chris Power: company is "early" (via Axios); "Production is now the frontline of deterrence."
- Not stated: revenue, margins, pricing, growth rate.

Heron Power, a U.S.-based manufacturer of medium-voltage power electronics, has announced a pilot agreement with RWE Americas to deploy Heron Link, its solid-state, transformer-based power conversion system. Under the pilot, Heron Link will replace low-voltage inverters and 60 Hz transformers at RWE's battery storage facility co-located with the Pyron Wind Farm in Scurry County, Texas. Installation is targeted for April 2027. The pilot is intended to test the technology ahead of a possible supply agreement supporting solar and storage projects.

A target of 500 megawatts of reserved capacity

The agreement outlines a path for RWE to reserve at least 500 megawatts (MW) of additional capacity annually from Heron Power's U.S. manufacturing facilities, subject to the parties finalizing definitive purchase agreements. If finalized, this would position RWE among the first utility-scale operators to adopt Heron's solid-state transformer technology at scale. According to Drew Baglino, chief executive officer and founder of Heron Power, this is the first utility-scale pilot agreement signed for Heron Link, and it was concluded with one of the largest power solutions providers in the country. He said the Link device is being manufactured in the United States, in partnership with critical infrastructure developers such as RWE. Andrea Hu-Bianco, senior vice president of engineering at RWE Americas, said Heron Power's solid-state transformers would advance the future power electronics ecosystem, enabling the transition to 800V DC data center architectures and serving as a key building block for the integration of solar photovoltaic and battery energy storage systems.

Why Heron Link

Today, utility-scale solar and storage projects use low-voltage inverters and medium-voltage transformers to convert direct current into grid-ready, medium-voltage alternating current. Heron Link replaces both components with a modular 5 MW medium-voltage inverter that delivers 98.6% DC-to-MV efficiency and 99.5% lifetime availability, according to Heron Power. The company says power plants that integrate Heron Link would benefit from stronger economics, a domestic supply chain, and native grid-forming controls that support local grid stability.

A California factory under construction

Heron Power, headquartered in California, also plans to manufacture there. In August 2026, the company announced Heron Factory One, a 286,000-square-foot facility in Morgan Hill designed to produce up to 10,000 Heron Links a year, or more than 40 gigawatts (GW) of annual capacity. The plant would require an investment of more than $100 million and is expected to add over 600 jobs, with mass production beginning in late 2027. According to Heron Power, domestic manufacturers currently supply less than 10% of the utility-scale U.S. solar inverter market, and few of them would be able to meet the demand of a large renewable energy portfolio.

A portfolio of more than 13 GW for RWE

RWE operates more than 13 GW of operating capacity across 27 U.S. states, including approximately 6 GW of solar photovoltaic and 1.2 GW of battery energy storage assets. As the company continues to expand its generation and storage footprint, it is evaluating technologies that could improve system performance, simplify project architectures and enhance lifecycle economics. The Heron Link pilot represents an opportunity to assess a U.S.-manufactured solution within a utility-scale operating environment.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement has finalized a contract to buy several of the highly recognizable Boston Dynamics “dog” robots from the Waltham robotics company for $1.3 million.
ICE plans to buy four remotely operated, quadruped Spot robots from Boston Dynamics to search tunnels at border crossings, according to the 
contract
 with Boston Dynamics, which was publicly listed Monday.
Since the robots can be used in areas unsafe for humans, “procuring the Quadruped Unmanned Ground Vehicle and accessories will provide [the Department of Homeland Security] with a remotely operated robotic capability” to combat transnational human trafficking and drug smuggling as well as the presence of cartels and foreign gangs, the contract said.
Advertisement:
ICE is using the robots for its investigations of transnational crimes through its Homeland Security Investigations and not its Enforcement & Removal Operations, a spokesperson for Boston Dynamics said.
“For several years, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has used Boston Dynamics’ Spot robots to help keep people safe,” Boston Dynamics said in a statement. “Regardless of any political implications or personal feelings about the organizations involved, if our robot can help keep people safe, we believe it should.”
The robots are being outfitted with a CBRNe kit, which has sensors that detect chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, and explosive hazards. Although the kit is made by the Connecticut-based radiation detection company RADeCO, the company is a partner of Boston Dynamics and must adhere to its terms and conditions, the Massachusetts company said.
Advertisement:
“The robots are legally prohibited from being weaponized or used for purposes of harm or intimidation. Any suggestion that these robots will be used to support immigrant detentions or deportations is false,” Boston Dynamics said. 
ICE hopes to use the customized robots to “detect radiation, toxic gas leaks, and explosive threats in urban environments or disaster zones, and also to enable remote observation, inspection, documentation, and hazard assessment,” the contract said.
 If ICE were to misuse the robots, “we would take immediate action. That would include specific steps like voiding the warranty, refusing all future software updates, terminating their license, taking any appropriate legal action available to us, and likely never selling to that customer again,” Boston Dynamics said.
DHS did not respond to a request for comment Wednesday night.

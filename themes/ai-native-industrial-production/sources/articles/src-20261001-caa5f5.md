UI sophomore Mia Boltz shoots and scores to tie the match up 1-1 | Idaho Athletics								

For University of Idaho women’s soccer midfielder Mia Boltz, the 2026 season has been a steady progression toward becoming an important contributor for the Vandals. The sophomore from Shoreline, Washington, has provided Idaho with energy and attacking presence from the midfield, and her performance against Gonzaga on Sept. 25 offered one of the clearest examples of her impact this season. 

As a midfielder, Boltz contributes on both sides of the ball, helping Idaho transition into attack while looking for opportunities to push the ball forward.

Boltz entered the Sept. 25 matchup with Gonzaga having already established herself as one of Idaho’s more active attacking players. Through the first 10 matches of the season, she had recorded one goal, three points and 15 shots, including seven shots on goal. 

Her seven shots on target ranked among the team’s leaders, demonstrating her willingness to get involved in the attack and put pressure on opposing goalkeepers. Her numbers showed that she was actively creating chances rather than simply waiting for opportunities to develop around her.

That attacking mentality became particularly important when the Vandals faced Gonzaga at our very own P1FCU Kibbie Dome.

Idaho entered the contest with a 6-2-1 record, while Gonzaga came in at 5-4-1. The Bulldogs struck first when Amelia Severn scored in the 36th minute, giving Gonzaga a 1-0 advantage heading into halftime. Idaho, however, responded quickly after the break.

Just three minutes into the second half, Boltz provided the equalizer.

In the 48th minute, senior Georgia Whitehead found Boltz with a pass through the box. Boltz finished the opportunity for her first goal of the 2026 season, leveling the match at 1-1. The goal was especially significant because it came when Idaho needed an attacking response after falling behind in the first half. 

The quick response gave the Vandals renewed momentum and allowed them to play the remainder of the match with the score level.

The goal also highlighted the connection between Boltz and Whitehead, who has been another important attacking presence for the Vandals. Whitehead’s pass gave Boltz the opportunity, and Boltz’s finish ensured that Idaho would leave the match with a point rather than a loss. 

The play showed the importance of chemistry within Idaho’s attack, with Whitehead recognizing the opportunity and Boltz finishing the chance.

Boltz finished the Gonzaga match with one shot, and that shot was on target, while playing 51 minutes. Idaho ultimately outshot Gonzaga 9-6 and earned four corner kicks compared with Gonzaga’s two. Neither team found a winning goal, resulting in a 1-1 draw and moving the Vandals to 6-2-2 on the season.

While a single goal does not tell the entire story of Boltz’s season, the numbers illustrate how she has contributed beyond simply scoring. Her 15 shots through the first ten matches showed a willingness to attack, while seven shots on goal demonstrated an ability to turn those opportunities into attempts that challenge the opposition. 

For a midfielder, that production can be valuable even when it does not appear on the scoresheet, as creating chances and making attacking runs can help open opportunities for teammates.

The Gonzaga match also fit into a strong stretch for Idaho. The Vandals had built momentum throughout September, including victories over Grand Canyon and UC Davis before facing Gonzaga. 

The team entered the Gonzaga game having won its previous two matches, and the draw extended Idaho’s unbeaten run to three games. The result kept Idaho from leaving the match empty-handed and gave the Vandals another positive result to carry forward.

For Boltz, the season represents another step in her development as a college midfielder. Listed at 5-foot-3, she brings a different physical profile to the field, but her statistics show a player willing to contribute offensively. 

Her 15 shots ranked third on the team through the Gonzaga match, behind Georgia Whitehead and Makenna Stuart, while her seven shots on goal tied Whitehead for second on the team. Her production suggests that her influence comes from her movement, willingness to attack and ability to take advantage of opportunities.

Her role against Gonzaga also illustrated the demands placed on Idaho’s midfielders. Boltz started the match and was substituted in the first half before returning to the field to begin the second half. 

She later re-entered the match in the final 15 minutes as Idaho searched for a potential winning goal. Despite moving in and out of the lineup, Boltz remained involved in an important match as Idaho continued looking for a second goal.

Although the Vandals ultimately settled for a draw, Boltz’s equalizer became the defining moment of Idaho’s night. It was her first goal of the season, her second career goal according to the available 2026 statistical record, and a goal that helped Idaho maintain its strong early-season record. 

Boltz did not need multiple chances to make an impact; when the opportunity came, she capitalized.

As Idaho continued its 2026 campaign, Boltz’s season offered a glimpse of the impact a developing midfielder can have without needing to dominate the stat sheet every night. 

Her willingness to shoot, ability to put attempts on target and timely finish against Gonzaga all contributed to Idaho’s success. As the season progresses, continued development in those areas could give Boltz an increasingly important role within the Idaho attack.

For the Vandals, the 1-1 result was another chapter in a promising season. For Boltz, it was a standout moment — a chance to step forward when her team needed an answer and deliver one of the most memorable goals of Idaho’s September schedule.

Investment of the decade: Anduril Industries kicks off $3.7B project in Maryland

10/6/2026

Daniel Leaderman

Article Topics:

Workforce

Job Creation

Central Region

Maryland Assets

Manufacturing

Military and Federal

Defense manufacturer 
Anduril Industries
 is bringing a major software-defined shipyard to Tradepoint Atlantic in Baltimore County, which will be the largest single private investment and the largest job creator in Maryland in over a decade.

Anduril plans to invest $3.7 billion in private capital in its new facility, which will expand America’s submarine industrial base and continue a legacy of naval shipbuilding at Sparrows Point, where Bethlehem Steel produced steel and ships during both World Wars.

The company’s investments are expected to deliver over $2 billion in annual economic output, including an estimated $312 million in wages each year. Overall, the project will support more than 14,000 jobs, with Anduril creating more than 3,100 direct jobs in Maryland.

“Sparrows Point helped build the fleet that won World War II. With Arsenal-2, Anduril will return large-scale shipbuilding to a site that can once again define American naval power. This facility will make Baltimore County a critical part of America’s submarine industrial base, adding the production capacity that the Navy needs to meet its Virginia-class requirements. Maryland offers the workforce, the infrastructure, and the industrial heritage this mission demands. We’re grateful to Governor Moore and state and local leaders for their partnership in making this investment possible.”
Brian Schimpf, co-founder and CEO of Anduril

“This massive new project will deliver the largest private investment and create the most jobs in Maryland in over a decade—driving billions of dollars in economic activity that will boost Maryland families not just in Baltimore County, but throughout the state,” said Gov. Moore. “With this facility, thousands of Marylanders will have new pathways to work, wages, and wealth through advanced training and high-skilled maritime manufacturing jobs. We thank Anduril for choosing Maryland, and we’re ready to build the future of the aerospace and defense economy together.”

Maryland’s history with shipbuilding dates back to the late 1700s, when the shipyards in Fells Point built the legendary Baltimore Clippers, the fastest wooden ships of their time which were used during the War of 1812. Sparrows Point rose as a global leader in the late 1800s, producing major vessels including the first ship to transit the Panama Canal, followed by the Bethlehem-Fairfield Shipyard of Baltimore becoming the nation’s top Liberty ship builder in the 1940s. In more recent years, Maryland began practicing advanced maintenance and autonomous vessel work, keeping the state at the center of maritime engineering.

Revitalizing naval manufacturing in the Baltimore region is a fitting task for Anduril – the company shares its name with a legendary sword in J.R.R. Tolkien’s The Lord of the Rings. The once-broken sword is re-forged and wielded in the fight to defend Middle-Earth.

Critical Materials Group Acquires Rocky Mountain Scientific Laboratory&nbsp;&nbsp;citybiz

[page text]
Critical Materials Group
 has 
acquired Rocky Mountain Scientific Laboratory
, combining defense-focused energetics research and testing with CMG’s manufacturing platform as the company seeks to shorten the path from laboratory development to domestic production.

Financial terms of the acquisition were not disclosed.

Rocky Mountain Scientific Laboratory, or RMSL, provides national security research and rapid-development capabilities for military and law enforcement agencies. The acquisition brings its research, energetic materials formulation and testing operations into the same organization as CMG’s modular, automation-ready manufacturing capabilities.

Travis Swanson
, CEO and co-founder of RMSL, will become chief science officer of Critical Materials Group. Swanson was already a co-founder of CMG while leading RMSL, and the transaction formally brings the two organizations together.

CMG CEO and co-founder 
Kevin Capozzoli
 said the combination is intended to address a persistent challenge in energetics manufacturing: moving newly developed materials from research environments into qualified, higher-volume production.

“Energetics programs lose most of their time in the handoff between the people who develop a material and the people who have to produce it at volume,” Capozzoli said. “Bringing RMSL into CMG puts that work in one place, with Travis leading the science and our platforms built to manufacture what his team develops.”

Energetic materials development involves more than discovering or formulating a new material. Moving into production can require additional work around manufacturing processes, qualification, safety engineering and scale-up.

When those functions are distributed among separate organizations, development data and manufacturing requirements must move between different teams and facilities. CMG is seeking to reduce those handoffs by integrating laboratory development and manufacturing within one company.

RMSL will provide the research, formulation and testing side of that model, while CMG will provide the production infrastructure required to manufacture energetic materials at larger scale.

“The time is now to invest in the development and production of energetic materials and munitions,” Swanson said. “This merger brings both sides of the problem under one roof: RMSL researches and develops the materials, while CMG manufactures them safely at scale.”

Founded in 2025 and based in Texas, CMG is developing modular production platforms intended to modernize domestic energetics manufacturing. The company’s approach incorporates automation and safety systems into manufacturing facilities designed to support scalable production.

The RMSL acquisition adds scientific and development capabilities upstream of those production systems.

That vertical integration is central to the strategic rationale for the deal. Instead of operating solely as a manufacturing company, CMG will now have internal capabilities extending from materials research and testing through production.

The company said the combined model is intended to accelerate the movement of energetic materials from initial concepts into qualified production while adding domestic manufacturing capacity alongside existing government-owned and prime contractor facilities.

The announcement did not disclose RMSL’s employee count, revenue, laboratory footprint or current contract portfolio. CMG also did not provide specific production capacity targets or a timetable for expanding manufacturing following the acquisition.

Swanson’s appointment as chief science officer establishes responsibility for the combined company’s scientific development activities while maintaining continuity between RMSL’s research operations and CMG’s manufacturing strategy.

The transaction also consolidates two organizations whose leadership was already connected through Swanson’s role as a CMG co-founder.

For Critical Materials Group, acquiring RMSL expands its business from production infrastructure into a more vertically integrated energetics platform. The company will now combine research, formulation and testing with manufacturing systems designed to move materials into production.

That structure positions CMG around a specific national security manufacturing challenge: reducing the organizational and technical gaps between developing energetic materials in the laboratory and producing them safely and consistently at scale.

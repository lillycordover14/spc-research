It remains to be seen how much the job market will be affected by Hurricane Isaias hitting the Panhandle.
New unemployment claims fell slightly in Florida to start October.

The U.S. Department of Labor (
DOL
) reports there were 5,111 initial jobless claims made in the Sunshine State for the 
week ending Oct. 3
. That’s down by 16 filings from the 
week ending Sept. 26
 when there were 5,127.

It’s the second week in a row there was a decline in new claims. While the drop in initial filings was slight, it’s still a reflection of the general trend that saw decreasing new jobless claims for most of the Summer heading into the Fall.

The latest Florida figures cut against the national trend that saw an increase in first-time unemployment claims across the country to start October. There were 170,333 new filings nationally for the week ending Oct. 3. That’s up by 11,994 filings, a 7.6% increase from the previous week. DOL analysts expected a slightly larger increase of 13,725.

In the year-over-year comparison, the national figure dropped substantially. The number of initial unemployment claims for the comparable week in 2025 was 207,124. That accounts for a drop of 36,791 first-time jobless claims in the annual figure.

While new jobless filings have been usually positive for Florida for the past few months, that could change with 
Hurricane Isaias
 hitting the Panhandle on Friday night. It’s traditionally taken several weeks to account for Florida workers filing for unemployment who’ve lost jobs due to hurricanes.

Unemployment numbers have been heading in the upbeat direction for the past three months. The Florida jobless rate registered 4.5% in August, according to 
FloridaCommerce
, the state’s economic development agency. That’s the latest decline that has been trending since Spring. The figure peaked in April and May when the rate topped out at 4.8% for each of those months.

That figure has been falling since, dropping one-tenth of a percentage point each month from June through August.

Florida’s unemployment rate still remains higher than the national level. September’s jobless figure across America came in at 4.2%, said the 
U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics
. While that’s lower than Florida’s figure, it’s the first time the national rate has ticked up in several months.

The rate across the country had been dropping consistently for most of this year until July and August when it held steady at 4.1% and then increased to 4.2% last month.

Florida’s September unemployment rate is scheduled to be released within two weeks.

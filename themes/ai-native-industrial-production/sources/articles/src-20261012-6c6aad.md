Quick Reference: Planting Spring Bulbs

Plant when the soil at 6 inches has cooled below 55F, usually about two weeks before the first frost

Bulbs need roughly six weeks in the ground before it freezes solid, so the first hard freeze is not the deadline

North: mid September to late October. Midwest and Northeast: October to mid November. South: late November into December

Zones 8 and warmer: pre-chill tulips and hyacinths for 10 to 12 weeks in the refrigerator, plant in December or January

Depth: three times the height of the bulb. Tulips 6 to 8 inches, daffodils 6 inches, crocus 3 to 4 inches

Pointed end up, then water once and leave them alone

Tulips and daffodils want a cold winter in the ground and a cool, moist autumn to grow roots first. That gives a planting window with a soft opening and a firm close: open once the soil cools below about 55F, close when the ground freezes solid. In most of the country that window runs four to eight weeks, and it opens later than most people think. Planting into warm September soil invites rot and early sprouting. Here is the window for every state.

Soil Temperature, Not the Calendar

The trigger is soil temperature at planting depth, about 6 inches down. Below 55F the bulb grows roots without pushing a shoot. Above it, especially with autumn rain, tulips in particular are prone to fungal rot. The soil at that depth cools about two weeks behind the air, which is why the first light frost is a handy signal: by then the ground is usually ready. Our 
soil temperature chart
 has the readings for vegetable crops if you keep a probe thermometer.

Below 55F at planting depth the bulb grows roots without pushing a shoot.

The deadline is the ground freezing solid, which comes weeks after the first hard freeze of the air. Bulbs need about six weeks of root growth before that. If you are late, plant anyway: a bulb in cold ground beats a bulb in a paper bag every time.

Warm Winter States: The Refrigerator Trick

Tulips, hyacinths and most crocus need 10 to 14 weeks below about 45F to flower properly. From roughly Dallas, Atlanta and Charleston southward, and across the low deserts and coastal California, the ground never gets that cold for that long. The fix is the crisper drawer: bulbs in a paper bag for 10 to 12 weeks, then planted in December or January into the coolest soil of the year. Keep them away from fruit, because ripening apples give off ethylene, which kills the flower bud inside the bulb. Treat them as one season’s display.

Warm winter states chill tulips for 10 to 12 weeks in a paper bag, away from fruit.

Daffodils are the exception and the reason southern gardens still have spring bulbs. Most varieties, and the tazetta and jonquil types especially, need little or no chilling and come back for decades. Dutch iris, alliums, snowflakes (Leucojum) and Spanish bluebells behave the same way.

How to Plant Them

Depth:
 three times the height of the bulb, measured to the base. Tulips and daffodils go 6 to 8 inches deep, small bulbs such as crocus and grape hyacinth 3 to 4 inches. Deeper is safer in cold states and in gardens with squirrels.
Spacing:
 4 to 6 inches apart for tulips and daffodils, closer for small bulbs. Odd-numbered groups of seven or more read better than rows.
Drainage first:
 bulbs rot in waterlogged soil. On heavy clay, plant on a slight mound or work in grit.
Pointed end up.
 If you cannot tell, plant on the side; the shoot will find its way.
Water once
 after planting to settle the soil, then leave them. Autumn rain does the rest.
Fertilizer:
 a little bone meal or bulb food in the planting hole is traditional and does no harm. The bulb already carries next spring’s flower; feeding matters more after flowering.
Squirrels and voles:
 they dig tulips and crocus, not daffodils. A sheet of chicken wire laid over the bed and removed in late winter is the old remedy that still works.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is it too late to plant tulip bulbs?

When the ground is frozen solid and you cannot dig. Until then, plant. Bulbs set out late, even in December in the Midwest, usually flower on time, if a little shorter. They need about six weeks of root growth before the soil freezes, so the earlier within the window the better.

Can I plant bulbs before the first frost?

Yes, as long as the soil has cooled below about 55F at 6 inches. In the North that is often mid to late September, before any frost. In the South, waiting for the first frost is a safer signal because warm, wet autumn soil rots tulips.

Do daffodils need to be chilled in warm climates?

Most do not, which is why they are the reliable spring bulb from Georgia to Texas. Tazetta and jonquil types need the least cold. Tulips and hyacinths do need 10 to 14 weeks below about 45F, so pre-chill them in a refrigerator in warm winter states.

How deep should tulip and daffodil bulbs be planted?

About three times the height of the bulb, so 6 to 8 inches for a full-size tulip or daffodil. Plant toward the deeper end in cold states and where squirrels dig.

Should I water bulbs after planting?

Once, to settle the soil around them. After that autumn rain is enough. Overwatering in warm soil is the quickest way to rot a tulip.

Will tulips come back every year?

Species tulips and Darwin hybrids often return for several seasons in cold states with dry summers. Most other tulips weaken after the first year. Daffodils, by contrast, multiply and can outlive the gardener.

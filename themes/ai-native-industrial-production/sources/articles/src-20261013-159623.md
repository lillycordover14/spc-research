AMCA agrees to $6.44 million settlement over 2018 data breach
News
13 Oct 2026
The
 
American
 
Medical
 
Collection
 
Agency
 
has
 
agreed
 
to
 
pay
 
$6.44
 
million
 
to
 
settle
 
a
 
class-action
 
lawsuit
 
over
 
a
 
2018
 
data breach
 
that
 
exposed
 
the
 
personal
information
 
of
 
more
 
than
 
20
 
million
 
individuals.

 

The
 
American
 
Medical
 
Collection
 
Agency
 
(AMCA),
 
legally
 
known
 
as
 
Retrieval-Masters
 
Creditors
 
Bureau,
 
was
 
a
 
US-based
 
debt
 
collection
 
firm
 
that
 
recovered small,
 
overdue
 
medical
 
bills
 
for
 
healthcare
 
providers
 
and
 
diagnostic
 
laboratories, including
 
Quest
 
Diagnostics
 
and
 
Labcorp.
 
It
 
collected
 
outstanding
 
patient
payments on
 
behalf
 
of
 
hospitals,
 
labs
 
and
 
medical
 
practices.

 

Between
 
August
 
2018
 
and
 
March
 
2019,
 
AMCA
 
suffered
 
a
 
major
 
data
 
breach
 
after hackers
 
accessed
 
its
 
payment
 
portal
 
and
 
web
 
systems.
 
The
 
attackers
 
stole
sensitive personal,
 
financial
 
and
 
medical
 
information,
 
including
 
names,
 
addresses,
 
birthdates,
 
Social
 
Security
 
numbers
 
and
 
payment
 
details.
 
The
 
incident
affected
 
an estimated
 
21
 
million
 
to
 
27.5
 
million
 
people,
 
mainly
 
patients
 
of
 
Quest
 
Diagnostics
 
and Labcorp,
 
making
 
it
 
one
 
of
 
the
 
largest
 
US
 
healthcare
 
data
breaches
 
and
 
contributing to
 
the
 
company’s
 
bankruptcy.

 

A class action lawsuit was 
brought
 before the
 
U.S. District Court for New Jersey following the cyberattack. The plaintiffs claimed that AMCA did not take adequate and reasonable precautions to safeguard their personal information and failed to implement sufficient cybersecurity protections, which they alleged helped lead to the data breach.

 

While AMCA denied any wrongdoing or negligence, both sides agreed to settle the case to avoid the costs and risks of continued litigation and allow Class members to receive benefits under the settlement.

 

As
 
part
 
of
 
the
 
settlement,
 
AMCA
 
will
 
establish
 
a
 
$6.44
 
million
 
fund
 
to
 
compensate eligible
 
claimants
 
and
 
cover
 
administrative
 
costs,
 
attorneys’
 
fees,
 
taxes
 
and service
 
awards.

 

Under the settlement, eligible individuals may receive an estimated $50 cash payment, subject to adjustments based on valid claims and remaining funds. Alternatively, they can seek up to$5,000 for documented breach-related expenses, including identity theft, fraud and credit monitoring costs. Class members may also opt for two years of medical and healthcare information monitoring through CyEx’s Medical Shield Pro service.

 

The proposed settlement has been granted preliminary approval by the court but has not yet become final. A final approval hearing is scheduled for January 7, when the court will decide whether to approve the settlement.

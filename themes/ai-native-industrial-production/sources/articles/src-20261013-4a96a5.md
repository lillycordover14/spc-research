American Rheinmetall is showcasing a range of combat vehicles, autonomous launchers, unmanned ground systems and ammunition at the Association of the United States Army (AUSA) Annual Meeting & Exposition in Washington, D.C., from October 12 to 14, with 
Zona Militar covering the event on site.
 The company’s display focuses on technologies supporting U.S. Army modernization, including the Lynx XM30 combat vehicle, the HX tactical truck configured for autonomous launcher operations and several robotic platforms intended for logistics and combat support.

At booth #2825, American Rheinmetall is presenting systems developed across the company’s international industrial network, highlighting its work in ground combat, tactical mobility, autonomy and precision effects. Its participation comes as the company expands its U.S. industrial footprint and pursues opportunities in Army modernization programs, combining vehicle manufacturing with technologies developed by Rheinmetall and its industry partners.

One of the principal systems associated with the display is the 
Lynx XM30
, American Rheinmetall’s candidate for the U.S. Army’s XM30 Combat Vehicle program, which aims to replace the M2 Bradley infantry fighting vehicle. The Lynx is built around a modular architecture intended to accommodate different mission systems and future upgrades, while its participation in the program places Rheinmetall alongside General Dynamics Land Systems in the competition to supply the Army’s next-generation combat vehicle.

The XM30 program remains in the prototype and evaluation phase, with the Army assessing competing designs before selecting a replacement for the Bradley. American Rheinmetall’s proposal draws on the wider Lynx vehicle family, adapting its architecture to U.S. requirements while emphasizing protection, mobility and the integration of digital systems for future battlefield operations.

Another major element of the company’s portfolio is the HX tactical truck configured for the 
Common Autonomous Multi-Domain Launcher (CAML)
 concept, which combines a mobile vehicle platform with a modular launcher intended to operate either autonomously or with a crew. Rheinmetall has reported more than 20,000 HX-family vehicles fielded worldwide, while its autonomous driving technologies have been integrated across multiple chassis types and are intended to support operations in environments where limiting personnel exposure is a priority.

The HX-based launcher concept incorporates autonomous navigation, convoy and waypoint driving, teleoperation and automated loading and unloading, with an open architecture intended to accommodate different mission systems and effectors. The platform is designed to provide a mobile launch capability without requiring a separate vehicle design for every weapon system, although specific payload configurations and integration status depend on the individual system being evaluated.

The CAML concept has also been linked to 
Furientis interceptors
, introducing a potential missile option for mobile air defense. A separate report from AUSA 2026 describes a live interceptor firing involving a containerized launch module mounted on a Rheinmetall HX vehicle; however, the available information does not establish that the missile used in that test was Furientis’s F1.0 Tactical Interceptor, nor does it confirm a procurement decision by the U.S. Army.

Furientis is developing the F1.0 Tactical Interceptor around a design intended to support scalable production and lower manufacturing costs, with the company working on key subsystems including the seeker, airframe, control system and solid-propellant rocket motor. The design incorporates forward-mounted canard control surfaces and guidance software using commercially available electronic components, while detailed performance specifications, including the interceptor’s engagement range, have not been publicly established in the material reviewed for this article.

The company’s unmanned ground vehicle portfolio provides another major component of the display, covering platforms designed for resupply, casualty evacuation, electronic warfare and counter-unmanned aircraft system missions. These vehicles are intended to extend the reach of dismounted and maneuver forces while allowing selected support tasks to be performed remotely or autonomously, reducing the need to expose personnel in contested areas.

Among these platforms is the Ox, a modular, autonomy-ready unmanned ground vehicle that Rheinmetall is positioning for squad- and platoon-level missions and which is competing in the U.S. Army’s Ground Ordnance Autonomous Technologies (GOAT) program. Its proposed roles include logistics, casualty evacuation, electronic warfare and counter-drone operations, with its modular architecture intended to support different payloads according to mission requirements.

The medium-class MUSKOX unmanned ground vehicle combines a hybrid-electric chassis with autonomous navigation and AI-driven controls for logistics, sustainment and combat support. Rheinmetall has also linked the platform to the Army’s Project Sustainment Program, while the Mission Master SP, also known as the Mission Master Silent Partner, has been down-selected for the Last Tactical Mile program and is designed to support autonomous resupply and casualty evacuation, with optional amphibious capability.

Rheinmetall’s display also includes the Wild Goose Infantry Modular Robotic System, a platform intended for dismounted forces with a stated payload capacity of 330 pounds and a terrain-dependent range of up to 25 kilometers. Its modular configuration allows it to be adapted to different infantry tasks, complementing the company’s larger robotic vehicles with a system designed for operations closer to the squad level.

Beyond vehicles and robotics, American Rheinmetall is presenting ammunition and weapons technologies, including 155 mm extended-range propellants and rocket-assisted V-LAP projectiles, airburst ammunition and the Highly-Advanced Multi-Mission Rifle, a shoulder-fired, magazine-fed 40 mm grenade launcher. The company is also highlighting its role as a supplier of components, structures and assemblies for U.S. defense programs, including the Multiple Launch Rocket System (MLRS), underscoring the breadth of its manufacturing activities beyond complete vehicle platforms.

Dirac Labs Joins Wisconsin Security Research Consortium to Advance Quantum PNT for Autonomous Defense Platforms&nbsp;&nbsp;Quantum Computing Report

[page text]
University of Wisconsin–Madison
 quantum sensing spinout 
Dirac Labs
 has joined the 
Wisconsin Security Research Consortium (WSRC)
 as an industry affiliate. The partnership focuses on deploying quantum-enabled Positioning, Navigation, and Timing (PNT) systems to support GPS-independent autonomous maritime, subsurface, and defense operations.

Dirac Labs is developing a quantum universal positioning system that utilizes diamond-based quantum sensors to measure Earth’s magnetic field with high precision. Integrated with artificial intelligence models for real-time signal processing, the system turns magnetic anomaly variations into drift-free position coordinates, offering a passive alternative that cannot be jammed or spoofed in GPS-contested environments.

Manufacturing Strategy
• Commercial semiconductor foundry processes
• Bypasses custom fabrication barriers to enable low-cost, scalable sensor production.

The affiliation aligns with the regional 
Area 52
 defense testing initiative, which leverages Lake Michigan’s deepwater geography and FAA-restricted airspace to validate uncrewed surface and subsurface maritime platforms. By manufacturing sensors on standard semiconductor foundry infrastructure and interfacing via standard GPS ports, Dirac Labs aims to bypass traditional cost and scaling barriers that have historically limited quantum sensing to laboratory environments.

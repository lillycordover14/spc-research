- Article: "Old factories could fuel Cincinnati's next manufacturing boom," Axios Cincinnati, Oct 16, 2026, by Casey Weldon.
- 1872 uses AI and robotics to run a steel fabrication factory; name refers to 1872, year Carnegie began his steel empire.
- Operates in a 123-year-old former stone masonry building in Camp Washington, Cincinnati (former David Hummel Building Company site on Spring Grove Avenue).
- Dan Summers, CEO, former SpaceX engineer; moved to Cincinnati in 2022 to be closer to family.
- Toured about 15 local properties before choosing the site. ~30,000 sq ft of production space; rectangular layout suits an automated production line; nearby rail access could eventually help move materials.
- Moved in April (2026), producing by August.
- Melissa Johnson (SVP real estate strategy, The Port): older buildings may need costly upgrades but existing infrastructure saves time and cost vs building from scratch.
- Seed round $15M led by the O.H.I.O. Fund, which also connected Summers with robotics partners. Mike Venerable (founding partner) quoted.
- Headcount: 18 employees; expects 22 by year-end.
- Expansion: Summers hopes to eventually build a much larger second factory in Southwest or Central Ohio.
- Robots handle repetitive tasks; company still needs skilled welders, engineers, technicians.
- Regional context: engineering talent, access to Midwestern steel suppliers, old underused industrial properties.
- Also cites Dainty Foods ($85M, Batavia Township, ~240 jobs) as a separate REDI Cincinnati example.
- Not disclosed: customers, pricing, lead times, margins, revenue, robot models, in-house software details.
- Quotes: Dan Summers: "We moved in here in April, and we were already producing by August"; "the definition of reindustrialization"; "Even an autonomous factory needs highly skilled subject-matter experts." Mike Venerable: "We believe Ohio is going to lead America's manufacturing comeback."

Preferred multiple of price-to-book: Is it justified?
 
 
Iambic Therapeutics is trading on financials that are hard to slot into neat textbook ratios. The stock carries a P/B of -4.9x because the business currently has negative equity, while peers in the US Biotechs group sit at 1.9x on the same yardstick.
 
 
Price to book usually compares a company's market value with its net assets on the balance sheet. For a young biotech like Iambic Therapeutics, heavy R&D spending and cumulative losses can erode equity, so the ratio becomes distorted and less useful as a yardstick for what investors are really paying for pipeline potential.
 
 
 
 
 
The key point is that the market is not lining IAM up against the average US Biotechs P/B of 1.9x at all. With equity in a deficit, the quoted multiple does not give a clean read on what buyers are assuming about future trials, partnerships, or eventual cash generation.
 
 
That kind of profile is better tested against cash flow scenarios than balance sheet book value. This is why investors often look to a discounted cash flow approach when P/B turns negative.

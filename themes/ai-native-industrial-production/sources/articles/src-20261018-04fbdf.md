Furientis selected for US Army IFPC Inc 2 interceptor fly-off
● 
5 days ago
What's the deal?
 
Furientis
, the Los Angeles-area missile startup founded in 2025 by former Virgin Orbit and Castelion engineers, has been picked as one of four vendors to fly interceptors in a US Army shoot-off at the end of fiscal 2027, competing to supply a second missile for the Indirect Fire Protection Capability Increment 2 (IFPC Inc 2) air defence system. The selection, announced by co-founder and CEO Brody Franzen on 17 October 2026, follows a busy fortnight in which the company flew its first horizontal flight test on a government range for the Army's low-cost interceptor effort and, at AUSA 2026, unveiled a containerised launcher developed with Rheinmetall for the Common Autonomous Multi-Domain Launcher programme.
Why it matters:
 IFPC Inc 2 is the Army's mid-range air defence system protecting bases and troops from cruise missiles, drones, rockets and artillery. Its truck-mounted Enduring Shield launcher, built by Leidos subsidiary Dynetics, currently fires RTX's AIM-9X Sidewinder, and the service has sought a second, more capable missile manufacturable at scale. Furientis came out of stealth in May 2026 with a $5m pre-seed round and, on 6 October 2026, announced a $25m seed round led by Benchmark alongside its first funded government contract — a prototyping agreement with the US Department of War for a 100-mile-range interceptor intended for homeland defence. The company targets roughly 30-minute final assembly per missile and flight-tests prototypes about every two weeks.

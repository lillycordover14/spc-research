Saronic Technologies Breaks Ground on $3.3bn Port Alpha Shipyard at Port of Brownsville

10/18/2026

Saronic Technologies, a U.S. startup specializing in uncrewed naval vessels, has held a groundbreaking ceremony for its shipbuilding facility, “Port Alpha,” located at the Port of Brownsville, Texas. With a total investment of approximately $3.3 billion, the project—one of the largest new shipbuilding facility developments in the United States in decades—has now officially transitioned from the planning phase to the construction phase.

In July of this year, Saronic Technologies selected the Port of Brownsville as the site for the Port Alpha shipbuilding facility. According to the plans, the new shipyard will be capable of undertaking both commercial and defense shipbuilding projects, as well as constructing autonomous and manned vessels, and is expected to begin operations in 2028.

The first phase of the project covers 835 acres and can be expanded to nearly 4,000 acres in the future. The first phase is planned to include 8 to 10 production buildings with a total enclosed production area of 1.2 million square feet. It will feature a deep-water pier exceeding 2,500 feet in length, equipped with 2 to 4 ship assembly berths.

In terms of production capacity and scale, this new shipyard will have an annual shipbuilding capacity of approximately 150,000 gross tons upon initial operation, which is said to more than double the United States’ current merchant shipbuilding capacity; Initially, it will have the capacity to build ships up to 850 feet (approximately 239 meters) in length, and after future expansion, it will be able to support the construction of ships exceeding 1,200 feet (approximately 365.8 meters) in length. Given its scale, this new shipyard will be capable of simultaneously constructing larger naval vessels such as destroyers, cruisers, and amphibious assault ships.

Saronic Technologies and the State of Texas anticipate that the new shipyard project will create 10,000 direct jobs—spanning fields such as welding, machining, robotics, maritime software engineering, and vessel design—with a projected long-term economic impact on Texas reaching as much as $264.5 billion.

Known for its small autonomous patrol craft, Saronic Technologies has achieved vertical integration across engineering, R&D, and manufacturing. The company owns the former Gulf Craft shipyard in Louisiana and has consistently signaled its ambition to become a core shipbuilder for large-scale U.S. Navy vessels. The commencement of construction at its Port Alpha shipyard marks a pivotal step in the company’s expansion into the realm of large-scale vessels and traditional naval shipbuilding.

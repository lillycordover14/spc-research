What's the deal?
 Copia Investment Partners has secured a strategic investment from 
Scarcity Partners
, which acquired a 30% stake in the Australian investment management group. The deal was announced on October 1, 2026.

What's the endgame?
 The investment will fund Copia's plans to expand distribution, improve scalability, and strengthen support for its affiliate fund managers. It also gives the group room to explore partnerships with additional specialist managers.

Who's Copia?
 Founded in 2000, it had $9 billion in funds under distribution across five affiliates and 13 strategies as of August 31, 2026. Its partners include Artisan Partners, Chester Asset Management, HSBC Asset Management, Mutual Limited, and OC Funds Management, spanning Australian and global equities, cash and fixed income, and global listed infrastructure.

What stays the same?
 Copia will keep operating autonomously, with its executive team retaining majority ownership and day-to-day control. Its affiliate managers will also keep control over their investment approaches.

Chief executive Sam Baillieu said the group wanted a backer that understood its model. "We wanted a strategic investor who understood our model and could help us take it further," he said, adding that Scarcity's relationships would help it "pursue new opportunities in Australia and internationally."

Who's joining?
 Scarcity founding partner Matthew Webb will join the Copia Holding Company board as a non-executive director, while Peter Polson steps down. Webb brings more than 25 years in asset management, including 15 years at Magellan Asset Management. Bruce Loveday remains as chair.

The signal:
 Scarcity Partners managing partner Adrian Whittingham called Copia "an important position within the Australian investment management landscape," pointing to its scalable growth platform. The deal underscores investor appetite for established multi-affiliate managers with distribution reach across advisers and platforms.

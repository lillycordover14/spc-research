Metamorphic Launches Morphe Grasshopper Plugin for Implicit Modelling in Rhino 3D

Metamorphic has created
 a new Grasshopper plugin that brings implicit modelling, voxel processing and vector-field capability into Rhino 3D's parametric modelling environment. According to 
TCT Magazine
, the design and engineering consultancy developed the Morphe plugin to address the fragmented nature of computational design.
The tool draws on Metamorphic's work in quantum technologies, bioprocessing and optomechanical systems. The company's stated aim is to reduce the need to retrace steps through a workflow when a significant design change occurs, rather than requiring users to regenerate a model, export it again and repeat downstream processing.
By operating inside Grasshopper, the plugin is intended to keep advanced geometry connected to the rules, parameters, optimisation processes, simulation data and engineering intent that produced it. Metamorphic says a designer can generate a complex manifold parametrically, smooth chosen regions with voxel methods, let simulation data shape the form, and keep developing the geometry without the process becoming a disconnected series of software hand-offs.
Morphe allows structures to be generated from curves, surfaces or mathematical definitions, varied spatially and linked to the wider Grasshopper definition. Metamorphic AM co-founders Laurence Coles and Manolis Papastavrou commented that not every useful engineering form starts from a clean mathematical description, noting that scan data, meshes and experimentally derived geometry also need to take part in advanced design workflows. They indicated that voxel methods offer another route for manipulating such inputs without forcing them into an unsuitable modelling paradigm, and that the opportunity lies not in choosing implicit over voxel modelling but in letting different representations do what they do best without each transition becoming a dead end.
Metamorphic suggests the plugin will let designers explore more alternatives before resources are committed to manufacture, while raising the likelihood that the geometry chosen is the right one from the outset. The company also says the plugin brings more advanced solid-modelling capability into an environment many computational designers already understand.
Metamorphic believes traditional CAD will remain indispensable for assemblies, interfaces, engineering drawings, dimensioning and large volumes of everyday mechanical design. It suggests the future of design for additive manufacturing is likely to involve increasingly connected toolchains, with conventional CAD, computational design, simulation, optimisation, implicit modelling and manufacturing software each handling the tasks they suit best.
The company is expected to share further information on Morphe in due course.

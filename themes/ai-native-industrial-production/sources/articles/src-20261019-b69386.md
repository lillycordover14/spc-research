Monty Don reveals 5 tulip varieties he would never garden without: 'It's like choosing your favourite children'&nbsp;&nbsp;Country Living Magazine

[page text]
Ben Montgomery
//
Getty Images
Choosing just five favourite 
tulip
 varieties is a tall order, even for Monty Don.
"Having to choose five favourite tulips is like choosing your favourite children," he says in a 
new YouTube video for 
BBC Gardeners’ World Magazine
. He admits he actually hates having to do it: "I feel disloyal to the other 10 or 15 tulips I'm not going to mention."
Nevertheless, the former 
Gardeners’ World
 presenter has managed to settle on five varieties he would be hard-pressed to garden without: ‘Queen of Night’, ‘Ballerina’, ‘Rasta Parrot’, ‘Dordogne’ and ‘Apricot Impression’. 
Here’s what Monty likes most about each one, and why they shine brightly enough to make his cut.
1
'Apricot Impression'
Alex Manders
//
Getty Images
Monty claims he is "always in pursuit of the perfect 
apricot tulip
" – and has yet to find one – but ‘Apricot Impression’ is a close contender, earning a spot on his shortlist. 
He describes it as "a good doer" that he enjoys topping up each year.
2
'Queen of Night'
REDA
//
Getty Images
The femme fatale of the tulip world, Monty's choice for a rich, dark tulip is ‘Queen of Night’. He says he "wouldn't be without" this variety, whose deep flowers bring a dramatic, 
moodier
 note to his spring displays.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
3
'Rasta Parrot' 
Alex Manders
//
Getty Images
For a flamboyant variety with a bit more "pizzazz", Monty looks to ‘Rasta Parrot’. He loves parrot tulips for their striking, ruffled petals and says this one is "bright, vibrant and fiery” – "an explosion of colour" you can't ignore.
4
'Dordogne'
REDA
//
Getty Images
In dreamy sunset hues with a subtle ombré effect to its pinky-apricot petals, Monty recommends ‘Dordogne’, a Triumph tulip. It is tall enough to "hold its own in a 
border
", he says, and reflowers well. He adds that he tops it up each year.
Advertisement - Continue Reading Below
5
'Ballerina'
DEA / C. SAPPA
//
Getty Images
Finally, Monty chooses an old favourite: ‘Ballerina’, a cheerfully architectural orange tulip that he says 
returns year after year
. He planted some in his garden more than 25 years ago, and they are still "happening" each season.
The five are not in any particular order, Monty says – a small mercy for anyone who finds choosing a favourite nearly as difficult as he does!

Furientis Selected for U.S. Army Interceptor Fly-Off

 
The young missile startup will compete to develop a second interceptor for the IFPC Inc 2 air defense system in 2027 field trials alongside other vendors.

Ami Rojkes Dombe

                                | 

19/10/2026

					Sponsored

			

Image credit: Photo by Furientis., The Defence Blog – Military and Defense News (
https://defence-blog.com
), "Year-old startup wins spot in U.S. Army interceptor fly-off" (documented on 19 October 2026)

Missile startup Furientis, founded just last year, has been selected as one of four companies to fly interceptors in a competitive U.S. Army live-fire test at the end of fiscal year 2027, aiming to compete for supplying a second interceptor missile for the service's Indirect Fire Protection Capability Increment 2 (IFPC Inc 2) air defense system.
According to a report by 
Defence Blog
, the IFPC Inc 2 is the U.S. Army’s medium-range air defense system designed to protect bases and deployed forces from cruise missiles, unmanned aerial vehicles, rockets, and artillery. Co-founder and CEO Brody Franzen announced the selection, describing it as a downselect leading to flight tests scheduled for the end of the fiscal year ending September 30, 2027, without naming the other three companies selected for this track.
The system's truck-mounted Enduring Shield launcher, built by Dynetics, a subsidiary of Leidos, currently launches RTX's AIM-9X Sidewinder missiles. In recent years, the Army has sought a second, more capable missile able to successfully counter fast, low-flying cruise missiles and be manufactured in higher volumes.
Lockheed Martin and a joint Boeing-Anduril team are already competing in the formal second-interceptor program, which entered its demonstration phase in September after Rafael's U.S. subsidiary was not selected to proceed. During the Association of the U.S. Army (AUSA) conference, Lt. Gen. Frank Lozano, the Army's Program Executive Officer for Fires, stated that the U.S. Army would open a parallel track for companies that did not pass the initial cut but continued independent development, inviting them to bring missiles to the test range for a shoot-off in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2027, as reported by Breaking Defense. Furientis’ selection appears to be part of this track.
Co-founders Franzen and Aris Simsarian, both former engineers at Virgin Orbit and Castelion, established Furientis in 2025 in the Los Angeles area to build interceptors designed for mass production using off-the-shelf components. The company estimates its final assembly time at roughly 30 minutes and reports that it conducts prototype flight tests approximately every two weeks, rather than spending years in design prior to a first flight.
Furientis emerged from stealth in May with a $5 million pre-seed round, and on October 6 announced a $25 million seed round led by Benchmark alongside its first funded government contract—a prototype agreement with the U.S. Department of War for a 100-mile-range interceptor designed for homeland defense.
According to Franzen, the IFPC selection capped an intensive two weeks during which Furientis flew its first horizontal flight test at a government range for the U.S. Army's low-cost interceptor effort and unveiled a containerized launcher co-developed with Rheinmetall for the Common Autonomous Multi-Domain Launcher program at the AUSA conference. He commended the Program Executive Office for Fires and the Defensive Fires team, led by Brig. Gen. Guy Yelverton, for opening the door to non-traditional vendors and providing resources to accelerate development, noting that the company is hiring ahead of the September 2027 tests.

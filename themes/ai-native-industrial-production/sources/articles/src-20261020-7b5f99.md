Arda Industries Emerges from Stealth with $232 Million to Automate U.S. Manufacturing&nbsp;&nbsp;citybiz

[page text]
Arda Team

Arda Industries
, an artificial intelligence startup founded by former OpenAI Chief Research Officer 
Bob McGrew
, has emerged from stealth with 
$232 million
 in total funding to develop software that automates manufacturing engineering and accelerates the production of complex hardware in the United States.

The funding includes a $160 million Series A round co-led by Altimeter, Accel and Khosla Ventures, valuing the company at $1.5 billion. XYZ Venture Capital, 8VC, SVAngel and Felicis also participated.

The financing follows a $72 million seed round led by Accel and Founders Fund, with additional participation from Khosla Ventures and XYZ Venture Capital.

Arda is developing AI-powered technology that converts product designs into manufacturing instructions and assembly plans, allowing factories to adapt more quickly when hardware designs change.

The company plans to use the capital to expand its engineering and research teams, increase deployments with manufacturers and develop AI models capable of programming robotic assembly systems.

Its initial focus includes defense manufacturing, electronics and the data center supply chain, with longer-term ambitions to support large-scale consumer electronics production in the United States.

Arda
‘s software addresses a major challenge in advanced manufacturing: the time and engineering resources required to translate new product designs into functioning production processes.

While product development teams can rapidly revise hardware designs, manufacturing engineers must determine how components should be assembled, establish production sequences and create detailed instructions for factory workers and equipment.

Those processes can require substantial manual effort, particularly for products such as drones, smartphones, robotics systems and data center equipment that undergo frequent design changes.

Arda uses AI agents to analyze computer-aided design files and other engineering materials, automatically generating assembly plans and work instructions.

The system is designed to update manufacturing processes as product specifications change, reducing the delays associated with preparing production lines for revised hardware.

“I kept asking: why can we design the next generation of products in America, but not build them here?” McGrew, Arda’s founder and CEO, said.

“When a product changes every year, the factory has to be able to change with it.”

McGrew said the company’s technology is intended to narrow the gap between the speed of product design and the pace at which factories can implement changes.

The platform is already being used by Anduril Industries, the defense technology company, as part of its ArsenalOS manufacturing software.

Arda and Anduril began collaborating before Arda was formally incorporated.

According to the companies, Arda has significantly reduced the time required for certain manufacturing engineering tasks.

Basic work instructions that previously required approximately 10 hours can now be generated in about 15 minutes, while more complex engineering workflows that once took up to 30 hours can be completed in roughly 2.5 hours.

These improvements are intended to help manufacturers accelerate production planning while identifying potential assembly problems earlier in the process.

“Our manufacturing engineers are doing two hard things at once. They’re keeping up with design teams that iterate constantly, and they’re scaling production to meet demand,” said 
Matt Grimm
, co-founder and chief operating officer of Anduril Industries.

“Arda helps them get design changes onto the floor faster and catch potential issues sooner. We would be behind on our production goals without it.”

Beyond defense manufacturing, Arda is working with companies in the data center supply chain, including manufacturers of transformers and electrical equipment used to support power infrastructure.

These industries face pressure to expand production capacity while adapting to changing product requirements and customer demand.

Arda’s technology is designed to improve the productivity of manufacturing engineers by automating repetitive planning and documentation tasks.

The company believes this approach could help address shortages of experienced manufacturing engineers in the United States while allowing existing teams to support more production activity.

A central part of Arda’s longer-term strategy involves extending its software from manufacturing planning into robotic automation.

The company is developing AI agents capable of translating assembly plans into instructions for industrial robots, with the goal of making automated production lines easier to reconfigure.

Traditional industrial automation often requires customized equipment, specialized programming and extensive calibration.

According to Arda, programming a conventional automated manufacturing station can take three to six months and cost nearly $1 million.

These requirements can make automation difficult to justify for products with short production cycles or frequent design changes.

Arda aims to reduce the time needed to update robotic assembly systems from months to days.

If successful, the technology could allow manufacturers to use automated production lines for hardware products that are currently assembled primarily through labor-intensive processes.

The company sees consumer electronics as a significant long-term opportunity, particularly products such as smartphones that are redesigned frequently and require large-scale assembly operations.

Arda’s stated ambition is to make domestic manufacturing more competitive for these rapidly changing products by reducing the engineering effort required to establish and modify production lines.

McGrew brings experience from two prominent technology companies.

He led product and engineering at 
Palantir
 during its first decade, helping establish the company’s forward-deployed engineering model.

He later joined OpenAI, where he led research from 2018 to 2024 before departing as chief research officer in September 2024.

Arda’s founding team also includes Chief AI Officer Augustus Odena, a co-founder of Adept who previously worked at Google Brain and Meta’s TBD Lab.

Chief Technology Officer Alex Mark previously held engineering leadership roles at Palantir and worked on the IRS Direct File initiative at the U.S. Digital Service.

Co-founder Jakob Frick brings experience from Palantir and SpaceXAI.

The broader team includes software engineers from Palantir, manufacturing engineers from Tesla and AI researchers from MIT.

Arda’s employees work directly with manufacturing customers to develop and test the technology against actual production requirements.

The company’s combination of AI research and factory-floor engineering is intended to support software that can operate within the practical constraints of industrial manufacturing.

With $232 million in funding and an initial deployment at Anduril, Arda is preparing to expand its technology across additional manufacturing sectors.

Its immediate priorities include improving AI-driven production planning, increasing adoption among industrial customers and advancing the robotic automation capabilities needed to support more adaptable assembly lines.

The company’s longer-term objective is to enable factories to respond to hardware design changes with greater speed, potentially expanding the range of advanced products that can be manufactured competitively in the United States.

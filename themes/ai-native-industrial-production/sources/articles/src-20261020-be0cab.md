A national machining company is investing tens of millions of dollars in Luzerne County.

SendCutSend Inc. — a company that manufactures computer-numerical-control, laser-cut metal and wood products — will lease space and renovate a manufacturing facility at 535 Research Drive in Pittston Twp. The company is set to invest $63.2 million in the space, which measures 170,500 square feet, in order to host its new CNC machining and laser-cutting operations.

The governor’s office announced the investment in a news release it issued Tuesday. The release indicated that the facility will be SendCutSend’s first in Pennsylvania, which was competing against Ohio to host the project. It will create an estimated 309 jobs over the next three years.

SendCutSend founder and CEO Jim Belosic described the importance of the new facility in the state news release. He said the facility in the Keystone State would be crucial in expediting shipping to its customers across the Northeast.

“Our customers need parts fast, so we move fast, and Pennsylvania gets that,” Belosic said. “We’re hiring and machines are landing.”

SendCutSend was founded in 2018 and is headquartered in Reno, Nevada, according to a news release Mericle Commercial Real Estate, the owner of the Research Drive property, issued Wednesday. The company specializes in producing custom metal and plastic components, using precision sheet cutting with laser, waterjet, and CNC routing and machining tools; and offers bending and secondary finishing services.

Its business model centers on having clients upload digital designs through the company website to receive pricing and place orders immediately, truncating the typically length custom-design process. Customers of SendCutSend range from individual inventors to major manufacturers and work in fields including electronics, industrial equipment, automotive, aerospace and robotics development.

The state Department of Community & Economic Development is subsidizing the project with a $3 million grant as part of the Pennsylvania First program – an initiative meant to “facilitate increased investment and job creation within the commonwealth.” SendCutSend will also be eligible for tax breaks through the state’s Manufacturing Tax Credit and Qualified Manufacturing Innovation and Reinvestment Deduction. Belosic thanked state officials for “making this process seamless” with their support.
 

“This investment by SendCutSend is a major win for Luzerne County and for workers across northeastern Pennsylvania,” DCED Secretary Rick Siger said in the state news release. “We’re laser focused on attracting businesses, supporting innovation, and investing in our workforce to ensure our Commonwealth remains competitive and our workers and communities can thrive.”

Mericle Vice President Bill Jones coordinated the real estate deal with SendCutSend in coordination with the international real estate brokerage firm Colliers.

In the Mericle news release, Jones said SendCutSend would be located alongside other companies drawn to the CenterPoint Commerce & Trade Park that Mericle has developed and currently employs over 8,000 workers. He said Mericle was already working with SendCutSend to renovate to suit its needs.

“SendCutSend is exactly the type of advanced manufacturing company we had in mind when we developed CenterPoint Commerce & Trade Park,” Jones said.
 

Officials for Penn’s Northeast, a regional economic development agency for Northeastern Pennsylvania, also praised the new development, crediting Mericle and the Shapiro administration.

“Pennsylvania is on a winning streak and as long as we’re working together, I see no sign of our economy slowing down,” Penn’s Northeast President John Augustine said in the Mericle news release.

The announcement of the SendCutSend announcement comes with Election Day only around two weeks away. Gov. Josh Shapiro, a Democrat, is running for reelection against state Treasurer Stacy Garrity, the Republican gubernatorial nominee. Some of Shapiro’s campaign advertisements have centered on what he has argued is his administration’s ability to attract business investment.

“Pennsylvania is competing with other states and winning,” Shapiro said in his office’s news release. “My Administration will keep working to attract new investments, grow our economy, and make sure Pennsylvania remains a place where businesses can succeed and workers can build a better future.”

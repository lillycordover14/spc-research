The International Monetary Fund (IMF) projected on the 12th that Korea's nominal gross domestic product (GDP) this year will be 3,256.546 trillion won, up 21.7% from last year. In contrast, the real GDP growth rate was forecast at 3.3%.
In general, subtracting the inflation rate from the nominal GDP growth rate gives an approximation of the real GDP growth rate. If the IMF's projection holds, it would mean this year's inflation rate exceeds 18%. That is a considerable gap from the recent inflation rate of around 3%. So how did the IMF arrive at its projection?
Previously, the year with the largest gap between nominal GDP and real GDP growth rate was 1980, at 24.2 percentage points. In 1979, oil field workers in Iran went on strike vowing to topple the Pahlavi monarchy, and international oil prices surged from $12–$13 per barrel to $34. This oil shock sharply pushed up domestic prices. At the same time, Korea's real GDP retreated 1.6%.
In recent years, the gap between nominal GDP and real GDP growth rate has stayed below 5 percentage points. From 2021 to 2025, it was tallied at 2.1 percentage points, 1.2 percentage points, 1.9 percentage points, 4.2 percentage points, and 3.3 percentage points, respectively.
The recent IMF outlook emerged under circumstances exactly opposite to 1980. Rising export prices centered on semiconductors are driving the gap between nominal GDP and real GDP growth rate.
According to the Bank of Korea's Economic Statistics System (ECOS), the DRAM export price index (2020=100) rose from 147.44 in January this year to 289.16 last month. Over the same period, the flash memory export price index climbed from 126 to 245.72.
Even if Samsung Electronics, SK hynix, and others export the same volume of semiconductors, the prices amount to double. Because semiconductors are produced domestically, nominal GDP increases accordingly. This is thanks to a surge in semiconductor demand as global big-tech corporations ramp up investment in artificial intelligence (AI).
Seok Byung-hoon of Ewha Womans University said, "Even if nominal GDP increases, there is a possibility that the income gains could be concentrated in the semiconductor institutional sector and may not spread across overall domestic demand."

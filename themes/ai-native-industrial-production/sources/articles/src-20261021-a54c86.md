UNC-Syracuse Preview: Tar Heels Look To Continue Momentum in ACC Home Opener&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
Fresh off back-to-back road wins, North Carolina (4-2, 2-1 ACC) welcomes Syracuse (2-4, 0-4 ACC) to Kenan Stadium as the Tar Heels look to secure three straight victories for the first time in the 
Bill Belichick
 era. Carolina has won three straight against the Orange in the all-time series, and are favored to do so again Saturday night.

How to Watch
Location: Kenan Stadium — Chapel Hill, N.C.
Time: Saturday, 7:30 p.m. ET
TV: ACC Network — 
Watch on Fubu (Try for Free)
Crew: Justin Kutcher, Max Browne, Madison Fitzpatrick

Analyzing the Orange

Syracuse enters without a power conference win, while sitting last in the ACC standings. From a wins and losses standpoint, the Orange will be the worst FBS team North Carolina has played to date, but the metrics suggest they may not be as weak as their record implies. Ranked 84th, Syracuse sits above three conference foes (Boston College, California, Stanford) in SP+ with three one-possession losses, including a 3-point defeat at Virginia. 

“They’re a few plays away from being 6-0,” Belichick said on Monday. “Every game has been very competitive.”

In the Orange’s 34-26 loss to Louisville last weekend, they tallied 462 scrimmage yards, a trend that has quietly emerged despite overall struggles. Syracuse ranks 27th nationally with 449.8 yards per game, the sixth-best mark in the conference. UNC is last in the ACC, tallying 329.8 YPG, 117th nationally.

Despite the ability to move the football, the Orange have thrown nine interceptions this season, tied for the most in the country. Syracuse enters with the 12th-most passing yards per game nationally but has dealt with struggles turning that production into points and wins. 

Defensively, Fran Brown’s unit surrenders 25.7 points per contest and 330.3 yards per game, both middle-of-the-pack turnouts in the conference. The Orange surrendered 41 points to UConn in their lone FBS win, but they also held California, Pitt and Virginia under 30 despite losing efforts. Syracuse has led by double-digits in three of its four conference games, but has struggled to seal the deal. Pro Football Focus isn’t kind to the Orange either, grading their offense 114th and defense 85th out of 138 FBS teams.

Positions, Players to Watch

Syracuse turned to its fourth-string quarterback 
Danny Lauter
 last week when 
Malachi Nelson
 suffered a fourth quarter injury — the second consective week he’s gone down during a start. The Orange have already lost both their opening day starter 
Steve Angeli
 and backup 
Amari Odom
 to injury, forcing Nelson and Lauter into the limelight in back-to-back weeks.

Based on Nelson’s health, him or Lauter should be in line to start on Saturday, but Brown noted in his weekly press conference that Syracuse does not plan to scale-back its offense on any occasion — three different signal-callers have already thrown for 300 yards in a game this season.

Regardless of who is under center, however, the Tar Heels need to be aware of the ACC’s leading receiver 
Umari Hatcher
. The 6-foot-3, sixth-year senior boasts 784 receiving yards and seven scores through six games, ranking inside the top-five nationally in nearly every pass-catching stat. 
Tylik Hill
 shoulders the bulk of the ground work for the Orange, rushing for 431 yards on 80 carries. Hill has one rushing touchdown this season, while three other backs combine for seven.

According to PFF, defensive lineman 
Rashard Perry
 is graded as one of the top run stoppers in the country, as a fifth of his 25 total tackles are for a loss. In the secondary, Syracuse leans on 
Davien Kerr
 amid a below-average coverage unit. The junior has three interceptions this season — tied with UNC’s 
Gregory Smith
 for 8th in the country — and has held up this year when thrown at. On 26 targets, Kerr’s allowed 10 receptions.

The Matchup

Momentum and record imply that UNC has a clear upper-hand against the Orange. North Carolina will certainly be the favorite in its first night game of the season at Kenan Stadium, but even though the Orange’s health at quarterback looms large, their offensive unit has moved the ball regardless of who is under center, posing a legitimate threat to the Tar Heels’ wounded secondary. 

Without 
Tre Miller
 for the forseeable future, 
Kaleb Cost
 also did not play against Duke, while 
Peyton Waters
 exited against the Blue Devils and did not return. Despite a career day from Greg Smith, he also entered with a questionable tag last weekend before being deemed a game-time decision.

PFF and SP+ say this is the worst FBS defense UNC has faced this season, offering the Tar Heels’ group a chance to find consistency on their home turf. In three ACC games, Carolina has scored 41 of its 65 points in the first half, with an underwhelming seven points coming in the third quarter. 
Billy Edwards Jr
. and company have done enough so far, but the stints of stagnation haven’t caught up to the Tar Heels’ since Clemson. With the Orange likely to score their dose of points, once again, the attention turns to UNC’s ability to match it.

After 12 sacks in its first three weeks, North Carolina’s pass rush has slowed, racking up three sacks in the last three weeks. Quarterback pressure isn’t always an inflection point, but Syracuse allows 2.5 sacks per game — tied for 107th nationally. Can 
Xavier Lewis
 and 
Melkart Abou Jaoude
 get back to wreaking havoc in opposing backfields? It could make a difference against the Orange’s banged up quarterback room.

“They’re a pressure defense like we’ve been seeing here for the last few weeks,” Belichick said. “We have do a good job taking care of the ball better than we have been. They can create some turnovers, and offensively, I don’t think I’ve ever coached against a team that’s had four quarterbacks that are all legitimate players.”

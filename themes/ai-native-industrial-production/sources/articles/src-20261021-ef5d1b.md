Arda Industries said on October 20, 2026, that it had raised 
$232 million
 for its manufacturing AI company: a $160 million Series A at a $1.5 billion valuation, following a $72 million seed round. CNBC separately reported a $230 million raise. The company’s announcement also described software that uses design files to create assembly-line plans and adapt factory workflows when products change.

Arda’s funding rounds

Arda said its $160 million Series A was co-led by Altimeter, Accel and Khosla Ventures. XYZ Venture Capital, 8VC, SVAngel and Felicis also participated. The company said Accel and Founders Fund led its earlier $72 million seed round, with Khosla Ventures and XYZ Venture Capital also participating.

What Arda says its manufacturing AI does

Arda says its AI agent uses product CAD files and other design artifacts to generate assembly-line plans and reflect design changes in factory workflows. Founded by former OpenAI research chief Bob McGrew, the company describes a mix of proprietary, open-source and frontier AI models.

Arda also says its product is used within Anduril’s ArsenalOS manufacturing software and that Anduril was its first design partner and customer. The company says basic work instructions that previously took 10 hours can be created in 15 minutes. For some engineering workflows, it cites a reduction from as long as 30 hours to roughly 2.5 hours.

Robot programming remains a prototype

Arda says it is prototyping AI agents that would program robots to carry out assembly plans. That work is part of the company’s roadmap, rather than a released robotics capability.

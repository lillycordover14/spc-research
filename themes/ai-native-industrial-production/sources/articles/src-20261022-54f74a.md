KAILUA (HawaiiNewsNow) - What started eight years ago as a small haunted house fundraiser in a Kailua home has turned into a full-blown wizarding world, built with painstaking detail over months by a local family and dozens of volunteers.
The wizards behind it are twin brothers and aspiring engineers Gavin Marotta-Cerwonka and Logan Marotta-Cerwonka, “I’ve been a tinkerer and a builder since I was very, very little,” Gavin said.
“I’m the head for building,” Logan said.
Before the wands and wizards, there were ghosts and ghouls.
The brothers created the Tunnel of Terror with friends to raise money after seeing families in need from the Puna lava flow.
“We wanted to do something to raise money for Hope Services Hawaii,” Logan said.
Their mother, Wendy Marotta, said: “And that really kicked them off on this journey of helping others.”
They donated to different non-profits, including Shriners.
For Gavin, that mission was personal:
“So, in 4th grade, I broke my ankle,” Gavin said. “And I saw an advertisement for Shriners Hospital for Kids on the television... my ankle’s gonna heal, but these kids, they’re not gonna get better. So, I felt so bad for them.”
While sitting out during P.E., Gavin said he read a bunch of Harry Potter books and that’s how the love of Harry Potter started.
That’s when the brothers conjured up a new idea: make the fundraiser full-on Harry Potter themed.
“And it started off really small,” Logan said. “So far, it’s been growing year after year, and now we have, what, like 8 shops in the front, two shops in the back, all of which we build and have to take down every year.”
All of it takes about two months to build and take down.
Wendy said it grew from a group of six kids at the first one to over 50. 
“The attraction was, I’d say, dazzling,” volunteer Noa Rendon Reiss said. “It was magical and I’d say it had a lot of detail and a lot of thought put into it.”
His mother, volunteer Casie Reiss, said she brought Noa for the very first time last year, “Noa had never had any experience with Harry Potter, but I read all of the books growing up, so I was excited to bring him for the first time.”
After visiting the attraction, Casie said her son had fallen in love with Harry Potter and wanted to learn more about it. They were also inspired to volunteer this year. 
For the last two months, elementary and high school students have given up four to five hours of their Sundays to build, decorate, and create products to sell — including book nooks, Baby Mandrakes and wands.
“Our low goal is 400 for the fundraiser, but we tend to sell around 150 a night and we’re doing it for four nights,” said volunteer, Nyssa Correia. 
Volunteer Molly Christensen has been helping with the fundraisers since the beginning.
“You grow with the process and so when you look back, we have a photo somewhere of the original kids and there’s just eight of us or so and now you realize, wow, it has probably...at least tripled in size,” said Christensen.
“It is at least tripled in size.”
Wendy added: “And they work hard, from start to finish.”
“I think it’s just so inspiring to see what students can do when they come together and put their minds to something,” said Casie. “What you see here is just absolutely phenomenal.”
But the real magic isn’t what they create; it’s what they give back. In the past three years alone, they’ve raised over $100,000 for Maui’s recovery.
“It feels really good to give back to the community and to be a part of something bigger than just regular extracurricular,” said volunteer Adrian Monda.
But soon the twins won’t be here. They’re off to college next fall, so this is the final chapter of Harry Potter sorcery along Kakahika Street.
“We are hoping that another entity, whether it be Habitat for Humanity themselves or Le Jardin Academy, someone picks this amazing tradition up,” Gavin said. “So, it doesn’t, all this material, all this energy and cause doesn’t just fade away.”

                            FITZROY MINERALS INC.                        
-1.00%
Fitzroy Minerals Inc. announced the results of an integrated seismic and machine-learning (?ML?) targeting study, completed with Fleet Space Technologies (?Fleet?), at the Buen Retiro Copper Project, Copiapó, Chile (?Buen Retiro? or the ?Project?). ML targeting generated a ranked set of priority domains by combining 3D Ambient Noise Tomography (?ANT?) data with prior geophysical surveys, geological models, and approximately 24,400 copper assays from 140 drill holes.
The model identified nine areas where the sub-surface characteristics closely resemble the signature of the rocks hosting Buen Retiro?s known copper mineralization. Fleet?s regional and infill ANT surveys mapped the Project?s deeper geology well beyond the depths of current drilling. The technique uses natural seismic noise to build a 3D picture of the rock beneath the surface.
Extensive alluvial gravel cover in the area makes surface geological mapping and therefore target-generation, much more difficult. Fitzroy has started a 5,000-metre drilling program using diamond and reverse circulation (?RC?) drill rigs. Fleet?s work at Buen Retiro began with a regional ANT survey commissioned by Fitzroy in early 2026.
The ANT survey was designed to build a better picture of the Project?s deeper geology. This identified structures and zones associated with alteration and brecciation at depth, and gave Fitzroy a 3D framework for further exploration. A higher-resolution, closer-spaced ANT survey was then completed over the main area of interest.
This infill survey improved the understanding of the faults and fractures that are interpreted to be the main control on both permeability corridors and associated mineralization. The infill survey also allowed closer correlation of the ANT data with existing geophysical and drilling data. The ML targeting phase then converted this geological and geophysical picture into a set of exploration opportunities.
The targets are designed to complement, not replace Fitzroy?s own geological interpretation and drill planning. The ML model learned the relationship between geophysical data and copper assays from existing drill holes in order to estimate a quantified uncertainty for any point in space within the survey area. The aim was to predict zones where copper mineralization with grades above a 0.5% Cu threshold within 25-metre blocks of rock across the study area might be located, together with an uncertainty value for each block.
The 0.5% Cu threshold is used to visually identify blocks at, or above, the target grade and does not represent a uniform estimate of copper grade per block. Blocks were removed from consideration if they fell within the mined-out open pit, mapped gravel cover, or within 200 metres of an existing drill hole, so that target selection would focus on ground with genuine exploration value. The remaining blocks were grouped into nine geographic areas (?domains?), each representing a cluster of blocks with a similar predicted response within high-confidence range and interpreted to be geologically significant.
All of the targets are within the top 400 metres of the surface and Fitzroy has allocated 5,000 metres of combined RC and diamond drilling to test the new areas. The new exploration domains are conceptual exploration targets that have not yet been tested by drilling. They mark areas where the combined geophysical and assay data look similar to Buen Retiro?s known zones of copper mineralization.
The targets do not confirm the presence, grade, size or continuity of mineralization.

NVIDIA and Tesla will invest nearly $3 billion in Samsung to develop substrates for artificial intelligence chips. The technology, called Flip Chip-Ball Grid Array (FC-BGA), flips the die upside down and connects it to the substrate in the package through solder bumps. The technology significantly improves performance over traditional technologies such as Plastic Ball Grid Array (PBGA). The report, courtesy of Maekyung, outlines that the two firms have expressed interest since the technology is the next expected bottleneck in the
 AI supply chain
.

Tesla Deepens Cooperation With Samsung To Develop Optimus Humanoid Robot 

In a chip-on-wafer-on-substrate (CoWoS) package, the FC-BGA technology plays a key role. The CoWoS package is the final step in an AI chip's assembly before it is ready to be mounted inside a computer to start working. It integrates multiple components, such as the GPU and the memory inside a single package.

This package is made primarily of three layers. On top of the system is the GPU or the CPU that is integrated with high-bandwidth memory (HBM). This stack sits on top of an interposer, typically made of a large piece of silicon or smaller pieces of ceramic or polymer with metal traces called the RDL. 

At the bottom of the stack lies the Flip Chip-Ball Grid Array, or (FC-BGA). The role of the interposer in the package is to ensure that the signals travel between the GPU/CPU and the memory chips. However, the interposer cannot communicate with the motherboard, and for this purpose, the package requires a substrate. 

A CoWoS package as illustrated by TSMC. Image: TSMC

In an FC-BGA configuration, the top of the package is connected to the substrate through solder bumps. Compared with other technologies that often rely on wire bonds, this approach reduces resistance and improves transmission capacity. 

Now, according to a 
report
 from Maekyung, Tesla and NVIDIA have collectively paid Samsung a whopping $2.8 billion to develop capacity for the FC-BGA packaging technology. The bulk of the bill is being footed by NVIDIA, which is paying $2 billion while Tesla's share is $830 million. 

The reason behind the extensive investment is the fear that the FC-BGA substrate (the bridge that takes data from the chip to the bottom) is expected to be the next area of shortage in the semiconductor supply chain owing to the demand for AI chips.

 

According to the details, Tesla's cooperation with Samsung is allowing the latter to expand its Sejong plant. The facility is slated to complete construction and enter production in 2028's second half and start supplying Tesla with the products in 2029. Samsung's total investment in the facility is 4.27 trillion won, while Tesla is footing the bill for 1.1 trillion won, or $830 million. 

Samsung's contract with Tesla is for five years and is expected to guarantee Tesla a supply of the substrates for its autonomous vehicles and 
humanoid robots
. Simultaneously, the burden on Samsung for developing the facility is reduced, says the report, which also discusses the possibility of using the FC-BGA technology for 
Tesla's A15 chip
.

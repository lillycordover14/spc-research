From Ailee to Fabri and Shiggy, 'Korea Expo LA 2026' Spotlights K-Beauty, Food and Culture

by 
Lee Sanghyun
 

Published 23 Oct.2026 13:30(KST) 

 Share

 Print 

View original image

Korea Expo LA 2026 opened in Los Angeles, California. The event was organized to showcase a range of Korean content and products, including K-beauty, K-food and K-pop, to U.S. audiences.
Organized by Exporum Co., Ltd. and the Korea International Trade Association, the event is the first U.S. stop on the Korea Expo series and runs through October 24 at Magic Box LA in downtown Los Angeles. A total of 85 Korean and local companies are participating with 102 brands across 120 booths.
 

View original image

On the first day, designated “Beauty Day,” singer Ailee visited the event and energized the crowd. During a talk show, she spoke with local fans about music and her skincare routine. Charlotte Cho, founder of Soko Glam, which helped pioneer the U.S. K-beauty market, also took part, discussing the strengths and challenges of Korean and U.S. beauty brands.
Sir John, Beyoncé’s makeup artist and creative director at Medicube, will take part in a discussion and live demonstration on K-beauty for melanin-rich skin.
On October 23, “Food Day” will feature Italian chef Fabri, who appeared on “Culinary Class Wars,” presenting dishes that incorporate gochujang, doenjang and ssamjang into California and Italian cuisine. Other activities include a K-sauce pairing game, a K-ramen cooking demonstration and a blind tasting of gochujang sauces.
On October 24, “Culture Day” will feature a performance by KO!, a project duo formed by Killagramz and Oceanfromtheblue, as well as “K-POP DANCE BLAST,” a K-pop cover dance competition. Shiggy and Eileen Kim will be among the judges, and K-pop dance classes and a mother-of-pearl lacquerware craft experience will also be held.
A business program aimed at generating export results is also underway. Buyers from major U.S. retailers, including Costco, H Mart, Amazon and Ulta Beauty, have begun one-on-one export consultations with participating companies. Charlotte Cho also held individual meetings with participating companies and shared strategies for entering the local retail market.
The event is significant in that it presents a business model combining cultural experiences with retail distribution opportunities, rather than simply serving as a one-off product exhibition.
“Los Angeles is a microcosm of the U.S. consumer market, where Korean Americans, Asians and Hispanics live together,” said Shin Hyundae, CEO of Exporum. “We will support Korean small and medium-sized businesses so they can meet local consumers and buyers directly and achieve results.”

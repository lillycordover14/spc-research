Bassist Christian McBride brings new ensemble Ursa Major to Athens 

Oct 23, 2026

—

By 
Mark Mobley

Campus News

The ensemble takes the stage Oct. 29 

Eleven-time Grammy Award-winning bassist, composer and bandleader Christian McBride brings his newest ensemble, Ursa Major, to Hodgson Concert Hall on Oct. 29 at 7:30 p.m. as part of the University of Georgia Performing Arts Center’s 2026-27 UGA Presents season. 

One of the most versatile figures in contemporary American music, McBride has built a career that moves freely among jazz, R&B, soul, pop, rock, hip-hop and neo-soul. With Ursa Major, he brings together four distinctive musicians — saxophonist Nicole Glover, guitarist Ely Perlman, keyboardist Michael King and drummer Savannah Harris — to create a new quintet rooted in jazz but open to a much wider musical vocabulary. 

The group takes its name from Ursa Major (Latin for big bear), the constellation commonly known as the Big Dipper. The name is fitting for a band whose members bring individual voices together to form something larger. “Ursa Major is a constellation — not a tangible object in the sky, but a product of human imagination, an array of celestial lights that used to guide travelers on their journeys,” writes jazz critic David R. Adler in the Performing Arts Center’s program book. 

The performance is part of UGA Presents’ season-long exploration of American music, which asks the question: “What Is American Music?” McBride’s career offers one answer. Raised in Philadelphia, a city shaped by both jazz and the soul and R&B traditions of Gamble and Huff and the Sound of Philadelphia, he has spent decades moving between musical worlds without treating them as separate. 

McBride, an 11-time Grammy winner, is best known as one of jazz’s premier bassists, but his work extends well beyond the instrument. He is jazz advisor of the New Jersey Performing Arts Center, co-curator of the TD James Moody Jazz Festival and co-director of the National Jazz Museum in Harlem. He also hosts NPR’s “Jazz Night in America” and “The Lowdown: Conversations With Christian” on SiriusXM. 

The musicians of Ursa Major bring similarly varied backgrounds to the ensemble. Glover is a New York-based performer, composer, and educator. She appears regularly with Wynton Marsalis and the Jazz at Lincoln Center Orchestra and is a member of the all-star collective Artemis, which performs at Hodgson Concert Hall April 29, 2027. 

Guitarist Perlman released his debut album, “Everything Should Be,” in 2025, blending jazz, folk lyricism and electronic textures. The album features guest appearances by McBride, Shai Maestro and Braxton Cook. 

King, an alumnus of Oberlin Conservatory and the Thelonious Monk Institute, has performed with Bobby Watson, Kevin Eubanks, Gary Bartz and Billy Hart, among others. Harris, a New York-based drummer, composer and producer, has worked with artists including Geri Allen, Jason Moran, Terrence Blanchard and Kenny Barron, as well as musicians from the indie and experimental scenes. 

This performance is supported by Greg and Pamela Mitsoff. WUGA is the media sponsor.

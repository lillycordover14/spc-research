Warp Sciences Emerges From Stealth With $215M Series A for US Chemistry CRO&nbsp;&nbsp;Wowtale

[page text]
Warp Sciences
 has emerged from stealth with $215 million in Series A funding at an $865 million valuation, positioning itself as a U.S.-based chemistry contract research organization (CRO) that pairs chemists with AI and robotics.

The announcement lands one day after OpenAI and Anthropic each acquired drug-discovery AI startups — OpenAI an agentic drug-discovery team, Anthropic an enzyme-discovery outfit — escalating what has quickly become a race among frontier AI labs to move into life sciences. Where those acquisitions push further into AI models for biology, Warp is making a different bet: that the bottleneck isn’t prediction, it’s the physical lab work of actually making and testing molecules.

Chemists and robots working side by side

Warp doesn’t develop its own drugs. Instead, it makes and tests the molecules biotech and pharma research teams need. “We’re not developing drugs of our own. We’re building the infrastructure for the people who do,” co-founder and COO DJ Patil said.

Its initial service is lead optimization analog synthesis — producing and comparing variations of a candidate molecule, one of the most labor-intensive steps in drug discovery. Much of that work has historically been outsourced to Chinese contract research organizations: a 2024 Biotechnology Innovation Organization survey found most U.S. biotechs had contracts with Chinese firms for preclinical and clinical services, and the 
BIOSECURE Act
, signed into law in December 2025, restricts federal procurement and funding tied to designated Chinese biotech companies. Warp wants to bring that chemistry capacity back onshore while automating it enough to compete on cost and speed.

Mechanically, the company trains chemists wearing cameras and sensor gloves, then uses that motion data to teach robots to take over repetitive lab tasks. Chemists increasingly shift toward designing and supervising experiments while robots handle round-the-clock synthesis and testing. Every result — including failures — feeds back into training the underlying AI models, the idea being that each experiment makes the next one smarter.

A team pulled from OpenAI, Meta and the White House

CEO 
Kevin Weil
 was most recently Chief Product Officer and VP of Science at OpenAI, after leading product at Twitter and Instagram. Co-founder and Chief Scientific Officer Sol Reisberg led medicinal chemistry at covalent-medicine developer 
Terremoto Biosciences
. CTO Manohar “Mano” Paluri was a VP at Meta Superintelligence Labs and a founding member of FAIR. COO DJ Patil was a founding executive at Devoted Health and served as the first U.S. Chief Data Scientist under the Obama White House.

Early hires brought robotics experience from 
Genesis
, 
Skild AI
 and 
1X
, plus scientific expertise from Genentech and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory.

“AI has dramatically accelerated software engineering and mathematics, but it has yet to make the same impact in the physical sciences,” Weil said. “Chemistry and biology move forward when you make something, test it and learn from the result. If we want to build more capable AI for science, we need to generate real-world data at scale.”

The round: Lightspeed leads a long investor list

Lightspeed Venture Partners
 led the round, joined by IVP, Mayfield, Lux Capital, Premji Invest, Menlo Ventures, Conviction, 01 Advisors, Standard, Lowercarbon Capital, Gigascale Capital, Angular Ventures, Scribble Ventures, Uncork Capital, Innovation Endeavors, Matter Venture Partners, Zero Shot Fund, SignalFire and Moxxie Ventures.

Angel investors include 
Coinbase
 CEO Brian Armstrong, Anthropic Labs co-lead and former Instagram co-founder Mike Krieger, former Tesla Robotics VP Milan Kovac, Databricks co-founder Ion Stoica, former OpenAI AGI Deployment CEO Fidji Simo, OpenAI Chief Research Officer Mark Chen, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar, Skild AI CEO Deepak Pathak and Figma CEO Dylan Field, among others.

Where Warp sits among AI-for-science startups

Companies chasing faster drug discovery with AI tend to cluster at different layers of the stack. At the computational layer, Google DeepMind spinout Isomorphic Labs, protein/antibody specialist Xaira Therapeutics, antibody-design startup Chai Discovery, and companies WowTale has previously covered — Enveda Biosciences and Superluminal Medicines — focus on predicting which molecules are worth pursuing, then hand synthesis off to outside CROs.

At the platform layer, Flagship Pioneering’s Lila Sciences and Periodic Labs aim to automate entire categories of scientific experimentation, spanning chemistry, biology and materials science — a broader scope than Warp’s.

Warp occupies a narrower, more specific lane: it isn’t building molecule-design models, and it’s betting on execution — who can synthesize a customer’s chosen molecule fastest, most reliably, and onshore. Its real competitive set looks less like AI drug-discovery startups and more like incumbent chemistry CROs such as WuXi AppTec and Pharmaron, except automated with robotics and kept inside U.S. borders. The bet also echoes a broader pattern of AI-lab alumni moving into physical-world infrastructure, seen recently in former OpenAI research lead Bob McGrew’s manufacturing-AI startup 
Arda
, which raised $160 million days earlier.

Warp is currently working with a small number of biotech and pharma design partners and says it will open up capacity to more customers as its labs scale.

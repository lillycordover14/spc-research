AMCA Maiden Flight May Miss 2028 Target Amid Pending Private Partner Selection and Technical Groundwork: IAF Chief&nbsp;&nbsp;Defence.in

[page text]
The Indian Air Force (IAF) may have to wait slightly longer to witness the first flight of its indigenous fifth-generation stealth fighter.

Air Chief Marshal A.P. Singh recently cautioned that the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) might not meet its projected 2028 maiden flight schedule.

Addressing a recent media conclave, the IAF Chief attributed this potential shift to the pending selection of a manufacturing partner and the complex technical prerequisites required before the jet can safely take to the skies.

The critical bottleneck in the current timeline is the finalisation of the Development-cum-Production Partner (DcPP).

The Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) is in the process of evaluating bids to select a private-sector entity to collaborate on the project.

The selected partner will be tasked with building five flying prototypes and one structural test airframe. This initial phase is fully backed by a ₹15,000 crore government allocation.

Currently, major industry players, including a solo bid from Tata Advanced Systems and consortia led by Larsen & Toubro (L&T) and Bharat Forge, are competing for this massive manufacturing contract.

Even after the industrial partner is firmly established, building a stealth fighter from scratch involves an unforgiving technical roadmap.

Air Chief Marshal Singh stressed that establishing the necessary manufacturing supply chains, integrating advanced sensors, and conducting exhaustive ground testing will consume significant time.

These ground validations are absolutely vital to ensure the aircraft's systems and structural integrity are flawless before it begins evaluating handling and aerodynamic performance in the air.

The AMCA is designed to be a highly survivable, low-observable platform capable of penetrating heavily defended hostile airspace, making it a cornerstone for India’s future aerial defence strategy.

As older aircraft are gradually phased out, the IAF is heavily relying on upcoming domestic platforms to maintain its combat edge.

Despite the cautious outlook regarding the prototype's first flight, the Air Chief expressed long-term optimism, projecting that the fifth-generation fighter jet will successfully join the active IAF fleet within the next decade.

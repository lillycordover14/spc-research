By R.L. Bynum

Caleb Wilson didn’t look much like a rookie with the game on the line Friday, taking charge in the final minutes to help Chicago complete a remarkable turnaround from its rough season opener.

The former Carolina star scored 19 of his 21 points in the second half, made four consecutive free throws in the final 76 seconds, and grabbed a critical rebound and steal as the Bulls rallied from 15 points down for a 132–125 road win over Milwaukee.

Wilson’s first two games also put him in select company. He joined Michael Jordan as the only rookies in Bulls history to record at least 15 points, four rebounds, one block and one steal in each of their first two NBA games. Like Jordan, 
Wilson went from scoring 16 points in his NBA debut to 21 points against Milwaukee in his second game.

Two nights after Chicago suffered a 135–92 blowout loss at Toronto, Wilson helped the Bulls survive a back-and-forth finish that featured 10 lead changes and six ties. Milwaukee led 123–122 with less than three minutes remaining, but Wilson’s four free throws put Chicago ahead to stay.

“I feel like I’m built for the moment,” Wilson, who put up a 15.5 game score, said in a postgame interview with Chicago Sports Network. “I’m going to be the most focused and most detailed player ever.”

Wilson, the fourth overall pick in June’s NBA draft, finished with six rebounds, one assist, one steal and two blocks in 33 minutes. He made 7 of 14 shots, including 2 of 6 3-point attempts, and went 5-for-6 from the free-throw line.

His biggest contributions came after Milwaukee regained the lead late. Wilson drew a blocking foul with 1:16 remaining and made both free throws to put Chicago ahead 124–123. He attacked the basket again on the next possession, drawing a foul against Myles Turner and sinking two more free throws with 35.2 seconds left.

Wilson followed with a defensive rebound and a steal on a Tyler Herro pass, helping the Bulls complete their comeback.

“I always feel like near the end of the game I’m going to do the right thing,” 
Wilson said, according to the 
Chicago Sun-Times
. “I love those moments because it shows who you really are.”

The finish was especially encouraging after Wilson struggled in his NBA debut Wednesday. He committed nine turnovers against Toronto, and the Bulls lost by 43 points. Against Milwaukee, he reduced his turnovers to three while scoring 19 points after halftime.

Chicago trailed 32–17 after the first quarter and 62–61 at halftime. The Bulls scored 44 points in the second quarter, including a 16–0 run, and kept pace with Milwaukee until Wilson and his teammates took control late.

Wilson had only two points at halftime after attempting three shots, but he remained patient as his opportunities increased.

“One thing about the NBA is the games are long,” Wilson said. “You might not touch it in the first half, but once I get my chance I’m going to go out there and do what I can.”

Wilson also delivered one of the game’s most emphatic plays in the fourth quarter. After Turner blocked one of his attempts, teammate Noa Essengue forced a turnover and got the ball back to Wilson, who threw down a powerful dunk to give Chicago a four-point lead.

His late-game composure drew praise from veteran and former Duke guard Tre Jones.

“He’s fearless, he’s a competitor [and] he’s a winner at the end of the day,” Jones said.

Coach Tiago Splitter said the Bulls needed to respond after their season-opening embarrassment.

“Nobody liked the way we played,” Splitter said. “We know that; the players know that. I think we hit bottom there, and it was bad.”

For Wilson, the victory was a chance to show that he could contribute when Chicago needed him most, something he believes will become a regular part of his NBA career.

“The thing for me is just proving myself right,” Wilson said. “Like, if I want to be the guy, I’ve got to show up and make free throws, do clutch things in the final minutes.”

Kessler stuffs stat sheet with six blocks

Walker Kessler scored 22 points and blocked six shots Friday as the Lakers defeated the Los Angeles Clippers 125–117.

The 7–2 center made 10 of 14 shots, grabbed eight rebounds, handed out three assists and collected three steals in 35 minutes. His plus-18 rating was the best on the team.

Kessler has quickly become a major factor for the Lakers after arriving from Utah. Over two games, he’s averaged 24 points, 8.5 rebounds, two assists, 1.5 steals, and four blocks while shooting 84.6% from the field.

Friday’s performance was particularly impressive because of his two-way impact. He needed only 14 shots to score 22 points and combined six blocks with three steals, giving Los Angeles a shot-blocking presence around the basket.

The Lakers (2–0) visit Utah at 5 p.m. Sunday, giving Kessler an early opportunity to face his former team.

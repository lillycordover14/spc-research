Jason Friesen finds his sound with new instrumental EP

Jayme Giesbrecht,

PembinaValleyOnline.com | 

Saturday, Oct 24 2026, 12:00 PM

Share:

Jason Friesen, who is using the name "Alamoth" for his latest solo EP release

A Winkler-based musician is sharing a collection of instrumental guitar music, after putting a lot of thought into the project.

Jason Friesen, who releases his music under the name 
Alamoth
, has released 
AIM
, a six-track EP featuring acoustic instrumental music.

Friesen said he began writing the songs around 2018 or 2019, gradually developing musical ideas into finished pieces while balancing work and family responsibilities.

"It's been a long process," Friesen said.

He initially hoped to release the EP in June, but delays pushed the launch back. He was determined to make it available before the end of the year, and the collection is now available digitally.

A name with a musical connection

Friesen chose Alamoth as his artist name after a lengthy search for something that reflected both his interests and the sound of his music.

He wanted to keep his personal musical projects somewhat separate from his work in Low German ministry, (
D'Friesens – Christian Music and Resources in English and Plautdietsch
) and he also wanted a name that was pleasant to say.

His search eventually led him to the Bible, where Alamoth appears as a musical term in the Book of Psalms.

"I wanted the name to reflect myself and a lot of work is a reflection of that as well," Friesen said.

The EP's title, 
AIM
, is an acronym for Acoustic Instrumental Music. Its final track, "Take Aim," was among the last songs he composed for the project.

Friesen said he began writing music by experimenting on the guitar after taking lessons and learning instrumental pieces by other musicians. Rather than setting out to write an entire collection at once, he develops individual ideas when a riff or melody stands out to him.

Inspired by Canadian guitarists

Friesen's musical influences include several well-known guitarists, among them Canadian musicians Bruce Cockburn, Don Ross and Leo Kottke. He has learned pieces by each of them, incorporating elements of their playing into his own approach.

He also credits guitarist Phil Keaggy as an early influence. His mother found one of Keaggy's CDs at a thrift store when Friesen was in high school, and he listened to it repeatedly.

Those influences helped shape the sound of the new EP, which Friesen sees as a snapshot of his musical development.

He is continuing to work on new instrumental pieces and explore different styles and techniques, including percussive guitar playing.

"An instrumental song is just a series of licks put together, right? In a way that makes sense," he said.

Where to find the music

The EP is available through 
Bandcamp
, where listeners can purchase a digital copy rather than relying solely on streaming services. Friesen said he does not currently have physical CDs available.

Friesen said setting up the project, including the album artwork, website and online music store, involved a significant learning curve. However, he expects the work involved in releasing future projects to be easier now that the initial setup is complete.

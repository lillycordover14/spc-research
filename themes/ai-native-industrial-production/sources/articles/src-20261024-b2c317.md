Nick Reiner Granted ‘Nominal’ Funds For Commissary Account Amid Trust Fund Battle
Nick Reiner
 is embroiled in a legal battle over access to finances, with the dispute centering on whether he can receive financial distributions from his trust fund, as he stands accused of murdering his parents, 
Rob and Michele Reiner
. While the ongoing fight over his funds remains unresolved, a recent development has allowed him to receive a nominal amount for his commissary account. The limited allowance comes as questions surrounding his access to the trust continue to play out.
Nick Reiner's Commissary Allowance Capped At $300
Brent Perniac/AdMedia/Newscom/MEGA
During a probate court hearing on October 23, a Los Angeles County Superior Court judge allowed Jodi Pais Montgomery, the successor trustee managing Nick's trust, to make "nominal distributions" to his commissary account not exceeding $300 at any given time.
Prior to the ruling, Nick's attorneys filed complaints stating their client has not received "even $5" from his trust to purchase necessities from the jail commissary.

Although the maximum cap is $300, the court order allows Montgomery to replenish the allowance as the amount is depleted over time.
Montgomery's lawyer noted that despite the pending decision on Nick's access to the trust, the allowance is "so de minimis [too small to be meaningful], and I think it would really be important for Nick Reiner's quality of life during his incarceration," per 
Rolling Stone
.
The Statute At The Center Of The Issue
Accused of killing both of his parents — acclaimed director Rob Reiner and Michele Singer Reiner — Nick Reiner is now fighting for access to money held in his family trust.

Nick's trust fund is valued between $1.5 and $1.7 million. It was established as an irrevocable trust in 1993, and terms dictate that half of the amount would become payable to Nick at 30 years old. He turned 33 in September.
However, different parties are opposed to Nick gaining access to his trust, citing the California 
Slayer Statute
, which prevents a person from inheriting or profiting from the estate of their victims.

Nick's legal team argued that he was owed half of the trust in 2023, two years before the murders of Rob and Michele occurred. Moreover, they noted that their client has not been found guilty of the crimes and must be presumed innocent.
Nick primarily wants access to his trust to rehire his former private criminal defense attorney, Alan Jackson. He is currently represented by public defender Kimberly Greene.

Paul Kanin previously handled Nick's trust before Montgomery took over, and according to him, he authorized $349,000 from the trust fund to be used for Nick's defense, per 
PEOPLE
.

Following Nick's arrest in December 2025 in connection with his parents' murders, he retained high-powered defense attorney Alan Jackson, who has represented clients such as Harvey Weinstein and Kevin Spacey.
In January 2026, Jackson withdrew from Nick's case, citing circumstances beyond his and his client's control. It was later reported that Nick's siblings, Jake and Romy, withdrew their agreement to pay their brother's legal fees.
Anita Wu, Nick's civil and probate attorney, argued, "Paying for Mr. Jackson's past work does not excuse withholding the rest of Nick's money," adding that Nick is owed at least $558,000 based on the terms of the trust.
A Mediator Will Decide Whether Nick's Trust Should Be Released
The dispute over Nick's trust may finally be resolved in a couple of months. Wu revealed that after discussions, the parties will meet for a "global mediation," wherein a private mediator will decide whether Nick can access his trust fund to pay for his legal defense.

Retired Judge Mitchell L. Beckloff, who supervised the handling of Michael Jackson's estate, will oversee the mediation, which is scheduled for December 16.
However, the results of the mediation and settlement will still require the formal approval of Judge Ruben Garcia, who is overseeing the trust case.
Inside Nick Reiner's Criminal Case
RCF / MEGA
Nick is accused of fatally stabbing his parents and was charged with two counts of first-degree murder with special circumstances. He pleaded 
not guilty
.
Los Angeles County District Attorney Nathan Hochman previously announced that prosecutors will not seek the death penalty in the case, noting that his office consulted with Nick's siblings, who did not want to pursue capital punishment.

If found guilty, Nick is facing a maximum sentence of life in prison without the possibility of parole.
A trial date for the criminal case has not been set due to procedural and pretrial setbacks, such as the trust fund mediation, an argument over whether or not to unseal transcripts from the grand jury, and Nick's mental health evaluations.

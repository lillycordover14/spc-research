Carolina can’t finish off Syracuse, suffers OT heartbreak&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

CHAPEL HILL — The Carolina red-zone defense that was so good in two consecutive road wins failed the Tar Heels when they needed big plays on a cool night at Kenan Stadium.

Syracuse tied it late in regulation and won in overtime with a short touchdown pass and a two-point conversion pass, handing UNC a heartbreaking 31–30 overtime loss on Saturday night.

“They made a couple more plays than we did today, and that was the difference in the game,” Coach Bill Belichick said during a three-minute press conference, the shortest of his tenure at UNC, about 90 seconds shorter than after the season-ending loss at N.C. State. “Hard-fought game, just like all the other ones we’ve seen Syracuse play, and we just came a little bit short.”

Carolina (4–3, 2–2 ACC) played clean football with no turnovers and only three penalties but couldn’t hold a 10-point lead midway through the third quarter in a game it could have put away. The Tar Heels couldn’t score a touchdown in the last 23 minutes of regulation, as Syracuse (3–4, 1–4) ended an 11-game ACC losing streak.

It was a particularly frustrating loss for UNC linebacker Derek McDonald, who had 16 tackles, including some big stops. But he was part of a defense that couldn’t slow his former Orange teammates down the stretch.

He said that problems came down to basic execution.

“It all just comes back to fundamentals,” McDonald said. “We preached that all week. I think we could have tackled better. We had a lot of leaky yardage. Could have covered better. Had better eyes.”

UNC quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. was accurate most of the night until his protection waned late, completing 27 of 44 passes for 277 yards, all season highs, with two touchdown passes and a 128.1 passer rating.

Carolina could have won the game with a 56-yard Rece Verhoff field-goal attempt on a fourth-and-three with 52 seconds left. But the Tar Heels chose to go for it, and Edwards was sacked for a two-yard loss.

Belichick said the risk of giving Syracuse favorable field position after a missed kick influenced his decision.

“If you miss it there, you give them another seven yards. They’re almost in field-goal range,” Belichick said. “So, fourth and three felt like we had a good play, and we just weren’t able to convert it.”

Wide receiver Jordan Shipp had another big game, catching eight passes for 102 yards and a touchdown, but he was more concerned with the opportunities Carolina squandered. The Tar Heels repeatedly moved the ball into scoring position but couldn’t finish drives with touchdowns.

“We’ve got to score. We get the ball in the red zone. We had a lot of great drives,” Shipp said. “But we need to turn those field goals into touchdowns.”

Shipp said Carolina had opportunities to build a more comfortable lead before Syracuse rallied, leaving the offense with its share of responsibility for the defeat.

“I don’t think there’s nothing holding us back. I mean, I think we’re holding ourselves back,” Shipp said. “Some self-inflicted wounds, some small stuff, just like it’s one small detail that could spring a play for a 60-yard touchdown.”

UNC scored on its opening drive in just 3:13, going 83 yards in seven plays for a 6-yard Edwards-to-Shipp touchdown pass as the Heels continued to flourish with scripted early drives. The second drive went 60 yards in 13 plays, but Verhoff booted a 27-yard field goal when it stalled at the Syracuse 7.

Syracuse got only 50 total yards in the first quarter but drove 88 yards in 16 plays (despite 25 yards in penalties) on a possession that started with 1:55 left in the opening quarter. Running back Ahmad Miller scored on a 2-yard run with 9:38 remaining in the first half.

The teams traded field goals late in the first half, with Verhoff’s 39-yard boot with 2:38 left and Shay Barker’s 30-yard kick as time expired, leaving UNC ahead 13–10 at halftime.

After tight end Jordan Washington dropped a pass alone in the end zone, Demon June bailed him out on the next play. June ran 25 yards for a touchdown on a fourth-and-one play, the longest UNC scoring run this season, to give UNC a 20–10 lead with 8:26 left in the third quarter.

Syracuse sputtered on successive drives that ended in fourth-down stops, a fourth-and-short with 4:24 left in the third quarter and a fourth-and-four at the UNC 30 with 12:08 left in the game.

Orange quarterback Steve Angeli was 9-for-20 for 83 yards through three quarters, and Syracuse wide receiver Umari Hatcher, who came into the game fourth in the country with 784 receiving yards, was mostly held in check.

That changed dramatically in the fourth quarter, starting with the two connecting on a 76-yard scoring play to slice UNC’s lead to three with 11:01 left to get Syracuse back into the game.

Belichick offered a brief assessment when asked what changed in Syracuse’s passing attack after the Orange struggled through three quarters.

“They hit a big pass,” Belichick said in one of many short answers.

Asked whether there was anything more to it, Belichick replied, “Well, I think if you take that play out of there, it’s probably about the same.”

McDonald acknowledged the Orange’s ability to produce explosive plays but said Carolina needed to maintain its defensive consistency through the final quarter.

“We did a good job, I thought, in the passing the first three [quarters], but gave up that big one,” McDonald said. “They have playmakers over there. I know that better than anyone. But we’ve just got to stay consistent and finish it through all four quarters.”

UNC’s offense revived after successive three-and-outs, going 71 yards in nine plays. But the drive stalled at the Syracuse 4 on consecutive inaccurate passes from Edwards, and Verhoff kicked a 22-yard field goal to give Carolina a 23–17 lead with 7:10 left.

Shipp said those missed opportunities were particularly frustrating because Carolina had shown it could sustain long possessions, move the chains and control the clock.

“We had some really good drives; some long drives took a lot of time off the clock,” Shipp said. “Now we just need to finish it and put it all together.”

The Orange drove 75 yards in 11 plays, with Tylik Hill’s 3-yard touchdown run with 1:54 left. But Barker’s point-after attempt went wide left, leaving it tied at 23.

In overtime, Edwards hit Shipp for 20 yards to the Syracuse 5 and connected with June on a 3-yard touchdown catch two plays later, giving UNC a 30–23 lead.

On Syracuse’s possession, Angeli’s 16-yard pass to Calvin Russell III put the Orange at the UNC 9. McDonald and Jaylen Harvey threw Miller for a two-yard loss, but Angeli connected with Russell for a fourth-down, four-yard touchdown pass.

Syracuse went for two, and Angeli hit Russell just at the goal line for the game-winning points, which were upheld after a replay review.

Belichick said he couldn’t tell from his position whether Russell had reached the goal line.

“It’s on the other side of the field. Man, I couldn’t see much,” Belichick said. “It’s hard to tell.”

McDonald said the Tar Heels anticipated the type of play Syracuse might run on the decisive conversion and believed they were in position to stop it.

“They’re going to get the ball to the pylons,” McDonald said. “I thought we were in pretty good position. It’s a game of inches, and it was literally an inch away from being a stop and us winning the game.”

The failure to finish off the Orange was especially painful after Carolina’s defense had produced multiple stops earlier in the game. Syracuse’s fourth-down conversions in overtime ultimately proved decisive.

McDonald said the Tar Heels repeatedly forced Angeli into difficult situations but couldn’t make the final play.

“I thought we did a good job getting him to third down. I thought we had good pressure on him, had him back there moving around a lot,” McDonald said. “But he made plays on third down and fourth down, that we just need to stop.”

Belichick pointed to the missed opportunities on both sides of the ball, including the long touchdown pass and Syracuse’s late conversions.

“We missed some opportunities offensively. Give a long touchdown. I mean, it’s pretty obvious,” Belichick said. “Had a couple chances to stop there on fourth down and two-point conversion. They made both those plays.”

For McDonald, the defeat was another reminder of how small mistakes can decide a close game, especially when the opponent keeps fighting for extra yards and converting critical downs.

“We need to drive our feet on contact and keep running through the runners,” McDonald said. “They ran pretty hard. Those flaws that I’m talking about, it’s just football, to be honest with you.”

Shipp said the Tar Heels had to resist pointing fingers after a loss in which both the offense and defense had chances to win. He emphasized the need to improve Carolina’s red-zone execution before the next game.

“We shouldn’t have put [the defense] in that position in the first place. We had an opportunity to score, didn’t take advantage of it,” Shipp said.

With No. 1 Miami coming to Kenan Stadium next week, McDonald said the Tar Heels must treat the loss as an opportunity to identify and correct their weaknesses.

“They expose some things that we need to get better at,” McDonald said. “So, we’ve got to come into this week even hungrier and ready to fix those things because we got a good team coming here this week.”

Shipp delivered a similar message to his teammates after the game, stressing that the missed opportunities mattered more than the frustration of another close defeat.

“Just we got to finish. That’s it,” Shipp said. “There’s nothing we can do to get this one back. But moving on to Miami.”

Notes

— UNC is back at home for another 7:30 game next Saturday (ABC), hosting the Hurricanes (7–0, 5–0), one of three remaining unbeaten teams, who rolled to a 52–23 home win Saturday over Pittsburgh.
— It was Carolina’s sixth consecutive loss in one-point games since a beating Pittsburgh 37–36 on Sept. 24, 2016, and the fifth time in seven games a UNC game has been decided by one score. The Tar Heels are 3–2 in those games.
— This was the sixth UNC opponent this season with at least one drive of seven minutes or more.
— The Tar Heels were without wide receivers Mason Humphrey and Keeyun Chapman; defensive backs Tre Miller and Jakob Weatherspoon; defensive linemen Viliami Moala, Vodney Cleveland and Jimmy-Phrisco Alo-Suliafu; and linebacker Ashton Blatt. Defensive back Kaleb Cost was listed as a game-time decision. Left guard Andrew Threatt came out late in the second quarter.
— Edwards completed 11 first-quarter passes, the first UNC quarterback with at least 10 completions in a quarter since Drake Maye in the third quarter against N.C. State in 2023. 
— UNC dropped two potential interceptions in the first half, one by Ade Willie and another by Jaden Patterson, and Jalon Thompson nearly snagged one in the third quarter.
— Verhoff handled kickoff duties instead of Aeron Burrell, who still did the punting.
—It was UNC’s first overtime game since a 17–16 home loss to Virginia last season on Oct. 25.
— The Orange are 3–0 in overtime against UNC, with all three games in Chapel Hill, earlier winning in three OTs in 2003 and 40–37 in two OTs in 2018.
— Syracuse has won three of five games at Kenan Stadium, but UNC leads the all-time series 5–4.

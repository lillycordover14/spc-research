Punch Brothers have made their seventh album without singing a single word, and for a band famous for its extraordinary musicianship, that might be the most exciting decision they’ve made in years. ‘The Unsung Adventures of Punch Brothers’, out now through Nonesuch Records, features 11 instrumental pieces that allow the Grammy-winning quintet to concentrate entirely on the musical conversations happening between their instruments. They’ve also released a performance video for “Song of the Water Kelpie (unsung)” and announced their first European tour in almost a decade, giving audiences another opportunity to witness just how adventurous acoustic music can become in the right hands.

The album is a significant first for Punch Brothers, whose recordings have long combined ambitious instrumental arrangements with songwriting that draws from bluegrass, classical music and contemporary folk traditions.

This time, the instruments do all the storytelling.

Eight of the album’s compositions are originals written by the band, while three are arrangements of traditional material. The recordings also mark the first Punch Brothers album featuring fiddler Brittany Haas, who joined the group in 2023.

Her arrival gives the project another creative voice, bringing fresh possibilities to an ensemble whose strength has always depended on the relationship between its musicians.

And that’s what makes an all-instrumental Punch Brothers album so appealing.

The group have developed a reputation for taking acoustic music into territory that few listeners would have associated with traditional bluegrass. Their arrangements can be intricate, unpredictable and extraordinarily demanding, yet there’s a playfulness to the way they work together.

A musical idea can pass between instruments, change character and return in an entirely different form. Rhythms shift, melodies become intertwined and individual performances contribute to a larger musical conversation.

When musicians have spent years developing that kind of relationship, an instrumental album gives them an enormous amount of creative freedom.

“Song of the Water Kelpie (unsung)” offers another introduction to the project, following the previously released “Ulysses Dances.” The band have also shared a performance video for the new piece, directed by Josh Goleman.

The title is particularly intriguing. A water kelpie belongs to Scottish folklore, where the mythical creature is associated with rivers and lakes. It’s a wonderfully evocative subject for musicians interested in the storytelling possibilities of instrumental music.

And a song without lyrics can leave plenty to the listener’s imagination.

The album was produced by Punch Brothers and engineered by Joseph Lorge, whose recent work includes Pino Palladino and Blake Mills’ ‘That Wasn’t a Dream’. Lorge received a Grammy Award for Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical, for that recording, bringing considerable experience to a project where the details of each instrumental performance are especially important.

Acoustic ensembles present an interesting recording challenge. Every instrument has its own attack, resonance and character, and the interaction between them is central to the music.

For a group as technically accomplished as Punch Brothers, capturing those details allows audiences to appreciate the nuances of their performances, from the smallest musical gesture to the most elaborate ensemble passage.

The new record also arrives alongside an extensive period of touring.

Punch Brothers embarked on their most ambitious North American schedule since 2019, with plans for 64 cities and performances at some of the continent’s most celebrated venues and festivals.

Their itinerary included New York’s Carnegie Hall, Nashville’s Ryman Auditorium, Newport Folk Festival, Telluride Bluegrass Festival and Spoleto Festival, bringing their adventurous approach to acoustic music before a wide variety of audiences.

And now Europe is next.

The band will return in January 2027 for its first European performances in almost 10 years, beginning in Bristol before visiting Glasgow, London, Düsseldorf, Paris, Amsterdam, Hamburg, Luxembourg, Berlin, Brno and Zurich.

The concerts will feature material from ‘The Unsung Adventures of Punch Brothers’ alongside selections from the group’s earlier catalogue, giving audiences a chance to hear how their music has developed across seven albums.

For longtime European listeners, it’s a particularly welcome announcement. Nearly a decade is a considerable gap between performances, especially for a band whose music comes alive through the interaction between individual musicians onstage.

There’s also something fitting about Punch Brothers taking an entirely instrumental record around the world.

Acoustic music has always travelled across borders through melodies, rhythms and instrumental traditions. Songs can change language and character as musicians share them, while familiar musical ideas acquire new qualities through different performers.

Punch Brothers have spent their career exploring those possibilities, bringing exceptional technique and a restless imagination to music with deep historical roots.

‘The Unsung Adventures of Punch Brothers’ gives that curiosity another setting, with 11 pieces that place the musicianship at the centre of the experience.

It’s a confident decision from a band that has already established itself as one of contemporary acoustic music’s most inventive ensembles. Their seventh album gives listeners the opportunity to appreciate the conversations between their instruments without the presence of lyrics.

And when Punch Brothers have something to say musically, they have plenty of ways to say it.

    Carrboro Town Commons playground reopens after $277,000 renovation

    The project replaced worn equipment at the existing playground and fixed other outstanding problems.

Hemadithya Pujari
, 
Staff Writer
                 
             •  
            
October 26, 2026
 

 

Carrboro officials and community members participate in a ribbon-cutting ceremony for the new playground at Town Commons on Friday, Oct. 16, 2026. Photo courtesy of the Town of Carrboro.
 

Families visiting Carrboro Town Commons now have a renovated playground with wheelchair-accessible equipment, musical features and panels that teach kids about local farming.

The Town of Carrboro held a grand opening for the playground earlier this month after construction began in August. The $277,000 project replaced worn equipment at the existing playground and fixed other outstanding problems. 

Town Manager Patrice Toney said the Town decides to renovate parks and facilities when safety concerns arise. At Town Commons, she said, the playground had drainage problems and its surfacing was coming up.

“We made this a priority through our budget process to budget capital improvements for this particular park,” Toney said.

Toney said the project was paid for through capital improvement funds in the Town’s fiscal year 2026-27 budget. Fixing the drainage was the largest expense, she said, so rain no longer damages the playground.

Accessibility was another major priority, Toney said, since most of the old equipment didn’t allow for wheelchairs. The new playground includes sensory and musical elements, an ADA-accessible spinner that can fit a wheelchair and a wider entrance.

Toney said those features make the playground more welcoming and accessible to people and children of all abilities.

The equipment uses purple and green, Carrboro’s branding colors, and is themed around the Carrboro Farmers’ Market, which sets up at Town Commons on Wednesdays and Saturdays.

Children and community members use the new playground at Carrboro Town Commons during its grand opening on Friday, Oct. 16, 2026. Photo courtesy of the Town of Carrboro.

Chloé Sherer, assistant manager of the Carrboro Farmers’ Market, said the market had no role in the design but was excited to learn about the theme. Panels throughout the playground teach kids about tillage, honeybees and mushrooms, and one asks kids to count donuts and pastries like the ones sold at the market.

“It’s got lots of different ways to engage the kids in not only playing but also learning and being able to connect that back to the farmers market,” Sherer said.

Sherer said Town Commons was built in the 1990s with the market in mind, but the old playground, a standard one with slides and monkey bars, wasn’t connected to it. Over time, she said, the playground became a gathering place for families who shop at the market. She said the market draws about 2,500 to 3,500 customers and about 50 vendors every Saturday during its main season from April through November.

On Wednesdays, Sherer said, families often join the market’s kids’ activities, which focus on nutrition and samples of produce from local farmers. Then they head to the playground.

“I’m really excited for the next generation of kids in Carrboro to just become a lot more connected to agriculture,” Sherer said.

She said she expects a rush of families now that the playground has reopened, and she hopes it brings in families who haven’t been to the market before.

Town Council member Jason Merrill said the council isn’t usually involved in decisions about individual playgrounds, but he said this one has always been popular, especially on weekends.

“I think it’s the type of facility that kind of demonstrates the best of what a well-functioning government can do, which is provide resources that are available to everyone,” Merrill said. “I think that’s doubly important when we talk about kids.”

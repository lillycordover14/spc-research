ELKINS, W.Va (WDTV) - Local leaders and guests gathered at the Rosie the Riveter statue in Elkins Monday to celebrate the memory of the women who stepped into critical workforce roles during World War II.
Elkins became the world’s first city to receive the new Rosie the Riveter tulip, designed to celebrate the women who embodied the spirit of Rosie the Riveter.
The tulip was designed by the Kingdom of the Netherlands last year, and a Dutch agricultural counselor joined the ceremony to present the tulip to the city of Elkins.
Families and friends of Rosies also had the chance to plant a tulip in their honor and memory.
Elkins Mayor Jerry Marco said some Rosies are still living in West Virginia today, and said it’s an honor to keep their spirit alive.
“This is a tremendous honor for me because, like I said, the spirit that the Rosie the Riveters had at that time, they stepped up and they really answered the call whenever their country truly needed them. And looking around, that’s the way West Virginians are wired as well, because we’re hard-working people that are willing to step up when our neighbors need something, we take pride. And that’s why, for me, it’s very personal to keep the Rosie spirit and the West Virginia spirit alive,” Marco said.
The statue is located at Veterans Memorial Park in Elkins.

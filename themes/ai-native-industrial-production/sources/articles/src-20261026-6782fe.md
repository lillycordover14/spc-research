Pregame Notes

Last Time Out
: Both squads will wrap up their preseason slates on Tuesday night in what will be North Carolina’s third October outing and Charlotte’s second. The Tar Heels fell to Indiana in Indianapolis last week, 76-58, in a sluggish setback after its opening-night 18-point win over USC. Carolina struggled with the Hoosiers’ size, getting outrebounded 53-31, while also seeing limited production from its bench, which tallied 19 of its 58 points.

The 49ers’ exhibition schedule tipped off with a trip to Athens, Ga. on Oct. 16 to take on Georgia. Charlotte allowed 17 Bulldog 3-pointers and lost, 87-67, despite dominating the points in the paint battle, 40-22. A 4-for-19 display from beyond the arc limited the 49ers, too, who were overpowered by a fringe top-50 Bulldogs squad. As the Tar Heels did in their opener, Charlotte rotated heavily through its roster, giving double-digit minutes to 12 different players. The 49ers kept pace with Georgia in the second half, but couldn’t overcome the 17-point hole they had dug themselves in the opening 20 minutes. (Note the Tar Heels host Georgia on Nov. 13.)

Analyzing Charlotte
: Miller, well known in Chapel Hill from his time as a member of the Tar Heel backcourt from 2004-07, is the new head man at Charlotte after stints at Cincinnati and UNC Greensboro. Also on Miller’s first staff in the Queen City is another familiar face in Marcus Paige, who spent three seasons on North Carolina’s staff under Hubert Davis following a decorated Tar Heel playing career from 2012-16. 

Assembling his first team with the 49ers, Miller landed a handful of transfers — three coming with him from the Bearcats — to elevate a group that went 17-17 overall and 9-9 in the American Conference a season ago. Charlotte’s starters in its lone exhibition consisted of four additions from the portal and 
Arden Conyers
, the lone returner in the lineup, who averaged 7.5 points per game last season.

As shown by its success down low against the Bulldogs, Miller assembled a team with intriguing positional size for the mid-major level. Eight of Charlotte’s 14 rostered players are 6-foot-8 or taller, including 7-footer 
Peyton Marshall
 from Georgia Tech, who didn’t attempt a field goal against Georgia but will likely be an asset for the 49ers down low and will be familiar with ACC size.

In the backcourt, former Cincinnati guard 
Keyshuan Tillery
 is poised to play a big role for Charlotte after averaging 3.8 points and 12.4 minutes a season ago under Miller with the Bearcats. In his Charlotte debut, Tillery notched a team-high in minutes while attempting 13 field goals. And despite a quiet night in Athens, Chaze Harris is the other key name to know, entering as a fifth-year senior from South Alabama, where he averaged 19.2 points, 4.7 rebounds and 4.9 assists on his way to winning Sun Belt Player of the Year last season. 

By the Numbers:
 Charlotte was picked to finish fifth in the American Conference, which would replicate where it finished in the 2025-26 season. Metrically speaking, KenPom places Charlotte at No. 147 nationally and Evan Miya closely follows suit with a No. 155 ranking, but Bart Torvik has Miller’s first team in the preseason top-100, coming in at No. 97. The Tar Heels rank in the 40s and 50s across different metric sites and are officially unranked in the preseason AP Top 25 poll.

The Matchup: 
UNC should clearly out-talent its opponent for the first time this exhibition season, making for a different lens to look through when evaluating Carolina’s performance. Starting with the boards, the Tar Heels will have to fend off another able rebounding team, who went toe-to-toe with an SEC opponent in that area of the floor in its first game of the season under a new head coach.

Still, it can’t just be 
Sayon Keita
 on the glass for UNC. Last weekend in Indianapolis, the 7-foot-1 freshman grabbed nine rebounds against Indiana, while no other Tar Heel had more than four. The matchup also serves as another data point for the backcourt trio of 
Terrence Brown
, 
Matt Able
 and 
Neo Avdalas
, who have yet to find success in unison through two preseason games.

But that task for the three coveted transfers isn’t just offensively. Tillery and Harris pose another legitimate matchup on defense for Carolina’s guards, which allowed a combined 47 points to Markus Burton, Darren Harris and Bryce Lindsay in Gainbridge Fieldhouse eight days ago.

Above all, though, the Tar Heels’ final tune-up offers a peek at how Malone may adjust and tinker after a loss — one that he clearly felt had much to improve on. Without 
Isaiah Denis
 available, Malone played all five starters 23 or more minutes against Indiana. As the season-opener rapidly approaches, how the UNC staff designates minutes and roles continues to be an evolving storyline as the pieces are coming together for the true dawn of the Malone era on Nov. 2.

Israeli media highlights Egypt’s Rafale deployment as part of broader regional deterrence strategy

0 Comments
 

October 26, 2026 at 1:39 pm

 

A Rafale fighter of Dassault performs at the 54th International Paris Air Show at Le Bourget Airport, north of Paris, France on June 22, 2023. [Mustafa Yalçın – Anadolu Agency]

Israeli media have highlighted the deployment of Egyptian Rafale fighter jets on the eastern front, describing the move as part of a broader military strategy aimed at strengthening Egypt’s deterrence capabilities and maintaining operational readiness across multiple regional fronts.

The Israeli platform 
Ntziv.net
 reported that Egypt possesses an advanced network of modern airbases, challenging the assumption that its most sophisticated fighter aircraft are positioned toward Israel because of insufficient infrastructure elsewhere in the country.

According to the platform, Egypt’s deployment of modern combat aircraft, particularly the French-made Dassault Rafale, reflects a strategy of multi-front deterrence, strategic independence and regional power projection extending beyond its eastern border.

The report identified Gabal al-Basur Air Base, northwest of Cairo, as the principal operating base for Egypt’s Rafale fleet, noting that it is located well inside Egyptian territory, away from the Sinai Peninsula and the Israeli border.

It emphasised that the Rafale is a multirole fighter aircraft with an operational radius of approximately 2,000 kilometres, allowing it to conduct missions across several strategically important areas without requiring permanent deployment at forward bases.

These areas include Libya to the west, the Mediterranean Sea, the Red Sea and the Sinai Peninsula.

The new Arab bargain: When Egypt offers to protect Saudi Arabia

The platform also pointed to Egypt’s extensive network of operational airbases, including facilities in Mersa Matruh and Sidi Barrani near the Libyan border, as well as Aswan in southern Egypt.

It noted that Cairo has invested billions of dollars in modernizing its air force infrastructure, including the development of Berenice Airbase on the Red Sea coast, a strategic facility designed to accommodate advanced combat aircraft.

The report argued that Egypt’s air force modernization should not be viewed exclusively through the lens of its military relationship with Israel, but rather as part of a wider doctrine of active deterrence and strategic autonomy.

It further suggested that Egypt has sought to diversify its military suppliers to reduce dependence on the United States, particularly given Washington’s restrictions on certain advanced weapons exports intended to preserve Israel’s qualitative military edge.

According to the Israeli platform, Egypt’s acquisition of Rafale fighters from France has enabled Cairo to obtain advanced radar and missile capabilities outside the framework of US arms export restrictions.

The report concluded that Egypt’s Rafale fleet and expanding military infrastructure reflect a broader effort to maintain operational flexibility across several regional theaters while strengthening the country’s independent defense capabilities.

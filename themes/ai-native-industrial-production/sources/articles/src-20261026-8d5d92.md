Chip designers AMD, NVIDIA and Intel are rumored to have paid upwards of $5 billion to substrate manufacturers in Taiwan, according to a new supply chain report from the region. The figure matches the investment by Taiwan's three biggest substrate manufacturers, Kansas, Nanya and Umicron, as they expand their production plants and factories to meet the expanded demand. The report follows an earlier one from the Korean media which had claimed that Tesla and NVIDIA were expanding investments in Samsung to lock in five-year substrate supplies. 

Taiwan's Substrate Manufacturers Step Up Capacity Expansion As Demand From Big Three Chip Designers Intensifies 

Substrates are a key part of the semiconductor supply chain as they enable firms such as TSMC to package different components such as memory and GPU chips in a single module. The most common substrate used in this area is the Ajinomoto Build-up Film, or ABF

The booming demand for AI chips and server equipment has also made its mark on the substrate market. For instance, a report in May claimed that ABF substrate manufacturers were raising spot prices amidst tight capacity and long lead times. Specifically, the build-up film, which is a key component of the substrate 
was set for a 30% price hike
, which was expected to translate into a 3% to 6% price hike for the final substrate product. 

Now, a r
eport
 from Taiwan claims that NVIDIA, AMD and Intel are eager to take steps to relieve the substrate capacity constraints. As per the UDN, the three are investing more than $5 billion in the Taiwanese substrate supply chain in order to reserve long-term capacity. The details outline that while the major substrate companies do not discuss specific customers, they have started to expand their production capacity. 

The report follows one from the 
Korean media last week
 which had outlined that NVIDIA and Tesla were investing in Samsung in order to reserve capacity for Flip Chip-Ball Grid Array (FC-BGA) substrates. These are a newer technology that is used primarily in AI chips, and the report outlined that the reason behind the expansion was because this region was expected to be the next area of shortage in the AI chip supply chain. 

 

 

Focusing specifically on Tesla, the report added that the supply of substrates would be for chips destined for the firm's autonomous vehicles and humanoid robots. At the firm's recent earnings call, 
CEO Elon Musk outlined
 plans to start producing the robots and his opinion about the value of his firm's Terafab chip manufacturing project.

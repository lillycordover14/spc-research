The shift from mining rigs to AI clusters
 
 
IREN's core business has undergone an aggressive transformation. Originally established as a renewable-powered Bitcoin miner, the company has spent the past year systematically decommissioning its mining hardware to install high-performance NVIDIA GPU clusters.
 
 
 
 
 
That strategy crossed an important milestone in the fiscal fourth quarter. AI Cloud revenue reached $70.5 million—accounting for 51.4% of total revenue—officially surpassing cryptocurrency mining as the company's largest business unit for the first time.
 
 
Forward revenue visibility has expanded alongside the infrastructure buildout. Contracted annual recurring revenue exited the quarter at approximately $500 million, reached $1 billion after Microsoft formally accepted the initial 50-megawatt Horizon 1 facility, and management expects contracted ARR to surpass $4 billion by the end of calendar 2026.
 
 
Major technology partnerships have helped validate the transition. Beyond an estimated $9.7 billion long-term agreement with Microsoft through 2031, IREN recently signed a multi-year cloud contract with Anthropic and secured a $2.4 billion GPU equipment financing package led by Blue Owl Capital. With access to over 5 gigawatts of grid-connected power across North America and Europe, the company has secured a valuable commodity in an electricity-constrained market.

Capital intensity meets the neocloud reality check
 
 
Despite rapid contract wins, funding an overnight industrial overhaul has created severe balance-sheet strain.
 
 
In its latest quarterly results, IREN's revenue of $137.2 million fell 12.7% short of analyst expectations, declining sequentially as mining hardware was unplugged faster than replacement AI server racks could be brought online. The quarter resulted in a steep $684 million net loss, driven by $552.5 million in non-cash asset impairments on retired mining equipment.
 
 
More critically, capital requirements are accelerating. IREN's total outstanding debt climbed to $7.84 billion at the end of fiscal 2026, while management guided fiscal 2027 capital expenditures at an unprecedented $25 billion to $30 billion.
 
 
Investor sentiment toward independent "neocloud" operators has also soured. A recent Gartner research report warned that more than 80% of independent AI cloud providers could cease operating as standalone entities by 2030 as soaring GPU procurement costs outpace enterprise billing.

Balancing contract momentum against balance-sheet strain
 
 
IREN has executed a remarkable operational shift, securing top-tier enterprise customers and locking down crucial power infrastructure ahead of competitors.
 
 
 
 
 
However, for investors evaluating where to deploy capital today, underwriting a company with $7.8 billion in debt, deeply negative free cash flow, and a $30 billion capex hurdle carries substantial downside risk. If credit conditions tighten or cloud pricing softens, the margin of safety for equity holders remains razor-thin.
 
 
IREN may eventually reward aggressive bulls, but buying into cash-burning infrastructure plays leaves zero room for error if project timelines slip. Rather than betting on whether a debt-fueled corporate pivot survives, data-driven strategies target proven businesses with established earnings and healthy balance sheets.

CSIRO’s Powder Lab supports end-to-end additive manufacturing processes, from feedstock development to prototype fabrication. Source: CSIRO 

The Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organization (CSIRO)
, Australia’s national science agency, has opened the Powder Lab, a collaborative facility supporting supports end-to-end additive manufacturing, in Melbourne, Australia.

Collaborators can leverage the Powder Lab’s resources for every step of the additive manufacturing process, from feedstock development and powder processing to powder metallurgy and prototype fabrication. The facility contains powder processing and characterization equipment, including technology that is, according to CSIRO, distinctive in Australia, to manufacture the specialized powders that power metal 3D printing. These technologies are intended to support collaborative research and development, as well as material testing and improved powder performance.

The facility is designed to provide cheaper, tailored feedstocks for lighter, stronger and higher-performing parts, as well as offer manufacturers access to specialized expertise and equipment. The goal is to help manufacturers reduce waste, accelerate innovation and build more products locally.

The Powder Lab also creates advanced powders that are used to manufacture complex parts that otherwise may have been difficult to produce using conventional methods, like those seen in the aerospace, manufacturing, resources and battery sectors.

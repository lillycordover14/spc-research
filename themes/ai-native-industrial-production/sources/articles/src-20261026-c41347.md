Tesla And Nvidia Are Reportedly Funding Samsung’s AI Chip Substrate Push
Maeil Business Newspaper said the pair will put 3.7 trillion won into Samsung Electro-Mechanics’ Sejong FC-BGA lines, with supply contracts running 2029-2033.
Finimize Newsroom
1 min
What's going on here?

South Korea’s Maeil Business Newspaper reports that Tesla and Nvidia plan to put 3.7 trillion won into Samsung Electro-Mechanics to expand production of FC-BGA substrates, a key material used to package high-end AI chips, at its Sejong site.

What does this mean?

FC-BGA substrates sit under the chip and help connect it to the rest of the system, and they’re tough to scale because the manufacturing is precise and the equipment takes time to install. Maeil said Tesla would contribute 1.1 trillion won and Nvidia 2.6 trillion won, with the new lines tied to a supply contract running 2029-2033. That’s notable because it looks less like a normal purchase order and more li..
ore like customers pre-funding capacity years in advance to avoid future shortages. Samsung Electro-Mechanics told MT Newswires it can’t share client details, and Tesla and Nvidia didn’t immediately comment, but the structure fits a wider 
trend
: as AI hardware gets more complex, “packaging” and its materials are becoming as strategic as the chips themselves.

Why should I care?

For markets:
 
A 3.7 trillion won commitment hints FC-BGA capacity is being reserved far ahead of demand.

If the reported timeline is right, Samsung Electro-Mechanics gets something manufacturers value highly: long-dated volume commitments that can make future factory utilization steadier and reduce the risk that new capacity sits idle. For Tesla and Nvidia, putting money up front is a way to lock in a hard-to-substitute input during 2029-2033, when competition for AI-related components could be intense. The bigger takeaway is where pricing power may sit: when customers feel they need to pre-fund, it’s usually a sign the bottleneck is the component supplier, not the end-market brand.

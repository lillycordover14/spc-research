Tar Heels in NFL: Big games for Maye, Williams bookend Week 7; Stewart, Ezeudu start&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

Drake Maye opened Week 7 with another 300-yard passing performance and three total touchdowns Thursday night, and Javonte Williams closed it Monday with the best rushing performance of his NFL career.

The former Carolina stars provided the week’s biggest highlights for Tar Heels in the NFL, although their teams had dramatically different results.

Maye threw for 321 yards and two touchdowns and ran for another score in New England’s 30–24 loss at Chicago. Four days later, Williams rushed for a career-high 155 yards and a touchdown on 25 carries to lead Dallas to a 38–21 road victory over Philadelphia.

Williams’ performance was particularly impressive against an Eagles defense that couldn’t consistently contain him. He averaged 6.2 yards per carry, broke off a 34-yard run and 
scored on a 2-yard carry with 2:21 remaining to help put the game away
. It was the first game this season he wasn’t targeted for any passes.

The Cowboys had struggled to establish their running game in their previous two losses, and Williams said the emphasis against Philadelphia was restoring balance.

“We were just trying to get back to a balanced offense, and I feel like we did a great job tonight,” Williams, who had 21.5 fantasy points, said, according to the 
Cowboys’ official website
.

Williams said Coach Brian Schottenheimer had emphasized reaching at least 25 rushing attempts, and his effectiveness helped Dallas stick with that approach.

“It was good to give [Schottenheimer] the confidence to keep calling the runs,” Williams said.

Quarterback Dak Prescott praised Williams for the qualities that make him valuable beyond his rushing statistics.

“He’s a true pro. One of the best teammates, the guy that does everything right, a smart football player, and a physical football player,” Prescott said.

Williams also earned the highest Pro Football Focus offensive grade of his six-year NFL career, an 88.0, surpassing his previous best of 81.9 against Jacksonville in Week 2 of his rookie season in 2021. His 85.9 rushing grade was his best this season, and his 82.4 pass-blocking grade reflected his contributions beyond carrying the ball.

The performance came three days after Williams expressed frustration with Dallas’ offensive inconsistency and challenged the Cowboys to demonstrate their potential.

“We just got to go out. Like I said, we just got to go out and show the world,” Williams said in a 
Friday interview with Dallas Sports Journal
.

Williams has rushed for 472 yards and eight touchdowns through seven games, averaging 4.3 yards per carry. He also has 19 receptions for 103 yards and another touchdown, bringing his total to nine touchdowns.

Maye was similarly productive Thursday, completing 24 of 31 passes (77.4%) with two touchdowns and one interception for a 117.8 passer rating. He also led the Patriots with 26 rushing yards on four carries.

His 7-yard touchdown run with 11:33 remaining in the second quarter gave him his second rushing score of the season. He later connected with running back Rhamondre Stevenson on a 48-yard touchdown pass as the first half ended and found tight end Eli Raridon for a 30-yard score with 3:46 left in the third quarter.

Maye accounted for 347 yards of total offense and produced 28.44 fantasy points despite the defeat.

Although Maye’s numbers were impressive, he focused afterward on the mistakes that kept New England from winning, particularly an end-zone interception.

“It definitely was a huge point in the game,” 
Maye said
 of the interception. “It’s those plays, I talk about all the time. And those plays that can swing the game, can impact the game, and that was definitely one of them right there.”

He also took responsibility for the offense’s difficulty handling Chicago’s pressure.

“Disappointed in myself. I got to have some answers to get the ball out and not have our guys holding up for that long and trying to get some stuff going,” Maye said.

Patriots coach Mike Vrabel praised his quarterback’s competitiveness despite the defeat.

“He gave us a chance. He competed and I know he’d like to have that throw back to Eli [Raridon] but, I thought he really competed,” Vrabel said. “I’m glad he’s ours.”

Through seven games, Maye has completed 141 of 214 passes (65.9%) for 1,784 yards, 13 touchdowns and eight interceptions. He also rushed for 211 yards and two touchdowns, bringing his combined passing and rushing yards to 1,995.

Stewart starts for Houston

Houston safety 
M.J. Stewart
 made his first start since returning from the physically unable to perform (PUP) list (torn quadriceps muscle) and was one of the Texans’ busiest defenders in a 27–21 home loss to the New York Giants.

Starting in place of injured safety Reed Blankenship, Stewart collected five solo tackles, seven assists, half a sack, a quarterback hit and a pass defended. He played 63 defensive snaps, playing in 95% of Houston’s defensive plays.

Stewart, who was activated Oct. 10, has played three games this season and made one start.

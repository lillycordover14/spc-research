Anduril Industries and Qualcomm launch BlueChip to rebuild U.S. computer manufacturing

By Martin Chomsky (Defence Industry Europe)

                    Corporate                

|

                October 28, 2026            

Photo: Anduril Industries.

Anduril Industries and Qualcomm Technologies are developing a laptop designed and assembled in the United States, aiming to rebuild domestic computer manufacturing for defense, government and other critical industries. Anduril has completed functional prototypes and plans to open orders in 2027.

The laptop is the first product in BlueChip, a new hardware line intended to build a U.S. supplier base and the capacity to manufacture complete computers at scale. Qualcomm will supply its Snapdragon X Series platform, providing general-purpose processing, graphics and on-device artificial intelligence.

Anduril plans to manufacture the laptops in the United States, with circuit board assembly planned for its Arsenal-1 facility in Ohio. It is qualifying suppliers and developing domestic production capabilities for enclosures, battery components and other hardware ahead of planned availability in 2027.

U.S. and allied-country suppliers account for more than 90% of the prototypes’ component procurement cost, according to Anduril. The company plans to expand American sourcing in stages, with a fully domestic supply chain as its long-term goal.

The engineering work reflects the limits of the existing U.S. supply base, with the laptop being designed around materials and manufacturing capabilities available domestically. That includes an enclosure suited to U.S. machining, metals that American industry can produce at scale, battery and power systems adapted to existing U.S. battery technology, and a bonding process for American-made display glass.

Anduril said dependence on foreign computer manufacturers leaves access to essential hardware vulnerable to conflict and overseas supply disruptions. While the United States has invested billions of dollars in semiconductor production, the company said complete computers also require circuit boards, displays, batteries and other components still largely made abroad.

BlueChip is intended to serve first responders and public safety organizations alongside defense and government customers. Anduril said the supplier relationships, designs and production processes developed for the laptop would support further products in the range.

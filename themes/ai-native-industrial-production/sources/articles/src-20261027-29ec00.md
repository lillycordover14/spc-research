About 300 Boston Dynamics employees protest ICE robot dogs deal&nbsp;&nbsp;Yahoo

[page text]
Radeco's modified Spot robot sits at the center of the ICE robot dog's controversy. | Image Credit: Boston Dynamics
About 300 Boston Dynamics employees want the company to cancel its ICE robot dogs deal, but company leadership is refusing to back down.
The standoff centers on a $1.3 million order for four modified 
Spot
 robots. It also tests how far a robotics maker can go in policing what customers do with its machines.
The ICE robot dogs contract covers four modified Spot units
ICE is buying these robot dogs through Radeco, a Connecticut-based company that customizes Spot. Radeco adds things like a robotic arm, a thermal camera, sensors for hazards, and even explosives.

ICE did not seek competing bids. Instead, it argued that only Radeco's version met its requirements.
Contract records point to inspecting tunnels along the southwest border and other dangerous spaces. ICE is yet to disclose where the robots will be deployed or how its agents will be trained.
Boston Dynamics leans on its terms of sale
The company says these robots will go to Homeland Security Investigations, which mainly targets transnational crime. According to WUBR, they won't be used to handle deportations.
A Boston Dynamics spokesperson rejected any suggestions that claim the robots will handle detentions or deportations. "Spot robots are tools that help keep people out of harm's way," the spokesman said.

The company's terms also ban weapons and any use meant to harm or intimidate people. Boston Dynamics said if ICE broke those rules, it would void the warranty, refuse software updates and end the license.
Employees think the deal will hurt hiring
The petition went to executives earlier last month, and almost half the signatures are anonymous. It says the risk to the company is "simply too great to continue with this deal.
It points to a September 2025 raid on a Hyundai site in Georgia. Federal agents had detained 475 people, most of them South Korean. Hyundai Motor Group purchased Boston Dynamics in 2021.
Signers say the deal would cost the company teammates and could make hiring harder.

Executive Amanda McMaster emailed staff, urging them to "respect and accept this decision, even if you do not agree with it."
The timing is particularly awkward for Boston Dynamics. Earlier this year in June, it announced a $100 million robotics and AI center in Waltham, Massachusetts.
For now, keeping these robots out of enforcement work rests on a contract clause and the company's word.

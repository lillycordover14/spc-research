Gratitude

The world feels like a difficult place at the moment, and it can feel very hard sometimes to find things to be thankful for; when we are overwhelmed, we have less capacity to notice and reflect on the good things. Yet, ironically, proactively cultivating gratitude is one of the things that can actually help us feel less stressed.

NHS GP dR RADHA

The science

Research has shown that feeling or expressing thanks can actually alter our brain.

The balance of neurotransmitters change; more serotonin and dopamine (feel good chemicals) are produced.

This can be long lasting if we make gratitude a regular habit.

We also get a decrease in our cortisol (stress hormone) levels which helps lower anxiety.

Being grateful can strengthen the nerve pathways which triggers positive emotion.

Golden rules for happy relationships

Start with your own behaviours
The foundation of a secure, connected life is being what I call CARRP: that means being consistent, available, responsive, reliable and predictable. All of these qualities can be enacted in small gestures every day.

Side view of affectionate happy couple sitting in the cozy cafe. – stock photo. (Photo: Getty)

Express your needs
People need consistency and connection to feel safe. This is not, ‘You hurt my feelings by never replying to me’, it’s more ‘this is the kind of contact that helps me feel secure’.

You don’t need hundreds of friends. You need people and places that regularly remind you that you belong.

Reach for a
secure script

When something unsettles you in a relationship, your first reaction is not always the most accurate one. 

If you’re anxious, your brain may jump very quickly to: ‘this is the beginning of the end’. If you’re avoidant, your brain may say: ‘this person wants too much from me, I need to get away’. So one very practical thing you can always do is pause and ask: ‘What would a secure person do here?’

When people talk about loneliness, they often assume it means you don’t have enough people in your life. But you can be surrounded by people and still feel lonely if those relationships leave you feeling excluded, ignored or disconnected.

What to do

Build a community that works for your temperament and gives you regular, reliable moments of connection.

You can still be kind to people while not treating them as someone who belongs in your inner circle. 

Explain what you need. Sometimes people simply don’t realise how you are experiencing them.

Check in with people: does this person actually feel that I’m reliable? Do they experience me as predictable? That’s the only way that you can start to become more secure.

Dividends are a payment that a company makes to its shareholders out of its profits and directors often take some of their income through this method, sometimes because it involves lower tax rates than regular income.

Ian said frozen income tax thresholds were problematic too. The amount people can earn before paying tax has been frozen at £12,570 since 2021 – when the Conservatives were in charge – as has the higher threshold at which people pay 40 per cent tax.

‘NI hike should be scrapped’

Samantha Evans, 59, lives in Henley-on-Thames with her partner, Benet Slay, 64, an investor and non-executive director, who has two adult children. 

Samantha runs a luxury countryside concierge business, Humphreys on Henley. Its turnover for this year is expected to be about £550,000, and she said is set to double next year. 

Samantha said: “We live in a large, detached home, and enjoy our holidays and a comfortable lifestyle. Everything is more expensive, so we are eating out less.”

Samantha said Labour should introduce policies “which enable 
businesses
 to grow”.

Samantha Evans said she had to employ fewer people as a result of employer national insurance contributions being upped in a previous Budget

She added: “There is no logic in forcing businesses to close through excessive taxation. The welfare system makes it more profitable for an individual to be on benefits than to work. 

“We’re also concerned about 
inheritance tax
. We work hard and are happy to pay taxes, but shouldn’t be penalised for contributing to the economy.”

Samantha said she believed the Government did not view her as a working person.

She said: “According to the Government, I am a non-worker. I work a 12-hour day and often on weekends. I expect a Government that is supposedly focused on growth to acknowledge the contribution I make to the economy.”

Samantha said she had to employ fewer people as a result of employer 
national insurance
 (NI) contributions being upped in a previous Budget. 

At the 2024 Budget, Labour increased the NI rate paid by employers from 13.8 per cent to 15 per cent.

She said: “I want to see a 10 per cent reduction in VAT for hospitality, a reversal of the NI contributions increase and realistic limits for the tourism taxes so local councils don’t have a free rein.”

‘Not all my income above £100,000 is disposable’

Graham Robinson, 45, lives in Timperley, Greater Manchester, and runs KeySIM, a firm supplying SIM cards to businesses. 

Last year, Graham’s gross annual income was £126,500. After deductions, it was £79,000. 

Graham said: “I expect the Government would regard me as well-off. The £100,000 personal allowance taper is a sign that the tax system treats that as a point where an allowance can start to be withdrawn. But that doesn’t mean the income above £100,000 is all disposable. 

‘Frozen personal tax thresholds and dividend taxes affect how much I can take home from my business,’ Graham Robinson said

“I do feel the pressure of higher costs and taxes. I’ve cut back on holidays and put off major home improvements. At times, it does feel as if people who work hard to grow a business are treated as an easy source of extra tax.”

Graham said he was negatively affected by frozen income tax thresholds and higher dividend taxes. 

He’s also affected by the 
£100,000 tax trap
, a tax quirk where earners face an effective marginal income tax rate of 60 per cent on income between £100,000 and £125,140, because they lose their personal allowance in between these two earnings figures.

The £100,000 threshold has not been changed in 16 years, despite high inflation over that time reducing the value of that figure. Both the Conservatives and Labour have had long periods of time in charge since it has been in place. 

Graham said: “I want tax thresholds to rise with the 
cost of living
 so the rules keep roughly the same value over time.

“Frozen personal tax thresholds and dividend taxes affect how much I can take home from my business. If I need to draw more income to maintain the same take-home pay as costs rise, there is less money left in the business to reinvest. 

“Recent employer NI changes are also a direct cost to the company. I want to create opportunities for people rather than grappling with an ever-heavier tax burden.”

‘I want more clarity on inheritance tax’

Dr Adrian Massey, 50, a food innovation consultant, lives in Banbury, Oxfordshire, with his wife and their two children. 

Adrian said: “We’re financially comfortable and I’m grateful for that. But it’s come through years of hard work, careful financial decisions and taking risks.”

Adrian, who owns ATM Food Innovations, said: “For me, the combination of frozen thresholds and higher dividend taxes makes it harder to plan how much income I can take from my business and put towards our family’s long-term financial security.”

In the upcoming Budget, Adrian said he was concerned about 
property taxes
, capital gains tax and inheritance tax.

Dr Adrian Massey said it sometimes felt the Government viewed people like him as a bit of a ‘cash cow’

Adrian said: “I’m exploring my first property investment, so the costs of buying, renting and eventually selling are important. 

“With higher tax rates on personally owned rental income already planned for 2027 [rates of tax on rental income are going up by 2 percentage points next year], further changes could affect whether an investment makes financial sense.”

He added: “I’m building our retirement savings, but I also want to help give my children a strong financial start in life, while hopefully passing on some of what we’ve built in the future. 

“Changes to inheritance tax, particularly the treatment of unused pensions from 2027, make that planning more complicated, and further changes could be introduced.”

Adrian said it sometimes felt the Government viewed people like him as a bit of a “cash cow”, though he believes everyone should pay their fair share of tax. 

He added: “I want greater stability and clarity around pensions, 
ISAs
, property taxes, inheritance tax and income tax thresholds that reflect inflation. I’d like to see a system that encourages people to save and plan for their future rather than continually moving the goalposts.”

With the Ravens surrendering 35 unanswered points to fall 35-13 to Cincinnati for a second straight loss in Week 7, I’ve offered a dozen thoughts, each in 50 words or less:

1. Where does 
Sunday’s second half rank
 among the worst performances in franchise history? The 2017 London game and the 2012 Houston trip come to mind, and Jacksonville scored 42 before halftime in a 1998 home game. But I’d still say 
the 2007 Monday night massacre in Pittsburgh
 takes the cake. 

2. There are too many near-nine-figure contracts and early draft picks on 
this Ravens defense
 to continue coming up small as regularly as this group does in the biggest moments. That Jesse Minter acknowledged a need for more player leadership speaks volumes about a veteran-laden group. The criticism is warranted.

3. I’m a huge 
Kyle Hamilton
 fan, but Cincinnati’s third touchdown drive began with him missing a tackle, continued with his holding penalty two plays later, and concluded with him whiffing again on Samaje Perine’s touchdown. If that’s your best player at a critical juncture, what can you expect from others?

4. 
Derrick Henry is averaging 3.5 yards per carry since the Week 1 opener
 as the running game just isn’t where it needs to be. Injuries to Lamar Jackson and the offensive line clearly haven’t helped, but the loss of blocking tight end Durham Smythe is probably an underrated factor. 

5. I’ve reflected on 
the decision to go for fourth-and-2 from the 12 to open the fourth quarter
. I’d usually agree wholeheartedly with needing a touchdown, but between Baltimore’s short-yardage misery and fragile psyche, you wonder if taking three points and a lead could have served as a mental reset there.

 
8

6. Just when the Ravens stabilize the center position with 
Cam Jurgens
 and have a suitable starting offensive line in place, 
Vega Ioane
 goes down with a knee injury expected to cost him some time. There just isn’t enough depth to continue to endure injuries to that group.

7. 
Declan Doyle
 has drawn much praise and shows great promise as a first-time play-caller, but the inability to sustain offensive production the last couple weeks has been disappointing. So many variables differ, of course, but Todd Monken put together game plans for Huntley resulting in 30-plus points twice last year. 

8. 
Winning with a backup quarterback requires others to step up
 to fill the void. Both Mark Andrews and Ja’Kobi Lane had opportunities to turn that final scoring drive of the first half from a field goal into a touchdown. Those weren’t easy catches, but someone has to make a play. 

9. There weren’t many positives defensively, but 
Travis Jones
 turned in his best game of 2026. He registered a tackle for a loss and a quarterback hit to go along with PFF crediting him with three pressures. The Ravens need more of that from him after a quiet start.

10. 
The special teams
 continue to do very little to make a positive difference. LaJohntay Wester isn’t providing much of a spark in the return game, and Baltimore surrendered a 31-yard punt return in the third quarter that helped turn the tide for Cincinnati completely.

11. Upon rewatching Sunday’s game, I couldn’t help but notice a fan sitting in the front row being the closest person in a Ravens jersey to Bengals tight end Drew Sample on the final touchdown. 
No wonder Ed Reed and Terrell Suggs couldn’t help but voice their disgust
 on social media.

12. Baltimore’s 
0-2 in the AFC North for the first time since 2007
. Jackson’s return will obviously deodorize some concerns, but Minter said it all: “It’s kind of one of those points in the season where you make a decision on the type of team you want to be.”

Samsung, Nvidia partner for AI chip substrate supply
Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Nvidia have formed a strategic partnership to strengthen the global AI semiconductor supply chain. The collaboration includes joint investment to expand dedicated manufacturing facilities for ABF package substrates.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics
 has partnered with 
Nvidia
 to bolster the global AI 
semiconductor supply
 chain and expand next-generation 
AI infrastructure
.
Under the agreement, both companies will jointly invest in expanding dedicated manufacturing facilities for ABF package substrates. These substrates are crucial for AI semiconductors.
Samsung Electro-Mechanics will supply Nvidia with ABF package substrates starting in 2026. Dedicated production lines will be established at Samsung Electro-Mechanics' facilities in Sejong and Busan, South Korea.
The supply agreement covers advanced package substrates for Nvidia's compute chipsets. It also includes substrates for 
high-speed networking
 switches essential for data center connectivity.
Supply volume is projected to increase at a double-digit annual rate over a multi-year period. By 2028, production allocated to Nvidia is expected to represent a significant portion of Samsung Electro-Mechanics' total annual package substrate capacity.
The partnership aims to mitigate supply chain bottlenecks in the rapidly expanding global AI infrastructure market. For Samsung Electro-Mechanics, this agreement accelerates its entry into the high-value AI semiconductor package substrate market.
Samsung Electro-Mechanics will leverage its expertise in multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs) and silicon capacitor technologies. These are vital for advanced packaging solutions that manage power and suppress electrical noise in AI chips.
Executive Commentary
Duck-Hyun Chang, President and CEO of Samsung Electro-Mechanics, stated the partnership is an honor. He affirmed the company's commitment to delivering high-performance solutions for Nvidia's AI platforms.
Debora Shoquist, Executive VP of Operations at Nvidia, highlighted the global need for expanded manufacturing capacity. She noted the collaboration broadens Nvidia's network of substrate partners.

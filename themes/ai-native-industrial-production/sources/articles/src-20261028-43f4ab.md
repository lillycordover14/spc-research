Unexpected debut was meaningful to John Williams
Packers offensive lineman missed rookie year with back injury
 Oct 28, 2026 at 09:00 AM
Wes Hodkiewicz
packers.com senior writer
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Copied!
 
Keller Nienau, packers.com
G John Williams
GREEN BAY â Nothing fuels the fire inside a football player more than nearly 18 months of waiting.
That's the spot 
John Williams
 found himself in this season, longing for his chance to play meaningful snaps again after a back injury sidelined the Packers' offensive lineman for his entire rookie campaign.
Countless hours of rehab and preparation were with Williams as he left the sideline on Sunday and jogged directly into the worst-case scenario for a Green Bay O-line already riddled by injury.
Minutes into a pivotal NFC North showdown against Detroit, Green Bay not only lost starting center 
Sean Rhyan
 (ankle) but also saw his replacement, 
Jacob Monk
, leave with a quad injury on the same series and not return.
Only 10 snaps into the game, Williams was suddenly the last man up for Green Bay at center, a position he'd only begun repping with regularity during training camp. Even then, none of those center snaps came with starting quarterback 
Jordan Love
.
Bring it on.
"Obviously, a nightmare scenario and, really, not the first time that's happened to us," said Williams on Tuesday. "Ain't got much time to really dilly-dally, panic, think about what's going on. Just got to go in there and get the job done."
That he did during an admirable 58-snap relief appearance against the Lions. While the 6-4, 322-pound Williams lamented the fact Green Bay came up short in the 20-17 defeat, his NFL offensive debut carried a lot of meaning for the 2025 seventh-round pick.
Shortly after Williams was drafted by Green Bay, he underwent a back procedure that sidelined him for his entire rookie campaign aside from practicing for 21 days while on the physically unable to perform list near the end of the season.
Once cleared, Williams played across the offensive line during the 2026 offseason program and training camp. There were even times during 11-on-11 when he'd finish a rep at center and then slide over to left tackle, the position at which Williams started 24 games at Cincinnati over his final two collegiate seasons.
That's where Williams mostly stayed until Sunday when the Packers had to break the developmental glass amidst an emergency situation inside one of the NFL's most challenging road environments at Ford Field.
"John, obviously he's a really smart guy, so him coming in, I knew that that wasn't gonna be an issue for him," running back 
Josh Jacobs
 said. "Making the right calls, making the right reads. Obviously, that was his first game, like really playing ever in this league, so just getting comfortable, I know that's different for him."
Williams had help, as Rhyan and Monk offered words of encouragement on the sideline and starting left guard 
Laken Tomlinson
 provided bits of salient advice on the field.
Tomlinson, whom Green Bay signed last month amidst a series of injuries up front, has played more than 10,000 offensive snaps over 177 games across 12 NFL seasons.
Still, the 34-year-old guard can't remember seeing anything quite like what unfolded in Detroit when the Packers lost Rhyan and Monk so quickly at the beginning of the game.
"I told (Williams), to come in like that and not blink in a hostile environment, loud stadium, it's awesome," Tomlinson said. "The way he carried himself, he was communicating and had a ton of confidence. You definitely appreciate that from the next man up mentality. He came out there and he did his thing, man."
Williams could quite well find himself back there this Thursday night against the Carolina Panthers, with Monk now on injured reserve and Rhyan listed as a non-participant on the Packers' estimated injury report.
Green Bay won't conduct a true practice due to the short week, but Love said he's getting reps with Williams in walk-throughs. He also sees lasting value in Williams' trial by fire in Detroit.
"We've got a lot of reps built from that game now, so I think the biggest thing is just snaps," Love said. "I thought he did a great job getting it back there and was consistent. For him, it's just dialing in the plan now, understanding the calls and stuff that he can improve off of from that game."
The Packers' offensive line has endured a lot this season, beginning with losing starting right tackle 
Zach Bako-Bewele
 (season-ending torn patellar tendon) and left guard 
Aaron Banks
 (toe) against the New York Jets in Week 2.
With Rhyan and Monk already sidelined, Green Bay also could be without starting right guard 
Travis Glover
 (knee) against the Panthers after he's been estimated as a non-participant this week.
Head Coach Matt LaFleur didn't offer any hints on how the starting lineup will sort out against Carolina, but Williams is ready. After forging his own path back to the field, the 24-year-old offensive lineman has no intention of turning back now.
"I just got to step up to the plate and fill in my for my guys, having their backs," Williams said. "It felt good being back out there (Sunday), especially after everything that happened last year. Just feels good to be a part of the team, feels good to contribute."

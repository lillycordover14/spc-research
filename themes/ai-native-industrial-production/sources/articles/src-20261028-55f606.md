Realio’s Ratings: Twelve yards of charity&nbsp;&nbsp;Sounder at Heart

[page text]
At Snapdragon Stadium last Saturday, the Sounders sleepwalked through the first half, where the “current best XI” resembled strangers at an airport baggage claim. After halftime, a tactical tweak sparked immediate fire, and for ten glorious minutes, the Sounders looked like a ruthless, playoff-hardened machine. Two goals in five minutes put Seattle up 2-0, totally in control against an eight-match-winless San Diego FC. Then Seattle wired its own penalty box with explosives and gave away two spot kicks like Halloween candy. Result: an annoying 2-2 draw.
Solving an opponent in open play, only to bail them out from twelve yards is vintage 2026 Sounders. San Diego now leads MLS with 12 penalties awarded this season, and Seattle happily padded that total. The second arrived deep into eight added minutes when a fragile roster was unable to close the door. Seattle remains winless in three, clinging to seventh place in the West by a fingernail.
Earned in Open Play:
 A booming strike from distance and a textbook right-channel overload built a 2-0 cushion that should have buried a desperate opponent.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Even with the current first-choice lineup, the pieces don’t fit cleanly. The first half was a slog, and the final half-hour became a clinic in turning offensive momentum into defensive box panic.
The Receipt:
 Dropping two road points from a 2-0 lead via two spot kicks is another reminder that this group looks like mismatched parts waiting for someone else to blink.
Goalkeeper
Andrew Thomas — 5 | Community — 6.7
Thomas encapsulated his frustrating 2026 duality in ninety-nine minutes. He guessed right to deny Anders Dreyer’s 60th-minute penalty (although it was put in) and saved a point in the 99th minute with a sprawling swat on Cédric Bakambu. Yet his distribution remains an active hazard, repeatedly turning hard-won stops into instant stress.
Earned in Open Play:
 Clawing the ball off Bakambu’s boot in the ninth added minute kept a blown lead from turning into full defeat.
Gifted from the Spot:
 His distribution after long possession moments demonstrated his struggles with his feet, undoing territorial relief with errant hacks.
The Receipt:
 Elite shot-stopping reflexes only go so far.
Defense
Nouhou — 6 | Community — 6.5
Nouhou had a classic low-floor, high-effort shift on the left, completing 43 passes and contributing to Seattle’s 43-clearance barrage. He stepped up in the 29th minute to clear danger after Dreyer rattled the post, though an over-aggressive step in the 17th minute briefly let Bakambu slip behind.
Earned in Open Play:
 Brought physical 1-v-1 bite on the flank and avoided the box grab-fests that doomed the right side.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Got caught high early when pressing, leaving a wide channel behind him that San Diego nearly exploited.
The Receipt:
 In a match defined by central defensive meltdowns, Nouhou quietly did his job without inventing new ways to hurt the team.
Jackson Ragen — 6 | Community — 6.4
Returning from illness, Ragen was the glue holding a makeshift backline upright. He completed 65 of 69 passes (94 percent), logged 11 clearances, and unlocked San Diego with several laser-guided vertical balls. None was better than his 55th-minute line-breaking dime that sprung Hassani Dotson for Seattle’s second goal.
Earned in Open Play:
 Elite vertical distribution bypassed San Diego’s midfield press to directly initiate attacks.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Communication across the central unit on the 60th-minute PK rebound scramble lacked urgency.
The Receipt:
 Ragen remains indispensable as an aerial eraser and deep playmaker, especially with Yeimar sidelined again.
Alex Roldan — 4 | Community — 5.1 
(off 64’ for Hawkins) 
Pressed back into emergency center-back duty, Alex handled the ball well before undoing Seattle’s momentum. Sliced open centrally in the 57th minute, Roldan got caught on the wrong side of Amahl Pellegrino and hauled him down, drawing a VAR review, a yellow card, and the penalty that revived San Diego.
Earned in Open Play:
 Navigated the first-half storm with eight clearances and steady short-range circulation.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Panicked when Dreyer and Pellegrino tucked inside, culminating in the clumsy 58th-minute takedown.
The Receipt:
 Utility value is great until a natural fullback gets isolated against physical forwards in central box traffic.
Kalani Kossa-Rienzi — 6 | Community — 5.9
Slotting in at right back, Kossa-Rienzi put in a decent two-way shift, completing 85 percent of his passes, winning both tackles, and logging five clearances. He pushed into the box in the 18th minute to cut back a clean look for Albert Rusnák, and made a vital 49th-minute aerial intervention on Pellegrino right before Seattle opened the scoring.
Earned in Open Play:
 His 49th-minute box composure snuffed out a dangerous surge and immediately sparked the 1-0 sequence.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Had his hands full whenever Dreyer drifted inside, committing two fouls as Seattle chased shadows early. Constantly makes dangerous passes across the middle.
The Receipt:
 A steady outing showing defensive maturity without sacrificing his willingness to overlap.
Defensive Midfield
Cristian Roldan — 6 | Community — 6.7
Anchoring the double pivot, Cristian did the unglamorous heavy lifting. He completed 51 of 55 passes (93 percent), added three clearances, and sparked the 50th-minute opener by winning a contested midfield header that fell to Dejan Joveljić. He also slipped Paul Arriola into the box in the 93rd minute for a chance to ice it.
Earned in Open Play:
 Won the aerial duel to assist Joveljić’s icebreaker and dictated tempo with 93 percent passing accuracy.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Cristian missed wide on a 21st-minute transition strike.
The Receipt:
 Kept the spine functional, so Hassani Dotson could hunt higher up the pitch.
Hassani Dotson — 7 | Community — 6.7 
(off 80’ for Kingston)
 
After a rough stretch, Dotson delivered a massive bounce-back shift that had him vying for MOTM. Given license to push forward out of the pivot, he dominated physically by winning all ground duels and 2-for-2 in the air. His highlight came in the 55th minute, latching onto Ragen’s vertical ball, burning Manu Duah on the endline, and sliding a pinpoint cross for Sebastian Gomez to tap home.
Earned in Open Play:
 Bullied San Diego in duels and delivered a gorgeous endline run and assist for 2-0.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Seattle’s defensive shield and box protection evaporated almost immediately after he subbed off in the 80th minute.
The Receipt:
 When Dotson plays with this kind of vertical decisiveness and physical edge, the entire midfield geometry changes for the better.
Attacking Midfield
Jordan Morris — 5 | Community — 5.4 
(off 68’ for Brunell) 
Starting on the flank in what was supposed to be Seattle’s premier attacking lineup, Morris struggled for space during a sluggish first half before pulling up lame in the 68th minute. Outside of a 13th-minute cut-inside shot that was deflected for a corner, he was starved of service and unable to stretch San Diego’s high line.
Earned in Open Play:
 Showed directness early to force a corner and occupied defenders out wide during the early second-half blitz.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Zero shots on target and little connective rhythm before another frustrating muscle tweak ended his night early.
The Receipt:
 A quiet shift capped by an injury concern right when Seattle needs healthy legs for the stretch run.
Albert Rusnák — 5 | Community — 6.0
Rusnák logged ninety minutes of tidy circulation without ever truly seizing the match. He had a golden opportunity to open the scoring in the 18th minute off Kossa-Rienzi’s cutback, but blazed his effort high into the stands. In the 77th minute, he laid off a pass for Joveljić at the edge of the area that culminated in a shot that went wide.
Earned in Open Play:
 Kept the ball moving cleanly during the first-half possession spell and helped orchestrate late keep-away.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Managed only one shot all night and couldn’t manufacture a killer final ball at 2-1.
The Receipt:
 Even with marquee attacking pieces together in the first half, the chemistry through the #10 spot still looks disjointed.
Jesús Ferreira — 5 | Community — 5.3 
(off 46’ for Gomez)
 
Given the start on the right wing, Ferreira was a passenger for 45 minutes in an offense that looked out of sync. He registered zero shots, zero key passes, and failed to threaten San Diego’s backline in behind, prompting a halftime hook that immediately transformed Seattle’s attack.
Earned in Open Play:
 Dropped deep to connect short passes during Seattle’s mid-first-half possession spell.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Provided zero vertical penetration on the right flank, making the attack predictable and easy to compress.
The Receipt:
 Getting pulled at the break, only for your replacement to score nine minutes later is evidence of a flat first-half shift.
Striker
Dejan Joveljić — 7 (MOTM) | Community — 7.5 (MOTM) 
(off 80’ for Arriola)
For 45 minutes, Joveljić made thankless runs while teammates failed to find him with a single line-breaking pass. Five minutes into the second half, he took matters into his own hands. Latching onto Cristian Roldan’s knockdown header, the Serbian striker drove into space and unleashed a thunderbolt from distance for his 13th goal of the season (and fifth since arriving in August). He finished 10-of-11 passing (91 percent), drew three fouls, and proved that a true #9 can manufacture goals out of thin air.
Earned in Open Play:
 Turned a routine midfield header into a low-xG laser from distance in the 50th minute to break the deadlock.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Picked up a 53rd-minute yellow card for a frustration foul and dragged a 77th-minute look wide.
The Receipt:
 In a lineup full of guys who want five touches and a committee meeting before shooting, Joveljić’s ruthless willingness to pull the trigger is a breath of fresh air.
Substitutes
Sebastian Gomez — 7 | Community — 7.3 
(on 46’ for Ferreira)
 
Gomez continues to be the most reliable spark off Seattle’s bench. Entering at halftime to inject pace into a stagnant left side, he needed only nine minutes to score for the second consecutive match, crashing the six-yard box to redirect Dotson’s cross into an empty net. He added a second shot on target in the 70th minute from a tight angle and stretched San Diego’s backline every time he touched the ball.
Earned in Open Play:
 Timed his back-post run to perfection in the 55th minute to double the lead and altered the geometry of Seattle’s attack.
Gifted from the Spot:
 As the game stretched late, the winger group got caught between pressing for a third and tucking in to protect the box.
The Receipt:
 Two goals in two games off the bench.
Stuart Hawkins — 4 | Community — 4.4 
(on 64’ for A. Roldan) 
Brought on to add size and fresh legs at center back after Alex Roldan’s yellow card, Hawkins was spotless with the ball right up until the 95th minute. Four minutes into stoppage-time, Hawkins grabbed a fistful of Marcus Ingvartsen’s jersey inside the box, handing the referee the excuse he needed to point to the spot at the death.
Earned in Open Play:
 Went a spotless 17-for-17 passing and handled his initial defensive positioning cleanly for thirty minutes.
Gifted from the Spot:
 You cannot grab a handful of fabric in your own box in the 95th minute when a referee is hunting for an equalizer, no matter how easily the attacker goes down.
The Receipt:
 Cruel rookie tuition for the young center back, whose late lapse in discipline erased two vital road points.
Snyder Brunell — 6 | Community — 5.7 
(on 68’ for Morris) 
Stepping in for Morris, Brunell brought lively legs in transition, completing 12 of 14 passes (86 percent) and adding two clearances. He nearly helped put the match away twice, floating a 70th-minute cross to Gomez and carrying an 83rd-minute layoff from Arriola into the right channel before firing just wide of the near post.
Earned in Open Play:
 Offered clean ball progression and dangerous late runs into the box that nearly produced a 3-1 clincher.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Needed to put his 83rd-minute look on frame to force Pablo Sisniega into a save rather than letting San Diego off the hook.
The Receipt:
 A bright cameo on the attacking side of the ball, even if the team’s collective late-game defensive grip slipped.
Peter Kingston — 5 | Community — 5.3 
(on 80’ for Dotson) 
Tasked with closing out the final ten minutes plus stoppage time in central midfield, Kingston ran hard and helped cycle the ball wide early in extra time, but the drop-off from Dotson’s physical presence was noticeable as San Diego threw numbers forward.
Earned in Open Play:
 Helped execute the four-minute possession freeze at the start of stoppage time by keeping the ball pinned near the touchline.
Gifted from the Spot:
 Couldn’t replicate Dotson’s dominant duel-winning shield in front of the center backs during San Diego’s late aerial siege.
The Receipt:
 A functional late-game shift that couldn’t prevent the chaos behind him.
Paul Arriola — 5 | Community — 5.6 
(on 80’ for Joveljić) 
Brought on to press from the front and bleed the clock, Arriola made an immediate offensive impact on his first sequence before getting beaten to Cristian Roldan’s 93rd-minute through ball by Sisniega.
Earned in Open Play:
 Connected cleanly in transition to set up Brunell’s 83rd-minute look and kept San Diego’s center backs honest on the counter.
Gifted from the Spot:
 A step slow to the 93rd-minute pass in the box, and the late veteran sub group ultimately failed in their primary directive of seeing out the 2-1 lead.
The Receipt:
 Useful transition flashes, zero points banked from the lead he entered to protect.
Referee
Allen Chapman — 4 | Community — 4.4
This was a masterclass in “reffing to the score” from a PRO veteran. For 94 minutes, Chapman swallowed his whistle on physical contact, letting both teams bang in midfield and calling just 21 combined fouls with zero yellow cards shown to San Diego. Then, five minutes into the absurd eight minutes of stoppage time, he discovered microscopic vision to call a feather-soft jersey tug a penalty, playing hero for the home crowd.
Earned in Open Play:
 Got the 58th-minute VAR review correct, as Roldan clearly fouled.
Gifted from the Spot:
 You cannot establish a loose, physical threshold for 94 minutes where everything goes, tack on a ridiculous amount of extra time, and then award a game-tying penalty on a soft tug at the death.
The Receipt:
 Hawkins gave him an excuse by grabbing the shirt, and Chapman couldn’t wait to act on it.
San Diego FC MOTM
This feels like about the fifth match in a row in which Seattle has faced a relatively new-to-MLS DP attacker. This time, it was Cédric Bakambu giving the Sounders grief. He was one of many on hand to make sure San Diego’s first penalty got bundled into the goal. Then, six minutes into stoppage time, he steped up and converted his side’s second penalty, this one to draw them level and pull back a point.

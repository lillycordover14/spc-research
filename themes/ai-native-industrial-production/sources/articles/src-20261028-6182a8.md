Netherlands reverses course, will sit out Eurovision again over Israel’s participation

Dutch public broadcasting officials said they could not secure enough broadcaster support to send an artist to the 2027 contest.

The Netherlands is withdrawing from the Eurovision Song Contest, reversing an announcement that it would join the event and citing “sensitivity” over Israel’s participation.
The Dutch organization that oversees public broadcasting, the Nederlandse Publieke Omroep, or NPO, said on Tuesday that it failed to find sufficient support from broadcasters to ensure a Dutch artist could go to Eurovision.
“We have known for some time that Israel’s participation elicits various reactions in society and causes discussion and division,” 
said
 Jurre Bosman, NPO’s media director. “This is also the case among broadcasters and staff, and we understand that sensitivity.” 

NPO 
said
 last month that the Netherlands would reenter Eurovision in 2027, when the world’s largest music competition will take place in Burgas, Bulgaria. The Netherlands boycotted the contest in May 2026, alongside Spain, Ireland, Iceland and Slovenia, because of Israel’s inclusion.

NPO’s pledge to rejoin Eurovision overrode a decision by the Dutch network Avrotros, which usually oversees the Dutch entry. Avrotros 
withdrew
 from Eurovision in August, saying the event “can no longer be considered neutral” with Israel taking part amid “the severe humanitarian suffering in Gaza and the ongoing restrictions on press freedom.”

Initially, NPO believed there was “sufficient support” among public broadcasters to allow the Netherlands to return, said Jet de Ranitz, the chair of NPO’s executive board. But the decision sparked backlash. Earlier this month, about 400 public broadcasting employees 
signed a letter
 titled “#NotMyEurovision” to NPO’s management and board, urging the organization to reconsider returning to the contest. 

“We only want to send an artist or artists and staff to the festival if they can count on the full support of one or more broadcasters,” Bosman said.

The contest will still be broadcast in the Netherlands by the network NOS, as it was this year.
Eurovision director Martin Green 
said
 he was “deeply sorry” that the Netherlands would not participate, adding that the organizers understood the decision and “respect the strength of feeling behind it in the Netherlands.” 

Ireland will also remain absent from Eurovision next year. The country’s broadcaster RTÉ 
said
 in September that “Ireland’s participation cannot be justified given the appalling and ongoing loss of lives in Gaza and the humanitarian crisis there.” In Spain, the head of broadcaster RTVE also 
said
 he favored a continued boycott. 

Czechia 
pulled out
 of the event this month, but its public broadcaster cited budget cuts as the reason. 

The controversy over Israel’s inclusion in Eurovision has prompted mass protests. The 2026 contest drew 131 million viewers, 
down 35 million
 from the previous year.

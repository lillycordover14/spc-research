The world premiere show runs now until Nov. 22
An Eden Prairie resident is exploring themes of family, grief and nostalgia through his involvement in the world premiere musical “Twelve Kinds of Ice” at Children's Theatre Company (CTC).
As the music director and conductor for the production, Justin Cook gets a front-row seat to the playful yet tender story that takes audiences along as Ellen (played by Katherine Fried) recalls childhood memories of winter adventures with her father (played by John-Michael Zuerlein) after his death.
“This show has a lot to do with nostalgia and also with processing grief,” Cook said. “And so there's an aspect of those emotions kind of coalescing in nostalgia and grief that feel like something we're really trying to expose in this work. I would say that the childlike delight is really important in this show and feels very true to it. Not that it's an emotion, but the feelings of warmth and of precious memory are all very much in this music and in this story.”
Cook began working with CTC in summer 2023, after connecting with former employees while attending graduate school at New York University. Though he's worked as a composer on several CTC productions in recent years, it wasn't until “Twelve Kinds of Ice” that he had his first experience with music directing for a world premiere.
In late 2025, Cook was approached by the CTC team, including Artistic Director Rick Dildine, who is also the show's director, to participate in an internal four-day workshop for the show, which had not been selected for public performance at the time. Workshop participants performed the musical for CTC staff, who then approved it for the following holiday season.
“We did it for four days with the same actors who are in the show now and we just did it internally for the folks at Children's Theatre, for their staff and for a few other people, just to see if it was a show that felt like it was appropriate for the season and for the audiences there and that type of thing,” Cook said. “I was really grateful that they brought me along with it, including both the actors as well.”
With a new world premiere production at his feet, Cook immediately began assembling a score that best encompassed both the childlike wonder and melancholic undertones of the storyline.
“I love the arrangement. I love taking a show that's never been done on its feet with sets and lights and all those types of things and realizing it for the first time,” he said. “And so, it's been a blast. It's been really fun. It's been an exciting process from start to finish.”
Though he won't be seen acting, audiences will occasionally find Cook on stage in full costume, playing the instruments that bring the musical's soundtrack to life.
Since opening in mid-October, the show's cast and crew have received overwhelmingly positive responses from audience members of all ages, with Cook saying many have said the emotional themes resonated with them.
Outside of touching audiences' hearts, the experience of working on a world premiere musical has taught Cook about his unique strengths and capabilities as a musician and composer.
“My background is as a writer and composer,” he said. “I feel like I had a unique perspective to bring to a new work, especially with a composer who joined us for the latter part of the process in the room, but wasn't there at the beginning. It allowed me to try to get into his head as a composer and try to do justice to that and try to also bring some compositional ideas for these underscorings.”
The new production is one of two world premiere musicals in CTC's upcoming 2026-27 season. Following its run in Minneapolis, Seattle Children's Theatre will show the production from Jan. 28 to Feb. 14, 2027.
“Twelve Kinds of Ice” runs through Nov. 22 on the Cargill Stage at Children's Theatre Company, 2400 Third Ave. S., Minneapolis. The show is recommended for audiences ages 7 and up.

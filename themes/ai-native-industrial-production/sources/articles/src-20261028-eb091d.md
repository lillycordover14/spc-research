The former NYPD detective accused of killing a man in a road rage incident on the George Washington Bridge is again facing a murder charge.
The Manhattan district attorney’s office said Tuesday that a grand jury indicted Victor Romero, 62, of second-degree murder after “an extensive investigation” since he appeared in court last week.
Police initially charged Romero with murder in the shooting death of 46-year-old Koran Irvine before prosecutors downgraded that charge to manslaughter. Romero 
posted $1 million bail
 on Friday, and prosecutors said his bail status did not immediately change with the increased charges. His next court date is scheduled for Nov. 23.
Romero’s defense attorney did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
At a 
court appearance last week
, Assistant DA Matt Sears said surveillance footage shows Romero’s car following Irvine’s after they almost collided near the entrance to the bridge last Tuesday around 7 p.m. Several minutes later, Romero fired a gun from the driver’s seat of his car, hitting Irvine behind his right shoulder, then kept driving into New Jersey, the prosecutor said.
The former detective’s attorney described him as a “hero police officer” with a “sterling record” at last week’s court appearance.
Irvine worked at Con Edison, according to a statement released by the utility company after his death.
The Irvine family's lawyer called it "an important step toward accountability"
"His family deserves a prosecution that reflects the gravity of this tragedy and the same justice afforded to every other family, regardless of the accused’s former position in law enforcement," Nick Liakas said.
Charles Lane contributed reporting.
This story has been updated with comment from the Irvine family's lawyer.

Foundry Robotics Raises $100 Million Series A to Expand Autonomous Manufacturing in California&nbsp;&nbsp;citybiz

[page text]
Foundry Robotics
, an Emeryville, California-based developer of AI-powered manufacturing systems, has raised 
$100 million
 in an oversubscribed Series A funding round to expand its autonomous assembly operations and accelerate the development of domestic manufacturing capacity for defense and industrial equipment.

The financing was led by Marlinspike Partners, with participation from existing investors Khosla Ventures, Red Glass Ventures, Hanabi and Zero Shot Fund. New investors SE Ventures, Wing VC and Axiom Ventures also participated.

The investment will support the expansion of Foundry’s 130,000-square-foot manufacturing facility in Emeryville, where the company is developing flexible robotic assembly systems designed to produce complex electromechanical equipment. Foundry has increased its manufacturing footprint twentyfold as it prepares to scale production for defense and commercial customers.

The company plans to use the new capital to complete development of its facility, accelerate deployment of artificial intelligence models into manufacturing operations and expand its robotics research, engineering and factory operations teams.

Founded in 2026, Foundry Robotics develops and operates autonomous factories that combine AI-powered robotic systems with experienced manufacturing personnel. Its manufacturing-as-a-service model enables customers to outsource complex assembly operations without investing in dedicated production facilities.

The company is targeting industries where product development is advancing faster than manufacturing capacity, including defense technology, aerospace, robotics, telecommunications and data center infrastructure. Initial applications include drones, autonomous vehicle components, underwater vehicles, satellites, wire harnesses and other specialized equipment.

Adarsh Kulkarni, founder and CEO of Foundry Robotics
, said the company’s approach involves redesigning manufacturing operations around robotic automation rather than adding individual automation systems to existing facilities.

“We believe the factories that revive America’s manufacturing sector will need to be rethought from the ground up rather than retrofitting one aspect in an existing factory,” Kulkarni said.

Unlike conventional manufacturing lines designed around specific products, Foundry’s technology uses modular robotic assembly cells that can be adapted to different production requirements. Its AI models are trained on fundamental assembly tasks, allowing robotic systems to perform operations across multiple product categories.

The company’s proprietary software platform, FoundryOS, connects robotic systems, factory operations and cloud-based infrastructure. Production data is incorporated into its AI models to improve performance and support new assembly requirements.

For example, a robotic cell configured to assemble a drone could subsequently be adapted to manufacture a satellite component using related assembly capabilities. Foundry said this flexibility can help customers move from product design to production within weeks, reducing the time and expense associated with establishing traditional manufacturing lines.

The approach is particularly relevant to defense manufacturers developing unmanned systems and specialized equipment, where production requirements can change as designs evolve and procurement needs shift.

Duane Fotheringham, president of HII Unmanned Systems
, identified domestic manufacturing capacity as a significant challenge affecting the deployment of unmanned equipment.

“Production capacity is a national constraint on getting unmanned systems to the fleet,” Fotheringham said.

Foundry is also targeting commercial industries requiring precision assembly of complex hardware, including telecommunications infrastructure, data center equipment and industrial robotics. By serving multiple sectors through a shared manufacturing platform, the company aims to increase factory utilization while accommodating different production programs.

Mislav Tolusic, managing partner at Marlinspike Partners
, said the investment reflects growing demand for domestic manufacturing capacity capable of responding quickly to new product designs.

“The next era of industrial strength will belong to nations that can turn new designs into finished hardware the fastest,” Tolusic said.

Tolusic also highlighted Foundry’s progress in assembling its leadership team, commissioning its first factory and securing customers.

Kulkarni previously worked at Ghost Robotics, where he helped develop legged robotic systems for military applications, before serving as the inaugural head of robotics at Scale AI. He co-founded Foundry with Emmanuel Lamacchia, a former program manager for Tesla’s Model Y vehicle program.

Lamacchia brings experience managing large-scale automotive manufacturing operations, including production associated with approximately 1.5 million vehicles annually. The leadership team also includes Anup Karmakar, a former Tesla software executive, alongside military veterans with experience at Raytheon and Northrop Grumman.

Foundry’s engineering and operations teams work directly within its manufacturing facilities to develop and improve robotic systems under real-world production conditions. The company combines robotics research, AI development and manufacturing expertise to address complex assembly tasks that have historically been difficult to automate.

Foundry has not disclosed its valuation following the Series A round, total funding raised to date or revenue generated by its manufacturing operations. The company also has not provided specific production capacity targets for its Emeryville facility or a timetable for additional factories.

With its new financing, Foundry Robotics is advancing a manufacturing model designed to provide flexible, AI-powered assembly capacity for defense and industrial companies seeking to scale production without building dedicated manufacturing infrastructure.

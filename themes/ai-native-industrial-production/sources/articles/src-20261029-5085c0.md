Introduction

America’s ability to prevent conflict depends on what it can build, how fast it can deliver and how long it can keep going. A new generation of defense companies is moving advanced systems from testing prototypes to full-scale production. Now the industrial base has to keep pace. That means factories that build at scale, suppliers that ship on time, and domestic sources for critical materials and energy.

The pressure is real. Russia’s war in Ukraine has sent NATO allies racing to expand weapons production and rebuild stockpiles. The conflict with Iran has exposed gaps in U.S. munitions inventories. China’s rare earth export restrictions have rattled manufacturers around the world. Strength is measured by the ability to keep producing under pressure.

There is no scaled defense industrial base without a strong industrial base. Founders must design for mass production from day one, weighing cost and manufacturability alongside performance. And bringing production onshore means rebuilding the full chain, from processing raw materials to machining precision components to assembling finished systems.

That is the theme behind this year’s American Dynamism 50: 
Production is Deterrence
. The list is organized around three connected pillars: Systems, Factories and Inputs.

Systems deliver capability. Factories provide the plants, tools and software to build them at volume. Inputs supply the materials and power that keep the line moving.

The payoff reaches well beyond defense. The same capacity that arms the country also creates skilled jobs, revitalizes communities and makes the supply chains behind energy, housing, and everyday goods more resilient.

The companies on this list are rebuilding the industrial foundation that protects America and strengthens her allies.

By Pillar

Inputs:
 the materials and power they run on.

Factories:
 the plants, tools and software that build them.

Systems:
 the drones, ships, missiles, aircraft and spacecraft that deter.

Ambrosia Energy

Headquarters

Austin, TX

Founded

2024

Pillar

Inputs

Ambrosia Energy builds solar and battery plants for businesses that can't wait for grid power, aiming to deliver electricity roughly a year after signing a contract at about $100 per megawatt-hour. Founders Ben Longmier and Sara Spangelo previously led Starlink Mobile after SpaceX acquired their company Swarm. Construction began in West Texas in January 2026. At their June launch, Ambrosia said completed sections were running and the next phase was funded, with 200 megawatts of solar designed to deliver 20 megawatts continuously.

Headquarters

Austin, TX

Founded

2023

Pillar

Inputs

Justin Lopas, who built rockets at SpaceX and led manufacturing at Anduril, co-founded Base Power with Zach Dell. The home battery company opened Factory 1 in Austin's former newspaper printing plant in October 2025, then began producing their Texas-designed Base Core there in 2026. Base Core is available in Texas and Illinois, and the company plans another Austin factory. Base installs and operates the batteries to support the grid while keeping energy in reserve for household outages.

Headquarters

San Bruno, CA

Founded

2024

Pillar

Inputs

Chariot Defense builds the power systems that keep drones, sensors, communications equipment, and electronic warfare tools running when vehicles and generators are switched off. Its Amphora platform combines energy storage, conversion, and software-controlled distribution in hardware designed to work with existing military vehicles, generators, and solar arrays. Founder Adam Warmoth previously led counter-drone programs at Anduril.

Endurance Energy

Headquarters

Seattle, WA

Founded

2025

Pillar

Inputs

Andrew Redd left SpaceX to found Endurance Energy, which develops modular generators that turn heat beneath the ocean floor into electricity for coastal grids and industrial sites. In summer 2026, the company expanded into a 25,000-square-foot Seattle facility for research and manufacturing. In September 2026, Endurance deployed its Adélie prototype at Axial Seamount off Oregon, using a natural hydrothermal vent to reportedly generate more electricity than it consumed. Endurance is developing a two-megawatt commercial generator and a project in Tonga targeting first power in 2027.

General Matter

Headquarters

El Segundo, CA

Founded

2024

Pillar

Inputs

General Matter is developing domestic uranium enrichment capacity to supply America's next generation of nuclear reactors. Their focus includes high-assay low-enriched uranium, or HALEU, a fuel that remains in limited domestic supply even though many advanced reactors need it. Founder Scott Nolan, a former SpaceX engineer and Founders Fund investor, spent a year searching for a company addressing America's nuclear fuel gap before starting General Matter. In January 2026, the Department of Energy awarded General Matter $900 million in milestone-based funding to develop domestic HALEU enrichment capacity over ten years. Site preparation is underway at the former Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant in Kentucky, where the company plans to establish a new enrichment facility.

Founded

Pillar

Inputs

Founder Drew Baglino formed Tesla's energy engineering organization in 2014 and oversaw Megapack's launch and production ramp. Heron plans to invest over $100 million in Factory One in Morgan Hill, California, with mass production targeted for late 2027 and a goal of up to 10,000 units a year.

Mariana is automating mine and refining operations using their MarianaOS software that spans the entire lifecycle of mining development and operations. Funding raised in August 2026 supports the work at Copper One and the development of Lithium One in East Texas.

The company says customer commitments already exceed six gigawatt-hours.

Pillar

Inputs

The reactors are designed with military installations and remote customers in mind, offering an alternative to diesel generators that need regular fuel deliveries. The company sent their first demonstration unit to Idaho National Laboratory in August 2026 and plans an Oak Ridge factory designed for up to 50 units a year. An Army Janus agreement worth up to $750 million covers the development and deployment of 15 units.

Founded

Pillar

Amca is an engineering and manufacturing company focused on re-engineering critical components for defense and aerospace supply chains. Founder and CEO Jai Malik saw an opportunity to uplevel  existing aerospace and defense component suppliers, combining their  manufacturing capabilities and deep technical knowledge with a world-class, centralized engineering team. Amca improves existing operations and develops new or replacement components for customers facing limited supply, long lead times, or missing technical data. Today, Amca  offers both direct component sales and re-engineered parts to government buyers.

Headquarters

San Francisco, CA

Founded

2025

Pillar

Factories

Bob McGrew, OpenAI’s former chief research officer, co-founded Arda to close the gap between designing hardware and building it. Its software turns product designs into assembly plans and work instructions, then updates them as designs change. Anduril already uses Arda within ArsenalOS, its manufacturing software, meaning instructions that previously took hours might be completed in minutes. Arda launched publicly in October 2026 with $230 million in total funding. The company is extending its planning software into robotic assembly, aiming to make production lines adapt as quickly as the products they build.

Atomic Industries

Headquarters

Detroit, MI

Founded

2021

Pillar

Factories

Aaron Slodov co-founded Atomic Industries to automate one of manufacturing’s slowest steps: making the molds that turn product designs into repeatable parts. The Detroit company combines tooling design, physics simulation, and factory production in a single operation. Its software models plastic flow, heat, and structural behavior before a mold is cut, then uses production data to improve subsequent designs. Atomic supplies injection-molded plastic parts for automotive, consumer, aerospace, and defense applications, with mold making as the foundation for scaling domestic production.

Headquarters

New York, NY

Founded

2024

Pillar

Factories

Davide Asnaghi co-founded Diode after working in Apple's Special Projects Group and studying electronics manufacturing in Hong Kong and Shenzhen. The company helps engineers get circuit boards built and delivered with fewer supplier handoffs, starting with schematics written as code. Engineers can export their designs into established engineering tools and check manufacturing requirements before the boards are built. Diode says their tools help customers including Saronic and Physical Intelligence move faster between revisions and produce more designs per engineer.

Founded

2025

Pillar

Factories

Foundry Robotics is building a manufacturing operation around flexible robotic cells that assemble complex hardware. Its focus spans defense and commercial robotics, battery packs, and mechanical subassemblies, with the aim of moving products from prototype to production without building a dedicated factory for each design. Founder Adarsh Kulkarni previously led robotics at Scale AI. Foundry combines manufacturing engineers, robot hardware, and AI models on the assembly floor, collecting data from each task to improve subsequent builds. In October 2026, it announced that Avea Robotics was joining the company, adding technology that lets human operators intervene when robots encounter unfamiliar situations.

Founded

2024

Pillar

Factories

Generalist builds AI models that help robots learn physical tasks from demonstrations, with an initial focus on dexterous manipulation. Its team brings experience from Google DeepMind, Boston Dynamics, and OpenAI. In August 2026, the company introduced GEN-1.5, showing robots learning simple tasks from a single brief demonstration and adapting to unfamiliar objects and tools. The demonstrations remain focused on short tasks, but they point toward a different way to automate factories: teach a robot what to do without writing a new program for every product change. Generalist is expanding access to the model as it works toward broader use.

Hadrian

Headquarters

Torrance, CA

Founded

2021

Pillar

Factories

Hadrian builds highly automated factories to expand domestic production of critical programs for aerospace and defense. From components manufacturing, to in-house production lines, to dedicated factories, Hadrian’s flexible manufacturing platform ensures customers’ capacity needs are met. Hadrian’s manufacturing is powered by its Opus software, which automates key manufacturing and inspection tasks, enabling its technicians to train faster and increase throughput. In January 2026, Hadrian brought Factory 3 in Mesa, Arizona, online less than six months after signing the lease. The company now has four facilities spanning nearly 3 million square feet. Hadrian plans to expand production for U.S. defense programs with factories and production lines for munitions and autonomous systems.

Mind Robotics

Headquarters

Palo Alto, CA

Founded

2025

Pillar

Factories

Founded by Rivian's RJ Scaringe in November 2025, Mind Robotics is developing AI models and hardware for robots to handle jobs fixed equipment still struggles with. Within months, they had raised more than $1 billion, including a May 2026 round to fund more deployments. Rivian's factories give Mind a place to test their technology as the company works toward serving manufacturers outside the auto industry.

Founded

2022

Pillar

Factories

Nominal develops software that gives hardware manufacturers a shared view of system performance from prototype development through production. Engineers can use the platform to combine sensor data with test results and automate workflows for testing systems and validating performance as production scales. Co-founder Cameron McCord experienced those testing challenges while working at Anduril. Nominal serves customers like Anduril, HII and AeroVironment. In April 2026, the Air Force Test Center awarded Nominal a contract with a ceiling of $53 million to modernize the center’s test data infrastructure.

Founded

2024

Pillar

Factories

Jamie Niu and Jacob Buser co-founded Podium Automation to make industrial control panel production faster and more reliable. Podium combines its own design software with CNC machining and automated wire processing, then assembles and tests the finished panels in Brooklyn. The approach reduces manual work and catches design problems before fabrication, helping customers bring new production equipment online without waiting on a traditional panel shop. The company supplies UL 508A-listed panels for manufacturing, energy, aerospace, and warehouse automation.

Founded

2025

Pillar

Factories

Prometheus is developing AI models for engineering and manufacturing. Co-founded by Jeff Bezos and Vik Bajaj, the company trains these models on specialized data to work with the geometry and physics of complex hardware, from jet engines to semiconductors. Its tools are intended to help engineers explore designs, simulate performance, and refine products before committing to physical prototypes and tooling. Prometheus aims to shorten this development cycle, helping manufacturers resolve engineering problems earlier and move new products into production faster.

Jim Belosic founded SendCutSend to get custom parts for his garage projects without waiting weeks or paying hundreds of dollars apiece. Drawing on his software background, he built a service where customers upload a design for an instant quote, dramatically improving the legacy email-based quoting process. SendCutSend grew largely on their own cash after an early friends-and-family round, then took their first institutional funding in May 2026. That month, they committed more than $250 million to facilities as part of a five-year, $1 billion investment in U.S. manufacturing, including not only the laser cutting business with which SendCutSend started, but also machining, bending and forming, welding, and more.

Pillar

Senra Systems makes the wire harnesses that carry power and signals through aerospace and defense systems. Their Amp software brings engineering and production work into one system that also manages the supply chain, helping teams assemble harnesses efficiently and consistently. Co-founder Jordan Black began his manufacturing career repairing roller coasters at the Santa Monica Pier before joining SpaceX, where he developed wire-harness manufacturing processes. In July 2026, Senra opened their second factory in Cypress, California, adding about 80,000 square feet and expanding their manufacturing footprint fivefold. Will Bruey worked on SpaceX's Dragon spacecraft before co-founding Varda with Delian Asparouhov. Varda develops orbital factories to process pharmaceuticals in microgravity and return them to Earth. Its reentry capsules also carry government experiments that collect data during hypersonic flight. The company builds its spacecraft and heat shields in-house in El Segundo. In March 2026, Varda leased a 205,000-square-foot industrial and office campus to expand spacecraft and capsule production, alongside a separate biopharma development facility. On October 20, 2026, it launched W-7 to study cancer-drug crystallization in orbit, its third launch that month.

David Hansen learned how the motor supply chain worked while sourcing components from China for Weel's self-balancing e-bikes. He co-founded Westmag in 2024, which manufactures and tests drone motors and robot actuators at Factory 01 in South San Francisco while working to control upstream parts and materials. At their June 2026 public launch, Westmag said production was growing to meet commitments for hundreds of thousands of units, with a target of more than 30 million a year before 2030.

Founded

Pillar

Systems

Anduril builds defense hardware around software, connecting their autonomous systems through the Lattice platform to support force protection and unmanned combat missions. In June 2026, the U.S. Air Force awarded Anduril development and production contracts for the Fury aircraft under the Collaborative Combat Aircraft program. The following month, the first Ohio-built Fury rolled off the production line at Arsenal-1, the company's 5 million sq. ft. manufacturing campus near Columbus. Anduril is expanding manufacturing as government demand for their systems grows. In March, the Army awarded Anduril an enterprise contract that simplifies purchases of the company’s software and hardware, with a ceiling of $20 billion over 10 years.

Apex

Headquarters

Los Angeles, CA

Founded

2022

Pillar

Systems

At Palantir, Ian Cinnamon kept hearing satellite customers complain about their spacecraft buses. He co-founded Apex to offer standard buses that handle power and propulsion for satellites of different sizes, allowing customers to concentrate on their payloads. Standardization also means Apex can mass-produce spacecraft, driving down cost as they drive up quality and earn flight heritage for their designs. At Factory One in Los Angeles, the company is targeting a peak capacity above 200 satellites a year for defense and commercial constellations.

San Francisco, CA

Founded

2015

Pillar

Systems

Astranis’s MicroGEO satellites stay fixed above a single spot on Earth by flying in geostationary orbit, giving telecom operators and governments a communications option solely dedicated to their needs. Co-founder and CEO John Gedmark founded the Commercial Spaceflight Federation in 2005, and teamed up with Ryan McLinko, who led both the electrical and mechanical engineering teams at Planet Labs, to start Astranis. 2024

Commercial components and local supply chains support the company's U.S. manufacturing, and they use the same approach in Germany and Israel. By Covenant's September 2026 public launch, Anthem had completed more than 200 flight tests, including a live-fire demonstration near Tan-Tan, Morocco, on August 6. In September, Covenant opened a 105,000-square-foot factory in Dallas to work alongside their German and Israeli facilities. The company plans to use these plants to supply long-range strike weapons in the quantities needed for sustained deterrence. Serial production in Dallas is scheduled to begin in early 2027, with a target of 5,000 missiles a year once production ramps up.

The company develops airborne platforms and payloads that integrate with existing military sensors and command systems. In 2026, CX2 moved into a 30,000-square-foot headquarters and manufacturing facility in El Segundo, expanding space for engineering, testing, and production.

By July 2026, the fleet had transported over 777,000 pounds and carried out 88 casualty-evacuation operations across more than 1,100 missions. In August, the U.S. Army selected Forterra for the Infantry Last Tactical Mile initiative, bringing Lancer's AutoDrive autonomy platform and Vektor communications system to the program. The effort focuses on rapidly deployable resupply and casualty evacuation.

The factories can retool as designs and requirements evolve. Mach expects to begin production on at least three programs in 2026. They’re expanding Forge and their Mach Propulsion division while developing second-generation systems so they can make more weapons and critical components for U.S. and allied forces.

Archer AI and Bandit add autonomous strike and interception, with both planned for combat deployment before the end of 2026. Led by MIT and Harvard-trained CEO and co-founder Bridgit Mendler, Northwood's antennas and software already operate across multiple continents. The company opened a new headquarters and factory in Torrance in August 2026 and is preparing to move production there in October, with three dedicated lines intended to make hundreds of antennas a year.

The contract will fund additional procurements of Perennial’s Merops interceptors, Bumblebee ISR quadcopters, and Hornet mid-range strike drones.

Saronic Technologies

Specter

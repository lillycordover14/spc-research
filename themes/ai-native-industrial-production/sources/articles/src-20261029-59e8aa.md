2027 Suzuki SV-7S and GSX-10R To Debut At EICMA

Dennis Chung

(IC: employee)

 
Published:

 Share
                    
 

New names for iconic models

 

 

Motorcycle.com
 can confirm that Suzuki is preparing two new models for EICMA to be called the SV-7S and the GSX-10R. The SV-7S is expected to be the replacement for the venerable SV650, while the GSX-10R will be a liter-class sportbike, though it’s not clear if it will be directly replacing the GSX-R1000 superbike, or the long discontinued GSX-S1000F.

The two new names appeared on a number of official Suzuki websites, the same way we first 
first discovered the existence of the SV-7GX
. The pages are publicly accessible, but only contain the new model names and various sub-headings, but devoid of specific details.

While it remains part of Suzuki Motor USA’s line-up, the SV650 was dropped from the European market for 2026 because it did not meet Euro5+ standards. As we 
previously reported
, an updated version of the SV has been certified by the California Air Resources Board for 2027 with the Euro5+-compliant engine from the SV-7GX. Rebranded as the SV-7S, we expect it will share the crossover model’s electronics package, including ride modes and traction control, which the SV650 did not offer.
The GSX-10R is a bit more of a mystery. The obvious conclusion is that it is the heir to the GSX-R1000, but rebranding the superbike just after the 
40th anniversary of the GSX-R family
 might prove to be an unpopular decision. The GSX-R1000 and GSX-R1000R were also just updated to meet Euro5+ for 2026, and Suzuki already announced that version will be 
coming to the U.S. as a 2027 model
.

 

 

Another possibility is the GSX-10R could be replacing the 
GSX-S1000F
, which was discontinued following the 2021 model year. The GSX-S1000F was more of a street-focused model than the GSX-R1000, sharing the same platform as the naked GSX-S1000, the  throwback Katana model, the GSX-S1000GX crossover, and GSX-S1000GT sport-tourer.
The GSX-10R would then follow the template of the 
GSX-8R
 as a street-focused sportbike with enough performance chops to represent Suzuki in the Sportbike World Championship. We can confirm that the official Suzuki GSX-10R pages include a section specifically dedicated for track use. Whether Suzuki sees enough room in its line-up to support both a GSX-10R and a GSX-R1000 is the big question.

 

 

Either way, the two new names are already part of a larger company-wide change in how Suzuki names new motorcycles. The change first started with the GSX-8S and the GSX-8R, followed by the DR-Z4S and DR-Z4SM, and most recently with the SV-7GX. Moving forward, it looks like all new models will be following the same formula.
We’ll have official details after Suzuki’s EICMA presentation on November 3. Check back here on

 for the full coverage of the SV-7S, GSX-10R, and other new model announcements from other manufacturers.

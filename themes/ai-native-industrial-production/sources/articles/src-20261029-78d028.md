The Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County will mark the start of a major renovation at the La Brea Tar Pits Thursday with a groundbreaking ceremony for a project that will reshape the museum and its 13- acre campus.

The ceremony is scheduled 10 a.m. Thursday at the tar pits in the Miracle Mile neighborhood.

Lynda Oschin, Los Angeles County Supervisors Holly Mitchell, Lindsey Horvath and Kathryn Barger, Rep. Laura Friedman, D-Glendale, Los Angeles City Councilwoman Katy Yaroslavsky and Natural History Museums of Los Angeles County President and Director Lori Bettison-Varga are among those expected to attend.

The event will be hosted by Steve Chiotakis and include a special appearance by Cali the saber-toothed cat, according to the museum.

The project includes renovations to the George C. Page Museum and the creation of the Samuel Oschin Global Center for Ice Age Research, named in honor of the late Samuel Oschin, according to museum officials. The center will bring research, fossil excavation, scientific analysis and collections work into the visitor experience.

Plans call for visible research laboratories, new exhibition spaces and collections storage, an immersive theater and a rooftop terrace. A new pedestrian loop will connect excavation sites, research and exhibition areas, gardens and open space across the campus.

The Page Museum closed to the public in July for its first major renovation since opening in 1977. The surrounding park remains open, and researchers continue excavating fossils and conducting scientific work. Educational programs, tours and visits by the La Brea Tar Pits Mobile Museum are also continuing, according to the museum.

The reimagined campus is expected to reopen in 2028, ahead of the Los Angeles Olympic and Paralympic Games.

La Brea Tar Pits is an active fossil site where asphalt seeps have preserved remains of Ice Age plants and animals. The site has yielded millions of fossils, including saber-toothed cats, dire wolves and mammoths, the museum said.

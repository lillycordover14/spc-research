In Palm Beach County, 
Christina Romelus
 and
 Daniel Zapata 
are vying to replace District 4 School Board Member 
Erica Whitfield
, who 
won
 a County Commission seat in August after three terms on the School Board.

Romelus is a former Boynton Beach Commissioner who now runs the government affairs and leadership training firm 
PolitiCALM
. She pitched her candidacy as a continuation of her years of public service, which include missionary work in multiple countries, a decade as a nurse and six years as a City Commissioner.

Zapata is an educator at Atlantic Christian Academy, a private school in West Palm Beach, where he 
teaches
 Spanish, government and economics. He has leaned into his family’s escape from Cuba, noting that his grandmother “went from waiting in line for a pair of shoes in Castro’s Cuba to casting her ballot for her grandson.”

School Board races are officially nonpartisan in Florida, though Zapata is a registered Republican and 
ran
 for House District 89 as a Republican in 2022 and 2024, narrowly losing both times. Romelus is a registered Democrat.

Four candidates ran in the 
August Primary
, but none cleared the 50% threshold needed to avoid a runoff. Romelus spent more than any of her opponents and took first with 41% of the vote, while Zapata finished second with 27%. 
Tiffany Bryant
, a registered Republican, took 19% and registered Democrat 
Anthony Hamlet
 won 14%.

Since Romelus and Zapata have shied away from overtly partisan labels, it is difficult to predict how voters who backed the two eliminated candidates will vote in the General Election.

Romelus’ 
platform
 is focused on boosting the District’s 54% reading proficiency rate, partnering with local businesses to increase opportunities for students after graduation and promoting an “academics, not agendas” approach to education.

She is supported by the Palm Beach County Classroom Teachers Association, the Palm Beach Treasure Coast AFL-CIO and the county’s firefighter union, the IAFF Local 2928. She has also notched endorsements from Democratic U.S. Rep. 
Lois Frankel
 and two dozen other state and local officials.

Zapata’s 
campaign website
 touts his experience as a school administrator and teacher. He is 
endorsed
 by the Police Benevolent Association of Palm Beach County, the nonpartisan group 
The Hispanic Vote
, several business organizations, former Republican state Rep. 
Rick Roth
 and nearly 20 other local elected officials.

Far-right former Republican gubernatorial candidate 
James Fishback
 also 
endorsed
 Zapata less than two weeks before Election Day, but Zapata did not add the endorsement to his campaign site.

With a contentious 
County Commission contest
 headlining Palm Beach County’s local races, the School Board race has been relatively low-key, with neither candidate embroiled in any significant scandals. A campaign marketing firm Romelus owned with her husband was 
linked
 to a 2016 voter fraud probe involving forged mail-ballot requests, but prosecutors closed the investigation without filing charges.

Romelus had a wide financial advantage going into the Primary, but Zapata has closed the 
fundraising
 gap since August.

District 4 stretches mostly east of Military Trail from West Palm Beach south to Delray Beach, including portions of Boynton Beach and Lake Worth Beach.

Election Day is Nov. 3.

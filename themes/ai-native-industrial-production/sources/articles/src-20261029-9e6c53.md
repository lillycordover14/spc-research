The official political arm of House Democrats just launched a new ad attacking the Republican now defending 
Cory Mills’ 
House seat.

The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (
DCCC
) launched a 
30-second ad
 undermining Republican 
Ryan Elijah’s 
working-class story.

“Millionaire Ryan Elijah thinks the economy is going great,” a narrator says.

Quiver Quantitative
 pegs the former Fox 35 news anchor’s net worth at as much as $3.11 million, based on his current stock holdings.

The ad also slams Elijah for checking a box on qualifying paperwork with the state saying he intends to trade stocks while in Congress. That’s notably a 
requirement
 supported this year by Republican Gov. 
Ron DeSantis
, and which has been used to hammer Elijah.

“Ryan Elijah won’t change Washington. He’ll fit right in,” the ad says, superimposing Elijah’s face alongside both controversial conservative Republicans like U.S. Sens. 
Ted Cruz 
of Texas and 
Mitch McConnell 
of Kentucky, and with disgraced Democratic former Sen. 
Bob Mendendez
 of New Jersey and former Rep. 
Eric Swalwell 
of California.

The video also runs a series of clips of statements Elijah made while challenging Mills in a Republican Primary.

“We’re in a great spot now,” he said at a debate. Tariffs are “controversial with some businesses, yes, but they are working.” And gas prices? “It could be worse.”

The promotion offers Democrat 
Bale Dalton 
as an alternative for voters in Florida’s 7th Congressional District, with video of the veteran and former NASA Chief of Staff speaking direct to camera, noting his service in Iraq and Afghanistan.

But more notable than any content is the backing. The ad is a coordinated ad buy with the campaign, with the DCCC putting six figures behind the buy in the Central Florida media market.

That follows clear 
tension
 this week about where dollars should be directed in the state of Florida by national groups. But the ad buy from the DCCC came a day after House Majority PAC, a super PAC associated with House Democratic Leader 
Hakeem Jeffries
, made $1.76 million in 
ad reservations
 in CD 7.

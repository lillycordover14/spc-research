[page text]
U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Japanese Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi in the Philippines. Photo: NHK 
 

Manila, Oct. 29 (SANA) 
The United States and Japan have agreed to strengthen their military alliance’s deterrence and response capabilities amid concerns over China’s military buildup and North Korea’s nuclear and missile programs, Japanese public broadcaster NHK reported.

Japanese Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth reached the agreement during talks Wednesday on the sidelines of an annual ASEAN defense ministers’ meeting in the Philippines.

Koizumi told Hegseth that Japan was reviewing its three key national security documents, with the process expected to be completed by the end of the year.

He said Tokyo intended to strengthen its deterrence capabilities and improve its ability to respond to potential aggression.

The two officials also agreed to establish a facility in Japan to assemble and inspect advanced medium-range air-to-air missiles as part of a joint production initiative.

They pledged closer cooperation on cybersecurity following a series of cyberattacks in Japan.

The ASEAN defense ministers’ meeting is expected to address regional security challenges and efforts to strengthen military cooperation among participating countries.

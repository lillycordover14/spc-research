Tar Heel Blog staff predict UNC men’s basketball season
Nervous anticipation as the Tar Heel men begin their 117th hoops season
by
 
 
David Starnes
Oct 29, 2026, 5:00 PM UTC
“Frightened of change? But what can exist without it? What’s closer to nature’s heart?”
Marcus Aurelius, 
Meditations
, Book 7.18
The watchword for UNC basketball this coming season will definitely be “change.” Fans demanded it. Boosters demanded it. And so, change has arrived. The suddenness of the change, and the timing of it, meant a coaching transition on the cusp of roster construction season. A coach who never seriously considered coaching college basketball on one Tuesday found himself coaching one of the college game’s biggest brands on the Tuesday next. That same day, portal season opened.
Six months and three exhibition games later, UNC sports a roster full of intriguing pieces and potential but also alarming questions at both the point and the post. The team’s best “back to the basket” player, Jarin Stevenson, entered college ball three seasons ago seeing himself as a versatile three. Experienced big Cameron Fens currently can’t play because a court in Tennessee ruled one way, while Seth Trimble can because a court 250 miles to the north ruled the opposite. In three exhibition games, the two most likely offensive facilitators, Neo Avdalas and Terrence Brown, have both flashed and struggled. Rebounding has been an issue. Turnovers have been rampant.
Reminder: Roy Williams went 19-11 in his first season at UNC. That was a seasoned, successful college head coach in a stable sport with five future NBA players on his roster. Change happens both suddenly and constantly. Results take time.
The potential of this roster remains clear. The path to reaching that potential appears long. How soon might things click into place? That’s the key question driving the varying season predictions for most. Here are staff predictions, with commentary following.

Team MVP
Sayon earning this would probably bode well for UNC, a future in which the raw freshman adapts quickly to the college game and dominates the post by the back end of the season. Neo earning it would validate the vision Malone has articulated for this team, with the tall, lengthy sophomore being a match-up nightmare for defenses while he runs the offense. Jarin earning it would be a season for the ages for the Chapel Hill native. Terrence Brown not being on this list suggests optimism that the talented point guard successfully adapts to stirring the drink rather than dominating the ball.

Best Win of the Season

Worst Loss of the Season
Here’s where the big question, “When does this all start to click?” starts to rear its head. West Virginia happens on November 27, @Louisville on January 5, and @Virginia Tech on February 27. Losing big at Louisville — a program that spent wildly on its basketball roster on the one hand and complained nonstop about its athletic deficits on the other — would suggest things start to click somewhere in the midst of the conference schedule. Losing to Virginia Tech, a team predicted to finish in the middle of the ACC, that late in the season would indicate the possibility things never really click.
Regular Season Record, ACC Finish, NCAA Tournament Seed and Outcome
Regular season predictions range from 21-11 to 27-5. ACC predictions land anywhere from second to fifth place. Postseason predictions vary as well, with a predicted NCAA seed somewhere from 3rd to 6th, with a tournament run ending in the second round at worst and the Final Four at best. None of those predictions seem a to be referendum on the hire of Michael Malone as the head coach. All of them recognize that the team’s ceiling will be defined by individual players elevating their games, while the floor will be defined by how well and how quickly those individual talents mesh into a sum exceeding the total of its parts — a meshing dependent, in turn, on who develops and how fast. The variables are many and all overlapping.
Again: Roy Williams finished 19-11 in his first season at UNC before unleashing a 33-win team the following season.

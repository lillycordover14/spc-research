Contenders vie for AMCA prototype contract; technical evaluation begins next
 By Dalip Singh
 Updated - October 30, 2026  at 09:32 PM.

The three shortlisted private sector-led contenders vying for the ₹15,000-crore project to develop prototypes of the indigenous fifth-generation fighter jet, the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA), have submitted their individual bids to a high-level review committee constituted by the Ministry of Defence.
Industry sources said the three contenders — L&T-led consortium of BEL and Dynamatic, Tata Advanced Systems Ltd (TASL), and Bharat Forge-led consortium of BEML Ltd, and Data Patterns — submitted their individual bids by Friday, the deadline for submission.
The bids will be opened on Saturday to select a development-cum-production partner (DCPP) for five AMCA prototypes and a structural test specimen designed and developed by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA), a DRDO laboratory.
“They will verify the submission of bids and all bid documents on Saturday. Technical evaluation should take till December-end”, industry sources said.
Additionally, the winning bidder must have a manufacturing ecosystem near Puttaparthi in Andhra Pradesh, where the AMCA core integration and flight-testing complex will be located.
The deadline was earlier extended from August 27 to October 30 after bidders sought more time to complete formalities for the complex fifth-generation fighter prototyping programme, which they are undertaking for the first time.
However, TASL is co-manufacturing the C295 medium tactical transport aircraft in India with foreign original equipment manufacturer (FOEM) Airbus, for the IAF. The other contenders, meanwhile, have no experience in assembling a complete aircraft, having only been part of the global aerospace supply chain.
Technical evaluation, followed by price negotiations, will now begin for the AMCA programme, sources told businessline.
For the first time, indigenous private sector companies will manufacture fighter aircraft, marking a shift from the traditional role of Maharatna defence PSU Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd (HAL), which has been the country’s mainstay of defence aerospace manufacturing.
In February, the MoD shortlisted three contenders to select the prototype production partner for the fifth-generation fighter aircraft, following a technical evaluation of bids submitted by seven initial competitors.
The Bengaluru-based ADA, which conceived the project, will select the production partner and lead the initial manufacturing of five twin-engine AMCA prototypes. The government will fully fund the development and production of the stealth aircraft. Depending on the outcome, separate tenders will be issued to address the Indian Air Force’s capacity requirements.
The AMCA is likely to be powered by twin F414 engines if the proposed deal between HAL and US-based GE for their joint production in India materialises. The aircraft’s stealth features include internal weapons bays, supercruise capability, advanced AI-driven avionics and next-generation sensor fusion.
Under the original timeline, the first prototype is expected to roll out within 24 months of the contract signing, with 2029 as the target year. All five prototypes are expected to be ready by 2031.

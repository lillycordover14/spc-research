Bill Belichick is the most successful coach in NFL history. He won 302 NFL games and six Super Bowls.

Now it is time to see if he can stop 
Miami star Malachi Toney.

“He gets open,” Belichick said Monday. “He finds ways to get open even when he’s double-covered. That doesn’t really stop him. You would think it would, but not really. He’s made several big plays off of double coverage because he knows how to beat double coverage. At his age, that’s pretty impressive. I don’t really see any weak points in his game.”

Stopping Toney and the Hurricanes’ high-flying offense is a huge task for Belichick’s North Carolina Tar Heels (4-3, 2-2 ACC), when they host No. 1 Miami (7-0, 5-0 ACC) 
at Kenan Stadium on Saturday at 7:30 p.m.

Toney has been one of the nation’s most productive players in recent weeks, leading Miami’s offense and scoring five touchdowns against Florida State and Pittsburgh. Toney and 
quarterback Darian Mensah
 could be in for another big game this weekend.

The Tar Heels have had trouble stopping opposing offenses. Although UNC ranks 41st in the nation in points allowed per game (20.9), it is 85th in yards allowed per game (372.7). And the offenses the Tar Heels have faced do not compare to the Hurricanes’ offense, which is first in the nation in points (49) and yards (551.9) per game.

But Miami coach Mario Cristobal is not taking the Tar Heels’ defense lightly, even though UM has the apparent advantage.

“You can just watch film on North Carolina. At the line of scrimmage, they’re beating up everybody they play,” Cristobal said. “It’s easy to see. Anyone (who) doesn’t recognize that chooses to be blind. We play in this conference, and conference play in general is playoff football. And I think instead of thinking about what’s at stake and being in defensive mode and protection mode, it’s more of (we have) got to stay in a hunting mode. You’ve got to attack because the teams that we play have really, really good players that can beat you and can beat anybody. And watching them on tape and the things they’ve done and some of the players and their capabilities, it’s going to take every ounce of preparation and great effort to make sure we get (a win).”
 

On defense, the Hurricanes have struggled recently at defending the deep pass, allowing big plays against Pittsburgh and nearly allowing a game-tying Hail Mary against Florida State. Fortunately for UM, UNC is 76th in passing offense nationally and 99th in the nation with only 19 passes that went 20 yards or further. Tar Heels starting quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. has completed just 6 of 18 pass attempts of 20 or more yards downfield and has a 57.3 passing grade from Pro Football Focus on those deep balls.

Although the Hurricanes are a 14-point favorite, history shows playing North Carolina on the road is not an easy task. UM is 3-8 all-time in Chapel Hill and has not beaten the Tar Heels on the road since 2017.

“North Carolina has a lot of NFL-talent players on their team,” safety Zechariah Poyser said. “They play for Bill Belichick, a great coach, a legendary coach. They have a great team and a great scheme. We’re playing on a Halloween night in Chapel Hill. It’s going to be a great environment. We’re excited for the challenge. This is a team you cannot take lightly. You know, you can’t look at the record. It’s a good team.”

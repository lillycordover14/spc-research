    Learning to love Halloween again

Caidence Raines
, 
Contributing Writer
                 
             •  
            
October 30, 2026
 

It’s that time of year again: Nearly all of the leaves across campus have shed their greenish hues in favor of something warmer, McKinley’s Food Court has started serving pumpkin pie and walks between morning classes have grown miserably cold. The Halloween season is upon us, and I couldn’t be happier.

That feeling of joy surprises me. I’m sure the length of the season here in Meadville plays some part in it — each year in Murrysville, my hometown, the leaves would go from green to brown, littering lawns in a blitzkrieg that lasted maybe two weeks. I grew up around two hours south of Allegheny, so I figured it wouldn’t be much different. My dread of the impending winter would keep me in bed, pre-hibernating and procrastinating up until the last week of the month, where I’d scramble to find the pieces for a Halloween costume. I’ve always enjoyed the holiday itself, but the preceding month has only served to make me anxious.

This year has been entirely different in that regard. I’ve been on the edge of my seat, trying and failing to keep my costumes to myself until the big day. My previous anxiety certainly came, at least in part, from a fear of how I’d be perceived.

When I was five years old, gathering candy in a cheap, polyester costume it didn’t bother me that there were hundreds of other Elsas roaming the streets. More often than not, children choose a costume based on a popular obsession over one based on aesthetics or originality. But as they grow older, things become more competitive. Polyester Elsa simply wouldn’t do when I entered adolescence — not when the more fortunate kids surrounding me were buzzing about in the handmade or expensive costumes their parents were willing to provide. As my standards rose, my enthusiasm for the Halloween season as a whole dwindled.

So, what changed? I think it has a lot to do with the tight-knit community that people are forced into when they enter college. At a school as small and far from the city as Allegheny, it is hard to go about life without making a few friends. Halloween with friends is always better than Halloween alone, especially when you factor in the exciting prospects of wearing matching costumes and doing seasonal activities as a group. Additionally, this can also work to reduce the holiday’s potential expenses. Chances are at least one of the friends you’ve made on campus is in the same boat as yourself financially, and they may even have a car or the ability to sew. Thrift and bargain shops within Meadville are great places to find cheap pieces for a costume, even if those pieces are just scraps of fabric waiting to be upcycled into something new. These trips and ones like them can help to reignite a person’s love for the season.

However, it’d be short-sighted of me to assume that everyone has had the same experience as myself regarding friendship. My experience is not universal — some people are more introverted than others, and some friends will be less enthusiastic to celebrate the season than mine have been. With that being said, I also think it’s entirely possible for anybody who wants to find joy in the season to do so. Several Halloween events have been hosted on campus this year, from Hallo’Gheny on Oct. 17 to Pumpkins and Ponies on Oct. 23. These events are not only places to connect with fellow students, but also the Meadville community as a whole. The town carries a strong enthusiasm for Halloween, hosting Pennsylvania’s largest nighttime parade each year in celebration.

On campus, the fraternities, athletic houses and more chip in to the celebration by hosting parties, though I hesitate to speak about those as eagerly as their alternatives throughout the town. When a person enters college, movies, television and social media may often have them believing that house parties are the only way to have fun on Halloween. This can definitely be discouraging. It’s not that I don’t enjoy parties, but I understand they’re not for everybody, and especially not for those who are more reserved. When I began college, I didn’t think I’d enjoy them myself, and I have friends even now who choose to sit out more often than not. They can be a good time and are worth attending if you’re feeling adventurous, but the implication within popular culture that they are the only way to enjoy Halloween in college is anything but true. Regardless of whether or not you choose to attend parties, there are countless ways to get involved in the festivities.

At Allegheny, finding joy in Halloween is anything but impossible. Holidays always have the potential to be stressful (especially for those with a tendency to get in their own heads preparing) but they don’t need to be. Holidays have the ability to unite people, whether it be through the nourishment of old friendships or the building of new ones. They’re a time to put yourself out there, escaping your comfort zone behind the guise of a flashy costume. Stress is natural, but so is joy. For anyone looking to enjoy the season, it’s worth checking out the events the college and local communities have to offer; you might even find some friends along the way.

Intel's Substrate Shortage Moves the CPU Supply Chain Bottleneck Into the Package
 

 

Semiconductors, Supply Chain, & Emerging Tech
 

 

Analyst(s): Brendan Burke

Publication Date: October 30, 2026

Intel told investors that a shortage of advanced organic substrates is limiting server CPU output and that the industry will stay supply-constrained into 2028. Futurum’s CPU Supply Chain market map traces more than 70 companies across design, manufacturing, and end markets to show where agentic CPU demand meets a supply chain sized for the chatbot era, and which suppliers gain from the squeeze.

The News:
 Intel reported third quarter 2026 revenue of $16.5 billion on October 29, with Data Center and AI revenue of $7.3 billion, up 17% sequentially and nearly 80% year over year. Management named substrates as a core constraint alongside Intel 3 yields and a Fab 28 shutdown in Israel. “Even as our Intel 3 wafer outputs ramps quickly, an industry-wide shortage of advanced organic substrates for our EMIB packaging is also impacting our ability to scale output,” said Lip-Bu Tan, CEO of Intel. CFO Dave Zinsner said Intel has secured 40% more substrate capacity, most of it arriving in the second half of 2027. “We expect the industry to remain supply constrained throughout 2027 and into 2028,” said Zinsner, who projected industry server CPU units will grow more than 30% in both 2026 and 2027.

Analyst Take:
 The binding constraint on the CPU recovery now sits below the wafer. Intel’s wafer output beat plan this quarter, and laminate from Japan and Taiwan still kept server shipments short. Futurum’s CPU Supply Chain market map traces the problem from design through manufacturing to the chips themselves. RISC-V and Multi-Die Design Reshape the Design Layer

The design column of our market map is where the instruction set duopoly opens up. AMD and Intel steward x86 and now jointly coordinate the ISA’s extensions, while Arm licenses the dominant alternative under royalty pressure. RISC-V removes the license fee and SiFive leads it into the agentic tier on Futurum’s map, so a hyperscaler or AI lab can license SiFive cores and build an orchestration CPU sized to its own agents. SiFive raised $400 million at a $3.65 billion valuation in April with NVIDIA among its investors, and in January it became the first RISC-V vendor to adopt NVLink Fusion. Most of its more than 10 billion shipped cores still serve embedded and accelerator control roles, and no SiFive-based server CPU ships in volume yet. Chiplets also make CPU design a multi-die physics problem, which pulls Cadence, Siemens EDA, and Synopsys thermal and power analysis earlier into every program.

Wafer Demand Outgrows CPU Units at the Foundry and Equipment Layers

Flagship data center CPUs have grown from 300 to 400 sq mm of silicon to about 1,600 sq mm as multi-die packages. Futurum estimates CPU wafer consumption has grown roughly 10% per year faster than units over 10 years. Gate-all-around transistors and backside power on Intel 18A-P and TSMC N2 add process steps per wafer, which raises equipment intensity for ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, and Tokyo Electron. Intel said firm purchase orders to equipment suppliers rose nearly 40% sequentially. TSMC builds most merchant and hyperscaler CPU silicon, with Samsung and Rapidus as further leading-edge options. Tan’s offer to build CPUs of any instruction set makes Intel Foundry a second source for rivals constrained at TSMC.

Substrates Become the Tightest Layer in the Chain

More tiles need larger ABF substrates with more build-up layers, and Intel’s EMIB bridges sit inside that substrate, which exposes Granite Rapids and Clearwater Forest directly. Analyses compiled by 
Tom’s Hardware
 project an ABF supply shortfall of about 21% in 2027 and possibly more than 40% by 2028, with GPU and custom accelerator packages drawing on the same pool. Ajinomoto supplies more than 95% of ABF film and raised prices about 30% in the third quarter of 2026. Ibiden is spending $3.1 billion over fiscal 2026 to 2028 to reach 2.8x its 2024 AI substrate capacity and plans EMIB-T substrate production by its fiscal year ending March 2028. Unimicron raised 2026 capex to a record $1.07 billion, and Shinko, Kinsus, Nan Ya PCB, AT&S, and Samsung Electro-Mechanics round out the base. CPU vendors now negotiate for supply and substrate makers write the terms.

Packaging, Memory, and Test Suppliers Collect a Toll on Every Added Chiplet

Advanced packaging makes the 1,600 sq mm dimension possible. Intel is adding capacity in New Mexico and Malaysia and its EMIB-T flow is drawing external customers seeking a second source to TSMC’s CoWoS, while ASE with SPIL and Amkor expand OSAT capacity. Memory forms a second bottleneck, since server CPUs ship with 12-channel DDR5 and growing MRDIMM attach while Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron steer the same DRAM wafers toward HBM. DPUs from NVIDIA BlueField, built on Arm IP, AMD, Marvell, and Broadcom offload networking and storage, freeing cores for agents. One defective chiplet can scrap a whole package. That raises test intensity for Advantest and Teradyne and multiplies probe card touchdowns for FormFactor, Technoprobe, and MPI.

Supply and Custom CPU Design Will Decide Share Across End Markets

The map’s chip column shows Arm and RISC-V entries in every segment from agentic to mobile. Futurum’s 1H 2026 Data Center Semiconductors Market Sizing & Five-Year Forecast put Intel at 45.3% of Q4 CY2025 data center CPU revenue, AMD at 29.7%, and an Arm-based cohort at 25.0%. In agentic and cloud, AMD builds EPYC Venice at TSMC with its own substrate allocations, so every quarter Intel cannot ship is one EPYC can fill, and NVIDIA Vera and Arm’s AGI CPU compete for the same TSMC capacity. AWS Graviton, Google Axion, and Microsoft Cobalt let hyperscalers fit the CPU to their own agents and negotiate allocation directly, and Meta’s Rivos purchase points that custom model at RISC-V. Enterprise moves slower, with IBM Power and Fujitsu Monaka serving mission-critical niches and Phytium and Loongson anchoring China’s substitution push. In consumer and mobile, memory prices are cutting PC units about 12% in 2026, which frees Intel 18A wafers for data center parts. Merchant x86 share will erode fastest in segments where buyers can design their own CPUs and secure their own supply.

The bear case comes from the 2021 to 2022 ABF shortage, which ended in a 2023 glut once PC demand fell. If software raises agent concurrency per CPU, units could undershoot while Ibiden’s new capacity lands in 2028, and buyers locking in long-term agreements at peak prices would absorb that risk.

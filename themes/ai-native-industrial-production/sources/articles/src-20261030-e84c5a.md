Substrate recognition and ion-coupling mechanism of human VIAAT&nbsp;&nbsp;Nature

[page text]
Dear Editor,
Inhibitory neurotransmission is essential for stabilizing neural circuit activity and preventing pathological hyperexcitability
1
 and plays a critical role in motor coordination, sensory processing, and cognitive function.
2
 γ-Aminobutyric acid (GABA) and glycine are the principal inhibitory neurotransmitters and must be packaged into synaptic vesicles for activity-dependent release.
3
,
4
 This process is mediated exclusively by the vesicular inhibitory amino acid transporter (VIAAT), which directly determines the strength and reliability of inhibitory signaling.
5
,
6
,
7
 Disruption of VIAAT function has profound physiological consequences: VIAAT knockout results in embryonic lethality and severe developmental malformations in mice,
8
 whereas in humans, missense mutations in VIAAT impair GABAergic signaling and underlie a spectrum of genetic epilepsies.
9
,
10
 Despite its central role, the mechanisms underpinning the recognition of two distinct neurotransmitters and the mechanisms of ion coupling remain unclear. In particular, the driving force underlying VIAAT-mediated transport has long been controversial, with previous studies suggesting either proton or chloride gradients as the driving force.
11
,
12
,
13
 By integrating cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM), molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, and functional assays, our study elucidates the structural basis of substrate recognition, ion coupling, and conformational transition in human VIAAT.

Data availability

The cryo-EM density maps of human VIAAT in the apo, GABA-bound, glycine-bound, and chloride-free apo states have been deposited in the Electron Microscopy Data Bank (EMDB) under accession codes EMD-67826, EMD-67799, EMD-67798, and EMD-67797, respectively. The corresponding atomic coordinates have been deposited in the Protein Data Bank (PDB) under accession codes 21MN, 21LK, 21LJ, and 21LI, respectively.

Four burning questions for UNC basketball
There’s quite a lot we don’t know about this year’s Tar Heels.
by
 
 
Matt Ferenchick
Oct 30, 2026, 3:00 PM UTC
No matter what any expert claims to know, there’s just so much that is unknown about the North Carolina Tar Heels going into the 2026-27 college basketball season. In addition to a new head coach, there’s a vastly different roster this season. Like anyone else, I have my hunches and beliefs on what this team might do, but I would hardly stake my life to them. The ceiling and the floor of this team is just so much more unknown that any recent season in UNC history, and this is a program where the ceiling has been the roof, to quote a certain alumn.
As we continue our season preview ahead of games starting, here are some questions I have about this year’s Tar Heels.
What does a Michael Malone college basketball team look like?
We can all look at Malone’s Denver Nuggets teams. We can look at the type of players he and the staff have brought in for this year. We can guesstimate what those two parts of the equation will result in on the court. We still won’t know what the end result will be until games begin.
Looking at the roster that Malone and UNC have put together, I’m very cautiously optimistic about this year’s Tar Heels. However, I still don’t necessarily know how all the pieces are going to fit together. With the Nuggets in the NBA, Malone had Nikola Jokić. While there are other big men who are capable of passing well and initiating offense, Jokić is a one of one in terms of playing style and ability. It’s been a long time since Malone has coached a team where Jokić is the centerpiece.
I’ve been somewhat intrigued by the Malone hire since the beginning based on the idea of having an NBA-style offense at the college level, but what that ends up looking like in practice this year remains to be seen.
How does Malone himself adjust to coaching on the college level?
College coaches going to the professional level is always a risk, as the number of them in either football or men’s basketball that have had success isn’t a long list. However, the same could be said about pro coaches dropping down to the college level. Quite infamously, UNC hired an even more famous NFL coach in Bill Belichick for football, and that hasn’t worked out so far. Some people made jokes about the Tar Heels returning to that well for the men’s basketball hire in Malone, although I’m more optimistic about him than I ever have been about Belichick.
That being said, this is still going to be a new experience for Malone. His last college coaching gig was at Manhattan College back in 2001. While that is the Division I level, it’s quite aways away from coaching a blue blood like UNC in a league like the ACC, in addition to it just being a long time ago.
There’s a difference in coaching a college player compared to an NBA one. The level is just different. That’s no insult meant to any UNC player, but there’s a reason why the answer to the “could the best college team beat the worst NBA/NFL/etc?” question is always no. Just by pure math reasons, not every college player is going to make it to the big league in their sport. Even the worst players on a pro roster were the best players on their teams in college or overseas or whatever. The players are also just younger in general. You need to be way more of a teacher in college than in the pros.
The roster building aspect is also just different. Unlike with long term contacts in the pros, you’ll only have players around for a maximum amount of time no matter how good they are. Plus as is often mentioned, college sports now have become something of the wild west and in some ways you’ll have to re-recruit the players you already have on your team throughout the season.
Everything I’ve seen from Malone makes me think that he’s smart enough to not have a major issue with all of that. Plus, he’s surrounded himself with both UNC-experienced assistants (Sean May, Brandon Robinson) and general college-experienced ones (Chuck Martin, Jorge Sanz). That’s something that hasn’t happened as much with Belichick and definitely gives me hope that Malone knows there’s stuff about the college game he’ll have to learn.
How will the international players fit in?
Players from overseas coming over to play college basketball is hardly a new thing, but we have seen an uptick in it in recent years, to great effect in some cases. UNC dipped their toe into that water last year with Luka Bogavac and Ivan Matlekovic. (Henri Veesaar too, but he had previous college experience at Arizona.)
This season, the team will feature Sayon Keita and Alex Samodurov, who have previously played in Spain and Greece respectively. (Neo Avdalas is another from overseas but is transferring in from another college.) The scouting reports around those two both think they both could be very good college players, with Keita especially having the raw tools that could turn him into an NBA Draft pick. However, how fast they settle and how good they become this season is something that we’re going to have to wait and see.
Who is the team’s leader?
Between just the state of college transfers and the new coaching staff, there’s just so many difference pieces on this year’s roster. As evidenced by our Tar Heel Blog staff predictions where several different players got votes for Team MVP, there’s a couple different players who could end up being the team’s focal point. At least for me, there’s like five different guys who if we got to the end of the season, I could see them ending up as the best player.
That could be a good thing if most of their players hit their potential. However, if no one asserts themselves and we’re left with a bunch of solid but not dominant players, that could be a recipe for a mid team.
Those are the things I’m most interested in, but what are your burning questions heading into the season?

AMCA Prototype Development Race Advances as TASL, L&amp;T and Bharat Forge Submit Bids for ₹15,000 Cr DcPP Contract&nbsp;&nbsp;Defence.in

[page text]
India's quest to build a homegrown fifth-generation fighter aircraft has reached a crucial juncture as three major private-sector groups have officially submitted their proposals for the ₹15,000 crore Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) prototype development contract.

This ambitious national project is being spearheaded by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA), a premier laboratory under the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO).

The Ministry of Defence is currently seeking a Development-cum-Production Partner (DCPP) to take the ADA's design from the drawing board to the skies.

The winning bidder will be responsible for engineering five flying prototypes and a single structural test specimen.

Additional programme outlines suggest that the timeline is highly demanding, with the maiden prototype expected to take flight within 30 months of the contract signing, followed by an intensive campaign of roughly 1,800 test flights.

The competition features three formidable industry contenders. Tata Advanced Systems Limited (TASL) has entered the race as the sole independent bidder.

Larsen & Toubro (L&T) is leading a consortium partnered with state-owned Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL) and Dynamatic Technologies.

The third competitor is a consortium led by Kalyani Group's Bharat Forge, which has teamed up with public sector enterprise BEML and electronics specialist Data Patterns.

These proposals were successfully filed before the extended October 30 deadline, which was pushed back from August to give firms adequate time to finalise their complex industrial strategies.

A high-level defence committee is now reviewing the submissions. Industry sources note that initial document verification is currently underway, and a rigorous technical evaluation process will run through the end of December. Commercial price negotiations will follow to determine the final winner.

The selected industrial partner will play a historic role in outfitting the Indian Air Force with next-generation capabilities.

The AMCA is engineered to feature advanced stealth geometry, internal weapons bays to minimise radar cross-section, supercruise capabilities, and highly sophisticated AI-backed avionics with advanced sensor fusion.

To support this massive endeavour, the chosen DCPP must build a dedicated manufacturing and testing ecosystem near Puttaparthi in Andhra Pradesh.

This new facility will become the central hub for prototype integration, structural testing, and all future flight-development operations.

This procurement process marks a landmark transformation in India’s defence-industrial policy.

For decades, fighter aircraft manufacturing has been the exclusive domain of the state-run Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL).

By awarding this prototype contract to private industry, the government aims to establish a robust second domestic fighter production line to run in parallel with existing aerospace infrastructure.

While none of the contenders have independently built a complete supersonic fighter from scratch, they bring significant strengths to the table.

TASL has valuable final assembly experience from its ongoing C-295 transport aircraft partnership with Airbus in Vadodara, while the consortiums led by L&T and Bharat Forge offer deep collective expertise across heavy engineering, radar systems, and precision aerostructures.

The successful execution of this prototype phase will pave the way for serial production in the coming decade, acting as the technological cornerstone of the Indian Air Force's future combat fleet.

28 points from Tyrese Maxey paces the Sixers to their fifth victory of the season, first of the NBA Cup

Six games in, the Sixers have a single loss and two victories over the defending champion Knicks.

They moved to 5-1 on Friday night with a 98-93 win over New York in their NBA Cup opener at Xfinity Mobile Arena. The Knicks, who also
 lost to the Sixers on opening night
, fell to 2-4. 

Tyrese Maxey led the Sixers with 27 points on 10-for-19 shooting. LeBron James posted 20 points, nine rebounds and eight assists. 

Anfernee Simons poured in 21 points off the bench. 

Knicks superstar Jalen Brunson had 24 points and six assists. Karl-Anthony Towns recorded 22 points and 12 boards. 

The Sixers' injured players included Jaylen Brown (left knee patellar tendinopathy).

The team will wrap up its back-to-back Saturday night in Charlotte against the Hornets. 
Their second NBA Cup game on the calendar i
s next Friday against the Cavaliers.

Here are observations on the Sixers' win over the Knicks: 

Signs of offensive progress 

The Sixers racked up 39 fourth-quarter points on Wednesday in their 
comeback victory over the Pacers.

While they didn’t come close to matching that in Friday’s first quarter, the Sixers read the action and reacted in encouraging fashion. The Knicks often denied the Sixers’ first option, but the stars worked together well to unlock the defense. 

Joel Embiid (10 points, seven rebounds, seven assists) was effective all over the floor in his first stint. He teamed up with James on a sharp two-man game late in the shot clock, leading to a layup for the 22-time All-Star. James nailed a wide-open three-pointer in the first quarter after Embiid freed him up with a strong ball screen. 

Maxey sunk a catch-and-shoot three assisted by James off a designed double drag action. Embiid drove along the baseline and threw a nifty jumping pass to Kentavious Caldwell-Pope for a layup. 

Across the board, the Sixers made sensible, intuitive plays in the first quarter. They had eight assists and zero turnovers over the first 12 minutes. 

“The offense was awesome in the first half,” Sixers head coach Nick Nurse said. “Just awesome.”

Wade starts, does his thing defensively 

Dean Wade moved into the starting lineup, replacing Brown.

The Sixers used a nine-man rotation, adding rookie Labaron Philon Jr. to their usual early-season crew of Simons, Caldwell-Pope and Adem Bona. 

The second unit had a nice stretch late in the first quarter. Bona swatted away OG Anunoby’s layup attempt. Simons knocked down a three to put the Sixers up 29-19. 

The Knicks settled for plenty of jumpers in the first half. Still, the Sixers competed hard on defense and on the boards. They held New York to 44 first-half points. VJ Edgecombe was excellent on Brunson.  

Wade was solid against Anunoby and nabbed three steals in the first half. Much of the focus on the 
Sixers’ defensive potential 
naturally centers on the stars, but Wade is a key piece who’s got 
ample experience guarding big names.

“The stat sheet doesn’t always determine winning plays and the things he was doing tonight defensively,” James said of Wade. “And the things he was doing offensively, getting us extra shots and things of that nature. Just being in the right place at the right time. 

“You can always count on him. … Dean is a perfect player for every team. He can play for any team and he’s always going to make winning plays.”

Sixers sweat it out 

Embiid took an apparent hit to the face from Josh Hart’s arm and subbed out with 1:05 left in the second quarter holding his face. However, he was good to go for the start of the third quarter.

The Sixers extended their lead to 58-44 early in the third, prompting a timeout by Knicks head coach Mike Brown. 

New York found the required response. Regular turnovers began to pop up for the Sixers. Brunson drilled a corner three and drove in for a layup that cut the Knicks’ deficit to five points. 

The Sixers’ bench then stepped up. James threw a beautiful skip pass from the post to assist a Caldwell-Pope three. Simons buried three triples in the third quarter and swished a pure baseline jumper. He's gotten hot and made a massive impact the last two games. 

Bona struggled in the second half, which helped the Knicks keep the game tight. Embiid subbed back in with 8:42 remaining in the fourth quarter after Bona was whistled for his fourth foul on a Jordan Clarkson and-one layup. The Knicks trimmed the Sixers' lead to 85-82 on a Clarkson put-back lay-in. New York wound up with a 20-7 edge in second-chance points. 

Embiid's re-entry did not create an automatic reversal in momentum. A Brunson jumper pulled the Knicks within two points and the visiting fans were very vocal.

The Sixers played ultra-aggressive defense on Brunson in the closing minutes, sending several full-on blitzes at the reigning NBA Finals MVP. They still couldn't stop him from making a tightly contested corner three over Edgecombe that gave the Knicks a 90-89 lead.

James replied with a mid-range jumper. Following a stop, the Sixers were truly back in business when Edgecombe drained a top-of-the-key three. 

Brunson then split a pair of free throws. Maxey tossed up a heavily guarded shot inside on the ensuing trip and missed it wide left, but James was there to snatch the ball and lay it in. 

Not the smoothest finish in the world for the Sixers, but they ultimately notched another high-quality win against a projected Eastern Conference contender and deserve their 5-1 record. 

“We’re resilient,” Maxey said. “We ain’t going to just give up.”

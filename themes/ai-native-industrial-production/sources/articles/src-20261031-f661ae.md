[page text]
By Boxing Bob Newman

Photo By Mark Robinson

In a tougher than expected fight, WBA super middleweight champion Jaime Munguia nearly suffered a monumental upset at the hands of unknown and unheralded Russian Nikita Zon.

Zon had success early with some of the most awkward punches one could imagine. It was not only that he was a southpaw, he threw wide punches off the wrong foot, almost novice-like, but they landed. He ate some blistering left hooks from Munguia, but weathered them with little effect. 

Munguia finally had a measure of success in round four, snapping back the head of Zon, forcing the Russian to finally retreat for the first time in the fight.

Late in round five, Munguia landed a hard right to the jaw of Zon, leaving him badly shaken but the durable Russian didn’t go down and made it to the bell. Early in round six, Zon rocked Munguia badly, and it looked as if referee Tony Weeks might step in and stop it. Munguia’s mouth was bloodied as Zon flailed away wildly, trying to end it. Incredibly, Munguia rallied and turned the tables, rocking Zon badly. Round six was definitely a candidate for round of the year!

Comparatively, the seventh appeared to be a recovery round for both fighters as neither man had a distinct edge. The pace remained pedestrian through rounds eight and nine, especially for Zon. Munguia pressed forward, while not necessarily effective. He was at least making an effort.

Munguia did retreat some in the tenth, but countered nicely off the ropes. Zon was visibly flat in the eleventh, getting hurt with body shots from Munguia. Having never gone past the eighth round, Zon was surprising getting through the final round, still throwing punches. 

The final scores were as follows: 116-112 and 117-11 twice for Munguia who retained his title and improved to 47-2, 35 KOs. Zon had the victory in his grasp in round six, but let it slip away, falling to 12-1-1, 10 KOs.

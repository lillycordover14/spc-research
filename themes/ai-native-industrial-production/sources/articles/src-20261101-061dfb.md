Film Review: Miami Hurricanes 38 - UNC Tar Heels 35
Miami survived a Halloween night fright at UNC. The ‘Canes strip sack was the difference maker.
by
 
 
Justin Dottavio
Nov 1, 2026, 1:49 PM UTC
Winning ain’t easy. The Miami Hurricanes (
8-0
) survived a scary encounter with the UNC Tar Heels (
4-4
) on Halloween night, winning 
38-35.
The Canyonero Keys to Victory main theme was to avoid self-implosion. Miami didn’t implode at all just played flaccid zone while getting picked apart by an average QB. The three individual keys were:
1- Block. Some. Bodee. 
The Hurricanes O-Line held a good UNC defense to 
one
 sack and 
one
 TFL. It helped that 
Darian Mensah
 was willing to scramble around for the cause as well.
2- Pressure Edwards. 
Edwards was sacked 
four
 times including the 
Marquise
 
Lightfoot
 strip sack that wound up the difference maker. The issue is it took more than four to get pressure opening up more holes in coverage.
3- Shutdown Jordan Shipp
. Shipp did catch a 
TD
 pass from Edwards but he caught only 
four
 balls for 
44
 yards. I would’ve thought that was a massive sign that Miami covered the spread but nope the 
Corey Hetherman 
defense 
Jelani
 
Thurman
 score 
twice
.
The Doppler
The biggest stat in this one was the 
lone
 
turnover
 of the night, the UNC fumble that gave Miami an extra possession. The ‘Canes used it to score with Fletcher on the ground and take a 
10
 point lead.
On money downs, UNC got the win finishing 
10-of-13 
while Miami was only 
8-of-13
 on the night.
Miami did win the penalty yardage game with 
seven
 less yards in flags than UNC, Miami was only flagged 
three
 times to UNC’s 
six
.
Jake Weinberg
 being perfect on his 
five
 PAT’s, and making his only 
FG
 attempt, were the difference in the game as Miami won by three.
The Miami Offense
Mensah finished the game nearly perfect as he rushed for 
36
 yards, averaged 
9.4
 yards per pass attempt and tossed 
three
 touchdowns 
without
 a turnover.
Mensah found 
six
 different Miami receivers with 
four
 going for double-digit yards per catch marks. Toney caught 
two
 TD passes and Fletcher the other. 
Elija Lofton
 had a game breaker with a hurdle over a defender for a 1st down.
On the ground 
Javian
 
Mallory
 led the way with 
4.3
 yards per carry while Fletcher handled the hard thumping yardage with 
3.3
 YPC and 
two
 TD’s. The OL allowed only 
one
 TFL against a really strong UNC front six.
Above- Miami forced UNC to switch multiple times on Sat. night with great success. Here Toney starts outside and they run a Run & Shoot switch call. In the Air Raid both guys go deep, just to crossed markers. In the R&S the outside guy crosses then settles in space.
Above- Lofton on the kick out. I’d like him slightly more square vs. using the sharp point of the shoulder more chest/shoulder but it works here and he buzzes his feet to avoid the ‘whiff’ miss if the guy moves or wrong arms it.
Above- So here in Q1 it’s 1on1 vs Toney and they call a fade. Mensah reads that S when he doesn’t bail to help the 1on1 and instead plays the run Mensah pulls and throws a TD. WE’LL BE BACK TO THIS LATER!
Above- Now unlike Lofton, Briggs doesn’t buzz his feet. He and the LB explode into each other and both fall down. Briggs wins because it’s a first down, but people worried about shoulder injuries should look at that clip. That’s not how it should be done, but at least his head is on the right side and it worked!
Above- It’s 4over4 which made it a tougher play, but it’s three TE’s if I’m not mistaken. Toney draws a CB and S, Mensah has been scrambling which holds the ILB in case it’s a draw. And Mensah slings it quick to Fletcher who scores.
Above- Barkate ran a hitch and go. It requires pass pro which Mensah got the ball off clearly just in time. Barkate got “even” so he’s leavin’ re the CB. nice explosive from the Duke Duo.
Above- This is a smart way to run mesh. Lofton was on the inside as the #2 and ran a corner. Upshaw was on the outside as the #1 but he runs the crosser for Mesh. The mesh, switch and attempted bracket on MT all collide here and he’s open on the corner route. Another great way to line up and get Toney free.
Above- Rodriguez has really flipped the switch and picked up his game. Here he grabs a DL and drives him back for the TD carry from Fletcher to go-ahead.
Above- WE’RE BACK. Exactly how it looked last time, except this time Toney knows the CB is going to worry about an outside release. MT baits him out and breaks in. The safety being there means do NOT run a slant or glance- it’s either a hospital ball or a pick.
Above- So MT runs it flat like a fin route and the S over runs thinking slant the CB is outside thinking fade and Toney scores once again.
The Miami Defense
On the other side of the ball Miami struggled mightily against an average UNC offense. Edwards averaged 
10
 yards per pass attempt with 
four
 TD’s and only the strip sack turnover.
Edwards found 
nine
 different receivers with 
five
 going for double-digit yards per catch marks. Thurman, the TE, torched Miami’s sloppy secondary and LB corps for 
14
 yards per catch plus the 
two
 TD’s.
Demon June, Benjamin Hall 
and
 Jaylen McGill
 weren’t exactly explosive. The trio averaged 
3.4
,

 and

The defense came away with 
four
 sacks, 
five
 TFL’s and once again a low number of PBU’s- 
ONE
, and it was a bat down by 
Ahmad Moten.

Above- So the S has first out of the backfield typically here between the RB and the TE. That’s the “#3” in football terms. His eyes never leave the RB so someone should have the TE on the insert. Rhett Lashlee ran this with great success at Miami, too. Having the TE insert and up the seam for a TD or explosive.
Above- In previous games Thornton had been ‘running through’ tackles rather than coming to balance, buzzing his feet, and making a secure tackle. Here he buzzes and scrapes to make the stop.

The Wrap
Win your clunkers meet survive your scares. In order to finish undefeated, even against this slate, you have to win a few scary games. It’s now time to prove you have what it takes. Miami travels to South Bend for a showdown with Notre Dame in primetime.
Even if Miami loses this one and wins out they’re in the ACC Championship Game and the CFB Playoff. But Miami needs to secure a bye in round one to allow the players to heal up and the only way to get that week off is to beat ND.

It feels the same, but there's always some odd road taken to this dark place.
This week's strange, haunting path included a fourth-and-18 conversion by Washington when one stop by the Blackshirts would've basically won it.
But of course.
Of course there was a brilliant 25-yard catch by Washington's Chris Lawson on a pass that was as much prayer as a 50-50 ball. Although, with the way it has gone for Nebraska in late games, other teams are fully aware such prayers can get answered.
"I was watching the rush and you see V9 come off the corner, and he beat the guy right off the bat, and I was like, 'Holy crap, he's about to smoke him, we're about to sack him,'" Riley Van Poppel said.
It felt like it was in slow motion to the Husker defensive lineman.
Oh, crap. We sure he caught that? He caught it.
No review was overturning that sideline-hugging catch or the sick feeling in the guts of Nebraska faithful.
Washington scored two plays later, finally cracking the code against a Husker defense that had otherwise dominated. Then came a Washington pick-six.
Some bottles were thrown from the stands. Some boos came with it. Frustrated fans were in disbelief, but believed it totally at the same time. That doesn't make sense. Yes it does. You know it does.
Just the latest twisted version of a story previously told: Washington 17, Nebraska 10.
"For however long in that game, that was as dominant of a defensive performance ... as we've had since we've been here," Rhule said. "Offensively just couldn't really get anything going at the level we wanted to in terms of scoring points."
Rhule lamented having 200-plus yards at the half, but just seven points.
"Didn't get anything in the second half. Really hard to win a Big Ten game like that," Rhule said.
Don't take advantage early and it can make you feel stomach pains late. The Huskers have now dropped leads in the fourth quarter in the last three games, outscored 14-3 in this one and 39-10 in the final frame of the last three games.
"We have to find a way to get more offensive production to win these games ... Obviously three turnovers and a missed field goal are not a recipe to win," Rhule said. 
What has to happen on offense? Or even changes before the next game? Rhule was asked.
"I'm not in position right now to think about what I would do moving forward. We didn't give up any sacks. I thought we did a nice job with those things. We came in thinking we would have some man-to-man shots and deep balls, we'd win on those ... Obviously got one to Carter, but never really got another one the entire night."
Rhule said he has to study the breakdowns of what led to not connecting yards with points in the first half.
Prior to the fourth-down Washington conversion, the Husker defense had saved the day all night for a Husker offense that had three turnovers on Anthony Colandrea interceptions.
The sequence that will tick Husker faithful off the most may have come when it was 7-3 Nebraska. The Huskers had a first-and-goal at the 5 after a 31-yard Jacory Barney catch.
Then, a delay of game? 
A costly delay of game.
"Coach had a man-beating play called ... The clock was running down. Obviously I was trying to save my timeouts for the second half. The way that typically works is the official looks at the clock when it hits zero. His eyes then go down. And so it felt like on the field we got the ball off. Because normally it's zero, then he looks down, so I thought we got it off so I didn't use the timeout there. I obviously argued about that, but they're not going to reverse that call."
First-and-5 became first-and-10, which became second-and-10, which became third-and-10, which became fourth-and-18.
Colandrea backtracked on the called third-and-10 run play, trying to improvise, and instead took an 8-yard sack. 
"On the third-down play ... Coach called a quarterback draw with an RPO. You have a five-man box, he tried to run the RPO. I'd have to look at the tape to see why we bounced it outside. We'd rather just not take that loss, obviously. Those are not things that should happen, but they did happen, so I'll have to go back and look at why they did happen."
That pushed it to a 36-yard field goal that Kyle Cunanan missed.
Washington coach Jedd Fisch pumped his fists. Stats were slanted, but just one touchdown by his side would win it.
Nebraska outyarded Washington 387-267 and held the Huskies to 32 rushing yards. 
But Washington got the clutch drive with Demond Williams coming off the bench and leading a 12-play, 81-yard drive that included that fourth-and-18.
Vincent Shavers was closing in on a sack. Williams felt the heat and just let it go. Lawson, who had 155 yards on 12 catches, made a tremendous catch in tight coverage against the sideline. 
Nebraska fans could see that anvil falling from the sky.
It landed two plays later. The Huskers blitzed Andrew Marshall from the corner spot. Washington threw to his spot and McDougle had a challenging tackle in space that he couldn't make. Lawson scooted in for a 25-yard touchdown.
Rhule was pointing that play at himself.
"That play is 100 percent on me," Rhule said. "I said to Coach, 'Bring the corner.' Brought the corner. Obviously, they completed the ball, (we) missed the tackle and they scored. So as we search for ways to get off the field at the end of the game, I have to live with that. That's not Coach Aurich, that was me."
Nebraska still had 1:30 left down 10-7 with two timeouts, but Colandrea threw an interception that Alex McLaughlin returned 48 yards for a touchdown.
The Huskers got a late field goal from John Hohl and a clever onside kick was almost recovered by Nebraska.
Almost, but almost doesn't matter.
"The things that you guys are bringing up are all things that we're going to have to look at tomorrow," Rhule said. "Like, hey, why did we do this? Or why did this happen? Again, you cannot win in the Big Ten scoring seven points. And we almost did. If fourth and 18 is out of bounds, you win that game. But you still have obviously those same problems. So we have to do a lot of work tomorrow until we play Friday."
Rhule's message to the fan base?
"I certainly understand how frustrating this is, that this has happened three games in a row. I can just say that I obviously feel it, too. I'm obviously responsible for it. It's my job. And so I thought it was an electric atmosphere. It was unbelievable for recruiting. It was dynamic and I would've thought that the offense would've matched the defense. And special teams, I thought Archie (Wilson) did a nice job punting tonight, but other than that — we have to make that field goal.
"So I understand exactly how people think, and how passionate they are and how frustrating three games in a row like that are. That's why I start off by saying, 'That call, that's on me.' But all I can do is show up tomorrow and get back to work for Illinois. And try to have a great November. And try to have a great 'this week.' That's what I'll do."

Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026 | 2 a.m.

The Raiders are paying the quarterback they face as an opponent today nearly $15 million more this season than the quarterback they’ll start for themselves in the game.

Geno Smith gets his chance to beat the Silver and Black a year and a half after the franchise traded a third-round pick to acquire him and signed him to a $75 million contract extension.

Smith’s New York Jets (2-5) host the Raiders (3-4) at 10 a.m. PT this morning at MetLife Stadium with the game airing on CBS (KLAS-TV Channel 8), streaming on Paramount+ and broadcasting on Raider Nation Radio 920 AM.

Needless to say, Smith didn’t work out as a Raider after coming in alongside last year’s head coach, Pete Carroll.

Las Vegas finished an NFL-worst 3-14 and traded Smith to the New York Jets in the offseason while agreeing to absorb $16.2 million of his salary this season.

The Raiders’ quarterback situation has certainly improved since then but doesn’t remain fully settled. They wooed free agent Kirk Cousins, signing him to the veteran minimum salary of $1.3 million with a guaranteed $10 million bonus set to be paid in the offseason. The Atlanta Falcons are obligated to pick up the bulk of his salary this season after releasing him.

Cousins has been a bargain for the Raiders, leading the NFL with 16 touchdown passes and sitting around league-average by most metrics, but fans are still calling for his job amid a four-game losing streak.

The calls for thrusting No. 1 overall draft pick Fernando Mendoza behind center are getting louder, and Cousins may need to beat the Jets to momentarily quiet them. The result could also influence whether or not the Raiders are interested in selling as the Nov. 10 trade deadline approaches.

The Jets sit as a 1.5-point favorite in a game that feels relatively high-stakes despite how poorly both teams are playing.     

Favorable matchup: 
Raiders running back Ashton Jeanty vs. Jets’ defensive weaknesses

It’s beginning to feel like now or never for last year’s No. 6 overall pick. Jeanty has been one of the NFL’s least efficient rushers with 3.1 yards per carry, a touchdown and no gains of more than 20 yards. But the Jets bring the easiest matchup he’s faced since before the Raiders’ losing streak began. New York rates No. 24 in the NFL in rush defense per the DVOA ratings. The Jets are fresh off giving up a career-high 103 yards to Miami Dolphins running back Ollie Gordon, who went 173 selections behind Jeanty in last year’s draft. Better yet for Jeanty’s prospects, the Jets are also deficient in defending running backs in the passing game, at No. 22 via DVOA. Jeanty has delivered as a receiver this season, tied for the team lead along with tight end Brock Bowers by hauling in 34 receptions. He’s turned that into 312 receiving yards and three touchdowns. For better or worse, Raiders coach Klint Kubiak’s game plan figures to feature Jeanty even heavier than normal against the Jets.

Problematic matchup: 
Raiders’ offensive line vs. Jets defensive setup

Some remain forgiving of Jeanty’s underperformance, and place most of the blame on the Raiders’ slumping offensive line. There’s a lot of statistical evidence to make that case, including the unit ranking No. 31 in the NFL in adjusted line yards, a measure that assigns how much the offensive line is responsible for the run game. Las Vegas had been stronger in pass protection to start the year but has since tailed off there too, putting Cousins under a lot more duress. Kubiak downplayed some of the issues earlier in the losing streak but sounded more generally alarmed after last week’s 35-21 loss to the LA Rams. He said everything was on the table to fix it going into the Jets game, including potential personnel changes. Kubiak has been complimentary of rookie third-round pick Trey Zuhn, who can play every offensive line position and might be inching closer to getting his first real chance to start. The guard positions have been the Raiders’ biggest weaknesses, as free agent signee veteran Spencer Burford has regressed on the left while a switch from Jackson Powers-Johnson to Caleb Rogers on the right hasn’t worked out. The Rams, led by future Hall of Famer Aaron Donald, tortured Rogers a week ago.

Gamebreaker:  
Wide receiver Tre Tucker

Receiver depth remains a major weakness for the Raiders, but they’ve figured out the top targets better than most anticipated. There was a lot of concern over who would fill the No. 1 receiver role coming into the season but, in everything except name alone, it’s been Bowers. The third-year All-Pro has lined up as a traditional tight end on less than 15% of snaps per Pro Football Focus. He’s frequently in the slot or out wide, and has not only produced but found a worthy sidekick in Tucker, who’s been above average for a de facto No. 2 receiver. Tucker has 10 catches for 200 yards and three touchdowns in the past two games, and both the Rams and Buffalo Bills — who beat the Raiders 33-30 two weeks ago — are better against deep passes than the Jets. Tucker has repeatedly flashed his world-class speed to get behind opposing defenses, and opportunities should be there for Cousins to catch him streaking down the field for a third straight week.     

Big Number: 86

Former starting defensive tackle Jonah Laulu had 86 tackles in his first two seasons for the Raiders, a franchise record for defensive linemen, before getting released in the middle of his third year last week. The Tennessee Titans promptly claimed the Las Vegas hometown hero, who graduated from Centennial High, off waivers. Cutting Laulu can’t quite register as a shock considering Kubiak and his staff never seemed as high on him as the past two regimes and made him a game-day inactive in two of the past three weeks. Laulu wasn’t an ideal fit in first-year defensive coordinator Rob Leonard’s 3-4 base scheme, so the Raiders moved on. Letting him go shows growing confidence in second-year former sixth-round pick JJ Pegues, who was active in front of Laulu in the aforementioned two games, and Tim Keenan, recently claimed off waivers from the Rams. The duo played a combined 35 snaps against the Rams. Both have much more size than the 289-pound Laulu at 325+ pounds apiece and should better address the Raiders’ weakness against the run. Laulu’s strength was more in pass rushing.    

Best Bet (5-2): 
Ashton Jeanty over 13.5 rushing attempts

Last week, we cashed Cousins over 33.5 pass attempts out of a belief that the game script would skew heavily toward the air attack. The Raiders are going to do the opposite this week. Kubiak is desperate to get Jeanty going and should see the clear path available against the Jets. Rookie running back Mike Washington deserves more of the carries but Kubiak doesn’t fully trust him. This should be a close game with the Jets unlikely to pull away, so the Raiders won’t need to abandon the run like they’ve done often during the losing streak. Jeanty should be successful, so over 49.5 rushing yards and an even-money anytime touchdown bet were also considered, but attempts might be the safest bet. It’s hard to see the Raiders not sticking with Jeanty even if he struggles against the Jets.

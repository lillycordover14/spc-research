		Eagles' Eli Stowers ready to build on his solid NFL debut 	

			Eagles rookie Eli Stowers got his feet wet against the Cowboys and is hoping to build on that the rest of the way.		

					By 
Dave Zangaro
 
•

Published 5 hours ago

						Eagles defensive tackle Jordan Davis spoke to reporters after Eagles practice on Friday and admitted that the Eagles played with poor effort against the Cowboys in Week 7.					

He played just a handful of snaps and didn’t have a catch. But at least Eli Stowers finally got his first game action on Monday night against the Cowboys.

And that’s something to build on.

“It was awesome, man. It’s a blessing,” Stowers said this week. “It’s a dream come true just to be out there, working, getting better. Just to be out on the field was a blessing.”

The Eagles are 2-5 as they head to Washington as 5 1/2-point underdogs against the one-win Commanders on Sunday Night Football. If this season continues to spiral, it’s going to be all about watching young players grow. Stowers is near the top of that list.

The second-round tight end from Vanderbilt began his rookie season on Injured Reserve with a quad injury and missed the first six games of the season. But the Eagles activated him to the 53-man roster against the Cowboys and gave him work on offense and special teams.

Stowers, 23, played 12 snaps on offense and was targeted one time without a catch. But Stowers did some positive things away from the football, which likely impressed his coaches.

“Every single play there’s something that you can do to help the offense be successful,” Stowers said. “Everybody’s got a job on every single play and so it’s every single rep. How can you be intentional about what your job is? Whether it’s getting open, catching the ball, blocking for somebody, running off for somebody. How can you do that to the best of your abilities? That’s what I try to do.”

Against the Cowboys, Stowers was the Eagles’ third tight end after Dallas Goedert and Johnny Mundt. That pecking order seems unlikely to change unless Goedert gets traded, but Stowers can continue to earn more snaps and targets.

Stowers is in a tight end room with Goedert and Zach Ertz and has been able to learn from two of the greatest tight ends in franchise history.

“He went out there, I feel like when he was in there, he did his job really well,” Goedert said. “But it’s the same thing. He works really hard, he was excited to get out there and I was just excited to see his progression. You learn a lot your rookie year, you see big improvements during your rookie year so we’re all looking forward to that.”

After a forgettable training camp — Stowers struggled even before suffering a hamstring injury in the preseason — the rookie from Vanderbilt showed some positive signs in his first regular season game action.

“I thought Eli did a good job,” offensive coordinator Sean Mannion said. “He stepped in there as his first opportunity to wear that jersey and be out on an NFL field. He'll never forget that. We felt comfortable with Eli jumping in and wearing multiple hats for us. 

“Similar to Makai (Lemon), he's still catching up on some of those reps that are lost due to injury, which is unfortunate, but he's smart. He really works at it. He's got great movement skills. I really think with more time, he's going to continue to grow. I thought when we asked him to go in there and step in and play a role, he did everything we asked of him.”

Stowers began his college career as a quarterback and then eventually became a star tight end at Vandy, so special teams is a new world for him. The Eagles had Stowers play 9 snaps on special teams — all on kick return and punt return.

How did he do?

“His first time really being out there against some good guys out there,” special teams coordinator Michael Clay said. “There's some stuff he felt good about, some stuff we could improve on, and we're just going to keep feeding him to make him feel comfortable in this area. May not have played a whole bunch of teams in his career, but like I always say, everybody in this room is liable to play some teams.”

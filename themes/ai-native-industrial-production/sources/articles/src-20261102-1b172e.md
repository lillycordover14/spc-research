Tar Heels in NFL: Maye sparks second-half comeback with three TDs; Downs scores in Colts loss&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

Drake Maye needed a half to find his footing in rainy Miami. Once he did, the former Carolina quarterback supplied every touchdown New England needed.

Maye threw two long touchdown passes and ran for another score Sunday, rallying the Patriots from a 13–3 halftime deficit to a 24–19 victory over the Dolphins. Josh Downs also reached the end zone, scoring Indianapolis’ only touchdown on a difficult afternoon for several former Tar Heels in Week 8.

The two long scoring throws continued a productive stretch for Maye, who has thrown at least two touchdown passes in five consecutive games. His 14 touchdown passes of at least 30 yards over the past two seasons 
lead the NFL during that span
.

Maye completed 16 of 20 passes for 199 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions, finishing with a 141.5 passer rating. His second-half turnaround helped New England improve to 5–3 and remain unbeaten in three AFC East games.

The breakthrough came with 9:23 left in the third quarter, when Maye found Kyle Williams for a 43-yard touchdown. Maye stepped up to avoid pressure and delivered the pass over the middle, with Williams holding on as he crossed the goal line. The play initially was ruled incomplete before a successful New England challenge.

“It was huge,” 
Maye said
. “We needed to open it up a little bit.” 

Maye’s next touchdown drive required more improvisation. He recovered a mishandled snap and still found DeMario Douglas for a 26-yard gain, then connected with Douglas again for a fourth-down conversion.

Maye finished the possession with a two-yard touchdown run
, showing linebacker Willie Gay Jr. a potential pitch before keeping the ball and scoring with 59 seconds left in the third quarter.

Douglas then turned a Maye pass into a 48-yard touchdown with 12:39 remaining, pushing New England’s lead to 24–13. Maye finished with eight carries for five yards, modest rushing production that nevertheless included the touchdown that put the Patriots ahead for good.

“Got to love Drake. He just picks it up and keeps playing,” 
Patriots coach Mike Vrabel said
.

Maye completed 9 of 10 second-half passes for 146 yards while playing without several regular targets.

Downs’ first touchdown of the season brought considerably less satisfaction.

The fourth-year receiver caught a six-yard pass from Daniel Jones with 3:59 left in the third quarter of Indianapolis’ 42–7 loss at Jacksonville. Downs was wide open for the score, but the Jaguars already led 35–0. He finished with four catches on five targets for 23 yards, with none of his receptions going longer than eight yards.

Downs also returned a punt 20 yards and played 46 offensive snaps. His touchdown came during a week in which his future in Indianapolis became a prominent question after he requested a trade.

Indianapolis struggled to give him many opportunities. Jacksonville opened with a 98-yard touchdown drive and built a 28–0 halftime lead. An attempted gadget play involving Downs ended when Jonathan Taylor fumbled before the pitch reached the receiver.

The touchdown prevented a shutout, but the Colts fell to 2–6. Downs has 30 catches for 330 yards and one touchdown through eight games. Indianapolis offensive lineman Charlie Heck was inactive, as was Jacksonville cornerback Alijah Huzzie.

Javonte Williams had plenty of work but little running room in Dallas’ 33–30 overtime loss to Arizona.

Williams carried 24 times for 56 yards, averaging 2.3 yards per attempt, and caught one pass for a yard. His longest run gained six yards, and an early fourth-and-one attempt from Dallas’ 32-yard line ended short of the first down.

His first-quarter fumble produced an unusual Dallas touchdown. Williams was running toward the end zone when the ball was knocked loose and bounced across the goal line. Tight end James Mitchell recovered it for the score.

Former UNC quarterback Sam Howell did not play for Dallas, and has yet to get into a regular-season game.

Omarion Hampton also found little room in the Chargers’ 24–9 loss to the Rams.

Hampton rushed nine times for 20 yards and caught two passes for seven yards while playing 18 offensive snaps. The Chargers led 9–3 late in the second quarter but never reached the end zone, surrendering 21 consecutive points as they fell to 0–7. Hampton’s longest carry gained six yards.

As the clock ran down in the Broncos' 28-24 win over the Chiefs, Kansas City aimed to play spoiler. And after marching the ball to the 1-yard line in a two-minute drive, the Chiefs handed the ball to a proven playmaker in Kenneth Walker.
On his 21st and final carry, the Super Bowl LX MVP took the ball and plunged forward toward the goal line. Skinner, though, charged through a hole and stopped Walker for no gain.
It was perhaps the Chiefs' last, best chance at scoring the game-winning touchdown â and Skinner erased the opportunity.
Perhaps even more impressive than the play? That Skinner made it after playing just two defensive snaps over the first seven weeks of the season. Primarily a special teams contributor, Skinner was pressed into nine defensive snaps on Sunday as he helped replace an injured Brandon Jones.
The fourth-year safety was just the latest in a series of Broncos defensive backs who have been called upon to serve larger roles as their teammates have battled injury.
Jahdae Barron, Kris Abrams-Draine, Devon Key and Taylor Rapp have all played significant snaps at times this season for Denver's defense, as starters in Jones, Pat Surtain II, Riley Moss and Talanoa Hufanga have missed time.
"They've been significant for us," Head Coach Sean Payton said Monday. "A lot of these young guys we've drafted that are getting more and more playing time. It's a little bit of our league, and you just hope you're in a position with depth at those spots."
On Sunday against the Chiefs, the Broncos' depth helped secure a win.

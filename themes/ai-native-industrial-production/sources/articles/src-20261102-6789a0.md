RONNIE'S REPORT

Pushed out by popular opinion
One of artificial intelligence firms’ biggest super PACs has been forced into near irrelevance amid an 
outpouring of public backlash
 to the AI boom. Research shared exclusively with 
The Lever 
shows that the affiliated PACs of Leading The Future — a pro-AI industry group backed by Big Tech leaders — have bowed out of tight Congressional races this general election cycle as it largely becomes regarded as a “politically toxic entity that candidates and donors do not want to be associated with.” 
According to the Guardrails Alliance, a labor-backed “counterweight” super PAC in favor of AI regulation, Leading The Future’s PACs did not direct any of the group’s $140 million war chest to the 50 most competitive House races or the seven most competitive Senate races this general cycle. That includes abandoning candidates in competitive general races that the group’s affiliates backed during the primaries.
“While the AI industry is likely to remain a major spender in elections moving forward, it is abundantly clear that Leading The Future has completely and unequivocally failed in [its] political strategy,” said the Guardrails Alliance. “Deep pockets were supposed to make [it] a political powerhouse, but the unpopularity of [its] agenda and [its] funders have made [it] a liability instead.” 
Leading The Future’s slowdown comes as 
three-quarters of voters
 in the Senate battlegrounds of Iowa, Ohio, and Michigan say that they are less likely to vote for a candidate backed by Big Tech-funded AI super PACs. It had a bigger net impact on voters’ likely choices than whether a candidate had voted to cut Medicaid.

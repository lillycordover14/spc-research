CircuitHub says its robotic factory can deliver some PCB orders faster than Chinese rivals

Monday, 2 November 2026, 22:28

Photo source: TechRadar

CircuitHub says incoming orders already exceed its factory’s current manufacturing capacity.

Massachusetts-based CircuitHub uses a robotic manufacturing system called The Grid to produce printed circuit boards for companies that need different electronic designs. Instead of fixed production lines, coordinated machines and mobile platforms move boards between manufacturing stations.

This is reported by 
TechRadar
.

CircuitHub describes The Grid as “one large robot”: equipment works together to move and process individual boards. Levitating platforms carry them between stations for solder paste application, component placement, heating, soldering and automated inspection. Machine learning models, AI agents and conventional optimization software coordinate the operations and determine how equipment handles each manufacturing assignment.

The system does not require the factory to reorganize its production arrangements when a new board design enters manufacturing. Each board follows its own route, allowing different products to be made at the same time without conventional production-line changeovers. CircuitHub says an order with 100 different designs can be processed as easily as 100 copies of one design.

The same equipment handles experimental prototypes and larger batches, without separate machinery for different production stages. CircuitHub says this setup serves hardware developers ordering limited quantities of electronics for testing, research, manufacturing tools and product development. The company says its customers include a major electric vehicle manufacturer, a leading artificial intelligence laboratory and established technology companies.

To compare delivery time and cost, CircuitHub used an example of five assembled motor-controller boards and compared The Grid with a typical quick-turn manufacturer in Shenzhen, China. Each four-layer board had 118 components across 38 distinct component types. The comparison included manufacturing, component costs and delivery.

In CircuitHub’s example, producing the five boards through The Grid costs $630, with delivery to a US address taking four days. The equivalent Shenzhen order costs approximately $465 after shipping and US import tariffs, with a median delivery time of nine days. The US factory therefore charges approximately $165 more, while delivering the boards five days earlier.

CircuitHub argues that the shorter wait gives engineering teams more time to continue development instead of waiting for overseas manufacturing. The comparison is the company’s and covers one order; it does not independently establish performance across different manufacturers.

CircuitHub says The Grid has operated commercially for five years, generating tens of millions of dollars in annual electronics manufacturing activity. The company reports delivering more than 2.2 million circuit boards, installing over 150 million components and serving 26,000 engineers. Despite this operating history, the company says incoming orders already exceed the factory’s current manufacturing capacity. CircuitHub said in a blog post:

“Demand is already well ahead of what we can build. This year we will 5x capacity, with another big jump coming in 2027,”

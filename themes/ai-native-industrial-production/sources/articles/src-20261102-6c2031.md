Finland has joined France’s nuclear deterrence initiative, according to Finnish President Alexander Stubb. He confirmed that official cooperation began on October 27, although Helsinki and Paris have yet to agree on the specific arrangements for their partnership.

Stubb explained that closer discussions with France, as a nuclear-armed state, would give Finland a better understanding of how major powers assess the consequences of their actions in the current security environment. He emphasized the importance of such exchanges as Helsinki explores its role in the initiative.

Several forms of cooperation are under consideration, including consultations on France’s nuclear doctrine, joint military exercises and the participation of Finnish armed forces in strategic deterrence activities. The final scope of Finland’s involvement remains to be determined.

The French initiative also allows for the temporary deployment of strategic aircraft to partner countries. However, Paris will retain exclusive control over its nuclear arsenal, including all decisions concerning the use of nuclear weapons. These powers will not be transferred to participating states.

According to analysts, Finland is gradually becoming part of a broader European nuclear deterrence framework being developed under French leadership. The proposed arrangement is expected to operate alongside 
NATO
’s existing collective defense system rather than replace it.

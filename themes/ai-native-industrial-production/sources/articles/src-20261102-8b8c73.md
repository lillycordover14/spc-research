    The ASL dog, Geno, has made a strong positive impact on FHC

Gabbi Jones
, 
Copy Editor
 

November 2, 2026

 

Kimberly Anderson
 
Geno has brightened the ASL classroom with his friendly personality.
 

 

Many students have never had a dog in their school, let alone a language class. This year,
 American Sign Language (ASL) students at FHC have the unique chance to help prepare a dog to be a service dog for a deaf person.

ASL teacher Kimberly Anderson has worked closely with Paws With a Cause, a nonprofit organization that trains dogs to become service dogs for people with disabilities. She volunteered with them and eventually decided to help train one of their dogs. The dog, Geno, has certain distractions that aren’t helpful toward the training process, since he is in a school setting. In any setting, there are pros and cons, but in a school, many parts of the training process can both help and harm Geno. 

“Geno’s main goal will always be socialization, which is great, and that’s what [Paws With a Cause] wanted for him,” Anderson said. “There are certain things that he can’t do, and the students don’t know [what to do or] what they should say [if he does]. So it’s not only training Geno, but it’s also training the [students].”

Geno makes whatever class he’s in light up with excitement. ASL student junior Leen Abdullahi has seen a difference in the class with Geno, and in her perspective as she spends more time with him. Her perspective has shifted to looking more deeply into why Geno is here. Geno reminds the ASL students of Deaf people’s needs as they help teach him small pieces of sign language.

“Some of the positives of having Geno in the class are [that] I think more about Deaf people and their everyday life,” Leen said. “Geno helps us experience that while we’re training him.”

Geno has to go through many different steps along the way, and the initial training is one of the first. With Anderson’s help, Geno will succeed in his final home with a Deaf person. School can be very hard for Geno because of how many people he is around, and his next step, called “college,” shifts him into harder training with fewer people around him than at school. Eventually, when he reaches his final destination, everything he has learned—and will learn—in school will help him.   

“

[Geno] has inspired me to look into Paws With a Cause and possibly look into getting my own dog [to train], he also allows other students to reflect on how they can help in different causes.

— Kristin Rissi

“He’s very well behaved; I’ve heard him bark three times total,” Anderson said. “So he’s gonna be a very well-behaved dog [in his future home], and he’s so friendly. He’s also so chill [if I’m] teaching, and he doesn’t have to be by someone; he’ll go and sit by himself.”

Geno is a positive addition to the class as he brings excitement into the classroom, and many students want to play with him during class time. Some students, usually those who are in Level Four ASL, can take Geno on walks or help train him. Senior Kristin Rissi, who takes Level Four ASL and has an independent study hour for the class, has experience with this. 

“I’ve taken him for walks and [helped train him],” Kristin said. “I give him treats when he sits when I sign ‘sit’… He has a list of commands that he knows vocally, [but we’re training him] to understand them as signs too.”

When students found out they were getting a dog in the class, they were thrilled. For Leen, it was not the best surprise when she found out Geno was coming to the classroom. Because of her anxiety about dogs, she was skeptical, but she quickly understood Geno’s personality and gravitated toward him. 

“At first I was kind of scared of Geno,” Leen said. “Then as the year progressed we developed more of a relationship, and now we’re friends… I think [we have] more of an ionic bond because I have a negative charge towards him and he has a positive charge towards me.”

Between the joy Geno brings into the classroom and the deeper purpose of having him, Geno has made a significant impact on students. Because of this impact on FHC, Geno has further inspired others to look into Paws With a Cause and try to help others through that.

“He has inspired me to look into Paws With a Cause and possibly look into getting my own dog [to train],” Kristin said. “[Geno] also allows other students to reflect on how they can help in different causes.”

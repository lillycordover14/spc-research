Todd Bowles: Coaching Beginning To Figure Out How To Use The Offensive Line

November 2nd, 2026 

Right tackle Luke Goedeke.

Back when almost everyone was stomping their feet about the Bucs’ offensive line, Joe had a hunch that Bucs offensive coordinator Zac Robinson either wasn’t using them right or had them in a wrong scheme.

Joe couldn’t see how five guys, who previously had been a top-three offensive line, suddenly forgot how to block all at the same time.

Joe doesn’t believe in coincidences in football.

A week ago in Carolina, the Bucs’ offensive line had a breakout party. Yesterday, it was a mosh pit.

The Bucs simply owned the Dixie Chicks defense. It was maybe the most dominating performance Joe has ever seen a Bucs offensive line have. Some 56 carries and 324 yards later, Atlanta was crying for their mothers and praying for the game to end. It was beautiful.

After the game, it seemed KitKat-eating Bucs coach Todd Bowles sort of confirmed Joe’s belief that the Bucs’ offensive line wasn’t being used right.

“I think we’re figuring it out,” Bowles 
said of the front line of the Bucs
. “You know, the coaches are figuring out the players and what they can and can’t do, what runs they like and don’t like, things like that.

“They’re really starting to gel.”

Joe doesn’t know why this would be a surprise to coaches. Don’t they watch tape? Or, were theyt convinced they had it all figured out when they first walked into One Buc Palace last winter?

Joe has learned long ago never to underestimate egos in the NFL.

And why did it take five games to figure it out? The season was damn near lost!

Welcome back, Bucs offensive line!

What wouldn’t work does work.  What did work doesn’t work anymore.

In the first six games, the Bucs racked up a 1-5 record passing 252 times and running 161 times – 61% passes and 39% runs; the 2025 NFL average was 55% passes and 45% runs.

In wins the last two weeks, the Bucs won passing 14 times and running 105 times – 13% passes and 87% runs.

For 2026, per Sumer Sports, the Bucs are tied at 27th in EPA/play at -0.14; they are 31st in EPA per pass at -.28; they have the 2nd highest scramble rate at 10.12%; and they have the 4th highest interception rate at 3.37%.  When the Bucs pass, they literally have been going backwards on average in terms of winning games.

Is it probable that the Bucs can keep winning running on 95% of plays on offense as they did against the Falcons.  I doubt it, and I am sure that it is not the most efficient way to run the offense.

But, can the Bucs continue to win and be efficient with something approaching an 82% run rate as they did against the Panthers?  

Against the Panthers, the Bucs had their third best game for yards per pass attempt at 7.6; the Bucs best yards per pass game came against the Cowboys when they ran on 61% of plays.

And, against the Panthers, the Bucs had their second best passer rating at 109.7 vs the 110.2 passer rating they had against the Cowboys when they ran on 61% of plays.

Last season, the team with the highest run rate was the Ravens with 54.7%

The Bucs need to Boldly Go where they have demonstrated that This Bucs Team can Win!  That place is a run rate of 61% and points north.

No other team is doing that?  

It’s the NFL.  If the Bucs continue to lead the way, the imitators will be thick as flies.  

But, that doesn’t mean that the imitators have the personnel to make it work.

The Bucs have found what their personnel can do well.  Pray that everybody gets the Hell out of their way and lets them do it!  🙂

But coach Ben Johnson summed it up nicely after presenting the newest Bear with his first game ball in the locker room.
"His impact was profound," Johnson said.
While wreaking havoc on opposing offenses is nothing new for Simmons â he built a reputation in Tennessee over the past eight years as one of the top players at his position across the NFL â it's no easy feat to join a new team and jump into a new defensive scheme in the middle of the week and

"He just wants to win, and that's his best quality," defensive lineman James Lynch said. "He's gonna give everything he's got for us to get a W."
Sure, Simmons' wealth of experience and high football IQ gave him an advantage when preparing to play in Dennis Allen's defense and in his first prime-time game since 2023. Yet as soon as he passed his physical Thursday afternoon and officially became a Chicago Bear, Simmons went right to work, determined to put himself in position to be at his best by Monday night.
"I was in the building 'til almost 10 o'clock Thursday night just trying to get the game plan and catch up," Simmons said. "Getting the game plan early in the morning Friday. "That's always my goal - just embrace the moment. That's what happened tonight. It was like a lift [off my shoulders] once I ran out there. Thank God they got the ball first as well. His presence alone helped the Bears defense record three sacks of Seahawks quarterback Sam Darnold, plus three additional quarterback hits and two tackles for loss.
"I think he's just â I don't know â you can feel his presence," linebacker T.J. Edwards said. "Especially me, I'm right behind him, so I can kind of just feel how they're trying to block him. I feel like [the defense] was just flying today. They made our job a whole lot easier. But he definitely helps, for sure.
"He's just himself. His play speaks for [itself]."
On the first play of Seattle's second possession, Simmons got home and recorded the first sack of the game on Darnold. On the next play, he and linebacker Devin Bush combined for a run stop, leading to Seattle punting the ball away two snaps later.
The Bears defense did not allow points on eight of the Seahawks' 11 drives, forcing five punts, a blocked field goal by defensive end Austin Booker, an interception by Lynch and a turnover on downs just before the two-minute warning to close out the win.
Even when Simmons wasn't making the play himself, he still affected the Seattle offense on seemingly every snap, bringing a jolt of energy to the entire defense.
"Phenomenal player," defensive end Daniel Hardy said. "Really charged up the locker room. Definitely boosted up the DL. He took a ton of reps tonight and made them all count. Â You gotta account for him. Give him a 1-on-1, it's not gonna end well.
"He frees stuff up for everybody just because he draws so much attention to him. So appreciate it, and we're all happy with the production."
The player who seemed to benefit most from Simmons's addition was his college teammate at Mississippi State, Montez Sweat, who recorded three quarterback hits and a sack on Darnold. He also had two additional sacks which were negated by penalties.
Sweat told reporters postgame that it's "a great feeling" to team up with his fellow Bulldog again and noted that the attention Simmons commands will continue to help him and the rest of the D-line find additional success.
"I definitely got my opportunities," Sweat said. "I definitely got some 1-on-1s. I was looking forward to taking advantage of that, and I'm looking forward to doing that the rest of the year.
"[Simmons has] definitely upped his game and his IQ. He's always had a real love for the game, and that affects you across the whole line and the whole team."
While the defensive players certainly felt Simmons' impact most, the Bears offense felt his energy too, whether it was him dapping them up after drives or watching in awe from the sideline as his signature bright sleeves flashed all over the field.
"We're always watching the Jumbotron, but we were 
really
 watching the Jumbotron," receiver Kalif Raymond joked. "Watching him play, watching the impact he has, I mean, he has a whole other element."
As Simmons continues to adjust to life as a Bear and settle in in Chicago, his understanding of Allen's defense will only grow, as will his opportunities to showcase his leadership skills inside the locker room.
Still, five days with his new team was more than enough for Simmons to buy into what the now 6-2 Bears are building and for his new teammates to welcome him into that process. And by the time it was all over Monday night in Seattle, players throughout the locker room had reached a firm consensus on their newest teammate.
"I'm happy he's with us," said Hardy.
"I'm glad he's on our team," said safety Dillon Thieneman.
"I'm happy he's on our side," said quarterback Caleb Williams.

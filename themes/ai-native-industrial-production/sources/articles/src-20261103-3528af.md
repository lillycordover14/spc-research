Russia Highlights Ukraine War Experience to Pitch Upgraded Su-57 to India, but Stealth Doubts and AMCA Priority Persist&nbsp;&nbsp;Defence.in

[page text]
Russia has actively begun leveraging the combat record of its Su-57 "Felon" fighter jet in the ongoing Ukraine conflict to court the Indian Air Force (IAF).

Seeking to capitalise on India’s need to modernise its fighter fleet, Moscow is pitching the Su-57 as a battle-tested, fifth-generation platform capable of bridging immediate capability gaps while India’s indigenous Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) remains in development.

However, lingering questions regarding the jet's true stealth capabilities and India's commitment to self-reliance make the acquisition a complex proposition.

The "Battle-Tested" Pitch and Make in India Offer​
Russian aerospace officials argue that the Su-57 has proven its worth in highly contested airspace over Ukraine.

According to Russian claims, the aircraft has successfully executed long-range precision strikes and missions aimed at suppressing enemy air defences (SEAD).

To sweeten the deal, Moscow has proposed a "Make in India" manufacturing model, offering licensed production at Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) facilities, similar to the successful Su-30MKI programme.

Proponents of the Russian offer note that adopting the Su-57 would offer the IAF valuable continuity.

The Indian military already possesses the airfield, storage, and maintenance infrastructure required for Russian aircraft, which could theoretically ease the integration of the Su-57 into active service.

The Stealth Dilemma​
Despite the marketing push, significant analytical doubts persist regarding the Su-57's stealth profile.

True fifth-generation fighters rely on minimizing their Radar Cross Section (RCS) to remain undetected.

However, patent documents from Sukhoi reportedly describe the Su-57 as having a frontal RCS between 0.1 and 1 square metre.

For context, this is comparable to an older F/A-18 Super Hornet and falls drastically short of the stealth benchmarks set by Western counterparts like the American F-35, which has an estimated RCS of just 0.001 square metres.

While the Su-57 excels in traditional aeronautical metrics—such as raw speed, extreme maneuverability, and dogfighting capability—modern aerial warfare heavily prioritises staying hidden, detecting threats early through advanced sensor fusion, and striking from a distance.

Without independent verification of the Su-57’s electronic warfare suites and sensor integration, military analysts remain sceptical about its ability to dominate a modern, high-tech battlespace.

The AMCA Priority and Fleet Shortages​
For India, the primary focus remains the indigenous AMCA programme, which aims to deliver a medium-weight, deep-penetration stealth fighter tailored specifically to the IAF's requirements.

Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh recently reaffirmed that the AMCA is India's definitive path forward for fifth-generation capabilities.

However, the IAF is currently grappling with a concerning depletion of its fighter squadron strength.

In October 2026, Air Chief Marshal AP Singh acknowledged that while no decision has been made regarding the Russian offer, the IAF might be forced to explore alternative foreign options if the AMCA programme faces significant developmental delays.

The IAF currently requires an induction of nearly two squadrons per year just to arrest the decline in fleet numbers.

Ultimately, any decision to procure the Su-57 will require a stringent cost-benefit analysis by the Ministry of Defence.

The IAF must weigh the immediate availability of a highly maneuverable, heavily armed Russian jet against its noticeable stealth limitations.

Furthermore, India must evaluate the geopolitical and logistical risks of expanding its reliance on foreign combat platforms at a time when national policy is heavily focused on long-term self-reliance.

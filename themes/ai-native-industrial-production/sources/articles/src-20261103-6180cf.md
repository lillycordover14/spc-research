Freshly planted spring-flowering bulbs provide an easy feast for hungry garden critters as temperatures drop, and are often eaten before they even get a chance to grow roots. Tulip bulbs are always the first to be sniffed out as they taste sweet and provide the perfect high-calorie meal for chipmunks as well as squirrels. Crocus bulbs, too, are carb-rich favorites on the garden menu in fall.
If you've experienced squirrels digging up tulip 
bulbs
, you'll know what determined diggers they are. Gardeners are just as dogged, however, and popular tricks to protect 
spring-flowering bulbs
 include laying steel mesh or chicken wire over the top, disguising the appealing scent of tulip and crocus bulbs by planting unappetising allium bulbs alongside, and using bulb baskets. But there's a far easier, cheaper and more effective way to 
stop squirrels eating bulbs
, and it helps the bulbs grow well, too.

The Secret Anti-Squirrel Ingredient
When you're planting bulbs in fall, the magic ingredient to keep these energy balls safe from squirrels is poultry grit made from crushed oyster shells. The sharp, jagged edges of crushed oyster shells means it's painful for paws to dig through, so it provides an effective barrier that stops critters eating 
tulip
 bulbs and even tempting 
crocus
.
What's more, as well as keeping bulbs safe from sharp teeth this natural material improves soil drainage, making bulbs less likely to rot through wet winter weather. Crushed oyster shells also break down very slowly to provide plants with a steady supply of 
calcium
, which supports strong root and stem growth.
Credit: Getty Images
Not all chicken grit is created equal when it comes to protecting fall-planted bulbs, however. Make sure you buy chicken grit like 
Pacific Pearl Oyster Shell Calcium Supplement for Chickens, available from Walmart
, that's made purely from oyster shells as many products are cut with cheaper materials like limestone. Choose crushed shells (sometimes sold as chick grit) over flaked shells as this is both sharper on paws and easier for plant roots and shoots to grow through.
How to Use Oyster Shell Grit When Planting Bulbs
Credit: Nicola Stockden / Gardenpix Ltd
When 
planting bulbs in pots
, simply add an inch-thick layer of chicken grit to the soil surface to deter squirrels from digging, where it will also act as a 
mulch
.

Opt for a double layer of poultry grit when 
planting bulbs in the ground
. Add an inch-thick layer of crushed oyster shells to the bottom of the planting hole, and sit the bulbs right on top. This helps to protects bulbs from burrowing pests who typically attack from below, when a landslide of sharp oyster shells will send them scurrying away in search of more easily accessible food. If voles, field mice and pocket gophers are a particular problem in your garden, you can also pack a shallow layer of grit all around the bulb to fully encase it.
Fill the planting trench with soil as normal but add a second inch-thick layer of oyster shell grit an inch or two below the surface. When squirrels or other critters hit this sharp, gravelly layer, they'll give up digging. This top layer can also help deter raccoons and skunks who don't eat bulbs but are attracted to freshly-dug soil to look for insects and grubs.
Credit: Getty Images
How Oyster Shells Affect Soil
Crushed oyster shells will naturally improve soil drainage and create beneficial pockets of air, loosening up heavy soil. The shells also release calcium as they break down, which plants need to build cell walls.
Adding a heap of oyster shells to your soil can affect its pH levels long-term but this hard material breaks down so slowly, that's not a problem unless you have soil that's already very alkaline. While some bulbs like 
camassia
 (wild hyacinths) prefer acidic soil, more palatable tulip and crocus bulbs both tolerate mildly alkaline soil just fine.

New IAF Chief Focuses on Tejas Mk1A Delays, Mk2 Trials, and AMCA Partnership to Bridge Fighter Squadron Gaps&nbsp;&nbsp;Defence.in

[page text]
Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit, who recently assumed command as the 29th Chief of the Air Staff on October 31, 2026, has outlined a clear and urgent roadmap for the Indian Air Force (IAF).

Navigating a critical shortfall in combat aircraft, the new IAF Chief is placing indigenous fighter jet programmes—specifically the Tejas Mk1A, Tejas Mk2, and the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA)—at the very forefront of his strategic priorities.

The Squadron Challenge and the Indigenous Solution​
The IAF is currently grappling with a significant gap between its active combat fleet and the sanctioned strength of 42 squadrons.

While fully restoring this authorized strength within his tenure—which extends to late 2028—is a daunting challenge, Air Chief Marshal Dixit views domestic manufacturing as the primary avenue to stabilize and eventually rebuild the fleet's combat edge.

Streamlining Tejas Mk1A Deliveries​
The immediate operational focus rests on the Tejas Mk1A programme.

The IAF Chief has prioritized resolving the ongoing developmental and supply chain delays that have impacted the delivery schedule of these light combat aircraft.

Ensuring a steady and uninterrupted flow of the Mk1A into active service is seen as an essential first step to arresting the depletion of fighter squadrons in the near term.

Accelerating the Tejas Mk2 Timeline​
Looking slightly further ahead, the Tejas Mk2 programme is entering its most critical phase.

Intended to be the future backbone of the IAF, the Mk2 will eventually replace the aging fleets of Mirage 2000s, MiG-29s, and Jaguars—representing roughly 180 aircraft set for retirement from the mid-2030s onwards.

The development of the Mk2 is nearing a major milestone, with the first prototype and subsequent ground trials anticipated by March 2027. If momentum is maintained, the aircraft’s maiden flight is projected for the latter half of 2027.

To ensure the IAF can begin integrating the new fighter without waiting for the full multi-year testing cycle to conclude, there are active proposals to induct early limited-production units.

This strategy would allow the Air Force to formulate operational doctrines while the final weapon trials are still being completed. The IAF has already committed to procuring 120 Mk2 jets, with the potential for increased orders if the programme can be successfully accelerated.

Advancing the AMCA Fifth-Generation Fighter​
Under Air Chief Marshal Dixit’s leadership, the IAF is also pushing forward with India's most ambitious aviation project: the fifth-generation Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA).

The process of selecting a Development cum Production Partner (DCPP) has reached an advanced stage following the opening of technical bids.

A final decision and a formal agreement with the chosen industrial partner are expected by March 2027, paving the way for the eventual rollout and maiden flight of the stealth fighter.

A Defining Tenure for Indian Aerospace​
Air Chief Marshal Dixit’s term in office is poised to be a transitional era for Indian military aviation.

By aggressively driving the timelines of the Tejas Mk1A, Mk2, and the AMCA, the IAF aims to not only mitigate immediate squadron shortages but also cement a long-term strategy built on self-reliance and advanced indigenous technology.

TOLEDO, Ohio (WTVG) - Rep. Marcy Kaptur, the longest serving woman in the history of Congress, will retain her seat and secure a 23rd term in the House of Representatives, the Associated Press projects.
Kaptur will defeat former state Rep. Derek Merrin and hold the seat that Republicans viewed as one of their strongest pickup opportunities of 2026.
Kaptur narrowly defeated Merrin in 2024. The AP did not project a winner in that election until Nov. 20, 2024, three weeks after Election Day. Kaptur won by just 2,382 votes out of more than 375,000 cast in 2024.
The Toledo native has held her seat since 1983. Her district has been redrawn several times since then, including leading up to this election to make it more favorable for Republicans. 
“I am deeply honored that the people of Northwest Ohio have placed their trust in me to represent them in the 120th Congress,”
 
said Congresswoman Kaptur. “Despite millions in outside spending and a map statehouse Republicans redrew three times to make this one of the toughest districts in our country, voters delivered us a clear victory last night. I return to the House more determined than ever to deliver for the people of Northwest Ohio — bringing home the investments our hardworking people and communities need and deserve, lowering costs for working families, rooting out corruption, and building an economy that rewards work, not just wealth. Here in Northwest Ohio, we make and grow what makes and grows America. As long as I have the privilege to serve in Congress, I will never stop fighting to ensure the hardworking people who power this nation have a voice in our future through the People’s House.”

Marcy Kaptur faces Derek Merrin in the 2026 race for Ohio's 9th District.

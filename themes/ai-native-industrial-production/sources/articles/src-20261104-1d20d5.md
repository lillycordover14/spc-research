Twelve South AirFly View Review: Bluetooth for Planes, Trains and Automobiles&nbsp;&nbsp;CNET

[page text]
These days, taking a flight has a lot of the perks you’d expect on the ground: You have Wi-Fi, coffee and charging facilities. Yet, while things are getting better in the audio department, Bluetooth is still relatively rare. The AirFly View from Twelve South is a tiny Bluetooth transmitter that lets you connect your BT headphones to any source with a standard 3.5mm headphone jack. The most obvious use is to connect your 
noise-canceling earbuds
 or 
headphones
 to the seatback in-flight entertainment systems on most aircraft. Alternately, the View can receive Bluetooth so you can stream audio from your phone to any device with a 3.5mm AUX input.

The View lets you connect two pairs of headphones at the same time in case you and your travel partner (or seatmate stranger, make new friends!) want to watch the same movie or show. A clever disappearing screen makes setup easy. It has 
Auracast
, so you can broadcast to others for your own listening party, though obviously this isn’t something you’d want or need to do on a plane.

The View is easier to use and connect than the 
Twelve South AirFly Pro 2
, but that convenience comes with a higher price of $80 (versus the $60 Pro 2). The View also has a lower battery life at 16 hours, but that’s still plenty for all but the longest of long hauls. The only real issue is that more and more planes do have Bluetooth at every seat, though that’s not guaranteed, regardless of what the airline claims before your flight.

I fly a lot, usually long flights over 12 hours. For me, noise-canceling headphones are a must. The problem is, nearly all noise-canceling headphones are Bluetooth. How are you supposed to listen to the in-flight selection of movies and shows? An AirFly is one option. I reviewed the company’s 
Pro 2
 a few years ago. The View is the latest version and aims to solve the one, admittedly minor, issue with the Pro 2: setup.

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

The View has a small screen on the front that’s cleverly hidden when it’s off. This makes setup and adjusting settings far easier. You might be thinking, “How hard could setup be for something that’s just Bluetooth?” Technically, you’re right. It was easy to connect headphones to the Pro 2. The issue is forgetting which combination of buttons are required to connect when you’ve replaced your headphones in the months, or years, between AirFly uses. The time spent using trial and error to connect your new earbuds in your plane seat is obviously better spent getting as comfortable as possible and zoning out to a movie.

So the View leads you through setup step-by-step and shows you the name of the connected headphones. This convenience comes at a $20 price premium but honestly, that doesn’t seem like a big deal to me. However, I am possibly a narrow niche of people who replace headphones quite often (I do review them, after all), travel enough that something like the AirFly makes sense, 
and
 I regularly travel on airlines that haven’t converted their fleet to Bluetooth in-flight entertainment. That’s not to say that if you don’t fit into all three of those categories yourself you shouldn’t consider the View, just that one of the cheaper options might make more sense. More on those in a moment.

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

Included with the View is a USB-C-to-USB-A charging cable and a dual-mono airplane adapter that’s less common, but still found on some older, and especially non-US based, long-haul aircraft. These thingies can live in the included hard-shell case that seems to me a little oversized for the AirFly, but I tend to travel light, so I might be a little biased.

Flyin’ high

Connecting headphones to the View is as easy as the company says, basically just like connecting to your phone or digital media player. The screen does make it easy – though, as I mentioned, it’s not like the older versions make it difficult. The View can transmit to two pairs of Bluetooth headphones at the same time and both of their names show up on the screen. Volume buttons on the side of the View do double duty navigating the menus. There’s 
aptX
 for potentially better audio quality for headphones that also have it (most non-Apple products).

Twelve South

The View also has 
Auracast
, a criminally under-marketed feature that many earbuds and phone (again, not Apple ones) already have. It lets a single Bluetooth transmitter act sort of like a tiny radio tower, letting anyone nearby with Auracast-enabled headphones to listen in. Not particularly useful on a plane of course, but since the AirFly has a standard 3.5mm plug you can connect it to basically any audio source and create your own little silent — to anyone without Auracast-capable headphones anyway — listening party.

Another use for the View is as a Bluetooth receiver. If you have an older car or speaker system you can send audio from your phone wirelessly to the View, adding BT connectivity and therefore streaming audio like Spotify to anything with an AUX input.  

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

Sound-wise the AirFly sounds, well, fine. Nothing better or worse than any other Bluetooth connection. While technically it has shorter battery life than its cheaper predecessors, the View has a battery indicator on its screen, so you’re not surprised by a low battery. Also, it charges quickly. Even if the battery is low when you take off, you shouldn’t have too long of a wait to be able to use it for the rest of your flight.

View, Pro 2 or… nothing? 

The View
 is the most expensive AirFly. The others, including the Pro and Pro 2, do basically the same thing. I’ve traveled with one of those for many years, and I’ve needed it infrequently enough that remembering how to connect the AirFly to new headphones has happened to me on several occasions. So the extra $20 for the View feels to me like an easy, if admittedly minor, quality-of-life upgrade. Also, I really like how cool the “disappearing” screen looks. When it’s off, the View doesn’t look like it has a screen at all. Compare the image above to the one below.

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

If that all seems like overkill to you, especially if you don’t travel a lot and want something “just in case,” one of the cheaper AirFlys like the 
Pro 2
 or the 
single-connection SE
 are probably fine. It’s also worth considering that many airlines are adding Bluetooth at every seat. This is probably a trend that will continue, but there’s no guarantee. Like I said above, I’ve only needed an AirFly a few times in the last few years, but I did need it. Even if the airline claims your upcoming flight has Bluetooth connectivity, there’s no guarantee you’ll get that specific plane since aircraft swaps happen all the time. Which is to say, this is a good backup to have regardless and certainly beats staring at the back of a seat with nothing but engine roar for 12 hours for company.

UNC’s Path to Bowl Eligibility: Ranking the Tar Heels’ Last 4 Opponents&nbsp;&nbsp;247Sports

[page text]
CHAPEL HILL — After opening the season 4-2, 
North Carolina
 looked like it could reel off seven or eight wins by the end of the year. The Tar Heels appeared well on their way to bowl six wins and bowl eligiblity.
With a fifth win seemingly within reach against 
Syracuse
, a team that was winless in the ACC coming into 
Kenan Stadium
, UNC needed just one more victory in its final five games to become bowl eligible.
Instead, the Orange outlasted the Tar Heels, and UNC fell in overtime. And last weekend, UNC's upset bid was slashed late against No. 1 Miami.
Now, UNC needs to win two of its final four games, none of which are cakewalks, to reach a bowl game.
Here are the Tar Heels' final four games, ranked from easiest to toughest on paper.
No. 4 (easiest) UNC @ UConn Nov. 7 at noon
The Tar Heels' best chance at getting one win closer to six is this weekend in East Hartford, Connecticut.
As an FBS independent, UConn is no pushover, though. UNC opened as a one-touchdown favorite on the road.
The Huskies are 4-4 on the season. They beat Lafayette, Southern Miss, Temple and UMass, while their losses were against Maryland, Miami OH, Syracuse in overtime and Air Force.
UConn's offense, led by quarterback 
Kalieb Osborne
, averages 34.4 points per game. Osborne has 21 total touchdowns this year (14 passing and seven rushing). He has only thrown two interceptions in eight games.
But the Huskies struggle defensively, allowing more than 25 points per game. UNC's offense, after scoring 35 points against Miami, will need to build on that performance this weekend to beat the Huskies.
No. 3 UNC @ Virginia Nov. 21 with time TBD
After Virginia dominated NC State in Week 0, 34-8, the Cavaliers looked like they would be a force in the ACC.
Since then, though, quarterback 
Beau Pribula
 and Virginia have struggled. Pribula has only thrown for 200 passing yards once against Power Four competition.
The Cavaliers, who sit at 4-5, including 2-4 in ACC play, have losses to West Virginia, Florida State, SMU, Duke and Wake Forest. Virginia's only Power Four wins were NC State and Syracuse, which it beat 28-25.
UNC has won its last two matchups in Charlottesville, and the Nov. 21 matchup will give the Tar Heels an opportunity to extend that streak to three games.
No. 2 UNC vs. NC State Nov. 29 with time TBD
No 
Dave Doeren
, no problem?
After Doeren was fired by NC State following its loss to Stanford, the Wolfpack came together and downed California 31-10.
Like the Tar Heels, NC State holds a 4-4 record with its key win over Louisville.
The Wolfpack are 10th in the ACC, and when they travel to Chapel Hill, the emotions of the rivalry will make this a tougher game than what might show on paper.
UNC has lost five straight games to NC State, so regardless of the head coach, the rivalry aspect will add pressure for both sides.
No. 1 (toughest) UNC vs. Louisville Nov. 14 at either 7 p.m. or 7:30 p.m.
There was a lot of preseason hype surrounding 
Jeff Brohm
's Louisville team.
The Cardinals took Ole Miss down to the wire in Week 1, falling 41-38. But back-to-back losses against Wake Forest and NC State put a major dent in the CFP hopes for Louisville.
At 5-3, including third in the ACC, the Cardinals will pose a big challenge to UNC, especially its offense.
Led by quarterback 
Lincoln Kienholz
, Louisville's offense sits No. 5 nationally, scoring more than 40 points per game.
The Tar Heels will need their defense to step up and limit Kienholz, who's a true dual-threat quarterback, making Louisville the toughest opponent remaining on UNC's schedule.

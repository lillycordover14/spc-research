UNC-UConn Preview: Tar Heels Aim to Get Back on Track for Final Stretch of 2026&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
North Carolina (4-4, 3-3 ACC) will look to shake its two-game losing spurt with a trip to Connecticut on Saturday to square off with UConn (4-4). The two teams met most recently in the 2024 Fenway Bowl, where the Huskies secured their only win in the series.

Here is everything you need to know before the Tar Heels’ final nonconference game of the 2026 season:

How to Watch

Location: Pratt & Whitney Stadium — East Hartford, C.T.
Time: Saturday, Noon ET
TV: CBS Sports Network — 
Watch on Fubu (Try for Free)
Crew: Jordan Kent, Robert Turbin, Tiffany Blackmon

Analyzing the Huskies

UConn’s nine-point loss at Air Force last weekend dropped the Huskies to 4-4 in head coach 
Jason Candle
‘s first season in East Hartford. Not affiliated with a conference, UConn has matched up with teams from seven different leagues, including a 24-point loss to Maryland and a one-point overtime loss against Syracuse.

The Huskies’ four wins have come by an average of 27.5 points per game, while each of their losses, excluding Maryland, are within nine points. However, according to ESPN Power Index, Connecticut’s strength of schedule ranks 129th of 138 FBS teams.

Candle’s unit is only 18 spots behind North Carolina in SP+, though, despite the Huskies’ tumble to 71st after Saturday’s loss. Connecticut’s offensive efficiency ranks above the Tar Heels’ at 49th, while its defense is 99th nationally. UConn has allowed 25.3 points per game, including 40 per game against power conference opponents.

The Huskies throw for 246.9 yards per game and rush for an additional 190.8, a balanced offense which has scored 21 or more points in seven of eight of its contests. Building on its stats, Pro Football Focus’ numbers are rather fond of Connecticut, ranking its total offense 20th (82.1 grade), highlighted with the 12th best pass grade (87.0) and the top-ranked rush grade (92.7) in the country. PFF grades its defense outside the top-100. 

Positions, Players to Watch

Quarterback 
Kalieb Osborne
, who came over from Toledo with Candle, is a big reason behind the Huskies’ offensive efficiency. The 6-foot-4, 218-pound junior has 1,857 pass yards, 14 touchdowns and two interceptions in eight games, paired with 330 yards and seven more scores on the ground. While 
Cyncir Bowers
 leads UConn in rushing with 571 yards, Osborne has more attempts, averaging 11.1 per game.

“He’s physically tough,” Bill Belichick said on Monday. “Hard guy to tackle. Runs hard. He runs like a running back. But I mean, he’s got a lot of long passes too. He’s got a good arm, and he can attack all the different spots in the field: the sideline, deep ball, end cuts, all that. He’s very resourceful.”

Connecticut has five different game-leading receivers, alluding to the spreading of the wealth that Osborne does with the football through the air. Senior 
Shamar Porter
 leads the Huskies in receiving with 308 yards, but his 16 catches this season — primarily as a deep-ball threat — hardly outpace four other pass-catchers who also have 10 or more grabs. Sophomore 
Ryder Treadway
 has a team-high 20 receptions this season.

One reason why PFF grades the totality of Candle’s offense weaker than its parts is UConn’s struggling offensive line that’s allowed 25 sacks this year, tied for 129th in the country. The Huskies’ have given up eight combined sacks in their two power conference games.

According to SP+, Connecticut will be the worst FBS defense North Carolina has seen this season, beginning with their warts in stopping the pass. Ranked 115th in passing yards allowed per game, the Huskies do not generate much pressure, and in turn, do not force many turnovers, tallying four interceptions — two coming in one game — this season.

Senior linebacker 
John Lista
 leads UConn in tackles with 58, five of which resulted in a loss. His counterpart, 
K’Von Sherman
, has 3.5 sacks, 8 tackles for loss and three fumbles forced, propelling the linebacker tandem to being a bright spot amid a struggling overall unit.

The Matchup

Saturday’s trip up north will present an opportunity for 
Billy Edwards Jr.
 and the Tar Heels’ offense to build off of back-to-back 30-point performances and the best game of Edwards’ career against Miami. But the Huskies may have a similar line of thinking with their high-powered offense against UNC’s seemingly regressing defense. Carolina has allowed 69 points in the last two weeks.

The only quarterback to eclipse five carries against the Tar Heels this season is Clemson’s Tait Reynolds. Osborne will certainly add his name to that list, and all eyes will be on UNC’s ability to contain him — especially after allowing the Hurricanes to convert 8-of-13 on third and fourth downs last weekend.

Carolina worked nine pass-catchers into the mix in its last outing, a large step forward from what was seemingly the 
Jordan Shipp
 and 
Jelani Thurman
 show for a handful of weeks. But UNC’s rushing attack, while keeping the offense ahead of the sticks last weekend, still failed to reach 100 yards in three of the last five weeks. UConn’s middling run defense presents an opportunity to flip that narrative.

The Huskies’ 68 penalties are among the most in the country this season, amounting for 620 yards, tied for 123rd in the FBS, presenting another storyline. If that trend continues, it doesn’t bode well for the home team, as the visiting Tar Heels are one of the least penalized teams, tallying 34 flags for 259 yards.

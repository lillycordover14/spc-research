Boston Dynamics Upgrades Stretch Robot for High-Volume Logistics&nbsp;&nbsp;Thomasnet

[page text]
Boston Dynamics has introduced 
Stretch Gen 2
, an updated version of its autonomous trailer-unloading robot designed to improve reliability and simplify maintenance in high-volume logistics operations. The redesign draws on more than 100,000 operating hours and tens of millions of boxes handled by first-generation Stretch robots.

Hardware Changes Target Downtime

Stretch Gen 2 incorporates automotive-grade connectors, an upgraded gripper, and continuous-rotation joints designed to reduce mechanical stress. 
Boston Dynamics
 also cut internal cabling by 81%, removing approximately a half-mile of wiring to address a common source of electrical faults.

New exterior panel materials and a recessed emergency stop are intended to reduce damage and unnecessary safety stoppages. The changes target issues identified through operating data from deployed robots.

Faster Repairs and Service

The robot’s modular design is intended to make component replacement easier. Boston Dynamics says a full swerve drive replacement can now take about one hour, compared with a full day previously.

Standardized fasteners, simplified cabling, and onboard diagnostic tools are also designed to shorten repair times. These features could allow customer maintenance teams to handle more routine service without relying as heavily on outside support.

Performance and Software Updates

Stretch Gen 2 can unload up to 1,000 cases per hour, depending on factors such as package weight and item stacking. Its battery is designed to last two shifts, while a fast charger can reach 90% capacity in about 75 minutes. Onboard lighting supports operation inside dark trailers.

The robot also uses a redesigned computing platform that can receive software updates to improve package detection, motion planning, picking, and environmental modeling. Boston Dynamics says fleet-wide performance can improve without hardware changes, extending the role of software in maintaining logistics automation over time.

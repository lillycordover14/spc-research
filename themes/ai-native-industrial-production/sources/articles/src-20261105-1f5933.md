Diana Collins is still 'trying to figure it out' as Kentucky's PG, but she stepped up in game one&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
Not long before the 2026-27 season tipped off for Kentucky women’s basketball on Monday, head coach 
Kenny Brooks
 said the point guard position was going to be run 
“by committee”
, at least early into the schedule. That’s not something he’s had to deal with over the last several years while coaching 
Georgia Amoore
 for five seasons and 
Tonie Morgan
 for one. 

This time around, Brooks has two options at the lead guard spot: three-year college veteran 
Diana Collins
 and five-star freshman 
Maddyn Greenway
. 

At least through one game for the Wildcats (an 85-42 steamrolling of a less-talented Morehead State group), the point guard “by committee” approach worked quite well. Most eyes were on the young and talented Greenway, who impressed in her college debut, but Collins stood out, too. 

“I’m so proud — what was it, a week ago we had our scrimmage? I was mad at her,” Brooks said of Collins after Monday night’s season-opening win. “I thought she came out tonight, she dictated the tempo, she got us going on both ends. She stepped up. She knocked down a couple threes for us. Very happy for her. Very happy for her because she’s trying to figure it out.”

Collins finished her night with 12 points on a perfect 5-5 shooting clip, including a pair of made three-pointers. She got the start at point guard, playing 22 minutes on the night while Greenway came off the bench for 22 minutes of her own. Collins is more of a score-first player compared to Greenway, which was put on display with her zero-assist performance, but she also didn’t turn the ball over a single time. 

For what it’s worth, Collins led Kentucky in plus-minus at +36. She had no problem flying past Morehead State defenders for easy shots at the rim. At 5-foot-10, she was tall enough to shoot over her defenders when getting good looks. Staying in front of Morehead State ball handlers wasn’t an issue, either. 

Remember, Collins played one season at Ohio State and two more at Alabama. She was a full-time starter with the Crimson Tide in 2025-26, averaging 8.4 points per outing on good efficiency for a guard. She went for 16 points in a first-round NCAA Tournament win over Rhode Island before dropping 14 in a one-point loss to Louisville in the Round of 32. 

So while Collins is still learning how to fit into Brooks’ style and system, she’s certainly no stranger to producing in high-level college hoops. As her season at Kentucky goes along, she should only get better. Even if Greenway is the PG of the future, Collins is here to help ease that transition for the rookie while also helping win games right now. 

“Coming in and following in the footsteps of Tonie and Georgia — like, Georgia figured me out, and it took her five years,” Brooks continued. “Tonie walked out of here in a year, and she was confused, but she loves me now. 
DC’s trying to figure it out on the fly.
 And it’s not always an easy thing to do. But her demeanor tonight was great, and I was very happy for her.”

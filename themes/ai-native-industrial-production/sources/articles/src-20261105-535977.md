Twelve flights have been diverted across Europe following a series of bomb threats.
Thursday’s disruption comes days after similar incidents in which eight planes, including three from the UK, 
were grounded
 over what Britain dismissed as a “malicious hoax”.
Three of Thursday’s incidents involved flights bound for Italy.
An Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome was diverted to Normandy following a bomb alert, the French prime minister’s office said.
The office of Sébastien Lecornu, the French prime minister, said all 151 passengers and six crew members were safe, and that military bomb defusal teams were examining the plane.

				An Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome was diverted to Normandy 
			

Credit

A Finnair flight bound for Helsinki landed in Rome after a threat was reported on board, while a Wizz Air service from Milan Malpensa to Warsaw was diverted to Vienna.
A Polish Airlines flight from Warsaw to Brussels landed at Zaventem airport in Belgium after the crew received a “suspicious report concerning a flight to Brussels airport”.
Three more aircraft were forced to land at airports in Bulgaria following bomb threats. 
Two of the planes – travelling from Copenhagen and Madrid respectively – landed in Sofia, the broadcaster bTV, citing the interior minister, reported.
A third aircraft, flying from London, landed in Plovdiv, according to reports. Four planes flying to Prague had been the subject of bomb threats and were preparing to land, according to Czech police. 

				Emergency response vehicles near the runway at Plovdiv airport
			

A Wizz Air flight from Milan to Bucharest was also targeted, Romanian intelligence services said.
On Thursday morning, Spanish authorities said three flights at Madrid were affected by hoax threats.
None of the threats reported this week have been found to be genuine, despite triggering dozens of unplanned landings as well and escorts by fighter jets.
The EU’s transport commissioner said it was “too early to draw conclusions” on whether Thursday’s spate of bomb threats were linked.
Apostolos Tzitzikostas said in a message posted on LinkedIn that he had asked EU security authorities “to cooperate closely with airport operators, airlines and law enforcement authorities” over the bomb threats.

				A still from video shot by a passenger of a grounded plane at Warsaw Chopin airport after take-off was aborted, showing military personnel and dogs outside the aircraft

On Tuesday, at least seven planes were subjected to threats. A flight to Sofia was similarly affected on Monday.
An airport in Warsaw was briefly shut and the Italian air force accompanied one of the flights to Rome. An Israel-bound cruise ship was also searched for bombs in Greece.
A British Government spokesman told The Telegraph that the threats against the UK flights had been a “malicious hoax”, though it was not clear how they had been orchestrated.
Speculation quickly centred on Moscow, with a former MI6 intelligence officer telling The Telegraph that it was his first thought when he learned of the bomb threats, which he said fitted “a pattern of behaviour” by the Kremlin.

The flight disruption follows a week of chaos, with dozens of supermarket 
fridges across Europe hacked on Monday
. The attacks caused large amounts of food to spoil after hackers gained remote access to in-store fridge units and turned up their temperatures, in some cases to as much as 80C.
Hackers also 
targeted France’s national blood bank
, raising the temperature of its cold storage rooms to boiling point.
Facilities at Coolworld were heated to 110C overnight on Sunday after hackers gained access to the temperature settings remotely, Le Canard enchaîné reported. The rooms are normally kept at -30C to preserve donor blood samples.
An on-duty technician detected the unusual rise in temperatures. France’s health ministry said there had been no consequences for patients and no plasma had been destroyed.
The Kremlin said European media accusations of Russian involvement in ‌cyber attacks on supermarkets were not backed up by any evidence.
Dmitry ⁠Peskov, a Kremlin spokesman, told reporters on Thursday: “These are absurd claims. As always, they are backed up by absolutely ‌no reasoning, no evidence whatsoever, and yet here we have ⁠such ⁠baseless accusations against our country, implicating it in everything.”

However, pro-Russian hackers have claimed responsibility for the cyber attacks on fridges.
The pro-Russian hacking collective NoName057(16) claimed to have worked with Z-Pentest, another pro-Russian hacker group, to launch the attack.
A video claiming responsibility for the attack was posted on Telegram, according to the Danish media outlet DR.
In the video, titled Operation Chronicle: Danfoss, the hackers claimed to have “dealt a crushing blow to Danfoss and Dixell XWEB equipment”.
Danfoss and Dixell XWEB were the refrigeration companies targeted in the cyber attack.
“We didn’t even need malware for that. We just exploited the systems’ built-in features,” the hackers said.

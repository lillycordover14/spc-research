According to the preliminary September Monthly Labour Survey (covering establishments with five or more employees) released by Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare on the 6th, real wages adjusted for price changes rose 2.2% year-on-year, securing a ninth consecutive month of gains. Slower inflation, combined with base-pay increases driven by Shunto wage negotiations, contributed to the result, and nominal wage growth outpacing prices is becoming entrenched as 2026 progresses.

The ninth straight monthly gain is the longest streak since the 12 consecutive increases logged between February 2016 and January 2017, roughly a decade ago. The ministry has signaled it will keep a close watch on whether the virtuous cycle of wage growth continues, alongside price trends.

Nominal wages post fastest growth in 34 and a half years

Total cash earnings (nominal wages), which combine base pay, overtime and bonuses, rose 4.6% year-on-year to ¥311,409 (approximately $2,000). This marks the 57th consecutive month of gains, with the growth rate the highest since January 1997. Growth of 3% or more has now persisted for eight months, the first such run since March 1992, or 34 years and six months.

Breaking down the components, scheduled earnings, centered on base pay, rose 3.7% to ¥279,182 (approximately $1,800), with growth of 3% or more continuing for nine consecutive months — the longest in 33 years and 11 months. Non-scheduled earnings such as overtime pay also rose 3.6%, reflecting labor shortages. Special earnings including bonuses surged 32.6% to ¥12,258 (approximately $78), lifting the overall figure.

The Consumer Price Index (all items excluding imputed rent for owner-occupied housing), used primarily to calculate real wages, rose 2.4%. This was slower than the 3.4% increase in the same month a year earlier, and nominal wage growth exceeding the inflation rate pushed real wages into positive territory. When deflated using the headline Consumer Price Index (up 2.2%), real wages rose 2.3%, marking a tenth consecutive month of gains.

That said, the erosion of purchasing power accumulated during the earlier bout of inflation has not been fully reversed. Many observers believe the level remains on a recovery path.

Summer bonuses up 5.3%, with disparities across industries

A special tally of summer 2026 bonuses released at the same time showed that the average per worker at establishments that paid bonuses was ¥448,765 (approximately $2,800), up 5.3% from a year earlier. Among establishments with 30 or more employees, the average was ¥522,724 (approximately $3,300), up 5.2%. The figures reflect strong corporate earnings, but it should be noted that they are averages for bonus-paying establishments, not for all workers.

By employment type, scheduled earnings for full-time workers rose 4.0% to ¥356,516 (approximately $2,300), while those for part-time workers rose 4.2% to ¥110,493 (approximately $700). Hourly pay for part-time workers rose 5.4% to ¥1,463 (approximately $9.3), exceeding the year-earlier level for the 63rd consecutive month.

By industry, total cash earnings in the finance and insurance sector stood out with a 12.5% increase. Scheduled earnings there also rose 12.0%, a substantial gain at the regular-pay level, indicating the increase was not driven by bonuses alone. Information and communications rose 6.8%, manufacturing 6.0%, transport and postal activities 4.9%, wholesale and retail trade 3.9%, and medical, health care and welfare 3.7%. Meanwhile, living-related and personal services and amusement services fell 1.2% from a year earlier, highlighting the divergence across industries.

Sustainability hinges on 2027 Shunto

Real wages in 2026 have consistently stayed positive year-on-year, from a 0.7% gain in January to the preliminary 2.2% increase in September. August was revised to 1.6%, July was 2.0% and June was 2.2%. Although the growth rate fluctuates month to month, the pattern of nominal wages outpacing prices continues.

On the statistical front, it should be noted that a partial replacement of surveyed establishments in January 2026 created a discontinuity of ¥1,582 (a 0.5% decline) in total cash earnings. Even on a common-establishment basis, which tracks the same establishments, total cash earnings rose 4.4% and scheduled earnings rose 3.3%, confirming the upward trend.

The focus for gauging the sustainability of wage growth is the 2027 Shunto. Last month, Rengo announced a basic framework calling for wage demands including regular pay increases centered on 6%, with a floor of 5% or more. If inflation settles in the 1-2% range, the positive trend in real wages is likely to be maintained. However, if prices accelerate again depending on overseas economic conditions, exchange rates or raw material prices, that could offset nominal wage growth.

The market is watching whether improved real wages will lead to a recovery in personal consumption. Whether wages and prices both move in a stable manner will influence the level of demands in the 2027 Shunto and corporate pricing decisions. The ministry has also indicated it will monitor the situation together with price trends, and statistics from next month onward will provide the basis for judging sustainability.

Paisley mum desperate to save daughter's eye after brain tumour mistaken for squint
Three-year-old Amelia Morisson received the devastating diagnosis in August.

A 
Paisley 
mum is battling to save her toddler's eye after a brain tumour was mistaken for a squint.
‌
‌
Amelia Morisson, three, was diagnosed with an optic pathway glioma (OPG) 
tumour 
in the summer. The young girl had been suffering from a squint in her left eye.
‌
Mum, Aimee Oneil, has taken the youngster to the ophthalmology department at the Royal Alexandra Hospital where medics found she was blind in her eye and needed glasses.
‌
However, 16 weeks later, Amelia's eye began to bulge. She was taken in for an urgent CT scan which found a tumour and neurofibromatosis type 1 - a genetic condition that causes non-cancerous masses to grow on nerve tissue.
Mum-of-three Aimee, 29, told the 
Record
: "It came as a shock. Amelia spent 16 weeks wearing glasses.
"Medics had several opportunities to spot her tumour but dismissed it as her just needing glasses. We feel that they missed several chances to spot this tumour.
‌
"The blindness in her left eye should have raised red flags. It wouldn't have prevented her diagnosis, but it could have brought the issue to the forefront much quicker."
View 2 Images
Little Amelia Morisson
(Image: Daily Record)
‌
Aimee was given several options following Amelia's diagnosis, including removing her eye with no prosthetic until she is about 12.
Now, the toddler is trialling new drug Trametinib in a bid to save her eye.
‌
Trametinib is a targeted oral cancer medication that helps to stop or slow the growth of cancer cells, but can also be used to treat benign tumours.
It is understood that there are only 17 people in the UK currently trialling the drug, including Amelia.
‌
Aimee says she remains hopeful the drug will work, if not her daughter could still lose her eye.
She added: "At an age when she should be carefree, playing with her toys and enjoying being a little girl, she is facing a journey that no parent ever imagines for their child. We are hoping and praying that this drug can give her the best possible outcome.
‌
"The risks associated with the other options, which could cause her to lose all vision in her right eye, cause her to be become epileptic or suffer a stroke, were just too much to gamble with. We want to give Amelia the best possible chance at keeping her vision and the best possible chance at recovery.
"As her family, we wish we could take all of this away and let her enjoy the childhood she deserves."
‌

A fundraiser has been launched to support the tot's family as she travels to and from the Queen Elizabeth University Hospital in Glasgow every week for bloods and heart checks.
‌
Aimee added: "The money raised will help towards hospital travel, accommodation, everyday expenses and the additional costs that come with caring for a little girl undergoing treatment. We want to give Amelia the best possible support, make special memories with her and make sure she never feels alone in this fight.
"Amelia is so much more than her diagnosis. She is our beautiful, precious little girl, and she deserves a future filled with happiness, laughter and love."
‌
A spokesperson for NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde said: "While we cannot discuss the details of any individual patient’s care, we would like to apologise to Ms O’Neil if she feels any aspect of her daughter’s care fell below the standard she would expect.
"We would encourage Ms O’Neil to contact us so we can discuss her concerns in more detail."

Yet Smith, after Wednesday's practice, didn't wish to entertain the argument that there are plenty of things he has done well, to the point where he is on track to challenge the achievements of some of the icon QBs in franchise history this season.
"I just want to win the game, and as of right now, my focus is on the Chiefs, and what we can do to go out there and give ourselves to win the game in a tough environment on the road," Smith said in response to questions about him potentially rewriting a lot of the Jets' single-season quarterback record book. "Anything else is outside of the realm of what I'm thinking about."
Such single-mindedness is one of several things that HC Aaron Glenn appreciates about the juice that Geno has brought to the Jets.
"He's really commanding that offense the way you want a quarterback to do," Glenn said. "The dropback game, he's doing a hell of a job, the play-action game, he's doing a hell of a job. As all the Jets acknowledge, the challenge is daunting in tackling the Chiefs, who figure to be ornery at home after starting the season 5-0, then hitting a two-game skid with road losses to the Seahawks and Broncos. But more than the number, as Smith said, he has belief in his team and his team has belief in him.
"I think he would tell you he still has room to improve, and that's what practice is for, to be able to do that," Glenn said, adding his admiration of what Smith has brought to the Jets: "I'm proud of where he's at."

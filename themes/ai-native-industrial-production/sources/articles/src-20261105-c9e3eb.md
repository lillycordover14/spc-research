"Over the last twelve months, 49% of online orders came from outside Spain"&nbsp;&nbsp;Floral Daily

[page text]
A family florist in southern Spain has been making bouquets by hand since 1975. Today, Persa Flores in Albox, Almería, serves customers across mainland Spain, with almost half of its online orders paid for from abroad. Its combination of traditional floristry and a personal online shopping experience is helping the business reach customers far beyond its hometown.
 
Persa Flores has come a long way since Maricarmen Sánchez and her husband, Paco, opened the family flower shop in 1975. What started as a neighborhood florist in Albox, in the province of Almería, is now also an online flower business connecting people abroad with recipients in Spain.
 
Today, Maricarmen's youngest son, José Pérez Sánchez, runs the business, while his cousin Jun manages the online store. Despite the expansion into e-commerce, the heart of the operation remains unchanged: bouquets are still made by hand in the original Albox workshop.
 
"My mother opened the shop as a neighborhood florist, and the shop is still the same workshop," says José. "What has changed is where the bouquets go. Since my cousin Jun built our online store, we have been able to sell across mainland Spain, in both Spanish and English."
 
The international dimension of the business is particularly striking. Over the last twelve months, 49% of online orders were paid for from outside Spain. Moreover, 47% of checkouts were completed in English, reflecting a customer base that often lives abroad but wants to send flowers to someone in Spain.

Bringing the personal touch to online flower orders
For Persa Flores, one of the challenges of selling flowers online is making sure customers feel confident about what they are sending, even when they cannot see the bouquet in person.
 
The florist has developed a straightforward approach: every bouquet is made to order, photographed before dispatch, and the picture is sent to the buyer. This gives customers a view of the actual arrangement rather than simply relying on a product image on the website.
 
"Each bouquet is made by hand to order in our workshop," José explains. "Before it leaves, we photograph it and send the picture to the buyer. That way, even if the customer is abroad, they can see the flowers that will be delivered."
 
To protect the flowers during transport, each arrangement is packed in a protective box with water for the journey. This allows Persa Flores to combine its workshop-based production with deliveries across mainland Spain.
 
The photo service is especially relevant for international customers. When someone orders flowers from another country, they cannot visit the shop or check the arrangement themselves. Seeing the finished bouquet before it leaves provides an additional personal connection.
© Persa Flores
 
Red roses remain the favorite
Although the online business has expanded its reach, customer preferences remain familiar. Roses are the most popular flowers, with red roses leading the way. Customers order them both in smaller bunches and by the dozen.
 
There are also a few popular additions that help make an order more personal. Vases, chocolates and instant photo prints are among the extras customers most frequently add to their bouquets.
 
Persa Flores sources flowers from different markets depending on availability and seasonality. "We buy from Spanish growers for the flowers that are in season," says José. "For the rest, we import stems, mostly from the Netherlands."
 
Looking ahead: keeping the workshop at the center
While Persa Flores continues to develop its online business, José's plans do not involve moving away from the traditional way of making flowers. Instead, the focus is on combining the existing hands-on approach with improvements to the digital customer experience.
 
One of the projects that has recently launched is an app that automatically sends every buyer a photograph of their bouquet before it leaves the workshop, along with a link to follow the delivery. Growing the English-language side of the business is another priority, particularly to serve people living abroad who want to send flowers to friends and family in Spain.

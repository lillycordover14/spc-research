NAPLES, Fla (WINK) — Quail Creek Country Club in Naples celebrated the grand opening of its three new padel courts Thursday, bringing a sport already popular in Europe and Latin America to the region.
The celebration included a ribbon-cutting ceremony and a professional exhibition showcasing the fast-paced game, which combines elements of tennis, squash and pickleball.

Played in doubles on a smaller, enclosed court with artificial turf, padel features glass walls that players can bounce the ball off during rallies. The sport also uses specialized rackets and balls.
"I think that when you look at it, first of all, it's unique, it's new. It's fun. You have the glass walls that you can play off of. But what I like the most about it is you can play at your own pace," said Chris Evans, Quail Creek's general manager.

Evans said the project was spearheaded by the club's director of court sports, Jose Pastrello, a Florida Gulf Coast University graduate and native of Brazil who wanted to introduce the sport to Southwest Florida.
Pastrello said the courts were completed in late July, allowing members to try the game ahead of the official grand opening.
"So, we bring a lot of people out, but once they touch the ball, and they try it, and they see how much fun it is. They come back," Pastrello said.
The sport is gaining momentum nationwide. According to the Sports & Fitness Industry Association, more than 1 million Americans played padel in 2025, with participation growing 14.5% in early 2026.
Quail Creek says it is the first private country club in Southwest Florida to offer padel, adding the sport to its existing tennis, pickleball and other court sports programs.
Evans said the new facility is already attracting interest from members who play other racket sports.

"Some people have been out playing, but you see the pickleball players coming over and flocking over here," Evans said.
The club plans to offer lessons, leagues and social events, with the possibility of hosting larger tournaments in the future.
For Pastrello, the goal is to introduce more people to a sport he believes can appeal to players of all ages and abilities.

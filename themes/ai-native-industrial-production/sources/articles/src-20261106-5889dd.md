ANN ARBOR, Mich. — The decision was surprising, the explanation even more so.
 Sarah Strong
 picked up her second foul with 2:55 still remaining in the first quarter, and she stayed in the game.

Was it the ultimate trust in Strong’s basketball IQ and discipline? Was it an admission that UConn couldn’t hang with their formidable 
opponent, Michigan
, if she came out? It turned out of be none of the above, but rather miscommunication on the UConn bench. Strong picked up her third foul just 30 seconds later.

“That was on me, that was my fault,” coach 
Geno Auriemma said.
 “The coaches on the bench were trying to tell me the previous one was her second and I was like, I don’t know, too focused on the game to pay attention. So that one’s on me. I told them, ‘if this ever happens again, just sub somebody in.’ Because that’s on me, I would have gotten her out with two, rather than get a third. So add that to the list of things I’m disappointed about.”

UConn guard Jovana Popovic (13) drives between Michigan forward Ashley Sofilkanich (15) and Dallas Loughridge (4) in the first half of an NCAA college basketball game, Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026, in in Ann Arbor, Mich. (AP Photo/Paul Sancya)

 

When even the Hall of Fame coach, the winningest in the history of the sport, can own such an out-of-character mistake, it tells you all you need to know about how long and frustrating a night the second-ranked UConn women’s team had at Michigan’s Crisler Center on Thursday, 
a 77-66 loss to the Wolverines.

Had Strong sat after her second foul, she might have been able to play at least some of the second quarter. As it was, the Huskies had to function 12 1/2 minutes without their best player, and though they trailed by a manageable six when she returned at the start of the second half, the nightmarish night continued.

“I think we have a lot to learn from this game,” Strong said. “We didn’t play UConn basketball at all, we just let Michigan do what they wanted to do the whole time. For the beginning, we let them do whatever they wanted to do. We didn’t fight back.”

UConn assembled a 
challenging nonconference schedule,
 partly because the Huskies have not gotten a challenge in Big East play in years, and partly because the Huskies are going to need to play opponents like the fifth-ranked Wolverines, and No. 10 Iowa Sunday in Storrs, to be ready for March Madness. Two more ranked teams, Ohio State and Louisville, follow that, and Auriemma warned after the Huskies beat Cornell, 100-35, in the season opener that even a really good team could lose all four of these games.
 

“With the way women’s basketball is,” Auriemma said, “I told the team when we got here, ‘We could be home beating somebody 90-30 or we could be here, where even if you play well you could lose.’ I think they’d rather do that, play here. We found out right away some of the things we have to address, where if we played somebody really bad at home, we would have to wait a lot longer to find out what the issues were.”

ANN ARBOR, MICHIGAN - NOVEMBER 5: Blanca Quinonez #4 of the UConn Huskies gathers with her teammates during a first quarter stoppage in play against the Michigan Wolverines at Crisler Arena on November 5, 2026 in Ann Arbor, Michigan. (Photo by Jaime Crawford/Getty Images)

Michigan, in the first top-five matchup ever at the Crisler Center, was more 
than up for its moment,
 perhaps a trajectory-changer for the program, and when the polls come out next Monday could rise up near the top, while the Huskies will drop a bit. The Wolverines exposed deficiencies the Huskies will have to address, correct, overcome or learn to mask. UConn led 57-55 with 8:30 to play, then fell apart. Te’Yala Delfosse scored a career-high 22 points, made 3-pointers on back-to-back possessions to give Michigan a six-point lead, then the Huskies turned it over five times in the closing minutes as Michigan pulled away.

Freshman Jovana Popovic’s star 
did continue to rise for UConn, as she scored 25 points, hitting 10-for-15 from the floor, with seven rebounds, four assists and three steals, though she had five of the Huskies’ 19 turnovers. Strong, scoreless in the first half, scored 13  in the second half, and had eight rebounds.

“We didn’t play together, we played separately,” Popovic said. “We need to talk about he game, watch the game and everything and talk about what happened the whole game, not just the fourth quarter.”
 

However, UConn’s complementary players were not up to the challenge of playing a Final Four caliber opponent in the second game of the season. Outside of Strong and Popovic, the rest of the Huskies’ cast was 12 for 39 from the floor, and 3 for 20 on 3-point attempts.

“Michigan was way more ready from the opening tip than we were,” Auriemma said. “They were better organized, more disciplined, they ran their stuff. And to me, it was disappointing, more than anything. I’m disappointed, not at the loss, we wouldn’t have scheduled the schedule we have if we didn’t anticipate some losses. But it was disappointing to see how our some of players reacted when it was time to step up and make plays. Everybody stands around, looks at Sarah, looks at Jovana, and says ‘bail us out.'”

UConn forward Sarah Strong (21) drives on Michigan forward Ciara Byars (23) in the first half of an NCAA college basketball game, Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026, in in Ann Arbor, Mich. (AP Photo/Paul Sancya)

Michigan gave UConn its toughest regular season game last season, nearly coming back from 20 points behind, but losing 72-69 at Mohegan Sun. The Wolverines went on to reach the Elite Eight, and Auriemma scheduled a home-and-home series, concluding in Connecticut next season. Kim Barnes Arico, who was missing her star point guard, Mia Holloway, also left a key player with two fouls in the game, and Kendall Dudley picked up her third, but ended up playing 18 minutes. Olivia Olson scored 19 and Syla Swords 10 as Michigan went 9-for-20 on threes.

“We couldn’t guard anybody,” Auriemma said. “We lost our defensive integrity.”

Barnes Arico
 played a lot of 2-3 zone, which confused UConn last year, but the Huskies scored 21 in the first quarter and 24 in the third, but broke down offensively in the second (eight points) and fourth (13) quarters.

“When it got bad, it stayed bad, because we don’t have the ability to respond,” Auriemma said. “… It didn’t look anything like what we had prepared, what we said we were going to do. I said to the players, ‘I don’t have an answer for you.’ We didn’t even attempt to do the things we had said must be done today, and that’s the most disappointing thing.”

There was much for UConn’s coaches to be concerned and disappointed about, and that’s not necessarily a bad thing in November, but the upcoming schedule — three Big Ten opponents at a moment when that league has nine ranked teams — could be unforgiving and push the Huskies into places the program hasn’t been in very long time.

“I said back home, we could be 0-4 when we finish this four-game stretch, and we could be last in the Big Ten, Michigan, Iowa, Ohio State,” Auriemma said. “We could be out of the top 20 and last in the Big Ten, but I knew when we put it together that could be a real possibility. But it’s also an opportunity for my players to say, ‘Okay, what’s going to change on my end?’ We can go back, watch film and say, ‘how do I get better from this game?’ And that’s a coach’s job now.”

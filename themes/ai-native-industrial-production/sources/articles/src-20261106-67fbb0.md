Positive Points
 
 
 
NDPR production increased 41% year-over-year to 1,018 metric tons, with sales volumes up 94% year-over-year to 1,020 metric tons.
 
 
 
Commercial scale production of dysprosium and terbium will begin in Q4 2026, supporting heavy rare earth needs for EV traction motor magnets.
 
 
 
Magnetics segment completed qualification and will begin shipping saleable magnets to GM in Q4 2026, with Apple deliveries starting in 2027.
 
 
 
Strong balance sheet with $1.34 billion in cash and short-term investments, providing ample liquidity for growth initiatives.
 
 
 
10X magnetics facility remains on schedule for commissioning in late 2028, with customer demand exceeding planned capacity.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Negative Points
 
 
 
Magnet sales are expected to be lumpy quarter to quarter, with volumes building gradually and modest adjusted EBITDA contribution in 2027, particularly in the first half.
 
 
 
Startup costs for magnet production will continue through at least the first half of 2027, impacting near-term profitability.
 
 
 
Chloralkali facility startup early in 2027 may cause lumpiness and additional downtime, affecting production and costs.
 
 
 
Heavy rare earth prices are currently elevated due to supply constraints but are not expected to persist indefinitely, posing a risk to future revenue.
 
 
 
The company expects a challenging financial environment ahead, with potential global macro setbacks and credit issues, requiring cautious capital management.

Q
: With GM as the anchor customer, what is the demand backdrop for NdPr magnets in the U.S. automotive market, and could MP upsize its commitments to GM? 
A
: CFO Ryan Corbett said demand from the foundational customer and across the automotive space remains very strong. GM is early in its localization strategy, with many more OEMs, Tier 1s and Tier 2s catching up, so automotive remains a big upside opportunity if MP chooses to keep investing there.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: How should investors think about the NdPr production ramp into 2027, the path to nameplate capacity, and normalized costs, including the chlor-alkali facility? 
A
: COO Michael Rosenthal said MP ran at over 450 tons per month for part of Q3, supporting confidence in its targeted run rate, though debottlenecking is still needed to sustain it. With chlor-alkali commissioning and other projects in 2027, MP expects to operate above a 5,000-ton-per-annum rate for the year. CFO Ryan Corbett added that cost leverage should become visible in the back half of 2027 as chlor-alkali and heavies stabilize and shared infrastructure costs are spread across more products.
 
 
 
 
 
Story Continues
 
 
 
 
Q
: When will Independence reach commercial production, and how is that defined given the 2027 magnet ramp? 
A
: CFO Ryan Corbett said MP will begin shipping saleable magnets in Q4 2026, with volumes building gradually based on how parts are phased into the foundational customer's programs and plants, followed by Apple production starting in 2027. Startup costs are expected to continue at least through the first half of 2027 before tapering as volumes rise.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: Could the new midterm election results affect policy support for the U.S. rare earth sector or MP specifically? 
A
: CEO Jim Litinsky said MP sees no change. He described MP as a nonpartisan national security company whose deal was driven by the Pentagon, operating in red, blue and purple states with strong bipartisan support across multiple government regimes.
 
 
Q
: Are any of MP's offtake agreements or price floors subject to renegotiation under new representatives? 
A
: CEO Jim Litinsky said no, stating simply that MP has a binding contract.
 
 
Q
: How are customer conversations and pricing structure for 10X evolving given the strong pricing environment? 
A
: CEO Jim Litinsky said demand expectations for 10X have accelerated, with specific customer RFQs now well exceeding 10X capacity. Because 10X is 100% contracted and can come online in 2028, MP can be selective and prioritize materially higher-margin opportunities, positioning it as a potential "kingmaker" in emerging multi-trillion-dollar markets.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: Given the recent share price weakness, is MP considering share buybacks? 
A
: CEO Jim Litinsky attributed sector pressure to macro and tax-arbitrage flows and said MP's contracted cash flows and strategic value differentiate it from peers needing capital. He stressed preserving a fortress balance sheet to stay opportunistic, warned of a potentially challenging macro backdrop, and said MP does not telegraph capital actions but will continue to be opportunistic.
 
 
Q
: What were the details behind the SEG+ sale this quarter, including cost and whether the tons will be reused for magnets? 
A
: CFO Ryan Corbett declined to give contract specifics but said filings indicate the sale was to an existing strategic customer. The product has been sold and will not be reused; it was not a magnet-focused use case, highlighting the broader heavy rare earth opportunity MP is building.
 
 
Q
: How much of Independence's heavy rare earth needs are covered by inventory and MP's own production, and how should heavy production revenue be modeled? 
A
: CFO Ryan Corbett said Independence is fully covered for its needs from MP's own ore body, and the heavy separation facility is designed to take third-party feedstocks, which MP is actively sourcing. Early heavy production will largely be intercompany and eliminated in consolidation, with revenue ultimately recognized through magnets; MP is not guiding specific Dy/Tb revenue yet but sees significant margin potential in intermediate streams and the 2028 gadolinium contract.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: What did MP learn from the GM qualification process that could improve margins and optimization in 2027? 
A
: CFO Ryan Corbett said the process was absolutely instructive. Automotive is technically demanding and the toughest from a quality standpoint, with qualification covering every piece of equipment, standard work instructions, safety attributes and material flow. He credited MP's magnetics team and GM's thoroughness, saying the process has made MP better with more work ahead.
 
 
Q
: Could advances in grain boundary diffusion and heavy rare earth thrifting open more third-party heavy rare earth sales? 
A
: CFO Ryan Corbett said MP's priority is supply security for existing magnetics customers, but there is a real path to selling excess heavies as thrifting progresses, closer to reality than three months ago. He said the SEG+ sale reflects embedded balance sheet value, with light and heavy concentrate worth hundreds of millions of dollars in potential value.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: What are the latest updates on recycling and Project Swarm? 
A
: COO Michael Rosenthal said recycling is progressing at both sites, with engineering advancing at Mountain Pass, construction underway, long-lead equipment procured, and magnet-from-recycled-material equipment being installed ahead of deliveries next year. CEO Jim Litinsky said Project Swarm's reception has been overwhelming, with MP helping standardize domestic defense drone supply chains on specific magnet grades so producers can avoid building unneeded capacity.
 
 
Q
: Are customers already approaching MP for robotics magnets, and does MP prefer defense or robotics business? 
A
: CEO Jim Litinsky said MP is in conversations with the names investors would expect, including world-class big tech entrants, and interest is becoming more specific. He expects robotics to be very high-margin business given MP's unique platform and balance sheet, and noted China's block of SoftBank's ABB robotics acquisition as a sign of global supply chain competition.

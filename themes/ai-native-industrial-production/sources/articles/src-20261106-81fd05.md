MP Materials Tops Estimates As Materials Surge And GM Qualification Lands, But A One-Time Sale And A 2028 Magnet Payoff Complicate The Beat


            By
                    
Earnings Whispers

            | November 05, 2026, 9:33 PM
        

MP


                +4.19%
            


        MP Materials Corp
    

Earnings Whispers
MP Materials delivered the kind of quarter that rare earth bulls have been waiting for, at least on the surface. Adjusted earnings of $0.04 per share topped the consensus estimate of a $0.02 loss and the Earnings Whisper number of a $0.01 loss, a 500.0% surprise. That compares with an adjusted loss of $0.10 a year ago, so earnings grew 130.8%. Revenue of $147.66 million beat the $116.52 million consensus by 26.7% and grew 175.7% year over year. The more consequential news was operational: MP completed General Motors' magnet qualification, and saleable deliveries and revenue begin in the fourth quarter. The central tension is that the upstream business is scaling faster than expected, while the downstream magnet story that underpins the long-term thesis will not produce meaningful earnings until 2028.
The quality of the beat deserves scrutiny. Revenue included a $31.6 million sale of SEG+ to a U.S. aerospace and defense customer, which management characterized on the call as a one-time inventory sale for a non-magnet use. Without it, revenue would have landed roughly in line with consensus. That does not erase the underlying progress. NdPr oxide and metal revenue rose 227% to $101.0 million on 1,020 metric tons sold, up 94%, and production hit a record 1,018 tons, up 41%. The release also showed $8.1 million of price protection agreement income. On a GAAP basis, MP still lost $20.7 million, or $0.12 per share, though that loss was half the prior year's. The gap between GAAP and adjusted results reflects several items:
$12.6 million of magnet start-up costs;
an $8.9 million unfavorable derivative fair-value swing;
rising stock-based compensation.
Cost of sales also absorbed a $6.4 million reserve on non-core inventories.
The call showed sequential acceleration in Materials. Revenue plus PPA income reached $155.8 million, up 23.6% from $126.1 million in Q2. Materials adjusted EBITDA climbed to $47.8 million from $32.5 million in Q2 and $36.7 million in Q1. Parts of the quarter ran above 450 tons a month toward the 500-ton target. For Q4, management guided to roughly 1,000 tons of NdPr sales at about $100/kg realized, up from the high-$90s guided for Q3. It also set a 2027 production rate above 5,000 tons annually. Commercial dysprosium and terbium production begins in Q4.
Magnetics moved the other way. Segment revenue fell to $18.6 million and adjusted EBITDA to $5.3 million, down from $21.1 million and $9.6 million in Q1, as precursor revenue rolls off. Deferred precursor revenue has shrunk to roughly $28 million from $74 million at the end of 2025. The CFO guided magnetics EBITDA to stay modest through 2027, especially in the first half, with full earnings power pushed to 2028. Magnet volumes will be lumpy and gated by GM's deliberate phase-in, and Independence start-up costs persist through at least 1H27. The Q4 PPA income guide also slipped to about $8/kg from roughly $10/kg.
The forward catalysts are real but back-end loaded. Robotics RFQs and customer sizings now exceed 10X capacity, a more concrete framing than prior calls offered. The 10X facility remains on schedule for late-2028 commissioning, and samarium and gadolinium production remains on track for 1H28. Bears have legitimate ammunition on the balance sheet:
Cash and short-term investments fell to $1.34 billion from $1.83 billion at year-end.
Nine-month capex reached $420 million against an unchanged $500 to $600 million full-year guide.
Operating cash flow remained slightly negative.
Management also flagged unplanned interruptions on some midstream circuits and said elevated heavy rare earth prices may not persist. The CEO himself warned of a possible macro or financial setback that could pressure sector share prices.
The market setup is more complicated than the surprise implies. Shares opened at $50.68 after the August report, spiked to $62.47 in late August, and then slid to $35.91 heading into this release. That is a 29.1% decline since the prior report and 42.5% below the quarter high. On the eve of earnings, the stock set a 52-week low of $35.56 and sat 34.3% below its $54.64 200-day moving average. Investor sentiment slipped from modestly positive to roughly neutral, a meaningful cooling but not outright pessimism. The Earnings Whispers trend readings are mostly negative on price and AVWAP, with momentum the lone positive. That backdrop suggests expectations were reset lower before a report that beat on nearly every headline measure.
The bottom line is that MP proved its upstream engine can scale and delivered on its long-promised GM milestone, but a sizable part of the revenue beat came from a one-time sale. The magnet earnings that justify the downstream investment were explicitly pushed to 2028. Investors entering at depressed levels get a stronger Materials business and confirmed execution. They also inherit a cash-consuming buildout, lumpy magnet ramps, and a management team openly cautioning about macro risk.

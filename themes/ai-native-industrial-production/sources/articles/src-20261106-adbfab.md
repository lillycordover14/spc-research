Pentagon taps Machina Labs to trial fast-build drone factories
Andre Savage
Published 
Nov 6, 2026
 
 
 
Share:

Summary: 

The Pentagon awarded Machina Labs a $9 million, 18-month pilot to form metal airframes for LUCAS, the military's Low-Cost Uncrewed Combat Attack System.

The Los Angeles startup says it can stand up modular factories in about a month, target about $15,000 per drone, and, over time, scale its process to tens of thousands a year.

LUCAS is modeled on Iran's Shahed, which CSIS estimates costs roughly $35,000, and which has been used by Russia in Ukraine and by Tehran across the current Middle East conflict.

Why the Pentagon hired a robotics startup

The Defense Department asked 
Machina Labs
 to test whether cheap attack drones can be built quickly in pop-up facilities as autonomy reshapes combat. The company, based in Los Angeles, makes AI-guided robotic arms for manufacturing and will run an 18-month, $9 million effort to form metal airframes for the LUCAS program.

Machina has backing from 
Lockheed Martin
's venture arm. Chief executive Ed Mehr said the pitch is to make larger drones near where they are needed, with modular factories that can be set up in roughly a month.

How Machina plans to build drones

Instead of labor-intensive composite shells, Machina uses robotic arms to press low-cost metal sheets into aircraft structures. Target pricing is about $15,000 per drone, with the company projecting that, over time, its production method could scale to tens of thousands of units annually.

The threat LUCAS is designed around

LUCAS drones trace back to Iran's Shahed platform. Russia has used Shaheds in Ukraine, and Tehran has employed similar one-way attack drones during the current Middle East conflict to strike US bases and regional energy sites, harass the Strait of Hormuz and commercial shipping, and sap allied air defenses. The Center for Strategic and International Studies pegs a Shahed at about $35,000.

What it means for your portfolio

If Machina proves it can spin up drone factories in roughly a month and hit about $15,000 per airframe, that changes the cost curve compared with Shahed estimates. It also favors suppliers that can deliver hardware fast and close to the front, not just the cheapest unit price at a distant plant.

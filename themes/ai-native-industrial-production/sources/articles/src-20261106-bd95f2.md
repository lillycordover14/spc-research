South Korea's Taesung Bets on Composite Copper Foil, Glass Substrate with $51.5 Million Zero-Interest Convertible Bonds

South Korea's Taesung is issuing 70 billion won (approximately $51.5 million) in zero-interest, no-refixing private convertible bonds. Of the proceeds, 40 billion won (approximately $29.4 million) will fund production facilities for composite copper foil materials and glass substrates, while 30 billion won (approximately $22.1 million) goes to raw material purchases and R&D. The conversion price is set at 51,931 won (approximately $38), and full conversion would dilute existing shares by 4.41%. Taesung, a South Korean printed circuit board (PCB) manufacturing equipment maker, is issuing 70 billion won (approximately $51.5 million) in zero-interest convertible bonds (CBs) to build a mass-production base for its composite copper foil materials and glass substrate equipment businesses. The move stands out for securing new-business investment funds without interest costs, but share dilution from conversion rights and the possibility of early redemption demands remain burdens.

Taesung disclosed on the 6th that it has decided to issue its second series of unregistered, unsecured private convertible bonds with no interest coupon. The issue size is 70 billion won, with a payment date of the 19th and a maturity date of November 19, 2031. Both the coupon rate and the yield to maturity are 0%, meaning only principal is repaid with no separate interest until maturity. The bonds also carry no refixing clause that would lower the conversion price if the share price falls, preventing the number of conversion shares from rising solely due to stock price movements.

The conversion price is 51,931 won (approximately $38), and full conversion would issue 1,347,942 new common shares, equivalent to 4.41% of existing shares outstanding. Taesung or a third party designated by the company may exercise a call option on up to 40% of the issue amount—28 billion won (approximately $20.6 million)—at a simple annual yield of 0.1%.

The proceeds are concentrated on new businesses. Of the total, 40 billion won (approximately $29.4 million) goes to expanding production facilities, with 25 billion won (approximately $18.4 million) allocated to composite copper foil material production facilities and related infrastructure and 15 billion won (approximately $11.0 million) to glass substrate production facilities and related infrastructure. The investment period runs from this month through December 2028. The remaining 30 billion won (approximately $22.1 million) is split between 20 billion won (approximately $14.7 million) for raw material purchases and 10 billion won (approximately $7.4 million) for R&D personnel and service costs.

Taesung recently completed a composite copper foil materials plant in Seonggok-dong, Ansan, Gyeonggi Province, and built seven production lines. Facility construction and commissioning preparations are underway, with related permitting procedures expected to wrap up this month. The company is currently in talks with customers on supplying composite copper foil materials and plans to use the funds to build facilities and secure raw materials to meet each customer's requirements.

It is also increasing investment in the glass substrate equipment business. Taesung has secured a supply track record for through-glass via (TGV) cleaning and etching equipment and is developing vertical development and etching equipment for glass core substrates while conducting demonstrations for customers. Funds will also be allocated to production facilities and related infrastructure ahead of expanded equipment supply.

A surge in orders for its existing equipment business is another reason for the fundraising. Taesung's cumulative orders this year total about 280 billion won (approximately $205.9 million), roughly 7.4 times its 2025 consolidated annual revenue of 37.9 billion won (approximately $27.9 million). The company said expanded facility investment by global substrate makers—driven by growth in the AI server and high-performance semiconductor markets—has translated into higher orders. Much of the secured volume is scheduled for delivery next year. Equipment manufacturing requires collecting the balance after delivery and installation, so funds for raw material purchases and equipment production must be secured in advance.

Taesung also reorganized its production base ahead of this fundraising. Its Cheonan New Plant in South Chungcheong Province, completed in July, has roughly six to seven times the production capacity of its Ansan plant and will produce PCB equipment as well as glass substrate and composite copper foil-related equipment. The move is aimed at handling large equipment orders while establishing a mass-production system for new equipment.

"The completion of the composite copper foil materials plant in Seonggok-dong, Ansan, has put us in full-scale preparations to launch the materials business, and we are carrying out commercialization preparations, including building production facilities and securing raw materials, to respond to supply talks currently underway with customers," said Taesung CEO Kim Jong-hak. "Because we raised funds on terms with a 0% coupon rate, 0% yield to maturity and no refixing clause, we can expand new-business investment while minimizing financial costs," he added. "We will actively push commercialization of the composite copper foil materials and glass substrate equipment businesses while stably handling the surge in orders for our existing PCB and FC-BGA equipment."

Zero-interest funding, however, does not eliminate repayment obligations. Investors can request share conversion starting November 2027 and, from November 2028, can demand early redemption every three months at 100% of principal. The first early redemption window falls before December 2028, when the production facility investment is scheduled to end, so repayment pressure could arise depending on the share price and the timing of new-business revenue generation. Dilution of existing shareholders' stakes if the bonds are converted into shares is another variable. The absence of a refixing clause only limits additional conversion shares from a falling share price; it does not eliminate dilution from conversion itself.

The two new businesses share the trait of leveraging existing equipment technology, but their revenue structures differ. Glass substrates connect etching and cleaning technology built in PCB processes to equipment supply, whereas composite copper foil is a business that uses plating technology to produce and sell the material itself. Glass substrate equipment depends on customers' facility investment and whether they adopt the equipment, while composite copper foil hinges on securing material performance and production yields and then converting that into long-term supply contracts.

Neither field has yet generated meaningful mass-production revenue, so future results depend on progress in talks with customers. "For both composite copper foil materials and glass substrates, once initial investment is completed and customer requests come in, we will be able to discuss long-term supply contracts," a company official said. "We expect production volumes to ramp up steeply from 2028-2029." Market watchers see the pace at which the 280 billion won order backlog is recognized as actual revenue, and whether funds raised on zero-interest terms translate into mass production of new businesses, as the variables that will determine the stock price and financial health going forward.

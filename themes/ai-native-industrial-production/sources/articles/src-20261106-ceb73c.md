Japan’s nominal wages post biggest rise in nearly 30 years

Friday, 6 November 2026, 03:02

The latest pay data could keep the Bank of Japan on track to raise interest rates as soon as December.

Nominal wages in Japan rose 4.6% in September from a year earlier, the biggest increase since 1997, the labor ministry reported Friday. Base pay was up 3.7%, while a more stable measure for full-time workers – excluding bonuses, overtime and sampling distortions – rose by a record 3.1%.

This is reported by 
Bloomberg
.

Real cash earnings, adjusted for inflation excluding rents, increased 2.2% year over year. That was faster than August’s revised 1.6% gain and above the consensus estimate of 1.3%. Economists had expected growth to slow because September had fewer working days.

Prince Harry
 and 
Meghan Markle
 have reportedly found a new home in the Cotswolds, less than three months after their return to the United Kingdom. But anyone who thinks they’ve bought a new home will be disappointed. The Duke and Duchess of Sussex, who had been staying at the residence of a wealthy entrepreneur friend since August 26, have reportedly left the main house on the property to move into another residence located within the same estate, according to 
Daily Mail
 journalist Richard Eden. “They were living in the main house,” a person close to the entrepreneur told the British newspaper. “They have now moved to a property on the estate.” The move reportedly took place before Harry and Meghan left for a vacation abroad with their children.
Since the couple returned to the United Kingdom, their search for a new home in Britain has been a hotly debated topic, especially in light of their disagreements with the royal family and security concerns. The mystery surrounding their residence has fueled speculation and various theories. Where would the Sussexes choose to settle? That was the question echoing everywhere on social media and in the press. For security reasons, the details of their living arrangements have always remained confidential.
Harry and Meghan in Nottingham

Christopher Furlong/Getty Images
Harry and Meghan live in the Cotswolds, a haven for many celebrities
As reported by the British media, the couple and their two children had been staying at a property owned by a wealthy friend, 
nestled in the Cotswolds countryside
—an area Harry and Meghan already knew well. In 2018, the year of their wedding, they rented a prestigious farmhouse in the region for two years—a historic property with an estimated value of 4.12 million British pounds (about $5.5 million), part of the Great Tew Estate. The residence included a four-bedroom farmhouse, a barn converted into living quarters, and a separate cottage with two additional bedrooms. The property also featured extensive grounds and a heated patio. Over the years, the Cotswolds, in the English countryside, have become a retreat for numerous celebrities, including Jeremy Clarkson, David and Victoria Beckham, and Kate Moss. A few weeks ago the Duchess of Sussex was photographed at The Three Horseshoes, 
a quaint pub near Burford
, in the company of 
Ellen DeGeneres
 and her wife, Portia de Rossi. In 2024, DeGeneres and de Rossi 
left the United States
 to move to the Cotswolds, where they live on a large estate surrounded by greenery.
The Sussexes’ 
housing history in the United Kingdom
, however, has been quite eventful. Before leaving the United Kingdom, the couple lived at 
Frogmore Cottage in Windsor
, a property made available to Harry and Meghan by 
Queen Elizabeth II
. In 2023, however, the two royals left the residence. The decision came a few months after the publication of the duke’s memoir, 
Spare
.
 To renovate Frogmore Cottage, approximately 2.4 million British pounds (about $3.2 million) went into transforming two existing buildings into a single-family home. The renovations were extensive: The ceiling beams and floor structures were replaced, the electrical system was redone, new gas and water lines were installed, and a soundproofing system costing approximately 50,000 British pounds (about $67,000) was reportedly put in place. Despite the 
significant spend
, the Sussexes left the property for good after moving overseas.
Even before settling in Windsor, Harry and Meghan 
lived at Nottingham Cottage
, within the Kensington Palace complex. It was a rather small residence that apparently hadn’t won Meghan over. In the Netflix documentary 
Harry & Meghan
,
 the duchess recalled with a touch of irony that the name Kensington Palace suggested something grand, while the reality was quite different: “Kensington Palace sounds very regal, of course it does. It says ‘palace’ in the name. But Nottingham Cottage was so small!” Harry added that the house had very low ceilings and was even slightly slanted.
The new home in the Cotswolds could therefore be a practical solution for the Sussexes, at least until they figure out their plans in the 
United Kingdom
. Meghan’s professional commitments may also take her away from England again. The duchess is expected to move temporarily to Australia next year to participate in the filming of 
Junior MasterChef,
 the cooking show she is set to cohost. Prince Harry and their two children might join her during her stay in Australia, but there’s no confirmation of that yet.
The couple in Nottingham

Pool/Getty Images
Who owns the estate where Harry and Meghan live?
The identity of the estate’s current owner remains a mystery. He is a friend of Prince Harry’s, with whom he shares a commitment to charities that support veterans and military personnel. Apparently, the businessman would be happy to continue hosting the prince, Meghan, and their children for a long time to come. Several theories have circulated regarding the benefactor’s identity, with some speculating that he is Ian Wace, a British financier with an estimated net worth of $1.2 billion and who has long been close to Harry. He owns the Rowler Estate, a vast estate in the Cotswolds. His private life has been marked by tragedy: In 1994 he lost his first wife, Joanna, and their two young children in a car accident in Hampshire.
However, being hosted by a wealthy friend is nothing new in the Sussexes’ lives. In 2020, when they decided to step back from their royal duties and move to the United States, the couple stayed at the 
Beverly Hills mansion
 of producer and media mogul 
Tyler Perry
. It was a Tuscan-style residence with eight bedrooms and 12 bathrooms, located within the gated community of Beverly Ridge Estates.

The Montecito mansion in California does not appear to be for sale
A few months later, in August of that same year, Harry and Meghan purchased 
a mansion in Montecito, California
, where they settled. Surrounded by greenery, the mansion is located in a residential area frequented by some of Hollywood’s most famous stars, including Oprah Winfrey, Ariana Grande, and Brad Pitt. It is a very luxurious home that, at the time of purchase, was dubbed the Sussexes’ new palace—one the couple seems to have no intention of selling, despite their return to the United Kingdom. According to reports, the maintenance staff had continued to care for the family’s chickens.
The couple is still holding on to the California property, purchased for around $14 million, which continues to serve as one of their family homes. The estate includes a wine cellar, a movie theater, a gym, a spa, a large swimming pool, a tennis court, a wood-fired oven, a barbecue, and more than 13 and a half bathrooms.
However, this is not the only residence owned by the Sussexes. Just several years ago, Harry and Meghan purchased a home on the Portuguese coast, near the one owned by Princess Eugenie—Harry’s cousin—and her husband, Jack Brooksbank. The property is located within the CostaTerra Golf & Ocean Club, an exclusive residential complex with about 300 ocean-view villas in Melides, south of Lisbon.

Harry and Meghan plan a trip to the European Parliament
Meanwhile, the Duke and Duchess of Sussex are expected in Brussels on November 10 for an event dedicated to protecting children from online risks and abuse. The heart of the initiative, organized by MEP Antonio López-Istúriz White as part of a parliamentary intergroup, will be the unveiling of 
The Lost Screen Memorial,
 a video installation created in memory of children who lost their lives after falling victim to online abuse and exploitation. The installation will consist of 50 large-scale portraits of the young victims. European Parliament president Roberta Metsola is also expected to attend the ceremony.
According to reports in the British media, Harry and Meghan will deliver a joint speech on the issue—which has long been a focus of the couple’s advocacy—before participating in a discussion with international activists. Among the scheduled speakers is Sandra Spitaels, the Belgian mother of Glenn, who, in 2017, took his own life at age 15 after a nude photograph of him was circulated online.
Harry and Meghan in Australia

Asanka Ratnayake/Getty Images
Thomas Markle’s transfer to a specialized dementia-care facility
These two developments involving the Duke and Duchess of Sussex come as Meghan’s father, Thomas Markle, is at the center of a horrific legal case. He is the subject of an investigation launched following a complaint filed by his son, Thomas Markle Jr., with the Philippine police and the FBI. The younger Thomas handed over to investigators a box containing several images and videos that allegedly depicted child sexual abuse, found among his father’s personal belongings in Cebu City, Philippines.
Another discovery reportedly took place in recent days at a storage unit that Thomas Markle allegedly rented near his former home in Rosarito, Mexico. The facility was reportedly inspected by local police as part of an investigative operation. Meghan’s 82-year-old father has reportedly been transferred in recent days to a facility in Florida that specializes in caring for people with dementia, according to 
The
 
Sun
.
His move to a care facility could now complicate the investigation, particularly the possibility of questioning Thomas Markle. His cognitive condition could make it more difficult to proceed with the investigation, especially given the protections afforded to residents of specialized facilities.
Meghan, who has had no contact with her father since 2018, has not commented on the latest developments. It had previously emerged that the duchess, who had been aware of the allegations against her father since 2018, wanted to speak out publicly. One of the agents from Royalty and Specialist Protection (RaSP)—the Scotland Yard unit responsible for the security of members of the royal family and other public figures—reportedly advised her not to speak out. The agent reportedly told her, “Leave it with us, we’ll investigate it.”
Thomas Markle was arrested in Florida on Friday on charges connected to the possession of child sexual abuse material. He has previously denied possession of such material.

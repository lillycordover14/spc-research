Iran’s New Doctrine: Deterrence and Making Enemies Regret

06 November 2026

6:23 PM

Foreign Policy
 
WANA News
 

 

 

WANA (Nov 06) – 
Iran’s new military doctrine is based on deterrence and making adversaries regret any hostile action, with the country prepared to use all its offensive and defensive capabilities without restraint, the head of Parliament’s National Security and Foreign Policy Commission said.

 

Ebrahim Azizi, a lawmaker, said Iran’s Armed Forces and all defense institutions were fully prepared for any scenario, including any uncalculated move by the enemy.

 

He warned that those seeking to preserve their remaining presence in the Middle East should avoid miscalculation, saying Iran, its people, and its Armed Forces were better prepared than ever to deliver a response designed to make the enemy regret its actions—one he described as more painful than the 12-day and 40-day wars.

 

Azizi said Iran had developed and prepared significant new capabilities in military equipment, offensive and defensive systems, and domestic production following the 40-day war, adding that these capabilities could be deployed if necessary.

 

He stressed that Iran faced no geographical restrictions in defending its territorial integrity or targeting infrastructure, military bases, and equipment in response to hostile actions.

 

“Any US interests in the region will face a decisive response by the Islamic Republic of Iran that will make them regret their actions,” he said, adding that Tehran had repeatedly conveyed this message and that Washington should by now recognize the credibility of Iran’s threats following the 40-day war.

 

New Doctrine Removes Restrictions on Response

The head of Parliament’s National Security and Foreign Policy Commission said that, under the new doctrine and in accordance with the directives of the Commander-in-Chief, Iran would no longer impose restrictions or considerations on its response to the enemy.

 

He said any party acting against the interests of the Iranian people would face a firm and regret-inducing response.

 

Azizi noted that geographical considerations had previously been among Iran’s limitations, allowing adversaries to take advantage of certain locations. He said Iran’s current position was that it must confront the enemy directly wherever and whenever its interests are targeted.

 

“We will use all our offensive and defensive capabilities without any reservations. This is the order received by the Armed Forces, and our responsibility is to support them in various sectors,” he said.

 

Azizi reiterated that Iran’s new doctrine was based not only on deterrence but also on making adversaries regret their actions.

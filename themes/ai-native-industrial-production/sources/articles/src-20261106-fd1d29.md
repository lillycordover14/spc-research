Quotables

We have really good stretches, but we’re unable to sustain it for long stretches, and we have to find a way to build those habits where we can start putting longer stretches together.” — 
Michael Malone
 after UNC’s win against Western Carolina.

“Got to be better with handling runs, got to be better at having urgency for 40 minutes, but I do like our team, I do like our guys. We’ve got a good team, we just didn’t handle that well tonight.” — Wofford head coach 
Kevin Giltner
 after loss at Tennessee

Pregame Notes

Last Time Out:
 
Matt Able
‘s 18 points led North Carolina to an 80-72 win on Monday night, officially securing Malone’s first collegiate victory. Still, the Tar Heels’ warts from the preseason didn’t go away against Western Carolina, who was picked to finish eighth in the SoCon. The Catamounts outrebounded the Tar Heels by nine in the season opener, while Carolina also missed 21 three pointers, enabling Western to stay within reach for most of the night. It wasn’t until a 14-4 UNC run with less than six minutes to play allowed the Heels to separate for the opening night victory. 

Meanwhile, the Terriers inherited a tall task, opening their season at No. 8 Tennessee, where they fell 90-59. Though in the first 20 minutes, Wofford went toe-to-toe with the Volunteers, entering the half trailing by three. But the Terriers couldn’t maintain it for much longer, shooting 15.4 percent from three-point range in the second half and surrendering 27 fast break points overall.

Analyzing Wofford:
 Projected to finish 3rd in the SoCon, Giltner enters his second year at the helm for the Terriers after a 19-13 (11-7 SoCon) debut season. Nationally, Wofford ranked 19th in turnovers per game and 49th in assist-to-turnover ratio in 2025-26.

Giltner reloaded this past offseason with nine newcomers, four by way of the transfer portal to go alongside five freshmen. Guard Luke Flynn and forward Brian Sumpter are the two returning starters for the Terriers, though both averaged under seven points per game last season — Sumpter was also sidelined against the Volunteers on Tuesday. Of Wofford’s four transfers, Chris Arias is the lone addition from the Division I ranks, coming from Jacksonville University. 

Cam Lowe, a Division II All-American last season, led the Terriers with 14 points in the season-opening loss to the Volunteers. But while Flynn and Chace Watley combined for 11 points in Knoxville, the returning duo and the addition of Lowe should be the heartbeat of Giltner’s second squad. Lowe and Watley were tabbed to the preseason SoCon All-Conference team, and Flynn is arguably the most experienced contributor on the roster, starting 18 games before suffering an injury that limited him to three appearances across the rest of the season.

Seven-footer Evan Ramsey, who went for eight points and 10 rebounds against the Volunteers, mans the frontcourt for Wofford. In height, Ramsey is also the outlier in the Terriers’ starting lineup, which debuted three players 6-foot-2 or smaller against Tennessee.

While a small sample size, the Terriers shot 22.2 percent from deep and 65.2 percent from the free throw line to tip-off their season. Wofford had the 311th-ranked defense in efficiency last season according to KenPom, and despite a new-look roster, those issues resurfaced in the second half on Tuesday, allowing 56 second half points and 52 total points in the paint.

Preseason metrics and one game of data have the Terriers ranked 290th in KenPom, 273rd in Bart Torvik and 205th on Evan Miya. As expected, its offense is supposed to outpace its defense for the second consecutive season under Giltner. 

The Matchup:
 North Carolina has a laundry list of areas to improve on from Monday night, many of which transcend the opponent, proving to be patterns throughout the three-game exhibition slate and the season-opener against Western Carolina. But the Tar Heels will have another opportunity Friday night to remedy those concerns against what should be an outmatched opponent. 

Assuming Wofford distributes its minutes similarly, UNC’s starting lineup will have three players taller than four of the Terriers’ five starters, posing another opportunity to get its rebounding struggles moving in the right direction. But Ramsey, Wofford’s starting big, has a 60-pound advantage on 
Sayon Keita
 and also had five offensive rebounds at Tennessee.

Malone continues to emphasize pace in postgame discussions, but the tandem of bad rebounding and poor offensive execution limited the Tar Heels to eight fast break points against the Catamounts. Against a defense that allowed more than triple that days ago, along with surrendering a 52.3 field goal percentage, UNC’s fast break offense and overall execution needs to see an uptick.

KenPom projects North Carolina as a 24-point favorite on Friday, a similar number it compiled against Western Carolina. After the shaky performance in Malone’s debut and an even closer scare, albeit in an exhibition, a week prior versus Charlotte, all eyes will be on the Tar Heels’ ability to put together a complete, 40-minute performance against lower-level competition. 

Last Meeting:
 North Carolina is 4-2 all-time against Wofford. The Tar Heels and Terriers have met four times since 2015, producing an even 2-2 split. The most recent meeting, in December of 2019, saw the Terriers take down the 17th-ranked Tar Heels, 68-64, in Carmichael Arena.

Geno: Loss to Michigan was 'worst UConn performance' in over two decades
The head coach provided an autopsy of the defeat on Saturday.
Daniel Connolly
Nov 7, 2026
Geno Auriemma isn’t upset that 
UConn lost a game at Michigan on Thursday
. Given the caliber of competition on the Huskies’ non-conference schedule, he expected to drop a few contests along the way.
Instead, it’s the way they lost that bothers the head coach.
"I told them with our schedule, I didn't expect to go undefeated. I didn't expect to win them all. But I expected us to represent Connecticut way better than that,” he said.
Auriemma remembers the last time UConn had such an uncharacteristic performance: Nov. 21, 2004. The first season after Diana Taurasi graduated, the Huskies traveled to Raleigh to take on North Carolina and lost 71-65. They coughed the ball up 28 times and let the Tar Heels close the game on a 16-5 run.
“
[The loss at Michigan] was the worst UConn performance since early November in [2004],” Auriemma said. “I actually remember the game. We played North Carolina in Raleigh in some tip-off tournament or something (the Jimmy V Classic). It's [22] years ago but I remember like it happened yesterday because it was just as bad. I walked out of that building going, ‘This is going to be a long year.’”
That proved to be true. The Huskies ultimately went 25-8, ended up as a 3-seed in the NCAA Tournament and lost in the Sweet Sixteen. They have higher expectations for this season.
So what made Thursday night’s loss so anomalous?

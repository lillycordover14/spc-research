Three takeaways from UNC’s win over Wofford
The Heels played with a level of hunger that we hadn’t seen until now.
by
 
 
Brandon Anderson
Nov 7, 2026, 4:00 PM UTC
Any Carolina fans that were upset that the Tar Heels didn’t blow out Western Carolina were treated to a dominant 109-70 win over Wofford. The Heels limited the Terriers to just 33% shooting from the field, and 28% from three. Meanwhile, the Heels’ offense caught fire by knocking down 52% of their shots from the floor and 40% from three. If there was any hair to split as far as scoring production, they “only” made 23 of their 29 free throws, but overall the Heels were able to put away this game way before the second half was over.
The problem with the kind of situations that UNC is in right now is that everything has to be taken game by game. Does this convincing win mean they’ll do it again next week? Not necessarily. But are they aren’t guaranteed take several steps backwards and only barely beat Wyoming either. The truth is the Heels had one really good game, and now they need to build winning habits with this new-look roster. Seeing the best version of this team will take time, but last night we at least got to have some fun.
That said, let’s discuss three takeaways from a good night in the Dean Dome.
Terrence Brown finally cut loose
Utah transfer Terrence Brown had a lot of good film from his time out West, and we all wondered when we would see some of that in a Carolina uniform. The unfortunate reality is that during the Hubert Davis era, he had a habit of getting transfers that didn’t end up performing nearly as well as anybody expected. It started to feel that way with Brown after what has been technically four games — we’re not going to argue how meaningful exhibition performance is vs. regular season — but he finally showed up and showed out against Wofford.
Brown finished his night with a team-high 21 points, one rebound, one assist, and two steals. He was a terror on offense, showing off a lot of his bag in the form of driving to the rim, hitting mid-range shots, and also knocked down a three. Analysts have pointed out this week that Brown is not used to playing off the ball, and so it has felt like his time at UNC has so far been an adjustment period. Tonight felt like an offensive revelation for the dynamic senior, but again, the keyword is sustainability. Can he repeat this next week, and for the rest of the season? Time will tell, but we do know this — when he’s feeling it, he’s really fun to watch.
Credit also goes to Brown for his performance on defense. He was very active against Wofford, disrupting passing lanes and getting out in transition when he was able to force a turnover. Michael Malone needs this backcourt to work if this team wants to do anything worthwhile in the postseason, and Brown gave us hope that he at least is showing progress.
Maxi Adams is a really fun player to watch
While there is no Caleb Wilson on this year’s Carolina team, Maxi Adams has one trait that fills one particular gap that Wilson left — he’s really fun to watch. He finished his night with 11 points, 11 rebounds, and two blocks. It was his first double-double, and the only double-double of any Carolina player so far this season.
What makes Adams really fun to watch is that he plays with the type of energy that this team needs. He was all over the floor against the Terriers on both sides of the ball, and he was able to do the one thing that this team has struggled with the most, which is rebound. The Venn Diagram of high-energy bigs and really good rebounders is a circle, and Adams fits the bill on two accounts. We previously mentioned that Adams will likely be the best sixth man for the Tar Heels, and it looks like so far that’s holding true. When Malone decides to start trimming minutes for his bench as we get deeper into the season, expect to continue seeing a lot of Adams, because UNC will need him big time.
Was Wofford bad, or was UNC good?
Shortly after the game, someone on Reddit asked if Wofford was just that bad, or did Michael Malone just light into the team between the Western Carolina game and Wofford. It’s a fair question, because the team we saw last night was not at all the team that we saw against the Catamounts. This team played with a lot of energy, a lot of focus, and a lot of fight. But to throw just a splash of cold water on the win, it is also true that Wofford is still trying to figure things out with their new head coach and a lot of new players, so it’s fair to say that they’re just not good right now.
Not to diminish anything the Heels did, but the Terriers had a number of good looks on the floor, but some of them just weren’t going in. Does that sound familiar? They had the same problem against Tennessee, and in that game they finished shooting 31% from the field. To state the obvious, there’s a talent discrepancy between Wofford and the Tar Heels, but that isn’t to say that they didn’t have opportunities within their control. But it’s still true that the Heels looked a lot better tonight — I could write a whole other article about some of the improvements that we saw on both ends of the floor, and especially how many other players had positive contributions.
So yes, UNC is moving in the right direction, but Wofford is fighting for their lives right now trying to figure everything out. Given the fact that these two teams went into the 2026-27 season with an identical situation, it’s good to see the Heels are figuring it out a bit quicker than they are, talent gap aside.

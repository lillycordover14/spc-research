Citrine Informatics is a company that sells an enterprise software platform for materials and chemicals product development.
 This page explains what the platform covers, what materials informatics is, how the vendor describes its adoption path, and how to weigh it against open alternatives. It is a neutral explainer, not an endorsement. Facts about the company come from Citrine’s own platform and about pages, read on 2026-11-07.

What is Citrine Informatics?

Citrine describes its platform as “a data management system and AI platform for materials and chemicals product development.” It combines composition, processing and product data so that teams can apply machine learning to formulation and materials questions. On its about page the company calls itself an enterprise SaaS business using generative AI and materials science, with the mission of speeding the development and deployment of next-generation materials and chemicals.

The company was founded in 2013 by Greg Mulholland and Bryce Meredig and has its global headquarters in Redwood City, California (Citrine about page, read 2026-11-07).

What is materials informatics?

Materials informatics is the application of computational and machine-learning tools to discovering and characterizing materials. In practice it means turning experimental and simulated data (compositions, processing conditions, measured properties) into structured, reusable datasets and then fitting predictive models to them. It sits between 
materials science
 and 
machine learning
, and it depends heavily on data practices such as the 
FAIR data principles
. The US Materials Genome Initiative pushed the field in this direction; see the guide on 
MGI and FAIR materials data
.

Who uses it and what it covers

Citrine’s platform page lists these industry groups: specialty chemicals; plastics; paints, coatings and adhesives; glasses, ceramics and metals; manufacturers; and government and research. The scope is product development in these areas, not general-purpose research data management.

How the platform is organised

The platform page groups the offering into four pillars. This is the vendor’s own description; no independent evaluation of any component is cited here.

Omni / Universal Data Harmonizer, Data Model, Materials History

Training and Support, Data Preparation Services

For sign-in and demo access, use the navigation on citrine.io, which includes “Request a Demo”.

The vendor’s adoption path

Citrine’s “Get Started” page frames adoption as five stages: Explore, Requirements Gathering, Evaluation, Adoption and Scaling. For a research office or lab considering any materials informatics system, the useful point is that the vendor itself treats requirements gathering and evaluation as separate steps before adoption. The same data questions apply as for a 
LIMS or ELN
 purchase: what data you hold, in what form, and who owns the models trained on it.

Strengths and limits

Scope.
 The platform is aimed at product-development data (composition, processing, properties) rather than literature search or simulation.

Delivery model.
 It is enterprise SaaS, so it is a commercial engagement with a vendor, not a free download.

Evidence.
 Capabilities on this page are as described by the vendor. Performance claims should be tested on your own data during the evaluation stage.

Alternatives

Option

What it is

Source

matminer

An open-source Python library for data mining materials properties. It offers 40+ ready-made datasets, retrieval from repositories such as The Materials Project and Citrination, and 70+ featurizers that convert compositions and crystal structures into numerical descriptors. It does not contain machine-learning routines itself; it prepares pandas data for other tools. BSD-style licence.

matminer documentation, read 2026-11-07

Materials data repositories

Shared repositories for storing and finding materials data rather than building proprietary models. See

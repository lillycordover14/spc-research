By Tim Booth

            
            Seattle Times
            

Instant impressions after the Seahawks beat the Cardinals 17-3 to improve to 5-4:

Drew Lock was a gamer

“Gamer” is an incredibly general term to describe a football player. It has no specific definition but everyone seems to understand the meaning. It’s someone who plays through – or with – some sort of ailment or injury. It could be someone dealing with adversity. Or it could be someone simply making the plays that are needed in the moment they are called upon.

Drew Lock was a gamer on Sunday. His right elbow is quite clearly not right and Seattle’s entire offensive game plan seemed to take that into account. He appeared to injure his right side or hip during the third quarter, grabbing at that part of his body after a rollout pass and seemed to bother him any time he needed to exert himself on a play the rest of the game.

Considering the limitations and the opponent, the task for Lock became to simply not lose the game. Which he did. Lock was 19 for 28 for 175 and one TD. It was far from perfect, but there weren’t any mistakes, either. A couple of throws led to some collective holding of breath, but rarely was the ball at risk.

Lock knew that he didn’t need to – and probably couldn’t – win this game with his arm. He didn’t lose it either and that was more important on this day.

Lock’s health, though, does raise the question of what Seattle will have to do a week from now against Las Vegas. Will Sam Darnold be back and ready to go? Will Lock’s ailments allow him to play? Stay tuned.

Defense does enough

Matching what Seattle did defensively to Arizona in Week 2 was going to be nearly impossible. And to that point, after not giving up a pass play of longer than 9 yards in that blowout win earlier in the season, it was notable that Arizona had to pass plays longer than that on its opening drive.

But the Seahawks didn’t allow much after that. Take away the end of the first half and Arizona punted on six straight possessions after kicking a field goal on its opening possession of the game. When the Cardinals finally put together another long drive in the fourth quarter, Seattle made it an arduous journey toward the end zone that took 14 plays and more than six minutes and concluded with a goal-line stand that turned away Arizona.

The magic of Gardner Minshew wasn’t very magical this time around. The former Washington State quarterback threw for only 166 yards, less than what he threw for in the fourth quarter and overtime last week against Dallas.

This won’t rank up there with the defensive effort against Kansas City a few weeks ago, but Seattle has held two of its last three opponents to just three points.

Charbs is back

Any questions about Zach Charbonnet’s health should be gone by now. Charbonnet looked terrific in his second game back from the torn ACL he suffered in the playoffs last January. He had some speedy and shiftiness. He bulldozed through some tacklers. He was able to turn some short runs into 6- and 7-yard gains. It’s too bad his catch-and-run along the sideline where he shed a number of tacklers was called back for a penalty, otherwise it would have gone onto the highlight reel. It still might be worthy of a spot.

Charbonnet finished with 77 yards on 16 carries and averaged nearly 5 yards per carry. He might not be all the way back but he looked to be pretty close.

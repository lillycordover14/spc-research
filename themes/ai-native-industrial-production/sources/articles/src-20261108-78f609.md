THE FLATS 
– For the second-straight week, a dreadful start was too much for Georgia Tech football to overcome in a 44-17 loss to Louisville on Saturday night at Bobby Dodd Stadium at Hyundai Field.

Louisville jumped out to a 27-3 lead and held a 306-66 advantage in yardage late in the first half, effectively putting the game out of reach for Georgia Tech before halftime. Tech rallied to outgain the Cardinals by a 284-150 margin over the final 30:56 of the game, but it was too little, too late for the Yellow Jackets, who dropped their fourth game in a row.

Justice Haynes
 
ran for 87 yards and quarterbacks 
Alberto Mendoza
 
and 
Grady Adamson
 
each threw touchdown passes for the Jackets. Wide receiver 
Isaiah Fuhrmann
 
and tight end 
Chris Corbo
 
were on the receiving end of the touchdown throws, with both finding the end zone for the first time the White and Gold.

Defensively, defensive end 
Taje McCoy
 
and safety 
Tae Harris
 
led Tech with eight tackles apiece.

However, the story of the game was once again Georgia Tech’s ineffectiveness in running the football and stopping the run, as Louisville ran for 251 yards (7.0 per rush) while holding the Yellow Jackets to 109 yards on the ground (3.4 avg.). Tech has rushed for less than 110 yards in each of its four-straight defeats.

The Yellow Jackets will look to rebound next Saturday when they travel to longtime rival Clemson. Kickoff is set for 12:30 p.m. The game will be televised nationally on The CW (Peachtree TV - Ch. 17 in Atlanta).
Isaiah Fuhrmann (13) and Chris Corbo both scored their first touchdowns as Yellow Jackets on Saturday night against Louisville (Michael Nance photo)
Postgame Notes

Team Notes

Georgia Tech fell to 2-7 overall and 1-5 in Atlantic Coast Conference play.

Georgia Tech’s four-game losing streak is its longest since it lost its last six games of 2021, the season prior to 
Brent Key
 
taking over as the Yellow Jackets’ interim head coach in 2022.

With the loss, Georgia Tech will not qualify for a bowl game for the first time in Key’s four full seasons as head coach.

The loss was Georgia Tech’s third-straight to Louisville and dropped the Yellow Jackets to 2-3 all-time against the Cardinals.

Until Georgia Tech’s final possession of the first half, the Yellow Jackets were being outgained, 306-66. The Jackets outgained Louisville by a 284-150 margin over the remainder of the game.

After not forcing a turnover in its first four games of the season against NCAA Division I FBS opponents, Georgia Tech has forced five turnovers in the last four games, including four in the last two games.

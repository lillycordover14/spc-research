Sometimes it is from the most unfortunate events that awareness arises.

In the 1970s, the Islamic revolution in Iran rejuvenated the Shi’a ideology that had been lost in a society engulfed in un-Islamic practices influenced by secular Western regimes. In the 1980s, the Iran-Iraq War pitted the Ba’athist secular-nationalist rule against revolutionary Shi’a Muslims. In the early 2000s, the 9/11 attacks triggered a sharp rise in Islamophobia, yet simultaneously prompted the media and various outlets to educate the masses on the fundamentals of Islam.

Moving to more recent times, the events of Oct. 7 opened the world’s eyes to longstanding injustices and systematic oppression existing for years. Beginning of this year, military strikes against Iran have once again refocused global attention on a nation resilient against intimidation and terror.

Their strength lies in their foundational belief as the “Twelver followers,” known in Arabic as Shi’a Ithnā ʿAshariyya. They are the largest branch of Shia Islam. This denomination holds a core belief in the spiritual and political leadership of 12 divinely appointed Imams, culminating in the occulted 12th Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi.

He is revered as the awaited Savior, expected to return at the end of time to establish universal justice and peace in the world. Thus, standing against tyranny is a cornerstone of the Shia faith; adherents continuously commemorate historical events and personalities who opposed oppression or defended the rights of the downtrodden.

One such individual is Lady Fatima, the beloved daughter of Prophet Muhammad. Lady Fatima, whose death anniversary is currently being commemorated, not only faced injustices from the ruling party but she also rose to speak against them. Her legacy of defiance continues to this day through the intentionally unknown location of her grave.

Per her final wishes, she was buried secretly at night by her husband, Imam Ali, and a small group of trusted companions. Centuries later this act alone would provoke questions for the unaware as to why.

Nevertheless, though her life was short, her accomplishments were profoundly impactful. From a tender age, following the passing of her mother, she was the pillar of support for her father during his propagation of his prophetic mission. So deep was her devotion that the Prophet fondly called her “Mother of her father.” She was not only a premier educator for the women of her era, but she also became the ultimate protector of the Imamat — the divine successorship — after her father’s demise. Furthermore, she was an exemplary role model for her children, inspiring them to later stand firmly against the unjust rulers of their own generations.
 

What is more, Lady Fatima’s spiritual legacy can be found in the supplications, prayers, and moral teachings she left behind. Often associated with Mary, mother of Jesus, as women of purity and devotion, Lady Fatima is also the ancestral mother of the Twelve Imams. This lineage concludes in the Awaited Savior, just as Jesus is deemed to be the returning Savior in the Christian faith. The Shia Ithnā ʿAshariyya or Twelver followers believe that Imam al Mahdi, the Twelfth Imam, with Christ along his side, will ultimately return to fill the earth with justice and equality just as it had been filled with injustice and tyranny.

As Lady Fatima famously articulated in her sermon, teaching humanity to establish harmony, good and prevent wrong doing as she explained its benefits: “God” she said, “executed and rendered justice for the sake of putting together and harmonization of the hearts. And (God caused) commanding goodness and forbidding to do evil for the amendment and correction of society and the common people (public).”

This is a contributed opinion column. Fatima Kermalli holds a master’s in Islamic Studies, is a Sunday School teacher and member of the Shia Ithna-Asheri Jamaat of Pennsylvania in Allentown.

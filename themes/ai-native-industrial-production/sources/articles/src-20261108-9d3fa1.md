AMCA Advances Pre-Prototype Work as ADA Tenders for Electrical Harness Design and Computing Hardware&nbsp;&nbsp;Defence.in

[page text]
The Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) is accelerating the groundwork for India’s ambitious 5th-generation stealth fighter, the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA).

Recent procurement activities indicate that the project is steadily transitioning through the complex detailed engineering phases required before physical prototypes can be assembled.

In a significant move, the ADA has published a pair of pivotal tenders on the Government e-Marketplace (GeM).

The first focuses on mapping out the internal wiring architecture of the jet, while the second aims to drastically upgrade the agency’s digital simulation and computing infrastructure.

Together, these steps underscore the intensive backend preparation currently underway.

Mapping the "Nervous System" of the AMCA​
Under tender reference GEM/2026/B/8164482, floated on October 15, 2026, the ADA is seeking consultancy services to design the electrical harness and release corresponding engineering drawings.

Requiring an Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) of ₹10 Lakhs, this two-year consultancy contract recently concluded its bidding window, with bids officially opening on November 6, 2026.

The electrical harness acts as the nervous system of any modern combat aircraft. In a 5th-generation stealth platform like the AMCA, this is a highly intricate engineering challenge.

Designers must precisely route the extensive network of cables that will supply power and transmit high-speed data between the flight control systems, radar, electronic warfare modules, and cockpit avionics.

Furthermore, this routing must strictly adhere to weight limitations and ensure electromagnetic compatibility, guaranteeing that distinct electronic systems do not interfere with one another.

Finalizing this internal wiring blueprint is a mandatory prerequisite before the structural dimensions and internal equipment bays of the prototype can be permanently locked in.

A Massive Computing Upgrade​
Simultaneously, the ADA is moving to bolster its digital backbone.

Through a separate tender (GEM/2026/B/8152801), the agency is procuring a substantial lot of 431 advanced computing units and network infrastructure.

While the initial deadline was slated for early November, a recent corrigendum extended the bid submission timeline to November 19, 2026.

This hardware acquisition highlights the immense computational demands of stealth fighter development.

Designing a radar-evading airframe, conducting sophisticated structural analysis, and running complex flight simulation models require massive processing power.

The introduction of these new machines will provide ADA scientists with the necessary resources to run high-fidelity simulations, minimizing the risk of costly physical errors down the line.

The Road to Prototype​
It is important to view these tenders in their proper context.

While they do not signal the immediate commencement of metal-cutting for the aircraft, they are vital foundational steps.

The AMCA programme has already seen major private sector defence players like Tata Advanced Systems (TASL), Larsen & Toubro (L&T), and Bharat Forge competing for the ₹15,000 crore contract to manufacture the physical prototypes.

By finalizing the electrical architecture and upgrading its simulation capabilities now, the ADA is ensuring that once the prototype manufacturing contracts are awarded, the chosen industrial partners will have a mature, deeply vetted digital design to work from.

Executing these pre-prototype engineering tasks flawlessly is essential to keeping India’s flagship aerospace project on track for its eventual maiden flight.

America's Rare Earth Clock Is Running Out, But the Bottleneck Isn't Where You Think
 
 
 
 
MP Materials (NYSE:MP) expects its rare earth production costs to decline as output rises and a chemical recycling facility comes online, though planned downtime will affect production early in 2027, Chief Financial Officer Ryan Corbett said at the D.A. Davidson Critical Minerals Conference.
 
 
Corbett said the company expects to produce between 5,000 and 6,000 metric tons of neodymium-praseodymium, or NdPr, next year. The company had operated above a monthly run rate of 450 tons during portions of the third quarter, according to D.A. Davidson analyst Matt Summerville, who hosted the Nov. 9 discussion.

Magnet Deliveries to Ramp Gradually
 
 
Corbett said MP expects a measured increase in magnet deliveries following its recent qualification by General Motors. Initial deliveries are scheduled to begin in the fourth quarter of 2026, but he expects the next couple of quarters to be "quite modest" as customers introduce magnets across products and plants.
 
 
Qualification requires more than meeting part specifications, Corbett said, citing work instructions, quality controls, material flow and equipment approvals. He added that the company had initially limited automation at its Texas magnet plant to preserve flexibility and is now automating more material handling.
 
 
Customer inquiries and requests for quotes have exceeded the planned capacity of MP's 10X facility, Summerville said. Corbett said the company has flexibility to select commercial partners because of its offtake framework with the Department of War. He identified robotics, hard disk drives and data center infrastructure as potential sources of demand.
 
 
 
 
 
Corbett also said secure NdPr supply remains a constraint for magnet makers outside China. He argued that prices would need to rise substantially to justify enough new upstream capacity to support planned downstream expansion.
 
 
Defense and Recycling Opportunities
 
 
Corbett pointed to defense uses for rare earth products in optics, lasers and coatings, as well as growing potential demand for magnets in drones and other technologies. MP recently announced a nine-figure, long-term gadolinium agreement with a U.S. aerospace and defense customer, Summerville said.
 
 
Discussing restrictions on defense sourcing from China and the associated waiver process, Corbett said policymakers were taking a tougher approach. He cautioned, however, that 10X would not be ready to fulfill an order for those customers on Jan. 2.
 
 
On recycling, Corbett said MP's partnership with Apple gives it a pathway to convert various feedstocks into usable NdPr oxide. The company can produce material that is not mixed with primary material or incorporate recycled feedstock into other production, he said.
 
 
 
 
 
MP does not currently collect or strip magnets from end-of-life products, Corbett said, adding that the economics of sorting and collection still need to be resolved. He expects recycling to become a larger part of the business over time as demand grows.
 
 
About MP Materials (NYSE:MP)
 
 
MP Materials operates the Mountain Pass rare earth mine and processing facility in California. The site is a major North American source of rare earth materials, which are used in products such as electric vehicles, wind turbines, electronics and defense systems.
 
 
The company mines and processes rare earth ore into concentrate and separated products, including neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr), a key input for high-strength permanent magnets. MP Materials is also developing domestic magnet manufacturing capabilities to extend its business further along the supply chain.
 
 
Mountain Pass has a long history of rare earth production, and MP Materials acquired the site's operating assets in 2017.

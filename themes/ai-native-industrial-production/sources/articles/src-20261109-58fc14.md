                    Martin Ignacio Zapata Laguado: New Perspectives on Bladder Cancer Imaging and Management at EMUC26

Martin Ignacio Zapata Laguado
, Medical oncologist and internal medicine physician at Instituto Nacional de Cancerologia, shared on 
LinkedIn
:

“Highlights from EMUC26 – Brussels

Europe’s largest GU cancer congress brought urologists, oncologists, radiologists and nuclear medicine physicians together.

My take-home messages on bladder cancer imaging:

Nectin-4 PET – Prof. Lena Unterrainer (Augsburg)

Nectin-4 is expressed in ~87% of urothelial carcinomas and linked to poor prognosis.

EV + pembrolizumab brings toxicity (rash, neuropathy) and high cost (~US$560k/year vs ~US$3k for chemo, as presented): selection matters.

CT remains the recommended staging modality; FDG PET/CT is an adjunct.

Nectin-4 PET could map the target across all lesions, reveal heterogeneity a single biopsy misses and open the door to radioligand therapy.

Still feasibility data. Withholding EV solely because of a negative PET is NOT evidence-based.

This can be interesting:

David Aggen: New Research Explores Nectin-4 Biomarkers and EV Plus Pembrolizumab

First-in-human ⁸⁹Zr-enfortumab vedotin PET (F. Grawe; ESMO 2026)

Higher lesion detection than CT (84.1% vs 67.9%) and FDG PET (89.1% vs 78.5%).

Early signal: high baseline uptake → PR on CT, CR on PET after 3 cycles of EV+P; low/heterogeneous uptake → progression. Hypothesis-generating.

Bladder preservation: how much tumour is too much? – Martina Pecoraro (Radiology)

Validated predictors of OS after TMT (31 studies, 9,416 pts): ECOG ≥2 (HR 2.47), N+ (1.90), CIS (1.81), hydronephrosis (1.65), cT3 – 4 (1.47), GFR ≤60 (1.47); complete TURBT protective (0.77). Size was not among them.

Diameter: no proven threshold (overlapping CIs). MRI volume never tested against TMT outcomes.

MRI: VI-RADS validated; volume promising. MRI does not see CIS.

AI segmentation is feasible but fails in residuals <0.5 cm³ – precisely our good responders.

nacVI-RADS: AUC 0.93 for ypT0 after gem-cis; once in the model, size lost significance.

Real-world gap – Paramananthan Mariappan (Urology)

NHS England 2013 – 2019: a substantial share of MIBC patients fell into ‘Other’, outside the defined treatment combinations.

The most beautiful lesson
: none of this works in silos. The best opportunities for our patients will always come from multidisciplinary care: urology, medical and radiation oncology, radiology, nuclear medicine and pathology at the same table, with the patient at the centre.”

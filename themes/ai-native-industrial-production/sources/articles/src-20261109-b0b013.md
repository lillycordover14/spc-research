Marion Clark Weathers immediately recalled the genesis of her becoming an artist.
“When I was in the third grade, my report card said: Marion doesn’t pay attention in class. She’s always drawing,” Weathers said Wednesday in an interview.
Weathers of Rocky Mount was speaking about the days when she was an elementary school student in Orangeburg, S.C.
The graduate of the University of Georgia’s art school has been an artist throughout her life.
Her creativity is readily evident locally. Her design of two trains is featured on a mural just off Southwest Main Street downtown. Artist Ashley Fabrizio painted the mural.
More of Weathers’s creativity is now displayed at the Tar River Art Gallery at the Mills.
The gallery opened Sunday in a building off Falls Road in what was the Larema Beverage Co. before the business closed in November 2025.
The building, which dates back to 1835, is part of the Rocky Mount Mills commercial and residential development.
“I’ve always believed in having art in Rocky Mount,” Weathers said, adding that the new location “will elevate us to where we need to be.”
The gallery is an expansion of the nonprofit Tar River Art Gallery, which has art displayed at the Gravely Gallery at N.C. Wesleyan University’s Dunn Center arts and event venue in the northern part of the city.
And the Tar River Art Gallery at the Mills is already filled with art for sale.
Weathers worked on a painting on site last week of a barn at Hayes Plantation in the Edenton area that’s now on display.
Playwright and artist Marilynn Barner Anselmi, who is a Nash County resident, said in a phone interview Tuesday that the gallery is almost two years in the making.
She and Jan Sullivan-Volz, who is the art curator at N.C. Wesleyan, were looking for a space to feature local artists.
Anselmi said that the Tar River Art Gallery has about 30 artists who have worked with the gallery to have their artwork displayed and sold there.
About three months ago, Anselmi said she received a phone call from Evan Covington Chavez, director of government and community partnerships for Capitol Broadcasting’s real estate division.
The Rocky Mount Mills is a development of Capitol Broadcasting, which is based in Raleigh.
Anselmi said that Chavez expressed an interest in having an art gallery at the Mills.
Anselmi said she and fellow artists were intrigued by the idea.
She noted that she built the original bar and some side tables at that Falls Road location for the opening of the then-Sweet Taters restaurant.
And she noted that, “I have a long history of working with the Mill. We’ve done conference tables and this and that and everything.”
“So, we were really excited,” Anselmi said.
She said she liked the gallery being in a prime location but also was quite aware of the difficulty of being able, as a small organization, to make rent payments.
Anselmi said negotiations were favorable and the gallery concept was able to move forward.
“So, basically, we’ve got the best spot in town,” Anselmi said.
And she said that the Rocky Mount Mills is going to get a percentage from the sales of art at the Falls Road location and that the Tar River Art Gallery at the Mills also is going to hold some events.
“We’re at a four-month trial run, really, at this point,” she noted.
Chavez in an email Friday said that she believes that this is a huge win for the Rocky Mount Mills campus.
“We are looking forward to seeing all the wonderful regional artists showcased both inside and outside of the space,” Chavez said. “The leaders behind this effort are phenomenal artists, creative thinkers and full of energy. We couldn’t be more proud to partner with them on this!”
Anselmi has previously cited the lack of a gallery in the Rocky Mount area since the closing of the Bel Air Art Center off South Church Street downtown.
Artist Ada Ghuman of Rocky Mount, who is a Cuban American, said on Wednesday that she believes that the location is amazing, especially for a gallery.
Ghuman noted the interior’s high ceilings and generously sized rooms.
“And the Mills has that characteristic,” Ghuman said. “It has that feeling of something special — and so, we’re really happy to be here.”
Ghuman said that she believes that the gallery is going to enhance Rocky Mount.
“And we’re hoping people will appreciate the fact that there’s art,” she said. “I hope they bring everybody they know — and realize that these artists are local or regional. And the artwork here is all original work.”
The gallery is open from 2-6 p.m. on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays.

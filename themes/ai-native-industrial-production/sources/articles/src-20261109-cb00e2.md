Nominal Inc., an Austin-based startup whose software helps engineers design and test complex hardware, has raised $240 million in a Series C round that values the company at $3 billion, roughly triple its valuation from earlier this year.

Lightspeed Venture Partners led the financing and Insight Partners co-led, according to Bloomberg. Lux Capital, Sequoia Capital and Lockheed Martin Ventures also participated. The round is expected to be announced Monday.

The new valuation follows a $155 million Series B extension in March that priced Nominal at $1 billion, led by Founders Fund's Trae Stephens. That deal was preemptive, initiated by the investor rather than the company.

Nominal's platform is aimed at companies building aircraft, nuclear reactors, smart factories and other advanced machines, letting engineers test designs and track progress. The company said revenue has more than tripled so far this year, helped by a surge in defense technology spending, and that more than 80 commercial and government organizations now use its software, including five of the seven largest defense contractors.

"The companies building the world's most advanced machines have decades of hard-won knowledge in their data," said Cameron McCord, Nominal's co-founder and chief executive officer. "We're building the platform that puts that knowledge to work, so engineers can take on harder problems and build things that weren't possible before."

McCord is a former U.S. Navy submarine officer and an Anduril alum, and Anduril, the defense company Stephens co-founded, is among Nominal's key customers. In October, the Air Force selected Nominal for a $71.2 million Strategic Funding Increase investment, a program that requires matching funds from outside the service branch.

Nominal plans to direct the fresh capital toward product development and expansion into new regions and industries. It is also weighing further acquisitions after buying two startups earlier this year.

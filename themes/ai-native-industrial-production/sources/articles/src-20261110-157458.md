NH-Amundi Asset Management has launched an exchange-traded fund (ETF) that concentrates on the value chain for multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs) and semiconductor package substrates, where demand is growing with the spread of artificial intelligence (AI) servers. South Korea's Samsung Electro-Mechanics, the key manufacturer, is fixed at 25% of the portfolio, while the remaining 75% is spread across nine substrate, materials and equipment companies selected using a large language model (LLM).

NH-Amundi Asset Management (CEO Kil Jeong-seop) said Nov. 10 that it listed the "HANARO Samsung Electro-Mechanics Fixed AI Core Substrate" ETF on South Korea's KOSPI market. The product is designed to target the high-value-added semiconductor package substrate market, including MLCCs and flip-chip ball grid arrays (FC-BGAs), whose content per system is rising rapidly as AI servers become more advanced. Samsung Electro-Mechanics, which mass-produces both MLCCs and FC-BGAs, forms the portfolio's core.

The Samsung Electro-Mechanics weight is fixed at 25%. Even if the actual weighting shifts with share-price moves, the fund rebalances quarterly to bring it back to 25%. To prevent money from piling into any single stock, the cap on individual holdings is set at 20%.

The remaining 75% is spread across nine stocks that make up the AI substrate ecosystem. Corporate disclosures, news and brokerage research reports are analyzed with LLM-based AI to calculate each company's relevance to the MLCC and substrate industries, and market capitalization is factored in alongside to finalize the top nine companies. The universe includes not only makers of semiconductor package substrates and high-layer-count printed circuit boards (MLBs) but also materials and equipment suppliers essential to substrate processes.

As of the end of last month, LG Innotek, Doosan, Isu Petasys and Daeduck Electronics ranked near the top after Samsung Electro-Mechanics, with Simmtech, Korea Circuit, Taesung, TLBi and Haesung DS also on the list.

Kim Seung-chul, head of NH-Amundi Asset Management's ETF investment division, said, "As the computing performance of AI accelerators and high-performance servers improves by leaps and bounds, high-capacity MLCCs that stably maintain power efficiency and ultra-fine, high-performance substrates are becoming increasingly important." He added that the product "lets investors gain exposure all at once to South Korea's entire AI substrate value chain, anchored by Samsung Electro-Mechanics, which has global competitiveness."

The product stands out for targeting the way expanding AI infrastructure investment is spilling over into components and materials. Unlike AI-related ETFs that have focused mainly on chip design, memory and finished servers, it broadly covers the value chain from substrates and passive components to the materials and equipment behind them. The design is seen as aiming to keep theme exposure by fixing the core holding while preventing money from crowding into a single large-cap stock.

The use of an LLM for stock selection also sets it apart from conventional thematic ETFs. The approach analyzes unstructured text such as disclosures, news and reports to quantify industry relevance, then combines that with market capitalization to determine holdings. However, the specific criteria and verification methods behind this quantification have not been disclosed, and some critics say it remains to be seen whether actual performance will prove the strategy's sophistication.

Market watchers see the pace at which the demand cycle for high-value-added AI server substrates and MLCCs feeds into South Korean component makers' earnings as the variable that will determine the product's performance. Since many holdings, including Samsung Electro-Mechanics, react sensitively to the investment plans of global server and accelerator customers, the direction of the AI capital-spending cycle is expected to dictate short-term returns.

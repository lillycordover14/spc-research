Boston Dynamics’ Atlas takes on complex factory tasks with AI

사진 확대
 
 
 
 
The humanoid robot ‘Atlas’ uses a power drill at the Robot Metaplant Application Center (RMAC) in Georgia, U.S. (Boston Dynamics)
 
 
Boston Dynamics is pushing its Atlas humanoid robot closer to industrial deployment, demonstrating its ability to handle intricate assembly tasks and adapt autonomously to changing factory conditions.
In a recently released video, Atlas picks up automotive engine covers, transfers them to a mobile cart and adjusts its movements when objects are misplaced or workstations shift. The robot uses cameras and sensors to assess its surroundings and make real-time decisions rather than relying on preprogrammed motions.
The demonstration highlights advances in physical artificial intelligence, a technology that enables robots to translate AI-driven decisions into physical actions and is creating new opportunities for chipmakers, automotive suppliers and robotics companies.
Pretraining in virtual environments
The first step in enabling a robot to perform physical tasks is perception.
Cameras identify the positions of components and containers, while sensors monitor physical contact. Computer vision models determine how objects are oriented and detect when the robot loses its grip or encounters an unexpected obstacle.
The industrial version of Atlas unveiled this year features cameras providing 360-degree environmental awareness and tactile sensing capabilities in its fingers and palms.
Continuous perception is essential for robots operating alongside humans in environments where objects and people are constantly moving.
Nvidia’s AI computing technology serves as a key part of the robot’s processing system.
Boston Dynamics has integrated Nvidia’s Jetson Thor robotics computing platform into Atlas, allowing complex AI models and camera data to be processed inside the robot in real time, reducing dependence on external servers.
Nvidia’s Isaac Lab and simulation technologies are also used to train robotic movements in virtual environments before they are deployed in the physical world.
Boston Dynamics is additionally pursuing joint research with Google DeepMind to incorporate models based on Gemini Robotics into Atlas.
The collaboration is designed to improve the robot’s decision-making capabilities, with Nvidia providing the computing infrastructure, DeepMind developing advanced perception and action models, and Boston Dynamics connecting those capabilities to physical movement through its control technologies.

Robot joint actuators
AI-generated decisions must ultimately be translated into physical movement.
Whole-body and manipulation controllers convert those decisions into signals specifying the force and direction required at individual joints.
Actuators, which combine motors, reduction gears and controllers, then generate the force and rotational movement needed to operate the robot’s joints.
Hyundai Mobis established a strategic partnership with Boston Dynamics earlier this year to supply actuators for Atlas.
During its earnings announcement on Oct. 30, Hyundai Mobis said it would begin producing prototype actuators for Atlas in November at its Uiwang Research Institute in Gyeonggi Province.
The company plans to validate their performance and reliability ahead of full-scale deliveries targeted for 2028.
Hyundai Mobis is also expanding its ambitions beyond mechanical components into semiconductors.
Plans unveiled on Oct. 21 include developing motor-driver integrated circuits to regulate joint motor speed and torque, along with power management ICs to ensure a stable electricity supply to individual components.
While Nvidia’s AI processors calculate what the robot should do, the chips Hyundai Mobis aims to develop would help translate those calculations into actual joint movements.
Robotic hands capable of performing delicate tasks are becoming another major area of competition.
Last month, Boston Dynamics unveiled a redesigned Atlas hand featuring four fingers and tactile sensors.
The new hand has 13 degrees of freedom, up from seven in the previous version, enabling more precise manipulation.
In a demonstration video, Atlas inserted a drill bit and turned a small nut, illustrating its ability to handle tasks requiring fine motor control.
Hyundai Mobis is also pursuing joint development of robotic grippers with Boston Dynamics while planning to expand its component business into perception modules and controllers.
Hyundai Motor to deploy 25,000 robots
South Korean component suppliers are increasingly securing positions in the Atlas supply chain as Hyundai Motor Group prepares for mass production.
The automotive group has reportedly assembled a network of suppliers for the humanoid robot.
Hyundai Mobis and Namuga are expected to collaborate on the main camera system, while MCNEX and Cammsys are reportedly responsible for cameras installed in the robot’s hands.
Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution have reportedly been selected as battery suppliers, with BH EVS, a subsidiary of BH, providing charging solutions.
Hyundai Motor Group aims to establish annual production capacity for 30,000 robots in the United States by 2028.
That year, the company plans to deploy Atlas at Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America (HMGMA) in Georgia, initially assigning the robots to parts sequencing tasks that involve arranging components in the order required for assembly.
The company intends to expand their responsibilities to actual component assembly operations by 2030.
Boston Dynamics has also outlined plans to deploy approximately 25,000 Atlas robots across Hyundai Motor and Kia’s global manufacturing facilities over the coming years.
An industry official said humanoid robots must operate safely even when they mistakenly identify similar-looking components or encounter workers moving through the same workspace.
“Success will depend not only on the computing power of the AI chips installed in these robots, but also on whether companies can build the components, data infrastructure and production networks needed to turn AI decisions into reliable physical movements,” the official said.

A fire at a massive industrial building near Vernon that had mostly been suppressed flared up for a second day, sending plumes of thick, black smoke into the air Tuesday morning.
The fire started in a 15,000-square-foot flooring business at South Alameda and East 55th streets in the Central-Alameda neighborhood about 1 a.m. Monday and took about 200 firefighters more than four hours to extinguish — or so crews thought.
On Tuesday at about 4:30 a.m., the Los Angeles Fire Department 
said the fire had kicked up again
 and was also burning power poles nearby.

Lyndsey Lantz, an LAFD spokesperson, said the enormous building housed an industrial flooring company and was filled with hardwood planking, cork and “huge pallets of flooring materials — very flammable stuff.” 
Although firefighters had the massive blaze under control Monday, there were “pockets of fire still in that structure and something happened that made that fire bigger for a little while,” Lantz said Tuesday. 
Fire-suppression crews stayed at the scene overnight, continuously spraying water, but before dawn  Tuesday, it flared back up and “essentially turned back into a structure fire, so we dispatched units as if we were fighting a new fire again,” she said.
 
Advertisement
 
 
 
 
 
Flames had not spread to neighboring buildings, she said. About 100 firefighters were on the scene Tuesday, with some stationed on the roofs of nearby strucutres, spraying water onto the burned building from above. 
While it was unclear what caused the flames to kick back up, the process of moving materials inside the building and the destruction of walls can introduce oxygen to smoldering ruins, and “some of that can kick off flames,” Lantz said. 
“At this point, there’s white smoke in the air, which is a really good indication that it is mostly steam, so it’s not as much active material burning,” Lantz said Tuesday. “We have a good handle on it.”
The fire started in one of four adjoining structures — the one housing the flooring business — and spread to a second two-story, metal-clad building. 
In the early morning darkness Tuesday, roads surrounding the building filled with firefighters and equipment, with crews spraying water into the piles of mangled metal and burning material on the lot. The Los Angeles Department of Water and Power shut down power lines that were posing a hazard, officials said.
“Crews faced the complicated task of accessing and extinguishing pockets of fire hidden within large quantities of palletized storage,” the LAFD said in a 
news release Monday
 when they initially announced the fire had been knocked down. “Special units including Heavy Equipment and RS3 [LAFD’s firefighting robot] were brought in to help expose, access, and pull apart the burning materials.”

The fire is not expected to affect train service on a nearby rail line and the smell of smoke should dissipate throughout the week, LAFD officials said.
Lantz said some streets remained closed in the area, and “we’re looking at a days-long process” for cleanup because “it’s just a lot of stuff” inside. All of the materials, salvageable and not, will have to be moved, she said. 
No injuries were  reported.

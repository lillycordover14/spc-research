Boston Dynamics has equipped the Atlas humanoid robot with an unusual modification: it now has a pair of wheels instead of legs. This experimental design allows engineers to test how the robot behaves during sustained high-speed movement and when handling heavy loads.
At the same time, the tests help assess the capabilities of its electric motors and machine-learning-based control systems.
 
The modified Atlas is not a prototype of a future commercial product. Boston Dynamics created it as a research platform to determine how accurately controllers trained in a virtual environment can operate real hardware. Another goal of the experiment is to test how the robot’s actuators perform under loads approaching their physical limits.
The engineers implemented the modification relatively simply. They used standard electric-scooter wheels and attached them to Atlas with a custom-designed adapter. The adapter connects directly to the robot’s existing ankle-joint actuators.
 
No other significant changes to the design were necessary. The engineers also did not create a new software model of the robot: they only had to replace the feet with wheels capable of continuous rotation in the simulation.
This simple modification was possible thanks to Atlas’s modular architecture. Boston Dynamics designed the humanoid from the outset with production scaling and easier maintenance in mind. The same design principles made it relatively easy to reconfigure the robot for the experiment.
 
The wheels also proved useful from a testing perspective. They require the motors to deliver substantial torque while maintaining a high rotational speed at the same time. During every acceleration, turn, and stop, the actuators are subjected to sustained loads.

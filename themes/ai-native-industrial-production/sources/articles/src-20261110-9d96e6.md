Nominal Raises $240 Million As AI Moves Deeper Into Hardware Engineering
0
shares
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
AI is no longer being funded only as a layer for text, images, and software workflows. Investors are also backing the systems that help build and test the physical world.
Nominal, an Austin-based startup founded four years ago, has raised $240 million in Series C funding at a $3 billion valuation. The round was led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and co-led by Insight Partners, taking Nominal from a $1 billion valuation in March to $3 billion in eight months.

AI for the machines behind the machine
Nominal is building an AI platform for engineering and testing complex hardware, including aircraft, nuclear reactors, and smart factories. That puts it in a different part of the AI market from the consumer assistants and workplace copilots competing for attention. Its value depends on helping technical teams work with the data, simulations, and testing processes that sit behind high-stakes physical systems.
The company says it now has 80 commercial and government organizations as customers. One of its most visible customers is Anduril, the defense technology company co-founded by Trae Stephens, who also led Nominal’s previous $155 million Series B extension through Founders Fund.
That relationship matters because it shows where AI infrastructure is becoming commercially credible: in environments where software is expected to improve engineering work, not simply generate content. The buyer is not looking for a more fluent interface. It is looking for faster testing, better coordination, and a clearer view of whether a complex system works.
The funding is following deployment, not just potential
Nominal’s latest round also arrives alongside a significant government signal. In October, the U.S. Air Force selected the company for a $71.2 million Strategic Funding Increase investment, a program that requires matching funds from outside the Air Force.
That combination of private capital, industrial customers, and public-sector funding gives the company a stronger story than valuation alone. It suggests investors see AI platforms for hardware as part of a longer procurement and infrastructure cycle, where contracts and deployments can matter more than consumer-scale user numbers.
There is still a familiar startup risk. A fast valuation climb does not prove that Nominal can turn early government and defense demand into a durable, broad-based business. But the direction is clear. AI’s next competitive layer may be built less around who has the most visible chatbot and more around who becomes embedded in the difficult systems that other industries cannot afford to get wrong.

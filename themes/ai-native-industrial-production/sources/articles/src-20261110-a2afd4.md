CASA Approves First 3D Printed Flight Components for Firefighting Helicopters

Markforged
 has announced that Australian aerospace engineering firm Plane Support, in partnership with aerial disaster responder McDermott Aviation, has secured Australian Parts Manufacturer Approval (APMA) from the Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA). The certification permits the manufacture of cabin air conditioning ducting for Bell 214B helicopters using additive manufacturing.

The approval addresses a critical challenge in aerospace supply chain resiliency. McDermott Aviation operates the world's largest fleet of the Bell 214B — heavy-lift aircraft widely deployed for aerial bushfire suppression — and holds the aircraft's type certificate. With legacy replacement parts increasingly scarce, unserviceable components had historically threatened to ground essential emergency aircraft. By shifting production of the ducts locally to Queensland, Australia, the operators have bypassed stalled global supply chains, establishing on-demand control over fleet readiness.

The project marks a shift from rapid prototyping into certified, flight-ready end-use production through advanced material innovation and virtual validation.

Plane Support built a dedicated aerospace additive manufacturing laboratory centered on the industrial Markforged 
FX10
 platform. Reverse-engineering the complex internal duct geometry required strict structural verification; the engineering team deployed Markforged Simulation software to analyze and optimize part performance virtually prior to printing.

The final ducting is manufactured using Onyx FR-A, Markforged's aerospace-grade, flame-retardant micro-carbon-fiber-filled nylon. For aviation compliance, Onyx FR-A provides complete lot-level material traceability, delivering the documentation and repeatability required by airworthiness frameworks.

Developed over nearly two years in direct consultation with the regulator, the initiative is said to set a benchmark regulatory roadmap. Plane Support established the procedures, quality controls and nondestructive inspection methods required under CASA Part 21K, demonstrating that industrial composite additive manufacturing can consistently satisfy primary aviation safety standards and paving the way for future component approvals across multiple airframes.

The project was supported by Markforged's Australian reseller partner, Invenio, reinforcing the local technical expertise and support network driving advanced additive manufacturing in the region.

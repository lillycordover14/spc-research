    ServiceFirst implementation timeline revealed at Employee Forum meeting

    Employees impacted by ServiceFirst will be notified Nov. 12. The forum also discussed AI integration at the University.

Hilton Swing
, 
Escritor
                 
             •  
            
November 10, 2026
 

 

Grace Davidson
 
Rebecca Howell is the chair of the Employee Forum. Photo Courtesy of UNC Media Relations.
 

The UNC Employee Forum met on Friday to discuss campus events, updates to the ServiceFirst timeline, the University’s integration of AI and a new partnership with the University of North Carolina School of the Arts. 

ServiceFirst: Official timeline revealed

Scott Savage, associate vice chancellor for organizational strategy and performance, gave updates and revealed an official timeline for the University’s implementation of ServiceFirst — the University’s effort designed to pinpoint institutional inefficiencies and cut overlapping departments to increase strategic cost savings across six functional areas: IT, HR, procurement, finance, research administration and communications.

Savage said leaders have spent the past several months identifying employees whose responsibilities and duties align with the new academic and administrative service centers.

Savage said the academic shared service center will support 10 units that collectively employ around 3,300 staff members, with fewer than 50 moving into the new center. Savage did not say what would happen with the other 3,250 employees. 

Savage then told the forum that impacted employees will be notified the week of Nov. 16, followed by the first all-staff meeting for shared service center employees on Dec. 1. Savage added that preparations throughout December will focus on logistics, including technology needs, work locations and employee schedules before the centers formally begin operating in January.

Lauren Partin, interim chief human resources officer, said she reviewed job descriptions and staffing structures across the University to identify employees performing transactional and process-oriented responsibilities that could be centralized. She said the service center model the University is moving to is to support multiple units rather than limiting expertise to a single department.  

Many different members of the forum expressed their concern for the timeline Savage laid out. When asked if impacted employees have already been notified or if the announcement on Nov. 12 will be the first they hear of it, Savage said he did not know the level of internal communications and transparency within the individual units, because they primarily communicate with senior leaders. 

Forum Chair Rebecca Howell said that the deans and senior leaders receiving the bulk of the communication and updates about the timeline is not helpful to employees who actually know how the day-to-day work gets done.

“It lacks empathy and reflects the lack of care for the human side of these choices,” Howell said. “It doesn’t change the decision makers’ holidays, but it does change those that it’s going to impact.”

New AI framework and the University’s AI ambitions

Vice chancellor and provost Magnus Egerstedt then presented the University’s new AI framework. He said this new framework is built to help students and staff across the University in five main ways: teaching and learning, research, computation and tools, operations and engagement. He added that the University was recently ranked No. 11 in the country in 
AIREDEX’s AI Readiness Index
. 

“So N.C. State, they are the [agricultural] extension, right? We’re thinking about being the AI extension,” Egerstedt said. 

Egerstedt added that the University recently received a $40 million grant from the OpenAI Foundation to conduct AI-driven personalized cancer vaccine development. 

Elizabeth DuBose, a social and clinical research specialist attending the forum, called for transparency on two fronts in response to Egerstedt’s comments: first, more transparency and an assessment of the environmental impact of AI, and second, transparency
 
on who the University will be outsourcing AI to. 

“We can’t dance around it, and we can’t always put it on — I hate to use the word — back burner,” Dubose said. “It’s a very real thing, and the University is a little vulnerable avoiding the topic, and it comes up a lot from people I represent, not just me.”

Egersted responded by saying that the University plans to keep its options open with different AI companies and currently does not want to lock in exclusive deals with any one corporation.

In addition, he added that the University currently has a UNC System-wide proposal going to the National Science Foundation around building low-energy AI hubs in North Carolina in order to reduce the environmental impacts. He also said that, in his view, AI could be the answer to the climate crisis that humans have struggled with. 

“All of a sudden, we have models for how climate evolves, thanks to AI, that we didn’t before,” Egerstedt added.

Dubose brought up climate change concerns in response, saying that forests are being destroyed to build data centers.

“And I appreciate that. What I want to say is I don’t think it’s enough,” Dubose said. “I think leadership needs to be harder on this.”

Campus initiatives, University settlement and new partnerships

Egerstedt spoke about campus initiatives, highlighting the research efforts from junior faculty and students, such as Research Week and the Research Fair. He added that the research these groups conduct helps the University create tangible impacts across the planet. 

Egerstedt then presented to the forum updates on the recent 
settlement
 the University reached with the U.S. Department of Justice and the Department of Education. He said the University, without admitting any wrongdoing, closed the pending investigations and avoided any adverse actions and fines. He added that the University has plans to move forward from this settlement.

“We’re tightening up some of the things around training, and that’s pretty much the outcome of it,” Egerstedt said. 

Egerstedt and Elizabeth Engelhardt, vice provost for arts and culture, informed the forum about the 
new partnership
 with the UNC School of the Arts in Winston-Salem. The new partnership includes programming between the University’s production companies and more structural components, such as helping them negotiate and scale up on things such as campus health and dining. 

Passing of proclamation condemning acts of harassment

The forum ended with the passing of Proclamation 2602. The proclamation was brought to the forum to discuss at the last meeting in October, but was revised and reviewed. The proclamation condemns all acts of harassment and intimidation occurring within the University’s campus and surrounding community. 

Earlier in the year, several Black student organizations were 
targeted
 in a racially motivated attack at Greenlaw Hall on Aug. 31. Additionally, members of the UNC Taiwanese Student Association and other Taiwanese students were 
racially harassed
 by a group of white men knuckle-walking and making monkey gestures during a campus tour led by the organization on Aug. 29.

The proclamation also said that the members of the forum were disappointed with the University’s response to acts of racial violence and harassment earlier in the semester, calling for a more timely, proactive and public approach to warning community members about these incidents of hate and intolerance. 

What’s next:

The next Employee Forum meeting will take place on Dec. 4.

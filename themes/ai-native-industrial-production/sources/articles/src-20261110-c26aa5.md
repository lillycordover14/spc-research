Through January 31, 2027, the facade of the tower at the Governor’s Palace in Parma will host UNICHE, a digital exhibition curated by Serena Tabacchi and Cesare Biasini Selvaggi. Twelve international artists explore the possibilities of artificial intelligence, code, and new visual languages.

In 
Parma
, the facade of the 
Palazzo del Governatore
 tower is becoming a testing ground for
contemporary digital art.
 Through January 31, 2027, every day from 4 p.m. to midnight, the building overlooking Piazza Garibaldi will host 
UNICHE.
 Featuring 
twelve international female artists working with code and artificial intelligence
, this video mapping exhibition brings the work of twelve leading figures in the international digital art, artificial intelligence, and new media art scenes into the public space.

The project, curated by 
Serena Tabacchi
 and 
Cesare Biasini Selvaggi

LEI: Portraits of Women, from Van Gogh to Modigliani and Beyond. Masterpieces from the National Gallery of Modern and Contemporary Art

 (1990, Vilnius, Lithuania),

Rosa Menkman

Chiara Passa

Kamilia Kard

Jenni

Through the technique of 
projection mapping
, the tower’s historic architecture is transformed into a dynamic visual canvas, upon which the works engage in a dialogue with history and the urban context. The project invites reflection on contemporary artistic languages, focusing on issues such as the relationship between artificial intelligence and creativity, ecological memory, speculative biology, and computational aesthetics. The research of the twelve artists thus forms a collective exploration of the expressive possibilities of digital technologies and the transformations in the way images are produced and perceived.

A work from the exhibition 
UNICHE: Twelve International Artists Between Code and Artificial Intelligence

The exhibition begins with 
*Structures of Being*
 (2024) by Sofia Crespo, which reworks the building’s architectural elements through organic forms in constant metamorphosis, drawing inspiration from Antoni Gaudí’s vision and his ability to translate the structures of nature into architecture. The relationship with the natural world reappears in Linda Dounia’s 
*Once Upon A Garden*
 (2021–2024), a speculative archive created using artificial intelligence that reconstructs the visual memory of extinct or endangered plant species.

Physicality and the relationship with technology, on the other hand, are at the heart of Kamilia Kard’s 
*La danza del sesto petalo*
 (2026), while Krista Kim’s 
*Continuum*
 (2017) transforms the screen into a contemplative space, evoking Zen gardens through subtle chromatic transitions. The exploration of virtual landscapes continues with Sara Ludy’s 
Clouds
 series, comprising 
*Deep Pond*
, 
*Sky Lapis*
, and 
*Sky
 
Ruby*
 (2017–2018): through generative processes, the artist simulates organic phenomena and creates moving paintings and artificial cosmic spaces. In 
*Horology*
 (2026), Rosa Menkman, on the other hand, addresses the theme of the algorithm, interpreted as a new contemporary myth and a tool through which to interpret reality.

The dialogue between human and digital creativity also emerges in Jenni Pasanen’s 
*Mirror Mirror*
 (2022), a work in which the two dimensions merge into a fluid relationship between human and machine. Chiara Passa’s animations— 
*Abstract Class*
 and 
*Null Void 0*
 (2017) and 
*Dimensioning*
 (2015)—reimagine architecture as a living entity, challenging the separation between physical and virtual space.

The cosmic dimension characterizes Anne Spalter’s 
*Meet Me in the Sun*
 (2018), which reworks materials from NASA’s archives into a psychedelic kaleidoscope dedicated to the Sun, taken as a universal symbol of unity. In 
*Khutulun*
 (2026), Sasha Stiles, on the other hand, draws on the figure of the legendary Mongolian warrior, forging a connection between poetry, tradition, and technological experimentation.

Ivona Tau addresses the identity and representation of the female body in 
*UnBeautiful*
 (2024) and 
*Bi(t)ological Clock*
 (2025), works in which the artist uses artificial intelligence and blockchain to challenge aesthetic standards and reflect on biological time. The exhibition concludes with Sarah Zucker’s works 
*Shadow Bodies*
 (2019) and 
*The Dance of the Rendered*
 (2021), which draw on VHS aesthetics and glitch art to explore digital presence as a possible extension of the human nervous system and its shadows.

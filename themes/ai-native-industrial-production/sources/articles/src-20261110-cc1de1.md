Swampscott Maple Trees Hit by Widespread Outbreak of Tar Leaf Spot Fungus&nbsp;&nbsp;Swampscott Tides

[page text]
The Swampscott Tree Committee reported that many of the town’s maple trees are showing largeblackspots on their leaves.

The spots are caused by a non-native fungus known as tar leaf spot, or Rhytisma acerinum. According to UMass Extension, Norway maples are particularly susceptible to the disease. The Swampscott Tree Committee said this year’s widespread outbreak across southern New England is reminiscent of a similar outbreak in 2017.

“The good news is that tar leaf spot, while unattractive, has little effect on the long-term health of affected trees,” the committee wrote. “Raking and disposing of fallen infected leaves can help reduce the spread of the fungus next spring.

Black spots typically begin appearing on red and silver maple leaves between mid-July and early August. The spots gradually grow larger, eventually resembling drops of tar.

Severe infections can also cause maple trees to lose their leaves prematurely, according to Cornell University’s College of Agriculture and Life Sciences.

However, research has shown that the fungus does not cause long-term damage to affected trees. The fungus stays on the leaf surface, never entering the tree’s vascular system. 

Removing and disposing of infected leaves in the fall can reduce the number of fungal reproductive structures that survive the winter and produce spores the following spring.

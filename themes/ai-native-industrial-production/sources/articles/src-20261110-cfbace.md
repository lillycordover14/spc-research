UNC-Wyoming Preview: Tar Heels Looking to Stack Performances Against Cowboys&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
North Carolina (2-0) vs. Wyoming (2-0)
Chapel Hill, N.C. — Dean E. Smith Center
Tuesday, Nov. 10 — 7 p.m. ET
TV: 
ACC Network
 (Anish Shroff, Terrence Oglesby)

Quotables

Michael Malone
 after UNC’s win vs. Wofford

“Another game where we shoot 50 percent from the field. I thought our rebounding and our effort was right. We just need that second unit to step up defensively. I thought our starting unit was great tonight.” — Sundance Wicks after Wyoming’s win vs. Idaho State

Pregame Notes

Last Time Out:
 North Carolina throttled Wofford, 109-70, behind a 52 percent shooting performance and a double-double from 
Maximo Adams
. The Tar Heels showed improvement on the glass and in their assist-to-turnover ratio while holding the Terriers to 33 percent from the field. Ten different Tar Heel players scored five or more points as UNC reached its highest scoring mark since Feb. 5, 2019.

Wyoming moved to 2-0 after a 31-point win over Idaho State in its first Division I outing of the 2026 season. The Cowboys dominated down low, outrebounding their opponent by 23 boards — tallying 21 offensive rebounds — and scoring 46 points in the paint. Sophomore guard Nasir Meyer led Wyoming with 18 points, nine rebounds and two assists.

Analyzing the Cowboys:
 The 2026-27 season marks the third for head coach Sundance Wicks, who served as an assistant prior to his hiring in 2024. Wyoming improved from a fringe KenPom top-200 team in Wicks’ first season to finishing 95th nationally following last year’s 18-15 campaign a season ago. 

The Cowboys welcome back two backcourt starters in Khaden Bennett and the aforementioned Meyer, both of whom averaged double-digit points, 4.9 rebounds and more than two assists in 2025-26. Gavin Gores, a 6-foot-10 forward, contributed off the bench a year ago and has started both games this season. Wyoming projects to round out its starting lineup with transfer forward Mike Mora from Divison II Cal State Monterey Bay and Parker Gerrits, who averaged 1.8 points per game at Washington State last season.

While grounded in experience, the Cowboys do welcome 10 newcomers, four freshman and six by way of the transfer portal. The majority of the impacts from those additions are in reserve roles, though.

Madden Smiley, a well-regarded recruit from Colorado, is one of Wyoming’s top freshman contributors, while sixth-man senior guard Uriyah Rojas has the third-most minutes through two games despite coming off the bench. Canin Jefferson started 18 games at North Alabama last season averaging 12.4 points, though he’s been limited to 14.5 minutes per game as a reserve after two contests.

Averaging 94.5 points per game, Wicks’ group is shooting 52.5 percent from the field. Defensively, the Cowboys are recording 11.5 steals per game and holding opponents to 34 percent shooting and 55 points.

Wyoming was selected to finish fifth in the Mountain West preseason poll, led by Meyer, who is apart of the preseason all-conference squad. KenPom ranks the Cowboys at 117th, while Bart Torvik and Evan Miya have similar interpretations of the Tar Heels’ upcoming opponent, grading them at 113th and 116th, respectively. Most notably for Tuesday’s matchup is the fact that Wyoming is No. 1 in the country in offensive rebounding percentage through its first two games, according to KenPom, at 63.6 percent.

The Matchup:
 Even with an improvement against Wofford, UNC’s rebounding troubles still loom large, making the date with Wyoming another noteworthy data point. The Cowboys aren’t necessarily imposing with length, primarily rotating two big men over 6-foot-7. But of the 10 Wyoming players who notched more than 20 total minutes across two games, nine of them have secured five or more rebounds. Meyer (6-foot-7) and Bennett (6-foot-4) are its leading rebounders. In one Division I contest against the 341st-ranked team in KenPom, the Cowboys put on a glass-cleaning clinic. By contrast, while Adams’ 11 rebounds elevated the Tar Heels’ numbers on Friday, the starting UNC frontcourt of 
Jarin Stevenson
 and 
Sayon Keita
 have combined for 11 total boards over the first two games.

One area Wyoming struggled in both prior years under Wicks is sending opponents to the free throw line. After ranking 343rd and 360th in consecutive years (of 365 D-I teams) in defensive free throw rate, Wyoming awarded Idaho State with 27 attempts last time out, as 20 of its 62 points came from the stripe.

UNC is up to 64 trips to the line in two games, tied for 28th in the country. With an increased emphasis on getting downhill last week and the Cowboys’ historical struggles in that area, Carolina’s ability to continue to attack the basket and draw fouls becomes an area to watch. The Tar Heels are shooting 75 percent from the line.

Matt Able
, who had a quiet night with the Terriers in town, will present an intriguing matchup on the wing with Meyer, Wyoming’s do-it-all sophomore. Meyer is 3-for-4 from 3-point range this season and 52 percent from the floor, tasking UNC with another high-volume perimeter player to slow down. Dating back to North Carolina’s three-game exhibition slate, it has allowed an opposing player to score north of 22 points in every game but one.

Last Meeting:
 North Carolina and Wyoming’s lone matchup came in the 2003 National Invitation Tournament (NIT), a game that resulted in a 90-74 win for the Tar Heels at the Smith Center.

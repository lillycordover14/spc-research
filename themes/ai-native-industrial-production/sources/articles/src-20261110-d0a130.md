Marathon County home sales: Three seven-figure deals snap the high-end lull, week of Nov. 2–6&nbsp;&nbsp;Wausau Pilot & Review

Wausau Pilot & Review
Three seven-figure sales — a Rib Mountain land deal, a Stettin home and a Brighton commercial property — pulled Marathon County’s high end out of last week’s lull.
The biggest transfer of the week never touched a house. BPW Development LLC sold undeveloped land in the village of Rib Mountain to Woodbury Custom Homes LLC for $1.16 million, recorded Nov. 2, the second-largest Marathon County deal of the past 30 days behind the $3.76 million County Road XX purchase in Kronenwetter. The record lists no property address, but a builder paying seven figures for raw ground signals another round of new construction in the county’s highest-priced community.
The top residential sale was 4402 Hilltop Ave. in the town of Stettin, which Bryan Austin sold to Eric M. Udermann for $1.15 million, recorded Nov. 3. One week earlier, nothing in the county had sold for more than $555,000.
The third seven-figure deal, recorded Nov. 6, was commercial: Matthew Oberholtzer sold the land and buildings at 100794 Kington Road in the town of Brighton to Timber Drying LLC for $1.02 million. Commercial activity continued down the list. Maki Investments LLC sold 6007 Municipal St. in Weston to RC Janik Properties LLC for $900,000, 2008 Grant Street LLC sold a commercial condominium at 320 Ross Ave., Unit 8, in Schofield to Huddle Up Housing LLC for $399,000, and a Main Street building in Mosinee went to Created Anew Properties LLC for $225,000.
Behind the Stettin sale, Dennis J. Moran sold 212510 Baywood Drive in the town of Cleveland to Robert Cagle for $820,000, a Strawberry Lane home in Rib Mountain brought $734,900 and a home on Old Highway 51 in Kronenwetter sold for $595,819, the village’s highest price of the week. In all, seven properties sold for more than $500,000, up from zero the week before.
The burst at the top runs against the statewide current. “The primary reason for reduced sales was rising mortgage rates, which softened buyer demand for existing homes,” said Amy Curler, 2026 chair of the Wisconsin REALTORS Association board of directors, in the association’s latest monthly report.
Investors and builders stayed busy in Marathon County regardless. Limited liability companies bought 10 properties this week, from the Rib Mountain land and the Brighton and Weston commercial deals down to Wausau’s starter stock: Sandquist Properties LLC paid $175,000 for 3417 N. 11th St., Kolbe Apartments LLC bought 704 Flieth St. from an estate for $146,000 and A & D Contractors LLC picked up 808 13th Drive for $59,500. Investor-held homes flowed the other direction, too. Schumacher Realty LLC, New Day Holdings LLC, Heindl Property LLC and DN and JN Holding Company LLC each sold a house to an individual buyer in Wausau, Rothschild or Hatley at prices from $133,000 to $229,900. The Dale E. Tauchen Family Legacy Trust bought on South 13th Avenue in Wausau for $210,000, and the George T. Snyder Survivor’s Trust sold a Camellia Street home in Rib Mountain for $372,500.
Two move-up chains surfaced in the records. A Weston seller on Normandy Street closed at $240,000 and bought on South Third Avenue in Wausau for $270,000. Another seller moved within Wausau, selling on North Sixth Avenue for $175,000 and buying on East Union Avenue for $250,000.
Land made up 13 of the week’s 67 transfers. Farmland in the town of Johnson sold for $400,000 and a parcel on State Highway 153 in the town of Elderon for $90,000; a single-family lot in the town of Wausau brought $290,000, and Bartgar Investments LLC paid $36,000 for a Merrill Avenue lot in the village of Maine. The city of Schofield, meanwhile, bought four small land-only parcels for a combined $29,694, two of them from Greenheck Fan Corporation on Ross Avenue and Greenheck property, and the others on Ross Avenue and Pine Street.
Those municipal purchases explain Schofield’s eye-catching $32,723 median across eight transfers. Elsewhere, Wausau led the week with 15 transfers at a median of $175,000, Rib Mountain’s five sales carried the week’s highest community median at $482,000, Kronenwetter’s six came in at $317,950 and Mosinee’s four at $255,000. Over the past 30 days, Wausau recorded 67 sales, and Kronenwetter, with 21, has now edged past Weston, with 19, one week after the two communities were tied for second place. Weston logged just two transfers this week.
The county’s middle 50% of sales over the past 30 days ran from $160,000 to $345,000, with a median of $250,000. This week’s 67 transfers skewed lower: the median was $200,000, 13 properties sold for less than $100,000 and another 20 sold between $100,000 and $200,000.
October closed with a Marathon County median of $251,000, down 3.8% from September’s 2026 high of $261,000 but up 5% since December and $11,500, or 4.8%, above the county’s trailing 12-month baseline of $239,500. The six-county regional median fell to $220,000 in October, leaving Marathon County $31,000 ahead, the widest gap since March. The first week of November reads $200,000 countywide against $210,000 across the region, the first time the county has trailed the region in the tool’s 12-month table, though a single week of recordings is far from a full month.
Statewide, the Wisconsin REALTORS Association reported September existing-home sales down 2.4% from a year earlier while the median price rose 1.5% to $342,000, the slowest annual increase since June 2014. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate averaged 6.86%, up 51 basis points over 12 months and the highest since January 2025, and affordability fell 5.5% over the year. Supply improved slightly, to 4.4 months statewide, with rural counties at 6.1 months and large metro counties at 4 months. Through nine months, Wisconsin sales were up 2.8% and the year-to-date median was $346,500, up 6%. “Mortgage rates have reached their highest level since January 2025,” said Tom Larson, WRA president and CEO. “If these trends continue, both buyers and sellers will be affected.”
Nationally, the National Association of Realtors reported existing-home sales rose 1.5% in September to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.99 million, down 2.2% from a year earlier. The national median was $417,500, up 1.3%, with 1.6 million homes on the market, or 4.8 months of supply. Midwest sales were flat for the month and down 2.1% from a year earlier at a 930,000 annual pace, while the Midwest median rose 3.1% to $329,600. “August and September marked the two slowest months for home sales this year,” said Lawrence Yun, NAR chief economist. “Further increases in mortgage rates in recent weeks mean fewer buyers can qualify to purchase a home.”
Marathon County’s $251,000 October median sits $91,000 below the Wisconsin figure, $78,600 below the Midwest and $166,500 below the national median, a gap that keeps the Wausau area among the more affordable corners of a market most buyers find increasingly out of reach.
Whether this week’s seven-figure burst marks a turn or a blip will become clearer when the next state and national reports arrive later this month, with the Federal Reserve’s December meeting looming over rates.
Recorded transfers may reflect a lag of days to weeks between sale, recording and Department of Revenue posting. Only arm’s-length sales of $1,000 or more are included.

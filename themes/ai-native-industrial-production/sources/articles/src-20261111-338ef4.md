Buyers are stepping back into a battered rare earth magnet maker after a rough post-earnings stretch. 
USA Rare Earth
 (
NASDAQ:USAR
) stock is at $8.82, up 11% in morning trading. The gain reverses a 10% drop on November 10, the first session after the company's quarterly report.
 
 
 
 
Pavliha / Getty Images
 
 
 
Shares of USA Rare Earth are bouncing amid a broad risk-on mood across growth stocks. Rare earth names had been squeezed on November 10 as a hotter-than-expected October Consumer Price Index (CPI) report lifted expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike, and Chicago Fed official Austan Goolsbee called that inflation report "borderline disturbing," and despite the rebound, USA Rare Earth stock remains well below where it traded a month ago.
 
 
 
 
 
How USA Rare Earth's Third-Quarter Miss Sparked the Sell-Off
 
 
USA Rare Earth reported its Q3 2026 results after the close on November 9, and the quarter fell short of forecasts. Analyst consensus called for $13.55 million in revenue, while USA Rare Earth delivered $3 million and an adjusted loss of $0.44 per share against an analyst consensus loss of $0.146. Cantor Fitzgerald cut its price target on the stock on November 10.
 
 
Management tied the shortfall to shortages of dysprosium oxide and samarium metal linked to China's export restrictions. CEO Thras Moraitis said on the November 9 call that the shortage "reinforces the very rationale for USA Rare Earth's existence: securing the long-term supply of these vital materials."

Why USA Rare Earth Bulls See More Than a Weak Quarter
 
 
The bulls' case starts with product reaching customers. USA Rare Earth booked its first commercial magnet sales from its Stillwater, Oklahoma, plant in September, and commercial-scale Serra Verde shipments from Brazil began in October. For Q4 2026, USA Rare Earth expects Serra Verde to sell at least 325 tonnes of total rare earth oxide.

Chief Financial Officer Rob Steele said the Serra Verde basket price has averaged around $97 per kilogram quarter to date, against a floor value of around $43 a kilogram, on record dysprosium and terbium prices outside China. Moraitis noted USA Rare Earth ended the quarter with $1.2 billion in cash and cited letters of intent and memos of understanding (MOUs) covering approximately 2,500 tonnes of magnets.
 
 
Execution risk hasn't gone away for USA Rare Earth. Management cautioned that magnet customer qualification can take six to nine months at the low end and as long as two years, and current prototype sales are low volume.
 
 
 
 
 
What the Peer Group Says About USA Rare Earth
 
 
Rare earth giant 
MP Materials
 (
NYSE:MP
) is climbing alongside USA Rare Earth, though by a narrower margin. MP Materials stock is at $38.01, up 4%. That gain suggests part of USA Rare Earth's jump reflects sector-wide strength.
 
 
Antimony specialist 
United States Antimony
 (
NYSE:UAMY
) is inching higher. United States Antimony stock is at $3.25, up 1%. With that producer posting only a modest gain, a meaningful share of USA Rare Earth's move looks like a stock-specific bounce from its post-earnings sell-off.
 
 
How USA Rare Earth's Business Model Stands Apart
 
 
USA Rare Earth is building a mine-to-magnet chain largely through acquisition, linking Serra Verde with its LCM metals unit, the Stillwater magnet plant and the Round Top project in Texas. MP Materials runs a more established version of that model, mining and separating rare earths at Mountain Pass in California, making magnets in Fort Worth, Texas, and counting a major automaker and a large technology company among its magnet customers.
 
 
 
 
 
United States Antimony works in a different corner of critical minerals, smelting antimony for munitions, flame retardants and semiconductors, with the Defense Logistics Agency as a key customer. 
Critical Metals
 (
NASDAQ:CRML
) is at an even earlier stage. The pre-revenue developer is developing the Tanbreez rare earth project in Greenland.
 
 
USA Rare Earth's Rebound Hinges on Serra Verde and Stillwater
 
 
The next test for USA Rare Earth is whether Serra Verde meets its Q4 sales target and Stillwater turns prototype orders into qualified, repeat magnet business. Bulls see a well-funded company with product shipping and realized pricing running ahead of its floor. On the flip side, the bears point to a sharply smaller revenue base, a wider adjusted loss and raw material shortages tied to China's export policy.
 
 
A rebound this sharp after a steep sell-off could fade if rate concerns return, and USA Rare Earth stock remains volatile. It may be worth tracking how USA Rare Earth's Serra Verde ramp-up progresses before drawing any firm conclusions.

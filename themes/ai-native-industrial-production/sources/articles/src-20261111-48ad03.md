The Twelve Kingdoms
 remains one of the most impressive 
isekai
 anime ever made. Long before the genre became filled with overpowered heroes and video game mechanics, Yoko Nakajima's journey offered a different kind of adventure. After being thrown into a mysterious world, she finds herself facing major obstacles. However, despite this anime’s excellent storytelling, it has some weaknesses.

The slow pacing can be difficult to tolerate, and the 45-episode series ends without adapting the whole story. Luckily, there are several isekai anime that tell fantasy stories of similar quality, while improving some aspects of 
The Twelve Kingdoms
. They have equally interesting main characters and explore themes of war, politics and survival. These anime offer adventures that are more ambitious than 
The Twelve Kingdoms
.

                        The Vision of Escaflowne Balances Political Drama With Exciting Mecha Battles
               

Close
Hitomi Kanzaki looking up in wonder at something not pictured on screen in The Vision of Escaflowne
Image via Studio Sunrise
Two massive mecha battling one another in The Vision of Escaflowne.
Image via Sunrise
Hitomi and Van stand in a ruinous battlefield in The Vision of Escaflowne.
Image via Studio Sunrise

Hitomi Kanzaki is a typical Japanese high school student whose life changes after meeting Van Fanel, a strange prince from another world. Soon, she finds herself in 
the fantasy world of Gaea
, where there are many warring kingdoms and giant mechas.
 
In 
The Vision of Escaflowne
, Hitomi gets entangled in the fight against the Zaibach Empire, and her unusual ability to predict future events makes her important to the conflict. The best thing about this anime is how it skillfully merges different genres. Van's battles in the powerful Escaflowne provide exciting mecha action.
 
Meanwhile, the complicated relationship between Hitomi, Van and Allen Schezar introduces romance to the story. 
The Twelve Kingdoms
 explores political responsibility deeply, but 
The Vision of Escaflowne
 has a more varied adventure with stronger romantic elements. With its gorgeous animation and catchy soundtrack, 
The Vision of Escaflowne
 is one of the most rewarding classic isekai anime.

                        Every Victory Comes at a Terrible Cost in Grimgar
               

Grimgar fantasy and ash
Image via A-1 Pictures

 
Grimgar of Fantasy and Ash
 follows a group of people who find themselves in a fantasy world without any memories of their past lives. They have no special powers or knowledge that could make their new lives easier. With few options available, Haruhiro and his companions become volunteer soldiers who hunt monsters to earn money to survive.
 
Unfortunately, even the meager task of defeating a goblin proves far more difficult than they expect. While Yoko's journey in 
The Twelve Kingdoms
 involves constant danger, 
Grimgar 
makes survival feel even more frightening. Its characters aren't naturally talented warriors. Their first successful goblin hunt is particularly disturbing because the creature desperately fights to stay alive.
 
The scene makes it clear that taking a life in this anime is nothing like playing a video game. 
Grimgar of Fantasy and Ash
 doesn't have the political depth of 
The Twelve Kingdoms
, but its attention to grief and the struggle to survive gives its fantasy adventure a more personal touch.

                        Now and Then, Here and There Explores the Horrors of War in a World Without Mercy
               

Shu tries to protect Lala-Ru in Now and Then, Here and There.
Image via AIC

 
Now and Then, Here and There
 is one of 
the darkest isekai anime ever made
. Shu Matsutani is an energetic boy who tries to help a mysterious girl named Lala-Ru after seeing her in danger. However, his attempt to protect her leads him into a ruined world ruled by a cruel dictator named Hamdo.
 
Shu is captured and forced to witness terrible acts of violence, including the abuse of children and the destruction of innocent communities. Unlike the fantasy adventures found in many isekai anime, his new world offers almost no opportunities for happiness.
 
Children are forced into military service and innocent people suffer because of their leaders' ambitions. 
The Twelve Kingdoms
 also explores war and political oppression, but 
Now and Then, Here and There
 presents those horrors without much relief. For viewers who appreciated the darker parts of Youko's journey, this anime delivers an even more devastating experience.
 

                        Log Horizon Gives Its Heroes More Than Monsters to Worry About
               

Log Horizon's Round Table Alliance
Image via Satelight

 Typically, getting trapped in a video game means searching for a way to escape, but Shiroe thinks differently in 
Log Horizon
. When a strange incident transfers thousands of players into Elder Tale's world, Shiroe accepts that it may be impossible to return to normal life. He begins developing a society where the stranded players can live peacefully.
 
His plans eventually result in the creation of the Round Table Alliance, which brings together some of Akihabara's most influential guilds. In 
The Twelve Kingdoms
, politics plays a very prominent role, but in 
Log Horizon
, audiences get to see the process of creating a functioning government.

The anime also gives supporting characters like Akatsuki and Naotsugu important roles in the community. Their different personalities help prevent the story from becoming a constant series of political discussions. Although 
Log Horizon
 occasionally moves slowly, its careful attention to economics and social order gives the world a level of detail that rivals 
The Twelve Kingdoms
.

                        Re:Zero Has Some of the Most Complex Character Development in Isekai
               

Close
Subaru Natsuki with a smile on his face as he explores a busy shopping section in town in Re:Zero
Image via White Fox
Subaru and his adventurer party in Re:Zero - Starting Life in Another World.
Image via Studio White Fox
Echidna in Re:Zero
Image via White Fox

Subaru Natsuki initially thinks that traveling to another world is going to bring him adventure, but 
Re:Zero
 quickly destroys his expectations. Subaru arrives in the Kingdom of Lugunica without useful combat abilities or any understanding of its dangers. His only unusual power is Return by Death, which sends him back to before he dies.
 
Although this ability allows him to change terrible events, 
he must endure the pain of dying
 while remembering tragedies that everyone else has forgotten. Subaru's repeated failures make him one of the most complicated protagonists in isekai anime. He often believes that knowing the future gives him control over everything, but his attempts to save people regularly create new problems.
 
The anime makes great use of its supporting cast, providing Rem, Beatrice and Otto with important roles in Subaru's development. Its combination of psychological drama, political intrigue, and supernatural mystery makes 
Re:Zero
 one of the most ambitious isekai anime ever made.

Journalism Matters: Are those 5 acres an hourglass about to flip again?&nbsp;&nbsp;Park Record

[page text]
Oh, I remain unimpressed with hyperbolic assertions about skyscrapers shorter than the trees on Park City’s 5-acre site, round-the-clock gridlock at Kearns and Bonanza that ends before 10 a.m., a loud corner as ideal setting for a symphonic concert, and my current favorite, icy shadows reaching shops across the street and parking lots at sunset as breathlessly drawn out by pro-bono lawyers honing their legal fictions in comments to the city’s planning commission.

Yes, also with arguments that there’s enough affordable housing already with a whole 12% of the workers in the city living in the city. Or that hordes of such renters surely will swarm here and then work out of town, while the morning trickle away against the tide headed in offers different evidence. Or that somehow people who live in the core would add to the commute when they’d, um, already be there. There’s a bit of a physics problem with that last one.

None of these complaints stands up to scrutiny, yet they have animated enough of the community that the developer paused its quest to pass a decent, if imperfect, plan for the property through the Planning Commission.

And now the City Council has decided to bring back the project for … further discussion.

Sounds like this deal might be dead.

 

 

Maybe that would be for the best. There are good reasons to question it, too.

Is there really enough public benefit to justify the city subsidizing Brinshore Development’s bid for a mixed-use development there? The number of affordable rental units has peeled away over time, down at last look to 88, roughly the number of jobs needed for the commercial uses of the project. The public costs look steep and steepening. The connection to Prospector Square across the street is incomplete and parking spillover inevitable.

Still, this plan is better than what most opponents suggest instead, and it could jump-start a renaissance throughout a tired 78-acre Bonanza Park in the center of town before the Olympic Games return.

The nostalgically ballyhooed arts and culture district was pooh-poohed as too expensive by the time COVID arrived, and the parcel is less than ideal as a thirsty, little-used park at $19.5 million for the land and a few million more to treat its toxic soil.

I’m still a fan of building a new City Hall there. It would be more accessible to where people in Park City live today, be roomy enough for a larger staff along with the Police Department, and free up the Marsac Building to help address that waning foot traffic on Main Street, maybe. That should leave plenty of green space, too.

 

 

Anyway, true public use would not be a question, though the expense and means of financing certainly would. And selling the Marsac Building to help pay for a new City Hall would stir up a whole ’nother hornet’s nest of citizen opposition.

A clique was starting to question the arts and culture district before Sundance and others balked at the cost, a NIMBY core has campaigned against this plan that leans more into affordable housing, and no doubt earnest citizen opposition lurks for whatever idea might emerge next. There is no pleasing the whole town, not even the Olympics itself.

This apparently is the true Park City way. No wonder the City Council in recent years has picked up a reputation for waffling with bigger ideas that build momentum until that other half of the community catches wind and rises up. 

Some critics go on how councilors don’t listen. I wonder whether they listen maybe too much.

The council took its time shaping the Brinshore proposal with quite a lot of public input. This current plan goes back a few years through the gristmill before councilors last spring sent it on to the Planning Commission. Previous work goes back to 2017, when the city bought the land.

 

 

Now, a pause, further discussion on tap … blood in the water?

It is possible that more council election seasons will pass, maybe an Olympics or two, before something better or something worse lands at last on this busy corner and possibly ripples throughout Bonanza Park.

Sand, in other words, in an hourglass flipped over and over. Our municipal version of purgatory.

UNC-Louisville Preview: Tar Heels Have Sights on Bowl Eligibility Against Red-Hot Cardinals&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
North Carolina (5-4, 3-3 ACC) is still searching for its first FBS win of the season at home, but it will have another stab at that on Saturday when Louisville (6-3, 5-2 ACC) travels to Chapel Hill for the first time since 2017. Here is everything you need to know before the Tar Heels and the Cardinals square off under the lights at Kenan Stadium …

How to Watch

Location: Kenan Memorial Stadium — Chapel Hill, N.C.
Time: Saturday, 7 p.m. ET
TV: ESPN2
Crew: Mike Couzens, Tom Luginbill, Lauren Sisler

Analyzing the Cardinals

Sitting in third place in the ACC standings, Louisville is riding a four-game win streak after throttling Georgia Tech last weekend, 44-17. Seven games into their conference slate, head coach 
Jeff Brohm
 and the Cardinals have one common opponent with North Carolina: Syracuse, which Louisville took down 34-26 on Oct. 17. Amid its recent run of success, the Cardinals have an average margin of victory of 21.8 points. 

Louisville’s best win was a mid-September 41-31 victory over SMU, which was ranked 16th at the time, and each of its three losses (Ole Miss, Wake Forest, at NC State) have come by three points. According to ESPN’s College Football Power Index, the Cardinals have the 50th-ranked strength of schedule.

The recent road domination of the Yellow Jackets catapulted Louisville up eight spots to No. 14 in ESPN’s SP+ metric. Its No. 2-ranked offense, though, is the tallest challenge to monitor for the Tar Heels on Saturday. Despite leaning more on its ground game (228.0 yards per game), the Cardinals boast one of the most explosive offenses in the country. They lead the country in plays of 20, 50 and 60 or more yards, while second in plays of 30- and 40-plus yards. 

“They’re pretty tough offensively,” Bill Belichick said on Monday. “A lot of explosive players: quarterback, running backs, receivers, tight ends. They use a lot of people and use three to four tight ends, multiple receivers, multiple backs.”

Defensively, Brohm’s group surrenders 27.5 points per game against FBS opponents. Louisville allowed 30 or more points in each of its first four power conference matchups, but is yet to do so amidst its four-game winning streak. The Cardinals have also tallied 15 sacks across their last four games, and have forced 15 turnovers on the season, which is the most in the ACC. 

Positions, Players to Watch

Senior quarterback and Ohio State transfer 
Lincoln Kienholz
 stirs the pot for Louisville’s high-powered offense, throwing for 2,276 yards (259.9 yards per game), 14 touchdowns and three interceptions through nine games. On the ground, he’s second in attempts and third in yards for the Cardinals as well, but the signal-caller is the clear frontrunner in rushing touchdowns with 11, despite Louisville featuring one of the ACC’s premier tandem of running backs in 
Isaac Brown
 and 
Keyjuan Brown

“(Kienholz’s) runs, I’d say, are based more on speed,” Belichick said. “They force you to defend sideline to sideline. They force you to defend the running game over here, and the quarterback keep over there. Or the running game over here, and an RPO back there, and so you get stretched.”

Isaac Brown, who leads the charge in the backfield, has four games with over 125 rush yards in conference play, averaging a staggering 7.53 yards per carry, the eighth best mark in the country. In the month of October (four games), the junior rushed 44 times for 510 yards and six touchdowns. Brohm’s offense has 32 rushing touchdowns, the second-most in the country, and 19 more than Carolina.

Elsewhere on offense, 5-foot-10 
Tre Richardson
 leads Louisville in receiving with 44 catches for 685 yards and three scores in nine games. In 
Lawayne McCoy
‘s — the Cardinals’ other top receiver — absence last week, Kienholz turned to 
TreyShun Hurry
, as the redshirt senior posted eight grabs for 104 yards and a score in Atlanta.

Clev Lubin
‘s 7.0 sacks and 
Tyler Thompson
‘s — a former Tar Heel — 5.5 sacks anchor an experienced defensive front. Louisville allows 138.6 rushing yards per game, ranked in the middle of the FBS. Senior linebacker 
Stanquan Clark
 leads the Cardinals in tackles with 64.

But in familiar fashion for UNC, another suspect secondary will make its way to Chapel Hill this weekend. Despite 
Kaleb Beasley
‘s five interceptions, Louisville allows 265.9 air yards per contest, the ninth-worst nationally. The Cardinals have allowed a 300-yard passer in three games this season. 

The Matchup

Louisville’s trip to Kenan Stadium presents some schematic shades of North Carolina’s matchup with Miami. The main difference, though, is the Cardinals’ domination in the ground game, which no other team the Tar Heels have seen has been able to reproduce.

UNC’s ability to stifle the run has been middling, at best. Every one of its FBS opponents has eclipsed the 100-yard mark, while the Tar Heels are allowing 3.98 yards per carry through nine games, ranking 67th in the country.

“The best way to limit points is to play good on third down and play good in the red areas,” Belichick added. “If you can get off the field on third down — get them into third down first — and then get off the field when you have a chance, and then keep them out of the end zone, that’s how you keep the points down.”

But its a familiar sentiment for 
Billy Edwards Jr.
 and the Tar Heels’ offense. Saturday’s date with the Cardinals will mark the third consecutive contest that UNC will see a pass defense ranked 121st in the country or lower. That’s good news for a Carolina offense that is trending upwards in recent weeks. Over his last three outings, Edwards has thrown nine touchdowns, matching his total from his first six starts. Additionally, UNC’s 164 rush yards at UConn was its most since Sep. 19 at Clemson.

Contrary to its full body of work, Louisville boasts the fourth-best red zone defense in the ACC and 18th-best nationally, allowing a score on 75 percent of its opponents trips. That creates an intrigiuing clash with the Tar Heels’ scorching red zone offense, turning in points on 93.9 percent of trips this season, but notably finding the end zone in eight of their last nine red area opportunities over the last two weeks.

Carolina’s defense is no stranger to facing highly-touted offensive players this season, ranging from Miami’s Heisman hopefuls in Darian Mensah and Malachi Toney, to CJ Carr, Nate Sheppard and Umari Hatcher. Kienholz and Isaac Brown will add their names to that list this weekend, and how well UNC can contain them will tell the story as the Tar Heels look to go bowling.

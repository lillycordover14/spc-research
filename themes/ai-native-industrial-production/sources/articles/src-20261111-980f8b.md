UTC once again named one of Tennessee’s top universities for military-connected students&nbsp;&nbsp;University of Tennessee at Chattanooga

[page text]
November 11, 2026
UTC Communications and Marketing
 
UTC’s Office of Veteran and Military Affairs is located on the third floor of the University Center. Photo by Angela Foster.

The University of Tennessee at Chattanooga has been recognized by Military Times as one of the nation’s top universities for veterans and military-connected students, placing second among Tennessee universities in the publication’s 2026 Best for Vets: Colleges rankings.

Military Times recognized 370 colleges and universities across the U.S. in this year’s rankings, up from 357 in 2025. The rankings evaluate how institutions serve service members, veterans and military families from application through graduation.

The recognition reflects UTC’s ongoing commitment to a student population that makes up more than 15% of University enrollment. UTC’s military-connected students include veterans, active-duty service members, National Guard and Reserve personnel, and military spouses and dependents.

“On Veterans Day, we recognize the service and sacrifice of those who have worn the uniform and the families who serve alongside them,” said Sylvana Matthews, director of UTC’s Office of Veteran and Military Affairs. “Being named second among Tennessee universities by Military Times affirms the work being done across UTC to make sure military-connected students have the guidance, support and personal connection they need to reach graduation and their career goals.”

UTC’s Office of Veteran and Military Affairs provides admissions assistance, GI Bill® and financial aid guidance, veteran-specific scholarships, mental health resources and a dedicated lounge in the University Center. The office also connects students with campus events, community partners, professional development opportunities and one-on-one support.

The Veteran and Military Affairs Student Ambassador Program provides peer mentorship and outreach for military-connected students. Ambassadors help new students navigate campus, connect with resources and build relationships with other members of the UTC community.

UTC also awards academic credit for military training or experience and offers personalized advising and career support. Partnerships with Career Connections, the American Job Center and the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs connect students with employment resources and services.

“Our students are veterans beginning a new chapter, service members balancing commitments and family members pursuing their own goals,” Matthews said. “They do not all need the same thing, so our responsibility is to listen, help them understand their options and connect them with the people and resources that can make a difference.”

According to Military Times, its Best for Vets: Colleges rankings are based on a detailed survey of each institution’s culture, student support services, academic policies, costs and financial assistance, and outcomes for military-connected students. The publication noted that many of this year’s recognized schools are expanding support for spouses and dependents who now represent the largest share of military-connected students.

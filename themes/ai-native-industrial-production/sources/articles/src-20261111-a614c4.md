Boston Dynamics VP & GM of Logistics Robotics Brian Donnelly has launched an updated model of its Stretch autonomous mobile robot
Inbound logistics remains one of the most labour-intensive and physically demanding nodes within global supply chain networks. Mobile robotics pioneer Boston Dynamics - a wholly-owned subsidiary of Hyundai Motor Group - has unveiled Stretch Gen 2, a next-generation autonomous mobile robot (AMR) specifically designed for warehouse activities, including trailer unloading.
Built upon 
operational
 data harvested from more than 100,000 industrial operating hours and tens of millions of handled cases, this second-generation platform introduces hardware, electrical, and structural upgrades designed to maximise unit availability, and improve dock-door throughput.
Hardened Hardware for High-Velocity Material Handling
The operational reality of trailer unloading involves navigating unstructured environments, uneven dock floors, and shifting cargo loads. To withstand these harsh physical conditions, Boston Dynamics has re-engineered Stretch’s core electrical and mechanical architectures. The robot now features heavy-duty, automotive-grade connectors specifically rated to withstand the continuous, high-torque vibrations of rapid box grasping.
"With Gen 2, we focused on the things that actually took robots offline in the real world. We made it tougher, we made it simpler to service, and we made sure it keeps getting better long after it's deployed," says Brian Donnelly, Vice President & General Manager of Logistics Robotics at Boston Dynamics.
The upgraded Stretch model has improved robustness and battery life, which will help to improve availability. Credit: Hyundai
Redefining On-Site Maintainability and Fleet Diagnostics

In modern fulfilment centers, every minute of robotic downtime creates a backlog at the receiving dock. Stretch Gen 2 addresses this by utilising a highly modular design that simplifies routine maintenance. By standardising high-strength fasteners and simplifying mechanical sub-assemblies, facility operators can perform rapid component swaps without relying on specialised external robotics field technicians.

This simplified maintenance framework is supported by an upgraded onboard computing platform running advanced, real-time diagnostics. The computing system enables over-the-air software updates that continuously improve case detection, spatial modelling, and motion planning across an entire warehouse fleet. 

To safely handle unpredictable case configurations, Stretch Gen 2 features an upgraded vacuum-based gripper head integrated with sensitive pressure valves and advanced spatial array sensors. These sensors detect case shifts in real time, adjusting the robot's approach angle and vacuum seal to prevent drops or carton punctures.
Boston Dynamics showcased the capabilities of its first-generation Stretch robot in 2025. Credit: Hyundai
Key facts about Stretch Gen 2
1,000 cases per hour throughput: Processing mixed-SKU container freight and variable-weight cartons autonomously under real-world dock conditions.
81% internal cabling reduction: Eliminating a half-mile of internal wiring to remove common electrical failure points and reduce maintenance overhead.
1-hour swerve drive replacement: Reducing a critical drive-unit maintenance procedure from 24 hours down to less than 60 minutes.
Dual-shift battery endurance: Operating continuously across two consecutive warehouse shifts on a single charge, supported by a 75-minute fast-charging cycle to 90% capacity.
Zero-programming vision deployment: Utilising a pre-trained machine-learning model that instantly recognises, segments, and picks diverse packaging types from day one.
Hyundai investment in robotics hub
Hyundai acquired full ownership over Boston Dynamics in August 2026, purchasing SoftBank’s remaining shareholding. The acquisition is in line with Hyundai’s strategic focus on robotics. 
Hyundai 
announced
 plans at CES 2026 in Las Vegas earlier this year that production of Boston Dynamics’ Atlas robot, an enterprise grade humanoid robot that can perform a wide array of industrial tasks, from material handling to order fulfilment, was beginning at its Boston manufacturing hub. Hyundai’s Robotics Metaplant Application Center (RMAC) and Google DeepMind were the initial customers for Boston Dynamics’ Atlas model.
In April 2025, Hyundai announced that it was allocating a proportion of a US$26bn investment to build a Robotics Innovation Hub in Georgia. The leading edge robotics facility will have an annual capacity of 30,000 units, and will act as a US hub for design, manufacturing, testing, and deployment.
Strategic Technology & Integration Partners
DHL Supply Chain (3PL Deployment Partner)
DHL
 is a global leader in third-party logistics, managing contract logistics and supply chain solutions across 220 countries. As an early adopter of Boston Dynamics' automation technology, DHL integrates autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) into its regional distribution networks to streamline retail fulfilment, mitigate labour shortages, and improve workplace safety for its warehouse associates.
Microsoft (Technology Partner)
Microsoft Corporation is a technology leader providing advanced cloud computing infrastructure, database software, and enterprise solutions. In October, Microsoft announced that it would integrate its Copilot AI system directly into Boston Dynamics’ fleet, helping warehouse operators to issue commands in natural language to improve obstacle navigation and spatial reasoning for units in warehouse environments.
Zebra Technologies (Industrial RFID & Scanning Partner)
Zebra Technologies specialises in enterprise asset intelligence, providing barcode printing, mobile computing, data capture, and indoor location solutions. Serving 100 countries, Zebra’s robust warehouse technologies integrate seamlessly with robotic systems, allowing automated guided vehicles (AGVs) and AMRs to instantaneously scan, track, and verify shipping labels during high-speed unloading operations.

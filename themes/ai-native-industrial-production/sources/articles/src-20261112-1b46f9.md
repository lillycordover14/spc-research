Figure Q3 volume rises 135% to $5.8B on Connect growth&nbsp;&nbsp;HousingWire

[page text]
Figure Technology Solutions
 on Thursday reported a sharp increase in loan marketplace volume in the third quarter, as the blockchain-based lending platform continued expanding its marketplace business and completed its 
acquisition
 of real estate investment lender 
Kiavi
.

The company’s Loan Marketplace volume reached $5.8 billion in the three months ending Sept. 30, up 135% from $2.5 billion a year earlier. The total included $677 million in volume contributed by Kiavi, which Figure officially acquired on Sept. 1.

Figure Connect
, the company’s capital-light marketplace platform, accounted for $4.3 billion of quarterly volume, up 276% year over year. The platform represented 72% of standalone Loan Marketplace volume during the quarter.

Net revenue increased 84% year over year to $287 million, compared with $156.4 million in the third quarter of 2025.

Net income was essentially flat at $89.3 million, compared with $89.8 million a year earlier. On a quarterly basis, net income rose from its 
second-quarter figure
 of $87.4 million.

Net income margin fell to 31%, down from 57%, partly because the year-ago quarter included a one-time $31 million tax benefit related to the recognition of deferred tax assets. Adjusted EBITDA, a non-GAAP measure, rose 91% to $163.6 million. Adjusted EBITDA margin increased to 58% from 55%.

“This was a record quarter for Figure, highlighted by another consecutive quarter of over 100% volume growth and the rapid expansion of our capital-light marketplace, with Figure Connect now making up 72% of our standalone volume,” CEO 
Michael Tannenbaum
 said in a statement.

More on Kiavi’s impacts

During the company’s 
earnings
 call, Tannenbaum said that Figure grew volume 107% year over year, posting a 58% EBITDA margin and maintaining a 3.7% net standalone take rate. Chief financial officer Macrina Kgil said that during the company’s Q2 2026 earnings, it guided Q3 standalone volume to a range of $4.8 billion to $5.2 billion, and that guidance proved reliable as Figure came in at $5.1 billion.

“The Kiavi acquisition … contributed approximately $670 million of volume for the one month in our results this quarter,” Kgil said. Figure Connect volume, including Kiavi, grew to 74% of overall volume, up from 65% last quarter and from 46% a year ago.”

The acquisition expands Figure’s presence in investor-focused mortgage lending, including residential transition loans (
RTLs
) and debt-service-coverage ratio (
DSCR
) loans. Figure said the deal added $8 billion in annualized volume and that it had realized $30 million in initial cost synergies since the transaction closed.

“With Kiavi, we have added residential transition loans, RTL, and debt service coverage ratio loans, DSCR, diversifying our platform beyond 
HELOC
 into a $200 billion-plus addressable market,” Tannenbaum told investors. He added that the acquisition had closed ahead of schedule and that Kiavi “also brought on a number of new full loan and securitized investors.”

Business lending acceleration

The company also reported continued growth in its small- and medium-sized business lending (SMB) operation, which surpassed a 
$500 million annualized run rate 
with more than 150 active partners.

Figure said it expanded its partner network to 612 standalone partners and 894 when including Kiavi. The network includes more than 20 active 
depository
 partners.

The company also introduced an artificial intelligence-powered loan officer assistant through a partnership with 
Sierra
. The tool has processed approximately $260 million in funded loans and increased funded dollars per loan approval by 29%, Figure said.

Separately, Figure’s $YLDS stablecoin had $504 million in circulation as of Sept. 30, compared with $328 million at the end of 2025. The company said 28 participants were using the 
stablecoin
 for marketplace transactions.

The company’s board also authorized a $200 million increase to its share repurchase program, bringing the total authorization to $400 million. 

“Today, we’re announcing an expanded $400 million share buyback plan that will start tomorrow,” Tannenbaum said. “Given our profitability, cash levels, balance-sheet liquidity, and access to the capital markets, we will be opportunistic about share repurchases on an ongoing basis, including beyond this initial $400 million program. That said, we see huge organic growth and look at inorganic opportunities as well, especially those that can accelerate our marketplace reach.”

For the fourth quarter, Figure forecasts Loan Marketplace volume of $6.2 billion to $6.7 billion, along with a gross take rate of 3.1% to 3.4%.

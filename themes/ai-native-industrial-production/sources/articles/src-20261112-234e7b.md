Saronic Details Over $100 Million Shiplift Contract for Port Alpha&nbsp;&nbsp;Securities.io

Saronic and Nekkar on November 12, 2026 disclosed that a Syncrolift shiplift contract worth more than $100 million will serve Saronic’s Port Alpha shipyard in Brownsville, Texas, identifying the customer and destination behind a previously announced award. Syncrolift AS, a wholly owned subsidiary of Oslo-listed Nekkar ASA, will design and supply the system, according to their 
joint announcement
.

The disclosure follows Nekkar’s 
November 4, 2026 stock-exchange announcement
, which reported a contract from an undisclosed U.S. customer. That notice described it as the largest contract in Syncrolift’s history, said work was commencing immediately and scheduled final delivery for 2028. The joint release adds Saronic’s name, the project’s value and the equipment’s specifications.

Saronic says the planned tandem shiplift will be the world’s largest, with a 300-meter platform, a lifting capacity of 67,000 tonnes and more than twice the capacity of the next-largest installation. It will be a central enabling system for a yard intended to build autonomous and manned ships for commercial and defense customers.

Built Around Parallel Production

Port Alpha’s software-defined, high-throughput design moves vessels between stations rather than building them in fixed docks, and consolidates final assembly into a small number of large-block lifts. The Syncrolift system pairs a rigid lifting platform with condition-monitoring and digital-twin control technology intended to support closely monitored operation throughout each lift.

The accompanying transfer system is designed so several vessels can be built and outfitted on flat land in parallel before being moved to the lift for launch. Saronic says this arrangement replaces the need for multiple graving docks and improves access for workers, materials and equipment. The lift therefore connects the yard’s land-based assembly process directly to launch, making its performance central to the production plan.

Saronic selected Syncrolift after evaluating docking and transfer technologies for capability and safety. “Syncrolift’s technology and track record made them the right supplier to deliver a system that is strategically important to how we’re approaching Port Alpha and the reindustrialization of U.S. shipbuilding,” said Doug Lambert, Saronic’s co-founder and COO. Syncrolift will manage the project from its headquarters in Vestby, near Oslo.

Capacity Plans Meet a Delivery Schedule

Port Alpha represents a planned investment of more than $3 billion, and Saronic expects it to create up to 10,000 direct jobs.

Saronic’s 
Port Alpha project page
 says construction has begun and sets targets of initial operations in 2028, 150,000 gross tons of annual shipbuilding capacity at opening and potential to scale beyond 2 million gross tons.

The contract gives that model a named supplier, defined specifications and a delivery schedule. With final delivery and initial operations both set for 2028, execution of the Syncrolift project will be an important test of whether Port Alpha can turn its planned throughput into operating shipbuilding capacity on that timetable.

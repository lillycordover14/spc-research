UNC first-year guard Kate Harpring (11) moves the ball past the defense and attempts to score a point during the game against NC Central University on Wednesday, Nov. 11, 2026.
 

The No. 23 UNC women’s basketball team (1-2) dominated N.C. Central (2-3), 109-40, Wednesday evening at Carmichael Arena.

The Tar Heels took a decisive victory in their home opener, which saw them hit 18 threes — the most in program history. It was a stark contrast to their game against No. 1 South Carolina, where they went 21.4 percent on 28 3-point attempts. Deep scoring contributed to the victory as the bench put up 40 points.

“To clarify our reads and our angles and let playmakers play, we have to have some semblance of our framework,” head coach Courtney Banghart said. “So we were much more accountable to those things, which gave you more pressure on the rim, more gravity on the rim, gave you more east-west, gave you clearer reads.”

The Eagles got on the board first with a 3-pointer. Senior forward Achol Akot led UNC’s response, posting its first 5 points. 

Sophomore guard Nyla Brooks and first-year guard Kate Harpring each contributed a pair of baskets from beyond the arc in the first quarter. Senior guard Sophie Burrows did the same coming off the bench, putting UNC up 30-11. 

The Tar Heels went on a 10-0 scoring run to begin the second quarter. Harpring was particularly effective, stealing the ball for a layup that pushed UNC to 48-13.

UNC shot 10-25 from the perimeter in the first half and had little difficulty getting past N.C. Central’s defense near the rim, scoring 16 points in the paint. The Tar Heels entered the half up 54-15.

Senior guard Reniya Kelly added 4 to the board, draining a 3-pointer while drawing a foul. Akot’s aggression at the rim, combined with Brooks’ knack for threes, earned them each eight points in the third quarter. 

Senior guard Laila Hull added to the barrage of scoring in the fourth quarter with back-to-back 3-pointers. Sophomore guard Taissa Queiroz went 2-for-3 on her free throws, sparking an extended rebounding effort that didn’t result in a basket. 

However, UNC’s lead was more than safe, especially as senior guard Sydney Barker and junior forward Blanca Thomas added their names under the 3-point column and brought the score to 109-40. 
Ultimately, UNC shot 46.2 percent on its 39 3-point attempts
.

“In that last five or so minutes, everybody was getting shots, so it just felt really fluid, it felt fun,” Barker said.

The Tar Heels are back on the road this weekend, facing Penn at The Palestra this Saturday at 4 p.m.

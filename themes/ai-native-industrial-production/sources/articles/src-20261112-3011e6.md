Saronic invests US$3Bn in shipyard set to build autonomous vessels
12 Nov 2026
by Martyn Wingrove
A Brownsville, Texas, shipyard will require a US$100M tandem shiplift with condition-monitoring and digital-twin control technology to build autonomous vessels for commercial and defence applications
 
Saronic is investing US$3Bn in Port Alpha, which is being constructed in Brownsville, Texas, to build autonomous vessels for commercial applications and defence missions.

Saronic expects Port Alpha to be the biggest and most advanced shipyard worldwide for building autonomous and manned vessels, with the world’s largest shiplift, according to Saronic.

In the shipyard, vessels will be engineered around a software-defined, high-throughput production model in which final assembly is consolidated into a small number of large-block lifts with vessels moving between stations rather than being built in fixed docks.

To achieve this, Saronic has ordered a shiplift from Syncrolift, a subsidiary of Oslo-listed Nekkar, with a lifting capacity of 67,000 tonnes and a 300-m-long rigid lifting platform.

This tandem shiplift will also have advanced condition-monitoring and digital-twin control technology that supports safe, closely monitored operation at every stage of a lift, said Syncrolift.

It will enable block transfers and parallel vessel construction and outfitting, replacing the need for multiple graving docks while improving access for workers, materials and equipment, according to Saronic.

"Syncrolift’s technology will be strategically important to how we are approaching Port Alpha and the re-industrialisation of US shipbuilding," said Saronic co-Founder and chief operating officer, Doug Lambert.  "The scope and scale of this shiplift is foundational to how the yard will operate and reflects the ambition of what we are building at Port Alpha and how we are rethinking shipbuilding from the ground up to deliver ships at a speed and scale," he said.

With investment of US$3Bn, Port Alpha will initially cover 835 acres, with room to expand to nearly 4,400 acres.

It is designed to build autonomous and manned ships and is expected to create up to 10,000 direct jobs.

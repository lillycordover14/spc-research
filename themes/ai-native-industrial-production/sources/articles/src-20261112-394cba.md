In a heavyweight bout with twelve lead changes, Florida Atlantic outlasts William & Mary basketball 95-90

    FAU basketball rolled with the punches in a physical matchup Wednesday night, as the Owls fought back in the second half and hit a season-high 14 three-pointers to remain undefeated at home.

Mikai Datilus

Sports Editor

November 12, 2026
 

Coming off a blowout loss, the pressure was high for FAU to rebound Wednesday night. Florida Atlantic men’s basketball (
2-1, 0-0 AC
) did just that, as they returned to Boca Raton with a big home victory against the William & Mary Tribe (
2-1
).

The Owls went down eight at one point in the second half, but made big shots and defensive plays down the stretch. Forward 
Josh Harris
 led the Owls with 25 points, and guards 
Kanaan Caryle
 and 
Jelani Hamilton
 provided 21 points apiece.

“The game is easy for him. He’ll be one of the best scorers in our league. When the ball lands in Jelani’s hands, you can see the excitement in the crowd,” said FAU head coach
 John Jakus
, pleased with Hamilton’s performance.

“There’s this moment where everyone stood up, because the assumption is that [his shot] is going in, and that’s the sign of a great shooter. When everybody’s in the air, that creates energy,

and that energy, I thought, created ball movement for us.”

Hamilton made his Owls debut after missing the first two games with an injury, and he started in the place of forward 
Mason-Porter-Brown
. Hamilton made his impact felt immediately. After the Owls won the opening tip, guard 
Felipe Quinones
 passed the ball to Hamilton, who immediately pulled up and made a three-pointer, which sent the FAU crowd into a frenzy.

“I think it just gave everybody confidence. When you make a shot like that in front of everybody, you watch the noise, and hear the crowd; it just opens things up from everyone,” Hamilton said.

Carlyle and Hamilton both hit two three-pointers in the first couple of minutes of the game, which gave FAU an early 12-5 lead. The Owls, however, were under pressure from the opening tip, as W&M played full-court defense the entire game. William & Mary quickly settled into the game, going on a 13-4 run to take an 18-16 lead with under ten minutes remaining in the first half.

The first half continued to be a pendulum swing, and Porter-Brown swung it FAU’s way with a corner three as he was falling down, putting the Owls back up 29-28. “FAU” chants erupted from the crowd after the shot. Center 
Keiran Mullen
 had a strong first half off the bench for the Owls, with four points, four rebounds, and a block. He calmly knocked down two free throws to put FAU up 37-34 with two minutes left in the half.

However, the Tribe struck back, and they scored eight of the game’s next ten points to go up 42-39 at halftime. FAU shot 43.8% from three in the first half, but they shot a horrific 33% on shots from inside the arc, and turned over the ball seven times. Josh Harris led the Owls in the first half with 12 points, five rebounds, and two three-pointers.

“I thought Kieran and Josh from Wright State until now had three days where they proved they can get better through failure, which is a really good sign, but, Josh, the game’s just gonna get easier for as the shooters come back, and we get all the guards on the floor,” Jakus said, reflecting on Mullen and Harris’ performance.

The back-and-forth continued into the second half, and so did FAU’s turnover problem. The Tribe capitalized on FAU’s mishaps with back-to-back layups to go up 53-45 with 16 minutes remaining in the second half. After going down eight and the game in jeopardy of getting away from FAU, the Owls turned to their leading scorer: Kanaan Caryle, who drilled a top of the key three pointer to cut the lead to five.

After a few scoreless possessions from both teams, Caryle was active again; he drove to the basket and banked home a layup while getting fouled. Caryle made the free throw, completing the and-one opportunity and cutting the deficit to two. Mullen continued to be impactful as well; with eleven minutes remaining in the half, the home crowd roared as Mullen made an and-one layup to tie the game.

However, Mullen missed the free throw, and the Tribe capitalized with a three-pointer to retake the lead. The Tribe kept trading baskets with the Owls to keep the lead, although Hamilton and Harris drilled back-to-back three-pointers to keep the deficit at one. Tribe players 
ZZ Clark
 and 
Brian Carter Jr.
 just kept scoring, though, making important layups for the Tribe to retain the lead.

Once again, Caryle put on his hero cape, as he hit a pull-up three-pointer to tie the game at 77. The Owls forced a pair of key stops, and Hamilton then made a wide-open three-pointer to give the Owls their first lead since the opening minutes of the half.

Hamilton was on fire; he then got the ball and made another jumper from the left hash of the three-point line, and he mean-mugged the sideline as the Owls were finally back into the game, and they led the Tribe 83-79 with three minutes to go.

Mullen continued his stellar performance, with a layup that extended FAU’s lead to six. Mullen then got an offensive rebound and laid the ball up again, putting FAU up eight with under two minutes remaining. Mullen totaled 12 points and eight rebounds off the bench for FAU.

The daggers fittingly came from Mullen, as Quinones lobbed it up to Mullen for an alley-oop dunk, which put the Owls up nine, and on the other end Mullen ferociously blocked a tribe layup.

Both teams traded baskets and free throws, and the intensity was at its highest, but Caryle coolly knocked down two free throws to clinch FAU’s 95-90 victory.

Following the victory, FAU will remain home for their next matchup, Saturday against 
Coastal Georgia
. Tip-off is at 2 p.m.

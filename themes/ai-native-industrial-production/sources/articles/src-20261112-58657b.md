When the first European surveying party came to lay the Fairfax Stone in 1746 near what is now the town of Thomas, West Virginia, they had trouble getting through the Canaan valley. In this valley, about six miles southwest of their final destination, a large number of huge red spruce trees had fallen. The trees were as big as 6 feet in diameter — almost 19 feet in circumference — and the crevices between them were so deep that the party risked losing their horses if they tried to cross.
Almost all of the big trees in our region were cut down between 1880 and 1930, mostly as the railroads made their way into our communities. Because we’ve grown up seeing young trees, many Pennsylvanians believe that big trees are native only to the western, but not the eastern, United States.

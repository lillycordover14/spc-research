For Week 9, Next Gen Stats outpaced the McAfee alt cast again

By

Mike Florio
,

By

Mike Florio

 
 
Published November 12, 2026 06:21 AM

 

Facebook

Twitter

        Email
    

The ESPN announcement of the viewership for a given Monday night game always requires a careful look for any information about the viewership of the multiple weekly alt casts on the Disney-owned networks.
This week, the release presented only the total viewership for all platforms, at 21.7 million. The release also mentioned that multppe alt casts “averaged more than 900K viewers.”
Andrew Marchand of 
The Athletic
 has 
gotten the numbers
. For the Next Gen Stats alt cast, 499,000 tuned in. For the McAfee ensemble, the number was 406,000. Making the total audience 905,000.
The final tally gives Next Gen Stats a 2-0 lead over the McAfee option. The gap was more narrow this time around; when they first squared off for the Week 6 Commanders-49ers game, 
NFLN attracted 469,000 and ESPN2 got 351,000
. (ESPN2 is in roughly 70 million homes; NFL Network’s distribution is at or below 50 million.)
The total viewership for that game was 17.9 million. With Bills-Vikings beating that figure by 21.2 percent, Next Gen Stats was up 6.3 percent over Week 6 and the McAfee cast increased by 15.6 percent.

Of the total audience for the game, 2.2 percent watched NFL Network and 1.87 percent watched ESPN2. Which raises, once again, a one-word question: Why?
The Monday night alt casts don’t draw in new viewers. It’s added expense, with no extra revenue for the broader operation. (Unless there’s a tangible benefit to siphoning viewers from ABC/ESPN for the other channels.)
Ultimately, it’s a zero-sum game. With alt casts, Bills-Vikings viewership was 21.7 million. Without alt casts, Bills-Vikings viewership undoubtedly would have been 21.7 million.
And that’s why the various ABC affiliates are upset that Disney will have a full slate of alt casts for Super Bowl LXI. The total number will be the total number, if the game is on one network or twenty. The alt casts will rob the Peter of ABC to pay the Paul of the various other platforms.

When Tali Hakas announced she would not return for her final year of eligibility, she talked about making that decision with help from the ones she loved.

“It was a decision that I had to think about with everybody,” Hakas said that day. “My parents were involved … and my best friend was involved.”

When she mentioned “best friend,” she motioned to Panna Ratkai, who was standing to the side of the media scrum.

The University of Hawaii volleyball players have known each other for less than a year, but have formed a bond on and off the court that will last forever.

Along with defensive specialist Leilani Lopez, they will be honored on Senior Night on Friday after Hawaii (21-4, 13-1 Mountain West) plays its final home match of the regular season against Air Force (10-15, 6-8).

Hakas, who has four years with the program and played in the NCAA Tournament twice, isn’t sure how her friendship with Ratkai began, but she has enjoyed every minute of it.

“I think we just clicked from Day 1,” Hakas said. “It’s just the way she makes me feel. She makes me feel special. She makes me seem understood and you know, being far from home, having a person that sees you and knows you and knows what to say at the right moment is very special.”

Ratkai, who previously played at Purdue Fort Wayne and Dayton, joined the Rainbow Wahine program in January.

The first time she met Hakas was in the weight room.

“I walked in and I was like, ‘Hi, I’m Panna,” and she was this bubbly personality who jumped into my neck with a hug and was like, ‘Hi, I’m Tali,” Ratkai recalled. “The first thing we connected over was our age because we were both 23 and kind of older than the rest of the team. So that was our first bonding moment.”

They have been instrumental in Hawaii’s success this season. The Rainbow Wahine need a win in any of their last four matches to clinch the No. 1 seed in the Credit Union 1 Mountain West Women’s Volleyball Championship on Nov. 24-25 in Las Vegas.

Hakas, who played in only three matches last season before a shoulder injury cut her season short, is averaging 2.06 kills and 1.69 digs per set while playing a mix of three and six rotations on the court.

Ratkai, who is also playing both three and six rotations in recent weeks, is averaging 3.10 kills per set while hitting .220. She’s also averaging 2.08 digs per set and leads the team with 40 aces.

Her success on the court has felt especially good after she didn’t play much last season for Dayton.

Coming to Hawaii has helped bring back her passion for playing volleyball.

“I lost the love for volleyball for a little bit,” said Ratkai, who had her family from Budapest, Hungary, in the arena for last week’s matches against Nevada. “I’m extremely grateful for that because I thought I would never be able to love the sport the way I used to again. For me to be able to play the sport I love with the team that I love and the coaches that I love, it means everything in front of all of these fans that I am extremely grateful for.”

First-year head coach Joshua Walker has been a part of UH Senior Night festivities as a player and coach on the men’s side.

Walker says “one’s a little bit louder than the other,” but otherwise coaching Hakas and Rakai is like coaching the same person.

“They’re just the ultimate competitors,” Walker said. “It’s a coach’s dream as far as how they compete, how they carry themselves, how they communicate to their teammates. They care. You can see it when things aren’t necessarily going right that they wear their emotions on their sleeve. So that part makes it that much better when things are going great, but they have been able to have more consistent moments emotionally, and that’s also contributed to us being more consistent as a team.”

Hakas, who is from Kfar Saba, Israel, got to play in front of her parents during last week’s home sweep of Nevada. They will be in the house again when she plays her final home matches tonight and Friday against the Falcons.

“My heart is going crazy right now because I’ve worked so much and just for them to be able to feel Hawaii — not just see it and experience it — but like, there is something about this place that you need to be here to understand and having them here to experience this with me, I will always have that in me.”

Twelve States and Other Organizations File Supreme Court Briefs Supporting 
America First Legal
’s Client Dr. Peter Navarro    

 

Washington, D.C.
 — 
                                
November 12, 2026

Additional Key Issues

This week, multiple organizations and States have filed amicus briefs with the U.S. Supreme Court in 
Peter K. Navarro v. United States
, supporting America First Legal’s (AFL) client Dr. Peter Navarro. The briefs urge the Court to review Dr. Navarro’s contempt of Congress conviction. 

In October 2026, AFL, in partnership with Holtzman Vogel PLLC, filed a 
petition for a writ of certiorari
 with the Supreme Court, challenging the D.C. Circuit’s interpretation of the word “willfully” in the federal contempt statute. AFL’s petition argues that the government must prove a defendant knew his conduct was unlawful and that Dr. Navarro’s good-faith belief that he was complying with the President’s assertion of executive privilege precluded any finding of “willfulness.”

AFL’s petition has been supported by:

The States of Alabama, Florida, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, South Carolina, Texas, and West Virginia, led by the State of Oklahoma. The States argue that the Circuit Court adopted a definition of “willfully” that Congress never enacted and that the Supreme Court’s decision will have significant implications for state law. 

Former U.S. Secretary of the Department of the Interior David Bernhardt, represented by the America First Policy Institute, who emphasizes the vital need for free, candid advice from presidential advisors and the importance of the protections afforded by executive privilege.

The Oversight Project, which argues that the Circuit Court’s interpretation of “willfully” is incorrect, that a senior presidential advisor’s assertion of executive privilege differs fundamentally from an ordinary person’s refusal to comply with a subpoena, and that the issue at hand is extremely important for the separation of powers.

Citizens United, Citizens United Foundation, and The Presidential Coalition, LLC, argue that Dr. Navarro’s assertion of executive privilege negated the “willful” element and that the Circuit Court erred in disregarding the January 6 Committee’s failure to negotiate with President Trump.

The Article III Project warns that denying a good-faith defense would undermine the longstanding accommodations process between Congress and the Executive Branch and may also empower Congress to weaponize subpoenas.

The briefs underscore the national importance of Dr. Navarro’s case and its implications for executive privilege, the separation of powers, and the rule of law.

“We are grateful for the strong support of these organizations and States for our client, Dr. Peter Navarro,” said Gene Hamilton, President of America First Legal. “A weaponized Department of Justice punished a political opponent for honoring what he believed was the President’s claim of executive privilege. The Supreme Court should take this case, reverse the conviction, and restore the proper meaning of ‘willfully.’”

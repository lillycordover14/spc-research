For most people, the mastery of a single musical instrument is quite an accomplishment. Consider, in contrast, the amazing skill set developed by the two multi-instrumentalists who are featured in a Nov. 17 program at USC Aiken’s Etherredge Center.
Bandleader Dennis Lichtman plays three primary instruments — clarinet, violin and mandolin — but he is also conversant with the guitar and tenor banjo. Jerron Paxton tops Lichtman’s admirable proficiency since he can claim mastery of the banjo, piano, harmonica, accordion, ukulele, guitar and bones.
Their tremendous, combined versatility comes into play when they perform together on stage in concerts that feature popular music primarily of the first half of the 20th century, a time when indigenous American genres often fused together. The instruments that Lichtman and Paxton have mastered were those favored by the primarily self-taught composers and performers of the period.

A typical Paxton-Lichtman concert might, for example, include the tune “Time Changes Everything” (1940), which is a staple of what came to be known as Western Swing, a subgenre of country music. The song, whose lyrics focus on the healing of a broken heart, exhibits the combined influence of big band dance music, polka, Dixieland jazz and blues.

It could be argued that both performers have become, in some respects, amateur historians, exploring the musical landscape of the American experience. Their standard repertoire features a variety of numbers familiar to patrons of the saloon bars and music halls of an earlier time, such as the classic ragtime tune entitled “Maple Leaf Rag” by the legendary Scott Joplan and the equally iconic “Muscle Shoals Blues,” first recorded by boogie-woogie pianist and composer George W. Thomas.

To replicate, to some extent, the conditions by which much of this music was first experienced, the auditorium has been bypassed for this program in favor of the lobby. Members of the audience will be seated at tables and served light hors d’oeuvres. They can also avail themselves of a cash bar.

It is difficult to predict what tunes the duo will choose to perform in the upcoming program; but if their 2021 album offers a clue, it could run the gamut from Irving Berlin’s Jazz Age song “Sunshine” to a fiddle medley incorporating traditional English and Irish folk songs like “Comin’ Thro” the Rye” and “The Girl I Left Behind Me.” There may also be some original compositions penned by Jerron Paxton or Dennis Lichtman.

For more information on what is sure to be a lively and varied musical experience, visit 
etherredgecenter.universitytickets.com.

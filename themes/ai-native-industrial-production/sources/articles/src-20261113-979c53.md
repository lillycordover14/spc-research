Aubrey Word
 

The women of the Trump administration do not deserve our sympathy.  

Be it Second Lady Usha Vance, a Yale University law graduate raised by an accomplished biologist, who has been confined to the role of second lady under a president notable for his history of sexual assault; Or ex-ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle, and a previous partner of Donald Trump Jr. and, notably, California Gov. Gavin Newsom, who was humiliated for a corrupt appointment and failed diplomacy. Or now-former Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, whose exit sparked op-eds about women’s power ⁠— someone who spent years guiding the administration’s actions at the heart of the empire. 

These women are not victims in need of our pity because they are women; rather, they’re using their positions to enforce the very systems of power built to keep them out of the room and support the authoritative men who run those rooms. Their identity as women, no matter how many “first” boxes they check, does not negate the weight of their choices. 

These women have used the concept and visual of progress to empower themselves and keep the rooms they enter functioning just as intended; their presence is no glass ceiling broken. It is not a feminist argument when mainstream media claims to be concerned about the critique or mean-spirited scrutiny that Barbera Thornhill, Susan Dell, Katie Zacharia or dozens like them have faced. 

As the finish line for a woman’s perfection continues to be moved back, we should acknowledge that a woman’s appearance is a reflection of the systems of power of which she is clawing for influence within. First coined “Mar-a-Lago face” and now more colloquially known as the general “MAGA-face,” the aspiration for an infinite fountain of youth within the fascist expectation of perfection and control has manifested in smoothed skin, overfilled facial features and uncanny valley expressions. Rather than mock their appearances, we should instead scrutinize what led them to botch their faces — what did they lose in subjecting themselves to the MAGA mold? What did they gain, personally, politically and systemically? 

These women salivating at the crux of power have calculated that an allegiance with the exploitative systems they — and their bosses — benefit from is an egoistic yet strategic choice. While we waste energy tearing down their appearances, they continue to consider more influence over our governance and public consciousness while our collective resistance to their power weakens. 

Spending time concerned about or in defense of these women is neither productive nor dispositive of reality. These are some of the most powerful women of the federal administration whose executive power has gone practically unchecked. Their presence, and the celebration of such, is a facade of progress. As they continue to prop up the very leaders that are stripping people of safety and dignity while funding racialized violence domestically and abroad, our failure to condemn their complacency and infliction of harm is a failure to pressure them to use their positions for progress.

While these women and their superiors continue to escalate the fascist game, our pity for its players absolves them of their blame and the critique they deserve for how they wield their power.

‘You’ author Caroline Kepnes talks upcoming directorial debut with Cape Cod-filmed ‘The Attentionist’&nbsp;&nbsp;The Boston Globe

[page text]
Caroline Kepnes
Scott Joseph Anthony
Caroline Kepnes
 was dining at Añejo Mexican Bistro in Hyannis in August when her phone pinged. A friend had texted her a link to the New York Times story on the 
50 best thrillers of the 21st century
. Kepnes’s hit book “You” was No. 42.
“I thought she was just sending it like ‘Hey, check out this list,’” the Centerville native, 50, told the Globe in a recent phone interview from her home in Los Angeles.
Kepnes
 received the good news while she was at the restaurant with producers after scouting locations for her upcoming directorial debut, an adaptation of her 2022 Cape Cod-set short story, “The Attentionist.”
“So when I got that text, it was joy on top of joy,” said Kepnes. “The staff will probably remember me because of the way I squealed.”
Kepnes returned to Cape Cod in September and October to film in and around her childhood neighborhood. Set on the Cape in the ’90s, the eight-episode short-form series drops on YouTube early next year, featuring a stacked cast that includes “Breaking Bad” star Dean Norris, as well as Katherine Mallen Kupferer (Maeve), Audrey Grace Marshall (Reg), and Jordan Ladd (mother).
According to 
Variety
, “The Attentionist” follows 14-year-old Maeve and her older sister, Reg, who start a secret phone romance with a wealthy boy from the local boat club. But when the voice on the phone takes a dark turn, Maeve pulls her family further into a monstrous secret.
The upcoming series marks the second major project for the Brown University alum this year. 
“You First
,” a prequel novel about her infamous character Joe Goldberg — one-time Harvard writing student, hilarious narrator, and full-time creeper in the “You” universe, played by Penn Badgley in the Netflix adaptation — marks the fifth installment in her best-selling series. 
Kepnes talked to the Globe about “The Attentionist” and how writing Joe has helped her cope with grief and loss.
Q. 
It feels so fitting that your directorial debut, “The Attentionist,” is set in your childhood neighborhood. 
A. 
Literally in my neighborhood. And we filmed at Bearse Pond, Hyannis Yacht Club, Pirate’s Cove Adventure Golf, Foreign Motors of Cape Cod, a home in Hyannis Port. 
Q. 
How did Dean Norris get involved?
A. 
We met at an Italian film fest in 2025, and I told him, “I’d love to work together someday.” That was in the back of my head. I sent him the script and a note and a picture of us in Italy. And he wanted the part.
It was great to see Dean in my friend Mark Hayes’s place, Foreign Motor of Cape Cod. Mark was a literal godsend with time period cars — Porsches, Volvos, BMWs. 
Q. 
You put out a call on Facebook for period clothes from Barnstable High. Did you get any?
A. 
Did we ever. People sent jackets, Class of ’93 shirts. I’m just impressed people knew where stuff was and sent it so fast.
Q. 
You’ve been on sets for Netflix’s adaptation of “You.” But how did you like directing?
A. 
I loved it. I did a lot of homework. But I really loved learning from actors in real time. Watching them find the characters and show you aspects of the characters.
Q.
 How did Joe first come to you?
A. 
My parents had a furniture store, Convertibles Plus. I could call basically any time and my dad would answer. We talked a lot. He’d tease me when I got uptight, made me laugh. 
When he died, I realized he’s never calling again to joke, to get me out of my head. I needed someone to get me out of my head. Joe came out of that. I need someone to talk to me with that same sharp, dark sense of humor. I think these books are always about loneliness and loss. 
Handout
Joe helped my mom when she was in mourning and laughing while reading the first book.
Q. 
This fifth “You” book comes after the loss of your mom.
A. 
This was the first “You” book she couldn’t be a part of. She was too sick to read. It was heartbreaking. She just kept saying, “Work on your book.” She was very much at peace that I finished it. She was my biggest champion.
But I also knew I needed a reason to write a prequel, story-wise — something we don’t know about Joe.
Q. 
What’s the biggest surprise in your career so far?
A. 
That I’m sitting here talking to you about a Joe Goldberg prequel 10-plus years after the first. When someone is your imaginary friend/security blanket that you dream up at a bad time in life, and that leads to more writing, and to so many people working in the universe around it — that’s a dream.

Investigation into Congressional medical office found lapsed inspections, poor oversight

The Pentagon watchdog says multiple Navy personnel assigned to the office that provides medical care to Congress and the Supreme Court requested “health and safety transfers” and a formal complaint was filed, reinforcing “the need to strengthen oversight.”

The inspector general’s report also says investigators weren’t able to confirm the Office of the Attending Physician’s pharmacy had undergone regular inspections. It says a lack of Pentagon oversight of the office’s clinical quality management and pharmacy operations increases risks to patients and Navy medical personnel.

The report follows the sudden departure of Dr. Brian Monahan, a Navy rear admiral and the office’s attending physician. The Navy is investigating but didn’t announce the departure.

The watchdog says Monahan, who held the post for 18 years, recused himself from the investigation.

“The recusal of the senior-most official in the OAP also significantly limited our ability to identify potential issues, deficiencies, and areas for improvement,” the report said.

Northrop Grumman, Hermeus to Jointly Develop Autonomous High-Speed Military Aircraft

Deal combines Northrop’s mission systems with Hermeus’ Quarterhorse aircraft to accelerate the development of autonomous platforms capable of flying beyond supersonic speeds

 Defensemirror.com Bureau

 06:44 AM, November 13, 2026

 642

Hermeus Quarterhorse aircraft 

Northrop Grumman and Atlanta-based defense startup Hermeus Corporation have signed a strategic teaming agreement to develop and field a new class of high-speed autonomous military aircraft, aiming to provide U.S. forces with faster and more survivable combat capabilities.

Announced on November 12,
 the partnership will integrate Northrop Grumman’s electronic warfare, communications, sensing and autonomy technologies with Hermeus’ Quarterhorse series of uncrewed aircraft.

The initiative addresses the U.S. Department of War’s demand for affordable autonomous systems that can be developed and deployed in months rather than years. The aircraft will use Hermeus’ open-architecture Mission Management System (MMS), built around U.S. Government Open Mission Systems (OMS), Universal Command and Control Interface (UCI), and Agile Mission Suite standards to facilitate rapid hardware and software integration.

Quarterhorse program developments

Hermeus’ Quarterhorse Mk 2.1 broke the sound barrier during a May 2026 test flight and demonstrated high-speed store separation. The Mk 2.2 is under development with a target speed exceeding Mach 2, while the planned Mk 3 is expected to feature the company's Chimera turbine-based combined-cycle engine for near-hypersonic speeds.

In September 2026, Hermeus unveiled Ramjet-X, a smaller ramjet-powered vehicle designed for air launch from Quarterhorse platforms.

Sowing begins at Asia's largest tulip garden

Two million blooms expected
Local harvest doubles to 5.75 lakh 
8.5 lakh from Netherlands

Srinagar, Nov 14: Sowing operations have officially commenced at Asia's largest tulip garden in Srinagar, with authorities preparing for a grander and denser floral showcase next spring featuring over two million tulips.
Garden authorities confirmed that plantation activities began in early November and will run through mid-December. The garden is currently utilising an abundant harvest of homegrown bulbs alongside fresh consignments en route from the Netherlands.
Speaking on the preparations, Imran Ahmed, in-charge of the Tulip Garden, said the local harvest yielded remarkable results this season, with production nearly doubling from roughly 3.07 lakh bulbs last year to approximately 5.75 lakh. 
"Right now, we are planting our own harvested stock from last year," Ahmed said. "Two fresh consignments from Holland are also arriving separately — one expected within the week by air and another via sea route towards the end of November."
Between the imported shipment of 8.5 lakh bulbs and local yields, the garden will plant nearly 1.4 million full-sized bulbs, up from 1.1 million last year. Factoring in smaller bulblets, the total tulip count will cross the two-million mark, representing an increase of 2 lakh to 3 lakh blooms over the previous season. An additional 1 lakh companion flowers, including muscari, daffodils and hyacinths, along with 17 to 18 hybrid annual varieties nurtured in polyhouses, will complement the display.
To house the surplus stock and enhance visual appeal, garden managers are significantly raising crop density across flower beds. Standard beds previously accommodating 4,000 bulbs now hold 5,000, while smaller plots have seen plantings rise from 1,200 to 1,800 bulbs. Officials noted that the closer canopy will not only create a more vibrant visual spread but also naturally suppress weed growth.
The department is also expanding the garden's cultivated land area. While keeping essential lawn corridors open for crowd movement, staff are introducing new wave patterns and converting underutilised green patches into active flower beds.
The Tulip Garden marks the traditional opening of Kashmir's spring tourism calendar. Despite various challenges, the 2026 tulip festival drew roughly 4 lakh visitors, and authorities expect next year's expanded showcase to draw even larger crowds across the Union Territory.
Plantation and bed preparation work are scheduled to conclude by December 15.

BOSTON — On Friday at the Minnesota Wild’s team hotel, defenseman Brock Faber was called into a long meeting with coach John Hynes, general manager Bill Guerin and the team’s training staff. The gist of it? Faber is ready to return seven weeks after fracturing his skull in a preseason game.
And the team’s certainly ready to have him back. So the 24-year-old will make his season debut against the Boston Bruins on Saturday night, in the Wild’s 22nd game.

“He’s ready to play, so we’ve got to play him,” Hynes said Saturday morning. “I think part of it will be putting him back into the situations that he can play in, and we will manage the minutes. If you can get him in the mid-to-low 20s, I think that’s where he should be, and I think those are minutes that he can manage. But he will be on PK and power play.”
The coaching staff was still settling on the defense pairs. Matt Kiersted will come out of the lineup. To have a lefty with a righty on each pair, Quinn Hughes will skate with either Faber, his partner last season, or Spurgeon. Brodin, who had been with Hughes, will drop to the second pair with the other of Faber or Spurgeon. Olli Maatta and Zach Bogosian will be on the third pair.

Faber spent the morning skate at TD Garden throwing himself into the boards, getting into board-and-puck battles and taking contact from teammates.
“Obviously, there’s going to be different intentions the next time I get hit later tonight,” Faber said. “So that’s just part of it, right? I’m just weirdly super excited for (the physicality) — just to get back into the swing.
“I’m just gonna try and keep it simple. Defend hard, play hard. Hopefully block a few shots. And I think just as the game goes on, I’ll get more and more comfortable with the speed and the pace of things. Obviously, I need to get hit a couple times. But no, I’m really excited. I’m really excited.”
Faber said he felt 100 percent not long after his Sept. 27 surgery. Once he was given clearance to begin exercising and skating, it was just a matter of getting back into game shape. However, a player can only do so much in practice, especially given how rarely NHL teams practice. Now that he’s medically cleared, it’s time to take the plunge.
The Bruins are one of the top teams in the NHL, so it won’t be easy. And they’re likely to be surly after not playing well in their last game — a loss to the Montreal Canadiens.
The Wild are 8-0-2 in their past 10 games overall and have won seven consecutive road games.

“It’s great for Brock,” Hynes said. “He’s gone through a lot and got himself back in shape and ready to go, and he’s excited to play. And I think for the team, he means a lot on the ice, off the ice. He’s a great personality in the room and around the guys. He’s got some good leadership qualities. And then obviously what he brings on the ice is tough to replicate.
“I thought the guys that played when Brock was out did an admirable job and did a great job, but it’s nice for Brock to get back in and get the team together with him as well.”
Added forward Bobby Brink, “I mean, we’re super excited. He’s obviously a core piece, huge piece, of this team, and we’re just happy to have him back and happy he’s feeling good.”

Malone blames loss on ‘bad habits,’ lack of ‘game plan discipline’&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

CHAPEL HILL — With defensive rebounds consistently eluding Carolina down the stretch and 3-point shots repeatedly clanking, a 21-game home win streak is history.

While UNC shot just 23.7% on a school-record 38 3-point attempts, Georgia hit 14 3s in a 78–70 win Friday at the Smith Center after trailing by nine with 10½ minutes left.

“At the end of close games, your habits come out. Do you have good habits? Do you have bad habits?” Michael Malone said. “We didn’t have attention to detail. We didn’t have game plan discipline. We didn’t have personnel discipline tonight.”

UNC (3–1) shifted gears early in the second half, scoring 11 fast-break points in the first 11 minutes. But that momentum faded as the offense stalled and the Tar Heels let the game slip away as Georgia (4–0) outrebounded UNC 52–43 and rebounded 18 of its 43 misses.

“Give them credit. They came in, they were able to execute down the stretch,” Malone said. “They were able to get stops down the stretch, and we were not, and that was the difference.”

Terrence Brown’s defense (team-high four steals) and Maxi Adams’s constant activity (team-high 11 rebounds and two assists) fueled UNC’s momentum, but the team found no rhythm in the final minutes.

Malone said that Adams’ numbers (4 points, 1-of-8 from the floor) didn’t change how the freshman affected the game.

“What I loved about Maxi is, OK, I’m not making shots. One thing I know I can do that requires no skill is I can rebound the ball,” Malone said. “And for him to go out there and get five offensive boards, 11 total, that was great to see.”

If 52% of Carolina’s shots are going to come from outside the arc, the Tar Heels will have to get 3s from more than two players. While Terrence Brown (4-of-8) and Isaiah Denis (3-of-7) combined to go 7-of-15, the rest of the team went 2-of-23.

Brown led UNC with 21 points, while Neo Avdalas added 14 points and three assists but committed four turnovers. Denis scored 11. Georgia forward Kanon Catchings, who made three 3-pointers combined in the first three games, hit five and scored 22 points. Guard Blue Cain added 20 points and four 3-pointers.

Brown didn’t see the school-record volume as a problem.
“I wouldn’t take a three if I didn’t have to,” he said. “They were just open today. The teams were heavy in the gaps, and the threes were open, so coaches were really saying the driving kicks were there. If you’re open and you feel like it’s a good shot, just take it.”

After a Brown 3-pointer gave UNC a five-point lead nearly two minutes into the game, the teams traded leads through the first 11 minutes, with neither leading by more than three during that stretch.

Malone called a timeout after a lazy Avdalas pass led to a layup by Georgia’s Marcus Millender, giving the Bulldogs a 24–20 lead with 7:13 left in the first half. In no surprise, Avdalas soon found a seat on the bench.

Adams swiped the ball away from a Georgia defender under the basket and converted a 3-point play. But the Bulldogs scored the last nine first-half points on three 3-pointers to take a 38–31 halftime lead.

Malone said he used the locker room to show his team one clip, a loose ball that Georgia won before getting a wide-open 3-pointer.

“I felt we were on our heels most of that first half, and they were the aggressor,” Malone said. “I just wanted us to play more aggressive.”

Carolina started running after every defensive stop. Avdalas scored a transition layup on a long Able pass, Brown converted an Avdalas feed, Able hit a corner 3-pointer and Brown completed a driving three-point play. Those baskets helped fuel a 12–3 run that gave UNC a 45–43 lead with 16:21 left.

Malone said the fast-break points dried up for a simple reason.

“We stopped getting stops,” he said. “That’s how we want to play: defend, rebound, run. And then, obviously, as that second half wore on, we stopped getting as many stops. We stopped getting as many rebounds, which prohibited us from getting out and running.”

Back-to-back Denis 3-pointers and an Alex Samodurov inside bucket gave UNC a nine-point lead with 10½ minutes left. But Georgia responded with a 7–0 run to trim the lead to two with 7:07 left.

A step-back Avdalas jumper with 6:04 left ended a 4½-minute UNC scoring drought, but Georgia pulled within one on a Millender 3-pointer. Georgia repeatedly earned second shots during a 21–8 run, capped by a Catchings 3-pointer that put the Bulldogs ahead 72–68 with 1:44 left.

Malone pointed to the Bulldogs’ 3-point shooting as the biggest problem, with Catchings and Cain accounting for nine of those makes.

“It was the 14 threes that really hurt us,” Malone said. “You cannot give up a three to a guy who’s got five threes on the night, who we’ve said we want to run off the three-point line, and we close short.”

Sayon Keita dunked off a driving Adams feed with 1:13 remaining. But after Cain’s 3-pointer 12 seconds later, Avdalas missed on a 3-point attempt. Millender put the game away on a pair of free throws with 34 seconds left.

Denis said UNC’s mistakes proved costly.

“Rebounding is one of our biggest issues right now,” Denis said. “Definitely felt like we beat ourselves today.”

Notes

— Carolina gets a week off before welcoming Marshall to the Smith Center for a 9 p.m. Friday game (ACC Network) in the fifth of six consecutive home games to start the season. The Thundering Herd (1–2) play at Youngstown State at 7 p.m. Saturday (ESPN+) and host ETSU at 7 p.m. Tuesday (ESPN+). After beating Buffalo at home in its opener 92–65 on Nov. 2, Marshall lost at home to Toledo 81–76 on Nov. 7 and at Ohio State 84–74 on Monday.
— The previous record for 3-point attempts in a game was 36 in an overtime win over Syracuse at the Smith Center on Feb. 28, 2022. The previous high for a regulation game was 35 in a 2022 NCAA tournament win over Marquette.
— The 52% of shots coming from outside the arc was slightly less than the 54.5% in the opener against Western Carolina (9-of-30).
— The Tar Heels shot 25.0% (9 for 36) from the floor in the first half. That tied the third-lowest field goal percentage by the Tar Heels in a half in the Smith Center. It was the lowest since UNC shot 25.0% in the first half in 2021 against Florida State.
— Georgia’s 52 rebounds marked the first time an opponent had 50 or more since Alabama had 58 in a four-overtime game on Nov. 27, 2022. It was the first time an opponent had 50 or more in a regulation-length game since Texas A&M grabbed 50 in an NCAA tournament game in Charlotte in 2018.
— Georgia’s 14 3-pointers were the most by a UNC opponent since Louisville hi 14 last season on Feb. 23.
— The Bulldogs shot 38.6%, ending a 31-game streak of UNC winning while holding opponents under 40%. 
— UNC still leads the all-time series with Georgia 13–6.

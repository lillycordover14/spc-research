Record-Setting Barrage of Attempted Triples Proves Costly For Tar Heels&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
CHAPEL HILL, N.C. — The analytics say a 3-point shot yields a higher expected value per attempt than most alternative locations on the floor, and 
Michael Malone
 is one of many coaches across the professional and college ranks representing that philosophy. What that’s meant for North Carolina — at least four games in — is a healthy diet of layups and triples that oscillate in volume based on its opponents’ defensive strategies. 

In theory, and maybe in the NBA with a higher floor of talent, that ideology works, at least according to the math. And to the Tar Heels’ credit, they responded to their barrage of 3-point attempts against Western Carolina with an attack-the-rim approach in the next two games, representing the bones of what Malone is attempting to install. 

But pro-analytics or not, no calculation promotes taking a record-setting 38 3-pointers and making 23.7 percent of them, which is what UNC did in its 78-70 loss to Georgia, marking the first of the season and Malone’s tenure in Chapel Hill.

“We had some pretty good looks,” Malone said. “We were just unable to knock them down. But it’s just being more disciplined with the game plan, more disciplined with personnel, and we’ll learn from it.”

North Carolina’s struggles from deep plagued it throughout its first power-conference tilt. A 5-for-20 showing in the first half brightened after four makes — two courtesy of 
Isaiah Denis
 — between the 16:53 and 11:24 marks of the second half, steadying its percentages and positioning the Tar Heels to secure a marquee early-season victory.

Then Carolina went cold from three — and in totality — missing their final 10 attempts from beyond the arc as a 9-point lead with 10:35 remaining vanished. UNC attempted three more triples than it did 2-pointers on the night and is shooting 33.6 percent as a team this season.

“I wouldn’t take a three if I didn’t have to,” 
Terrence Brown
 said. “They were just open today. Like (Georgia) was heavy in the gaps, and the threes were open. So the coaches were really saying the drive-and-kicks were there. So if you’re open and you feel like it’s a good shot, just take it.”

Brown’s initial diagnosis postgame was correct. North Carolina did inherit an abundance of open looks, oftentimes from presumably reliable players such as 
Matt Able
, 
Jaydon Young
 and 
Jarin Stevenson
, that didn’t fall.

The point guard, less decorated as a flamethrower, went 4-for-8 from distance and led the Tar Heels in scoring. But his counterparts in the starting lineup tanked the numbers, drilling two of their 17 attempts. Additionally, Young and 
Maximo Adams
 shot 0-for-6 collectively off the bench.

Isaiah Denis, though, added three 3-pointers off the bench and is up to 38.9 percent in 2026-27. However, the sophomore did not play in the final 7:07.

“We pride ourselves on kicking ahead in transition,” Denis explained. “Trying to play in transition, kicking ahead threes, kicking ahead paint touch threes, rim threes, layups, running out, trying to get layups, (and) trying to get threes. So that’s what we want to be, and I think we got a lot of good looks.”

Certainly, a few more made 3-pointers could have changed the outcome on Friday night. But Malone’s emphasis on threes and layups is well-documented, and Carolina’s perimeter-weighted talent is hardly a secret, and Georgia reacted to it well in crunch time.

The Bulldogs built a blueprint to stifle the Tar Heels when they face those shooting slumps — taking away the rim, sitting in the gaps and continuing to force UNC into desperation triples. And why did it work? Because three of North Carolina’s four starters with 3-point volume are shooting at or below 30 percent from deep this season, a trend that worsened versus the 11th-ranked team in the SEC preseason poll.

“They were switching everything on the ball and off the ball (down the stretch), making it hard,” Malone said. “So we were trying to attack the mismatch at times.”

The Tar Heels shot 40 percent and 45 percent from range against Wofford and Wyoming, respectively, in 45 combined attempts. In their other two regular-season outings, UNC took 68 3-pointers and converted on 19, for 27.9 percent.

But this is more than streaky shooting. It’s a trend that represents North Carolina’s fatal habit of firing a careless number of rushed, questionable attempts just because that’s what the defense is giving it.

To fix it, the Tar Heels will either need a serious uptick in their shooting or a steadier balance in their downhill attack, keeping defenses less certain about what UNC is looking to exploit. Because while it may not have the personnel to pound the ball inside, North Carolina also hasn’t proven that its excess of 3-point volume matched the quality of its shooters on the perimeter.

“I think we shot maybe seven or eight air balls tonight,” Malone said.

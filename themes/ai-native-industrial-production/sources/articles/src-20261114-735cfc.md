Hands-On Debut: The 04Series Watches Are Something Entirely Different For Studio Underd0g&nbsp;&nbsp;aBlogtoWatch

[page text]
In handling the new 
Studio Underd0g
 watch, I’m remind of a handful of bands I used to like and still do, and others that I used to like and no longer do. A few of them have shown little change in the music they make, pumping out the same sound that made me like them in the first place. Others changed their sound noticeably, and I’ve stuck to their earlier hits that give me a nice dose of nostalgia. Others, and this is the smallest group, have evolved from the music that originally hooked me, yet have done so (to my ear) successfully, and I’ve stayed along for the ride. That final route is arguably the hardest to navigate, and there’s a good bit of luck involved. I think the challenge of change is something that any person or company or group that creates a product has to confront at one point or another. What does it look like for a company to evolve, or mature? Will fans and customers stay along for the ride? Ultimately, I think that is the question being asked by Studio Underd0g with the release of the calendar-focused 
Studio Underd0g 04Series
.

Studio Underd0g started as what I think we could call a fun, affordable brand: 
$675 chronographs themed around food
. It impressed with 
its second collection
, but still mostly maintained a culinary connection in each piece, even if it was a bit looser. The next one went 
way
 upmarket with a Swiss monopusher movement and a price tag to match, but still, those watches were inspired by salmon and 
passionfruit
. So when the new 04Series dropped, I was a bit surprised. The aesthetic and narrative fun of previous models seems lacking and there’s not the slightest aroma of fresh-baked anything. What the 04Series seems to introduce is a more mature version of the brand, or at least it reveals a more serious path it’s chosen to explore.

More peculiar than anything Studiop Underd0g has done before is the release of all four 04Series models in the buyer’s option of 37mm or 38.5mm. For review, I had the 37mm Shepherd’s Delight (from the saying, “Red sky at night, shepherd’s delight.”) and the 38.5mm Arabian Sky. In the press release, the brand states that the seemingly small size difference has a real impact, but I’ll be honest and let you know that by the time I received the watches, I had completely forgotten they came in two sizes. Nor did I notice a significant difference while handling them or photographing them, and it was only when I consulted the release to get the exact specs for this review that I was reminded. That’s likely because the only visible difference between the two models is the width of the bezel, slightly thicker lugs, and that on the 37mm versions, the correctors seem to stick out instead of being flush with the case.

The Studio Underd0g 04Series measures either 37mm or 38.mm in diameter and a somewhat bulky 13.5mm thick with its domed sapphire crystal. A longer case may mitigate the thickness, but at 44.5mm lug-to-lug, you certainly feel a prominence on the wrist. I wore the Arabian Sky around for several days and never found it to be overwhelming or bulky, but it’s far from the sleek, low-lying designs I usually prefer. The height is emphasized, or at least not helped, by the flat bezel with its faceted sides, which stands proudly atop the midcase with a mix of polished and brushed surfaces. The midcase itself is crisply brushed with a thin polished chamfer, and the crown shines brightest with a fully polished surface. As mentioned above, the correctors jut out a bit on the 37mm, and for that reason, I’d definitely opt for the 38mm. I also think I prefer the wider bezel, now that I realize it’s there.

 

The watches come with a color-coordinated strap, and I must say these straps are lovely. Thick but not too stiff, soft but not silky, they strike the perfect balance. Made in England by the Strap Tailor (which has made all SU straps since the beginning), the 20mm leather straps have custom pin buckles and quick release pins. I especially liked the deep green on the Arabian Sky.

 

Instead of food, the dials for the 04Series appear to be inspired by the skies above: the light blue Cloud10, the red Shepherd’s Delight, the sandy orange Arabian Sky, and the ominously black 0vercast. It’s hard to pick a favorite, but I think I’d have to opt for Arabian Sky if I were buying one. The red is a bit too red for my personal preferences, and the Overcast has Halloween vibes I can’t ignore.

Colorways aside, the dials are all the same, and arguably the most interesting part of the watches. Altogether, the dial displays the moonphase at 6 (adjusted by a push corrector at 8 in the caseband), the date via central pointer hand along a retrograde scale (adjusted with a push corrector at 2), and the weekends via apertures along the same date arc (quick-set with the crown). Finishing is varied, providing quite a show of texture: the outer ring has circular brushing, the central dial is satin-polished, and the recessed moonphase has radially grooving. The retrograde weekend apertures are apparently CNC-milled instead of stamped or otherwise cut, and they have beveled slopes that give the openings a more pronounced, dramatic feeling. There’s also lots of color coordination. Regardless of the colorway, the moonphase and weekend wheel share a color scheme that contrasts with the main dial for an added pop of color. I especially like the way the Arabian Sky model uses teal.

So where does this design succeed, and are there places it falls short? I wouldn’t ask the question if I didn’t have thoughts. Overall, I love the balance here, the blend of dial finishes and textures, and the use of color. These are things we knew Studio Underd0g could do well, and it hasn’t let us down with this new design.

The weekend functionality is a fun addition that you don’t often see (the most recent example I can recall is the 
Krayon Anyday
). You may not think it’s useful, but it’s nice to be able to glance at the wrist to have a visual reminder of how close (or, less nice, how far) the weekend is. And while I never did, I suppose you could use it for at a glance planning and reminders of the date on the next weekend. Whether you use it or not, it’s a rare complication and it’s implemented beautifully.

So where does this design fall short for me? The first is common to almost all pointer dates, and my worsening eyesight may well be a factor: The date numbers are too damn small. From a design perspective, you really don’t want them being too big, and of course I’m generally aware of the date on any given day, so it’s not like I’m 
using
 the complication. But when I tried, there was squinting. There’s also no lume, which doesn’t much bother me and fits the look of the watch, but I know is a dealbreaker for some.

The date hand itself does an excellent job standing out, and the beveled leaf-style hands are beautiful, reminding me of a restrained version of what we saw on the 
Atelier Wen Ancestra
. Of particular note is how the date hand functions. The brand explicitly states that it wanted to avoid the date hand just sitting there obscuring the moonphase (“Talk to the hand, because the phase don’t want to hear it,” it cleverly quipped.) To solve that problem, it used a retrograde date scale (and corresponding weekend arc) and the hand jumps from 31 to 0. Note that the hand itself is not a retrograde hand; it doesn’t swing 
back
 around the arc, but rather jumps the arc segment occupied by the moonphase. It gets the job done, but I think it’s important to clarify.

Finally, while the weekend indicator is thankfully set easily by the crown, you do need to set it every month, unless the dates and days of the week just happen to align from month to month. At this price point, I don’t think that’s unreasonable, but I was obviously hoping it corrected itself. And let’s be real: if it could do that, we’d be talking about something like an annual or perpetual calendar, and that would be an entirely different (though welcome) proposition.

So how has Studio Underd0g powered the 04Series? That’s almost its own headline. Five years ago, SU was one of innumerable brands using Seagull ST19 manual wound movements to power its affordable chronos. Then Seagull raised the MOQ to 10,000 movements, and SU didn’t just commit, but it got its own version of the movement from the Chinese manufacturer. For the 04Series, it’s gone a step further, while still working with Seagull. The watches use the Seagull Automatic ST-2115B, which is not only Studio Underd0g’s first automatic movement, but also was custom-built for and is exclusive to the brand. You can see it right on the rotor: “SEA-GULL for Studio Underd0g.” The new caliber reportedly involved the development of 34 additional components to deliver the required functionality, and it looks rather good with some. For specs, the movement delivers hacking, 42 hours of power reserve at 28,800 vph, and accuracy of -10/+15 seconds per day.

Evolution is a good thing, if only because the alternative is stagnation. The problem for a brand with an identity is how to navigate a path of change without alienating those that got you there. I think the new 04Series is a strong step in the shift from being a fun, affordable brand to a brand that perhaps even more people can and will appreciate. Whether it will see the same success as it has with the 01Series and the 02Series, we’ll just have to see. The Studio Underd0g 04Series is priced at 
$1,100 USD
. For more information, please visit 
the Studio Underd0g website
.

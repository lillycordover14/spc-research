Korea Circuit Jumps 51% on Broadcom Tailwind; AI Substrate Growth Seen Doubling Up

The rally was driven by a rebound in semiconductor stocks after CoreWeave's strong results and by the rise of the non-Nvidia camp—Broadcom in particular—after Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO. Shares of semiconductor printed circuit board (PCB) maker Korea Circuit jumped more than 50% in a week, nearly erasing its recent correction. The move reflects revived expectations for simultaneous growth in AI server memory module substrates and package substrates, along with growing bets that the company will benefit indirectly as Broadcom emerges as an alternative customer to Nvidia.

Korea Circuit closed at 91,500 won (approximately $68) on the KOSPI on Nov. 13, up 3.27% from the previous session. Its gain over the five sessions from Nov. 9 to 13 reached 51.09%. The stock had fallen from 88,700 won (approximately $66) on Oct. 6 to 59,700 won (approximately $45) on Nov. 6 before recovering the entire decline in a single week.

The rebound began with a recovery in investor sentiment toward substrate stocks broadly, which had been weighed down by concerns over delayed data center construction. Buying returned to the semiconductor sector after CoreWeave reported strong results, lifting substrate shares in tandem. Korea Circuit stood out with a particularly sharp gain, as investors focused on the fact that it is the only South Korean substrate maker with two growth engines at once: SOCAMM for memory modules and FC-BGA package substrates for AI accelerators.

News of Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO also worked in the company's favor. After Huang acknowledged an inappropriate relationship with a figure suspected of Chinese espionage, expectations spread that global Big Tech companies would diversify their semiconductor suppliers. Nvidia shares plunged while Broadcom and AMD surged, making Korea Circuit—which counts Broadcom as a key customer—a standout beneficiary.

Earnings improvement expectations also underpinned the stock. Analysts expect South Korean capital investment to shift toward more multi-layered, highly integrated memory modules starting in the first quarter of next year, driving up product unit prices. Spillover effects from a deepening FC-BGA shortage are also expected to kick in. Korea Circuit's Broadcom supply business is projected to expand from conventional communications components into higher-margin AI applications.

Park Kang-ho, an analyst at Daishin Securities, said, "Korea Circuit is expected to enter a full-fledged earnings growth phase starting in the fourth quarter." He added, "With additional capacity expansion in SOCAMM2 and server memory modules, revenue is expected to increase sharply in 2027, while orders centered on FC-BGA should drive further operating margin improvement in the package segment."

The rally dovetailed with a broader recovery in risk appetite across South Korean markets. The KOSPI closed at 5,450.60, up 2.02% from the previous session, while the KOSDAQ rose 3.53% to 721.9. Foreign investors and institutions net bought 135.2 billion won (approximately $101.2 million) and 98.5 billion won (approximately $73.7 million) of KOSPI shares, respectively, and Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each rose around 2%. The moves reflected a widening view that the shock from Nvidia is not spilling over into damage to the growth prospects of the broader AI ecosystem or into a decline in GPU demand.

External variables remain, however. International oil prices rose for a second straight day after U.S. President Donald Trump signaled that negotiations with Iran are unnecessary, and U.S. Treasury yields held above 5%. Seo Sang-young, head of a division at Mirae Asset Securities, cited the risk of inflation concerns flaring up again ahead of the U.S. producer price index (PPI) release, and noted that key events next week—including Nvidia's earnings report and the U.S.-China summit—could affect investor sentiment.

Market participants say whether Korea Circuit extends its gains will depend on whether actual order growth in SOCAMM2 and FC-BGA confirms the optimism. As long as the AI server investment cycle holds, substrate demand can grow structurally, but capacity expansion and certification procedures remain hurdles before customer diversification expectations translate into actual revenue.

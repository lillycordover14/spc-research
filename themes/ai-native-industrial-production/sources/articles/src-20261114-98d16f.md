PHOENIX (AZFamily)
 — Airports 
across Arizona
 were affected by a nationwide flight ground stop for several hours Friday.
The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) ordered a ground stop on all departing flights across the United States around 2:15 p.m. Mountain time. The 
ground stop was lifted
 about four hours later, but flight delays and cancellations are continuing.
Federal officials describe outage
“There has been a temporary telecommunications outage - out of an abundance of caution all flights have been halted,” the FAA said when it issued the ground stop. “Every flight has been accounted for and international flights will be handled on a case by case basis.”
Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said there was a communications issue affecting the regional air traffic control centers that direct planes between airports nationwide. FAA technicians are investigating the cause.
Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication that a national security threat caused the outage, though he did not rule out any possible cause. 
Some pilots said they did not feel fear for the safety of anyone in the air or on planes at any point.
Flights begin to depart at Sky Harbor
Friday afternoon is typically one of the busiest travel times of the week. The outage disrupted plans for people traveling to weddings, international trips, sports tournaments and more.
Some flights have started to take off at Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport. The departure board in Terminal 4 showed a Southwest flight to Dallas, originally scheduled to leave at 2:25 p.m., as departed.
Many other flights remained delayed
, some into the evening hours.
Announcements over the loudspeaker at Sky Harbor said the ground stop was expected to last until 9 p.m., but it was lifted before then. Some passengers made other plans in the meantime, including leaving the airport for their homes, a hotel or a friend’s house. Some retrieved their luggage.
Passengers describe concern, confusion
Passengers stuck while the ground stop was in effect described their reaction.
“I’m worried,” one passenger said. “It sounds like something really serious is going on. But there’s nothing I can do. Stressing will not make it easier. But I do have concern about what’s going on.”
Another passenger said: “Kind of confused on what’s going on, whether it’s just communication, like their communications are down, or if something actually bigger is happening.”
Many people spent much of the day on the phone with family and friends or searching online for answers.
Lines continue at Terminal 4
At the Southwest check-in line in Terminal 4, people said they waited in a line of an hour to an hour and a half after the stop was lifted. 
Some people were waiting to check in for flights Friday night, some were trying to rebook travel for Saturday morning, and others were trying to determine their travel plans.
A backlog of planes must be put in place, and the effects are being felt at Sky Harbor and across Arizona.
Baggage claim waits
At Sky Harbor baggage claim, some people were waiting for bags or items they checked. At one point, carousels turned on and then stopped, while monitors flickered.
Travelers were checking airline apps and keeping email tabs refreshed for updates from their airlines. The airport also was working to determine next steps.
Many travelers at baggage claim did not show panic or a bad attitude. One man said he was told he would have to wait four to five hours for his luggage.
“I’m relieved that it’s over, but this is obviously going to take a long time to resolve, so nobody’s getting anywhere they need to go right now,” he said.
It will take about the next day or so to get things back on track, officials have said. 
Hotels fill up with stranded guests
A travel expert said Valley residents with hotel reservations at their travel destinations should call the hotel right away and say they are affected by the outage. The expert said hotels are likely receiving hundreds of calls.
The expert said visitors stuck in the Valley overnight should start calling airport-area hotels right away because they will fill quickly.
Susan, a traveler trying to get home to Boston, said she had spent enough nights in an airport.
“It’s like much better here,” Susan said. “But there were only three rooms left when we did it. So it’s not going to get much crazier here.”
It is not clear whether travelers will be 
refunded for canceled reservations or an extra night’s stay
. Coverage will likely depend on what caused the outage.
Travelers are advised to keep all receipts and documents, such as for toiletries bought because a bag was checked, to submit to a credit card company or travel insurance company later.

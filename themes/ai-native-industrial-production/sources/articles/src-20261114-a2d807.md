The UNC men’s basketball team (3-1) falls for the first time in the Michael Malone era, 78-70, against Georgia (4-0). 

North Carolina defeated its first three opponents by a combined total of 60 points, but in its first real test against a Power 4 conference program — the Tar Heels put up a fight, but it was not enough to overcome the 14 threes made by the Bulldogs. 

Senior guard Terrence Brown had eight points and two rebounds by the 12-minute timeout, and fueled North Carolina’s 14-11 advantage by that point. 

As a team, UNC finished shooting 9-of-36 after the first half and 5-of-20 from beyond the arc — not pretty by any means. Brown started to cool off, too — concluding the first 20 minutes going 3-of-10 from the field. 

“I wouldn’t take a three if I didn’t have to,” Brown said. “They were just open today. The teams were heavy in the gaps and the threes were open so coaches were really saying the driving kicks were there so if you’re open and you feel like it’s a good shot just take it.” 

Tonight was the second time the Tar Heels and the Bulldogs faced off ever, as the first contest on Nov. 18, 1998, ended with UNC pulling out a 65-58 victory at home. Forward Kris Lang scored 21 points on 8-of-13 shooting and 5-of-9 from the free throw line. Not only that, North Carolina took charge with a 32-26 rebounding difference, unlike tonight’s outing. 

The rebounding issues continued for North Carolina, as it fell behind the Bulldogs, 27-21. Sophomore guard Matt Able led the team with eight, while forward Sayon Keita had just two despite standing at 7-foot-1.

Although the second half saw a much-improved group of Tar Heels — they came out of the locker room with something to prove. By the 11:23 mark, UNC was up, 58-51, after back-to-back threes by sophomore guard Isaiah Denis. 

On offense, UNC piled on 14 fastbreak points but lost that source of scoring during crunch time. 

“Honestly, I don’t know,” Denis said. “Obviously we got away from what kept us when we were up seven.” 

North Carolina shot 15-of-37 from the field and 4-of-18 from beyond the arc. But more importantly, crashed the glass — reeling in 22 rebounds, including eight offensive boards. Still, UNC fell behind in that category when it was all said and done — losing 52-43 overall. 

Sophomore guard Neo Avdalas finished a layup in traffic that gave UNC a 68-67 advantage. But that served as the last time the Tar Heels led before eventually allowing an 11-2 scoring run to close out regulation time. 

“Hopefully we can learn from it — is that at the end of close games your habits come out,” Malone said. “Do you have good habits? Do you have bad habits?” 

The bad habits appeared for North Carolina and were no good in a tight-knit competition. 

Despite the improved performance in the second half, it was not enough as
 Kanon Catchings led the way for Georgia with a double-double of 22 points and 10 rebounds. Blue Cain supported Catchings with 20 points and seven rebounds. 

North Carolina will return to action on Friday, Nov. 20 against Marshall at 9 p.m.

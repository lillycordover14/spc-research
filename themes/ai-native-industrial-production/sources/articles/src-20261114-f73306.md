    Previewing UNC football’s home matchup with Louisville

    On Saturday, North Carolina takes on the Cardinals under the lights of Kenan Stadium with a chance at bowl eligibility.

Josh Markotich
, 
Assistant Sports Editor
                 
             •  
            
November 13, 2026
 

 

Bryson Smith
 
UNC redshirt junior running back Benjamin Hall (7) runs with the ball during the game against Miami at Kenan Stadium on Saturday, Oct. 31, 2026. Miami won 38-35.
 

The magic number is one. 

One game to become bowl-eligible — a complete turnaround from the dismal 4-8 outcome only a year ago — and on Saturday night, UNC plays the strongest opponent of its remaining three-game stretch. 

If North Carolina hopes to win its home matchup against Louisville, it will have to score at a pace it’s unaccustomed to. The Cardinals currently boast an average of 40.78 points per game and hold their opponents to 25.89. 

Even in their losses, Louisville has neared the 30-point mark in every game, which will test a UNC team that keeps matchups low-scoring. 

Leading the Cardinals’ charge is senior Lincoln Kienholz, the dual-threat quarterback, who has 2,276 passing yards and leads his team in rushing touchdowns with 11 — the next closest is junior running back Isaac Brown, at eight. 

“Getting in on Louisville here, these guys are a really good team,” head coach Bill Belichick said at Monday’s press conference. “They’re on a good run here. So a lot of good players; they’re pretty tough offensively.”

Meanwhile, graduate quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. is coming off two of his best games as a Tar Heel: a 35-38 loss to then-No. 1 Miami and a dominant 38-21 win over UConn in Connecticut. 

Edwards has shown glimpses of a promising quarterback who can perform when the pressure is highest, and will need to show up if UNC wants to match Louisville’s productive offense. 

“We want to go out there and, you know, put our best product out on the field and win,” Edwards said at the press conference.

Playing off of Edwards is junior tight end Jelani Thurman, who matched Mike Chatham’s 1979 record of eight touchdowns in a season — posting two last weekend. 

Thurman and Edwards have played as a dynamic duo all season, with Thurman being a top receiving target for the graduate quarterback. 

Alongside a strong offensive showing, the Tar Heels will also have to put history behind themselves, having a 3-5 all-time record against Louisville, with the last game being in 2017 when Lamar Jackson put on an all-time performance in Kenan Stadium. Jackson totaled 525 yards in a 47-35 Louisville win.

There is no doubt that the UNC offense can succeed against the Cardinal defense; but it is a matter of whether they can keep up with their offense, or if the UNC defense can stifle a team that hasn’t scored below 27 points this season. 

“It’s an out-execution kind of game now,” Edwards said at Monday’s press conference. “That’s what it will come down to at the end of the day.”

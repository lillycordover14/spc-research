What is this?

HONOLULU — North Dakota State won the game. Let’s make that clear. The Bison are 8-2 in their first year in FBS and remain in contention for a berth in the Mountain West Conference championship game.
NDSU’s 21-13 triumph over scrappy Hawaii late Saturday night on a rainy, windy night in paradise was a hard-fought victory after a 2,800-mile road trip that was delayed for 13 hours by an airplane issue, coming after two (more) offensive starters were lost to injury during the game.

ADVERTISEMENT

Can’t take that away.
Don’t want to take that away.
But what in the name of Duke Kahanamoku is with that offense?
The historical surfing king of Hawaii, immortalized by a statue on Waikiki Beach, would’ve covered his eyes with poi having to watch the Bison struggle as they did.
NDSU’s defense? Lights out.
NDSU’s offense? Three and out.
The Bison had 183 total yards. That’s 86 running and 97 passing. In four quarters. In 2026 college football.

The offense, really, put together one good drive and, really, accounted for only one of the three touchdowns the Bison put on the board. The other two went to the defense, one interception that provided an extremely short field and another that went for a pick-six.
What we had here, in this land of indescribable beauty, was a battle of punts and field position.
Bet that helped keep Bison fans back home awake until the game ended at 1:27 a.m. Sunday, Fargo time.
“When things are going like that, when things aren’t going our way and we’re punting a lot and getting off the field a lot, you just gotta keep your head down and keep reminding the guys it’s a four-quarter game,” said Bison quarterback Nathan Hayes, who completed 13 of 20 passes for 97 yards.
His long completion was 17 yards. NDSU’s top playmaker, Jackson Williams, had two catches for 8 yards and was targeted just four times.
Hayes was right. The game did, indeed, entail four quarters and for that the Bison should be eternally grateful. After three quarters, NDSU trailed 13-7 and it wasn’t until the fourth that NDSU’s offense found a rhythm. One drive, 16 plays, 77 yards, nearly 9 minutes. It ended with DJ Scott’s 1-yard touchdown plunge with 8:34 left in the game.
To save you the trouble of doing the math, that means outside of that drive the Bison totaled 106 yards the rest of the game.

That’s what had Bison fans howling. It’s just not good.
“It’s a combination of everything right now,” head coach Tim Polasek said when asked what the problem is. “I’m just, you know, not beyond frustration but we gotta get some things fixed and we gotta be a little more consistent on offense in order to win next week.
“Right now, the defense is playing at such a high level that we want to leverage that, but it’s also like, we gotta join the party here a little bit offensively.”
Judging from the inbox and social media, offensive coordinator Dan Larson has replaced Hayes as the root of all offensive evils … so the two usual suspects have been covered.
We’ve covered all these bases multiple times already this season, but the three indisputable facts are that NDSU’s offensive line isn’t nearly what it once was, its running backs lack any kind of burst and aside from Williams and tight end Drew Kessel there aren’t any consistent playmakers.
And the Bison lost Kessel to an apparent ankle injury in the first quarter Saturday night, then decided to not target Williams.
Where Larson and Polasek deserve some finger-pointing is that at times the play-calling seems rigid. They are going to do what they are going to do, even if opposing defenses are geared toward what they are going to do.

How often since the Air Force game have you said, “Whoa, didn’t expect that?” How often are defenses fooled by NDSU?
There was a huge example Saturday: On third and 1 from the Hawaii 20, trailing 13-7 midway through the fourth quarter, Hayes hit tight end Jett Zabel on a 17-yard pass to the three. Scott scored two plays later for the lead.
The Rainbow Warriors clearly didn’t expect that.
Fairness time. The Bison had five straight drives start inside their 10 yard line. That’s tough sledding.
But the running game, again, never got going. NDSU averaged 2.4 yards a carry on 36 attempts.
Pressed about the running game, Polasek said "miscommunication and misidentification" were factors in the struggles.
On fourth-and-2 from the Hawaii 35 on NDSU’s first drive, which was its second-best of the game, Scott ran right and was smothered for a 7-yard loss.

ADVERTISEMENT

“Especially early in the game, that’s detrimental to how the flow’s going to go. We’re pushing a combination outside and we’ve got the combination that should be wrapping inside, building a nice big wall there. And so that’s part of it,” Polasek said. “Right now, without any stability in the quarterback run game, you can see we’re struggling a little bit.
“I will say this: In that one particular drive, we’re creating some rhythm and momentum so we gotta duplicate that more often. It’s not up to our standard right now.”
No, 86 rushing yards and 183 total yards is not up to anybody’s standard.

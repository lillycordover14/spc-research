Three takeaways from UNC’s narrow loss to Louisville
Travis Burgess makes his debut in a loss to Louisville.
by
 
 
David Starnes
Nov 15, 2026, 2:00 PM UTC
UNC this season with three exceptions - ETSU, Notre Dame, and UConn - plays every game to the wire, one possession affairs that come down to a play or two. Not surprisingly. UNC stands at 5-5 after 10 games, with stretches of impressive play combined with frustrating lapses. This team is a few plays away from both 7-3 and 3-7. Homecoming against Louisville offered UNC plenty of chances to knock off a (via the metrics) top 25 opponent. Once again, UNC came up a couple of plays short. What do we take away from this game?
UNC’s defense played perhaps its best game of the season
Louisville came into this game averaging 7.76 yards per play, best in the nation. UNC held Louisville to 4.7 yards per play, a full three yards lower than their season average. It was Louisville’s lowest output of the season in that metric.
Louisville had 302 total yards, their lowest total for the season by almost 100 yards.
Louisville was averaging 38.6 points in conference play, with a low of 27. UNC held the Cardinals to 16.
Louisville QB Lincoln Kienholz slightly overthrew a streaking Treyshon Hurry on the first play of the game, and it ended up being Kienholz’s best throw of the night. Outside of a short field touchdown provided by a sack-fumble on Edwards, UNC stymied Louisville’s offense all game long.
Unfortunately, UNC’s offense played its worst game of the season
UNC’s 293 total yards was Louisville’s best defensive showing of the season outside of their game against Villanova. Stanford and Georgia Tech both produced north of 350, while NC State, Wake Forest, and Ole Miss all approached 500 total yards. Louisville’s conference opponents average 25.6 points coming into this game, with a low of 17.
UNC’s offense also handed Louisville two crucial turnovers. The sack-fumble of Edwards one minute into the second quarter gave Louisville the ball on UNC’s 21. Four plays later, Louisville had its lone touchdown of the game. On the final drive, Travis Burgess threw a 4th down pass behind Jordan Shipp, who managed to turn 180 degrees and make a valiant attempt to catch the ball behind him, with the bauble resulting in an interception.
The over/under for this game was 59.5, with Louisville a 3.5 point favorite. That implied a final score around 31-28, but both defenses dominated the offenses in this game. After scoring 30 or more points in three straight games, UNC couldn’t manage even half that against an average (at best) Louisville defense.
Travis Burgess flashes some promise for next season
Burgess threw two slightly off-target balls on fourth down slants to Jordan Shipp. He also took a couple of bad sacks when he failed to diagnose defensive pressure. On the other hand, his athleticism helped propel UNC to its first points in the game since the opening drive of the game. His first pass to Trech Kekahuna in the end zone was a well-placed touch pass that easily could have been a touchdown pass with a slightly better hands.
Billy Edwards handles defensive diagnosis in ways that will keep him on the field, assuming he’s healthy. Edwards was 10-14 prior to his injury, while Burgess was 6-11 in relief. Still, UNC fans have been very curious about the touted true freshman, and some called for Burgess to take the starter spot after some early disappointing performances by Billy. Burgess played well as a true freshman thrown into a tight game, and that bodes well for next season (assuming UNC holds onto him).
If UNC’s defense and offense could put together the kind of game
 they’ve both proven they can play, such as UNC’s offense against Miami and UNC’s defense last night against Louisville, this team could be competing for a spot in the ACC title game. That’s not abnormal in college football, where week to week variance can cause fans to tear their hair out. UNC now has to regroup and try to put together complete games against Virginia and NC State to finish the season on a high note.

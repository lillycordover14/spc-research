Defensive gem, offensive dud and ugly finish at Kenan&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

CHAPEL HILL — One of Carolina’s best defensive games of the season should have been enough, but a sputtering offense didn’t do its part.

The Tar Heels held a Louisville team that came in averaging 40.8 points to just 16, with its lone touchdown coming after a Billy Edwards Jr. fumble.

UNC tried to rally behind freshman quarterback Travis Burgess after Edwards’ apparent upper-body injury, but fell short in a 16–14 Cardinals win on a cool Saturday night at Kenan Stadium.

Burgess’ first career pass after replacing Edwards was beautiful and could have given UNC the lead. He found Trech Kekahuna in the end zone on a third-and-10 at the Louisville 16, but officials ruled his terrific catch incomplete.

Burgess came close to giving UNC the lead with his first college pass, but Kekahuna’s grab (top photo) in the end zone was ruled no catch.

Coach Bill Belichick credited the group effort against a deep Cardinals offense.

“It’s a team defense,” he said. “You have to play team defense, and I thought our guys did a pretty good job of that.” He said the Tar Heels “hung in there and were able to play pretty good in the red area and pretty good on third down.”

As ugly as the Tar Heels’ offensive struggles were, the aftermath was uglier, with punches thrown and an unfortunate melee on the field. It all started when UNC defensive tackle Xavier Lewis and Louisville offensive lineman Johnnie Brown II went after each other.

Belichick said his focus was getting players out of the scrum.

“Just got everybody out of there as quickly as possible,” he said. “Played the game between the whistles, and that’s not what we’re here for.”

Wide receiver Jordan Shipp said he didn’t see what happened, but added, “From our end, that’s not the way you want to end the game.”

Edwards has taken a beating all season, but Louisville’s T.J. Capers clobbered him in the third quarter. After being down for several minutes, he walked off, and Travis Burgess took his place. Edwards practiced passing at one point, but then ran to the locker room. Belichick had no insights on Edwards’ condition.

Both were sacked three times: Edwards 10 of 14 for 120 yards. Burgess 6 of 11 for 59 yards and an interception, showing potential and exceptional arm strength. Only six Tar Heels caught passes, led by Shipp, who caught seven for 61 yards, and Jelani Thurman, who had five catches for 72 yards.

Shipp was among the Tar Heels who went to check on Edwards as he lay on the field.

“I’m praying for Billy and his family,” he said. Shipp also praised the freshman who replaced him. “Trav came in and did an amazing job. He took command of the huddle.”

Running back Benjamin Hall ran for 48 yards and his first two-touchdown game, but it wasn’t enough.

Linebacker Derek McDonald led the defensive effort with eight tackles and three assists. He said the plan was to limit the explosive plays that had fueled Louisville’s season.

“They have a ton of explosive plays on the season, so just making sure we have a bunch of guys around the ball,” McDonald said. “If someone misses a tackle, it’s no big deal. There’s another guy right there to clean him up.”

UNC (5–5, 2–4 ACC) held Louisville (7–3, 6–2) to a 46-yard Carson Hilbert field goal on the game’s opening drive, then drove 75 yards in 12 plays for a touchdown. After converting two third downs and a 15-yard dump pass to Jaden Jefferson, Hall ran up the middle, then cut left for an 11-yard scoring run with 4:54 left in the opening quarter.

Carolina went without a first down for the next five drives with 13 plays and only 22 yards.

“We were in long yardage on three straight drives,” Belichick said. “Sacked on a screen pass, got a penalty, had another negative play.”

A Greg Smith tackle for a loss on third-and-two at the UNC 12 forced the Cardinals to settle again for a 32-yard Hilbert field goal. But an Edwards fumble gave Louisville the ball at the UNC 21, and quarterback Lincoln Kienholz threw a wide-open pass to Jaleel Skinner in the end zone to give the Cardinals a 13–7 lead with 12:15 left in the first half.

UNC ran only 23 plays and gained just 89 yards, but somehow trailed only 13–7 at halftime after Hilbert missed a 48-yard field-goal attempt with two seconds left in the first half.

A 34-yard Thurman reception with 11:23 left in the third quarter marked UNC’s first first down since the opening drive. After Kekahuna’s play was ruled no catch, Rece Verhoff missed a 39-yard field goal attempt.

Carolina drove to the Louisville 21 with 12:50 left, but Burgess’ fourth-and-four pass to Shipp was incomplete after UNC opted not to attempt a field goal.

Asked about the decision to go for it on fourth down while trailing by six, Belichick said, “We weren’t down there very much today, so we got down there. I thought that was an opportunity for us.”

The Cardinals drove 10 plays for a 36-yard Hilbert field goal, pushing their lead to nine with 7:28 left.

UNC responded with a nine-play, 75-yard, 3:45 drive for a six-yard Hall touchdown run with 3:43 left to make it a two-point game, 16–14.

The Carolina defense gave the offense one more chance from the UNC 30 after forcing a Louisville punt. On fourth-and-12, Burgess’ pass deflected off Shipp and Jabari Mack intercepted it to seal the Cardinals’ victory.

Shipp took responsibility for the play.

“They trusted me with that play,” he said. “It’s what it is. You gotta make the play.” He said Burgess was the first teammate to find him afterward. “After the fourth-down drop, he was the first person to me. That’s the future of this program.”

Carolina’s offense managed only 14 points on a night when the defense held Louisville well below its season scoring average.

Belichick said he believes his team is close to turning those games around. “We’re a couple plays away,” he said. “Just need to make those plays.”

Notes

— Carolina plays its final road game of the season at 12:30 Saturday (The CW) against Virginia (5–5, 3–4). The Cavaliers snapped a three-game losing streak Saturday with a 36–7 home win over California.
— UNC has lost both games to Louisville since the Cardinals joined the ACC but t and trails in the series 6–3, including 4–2 in Kenan Stadium.
— The Tar Heels scored on their opening drive, extending their streak to 14 consecutive drives with a red-zone score. During the stretch, UNC recorded 11 touchdowns and three field goals.
— Saturday marked the fewest points scored by the Louisville offense this season. The Cardinals also recorded their second-fewest passing yards (154) and third-fewest rushing yards (148) of the season.
— Belichick is 3–8 at home in his two seasons at UNC.

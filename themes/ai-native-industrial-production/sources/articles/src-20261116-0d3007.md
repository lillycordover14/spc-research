India: New fungal disease found in Tulip Garden for first time&nbsp;&nbsp;FloralDaily

Scientists have identified a fungus responsible for leaf rot in tulips at Srinagar's Indira Gandhi Memorial Tulip Garden for the first time, bringing to light a previously unreported plant disease in Kashmir's tulip cultivation. The researchers have also found that a naturally occurring fungus can suppress the disease-causing organism by nearly 88 per cent under laboratory conditions.
 
The findings, published in the scientific journal Indian Phytopathology, accessed by Rising Kashmir, identify Irpex lacteus, a fungus generally associated with the decay of wood, as the cause of leaf rot in tulip plants collected from the garden. The discovery is significant for Kashmir's floriculture sector, particularly the Tulip Garden, which attracts large numbers of visitors during the spring flowering season. The study provides new information about a disease affecting tulip leaves and identifies a possible biological method of controlling the pathogen.
 
The research, titled First Report of Irpex lacteus Causing Leaf Rot of Tulip in Kashmir Himalaya, India, and In-Vitro Antagonistic Activity of Trichoderma spp., was conducted by Tabia Mushtaq, Shayiestah Maqbool, Abdul Hamid Wani, Zahara Sultan, Mohd Yaqub Bhat and Wasim Sajad Malik.
 
According to the study, affected tulip leaves developed irregular brown to dark-brown patches that gradually increased in size. As the disease progressed, the patches merged, causing portions of the leaves to die and collapse. The researchers collected affected leaf tissue and carried out laboratory examinations to identify the organism responsible for the damage.

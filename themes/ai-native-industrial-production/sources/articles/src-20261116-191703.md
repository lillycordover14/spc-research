Aging Untold
) — A Kansas man who doctors said would never walk again was determined to show he could. Eleven years later, he finished his first marathon with support from his family. 
James McGinnis, 29, completed the race in 8 hours, 10 minutes, with his father and best friend beside him. Resistance bands kept him upright during the course. 
Injury during televised football game 
Eleven years ago, McGinnis, then a senior at Olathe East, collapsed on the field after a hit during a televised high school football game against Olathe South. 
His father, Patrick McGinnis, said he knew the injury was serious. 
“When they turn him over on his back, though, I knew then it was serious,” Patrick McGinnis said. 
James McGinnis suffered a traumatic brain bleed. Doctors gave him less than a 7% chance to survive. He spent weeks in the ICU. 
Patrick McGinnis said he began researching brain injury recovery and the next steps. 
On day five, James McGinnis signed “I love you,” which gave his parents a glimpse of hope. 
Doctors called it a reflex, Patrick McGinnis said 
“And I was like, no, I don’t think so. I think it’s purposeful,” he said. 
From walking to running
 
Patrick McGinnis said doctors initially told the family that his son’s recovery would not be a sprint — “it’s a marathon” — and that James would never walk again. 
It took James McGinnis nine months to walk on his own and nine years to run. 
He started with a mile, then ran a 5K and a 10K. He completed a half marathon in 2024, then set his sights on a marathon. 
Patrick McGinnis now runs three times a week with his son. 
“I do it because every time we go out, James gets better,” he said. 
Quotes at every mile
 
Along the marathon course, Patrick McGinnis recorded what the family calls “James-isms,” which are his son’s core beliefs. He recorded one at every mile. 
The James-ism at mile 10 was: “Tell me I can’t, and I will show you I can.” 
After the race, James McGinnis said doctors told him and his family the journey would be a marathon. 
“Well, I did my marathon,” he said. 
Rehabilitation doctor responds
 
Dr. Brad Steinle, James McGinnis’ rehabilitation doctor, said McGinnis’ prognosis was grim and his recovery has been remarkable. Steinle said McGinnis’ strength has made him a better physician. 
“I have hundreds of patients that, while I think that we help them, they help us,” Steinle said. “I haven’t had anybody quite like James.” 
Next goals
 
James McGinnis’ next goals are running the Boston Marathon and possibly competing in the 2028 Paralympics

		AMCA bid winner to be declared in a month: Defence Secretary

The winners of a bid for the indigenously designed fifth generation Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) programme will be declared in a month, Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said on Monday.
The three shortlisted private sector-led contenders vying for the ₹15,000-crore project to develop prototypes of the indigenous fifth-generation fighter jet submitted their individual bids on last date of the extended submission deadline of October 30 to a high-level committee constituted by the Ministry of Defence.
The three contenders are L&T-led consortium of BEL and Dynamatic, Tata Advanced Systems Ltd (TASL), and Bharat Forge-led consortium of BEML Ltd, and Data Patterns.
Faster Development
The bids will be opened to select one of the consortium as a development-cum-production partner (DCPP) for five AMCA prototypes and a structural test specimen designed and developed by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA), a DRDO laboratory.
Speaking at an ANI event, Singh said that the government is hoping for a faster development of the AMCA prototypes following the announcement. He, however, ruled out any chance as of now over government considering proposals from Russia for Su-57 and the US’s F-35 fifth generation stealth aircraft.
“Whether we are going for a fifth generation programme other than AMCA, I don’t have clarity as yet. We have not taken a view on this. As of now, our fifth generation programme is AMCA. I am happy to say that the bids have been received from three shortlisted bidders, including our fellow panellist (Arun T Ramchandani, Head of L&T Defence) in consortium with a PSU,” he said.
“In a month time, we will be ready to announce the winner of the race and after we hope it will lead to faster development of the AMCA prototypes for induction. Meanwhile, we will have to manage with the equipment we have and the programme we have... the large contracts for the Tejas Mk-1, which are now seeing some visibility in terms of engine deliveries,” he stated.
Extended Deadline
The winning bidder must have a manufacturing ecosystem near Puttaparthi in Andhra Pradesh, where the AMCA core integration and flight-testing complex will be located.
The deadline was earlier extended from August 27 to October 30 after bidders sought more time to complete formalities for the complex fifth-generation fighter prototyping programme, which they are undertaking for the first time.
Earlier in the day, Chief of the Air Staff, Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit, said that the Indian Air Force will have a squadron of indigenous Light Combat Aircraft (LCA) Mk 1A joining its fleet within the current financial year, adding that work on the next-generation LCA Mk 2 and AMCA is also progressing.
Dixit also stated  that the LCA Mk 2 was expected to roll out next year.
“And then we will continue developing, and we will take it further, not only LCA Mark 1A. Now LCA Mark 2 will roll out maybe next year, and we are working very hard for AMCA also,” he said.
But, on a question raising concerns that by the time LCA Mk 2 may get inducted into the IAF, the technology will get obsolete, the Defence Secretary stated that we will think over it when the time comes.

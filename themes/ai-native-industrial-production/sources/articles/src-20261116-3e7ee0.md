Financing led by J.P. Morgan and Apollo will support three new factories as the Devens-based company scales production for multibillion-dollar commercial programs

BOSTON — VulcanForms, an advanced manufacturing company founded out of MIT, has secured $500 million in financing led by J.P. Morgan and funds managed by Apollo to expand its U.S. manufacturing capacity, including the construction of three new factories.

Announced November 12, the financing will support the company’s expansion as it responds to growing demand across medical devices, consumer products, aerospace, defense, space and industrial markets. VulcanForms currently operates manufacturing facilities in Devens and Newburyport, Massachusetts.

The company said it has secured multibillion-dollar commercial programs across these industries, creating demand for its integrated manufacturing platform. The three new factories will add to its existing production network and support larger-scale manufacturing of complex metal components.

VulcanForms combines metal 3D printing, precision machining, automation and physical artificial intelligence in a single production platform. The company aims to make the domestic production of high-performance components more competitive and reduce reliance on fragmented overseas supply chains.

“America cannot lead the next industrial revolution if we can’t manufacture what we invent,” said CEO Kevin Kassekert. “For too long, breakthrough technologies developed here have been manufactured overseas. VulcanForms is proving we can change that by turning American innovation into industrial-scale production and demonstrating that the U.S. can not only compete, but also win on the global stage. We’re proud to work with J.P. Morgan and Apollo on helping to build America’s industrial future at home.”

John Zito, co-president of Apollo Asset Management, said the company has developed a distinctive manufacturing approach to serve a broad range of industries and meet rising customer demand. He described the financing as a tailored solution to support capacity expansion and said private capital can help advance the return to large-scale manufacturing in the United States.

The transaction is also part of JPMorganChase’s Security and Resiliency Initiative, which supports businesses and supply chains considered important to U.S. economic security.

“Through our Security and Resiliency Initiative, JPMorganChase is working with companies like VulcanForms to strengthen the industries and supply chains that underpin economic security. These efforts can help to bridge the gap between promising technology and industrial-scale production in the United States, as demand grows across critical industries,” said Jay Horine, global head of the initiative.

The additional capital will also support further integration of physical AI into VulcanForms’ manufacturing platform, with the aim of improving automation and production efficiency.

Founded in 2015 out of MIT, VulcanForms says it has raised more than $1 billion in funding to date. Its investors and lenders include Eclipse, J.P. Morgan, Apollo, Hudson Bay Capital, D1 Capital and 1789 Capital.

The company develops and operates advanced manufacturing infrastructure for engineered metal parts used by companies across medical, consumer, mission systems and industrial markets. It says its approach is designed to make domestic production of complex components commercially viable while supporting skilled manufacturing jobs in the United States.

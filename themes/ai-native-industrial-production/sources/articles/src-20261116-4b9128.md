Key Points
Situational Awareness' latest 13F filing shows a renewed public-markets book concentrated in AI-linked stocks and options even after the fund’s near collapse in July.
The filing does not show whether the fund kept or sold those positions during the October sell-off.
AI stocks have suffered in recent weeks amid worries about financing for the buildout as well as a potential slowdown in its development following the midterm election results.
Leopold Aschenbrenner
Photo: Josh Edelson
Leopold Aschenbrenner's artificial intelligence-themed hedge fund Situational Awareness, which nearly collapsed in July, loaded back up on AI stocks as it returned to the financial markets at the end of the third quarter. But all of its holdings are down big the last month and a half. 
According to a new regulatory filing, as the fund got back on its feet Aschenbrenner took an equity position in Australian neocloud company 
SharonAI
 valued at more than $440 million, along with equity stakes in AI-centric plays 
Cleanspark
, 
IREN
, 
Bitdeer Technologies
 and 
Fermi
. The Cleanspark stake is worth almost $39 million, while Iren and Bitdeer stakes are more than $21 million and more than $20 million, respectively. Fermi, meanwhile, has a value of more than $11 million.
The fund also owned call options at the end of September in neocloud 
Nebius
, memory chip name 
Sandisk
, the 
Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM)
 and data center power play 
Bloom Energy
. Sandisk and Nebius were key holdings that almost took out his fund over the summer.
All of these Situational Awareness holdings have been routed since Sept. 30. SharonAI is at the top of that list, seeing a 58% loss in October. It's also fallen 11% so far this month. IREN was down 41% in October, while Nebius declined 35%. Last week, Nebius plummeted more than 16% in a single day after the company issued a 
weak full-year forecast
.
It's not known whether the hedge fund manager got out of those positions during the October AI selloff. The 13F filing is just a snapshot of what the hedge fund held at the end of the quarter.
Aschenbrenner — who launched the fund in 2024 after rising to fame that same year with his AI manifesto also entitled Situational Awareness — 
re-entered financial markets
 a bit in September, sources told CNBC at the time.
The Monday filing shows he picked up right where he left off. 
The near-collapse of his hedge fund over the summer forced him to sell all of its public equity positions to Ken Griffin's Citadel. The fund had $45 billion in assets at its peak, but that tumbled to 
around $10 billion
, consisting mostly of private stakes.
Stocked tethered to the AI trade took a hit in the most recent quarter as investors grew concerned about a 
slowdown in development of the technology
. Those fears were spurred by 
comments from industry leaders
 such as Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman back in September, with the latter saying that the company going public this year would be "ill-advised."
Since then, pressure on the trade has only continued.
In recent weeks, investors have worried about how Democratic wins in the midterm elections could 
hinder data center development
, whether some technology companies are 
using too much debt
 to finance the AI buildout and what 
role Nvidia will play in that financing
 with Jensen Huang now 
out as CEO at the company
.
Losses in 
Nvidia
 as well as other AI-related stocks 
dragged down the three leading U.S. indexes
 during Monday's session as investors continued to assess the fallout of Huang's departure.

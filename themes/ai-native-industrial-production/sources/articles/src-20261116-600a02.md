Anduril Industries says it will develop a containerized launcher for the U.S. Air Force’s Counter-Air Missile Program

By Lukasz Prus (Defence Industry Europe)

                    Air and missile defence                

|

                November 16, 2026            

Photo: Anduril Industries.

The U.S. Air Force has awarded Anduril Industries a prototype contract for a containerized launcher that could fire counter-air missiles from several manufacturers, the company said. The Counter-Air Missile Program, known as CAMP, aims to expand the Air Force’s stock of lower-cost missiles that industry can produce in large numbers for sustained air combat.

Anduril will spend the first 10 months working with the Air Force on the preliminary design and preparing the launcher for tests with missiles from other companies. It will adapt the launcher for the program’s Ground Launched Counter Air effort, which is developing lower-cost, long-range missiles to engage enemy aircraft from the ground.

The launcher would provide shared power, computing, cooling, communications and software for missiles from different suppliers. Missile makers could use that infrastructure to integrate their weapons without having to build a separate launch system for each one.

Anduril is designing the launcher to operate for extended periods at dispersed sites with few personnel. The design draws on the company’s Menace-I computing system, which houses power, cooling, computing and communications equipment in a rugged shelter that can be moved by military aircraft or ground equipment.

The Air Force program draws on mature technology to shorten development and simplify manufacturing. Anduril is factoring component sourcing, assembly, testing and tooling into the design.

A successful prototype could lead to a production award. Anduril says planning for manufacturing now would help cut costs and speed up deliveries if the Air Force selects its launcher.

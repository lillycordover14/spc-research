LanzaTech's Cost Reset Holds And EU Certification Lands, But Q3 Stalled Sequentially And The Full-Year Plan Hinges On One Q4 Transfer


            By
                    
Earnings Whispers

            | November 16, 2026, 10:41 AM
        

LNZA


                +1.75%
            


        LanzaTech Global Inc
    

Earnings Whispers
LanzaTech's third quarter showed a company that has fixed its cost structure but not yet its revenue engine. The company reported a loss of $0.89 per share on revenue of $9.51 million. Earnings fell 189.9% year over year, a swing from the prior-year quarter's profit to a loss. Revenue grew just 2.5%. No consensus estimate was available, so there is no surprise to measure. The more useful comparison is with LanzaTech's own trajectory. Revenue growth slowed from the 10% pace the company has posted year to date, and the quarter weakened against Q2 on several key measures. Management's long-promised European fuel certification arrived and full-year guidance was reiterated. That is the central tension: the strategic story improved while the quarterly economics stalled, and the full-year plan now rests on a single back-end-loaded event.
The GAAP numbers need context. Net loss was $178.0 million, or $13.60 per share, compared with net income of $2.9 million a year ago. That loss was driven overwhelmingly by a $166.4 million non-cash fair value loss on the company's SGLT stake. The same holding produced a $41.7 million gain over the first nine months, so it is a volatile, non-operating swing rather than an operating deterioration. The prior-year comparison also benefited from $18.4 million of other income that did not recur.
Stripped of those items, the cost discipline is real. Operating expenses fell 35% to $11.7 million and were held flat for a second straight quarter. The adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to $8.8 million from $13.5 million. Year-to-date operating cash use dropped to $22.9 million from $58.7 million.
The quality of the revenue deserves more scrutiny. CarbonSmart product sales grew about 70% to $5.1 million, up from $3.8 million in Q2. However, the cost of those sales was $5.09 million, so the fastest-growing line produced almost no gross profit. Meanwhile, higher-margin engineering and LanzaJet licensing revenue declined, pulling biorefining revenue down to $2.9 million from $5.1 million. The result:
Gross profit slipped to roughly $1.6 million, about a 17% margin.
That compares with about 20% in Q2 and $2.4 million of gross profit a year earlier.
The adjusted EBITDA loss widened sequentially from $7.6 million in Q2.
Volume is growing, but the mix is diluting margins.
The strategic news is where the call delivered. The Hebei facility running LanzaTech technology secured the world's first ISCC EU Recycled Carbon Fuel certification for ethanol made from steel mill waste gases. The release says the certificate was published September 15, while the call material cites October 21. Either way, the milestone that was still pending at the August call has been reached. A second plant is expected to be certified in Q4. Management framed the two plants as about 40,000 tons per year of certified ethanol from 2027, worth roughly $45 million of gross annual revenue. It is targeting more than €15 million of LanzaTech product sales in 2027, with offtake talks underway with European and UK energy and chemical companies. Germany's inclusion of recycled carbon fuels in its compliance framework from 2027 adds potential credit value on top of the physical ethanol sale. For now, these are management expectations, not booked revenue.
The guidance is where bears have legitimate ammunition. LanzaTech reiterated full-year targets of revenue of $50-55 million and an adjusted EBITDA loss of $22-26 million. The fourth-quarter outlook calls for revenue of $20-25 million, more than double the current run rate, and adjusted EBITDA of negative $2 million to positive $2 million. Management explicitly tied that outlook to completing the first Brookfield project transfer before year-end. The call analysis noted that this echoes 2024, when slipped project events produced a large Q3 shortfall and a back-end-loaded Q4.
Liquidity adds pressure. Cash fell to $36.3 million from $48.9 million in a single quarter, despite the $50 million equity raise earlier in the year. Share count rose to about 13.1 million from 2.3 million at year-end, a measure of how heavily shareholders have been diluted. LanzaJet also required a recapitalization, and Freedom Pines has not reached sustained continuous operation. CEO Jennifer Holmgren's added role as LanzaJet CEO raises execution and bandwidth questions, even as LanzaTech's ownership stake increases.
Investors entered this report deeply skeptical. Earnings Whispers sentiment stood at -0.52 after the August report, and no updated reading was available. Shares opened at $6.38 after that report, well below the current 200-day moving average of $10.26. That gap suggests the market has spent months discounting the turnaround narrative.
The bottom line is that LanzaTech has proven it can run lean and deliver regulatory milestones, but it has not yet shown it can convert either into profitable revenue. CarbonSmart growth is coming at near-zero margin, sequential trends softened, and the cash cushion is thin. The reiterated outlook depends on one Brookfield transfer closing on time. If it does, and the EU certifications turn into signed offtake agreements, the skeptics will have to reassess. If the transfer slips, the 2024 pattern will look uncomfortably familiar.

Who will develop India's AMCA fighter jet engine? Govt weighs public and private sector proposals

India is pursuing a collaborative, 'whole-of-industry' model to develop an indigenous aero-engine for the 5th-generation Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft

Highlights

India is evaluating both public and private sector proposals to develop an indigenous aero-engine for the fifth generation Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft

A public sector proposal led by the Gas Turbine Research Establishment in partnership with Safran is currently under consideration

There is also a private sector proposal for the engine

India is considering both public and private sector proposals for developing an indigenous aero-engine for the fifth generation Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA), and the government favours a collaborative approach involving the defence industry, research laboratories and the Indian Air Force.

Speaking at the ANI National Security Summit 3.0 in Delhi, Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said a public sector proposal led by the Gas Turbine Research Establishment (GTRE) in collaboration with French aerospace company Safran was already under consideration.

However, the private sector has now entered the race, giving the government another option for the ambitious engine development programme.

"As you mentioned on the engine programme, there is a public sector proposal led through GTRE with Safran, but now there is also a private sector which has entered into this race. As of now, we are looking at both those options," Singh said.

The defence secretary revealed that a major workshop would be organised in Bengaluru next month, bringing together stakeholders to explore how the aero-engine development programme could become a national effort.

The proposed initiative would involve DRDO laboratories, private industry and the Air Force working together to develop an indigenous aero-engine.

Singh said the objective was to ensure that different organisations complemented each other's efforts rather than duplicating work.

"We would prefer that they work together. We would prefer a whole-of-industry approach," he said.

On the AMCA fighter aircraft development programme, Singh said bids had been received from three shortlisted bidders, including a consortium involving a public sector undertaking.

"I hope that in about a month's time, we'll be ready to announce a winner of that race," he said.

The government hopes the selection will accelerate the development of AMCA prototypes and their eventual induction.

Tejas Mk1A to Join IAF this financial year

Meanwhile, Chief of the Air Staff Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit said the Tejas Mk1A fighter aircraft would join the Indian Air Force within the current financial year.

He said developing, testing and producing an indigenous fighter aircraft was a major challenge that only a handful of countries had successfully undertaken.

The Air Chief said four areas requiring improvement had been identified in the original LCA programme and work had been undertaken jointly with Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL).

"Those four areas are almost complete," he said.

"Very shortly, we will have LCA Mark 1A joining Indian Air Force within this financial year. We will have a squadron," Dixit said.

He added that the Tejas Mk2 could roll out next year, while work on the AMCA programme was also progressing.

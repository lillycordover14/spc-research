ARLINGTON, Texas — Deep in the heart of Texas, 
Dallas Cowboys
 quarterback 
Dak Prescott
 is ripping it as well as ever.

In fact, in his 11th season, the 2023 All-Pro continues to demonstrate he’s among the elite passers of his generation. Prescott’s biggest problem is that he too often must overcome, well, 
the Cowboys
 — from the owner’s suite to the field.

Yet again Sunday evening, they conspired against him in an embarrassing 46-40 loss to the visiting San Francisco 49ers at AT&T Stadium that dropped the Cowboys to 4-6 entering Week 11.

Don’t be fooled by the one-score final margin. Early in the fourth quarter, the 49ers held a 38-19 lead. With just 43 seconds remaining on the game clock, Prescott and wide receiver CeeDee Lamb teamed on a 1-yard touchdown pass. After Prescott’s 2-point conversion run, San Francisco recovered an onside kick.

The 49ers were unable to run out the clock, however. They punted, and Dallas got the ball back at its 9-yard line with nine seconds left. Following a series of laterals on the game’s final play, San Francisco recovered the ball with the clock at zero.

For any NFL team with a competent defense, Prescott played well enough to secure a victory, passing for 343 yards with five touchdown passes and one interception, being sacked just once. Clearly, though, the Cowboys don’t qualify. Not even close.

The NFC West-leading 49ers improved to 8-1 while amassing 584 total yards — the most the Cowboys have ever allowed in a home game. Dallas has been in the NFL since 1960.

San Francisco averaged an eye-opening 9.4 yards per play, delighting its fans who were out in force among a crowd of 90,763.

The 49ers scored five — five! — touchdowns on explosive plays, including three of more than 60 yards. Brock Purdy became the latest opposing passer to torch the Cowboys. He finished with 389 yards and three touchdown passes without an interception, producing a phenomenal 154.5 passer rating. The maximum is 158.3.

San Francisco star running back Christian McCaffrey had four receptions for 161 yards. He and Purdy connected on 74- and 66-yard catch-and-run touchdowns. On a reverse, wide receiver Deebo Samuel scored from 70 yards out.

Plenty of other numbers illustrate the Cowboys’ defensive ineptitude Sunday and throughout the season, but head coach Brian Schottenheimer summed it up best.

“The second half [defense] was unacceptable. Tackling was atrocious,” said Schottenheimer, whose team trailed 17-16 at halftime. “You can’t give up three explosive plays of over 65 yards for touchdowns and expect to win in this league. … Totally unacceptable.”

Nonetheless, the NFC East is so weak that Dallas is in second place. Still, with the team’s defense breaking the wrong records, Prescott, wisely, isn’t looking too far down the road.

“They do have to get us a stop. They know that,” he said. “This is a team game. The last thing I’m going to do is just completely point my fingers at the defense. However, they’ve got to be accountable, and they are accountable. They know they’ve got to be better.”

The Dallas Cowboys had no answer for Christian McCaffrey and the San Francisco 49ers, who amassed 584 total yards – the most the Cowboys have ever allowed in a home game.

Pick any all-time great quarterback. Tom Brady, Joe Montana, Patrick Mahomes. None of them could overcome both the Cowboys’ defense and organizational culture to reach a Super Bowl, let alone win one.

Granted, Prescott isn’t without blame for the glamour franchise’s lengthy Super Bowl drought. What was clear again Sunday, though, is that Prescott passes the eye test as a quarterback who, when playing at his best, is capable of winning a championship with the right supporting cast.

Unfortunately, that’s not a description that fits the Cowboys.

This season has unfolded much like the last, and that isn’t a good thing. Despite making major defensive changes since last season, the Cowboys have demonstrated little beyond their ability to remain consistently awful on defense.

Entering Sunday, the Cowboys’ defense ranked tied for 25th in points per game (26.2) and 30th in yards per game (372.7). The NFL has 32 teams.

Compared to last season, Dallas has actually 
improved
: In 2025, Dallas ranked last in points (30.1) and 30th in yards (377). Those aren’t the statistics of a Super Bowl contender.

Former Cowboys running back Calvin Hill understands that reality.

A member of the Cowboys’ Super Bowl VI championship team in 1972, Hill fondly recalls how its defense overwhelmed the Miami Dolphins in a 24-3 victory to secure the franchise’s first title. A former consultant in the Cowboys’ player development program, Hill is quick to emphasize the importance of defense in each of Dallas’ five Super Bowl championships.

“During the season, you need to play good defense to win consistently. And in the playoffs, you need a great defense to go far,” Hill, the father of Pro Basketball Hall of Famer Grant Hill, said in a phone interview late last week. “From when I played until now, that hasn’t changed. You know the old saying? Offense sells tickets and defense wins championships.

“It’s just a fact they [the Cowboys] haven’t been at the top in defense recently. When I played, we had [Pro Football Hall of Famers] Bob Lilly, Mel Renfro, Herb Adderley, and so many other great defensive players. It was the same thing when Troy [Aikman], Emmitt [Smith] and Michael Irvin were winning [in the 1990s]. There were strong defenses on those teams, too. That’s what you just have to have.”

It starts with roster construction.

In that regard — and in every other one, for that matter — the Cowboys aren’t exactly a democracy: Jerry Jones is the owner, president and general manager.

Dak Prescott (right) played well enough to lead the Dallas Cowboys to a victory on Nov. 15, passing for 343 yards with five touchdown passes and one interception.

Dallas last played in the Super Bowl more than 30 years ago. When assessing the performance of the franchise’s primary roster architect, that’s all you need to know.

What’s more, Jones’ influence extends beyond the draft room and into the coaching staff. That hasn’t helped, either.

Schottenheimer is the Cowboys’ third head coach since Prescott entered the league in 2016. Along the way, Prescott has adapted to different offensive philosophies and playcallers. For a quarterback’s development, continuity matters.

During his coaching days, Joe Gibbs understood that.

A three-time Super Bowl winner while leading the then-Washington Redskins, Gibbs emphasized continuity during his Hall of Fame coaching career. He admires what the 49ers have built under longtime head coach Kyle Shanahan, and also believes Purdy has benefited immensely from playing in the same offensive system throughout his NFL career.

“It definitely helps any quarterback [to be] in a great system, because the quarterback is dependent on a lot of things [to win],” Gibbs said in an interview last week. “He’s got to have a great scheme. And that takes, of course, a real knowledgeable coaching staff.

“In today’s football, we see a lot of quarterbacks [who] are under pressure a lot. Some coaches are very conscious of protecting the quarterback and having a great scheme. The 49ers have that. Their quarterback benefits from that.”

In Purdy’s second season, he helped the 49ers reach the Super Bowl. Shanahan, currently in his 10th season as the 49ers’ head coach, has led them to four NFC Championship Games appearances and two Super Bowl appearances.

During Prescott’s career, the Cowboys have never advanced past the divisional round. The four-time Pro Bowler’s playoff record is 2-5. And make no mistake, there have been times in the postseason when Prescott needed to perform better.

But how might he have benefited from playing under the same highly successful head coach throughout his career, while being supported by a consistently strong defense? Obviously, we’ll never know.

Prescott’s next start will be his 150th in the regular season, potentially putting him less than a year away from catching Aikman’s franchise record for quarterbacks. He already owns franchise records for passing yards and touchdowns, and his 66.7 completion percentage clears every other Dallas quarterback who has started at least one full season.

Yet despite their quarterback’s best efforts, the Cowboys don’t seem destined for a return to the promised land. 

“Quarterbacks are judged on whether they won the big game, the Super Bowl. But you can’t get to the Super Bowl without having all the pieces around you,” Hill said. “Before [John] Elway won Super Bowls, he was a great quarterback. But when he won Super Bowls, he was looked at differently. What changed? Getting all the pieces he needed to win.

“I see what Dak does [on the field]. I see what he does in terms of his work habits, his leadership and everything you need out of the position. He has proven what type of player, what type of quarterback, he is. He hasn’t had the kind of defense you need to get further in the playoffs. Unless that changes, unfortunately, [the perception] won’t change. But Dak has proven who he is.”

And what infuriates their long-suffering fans is that the Cowboys continue to show exactly who they are.

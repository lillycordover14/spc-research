Video Transcript

Industrial robots can grasp and move heavy objects, but manipulating them with humanlike dexterity remains challenging. 
Boston Dynamics
 has introduced a new robotic hand for its Atlas humanoid, featuring 13 degrees of freedom.

The 
four-finger design
 increases dexterity over the previous seven-degree-of-freedom version while retaining comparable strength. Engineers deliberately omitted the pinky, avoiding three additional actuators and reducing cost, bulk, and potential failure points. The remaining fingers enable precise pinching, object reorientation within the hand, and tool-trigger grasps for equipment, including drills and torque drivers.

Directly actuated joints support responsive movement, while pressure sensors detect subtle contact at the fingertips and palm. A backdrivable transmission also allows joints to yield under external impacts, helping prevent mechanical damage.

Beyond hardware, the design supports high-fidelity simulation for reinforcement learning. 
Boston Dynamics
 reports promising early results in transferring simulation-trained control policies to physical hardware.

Rather than replicate every feature of the human hand, the company prioritizes dexterity, reliability, and manufacturability. This approach could extend robotic automation beyond specialized grippers to industrial tasks requiring flexible object handling and tool use, particularly when components arrive in varying positions and orientations.

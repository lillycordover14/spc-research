William Blair Pushes USA Rare Earth’s Timeline Further Out

William Blair cut USA Rare Earth to Market Perform from Outperform on November 16, citing feedstock issues. The firm projects near-term challenges and pushed its sales and margin estimates out to 2028. That single downgrade brought down 
rare-earth stocks across the group
 ahead of a Trump-Xi meeting.

Feedstock is the raw rare earth material a magnet maker needs to process, and a reliable supply of it sets the pace for everything downstream, so when that supply runs late, revenue and margins get pushed out with it. USA Rare Earth’s plan leans on the Pela Ema mine at Serra Verde in Brazil, a deal that closed on September 3
[
1
]
 and contributed virtually nothing in Q3.

Management guided for Serra Verde sales of at least 325 tonnes
[
1
]
 of total rare earth oxide (TREO) in Q4 2026, with Stage 1 scaling toward a 4,000-tonne annual run rate by Q2 2027. A second finishing line and Line 1b at Stillwater, Oklahoma are slated to lift USA Rare Earth’s magnet capacity to 1,200 tonnes a year.

Critical Metals and MP Materials Slide With USA Rare Earth

Critical Metals
 (
NASDAQ:CRML
) stock is at $4.24, down 13%. That puts Critical Metals down 39% YTD, a larger slide than USA Rare Earth on both measures. As a pre-revenue rare earth developer, the company is moving with the sector the downgrade knocked lower.

MP Materials
 (
NYSE:MP
) stock is at $36.65, down 5%. Shares are off 27% YTD, a smaller drawdown than either developer’s. The softer decline fits MP Materials’ standing as the group’s established producer, and it shows the market is repricing each name differently.

United States Antimony
 (
NYSE:UAMY
) is also part of the critical-minerals group. Speculative growth names are selling off across the market, with the NASDAQ 100 down 2% and the S&P 500 down 1%, which adds weight to the pressure on the critical-minerals group.

How MP Materials Differs From Earlier-Stage Rare Earth Names

MP Materials already mines, separates and refines rare earths at its Mountain Pass operation in California and makes magnets at its Independence facility in Fort Worth, Texas. The company completed its magnet qualification with 
General Motors
 (
NYSE:GM
 | 
GM Price Prediction
) and has a price protection agreement with the U.S. Department of War.

USA Rare Earth is putting together a similar mine-to-magnet chain across Brazil, the U.K. and the U.S., largely through acquisitions, and much of it is still scaling. Critical Metals sits further back as a pre-revenue developer of the Tanbreez rare earth project in Greenland and the Wolfsberg lithium project in Austria. That company has disclosed substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern.

The critical-minerals trade was built on a long-term national-security story about non-China supply. William Blair’s call on USA Rare Earth is a reminder that timelines matter for companies that aren’t producing much yet.

Can USA Rare Earth Prove Its Feedstock Timeline?

Next up for USA Rare Earth is whether Serra Verde hits its Q4 sales guidance and Stillwater delivers finished magnet prototype sales to multiple customers by the end of 2026

1
]
. The bulls can point to the company’s $1.09 billion
[

 in cash and U.S. government backing that includes a $750 million Department of War investment in the offtake special purpose vehicle.

USA Rare Earth’s own disclosures arm the bears, including a $143.69 million
[
1

 operating cash outflow in Q3, substantial debt, possible optimization delays at Pela Ema and dilution risk. The open question this downgrade raises is whether investors now demand proof of execution from early-stage critical-minerals names. The feedstock ramp at Serra Verde could be a key data point for the group from here.

Church clock chimes again after outcry over single noise complaint
5 hours ago

Ethan Saunders
West Midlands
BBC
The church's chimes had been ringing for 137 years before they were silenced last weekend
A church clock is chiming once more after thousands of people signed a petition against its silencing due to a single noise complaint.
The clock at St Margaret's in Wolstanton, Staffordshire, had chimed since 1889 without issue, apart from during the two world wars and periods of maintenance, the church said.
In a post on social media, the church said the chimes were silenced from 18:00 GMT on Thursday after an officer from Newcastle-under-Lyme Borough Council instructed it to do so while a complaint was investigated.
In a statement, the authority has confirmed the chimes had been restored earlier on Monday but it was still looking at a longer term solution.
Jessica Nixon started the petition after finding out the chimes had been silenced
While the quarter-hourly chimes were silenced, the bells still rang for church services over the weekend.
Jessica Nixon, from Newcastle-under-Lyme, launched a petition which had attracted 2,630 signatures by Monday morning.
"They have been going for 137 years and I just thought, why has one person been able to make a complaint and very quickly they have been stopped?" she said.
"The community of Wolstanton should have been made aware."
Nixon said she initially started the petition out of frustration and had not expected it to attract so much support.
Although not a regular churchgoer, she attended a service after learning about the issue.
"A lot of the people there I was speaking to were there because of the situation with the chimes," she said.
"If you can get people there for that reason then it's worked."
She added: "Why should one person make that decision? If you don't like the sound of them, don't live by them."
Pat Taylor, whose husband is buried in the church's graveyard, said the decision to silence the bells should not have been based on one complaint
Pat Taylor, whose husband is buried in the church graveyard, said she was saddened when the chimes were stopped.
"When I go to the graveyard and the chimes go, I think that's my husband talking to me and letting me know I've been," she said.
She said she was shocked the chimes had been silenced following a single complaint.
"They shouldn't have stopped it for a single complaint. They have been going for years," she added.
Council leader Jonathan Gullis posted on Facebook to say the chimes were back
Jonathan Gullis, leader of the council, said officers had followed the correct procedures when responding to the complaint.
However, he said he was exploring possible solutions which could allow the chimes to return.
"I don't want to promise the mechanical solution can work yet because we haven't even found out what technology is needed, how easy it is to install, and what the cost of that comes to," he said.
"But if I can do it, and if I can afford it, 100% we'll do that."
A spokesman for the council confirmed the chimes had been restored.
He said there was a duty to investigate because a statutory noise complaint had been made, but no formal notice was issued. 
"We recognise that the clock chimes are an intrinsic part of the life of the church and the village, and we have arranged for them to continue as usual," he said.
He went on to say the council would continue to work with residents and the church  on a longer term solution if legally required.

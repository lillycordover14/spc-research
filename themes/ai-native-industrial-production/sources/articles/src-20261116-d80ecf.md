Saronic Technologies
, Austin, Texas, has received a prototype contract from the Defense Innovation Unit to develop and deliver an Autonomous Low Profile Vessel for U.S. Marine Corps logistics missions in contested waters.
The Other Transaction agreement covers Saronic’s ALPV, a long-range unmanned vessel designed to move supplies ship-to-ship and ship-to-shore without putting a crew aboard.
Saronic did not disclose the contract value, number of vessels, or delivery schedule.
The company said the design will combine autonomous navigation and passive sensing with a low-profile hull built for maneuverability, beach landings, and serial production. A modular open-systems architecture is intended to support additional equipment and mission systems as requirements evolve.
“This award recognizes that winning in contested waters starts with keeping forces supplied,” said Rob Lehman, co-founder and chief commercial officer at Saronic. “Saronic’s ALPV gives Marines a low-signature, autonomous way to move critical cargo across contested seas, without putting a crew in harm’s way.”
The Marine Corps has been experimenting with ALPV concepts as part of its effort to sustain distributed forces operating across island and coastal environments. Its 2025 Force Design Update identified the capability as moving toward a program of record for autonomous, long-range logistics distribution.
Marines have previously tested ALPV systems in California and Okinawa, Japan. In January, logistics specialists at Camp Pendleton evaluated an ALPV for moving supplies and equipment through littoral environments, while Marine units also conducted rehearsals at Naha Military Port in Okinawa in 2025. 
Those earlier evaluations were part of the broader Marine Corps ALPV effort and were not identified as tests of Saronic’s newly awarded vessel.
Under the new contract, Saronic will work with DIU and the Marine Corps on testing and planning for how the system could eventually be operated, staffed, supported, and integrated into service.
Saronic said the autonomy technology feeding into the ALPV has accumulated more than 350,000 nautical miles and 55,000 operating hours across its vessel programs.
The award adds another military vessel program to Saronic’s portfolio. The company also holds production contracts for its 24' 
Corsair
 autonomous surface vessel and 180' 
Marauder
 autonomous ship. The Navy has also selected Saronic to construct Landing Craft Utility vessels at Port Alpha, the company’s Brownsville, Texas, shipyard.

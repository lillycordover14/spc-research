A Simulation Chemists Can Run Themselves
“Until now, simulations were performed exclusively by computational chemists,” said 
Kazuya Nagaoka
, an Associate Director in Astellas Pharma’s DigitalX research and development team. Medicinal chemists design compounds and decide what to make next. Astellas built a browser app so they could run routine predictions as they worked through those decisions.
Boltz-2
, developed by researchers at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Recursion, predicts how a protein and a compound fit together in three dimensions and estimates how strongly they bind. Astellas runs NVIDIA’s packaged version, Boltz-2 NIM, on Tokyo-1, a shared computing platform for drug discovery in Japan. A separate application developed by Astellas lets chemists draw compounds and queue predictions for several protein-compound combinations.

Keigo Ide
, Digital Biological Modality Discovery Team Lead at Astellas, said “results were obtained in just 7 seconds” for one workload using the version optimized with NVIDIA TensorRT. The open-source Python implementation took more than 20 seconds on that workload. For a chemist weighing several compounds, the app also removes the handoff for a routine run. 
How Far the Workflow Can Travel
By the end of February 2026, the browser app had reached more than 2,000 users, with more than 50 using it regularly, according to NVIDIA. Astellas has also put Boltz-2 into a protein and peptide design workflow. RFdiffusion proposes structures and ProteinMPNN proposes sequences to fit them, then Boltz-2 predicts how the designs might bind. 
The membrane-protein project gives Astellas a result to compare against future screens. Across more targets, the measure to watch is how many assays it takes to find a hit worth pursuing. For 
Yosuke Matsumura
, a Senior Researcher at Astellas, complex simulations still go to specialists. Running the routine ones himself means he can “apply the results to our next ideas.”

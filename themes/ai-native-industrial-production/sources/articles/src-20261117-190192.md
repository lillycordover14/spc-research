OECD Household Income Per Capita Edges Lower in Q2 2026 as Prices Offset Nominal Gains

Real household income per capita across the OECD slipped by 0.1% in the second quarter of 2026, according to the OECD, ending a stretch of moderate growth as rising consumer prices offset higher nominal household income in many countries. Real GDP per capita, by contrast, expanded by 0.6% in the same quarter, after a 0.4% rise in the first quarter of 2026. The two measures track different things: GDP growth captures changes in aggregate economic activity, while household income growth reflects the income households have available to spend or save.
Of the 20 countries with available data, 12 posted growth in real household income per capita and 8 recorded a contraction.
G7 Picture Mixed
Among G7 economies, growth in real household income per capita slowed from 0.4% to 0.0% in the second quarter, with results diverging by country.
Italy saw real household income per capita fall 1.0% in the quarter, reversing a 0.4% expansion in the first quarter. The decline reflected inflation of 1.4% together with a reduction in net social benefits, while real GDP per capita rose 0.2%.
In France, both real household income per capita and real GDP per capita continued to contract in the second quarter, by 0.6% and 0.1% respectively, after each fell 0.2% in the prior quarter.
The United States recorded a milder contraction in real household income per capita of 0.1%, following several quarters of growth, while real GDP per capita increased 0.5%.
Canada, the United Kingdom and Germany all posted gains. In Canada, higher employee remuneration, notably in finance, real estate and company management, and trade, along with increased net social benefits, supported a 1.5% rise in real household income per capita. The benefits increase included a one-off government payment aimed at helping households with higher grocery and essential costs. Real GDP per capita rose 1.0%.
In the United Kingdom, higher net social benefits and lower payments on taxes on income and wealth contributed to a 1.0% increase in real household income per capita, recovering from a 0.8% contraction in the previous quarter, while real GDP per capita continued to grow by 0.5%. Germany recorded a slight 0.1% rise in real household income per capita after a 0.3% contraction in the prior quarter.
Poland Leads Gains, Chile Posts Largest Fall
Among other OECD countries, Poland recorded the largest increase in real household income per capita, up 2.5%, driven by higher employee remuneration and net property income. Real GDP per capita grew at a slower 1.2%.
Chile recorded the largest decrease in the second quarter, down 3.5%, driven by higher inflation and rising 
unemployment
, following a large 4.4% increase in the previous quarter. Finland also saw a sharp contraction in real household income per capita of 2.0%, while its real GDP per capita rose 0.4%.

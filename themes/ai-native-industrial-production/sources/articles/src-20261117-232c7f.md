Saronic wins deal to build autonomous resupply vessel for USMC&nbsp;&nbsp;Naval Technology

[page text]
Saronic Technologies to deliver ALPV for US Marine Corps forces. Credit: Saronic Technologies. 

Saronic Technologies has secured a prototype Other Transaction agreement with the US Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) to develop its Autonomous Low Profile Vessel (ALPV) for maritime resupply operations. 

The long-range autonomous vessel is intended to move food, fuel and other supplies to dispersed US Marine Corps forces operating within the reach of hostile weapons, according to the company announcement.

The agreement will support testing by the US government and planning by the Marine Corps on how the vessel could be operated, staffed, trained on and maintained.  

Saronic said it would work with the DIU and the Marine Corps to develop a capability that can be produced at scale. 

The ALPV is designed for manoeuvrability and beaching, allowing it to support ship-to-ship and ship-to-shore logistics. Its autonomy systems include navigation and collaborative operating functions, while its modular open architecture is intended to support different integrations. 

According to the company, the vessel would use passive perception, which detects and interprets its surroundings without actively transmitting signals.  

The ALPV uses autonomy systems that have been validated across more than 350,000 nautical miles and 55,000 hours of operation on Saronic’s other vessel models. 

The award comes as the Marine Corps and other US military services examine ways to sustain forces in contested maritime environments without exposing personnel to the same risks as crewed logistics vessels. 

Saronic co-founder and chief commercial officer Rob Lehman said: “This award recognizes that winning in contested waters starts with keeping forces supplied. Saronic’s ALPV gives Marines a low-signature, autonomous way to move critical cargo across contested seas, without putting a crew in harm’s way.” 

The agreement adds to Saronic’s existing US Navy work, which includes production agreements for the 24ft Corsair autonomous surface vessel and the 180ft Marauder autonomous ship.  

The company has also announced plans to construct Landing Craft Utility vessels at its Port Alpha shipyard in Brownsville, Texas. 

In September, 
Saronic began construction
 of Port Alpha, a facility the company says is supported by more than $3bn in private investment and aimed at expanding US naval and commercial shipbuilding capacity.

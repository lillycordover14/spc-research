Nvidia has signed a new semiconductor package substrate supply contract with South Korea's Samsung Electro-Mechanics to expand its AI chip supply chain, and the two companies will broaden their cooperation to include multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs). The news sent Samsung Electro-Mechanics shares surging in early trading on the 18th, reflecting market expectations.

As of 9:25 a.m. on the 18th, Samsung Electro-Mechanics (009150.KS) was trading at 801,000 won (approximately $600), up 47,000 won (approximately $35), or 6.23%, from the previous session. The stock, which had fallen 3.95% the previous day, rebounded to recover its losses.

The contract was disclosed during Nvidia's third-quarter earnings conference call held on the 17th local time. Colette Kress, Nvidia's interim chief executive officer, said the company had signed new contracts with Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Taiwan's Kinsus in the substrate segment, and with Taiwan's SPIL in advanced packaging. The specific size of the contracts and supply schedules were not disclosed. Kress was appointed interim CEO after former CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12, and Nvidia is currently searching for a successor.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics also confirmed in a separate announcement the same day that it will strengthen cooperation to support the expansion of production of Nvidia's accelerated computing platforms. The two companies had signed a strategic partnership in October this year for the supply of semiconductor package substrates, and have now expanded its scope.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics produces FC-BGA, a semiconductor package substrate that connects high-performance semiconductors to motherboards to transmit electrical signals and power. Under the expanded cooperation, the company will increase supply of advanced package substrates used in Nvidia's next-generation GPUs and high-speed networking products. The company said it will supply network package substrates for high-performance computing (HPC) and data centers based on advanced packaging technologies such as ultra-fine circuit implementation, flatness control and embedded passive components.

Additional funding from Nvidia to expand production infrastructure is also under consideration. Nvidia had agreed at the time of the October partnership to provide large-scale funding for the expansion of dedicated AI semiconductor package substrate plants at Samsung Electro-Mechanics' Sejong and Busan sites, and is said to be reviewing additional support this time. The funds will be used to expand production lines and introduce advanced equipment. Based on this, Samsung Electro-Mechanics plans to establish a full-scale mass production system starting in 2029.

Along with the contract, Nvidia also unveiled a broad supply chain expansion plan. In a statement, Nvidia said Taiwan's Kinsus Interconnect Technology will expand its Nvidia-dedicated ABF substrate production capacity at its Taoyuan plant to more than 3.5 times the current level, with production to begin in the second half of 2027 and additional capacity to be secured by the third quarter of 2028. With Taiwan's Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL), Nvidia signed a multi-year contract including billions of dollars in support to expand advanced packaging capacity by mid-2029. This includes bumping, interposer wafer manufacturing, assembly and test lines.

Earlier, in October this year, Nvidia also announced expansion plans with Amkor, Ibiden, King Yuan Electronics (KYEC) and Unimicron Technology. Nvidia set targets with these partners to increase substrate production capacity 2.5-fold within three years, double semiconductor testing capacity in 2026-2027, and expand SPIL's optical engine production capacity 15-fold by mid-2027.

The two companies will expand their cooperation beyond package substrates to MLCCs. As AI accelerated computing platforms require stable power supply during high-density, high-output computations, Samsung Electro-Mechanics will use its high-reliability MLCC technology to supply components needed for Nvidia's computing platforms. MLCCs are key components that smooth voltage fluctuations in electronic circuits and supply stable power. According to a Korea Times report, a single AI server contains more than 10 times as many MLCCs as a general server, and Samsung Electro-Mechanics is estimated to hold about 40% of the AI server MLCC market.

Chang Duck-hyun, president of Samsung Electro-Mechanics, said, "By combining world-class substrate micro-processing technology with high-reliability MLCC technology, we will fully fulfill our role as a total solution provider so that Nvidia's AI platforms can be supplied to the global market with the highest performance and quality in a timely manner."

Deborah Shoquist, Nvidia's executive vice president of operations, said, "Close cooperation across the global supply chain is essential to respond to long-term demand for AI infrastructure," adding, "Our cooperation with Samsung Electro-Mechanics is aimed at supporting the expansion of production of Nvidia's current and next-generation accelerated computing platforms."

The contract, combined with Nvidia's strong confidence in AI demand, boosted investor sentiment. Nvidia raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth forecast to 75% from 70%. Interim CEO Kress said the cloud industry's total order backlog increased to $2.5 trillion from $2 trillion three months earlier. Nvidia's purchase commitments rose to $390 billion in the third quarter.

Nvidia's third-quarter results also beat market expectations. Revenue came in at $115.8 billion and adjusted earnings per share (EPS) at $2.68. The fourth-quarter revenue forecast was also presented at $130 billion, above market expectations.

The cooperation shows that expanding AI data center investment is driving demand across the component supply chain. AI accelerators, server CPUs and high-speed networking equipment all require high-value package substrates and high-reliability MLCCs. Samsung Electro-Mechanics aims to strengthen its position as a comprehensive component supplier spanning package substrates and MLCCs for AI semiconductors through this contract. However, some note that actual revenue contribution will need to be confirmed later, as the specific contract size and supply schedule were not disclosed.

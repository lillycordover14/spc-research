Tuesday night didn't just feature shocking results from the mid-terms, but shocking results in the first edition of the 2026 CFP Rankings, and Craig Carton can't believe they had Alabama ranked so high!
 
We've had two weeks of the College Football Playoff 
selection committee's top-25 poll
, and two weeks of well-deserved controversy over their rankings. And the controversy extends well past just the positions each team sits, to the reasoning behind it.
 
 
Both weeks have had one consistent theme: the SEC has received an enormous, and undeserved, boost for their teams. Losses don't count for as much in the SEC, while every win is assumed to be a "quality" one. Injuries are given more deference, and preseason expectations are still treated as meaningful.

All while teams in other conferences are punished for ... not playing. Like the 
Indiana Hoosiers
, who dropped five spots in the rankings from No. 4 to No. 9 while on a bye week.
 
HERE'S HOW BAD THE COLLEGE FOOTBALL PLAYOFF'S SELECTION COMMITTEE RANKINGS ACTUALLY ARE
 
 
Indiana head coach Curt Cignetti speaks during news conference ahead of the College Football Playoff national championship game between Miami and Indiana, Sunday, Jan. 18, 2026, in Miami. The game will be played on Monday.
 
(AP Photo/Chris Carlson)
 
Head coach Curt Cignetti
, the defending national champion, brought up how many other coaches are thinking and feeling about the poll in a Monday press conference. Roasting the selection committee for the disparate treatment and how they handle losses and wins dependent on brand names, stadiums, and marketing efforts, not team quality.
 
"You get tired of the narrative sometimes," Cignetti said. "A quality win because Joe Smith was playing at the time. They ain’t won a game since, but it was a quality win back then. I guess bad losses don’t count. Look, it’s all going to play out on the field. It’s good for entertainment. I’m sure this’ll get a lot of nice hits and play.
"But our guys understand that it’ll play out on the field. The good thing about it is, I think it feeds into our motivation and our chip. That’s why I’m not afraid to keep it alive. And you know what? I’m 65 years old and I really don’t give a s--t."
 
How the committee handled Cignetti's Indiana team is one thing. How they justify it is another. And that justification is why the committee faces a major test of their legitimacy and remaining shreds of credibility.
NEW COLLEGE FOOTBALL PLAYOFF POLL PROVES THE SELECTION COMMITTEE IS JUST MAKING IT UP AS THEY GO
 
During committee chair 
Hunter Yurachek's post-release interview
, he "explained" why the Hoosiers fell five spots without playing.
 
 
"Indiana was probably a victim of having a bye week at the wrong time with seven games between Top 25 teams," he said. "The committee is really impressed with what UCLA, Texas, and Notre Dame did as well as 
Alabama and LSU
."
"Every team that Indiana had played to date in the Big Ten all lost and that affected their strength of schedule rank and the committee's review of Indiana," he continued. "The quarterback play in general and the offensive play in general is kind of a discussion point of Indiana ... They've won games because of their defense and not their offense."
As ridiculous as it is to knock a team for having an elite defense, that wasn't the worst of it.

This is: "every team that Indiana had played to date in the Big Ten all lost and that affected their strength of schedule rank."
First, we have no idea how the committee evaluates the strength of schedule, because 
there is no transparency
 around what metrics they use, or even if they just make it up as they go and don't use any. But the problem is that Yurachek made it clear that Indiana's opponents all losing, hurt their strength of schedule in the eyes of the committee.
 
Well, we've got good news for them, because they have an opportunity to demonstrate their consistency and lack of favoritism this week. With their beloved 
Alabama Crimson Tide
.

Let's take a quick look at how Alabama's prior SEC wins did this week:
Kentucky, lost 21-14 at home to Florida
Mississippi State
, lost 41-9 at home to Auburn
LSU, lost 22-21 at home to Texas
South Carolina, lost 34-6 at Arkansas
Texas A&M, 22-7 at home to Tennessee

Well then! Isn't that interesting. All five of Alabama's non-Vanderbilt wins lost on Saturday, four of them at home in the Awe-Inspiring Cathedral, which is an SEC stadium. Those five teams are now a combined 27-23, and eight of those wins came against Kent State, Towson, Louisiana-Monroe, Youngstown State, Louisiana Tech, McNeese State, Missouri State and The Citadel.
So will the committee stay true to their word and penalize the Tide for all of their "quality SEC wins" losing over the weekend?
 
Then there's an argument to be made that Alabama deserves to move down because their win over Vanderbilt might have been less impressive than Indiana sitting at home. They had just 4.5 yards per play on offense against one of the worst defenses in the conference and the 110th worst defense in FBS when it comes to points allowed. Take out three plays of 20+ yards and they gained 227 on 65 plays, an average of just 3.49 per play. Their touchdown drive in the third quarter was 12 yards, after a long kickoff return. And they were fortunate to go into halftime leading after a flag on 3rd-and-9 set up a touchdown instead of a field goal.
 
It was about as unimpressive as a win can be. And all their SEC wins lost on the same weekend. So will the committee move them down five or six spots? Somehow it seems doubtful, doesn't it? This is, after all, the same organization that put the Tide in the 12-team tournament last year after getting obliterated in the SEC Championship Game.
Alabama's only two games against top-10 opponents, Georgia and Tennessee, they lost. In 2025, we were told that Notre Dame didn't deserve to make the tournament because they lost two games against top quality opponents, Miami and Texas A&M. Interesting!

The entire SEC took a major hit this weekend, with many of its supposedly good teams losing. Ole Miss is now 6-4 after a bad loss to a mediocre Oklahoma team. Texas A&M is 1-6 in the SEC and 4-6 overall. Missouri, which seems to exist solely to be ranked in the 20s in order to provide a "ranked win" for other teams, got annihilated by Georgia and fell to 6-4. They'll probably still be ranked, but nobody outside the selection committee room thinks they deserve it. LSU is now 6-4 and 3-4 in the SEC. South Carolina, which inexplicably moved up to No. 17 last week, was blown out by a very bad Arkansas team to drop to 6-4. Mississippi State, which still gets treated as a patented "quality win" because of the injury to Kamario Taylor, showed that they are not, in fact, a quality team, losing 41-9 to Auburn.
 
 
The Bulldogs defense, which does not include Kamario Taylor, has now given up 56 to Bama, lost 31-0 to LSU, 23 points to Oklahoma, 31 to Texas, 25 to Vanderbilt, and 41 to Auburn. Nearly 35 points per game as they've fallen to 4-6.

None of this is to say that Alabama isn't a good team deserving of a playoff spot. Based on underlying performance and predictive metrics, they are clearly one of the best 5–10 teams in the country. But when the committee sets out its "standards" in plain English, it warrants monitoring how they live up to them if one of their favorite teams is affected.
 
Will the committee stop 
overrating the SEC now?
 Will they stop giving them the benefit of the doubt? That's the question facing them on Tuesday night.
 
And how they answer it will determine how much credibility they have left. It probably won't be much.

By Scott Mammoser, Team FSO writer

Photos courtesy of the Mohrman Family

When Pacific Coast Sectionals begin in San Jose, Calif., next week, one preliminary skater to keep an eye on is Hayden Mohrman from Scottsdale, Ariz. The 11-year-old has already collected a pair of top-four finishes in national events this year and is looking forward to more.

“I didn’t like it at first,” Hayden said of her four-year skating career. “The more I got into it, the more I enjoyed it, especially after I (achieved) my single Axel.”

The sixth grader said she attributes her improvement this season to skating every day and the fact that she doesn’t stop working on a jump until she lands it. This resiliency showed this summer at the U.S. Figure Skating Excel National Final in Boston, where she placed fourth among 24 in the Preliminary Excel Plus division. The Excel Series 
offers a non-qualifying singles track designed to motivate technical skill and program component advancement while introducing skaters to the International Judging System (IJS) through a phased approach.  

“My mom told me to relax, and that it was just for the experience,” Hayden continued. “Then, I was surprised I took fourth, after I thought I would take 100
th
.”

On she went to the National Showcase competition in Denver and won the bronze medal for comedic program, where she portrayed Oscar the Grouch. Showcase blends traditional ice skating skills with artistic creativity, costumes, props, and musical theater.

In September, she qualified for the Pacific Coast Sectionals from scoring a 29.38 in the championship preliminary girls event at the Austin Autumn Classic. The event in Texas was the weekend before the last national qualifying competitions, so Hayden had to wait for those to conclude to see if anybody would best her score, which they did not. Thus, she will take her ‘Piano Fantasy’ free skate program to the Bay Area.

“I’m super excited,” Hayden exclaimed. “We saw where I was on the board and that there was a chance I might go. I am going against all California kids in the competition.”

Hayden is the middle of three sisters who all skate at the Coyotes Skating Club of Arizona. Her older sister, Henley, is 14 and at the intermediate level, while 9-year-old Charley is a pre- preliminary.

“I feel like Henley and I have a little competition,” Hayden said of her older sister. “I try to get better like her, so I feel like she is pushing me. I feel like that also helps me get to where I am right now. I am working on the double Axel and working on getting my double-doubles consistent. I stand up on it, but it’s never fully rotated. It’s hard to get two-and-a-half rotations in the air, plus, you need the height. I’m not good at height. I have fast rotations and no height.”

Karen Gesell was actually coaching Charley, when the girls’ mother, Heather, asked if she could take on Hayden, as well.

“When I started working with Hayden, she didn’t care to skate too much.” Gesell remembered, “She didn’t try very hard, so the lessons were a little challenging. As she said, once she started getting better and she got her Axel, it was like a light bulb went off, and she works so hard every day. She does her jumps over and over again until they are perfect for her. She has required a great love for the sport, and she also has a great competitive attitude out there. She is probably one of the most-improved skaters I have had in years for the amount of time she has skated.”

Max Aaron, Julie Patterson, Danielle Leong, and Camden Pulkinen also assist in the coaching of Hayden. Aaron is the 2013 U.S. champion, while Pulkinen was fifth at the World Championships in 2022 and was coached by Gesell early in his career. Pulkinen, who now lives in New York City, comes into town a few times a year and works with Gesell’s athletes.

In addition, Hayden competes with the Ice Denettes Juvenile synchronized skating team. The team is hoping to make it to Synchro Nationals this season. She grins at the possibility of receiving the scholarship that synchronized skating offers. It’s something that could prolong her studying of math, which is her favorite subject at Jones-Gordon School.

Off of the ice, Hayden enjoys acting and has already appeared in three movies: ‘Blame It on Midnight,’ ‘Immortal Echoes’ and ‘Rent a Grandpa,’ in addition to a Peter Piper Pizza commercial. She also enjoys tumbling and is an 
Instagram influencer
 for toys and clothes under the Justice brand. Influencing is also a family affair, with Henley influencing makeup and skin care and Charley focusing on toys.

Still, with all of these unique talents, Hayden has her eyes set on hitting triple jumps and upgrading to juvenile.

“I see great potential,” Gesell added. “She rotates very fast, and her height is getting better. She is a very good spinner. Because of the Showcase, she has a lot of drama and presentation out there. She has everything she needs to be a great skater.”

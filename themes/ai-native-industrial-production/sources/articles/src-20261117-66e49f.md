VulcanForms factory. Credit: VulcanForms
Digital manufacturing company 
VulcanForms
 has secured 
$500 million
 in financing, with 
J.P. Morgan
 and funds managed by 
Apollo
 leading the deal. The VulcanForms financing will pay for 
three new factories
, on top of the three manufacturing sites the company already operates in Devens and Newburyport, Massachusetts.

Scaling metal additive manufacturing to industrial production is as much a question of capital as of technology, since each factory combines printers, machining, automation and inspection.

At the heart of VulcanForms’ model is 
a single production platform
 that brings together metal additive manufacturing, precision machining, automation and what the company calls 
physical AI
.

According to the Boston-based company, the new capacity responds to demand from multibillion-dollar commercial programs in a wide range of markets, from aerospace, space and defense to medical devices, consumer products and industry.

Part of the money would also go into physical AI, to further automate the production of complex, high-performance metal parts.

“
America cannot lead the next industrial revolution if we can’t manufacture what we invent,
” said 
Kevin Kassekert
, CEO of VulcanForms.

A second financing in less than a year

For 
John Zito
, Co-President of Apollo Asset Management, the deal is “
a bespoke financing solution to fund capacity expansion alongside J.P. Morgan
”. On the bank’s side, the transaction falls under JPMorganChase’s Security and Resiliency Initiative.

In February, 
VulcanForms closed a $220 million round
 led by Eclipse and 1789 Capital. Founded out of MIT in 2015, the company says it has now raised more than $1 billion from lenders and investors.

The company also recently teamed up with Specter Aerospace to 
produce airbreathing hypersonic weapons at scale
.

Needless to say, three new factories in a single financing step is a change of scale for a company that has so far operated from Massachusetts only.

VulcanForms ordered 200 EOS M4 ONYX systems in the largest single purchase in EOS history.
The order expands an existing EOS fleet of 35 machines rather than introducing new technology.
VulcanForms must convert added capacity into repeatable output across its integrated production workflow.
 

VulcanForms and EOS announced on November 17, 2026 that VulcanForms had ordered 200 EOS M4 ONYX metal additive-manufacturing systems and ancillary equipment, a purchase 
EOS described as the largest single order in its history
. The systems will significantly expand metal additive-manufacturing production at VulcanForms’ facilities in Devens, Massachusetts.

The Devens facilities already house 35 EOS M4 ONYX systems in production alongside other metal additive-manufacturing platforms. EOS Chief Technology Officer Joachim Zettler described the resulting installation as a fleet of 235 EOS M4 ONYX systems. The purchase therefore expands a technology VulcanForms already uses rather than marking its first deployment of EOS equipment.

VulcanForms’ production facilities combine metal additive manufacturing, precision machining, automation, inspection and proprietary software in an end-to-end platform. The company produces complex, high-performance metal components for customers in consumer goods, medical devices, industrial equipment and mission systems. The additional systems will expand the additive-manufacturing stage within that broader production workflow as the company grows its US manufacturing operations.

Chief Executive Kevin Kassekert said the investment is intended to deliver high-performance components with consistent quality and competitive economics, accelerate VulcanForms’ growth and support manufacturing reshoring to the United States, with the EOS systems complementing the company’s proprietary printer technology. His comments connect the purchase to both production scale and the economics of supplying precision metal parts.

Equipment and Fleet-Scale Production

Launched in late 2025, the EOS M4 ONYX combines six 400-watt lasers with a build volume of 450 × 450 × 400 millimeters. It also includes intelligent process control and the RFS Pro advanced filtration system. EOS said these capabilities are designed to deliver the throughput, process stability and consistent part quality required for demanding, high-volume manufacturing environments.

EOS will supply materials, software and application expertise in addition to the machines as VulcanForms scales repeatable production and brings the added capacity online. That support extends the relationship beyond equipment delivery and into the processes required to operate the expanded installation.

Zettler said running the enlarged fleet requires more than throughput: “It takes repeatable processes, consistent part quality across the entire fleet and reliable integration into the wider manufacturing environment.” His emphasis on fleet-wide consistency is significant because VulcanForms is adding the machines to an operation that also includes machining, automation, inspection and software, rather than measuring capacity by printer count alone.

The order leaves VulcanForms with a clear execution task: convert the equipment addition into usable production while maintaining consistent quality across the enlarged fleet. The integration of the new systems with the rest of its manufacturing workflow will determine how much of the added machine capacity becomes repeatable output for customers.

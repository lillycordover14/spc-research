AMCA Programme Winner To Be Announced Within A Month: Defence Secretary
2 min read
The selected consortium will serve as the development-cum-production partner for five AMCA prototypes and a structural test specimen
The government will announce the winner of the bidding process for India's indigenous fifth-generation Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) programme within a month, Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said on Monday.
Three shortlisted private sector-led contenders have submitted their bids for the Rs 15,000-crore project to develop prototypes of the indigenous stealth fighter aircraft.
The contenders are an L&T-led consortium comprising Bharat Electronics (BEL) and Dynamatic Technologies, Tata Advanced Systems (TASL), and a Bharat Forge-led consortium comprising BEML and Data Patterns.
Speaking at an ANI event, Singh said the government expects to announce the selected bidder within a month, paving the way for faster development of the AMCA prototypes.
"In a month's time, we will be ready to announce the winner of the race and after that, we hope it will lead to faster development of the AMCA prototypes for induction," he said.
The selected consortium will serve as the development-cum-production partner (DCPP) for five AMCA prototypes and a structural test specimen. The aircraft has been designed and developed by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA), a laboratory under the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO).
Singh also clarified that the government has not yet taken a decision on pursuing fifth-generation fighter aircraft programmes beyond AMCA, including Russia's Su-57 or the US-made F-35.
"As of now, our fifth-generation programme is AMCA," he said, adding that the bids had been received from all three shortlisted contenders.
The winning bidder will be required to establish a manufacturing ecosystem near Puttaparthi in Andhra Pradesh, where the AMCA core integration and flight-testing complex will be located.
The bid submission deadline was extended from 27 August to 30 October after the shortlisted companies sought additional time to complete the formalities for the complex fighter development programme.
Meanwhile, Chief of the Air Staff Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit said the Indian Air Force expects to induct a squadron of indigenous Light Combat Aircraft (LCA) Mk-1A within the current financial year.
He also said development work on the LCA Mk-2 and AMCA programmes was progressing. The LCA Mk-2 is expected to roll out next year, he added.
The Defence Secretary, meanwhile, said the government would assess concerns over the technology relevance of the LCA Mk-2 closer to the time of its induction.
Singh also pointed to the large contracts for the LCA Mk-1A, noting that engine deliveries were beginning to provide greater visibility for the programme.
The AMCA programme is a key component of India's efforts to develop indigenous fifth-generation combat aircraft capabilities.

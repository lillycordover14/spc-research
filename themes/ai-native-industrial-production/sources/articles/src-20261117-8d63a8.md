PITTSBURGH -- Several members of the Pirates’ coaching and development staff hopped on a Microsoft Teams call last Tuesday to discuss the future with 
Seth Hernandez
, Pittsburgh’s 
top prospect
 and the 
No. 9
 prospect in baseball per MLB Pipeline.

With Hernandez’s first professional season behind him, he and the Pirates’ staff are seeking ways to help him reach his potential. His offseason work at the Futures Training Center in his hometown of Corona, Calif., is part of the formula.

“He's been in the facility. A lot of his work has been done on the strength and conditioning side,” said Anthony Antolin, Hernandez’s pitching coach at Futures. “He's throwing a couple times a week right now, just kind of building things up. But definitely over the next six to eight weeks is when things are really getting to full swing.”

Pittsburgh’s top selection (sixth overall) in the 
2025 MLB Draft
 didn’t disappoint in his debut season. The 20-year-old posted a 2.43 ERA in 74 innings (18 starts) between Single-A Bradenton and High-A Greensboro before a 
left oblique strain
 ended his year in late July.

Ranked as MLB Pipeline’s 
top right-handed pitcher
, Hernandez flashed all the makings of a future ace. Antolin, who has worked with him since January 2024, is trying to help position Hernandez to take the next steps toward reaching the Majors. 

“We see each other pretty much every single day at the facility, and from the meeting that we had Tuesday, the big focus is understanding that when he can put himself ahead in counts, the results end up being really good in terms of his ability to put guys away,” Antolin said. “So that was at least one of my big takeaways was winning neutral counts, winning leverage counts and just putting him in a position to be able to use the weapons that he has.”

When Antolin started working with Hernandez, the righty was still a year away from his Draft selection. Still, he was already regarded as one of the top amateur pitchers in the country.

Antolin saw fluidity in Hernandez’s delivery and electricity in his arm. The fastball and changeup were already established. The next step was understanding the relationship between his curveball and slider.

Hernandez was still tinkering with his changeup last season after feeling like it was flattening out too much. 

“When I got up to Greensboro, we kind of made a little bit of a grip adjustment on that pitch,” Hernandez said. “That was a pitch that … ended up getting even better throughout the year, which is super important for me.”

Antolin believes that Hernandez’s pitch arsenal is in a good spot now. They’ll work throughout the offseason to maintain their quality rather than make any tweaks before Spring Training. There also don’t seem to be any lingering concerns about the oblique injury that Hernandez sustained when he slipped off a wet mound last summer.

“When I first saw him a few weeks ago, he was already feeling pretty good. He was moving around a little bit. He was kind of finishing up that last bit of the rehab process,” Antolin said. “But as of now, I mean, he's in a good spot. He's full go. He's starting to get into the throwing; he’s in the weight room. So all signs point to him being in a really, really good spot as of now.” 

Part of Hernandez’s work at Futures will be putting on clean muscle mass to produce more force and less stress on the arm. 

Hernandez 
told MLB.com
 in early July that he hopes to make the Major Leagues before his 21st birthday on June 28. It’s a lofty goal. But a strong offseason could have him knocking on the door sooner rather than later.

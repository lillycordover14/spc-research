The Bills are riding a five-game winning streak, and several of the team's biggest recent strengths were on display again in Week 10.
From Amani Hooker's impressive debut after arriving less than a week earlier to the defense's continued improvement and an offensive approach designed to wear opponents down over 60 minutes, here's what Buffalo's coaching staff is saying about the team's latest win.
Amani Hooker leads Bills defense in snaps
Less than a week after being acquired by the Bills, safety Amani Hooker was on the field for every defensive snap in Sunday's win over the Jets, playing all 71 snaps in his Buffalo debut.
Leading up to the game, head coach Joe Brady was careful not to put expectations on how large of a role Hooker would have while learning a new defense on the fly. By kickoff, however, both Hooker and the coaching staff felt comfortable moving forward without restrictions.
"We tried not to set a like, 'Hey man, you're going to play this many plays,'" Brady said. "It was just like whatever he can handle and the comfort level he has and how he's going. We're going to let him roll with it."
Hooker's ability to quickly digest Buffalo's defensive system allowed him to earn a full workload immediately. Defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard credited the veteran's football IQ and understanding of the game for helping make that possible.

"I have not seen that situation where a guy comes in at this point of the season and goes out and plays every snap and functions," Leonhard said. "He does a great job. He sees space really well. He understands what's going on around him. He understands where backers alignments and defensive ends alignments are."
Even Leonhard admitted he was surprised by how seamlessly Hooker transitioned into the defense.
The veteran nearly came away with an interception in his first game as a Bill and finished with four tackles while helping Buffalo hold the Jets out of the end zone.
"He's going to help this defense out a lot and he went out there and played a great game, got his hands on the ball a couple times. He's going to be good for this defense," Leonhard said.
What does the tape say about the Bills recent defense?
For the third consecutive week, the Bills faced a backup quarterback. In two of those instances, as was the case against the Ravens and Jets, Buffalo's defense made legal plays that just so happened to injure the opponents' starting QB.
"Things have changed really fast within the game," Leonhard said of having to adjust to different QBs on the fly. "And I think our guys have been able to handle that."
But because of the string of backups, it can be hard to know how to weigh Buffalo's improvements on that side of the ball. Since Week 8, the Bills rank second in total yards allowed per game (232.7), fifth in yards allowed per play (4.3) and second in total points allowed (28).
Leonhard said his focus has been studying the tape in order to make observations about his unit, and not relying on pure statistics.
"You put on the tape and our guys executing. Is it clean football? Is it physical football? Are we stopping the run? Are we forcing teams into advantageous situations for us? And I think we've been doing that," he said.
One of the areas where Leonhard has seen specific improvement on tape and in the result is in long distance situations. Going as far back as Week 6, the Bills defense ranks third in the NFL in opponent third down conversions (28.3%). The only teams better since Week 6 have been the Rams and Bears.
"We've talked early in the season, second and long, third and longs. Those are situations that as a defense you have to take advantage of â and we weren't. So those were some key situations that we had to dial in on and improve. And we've had a lot more success. Therefore, you're getting off the field, you're getting the ball back to the offense and you're not playing as many snaps just based on one of those situations," the defensive coordinator said.
Built for 60 minutes
One of the themes for the Bills this season has been their second-half dominance, particularly on offense. Buffalo ranks second in yards per play, second in EPA per play, third in explosive play rate and tied for first in points scored.
Brady was asked this week about why that has been the case and he explained his play-calling process.
"Right now we're doing a great job from an explosive pass, thought process, but it's not like we're calling it and [Josh Allen] is throwing it no matter what. If it's there, take it. And if it's not, progress and check the ball down and we'll live and be able to do it again," Brady said.
Particularly against the Jets, Brady said the Bills attacked horizontally with screens and routes to the perimeter, then used that to attack the middle and down the field later on.
"I know some of the plays end up getting broken down as runs in the stats, but a good job just, getting the ball in the perimeter to DJ to start the game, the play to Khalilâ¦ just getting the speed and space," Brady said.
"I thought that has been good to us spreading it out and it forces teams to have to play every blade of grass and then it opens up in the insides and the shots down the field."
In each of the last two weeks, Brady has brought up unprompted that he believes the Bills are built to be a 60-minute team and are at their best late in games. That's been part of his vision for the Bills since he became the head coach.
"We've been preaching conditioning since in the offseason. It was a big component of who we were going to be. And we knew it was important that when you've got to play us for 60 minutes, I think there's a lot that comes into that," Brady said.
"It's more of a mindset and it's a mentality, but it's also an approach for the guys to not panic and just know that, whether it's going well earlier, whether it's not, just kind of sticking with it and eventually it's going to go our way."

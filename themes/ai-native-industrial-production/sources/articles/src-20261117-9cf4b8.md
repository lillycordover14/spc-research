The biggest problem about Carson Beck’s NFL debut wasn’t his stat line

Carson Beck's NFL debut didn't have nearly the amount of opportunities Cardinals head coach Mike LaFleur and the rest of the team envisioned.

TEMPE — The stat line might not jump off the page, but there was a lot to like from Arizona Cardinals rookie quarterback 
Carson Beck’s NFL debut
 on Sunday.

Whether it be the poise he showed, ability to look downfield or overall confidence, head coach Mike LaFleur summed it up Monday as a “sharp” debut for the 2026 third-round pick. It was a major positive.

There is also a lot to clean up for the rookie, who will get another shot this week against the Kansas City Chiefs.

ADVERTISEMENT

But the biggest negative from 
Beck’s debut
 wasn’t his numbers or overall play after reviewing the tape. It was the sample size.

“It would have obviously been nice to get out there in kind of a semi two-minute mode there in the fourth quarter. We couldn’t get back out there,” LaFleur 
said
 Monday. “That would have been good for that whole group. Some guys are still kind of learning the two-minute menu a little bit and guys are kind of mixing and matching around. There are things to improve, but it was a sharp debut for him.”

The most glaring part of the day for Arizona as a whole was a franchise-low 34 plays run, highlighted by just 12 in the second half. Arizona had the same total plays as the Rams did points. Hard to do much of anything or find a groove when the Cardinals possessed the ball for just 17:27.

The blame goes all around on that one, too.

The offense failed to do much of anything with a beat-up offensive line that is struggling to pass block and a wide receivers room that looks nothing like it did at the start of the year due to injuries and Marvin Harrison Jr.’s departure.

Through the past two games, Arizona has scored just nine points, all coming off Chad Ryland field goals. Not even tight end Trey McBride’s seven-reception streak was safe this past weekend. Not having more opportunities was key in that.

Defensively, the Cardinals watched the Rams chew up more than 12 minutes of the fourth quarter on a single drive with Ronnie Rivers, of all people, leading L.A.’s backfield. Sunday also marked the seventh game in which Arizona gave up at least 30 points.

As LaFleur said after the game Sunday, there is no magic savior coming to save the season. There are still seven games to play, and it’s on the Cardinals to figure out how to put their best foot forward and try to stay competitive.

ADVERTISEMENT

For now, it’s about trying to shake something loose on defense while continuing to push Beck in the right direction as he settles into his new role.

“I’m very encouraged about what I saw and I’m excited to continue to work with him and excited to continue to work with this offense to try to get this thing going,” LaFleur said. “Let’s get good at something and let’s gain just some sort of continuity over these next seven weeks if we can. 

“Injuries are still going to happen and all that, but it is what it is. Excited to potentially get a few guys back up front this week, build the continuity and go.”

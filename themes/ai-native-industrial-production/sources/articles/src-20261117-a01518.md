Rare-Earth Selloff Extends Ahead of Trump-Xi Meeting

The new losses follow a William Blair downgrade of USA Rare Earth on November 16 over feedstock issues, which pulled the whole group lower. That selloff came ahead of a meeting between President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping. Much of the bull case for U.S. rare-earth names depends on reducing reliance on China, and USA Rare Earth’s strategy is built directly around that theme.

USA Rare Earth said recent non-China dysprosium oxide prices ran 14 to 16 times China prices. Output from Serra Verde is sold under a 15-year

 take-or-pay contract priced on non-China prices, which links those sales to that premium. The wider market is also cautious, with the S&P 500 down 1% and the materials sector down 0.2%.

Under the company’s current plan, mining at the Tanbreez rare-earth project in Greenland is set to begin by the end of 2028.

How USA Rare Earth’s Business Model Differs

USA Rare Earth is building a mine-to-magnet supply chain across the U.S., Brazil and the U.K. It includes Serra Verde’s Brazilian mine, the metal and alloy plants run by its Less Common Metals unit and a magnet plant in Stillwater, Oklahoma. Several of those pieces came through acquisitions that closed in recent months, so the company is still absorbing integration costs while output ramps up. Chief executive Thras Moraitis stated that “Demand for a secure and responsible supply of rare earth products is becoming increasingly acute across a broad range of technology-driven sectors.”

MP Materials runs one main mine and processing site at Mountain Pass in California and is building magnet capacity in Texas, with a Department of War partnership that includes price protection helping shield it from swings in rare-earth prices. Critical Metals is at the earliest stage of the three, with a Greenland rare-earth deposit and an Austrian lithium project that both still need financing.

What’s at Stake for USA Rare Earth Ahead of Trump-Xi Talks

Are traders trimming rare-earth names before the meeting in case a U.S.-China deal eases supply tensions and undermines the case for USA Rare Earth’s non-China supply? The bulls can point to federal backing, including a $750 million Department of War investment in the company’s contract vehicle and a government forward purchase of at least $300 million of product.

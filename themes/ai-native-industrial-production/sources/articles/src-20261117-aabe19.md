LanzaTech: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors
 
 
 
 
 
 
  The Motley Fool Team, The Motley Fool 
 
 
November 17, 2026
 
4 min read
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
LNZA
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
On its third-quarter of 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 16, 2026, 
LanzaTech
 (NASDAQ:LNZA) reiterated its full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $50 million to $55 million. Operating expenses declined 35% year over year as management cut structural overhead to reach profitability. Three key takeaways from the call highlight how management plans to turn technical milestones into long-term commercial growth.
 
 
 
 
 
1. Structural Expense Cuts Reset the Operating Cost Base
 
 
Management reduced operating expenses (OpEx) to $11.7 million in the third quarter, down from $18 million a year ago, by cutting selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) costs and research spending. The lower expense level helps reduce quarterly adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) losses while the company advances project developments.
 
 
 
The actions we have taken to reshape LanzaTech for the current market are now showing up in our results quarter after quarter: stable revenue, a structurally lower cost base and improved adjusted EBITDA.
-- Dr. Jennifer Holmgren, Chief Executive Officer
 
 
2. European Fuel Certification Unlocks Monetizable Product Demand
 
 
LanzaTech achieved International Sustainability and Carbon Certification for the European Union (ISCC EU) for recycled carbon fuel produced from steel mill waste gases at its Hebei Shoulang joint venture facility. The certification permits the company to sell certified ethanol into regulated European road and marine transport fuel markets at premium prices.
 
 
 
Historically, our supply could not be matched with market demand because demand has been constrained by the absence of certified product. In mandated markets where verified carbon intensity carries real value, certification turns that interest to revenue opportunity and accelerates our path to profitability.
-- Dr. Jennifer Holmgren, Chief Executive Officer
 
 
3. Increased LanzaJet Stake Secures Aviation Conversion Capacity
 
 
LanzaTech agreed to increase its ownership stake in LanzaJet as part of a debt-reduction and capital-restructuring effort. LanzaJet owns alcohol-to-jet (ATJ) conversion technology that turns LanzaTech ethanol into sustainable aviation fuel (SAF), securing access to high-volume aviation fuel markets.
 
 
 
Through these transactions, LanzaTech is expected to significantly increase its ownership of LanzaJet. I will remain Chair of the LanzaJet Board and will also serve as CEO as the company moves into its next phase.
-- Dr. Jennifer Holmgren, Chief Executive Officer

Looking Ahead
 
 
Quarterly operating costs are holding steady at lower levels, and management is focusing resources on commercial project execution. LanzaTech relies on its proprietary gas fermentation technology, strategic project development partnerships, and capital-efficient infrastructure financing to reach commercial scale. Management is watching final investment decision (FID) milestones for European projects and the timing of initial certified ethanol sales contracts.
 
 
 
As we move forward, we believe the actions we have taken have created a more efficient operating model and strengthen the foundation from which we can pursue sustainable profitability.
-- Sushmita Koyanagi, Chief Financial Officer
 
 
Long-term execution depends on converting technical progress into bankable commercial agreements across global transport markets.
 
 
 
SAF and sustainable marine fuels remain our focus, and we will keep converting our technical progress into commercial and financial results.
-- Dr. Jennifer Holmgren, Chief Executive Officer

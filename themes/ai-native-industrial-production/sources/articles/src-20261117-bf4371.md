Each time a Penn State Figure Skating Competition Team member took the ice for its Exhibition Skate Fundraiser Friday night, teammates drummed their fists against the glass and cheered.
The sounds rolled throughout Pegula Arena, which was filled with families, friends and fans. The event was raising funds to support the team in their season ahead. Unlike traditional figure skating teams, Penn State doesn’t compete for individual placements, and instead competes for an overall team score.
The team is led by President Veronica Bellorin, a fourth-year studying biobehavioral health. Her responsibilities include maintaining Penn State’s status as a club sport, handling community service hours, utilizing IMLeagues, planning ice time for the year and overseeing all of the other positions.
“The biggest thing is that, at competitions, we’re all one. So even if it isn’t our day, we still support each other, and we all want what's best for each other,” Bellorin said. “You really form that bond, and it feels like family.”
Bellorin is also responsible for motivating her teammates and imparting words of wisdom.
“It’s always an honor to represent Penn State, and that's the biggest thing I tell everyone, is to have fun and relax. Even though we're all standing behind you, focus on yourself in the moment," Bellorin said.
Last season was especially exciting for the blue and white. At their qualifying competition hosted at Case Western Reserve University, they earned a total of 257 team points, the most they’ve ever scored at a single competition. That accomplishment helped them finish second in the Southeast section, the highest sectional finish in team history.
Penn State carried that momentum through the year and ended its season on a high note, competing in the 2026 National Intercollegiate Final, where it finished as the highest-placing Big 10 school.
Its goal this upcoming competition season is to qualify for the National Intercollegiate Final this year, which will be hosted at the University of Delaware this April.
Bellorin works hand-in-hand with Vice President Christina Romonoski-Dao to create a welcoming and supportive environment, she said. Romonoski-Dao, a third-year studying biomedical engineering, oversees away games, including planning out travel logistics and registering for competitions.
Romonoski-Dao said her teammates are what make the experience worthwhile.
“You want everyone to perform their best, and you always want to cheer on your teammates,” Romonoski-Dao said. “My favorite part is to travel and compete with so many people I’ve grown close with.”
Alexa Winter’s experience as a seasoned member has allowed her to develop an easygoing and laid-back pre-skate ritual. During competitions, or rather right before they start, she said she takes a second for herself: She jumps around, tries to calm her breathing and doesn’t “overdo anything.” It helps her get in the zone, she said.
Winter, a fifth-year team member studying architectural engineering, said there’s an undemanding, supportive energy she feels when competing with her teammates.
“It’s very different for most people when they come in, because you're used to being in the sport alone,” Winter said. “I think a lot of people don't realize how close you guys get just from training together and being at comps the whole weekend.”

The prediction markets are questioning Mike Vrabel’s job security, again

By

Mike Florio
,

By

Mike Florio

 
 
Published November 17, 2026 11:11 AM

Add

        Email

During the offseason, the leading prediction markets were calling into question the future of Patriots coach Mike Vrabel. At one point, Kalshi had his chances of being the head coach as of Week 1 
at only 62.2 percent
.
Now that the Patriots have suffered a pair of embarrassing losses, the possibility of Vrabel not being the head coach of the Patriots after the season is creeping up again.
On Kalshi’s master list of 
coaches to be fired by March 2027
, Vrabel is at nine percent. On Polymarket, the specific question of whether Vrabel will be out by December 31, 2026 
currently stands at 13 percent
.
Since the regular season doesn’t end until January 10, the notion that he’s regarded as having a 13-percent chance of being fired by December 31 suggests that, if the date were shifted to Monday, January 11, the number would be higher.
Could it happen, one year after a Super Bowl berth in Vrabel’s first season with the team? The chances seem to be much lower than 13 percent, on the surface. However, there’s a weird vibe coming from the team right now, as illustrated by some of the things Vrabel said both 
after Sunday’s 35-10 loss
 to the Lions 
and on Monday
.

It’s fair to ask whether Vrabel has lost his touch. It’s fair to wonder whether his offseason entanglement has eroded some of his authority within the locker room.
Coaches routinely rail against distractions. Vrabel created a significant one that crossed over into mainstream news. While plenty of players would likely shrug at the situation, some may see hypocrisy. Some may have become exhausted by the endless questions from family and friends about the situation.
The failure of any team in the NFL bubbles over from a complex cauldron of factors. The players may not be good enough. The preparation may not be effective enough. Bad luck plays a role.
Still, the coach needs to be able to press the buttons of the players in order to get the most out of them. And it doesn’t take much to tune someone out — even a football coach.
Those are the critical questions. Has a critical mass of players tuned him out? Are they not listening to him? Are they not responding to him?
Ultimately, that’s something for ownership to monitor and to diagnose. While the organizational play was to sharply turn the page and move on from the scandal, that only works if the team wins. With the Patriots at 5-5 — and with the defense surrendering 881 yards in losses to the Packers and Lions and being outgained by Detroit, 494 yards to 151 — it’s important to get to the “why?” residing at the center of the “what the f—k?”
The Patriots still have seven games to play. Four of them are against the 8-1 Bills, the 7-2 Vikings, the 7-2 Chiefs, and the 7-2 Broncos. And they may not have Maye for any of them.
The chances of a playoff berth are slipping away. A winning record may not happen, either. When the season ends, the powers-that-be will have to decide whether they believe that things will be any better in 2027.

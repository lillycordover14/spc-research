ACC seeks Interpol red notices against Hasina, Joy, Putul, Tulip

Anti-graft watchdog also freezes assets worth Tk 1,062 crore, traces Joy's assets in the US and seeks details from American authorities

•

                Updated : 17 November 2026, 18:19

The Anti-Corruption Commission (ACC) has sought Interpol red notices against ousted prime minister Sheikh Hasina, her son Sajeeb Wazed Joy, daughter Saima Wazed Putul and niece Tulip Siddiq in connection with corruption cases.
ACC Secretary Md Saidur Rahman Khan disclosed the move while speaking to reporters today.

The commission has also frozen movable and immovable assets worth Tk 1,062 crore linked to the cases. It has additionally traced assets belonging to Joy in the United States and sought details from the relevant US authorities through a Mutual Legal Assistance Request (MLAR).
Saidur said 24 corruption cases had so far been filed against Hasina and her family members. Investigations into 17 cases are ongoing, while charge sheets have been submitted to courts in seven. Verdicts have also been delivered in six cases relating to plot allocations in Purbachal.
According to ACC data, the frozen assets include 60 katha of land in Purbachal, a house in Baridhara, nine upscale flats and 204.40 decimals of land.
The commission has also imposed travel restrictions on 15 individuals linked to the cases.
Regarding efforts to bring the accused back from abroad, the ACC said applications for Interpol red notices were submitted through the home and foreign ministries.
The objective is to facilitate the return of the individuals concerned through legal procedures. However, the commission could not specify when they might be brought back to Bangladesh.
Asked about the move against Tulip Siddiq, a British MP and former minister in the UK government, Saidur said the commission had submitted the application following its own procedures. Further steps would depend on the home ministry and the relevant Interpol authorities.
Meanwhile, the ACC has received 19 complaints against Hasina and her family members. Of these, seven have been resolved through the filing of cases, while inquiries or investigations into the remaining 12 are ongoing.
An ACC official said some of the complaints could be split into separate cases. The commission has preliminarily identified allegations that could lead to another 37 cases.

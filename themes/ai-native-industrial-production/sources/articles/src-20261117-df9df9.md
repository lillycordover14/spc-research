Dog Unit have made an album that sounds like four musicians were handed the keys to a spaceship, a collection of vintage synthesizers and absolutely no instructions about where to go. Their second full-length record, ‘Runners’, is out now through Brace Yourself Records, delivering 42 minutes of gloriously inventive instrumental rock that moves between hypnotic grooves, cinematic melodies, psychedelic adventures and moments of genuine beauty. The London quartet are also taking their wonderfully unpredictable musical universe around the UK in early 2027, including a special hometown performance at London’s ICA on February 23. The really fun part is that Dog Unit don’t need a single lyric to tell a fantastic story.

Guitarists Sam Walton and Henry Scowcroft, bassist James Weaver and drummer Lucy Jamieson have developed a musical language built around repetition, melody and the thrilling possibilities of four musicians listening closely to one another. Their 2024 debut ‘At Home’ introduced an irresistible blend of post-rock, krautrock and indie experimentation, but ‘Runners’ finds the band expanding their ambitions in just about every direction imaginable.

And yes, somebody remembered to bring the runner beans.

In one of the most charming physical-release ideas around, Dog Unit have created a special vinyl-free Greensleeve Edition of ‘Runners’. Instead of a traditional record, fans receive the album’s photography and liner notes, a high-resolution download code and a packet of runner beans to plant. It’s a playful response to concerns about unused vinyl and the environmental impact of record manufacturing, giving listeners an opportunity to grow something while enjoying the music.

A band that can turn an album title into a gardening opportunity deserves some serious applause.

Musically, ‘Runners’ is every bit as imaginative. The album was recorded during the first four months of 2026 at Dog Unit’s studio in Limehouse, East London, with longtime producer Mark Estall. This time the group welcomed a string quartet, brought back experimental bass clarinetist Heather Roche and filled the studio with vintage synthesizers, drum machines, pianos and a harmonium. The expanded instrumentation opens up an enormous range of textures while preserving the tight rhythmic chemistry that makes Dog Unit so much fun.

“Tiny Diversions” provides a beautiful introduction, balancing the group’s propulsive guitar-bass-drums foundation with delicate strings and bubbling synthesizers. Its simple, emotionally engaging melody gradually reveals the broader musical possibilities of the album, making it a natural connection between ‘At Home’ and this exciting new chapter.

Then “Everybody Has a Favourite Pin” arrives and throws a musical party with absolutely no respect for genre boundaries.

The track begins with an irresistible indie-disco groove before wandering through jazz-inspired detours, pumping electronic bass and an exhilarating double-drummer space-rock finale. By the time the music settles into chiming guitars and twinkling synthesizers, it’s travelled through enough musical territory to justify its own passport. Dog Unit make these adventurous turns feel joyful and spontaneous, allowing their individual instrumental personalities to shine throughout.

“The Knowledge” takes a different approach, offering something close to an instrumental pop song. Multiple melodies weave through its four-minute runtime, allowing listeners to focus on different musical threads with each replay. It’s a wonderfully clever piece of songwriting inspired by the intricate navigation skills of London cab drivers, with the music finding several different routes through the same fascinating landscape.

Across the remaining tracks, the band explore shimmering jangle pop, atmospheric drones, intricate rhythmic patterns and miniature sonic adventures. The album is designed to be experienced as a complete 42-minute journey, with each composition contributing to the larger picture. It’s an approach that rewards uninterrupted listening, particularly for anyone who loves discovering fresh details each time a favourite record plays.

What makes Dog Unit so appealing is their ability to combine extraordinary musical precision with a genuine sense of play. Their arrangements can sound beautifully intricate, yet there’s always a human warmth beneath the machinery. The rhythms make you move, the melodies give you something to hold onto, and the unexpected twists keep your imagination wonderfully busy.

The band’s instrumental storytelling has already earned enthusiastic praise from MOJO, The Times and Radio X, with John Kennedy calling them “One of the best bands on the planet right now.” It’s easy to understand why such imaginative music has inspired such affection.

Fans will get to experience that creative chemistry firsthand during the band’s 2027 UK tour, which opens February 23 at London’s ICA before visiting Brighton, Norwich, Ramsgate, Oxford, Manchester, Edinburgh and Bristol. It’s a terrific opportunity to hear these intricate recordings transformed into the energy and spontaneity of a live performance.

‘Runners’ is available digitally, on CD and vinyl, alongside the inventive Greensleeve Edition and a special ice-blue Dinked Edition featuring an art print and bonus seven-inch record of synth-and-strings re-edits. Whatever format listeners choose, Dog Unit have delivered a richly imaginative collection that celebrates the endless possibilities of instrumental music. Put the album on, follow the rhythms wherever they lead and enjoy the ride. These four musicians have built a magnificent little universe, and everybody’s invited to explore it.

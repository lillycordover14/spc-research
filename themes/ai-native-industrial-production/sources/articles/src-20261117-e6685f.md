TAMPA BREAKS PAVING RECORD, AGAIN
For the second year in a row, the City's two paving crews, along with some contractor help, has broken the record for lane-miles paved in a calendar year. 
As of October, the City has paved at least 
86 lane-miles
 (and counting) across Tampa, surpassing 2025's record of 82.
(A lane-mile is a standard industry-term that refers to one mile of a single traffic lane. For example: A one-mile stretch of a two-lane road equals two lane-miles)
Currently, City paving teams are working on two neighborhood projects: Riverside Heights Phase 2 and West Riverside Heights Phase 2, which together will add another 10 lane-miles to the total. 
While these totals represent just a portion of the road work still ahead, they highlight the City of Tampa's continued commitment to smoothing out it's roads.
This is the new bar going forward. 
Thanks to investments by the Tampa City Council, the Mobility Department was able to launch a second in-house paving team in 2025 along with with new equipment, which doubled our paving capacity. By handling much of the work in-house, residents are saving up to 50% versus hiring outside contractors. 
The City's internal crews deserve a lion-share of the credit. Paving is a tough job. It's labor intensive and requires precision, planning, and execution, often in the Florida heat and under tight deadlines. We're proud of their efforts everyday to improve the communities we serve.
"Every day, we're working to improve the quality of life for Tampa residents, and that starts with the roads and infrastructure our neighbors depend on," s
aid Mayor Jane Castor. 
"I'm incredibly proud of our teams breaking paving records two years in a row, and we're not slowing down. We've set a new standard for what can be accomplished, and it is my hope that future administrations continue building on our achievements...our residents deserve it!"
Since 2019, the City has paved more than 
390 lane-miles
 across Tampa. 
And the work continues. Planning is already underway for next year's paving projects, with work expected in neighborhoods like: North Tampa, Old Seminole Heights, Riverside Heights, Westshore Palms and more.

Kinsus will expand NVIDIA-dedicated substrate capacity more than 3.5 times current levels by 2028.
SPIL's packaging deal adds turnkey bumping interposer assembly and testing capacity dedicated to NVIDIA by mid-2029.
Vera Rubin shipments are already ramping while Kinsus and SPIL prepare capacity for later platform ramps.
 

NVIDIA announced
 on November 17, 2026, new and amended supply agreements with Kinsus, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Siliconware Precision Industries, including a Kinsus plan to expand NVIDIA-dedicated substrate capacity by more than 3.5 times current levels and expanded advanced-packaging operations in Taiwan for AI infrastructure platforms.
Substrate Capacity and Factory Upgrades
Under the new multiyear agreement, Kinsus will expand advanced ABF package-substrate manufacturing at its Youth campus in Taoyuan, Taiwan. Kinsus expects production to begin in the second half of 2027 and is targeting the additional capacity for the third quarter of 2028. CEO Scott Chen said the agreement builds on a supplier relationship with NVIDIA that has lasted more than a decade.
The Samsung Electro-Mechanics amendment focuses on existing factories, improving their operational capabilities to support increased production of NVIDIA’s accelerated-computing platforms. It builds on the companies’ multiyear substrate-manufacturing collaboration in Sejong and Busan, South Korea, which supplies NVIDIA’s compute and high-speed networking technologies.
Advanced Packaging and Earlier Commitments
NVIDIA and SPIL are expanding advanced-packaging operations in Taiwan under a separate multiyear agreement supported by multibillion-dollar NVIDIA funding. The companies aim to increase contracted advanced-packaging capacity dedicated to NVIDIA by mid-2029. Planned production lines will provide turnkey support spanning bumping, interposer wafer fabrication, assembly and testing.
The SPIL packaging project is distinct from the optical-engine expansion in 
NVIDIA’s October 21, 2026 supply-chain announcement
. That package targeted a 15-fold increase in SPIL’s optical-engine capacity for co-packaged optics by mid-2027 and an aggregate 2.5-fold substrate-capacity increase over three years. The new terms add a dedicated Kinsus expansion and factory-specific Samsung Electro-Mechanics changes to that broader buildout.
Amkor Technology’s previously announced financing provides a comparison for the SPIL funding structure. Its 
July 23, 2026 announcement
 covered a $1.5 billion multiyear agreement with an NVIDIA prepayment supporting expanded U.S. advanced-packaging capacity in Arizona and development work on high-density interconnects and heterogeneous integration. An 
October announcement
 added a $1.3 billion South Korea agreement; Amkor stated that the Arizona and South Korea multiyear agreements total $3 billion.
Vera Rubin Shipments and System Production
NVIDIA said Vera Rubin NVL72 and Vera CPUs began volume shipments in the third quarter, using TSMC’s 5nm and 3nm process technologies and CoWoS advanced packaging. TSMC senior vice president and deputy co-COO Kevin Zhang said the collaboration allows the companies to plan future technology requirements and prepare manufacturing capabilities as NVIDIA introduces new platforms.
Foxconn chairman Young Liu said the manufacturer is deepening its collaboration with NVIDIA across next-generation GPU and networking platforms, drawing on a production network across Asia, North America and Europe to support the volume ramp of next-generation AI infrastructure.
The practical effect is a staggered buildout: TSMC is supporting the current 
Vera Rubin
 shipment milestone, while Kinsus and SPIL are preparing additional capacity for later platform ramps.

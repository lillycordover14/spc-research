Maxence Melo Wins Netherlands Human Rights Embassy Tulip Award

Dar es Salaam
 – Maxence Melo, Executive Director of Jamii Africa and founder of JamiiForums, has won the 2026 Netherlands Human Rights Embassy Tulip Award in Tanzania in recognition of his contribution to advancing freedom of expression and protecting digital rights.

The award recognises Melo’s work in creating platforms for public debate, promoting access to information and defending digital rights through JamiiForums, one of Africa’s leading Swahili-language digital platforms.

Announcing 
the winner, the Ambassador of the Kingdom of the Netherlands to Tanzania, Marjo Crompvoets, said Melo’s work had made a significant contribution to strengthening freedom of expression and creating opportunities for citizens to participate in public discussions.

He was also recognised for his contribution to policy reforms, particularly his leadership in a coalition that advocated for the enactment of Tanzania’s Personal Data Protection Act, a key piece of legislation aimed at safeguarding individuals’ personal information.

Speaking about the selection process, Chikulupa Kasaka, a Senior Policy Officer for Governance at the Embassy of the Kingdom of the Netherlands in Tanzania, said nominations were received from five priority areas identified by the Dutch government in its human rights agenda.

These included freedom of expression and freedom of the media; freedom of religion and belief; the protection of human rights defenders and civic space; accountability for human rights violations and other crimes that often go unpunished; and the protection of the rights of women and children.

Kasaka said the award was based not only on the areas of human rights work in which nominees were involved but also on the tangible difference their efforts had made in society.

She explained that the selection process placed particular emphasis on initiatives that had produced positive social impact while incorporating technology, innovation and sustainability into efforts to advance and protect human rights.

The Netherlands Human Rights Tulip Award is presented annually by Dutch embassies in approximately 26 countries to individuals and organisations whose work has made a significant contribution to promoting and protecting human rights.

As the Tanzania winner, Melo will receive a bronze tulip trophy, a cash prize of 5,000 euros, as well as opportunities for networking, professional training and media exposure.

His recognition also extends beyond the national level, as he has been shortlisted among five outstanding candidates worldwide for the final round of the international Human Rights Tulip Award.

The global award ceremony is scheduled to take place on December 17, 2026, in The Hague, the Netherlands, bringing together the five finalists in recognition of their contributions to human rights.

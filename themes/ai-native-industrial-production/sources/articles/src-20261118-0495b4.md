"Win an eight-period game in Pittsburgh in the playoffs, five overtimes (in 2000). But this is better just because it's your kid, and we're all so proud of our children."

Tyler's next game will fulfill one of his father's hockey dreams.

The Senators are home against the Philadelphia Flyers on Thursday (7 p.m. ET; RDS2, TSN5, NBCSP). Brian will be in the press box at Canadian TireÂ Centre calling the game with Jim Jackson for the Flyers' local broadcast on NBC Sports Philadelphia.

Tyler is expected to be in the lineup against Philadelphia, one of his father's former teams. He was selected by the FlyersÂ in the first round (No. 22) of the 1995 NHL Draft and he spent six of his 13 NHL seasons with the team.

"I mean, I'm going to try to keep it as normal as possible, treat him like another player, but I am going to be keeping a close eye on him for sure," said Brian, who is in Utah as part of the TNT broadcast team for the Colorado Avalanche-Utah Mammoth game (10 p.m. ET; HBO MAX, TNT, ALT). "I've got to be honest with you, I've dreamt about this moment since he's been drafted. For me personally, I don't know about for him, but since I got into this side of the game I've always been like, 'Man, it would be so cool to call his games.' It would be really meaningful to me. I've never told him that because I've never wanted to put pressure on him, but for me personally it's going to be a special moment and I'm looking forward to it if it does happen."

Brian, who typically is between the benches when calling national games for TNT, joked that being up in the booth with Jackson will be better for him Thursday.

"I like to think I'm a calm hockey dad, but my buddies will tell me that I'm a maniac just like every other hockey dad, so, yeah, better to be upstairs so he doesn't see my facial reactions," Brian said. "So, I'll be upstairs and he won't see my reactions if he makes a boneheaded play or something like that."

Even if that does happen, the fact that Tyler is doing it in the NHL is all that matters considering everything he has gone through to get here, a path that had him emotional and nearly in tears as he discussed it prior to making his NHL debut.

He had an MCL injury, COVID, pneumonia and finally another knee injury that resulted in season-ending surgery in 2020-21, when he was playing with the USA Hockey National Team Development Program.

He separated his shoulder in his first game with Ottawa of the Ontario Hockey League in 2021-22 and missed nearly a month of action.

The following season, when playing for the United States at the 2023 IIHF World Junior Championship, Tyler sustained a right shoulder injury that required surgery.

He hurt his groin before going to his first training camp with the Senators in 2023 and missed two months. Later that season, while playing with Belleville in the American Hockey League, he injured his left shoulder in a fight and had to have another surgery.

He had a knee operation to clean out debris following the 2024-25 season and last season he missed time because of a shoulder separation.

"It's just unbelievable the stuff that has happened to him," Brian Boucher said. "It's been non-stop. He's only played 132 games in the American league and he's in his fourth year. Two full seasons would be 144 games. He hasn't even done that."

But he has one in the NHL now, and a game-winning goal to show for it.

Mom, dad and sister were in the crowd for it, celebrating together during the game and with Tyler when it was over.

Brian, in fact, went down to the Senators dressing room after the game to take a picture with Tyler and forward 
Claude Giroux
. Brian played with Giroux in Philadelphia. Now he has Tyler as a teammate in Ottawa.

He will again Thursday against his father's and Giroux's former team.

Brian will be there in a working capacity for the Flyers, but sticking his chest out as the proud hockey dad he is, quietly rooting for Ottawa's No. 54 to score again.

"He texted me today and he said he hardly slept a wink last night," Brian said. "I told him me too, I couldn't sleep at all. He just said he couldn't stop thinking about it. So, he's excited. I know deep down inside it feels good for him. So proud of him."

Raytheon awarded Ursa Major and Avio USA a joint multi-year contract to develop a Tomahawk solid rocket motor booster.
Qualifying the jointly designed booster is the decisive commercial gate before any future production orders materialize.
Avio USA's planned Virginia factory is scheduled to begin production in early 2029 after construction finishes in late 2028.
 

Raytheon, an RTX business, has awarded Ursa Major and Avio USA a joint multi-year contract to design, develop and qualify a new solid rocket motor booster for the Raytheon-produced Tomahawk cruise missile, 
Avio USA announced
 on November 18, 2026. The award creates a pathway for both companies to become qualified U.S. suppliers for the U.S. Navy program.

The work is a development-and-qualification effort. The companies intend to qualify their jointly designed booster and demonstrate eligibility for future production orders, making supplier qualification the next commercial gate for the program.

Raytheon Naval Power president Sam Deneke said dual-sourcing the component with Avio USA and Ursa Major is intended to strengthen the supply chain, encourage innovation and build capacity for Navy requirements. The broader aim is to expand competition and production flexibility, improve resilience and help meet growing military demand for munitions.

Complementary Propulsion Capabilities

The partnership pairs Avio’s solid rocket propulsion heritage, manufacturing expertise and investment in next-generation U.S. production with Ursa Major’s design approach and advanced manufacturing processes. It also builds on Ursa Major’s expanding solid rocket motor portfolio and investment in manufacturing capacity, including propulsion systems across multiple tactical missile programs.

Avio USA is headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, and is a subsidiary of Avio S.p.A. It is governed by a U.S.-based board and structured to comply with U.S. security and export-control regulations. That structure is relevant to an award intended to establish qualified domestic suppliers.

For Avio USA, the selection also supports its planned manufacturing buildout. CEO James Syring called the award “a significant step forward for our planned U.S. manufacturing facility,” which he said the company is building as a long-term asset for the U.S. defense industrial base.

Virginia Factory Extends the Capacity Plan

The company 
broke ground on the Hurt, Virginia, facility
 on September 29, 2026. Avio USA expects the approximately 900,000-square-foot plant at the Southern Virginia Multimodal Park to have the capacity to produce thousands of solid rocket motors annually for U.S. tactical missile programs and to create roughly 1,500 technical jobs in Southern Virginia.

HITT Contracting is the general contractor. Avio USA expects construction to finish by late 2028 and production to begin in early 2029. That schedule places the factory’s contribution in the longer-term capacity buildout, while the 
Tomahawk
 work begins with design, development and qualification.

Avio USA is developing the site for solid rocket motor requirements across the U.S. defense industry, including Lockheed Martin 
(
LMT
 
)
 and 
Raytheon
 programs. At the groundbreaking, Syring said: “Our goal is to be an independent, open-source merchant supplier, able to support multiple customers and programs and provide major defense primes with an additional source for this critical capability.”

Qualification is the decisive commercial milestone for the joint team. It will determine whether the new booster can move toward future production orders and give Raytheon another domestic sourcing option for Tomahawk propulsion, connecting the development contract to the companies’ broader manufacturing investments.

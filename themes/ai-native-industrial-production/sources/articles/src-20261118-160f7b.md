Selling pressure on AI concept stocks has kept widening since early October, with the ABF substrate group becoming the worst-hit segment of the market. Unimicron Technology (3037.TW), Nan Ya PCB (8046.TW) and Kinsus Interconnect Technology (3189.TW) each locked limit-down for a second consecutive session, while Zhen Ding Technology Holding (4958.TW) also plunged. All four stocks lost their annual moving average lines, with swing declines exceeding 45%; Unimicron has halved from its peak. Liao Wan-ting, president of President Capital Management, believes the market has over-amplified policy uncertainty, inflicting a "Davis double-kill" on the sector's valuations. But the industry trend of AI specification upgrades is unchanged, and on next year's earnings the stocks have fallen into relatively cheap territory and should still rebound — though the pattern of gains will differ from the past.

Comparing Nov. 17 closing prices with historical highs, the correction in the four substrate stocks is striking. Kinsus, still trading limit-down at NT$542 after emerging from disposition, gapped down and broke its annual line, down 52.87% from its NT$1,150 peak. Nan Ya PCB also fell limit-down to NT$792, down 49.23% from NT$1,560. Unimicron dropped to NT$637, down 55.14% from NT$1,420 — the deepest correction among the four. Zhen Ding hit an intraday low of NT$361.5, down 45.14% from its NT$659 peak, also testing annual-line support.

Speaking on a program, Liao said the core reason for the recent slump is that the market is cutting both earnings expectations and valuations at the same time. Over the past two years, the AI supply chain enjoyed a "Davis double-play" as earnings were revised up and price-to-earnings ratios stretched to as much as 30x. But with capital expenditure growth slowing from its peak, U.S. policy uncertainty rising after the midterm elections, and doubts over a "post-Jensen Huang era" triggered by Nvidia founder Jensen Huang's resignation on Nov. 12 as president, CEO and director — with CFO Colette Kress serving as interim CEO and a successor search underway — the market began slashing valuations sharply, producing a "Davis double-kill" of simultaneous EPS and valuation cuts.

Still, the correction has not changed the direction of industry upgrades. Liao said global data centers and AI infrastructure face power bottlenecks, and chipmakers and cloud service providers (CSPs) increasingly focus on computing efficiency per unit of power (tokens per watt). That is accelerating the retirement of older chips and pushing AI systems toward a "large-package era" with bigger die areas and higher technical barriers. Larger die sizes, integration of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and the future development of co-packaged optics (CPO) will all drive simultaneous expansion in substrate layer counts and area, making ABF substrates and copper-clad laminate (CCL) core beneficiaries of the specification upgrade. PCB research firm Prismark estimates the global substrate market will grow from $14.9 billion in 2025 to $40.2 billion in 2030, and expects the supply-demand gap to widen further after 2027. Some foreign brokerages estimate the global AI ABF substrate market will reach $13 billion in 2027 and $18 billion in 2028, growing 100% and 41% year over year, respectively.

On individual company positioning, Liao noted that ABF is an indispensable key material for packaging AI main chips such as GPUs and ASICs. Unimicron and Nan Ya PCB have higher ABF revenue shares and can directly capture the benefits of packaging specification upgrades. Kinsus is mainly focused on BT substrates but also has ABF capacity, and plans to prioritize the introduction of new ceramic substrate materials in 2028. As for the next stage — glass substrates — Taiwanese makers are currently focused on the more technically demanding build-up and lamination processes, with related products expected to roll out starting in 2029.

On CCL, she is particularly bullish on global leader Elite Material (2383.TW). The company specializes in low-signal-loss LoDK and LoDK2 high-end materials. Substrate-grade CCL was previously almost exclusively dominated by Japanese suppliers; Elite Material is expected to formally enter the substrate CCL market next year, potentially opening an entirely new second growth curve.

Notably, there are no clear signals yet of weakening demand at the upstream materials end. Japanese materials giant Ajinomoto said on Nov. 9 that ABF sales for high-performance applications such as AI, servers and networking remain strong, with an improved product mix, and it raised its full-year forecast for its functional materials business for the second time this year: first-half revenue for the business rose 63% year over year and operating profit expanded to 181% of the year-earlier level. The company lifted its group net profit forecast by ¥11.5 billion (approximately $72.2 million) to ¥135 billion (approximately $847.2 million), on track for a second straight record year. Ajinomoto also plans to invest about ¥21.5 billion (approximately $134.9 million) in a new varnish plant at its Gunma site, with mass production targeted for fiscal 2030; until then it will expand supply by adding shifts. Ajinomoto supplies ABF build-up insulating film, while Unimicron, Nan Ya PCB, Kinsus and Japan's Ibiden sit in the substrate manufacturing segment, so upstream material sales are seen as an important gauge of high-end substrate demand.

The supply side also shows no sign of loosening. Unimicron's capital expenditure this year totals NT$53.7 billion (approximately $1.6 billion), and it estimates ABF capacity can increase about 30% by 2027. Nan Ya PCB's capital spending this year is NT$46.8 billion (approximately $1.4 billion), a record high for the company; it has won new server CPU substrate orders from AMD, with its Shulin plant undergoing certification, and AMD-related revenue could account for more than 20% next year. Kinsus has raised capital expenditure twice this year and won large high-end ABF substrate orders from Nvidia and AMD. Zhen Ding's ABF capacity for 2027-2028 is already fully sold out. Supply chain sources say AI demand will not disappear because of a single corporate leadership change; cloud providers' existing orders and capacity allocations were mostly planned in advance, and Nvidia has already made capacity prepayments to major substrate makers including Ibiden, SEMCO and Unimicron, showing end customers are actively locking in capacity ahead of time. At this stage, the key raw material supply matters more than order losses. Some foreign brokerages also argue the AI capital expenditure cycle remains resilient, and that the market's focus will shift from the scale of cloud providers' investment to whether AI applications can generate real investment returns.

There is still noise in the market about new platform progress. Industry sources say supply of HBM4 memory for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform has hit a bottleneck, and the ramp originally slated for year-end may be delayed to the first half of 2027. GB300 servers remain the mainstay for now, which could in turn affect the growth pace of 800VDC power supplies, M9 high-end substrate materials and optical communication components.

Looking ahead, Liao believes market pessimism reached a relatively extreme level in early November, and expects U.S. related review policies to gradually become clearer from December through the first quarter of next year. Measured against next year's profitability, Unimicron, Nan Ya PCB, Kinsus and Elite Material all have long-term specification-upgrade themes, and their share prices have fallen to "very cheap and relatively sweet" levels — a rebound is certain. But she cautioned that with valuation corrections and a prolonged policy review period, the sector will no longer surge in one breath as before, but shift to a "jagged, wide-range oscillation upward," and investors should watch for volatility along the way.

Overall, the share-price correction in the four substrate leaders reflects not only demand concerns but a repricing of the market's high-growth expectations for AI. As investors begin to examine AI investment returns, the ramp of new platforms and the benefits of capacity expansion, whether substrate makers can convert strong demand into actual orders and profits will be the key focus of the next stage.

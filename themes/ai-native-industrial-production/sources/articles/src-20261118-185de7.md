The Group Acquires Remaining SoftBank Stake of Boston Dynamics Along with Selling RAI Stake to SoftBank While Atlas Begins Tests with GlobalFoundries, First Semiconductor Customer

Boston Dynamics’ humanoid robot Atlas (right) and quadruped robot Spot (left). (Photo courtesy of Hyundai Motor Group)

Hyundai Motor Group has completed its business reorganization centered on commercial robots by securing a 100% stake in its United States robot subsidiary Boston Dynamics and selling its stake in an advanced research enterprise.

In addition, Boston Dynamics has begun expanding the application area of Atlas, a humanoid robot set for mass production in 2028, by welcoming the United States semiconductor company GlobalFoundries as its first non-automotive customer.

According to the automotive industry on Nov. 18, Hyundai Motor Group Chairman Euisun Chung, HMG Global, and Hyundai Glovis recently completed the acquisition of the remaining 9.65% stake in Boston Dynamics held by Japan’s SoftBank. Consequently, Chairman Chung and the group’s affiliates now hold a 100% stake in Boston Dynamics. HMG Global is an investment corporation funded by Hyundai Motor, Kia, and Hyundai Mobis.

Along with this, the stake in the Robotics and AI Institute (RAI), a robot and artificial intelligence (AI) research enterprise operated by Hyundai Motor Group affiliates, was also sold to SoftBank.

The group has established a structure that focuses on the mass production and commercialization of Atlas by clearing its equity investments in separate research enterprises and increasing its dominance over Boston Dynamics, which develops and sells commercial products.

RAI is a separate research enterprise established in 2022, the year following Hyundai Motor Group’s acquisition of Boston Dynamics’ management rights. Led by Boston Dynamics founder Marc Raibert, it has been researching advanced technologies necessary for robot cognition, judgment, and movement.

In conjunction with the business reorganization, securing external customers for Atlas is also showing progress. Boston Dynamics announced on November 17 (local time) that it will pursue Atlas field tests and the expanded application of the quadruped robot ‘Spot’ with semiconductor contract manufacturing enterprise GlobalFoundries. GlobalFoundries is the first customer to verify Atlas in a non-automotive industry and the first semiconductor manufacturing enterprise to participate in the Atlas adoption program.

Starting early next year, the two companies will test the field application feasibility of Atlas at major GlobalFoundries business sites. They plan to discover tasks that humanoids can handle in semiconductor manufacturing sites and verify the customer’s requirements and equipment connection conditions. Initial learning and verification to perform specific tasks will also be conducted.

Spot will be expanded and deployed to GlobalFoundries’ global production facilities in New York and Vermont in the United States, Dresden in Germany, and Singapore. GlobalFoundries has already operated Spot at its Vermont plant for over 4 years, and it was also deployed in Singapore in April of this year, moving about 900 km and performing over 90,000 inspections in the auxiliary facility space beneath the production facility.

Pradip Singh, Chief Manufacturing Officer (CMO) of GlobalFoundries, stated, “The expanded application of Spot and the Atlas test are the next step toward advancing to artificial intelligence (AI)-based manufacturing.”

Preparation for Atlas production by Hyundai Motor Group affiliates is also in full swing. Hyundai AutoEver included a plan in its third quarter report to pursue the ‘development of a Robot Manufacturing Execution System (MES)’ from this month until April next year. The purpose is to reduce the workload and time required when applying the manufacturing system to a new robot factory and to secure the manufacturing system technology necessary for the group’s robot business in advance.

MES is a system that manages work orders, process progress, and quality and production results at a production site. Going beyond developing robots, it is a task to prepare the foundation for consistent, repeatable production with uniform quality.

Hyundai Motor Group plans to establish an annual mass production system for Atlas in the United States in 2028 and increase its annual maximum production capacity to 30,000 units by 2030. Along with mass production, the goal is to deploy it to the manufacturing process of the Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America (HMGMA) in Georgia, United States, and also to other automobile factories or non-automotive industrial factories. The Robotics Metaplant Applied Center (RMAC) within HMGMA has already started learning and verifying the tasks necessary for actual manufacturing sites.

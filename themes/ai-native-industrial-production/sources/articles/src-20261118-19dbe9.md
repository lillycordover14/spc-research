SAN DIEGO, Nov. 18, 2026 
(GLOBE NEWSWIRE)
 -- Iambic (Nasdaq: IAM), a clinical-stage life sciences and technology company developing novel medicines using its AI-driven platform, today announced two preclinical poster presentations at the 38th EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium on Molecular Targets and Cancer Therapeutics (ENA 2026), known as the Triple Meeting, taking place November 18–20, 2026 in Barcelona.
 
 
The posters present preclinical data for IAM-C1, a selective dual CDK2/4 inhibitor in development for HR+/HER2- breast cancer and CCNE1-amplified cancers, and IAM217, a brain-penetrant KIF18A inhibitor in development for cancers with chromosomal instability (CIN). Both wholly owned drug development candidates were designed using Iambic's molecular superintelligence platform, which integrates Enchant® and NeuralPLexer®, its flagship AI technologies for multimodal endpoint and protein-ligand structural predictions, with automated chemistry and biology experimentation.
 
 
 
 
 
"IAM-C1 and IAM217 are Iambic's second and third drug development candidates and demonstrate what our molecular superintelligence platform is designed to do: discover and advance differentiated molecules to address urgent unmet patient need," said Tom Miller, PhD, Co-Founder and CEO, Iambic. "For IAM-C1, it meant dual CDK2 and CDK4 activity while sparing other CDKs associated with dose-limiting toxicities. For IAM217, it meant selective KIF18A inhibition with brain penetrance. The preclinical data presented at ENA reflect both design goals, and we look forward to advancing these programs to and through clinic."
 
 
"Resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors remains a key limitation in HR+/HER2- breast cancer, and increased CDK2 signaling through cyclin E is one route to that resistance," said Pete Olson, PhD, Chief Scientific Officer, Iambic. "In our studies, IAM-C1 was active in models driven by CDK2, CDK4 or both, including a patient-derived model from a patient previously treated with abemaciclib and palbociclib. With IAM217, we saw selective activity against chromosomally unstable tumor cells in vitro and deep regressions in vivo, including in an intracranial model. We are encouraged by these preclinical data and look forward to submitting an IND for IAM-C1 later this year and initiating the Phase 1/2 trial for IAM217."

POSTER PRESENTATIONS
Title: IAM-C1, a Potent and Selective Dual CDK2/CDK4 Inhibitor, Demonstrates Robust Antitumor Activity and a Favorable Preclinical Tolerability Profile
 
Abstract: 234 | Poster: PB-129 | Session: Molecular Targeted Agents | November 19, 2026 | Exhibition Hall, CCIB| Presenter: Pete Olson, Iambic
Summary:
 
 
 
IAM-C1 inhibited CDK2/cyclin E1 and CDK4/cyclin D1 with biochemical IC50 values below 1 nM. Across a broad kinome panel at 30 nM, four non-mutant off-target kinases met the binding-hit criterion.
 
 
 
In cancer cell lines with distinct CDK dependencies (MCF-7, CDK4; OVCAR-3, CDK2; MFE-296, CDK2/CDK4), IAM-C1 suppressed RB phosphorylation (IC50 18–19 nM), inhibited proliferation (IC50 40–100 nM) and depleted S-phase cells.
 
 
 
As a single agent, IAM-C1 achieved tumor growth inhibition (TGI) of 138% in MCF-7, 97% in OVCAR-3 and 94% in MFE-296 xenograft models. In the CDK2/CDK4 co-dependent MFE-296 model, TGI with IAM-C1 exceeded that of single-agent CDK2, CDK4 and CDK4/6 inhibitors and was comparable to a combination of selective CDK2 and CDK4 inhibitors.In the ST4316B ER+/HER2- breast cancer patient-derived xenograft model, established from a patient previously treated with abemaciclib and palbociclib, IAM-C1 achieved 78% TGI versus 24% with palbociclib.
 
 
 
In the MCF-7 model, IAM-C1 combined with fulvestrant or imlunestrant produced significantly greater antitumor activity than either selective estrogen receptor degrader alone.
 
 
 
In rats, IAM-C1 reduced neutrophil counts by a mean of 13% at exposure multiples of 1.3–1.4x across two studies, compared with 48% for combined selective CDK2 and CDK4 inhibitors at 0.65–0.75x and 69% for palbociclib.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Title: IAM217: a Novel, Brain-penetrant KIF18A Inhibitor for Targeting Chromosomal Instability in Cancer
 
Abstract: 330 | Poster: PB-122 | Session: New Drugs | November 20, 2026 | Exhibition Hall, CCIB | Presenter: Jill Hallin, Iambic
Summary:
 
 
 
IAM217 inhibited growth of CIN-high OVCAR-3 ovarian cancer cells (IC50 24 nM) while sparing CIN-low MCF-7 cells (IC50 >10,000 nM) and bone marrow hematopoietic progenitor cells (IC50 7,700 nM to >10,000 nM). Anti-proliferative activity tracked with mitotic arrest.
 
 
 
IAM217 was negative in a mini-Ames assay, showed a clean kinesin and kinome selectivity screen, and achieved CNS exposure in rodents.
 
 
 
Once-daily oral dosing in ovarian cancer xenograft models produced dose-dependent plasma and tumor exposure that correlated with dose-dependent tumor growth inhibition and modulation of the mitotic marker phospho-histone H3. IAM217 was well tolerated at all dose levels tested.
 
 
 
At 60 mg/kg once daily for approximately 21 days, IAM217 showed antitumor activity across a panel of breast cancer cell line-derived and patient-derived xenograft models, as well as ovarian, colon and osteosarcoma models, with deep regressions in multiple models.
 
 
 
In an intracranial JIMT-1 breast cancer model of brain metastasis, IAM217 achieved approximately 90% regression. Unbound plasma and cerebrospinal fluid concentrations exceeded the free IC90 for more than 24 hours.
 
 
 
In vitro, IAM217 combined with eribulin showed synergy in Kuramochi (ovarian cancer) and HCC1187 (triple-negative breast cancer) cell lines.

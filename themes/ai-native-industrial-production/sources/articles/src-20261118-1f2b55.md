Northrop Grumman will 
bring
 its military aircraft systems expertise to 
Hermeus’ Quarterhorse program
, which is developing reusable drones designed to fly at supersonic and higher speeds.

Under a strategic teaming agreement, Northrop Grumman will design, develop, integrate, and sustain mission systems for Hermeus’ high-speed unmanned aircraft.

The work will cover sensing, communications, navigation, and command and control. Northrop Grumman also brings expertise in electronic warfare and autonomy.

The systems will be integrated with Hermeus’ mission management system, an open and modular architecture designed to keep flight-critical functions separate from mission systems. 

“Together, we’re giving the military a new operational edge by putting advanced mission kits on aircraft built to move beyond supersonic speeds,” 
Kyle Mimken
, program manager at Northrop Grumman, said.

Hermeus’ Quarterhorse Drone Program

Quarterhorse is a series of unmanned aircraft that Hermeus is developing to test technologies for high-speed flight, with the longer-term goal of achieving sustained hypersonic flight.

The program uses successive prototypes to test different technologies and gradually increase performance. Its Mk 2.1 aircraft reached Mach 1.21 during 
a May test flight
. Hermeus later 
reported
 that the aircraft reached Mach 1.6 on its fourth flight and deployed a demonstration payload on a subsequent sortie. 

The next prototype, Mk 2.2, 
began flight testing in September
. It features upgraded avionics, additional fuel capacity, and the company’s Gryphon propulsion system, which is intended to support speeds beyond Mach 2.

The program has received support from the Defense Innovation Unit (DIU). In May, Hermeus said a 
$159-million contract modification
 raised the total ceiling of its DIU agreement to $219 million for high-Mach flight and payload-release demonstrations in 2026 and 2027. The flight data will inform future military experimentation and acquisition decisions.

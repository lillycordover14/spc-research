Satire: I changed my mind, again. Winter is back
 

Deputy Opinion Editor Sadie Harlan has changed her mind about winter. Again.

 

 

 

 

By 

Sadie Harlan

 | 
November 18, 2026
, 
3:17 am 
 

 

Second-year history major David Bolotin applies sunscreen in front of a Christmas tree. (Maddie Callaghan | The Phoenix)
 

Last winter, I wrote a 
piece about my excitement
 for winter and the joy — and bitter cold — I was prepared to experience as a Southerner living through her first real winter outside of Georgia’s balmy weather. 

That November, I spoke at length about my childhood dream for more than the occasional flurry of snow, but later, 
in March
, I changed my mind. The harsh winter had gotten to me, and my mood was about as chilly and frostbitten as my fingers. 

However, after months spent enduring the humid Atlanta summer, and the notably less humid Chicago autumn, I’m ready for winter again.

Something about the holiday decorations bedecking homes and stores along my running route has once again sparked my holiday spirit and excitement for snow. Never mind that the runs in which I see those decorations have become increasingly shorter as my tolerance for the frigid weather wanes. 

I can almost smell the wintry scent of pine and frost in the air and hear the muffled holiday-adjacent music streaming from various restaurants and public areas. Yes, it really does seem like winter is back.

After last year’s disaster regarding my inability to stay warm, I’ve now found myself bundled in multiple layers, completely ready and willing to take on the seasonal sense of doom and despair — sorry, childlike excitement and joy — regarding winter. 

I’m particularly looking forward to the snow. I can’t wait to walk around Chicago’s warmly-lit streets holding a steaming mug of hot chocolate. I don’t really have shoes prepared for said walk in the snow, but I’m sure this won’t be an issue in any way, shape or form. 

I can also enjoy the wintry outdoors from within the comfort of my own dorm. I’ve recently noticed my room’s thin windows allow me to feel the Christmas spirit — and cold weather — even while I’m actively trying to avoid it. 

In last semester’s bitterly voiced article, I expressed particular distaste for Groundhog Day and Puxtaswany Phil’s evil sentence to six more weeks of winter. However, while sweating in the hot Southern sun this August, I realized I would do just about anything for even six hours of chilly weather. 

I also made it known I resented my plane’s snowy diversion to Romulus, Mich., without giving any thought to how the so-called catastrophe allowed me to explore an airport I’d never visited, and stay in an only somewhat seedy hotel overnight. 

See, former Sadie, there are many enjoyable aspects to the season, should one know where to look. In March, I willfully turned away from any form of snow-specked joy, but now I’m searching for flurries backlit by somber gray clouds. 

When the snow melts to slush, and the Christmas bells are no longer ringing, I’m positive I’ll mourn the holiday season, yet still manage to find some sort of excitement in the grayish sludge accumulating on the sidewalks. 

Hopefully. 

I’m just so excited to feel the stiffness in my fingers and the freezing burn of my ears should I neglect to wear a hat outside. All of winter’s imperfections are only a product of the parts I do like, the parts which make winter enjoyable. 

After last year, I can safely say winter is definitely in the top four of enjoyable seasons, neck and neck with delightful summer, serene spring and exceptional autumn. 

This winter, I take back every negative thing I’ve ever said about Chicago’s cold season and eagerly anticipate the first snowfall. I’ll be sure to remember this when I’m getting battered by freezing winds and enjoying a thoroughly not-depressing run on a treadmill due to the icy sidewalks.

 

Sadie Harlan

Sadie Harlan is the deputy opinion editor and a second-year molecular/cellular neuroscience major on the pre-med track. An Atlanta native, she enjoys re-reading books, running along the lake and soaking up sunshine while she still has the chance.

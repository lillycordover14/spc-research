Boston Dynamics Expands GlobalFoundries Partnership, Plans Atlas Testing in 2027&nbsp;&nbsp;AI Insider

[page text]
Credit: Boston Dynamics

Insider Brief

Boston Dynamics and GlobalFoundries are expanding Spot robot deployments across semiconductor manufacturing facilities in the U.S., Germany and Singapore, with Atlas humanoid testing planned for early 2027.

GlobalFoundries will become the first semiconductor manufacturer and the first customer outside the automotive industry to test Atlas through Boston Dynamics’ early adopter program.

At GlobalFoundries’ Singapore facilities, two Spot robots have completed more than 90,000 inspections since April, achieving a reported 99% mission success rate.

Boston Dynamics
 and 
GlobalFoundries
 are expanding their partnership to deploy Spot

 across the semiconductor manufacturer’s global factory network, with plans to begin testing the Atlas

According to Boston Dynamics
, the agreement covers GlobalFoundries facilities in Malta, New York; Burlington, Vermont; Dresden, Germany; and Singapore. GlobalFoundries will also become the first 
semiconductor
 manufacturer and the first customer outside the automotive industry to participate in Boston Dynamics’ Atlas early adopter program.

The companies have worked together for more than four years, beginning with Spot deployments at GlobalFoundries’ Burlington facility. The four-legged robots conduct automated inspections to monitor factory operations and identify potential maintenance problems.

In Singapore, two Spot robots have traveled nearly 900 kilometers and completed more than 90,000 inspections in areas beneath the manufacturing floor since April, achieving a 99% mission success rate, according to the companies.

Using updated hardware and software, GlobalFoundries also redeployed 875 routine inspections in two weeks and plans to more than double that number using Spot’s visual inspection capabilities.

“Spot has helped us shift from reactive to predictive maintenance, collecting data across thousands of inspection points to prevent downtime and keep our fabs running more efficiently,” noted GlobalFoundries chief manufacturing officer Pradip Singh. “Scaling Spot and testing Atlas are the next steps in our journey to AI-enabled manufacturing, putting physical AI to work across our operations worldwide.”

Beginning in early 2027, the companies plan to test Atlas, which is in early production, at GlobalFoundries facilities to identify potential applications for humanoid robots in semiconductor manufacturing.

Boston Dynamics pointed out that the program will include workshops to evaluate possible tasks, assess how Atlas could be integrated into existing operations and begin training the robot to perform specific jobs, plus complement GlobalFoundries’ wider program to train employees in AI.

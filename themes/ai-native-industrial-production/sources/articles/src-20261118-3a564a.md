Key Takeaways

        On November 16, the Senate Appropriations Committee released the remaining draft appropriations bills for FY 2027
        

        Congress has until December 11 to pass an appropriations package and avoid a government shutdown
        

On November 16, the U.S. Senate Appropriations Committee completed its release of draft appropriations text for all twelve Fiscal Year (FY) 2027 funding bills. Currently, the government is funded at FY 2026 levels through a Continuing Resolution (CR) (
H.R. 6500
 / 
P.L. 119-103
) that is set to expire on December 11. Congress will need to either pass the FY 2027 appropriations bills or temporarily extend federal funding at current levels before December 11 to prevent a government shutdown. 
Additionally, the CR includes a provision delaying the implementation of a proposed rule by the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) on Uniform Guidance that would vastly change federal grant administration if adopted as a final rule. Counties support extending the delay on implementation of the OMB rule until September 30, 2027, and urge Congress to include such language in the end of year appropriations package.
See NACo's Letter on County FY 2027 Funding Priorities
How could the bills affect counties?
The following draft appropriations bills released by the Senate Appropriations Committee include various funding proposals for key county programs and initiatives.

Includes $12 million for Farm and Ranch Stress Assistance Network (FRSAN)

What's ahead
The bills released by the Senate are drafts and have not been passed in subcommittees, the appropriations committee or the full Senate chamber. To fully fund the government through the appropriations process before the CR expires, the Senate would have to pass all twelve bills through the chamber, which would then move to the U.S. House of Representatives for passage before heading to the president’s desk for a signature. 
The House has already passed all 12 of its appropriations bills through the committee and passed three bills – 
Agriculture, Rural Development, Food and Drug Administration, and Related Agencies
; 
Military Construction, Veterans Affairs, and Related Agencies
 and 
National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs
 – through the whole chamber. Any differences between these bills and the ones passed in the Senate would also need to be reconciled. Congress has less than 10 days in session to either pass all appropriations bills or pass another temporary funding measure to avoid a government shutdown. 
Notably, the Senate’s draft appropriations bills do not include a further delay to OMB’s proposed rule. NACo 
sent 
a letter on November 13 to Senate leaders and appropriators asking them to include language in the next government funding measure to continue to delay the implementation of the OMB proposed rule until Sept. 30, 2027, as well as block federal agencies from implementing provisions of the rule until that date.

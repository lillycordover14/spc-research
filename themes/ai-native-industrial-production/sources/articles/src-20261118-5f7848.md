Nvidia Invests Over $1 Billion in Kinsus; Taoyuan ABF Substrate Capacity Dedicated to Nvidia to Expand 3.5-Fold
Add preferred source
On the 17th, Nvidia announced it had signed a multi-year agreement with Kinsus Interconnect Technology, injecting more than $1 billion to purchase equipment for the Taoyuan Youth plant, expanding advanced ABF substrate capacity dedicated exclusively to Nvidia to more than 3.5 times current levels, with production starting in the second half of 2027 and full capacity in place by the third quarter of 2028. Nvidia is simultaneously providing several billion dollars to support SPIL's expansion of advanced packaging capacity in Taiwan, and has amended its substrate supply agreement with Samsung Electro-Mechanics; Taiwan-based players including Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing, Hon Hai, Unimicron Technology and King Yuan Electronics are also part of the lineup. Nvidia also disclosed a blueprint to raise total substrate capacity to 2.5 times within three years and double testing capacity between 2026 and 2027. The Vera Rubin NVL72 and Vera CPU have already entered mass production and shipping in the third quarter.

Nvidia announced on the 17th (U.S. time) that it had signed a multi-year agreement with IC substrate maker Kinsus Interconnect Technology (3189.TW) and would directly inject more than $1 billion (about NT$33 billion) in cash to purchase equipment needed to expand capacity at Kinsus's Taoyuan Youth plant. This is the first time Kinsus has been named on Nvidia's official supplier list, and it also means Nvidia, with its CEO position vacant and its board still selecting a successor, has chosen to shore up confidence in the AI supply chain with a concrete funding commitment.

According to a joint statement issued by both sides, Kinsus plans to expand advanced ABF packaging substrate capacity at the Taoyuan Youth campus dedicated exclusively to Nvidia to more than 3.5 times the current level. The new lines are expected to begin production in the second half of 2027, with the additional capacity targeted to be fully in place by the third quarter of 2028. Nvidia said the agreement extends the two companies' existing supplier relationship and supports Nvidia's long-term supply planning for its accelerated computing platforms; for any shortfall, Nvidia will continue to participate in Kinsus's subsequent expansion plans, aiming to secure stable substrate capacity at least through 2030.

Kinsus Chief Executive Officer and President Chen Ho-hsu said: "We believe AI will be one of the most important technology transformations of our lifetime. After more than a decade of working with Nvidia, we look forward to continuing to build the future together." Debora Shoquist, Nvidia's executive vice president of operations, said: "Our supply chain is a strategic advantage." She said cooperation with these partners is built on the manufacturing foundation needed for long-term growth, expanding capacity in areas including components, advanced packaging and system production to deliver the accelerated computing platforms customers need.

The push is not limited to substrates. Nvidia also announced it had signed a multi-year agreement with SPIL, a subsidiary of ASE Technology Holding, under which Nvidia will provide several billion dollars in funding to support SPIL's expansion of advanced packaging operations in Taiwan. The expansion plan is expected to increase contractually agreed advanced packaging capacity dedicated exclusively to Nvidia by mid-2029, with new lines covering one-stop support from bumping and interposer wafer manufacturing to assembly and testing. SPIL Chief Executive Officer and Chairman Tsai Chi-wen said: "SPIL is proud to deepen its cooperation with Nvidia. This agreement enhances our ability to support next-generation packaging technologies and to deliver the performance and reliability required by future AI systems."

On the substrate side, Nvidia also amended its existing supply agreement with Samsung Electro-Mechanics to raise production capacity at its existing plants, extending the two companies' prior multi-year cooperation to expand substrate manufacturing in Sejong and Busan, South Korea. Samsung Electro-Mechanics Chief Executive Officer Chang Duck-hyun said the company will combine world-class fine-processing substrate technology with high-reliability MLCC technology so that Nvidia's AI platforms can be supplied to global markets on time.

Nvidia also disclosed a capacity expansion blueprint for each segment, with a timeline running from 2026 through 2029, forming a complete supply network spanning substrates, packaging, testing and system assembly.

On the wafer manufacturing and system side, Nvidia confirmed that the Vera Rubin NVL72 system and Vera CPU, aimed at agentic AI, entered mass production and shipping for the first time in the third quarter of this year, using Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing's (2330.TW) 5-nanometer and 3-nanometer processes and CoWoS advanced packaging technology. Chang Hsiao-chiang, senior vice president and co-chief operating officer of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing, said the two companies' long-term cooperation is built on semiconductor innovation, and this deep collaboration allows Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing to plan ahead for future technology needs and prepare manufacturing capacity in advance when Nvidia launches new platforms.

Hon Hai (2317.TW) Chairman Young Liu said Hon Hai continues to deepen cooperation with Nvidia on next-generation GPUs and networking platforms, and through an integrated global production network spanning Asia, North America and Europe is ready to support expanded production of next-generation AI infrastructure.

Notably, the supplier list also includes Unimicron Technology (3037.TW) and King Yuan Electronics (2449.TW), continuing cooperation plans announced in October. Unimicron Technology is expanding high-end IC substrate capacity; Chairman Chien Shan-chieh said the investment in advanced substrate capacity highlights the strength of the partnership. King Yuan Electronics, meanwhile, will extend its testing business to the United States; President Chang Kao-hsun said the move brings the company's capabilities closer to Nvidia's continuously expanding U.S. manufacturing supply chain.

In background, Nvidia only announced a round of expansion plans at the GTC conference in Germany on October 21, when it planned to raise substrate capacity to 2.5 times the original within three years and expected to double semiconductor testing capacity between 2026 and 2027. Less than a month later, Nvidia has added more investment amid management changes, effectively confirming to the market that its expansion roadmap has not changed course.

From an industry impact perspective, ABF substrates carry high-performance chips and connect chips to external circuits, making them an indispensable component of AI chips. As new AI platforms increase package area, substrate layer counts and structural complexity, substrate demand is growing exponentially. By directly participating in equipment investment with cash and locking in capacity from the second half of 2027 onward, Nvidia is on one hand reducing its own risk of high-end substrate supply chain disruption, and on the other hand giving Kinsus clear long-term production planning and financial support, helping it secure a core position in the AI supply chain amid competition with substrate makers such as Ibiden and Unimicron Technology. For investors, the agreement advances Nvidia's ties with Taiwan-based companies from "procurement" to "co-investment"; in the coming years, capacity utilization and capital expenditure pacing across Taiwan's substrate, packaging and testing sectors will track Nvidia's platform ramp-up timelines even more closely.

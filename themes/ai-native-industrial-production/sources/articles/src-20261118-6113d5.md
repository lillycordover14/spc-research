Babcock and Isembard sign MoU to find spare UK manufacturing capacity
Reindustrialising the UK has become a cardinal policy of the labour government. Success will likely come in many forms, and this MoU between Isembard and Babcock could help to unlock industrial capacity, supporting that policy goal. 

By Sam Cranny-Evans, editor of Calibre Defence, published on November 18, 2026.

Babcock International Group has signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) with British manufacturing scale-up Isembard to explore how to unlock manufacturing capacity across Babcock’s UK facilities, according to a November 18 
press release
.

Using real-time visibility of resource utilisation the two companies will assess how Isembard’s agentic manufacturing platform, MasonOS, could be deployed across Babcock’s operations to identify extra capacity, improve efficiency and increase production resilience, the press release states.

The MasonOS platform draws on data from production lines, machinery, workforce schedules and supply chains, and analyses available capacity against current and forecast demand.

A feasibility study is due to begin in early 2027, and it will also look for ways to release capacity that Babcock’s SME ecosystem and suppliers already supporting its critical programmes could use. The companies estimate that around £150 million in additional revenue could be generated between them by allocating available space.

Babcock’s global technology director, Andrew Munday, said the partnership has “the potential to identify and unlock manufacturing capacity across our UK operations”.

Alexander Fitzgerald, Isembard’s chief executive, said Babcock operates “some of the most important manufacturing sites supporting defence and national security”, and that MasonOS could help identify ways to increase capacity and improve utilisation.

Software-based operating systems are increasingly common in manufacturing. Tesla has a system that helps its production lines, for example, and Anduril has discussed the Arsenal OS, designed to run its Arsenal-1 hyperscale factories. All three systems provide slightly different functions, and Arsenal OS is not thought to be available outside of Anduril, but they do reflect a growing desire to increase supply chain visibility and efficiency.

Calibre Comment: Full utilisation vs reindustrialisation

The British government under Labour has repeatedly emphasised the goal of reindustrialising Britain. However, the UK is home to a range of engineering companies producing precision components for a variety of industries. They will often already have spare capacity or the knowledge needed to provide additional production bandwidth if required. Examples of this include 
Pearson Engineering
, which has invested £40 million in its Newcastle plant in the past four years, as well as 
Chasestead, which supports Fering
 in the production of its Pioneer X vehicles. However, even if the UK’s existing industry was better-utilised, it continues to face pressures such as very high energy costs, definitions of sovereignty that are considered too flexible, and high interest rates on borrowing.

Under Prime Minister Andy Burnham, Labour has pledged to address this, however, the industrial policies are taking time to fully emerge. The UK’s defence industrial landscape is also not without risk; many manufacturers are able to service different industries, but some are betting on defence for their survival. If the promised spending increase does not materialise, and if it does not get spent in the UK, some companies could find themselves in difficulties in the near future.

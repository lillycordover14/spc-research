Key Terms

ic50, patient-derived xenograft, mini-ames assay

3 terms

ic50

technical

"biochemical IC50 values below 1 nM."

IC50 is the concentration of a drug or compound needed to reduce a specific biological activity by half, commonly used to compare how potent experimental treatments are in laboratory tests. For investors, a lower IC50 often signals a stronger candidate that may require smaller doses, influence development costs, safety margins and competitive positioning, so it offers an early measure of technical promise though it does not guarantee clinical or commercial success.

patient-derived xenograft

medical

"breast cancer patient-derived xenograft model"

A patient-derived xenograft (PDX) is a laboratory model created by implanting a tumor or diseased tissue taken directly from a human patient into an animal host that can support its growth. Because PDX models tend to mirror how a human tumor behaves and responds to treatments better than simple cell cultures, investors view their use as a stronger signal that preclinical drug results may translate to patients, lowering development risk much like a full-scale dress rehearsal raises confidence before opening night.

mini-ames assay

technical

"IAM217 was negative in a mini-Ames assay"

A mini‑Ames assay is a scaled‑down, higher‑throughput version of the classic Ames bacterial reverse‑mutation test used to screen chemicals for mutagenic potential. It uses strains of Salmonella (or sometimes E. coli) that cannot grow without a specific mutation and measures whether exposure to a test substance (with or without a mammalian metabolic activation mix, e.g., S9) increases the number of colonies that revert to growth; an increase in “revertant” colonies indicates the substance caused DNA changes in the bacteria. Because it uses bacteria and a short exposure, it detects many direct‑acting mutagens efficiently but cannot capture all modes of genotoxicity in human cells and may require metabolic activation to reveal some pro‑mutagens.

SAN DIEGO, Nov.  18, 2026  (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Iambic (Nasdaq: 
IAM
), a clinical-stage life sciences and technology company developing novel medicines using its AI-driven platform, today announced two preclinical poster presentations at the 38th EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium on Molecular Targets and Cancer Therapeutics (ENA 2026), known as the Triple Meeting, taking place November 18–20, 2026 in Barcelona.
 
The posters present preclinical data for IAM-C1, a selective dual CDK2/4 inhibitor in development for HR+/HER2- breast cancer and CCNE1-amplified cancers, and IAM217, a brain-penetrant KIF18A inhibitor in development for cancers with chromosomal instability (CIN). Both wholly owned drug development candidates were designed using Iambic’s molecular superintelligence platform, which integrates Enchant® and NeuralPLexer®, its flagship AI technologies for multimodal endpoint and protein-ligand structural predictions, with automated chemistry and biology experimentation.
 
“IAM-C1 and IAM217 are Iambic’s second and third drug development candidates and demonstrate what our molecular superintelligence platform is designed to do: discover and advance differentiated molecules to address urgent unmet patient need,” said Tom Miller, PhD, Co-Founder and CEO, Iambic. “For IAM-C1, it meant dual CDK2 and CDK4 activity while sparing other CDKs associated with dose-limiting toxicities. For IAM217, it meant selective KIF18A inhibition with brain penetrance. The preclinical data presented at ENA reflect both design goals, and we look forward to advancing these programs to and through clinic.”
 
“Resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors remains a key limitation in HR+/HER2- breast cancer, and increased CDK2 signaling through cyclin E is one route to that resistance,” said Pete Olson, PhD, Chief Scientific Officer, Iambic. “In our studies, IAM-C1 was active in models driven by CDK2, CDK4 or both, including a patient-derived model from a patient previously treated with abemaciclib and palbociclib. With IAM217, we saw selective activity against chromosomally unstable tumor cells in vitro and deep regressions in vivo, including in an intracranial model. We are encouraged by these preclinical data and look forward to submitting an IND for IAM-C1 later this year and initiating the Phase 1/2 trial for IAM217.”
 
POSTER PRESENTATIONS
Title: IAM-C1, a Potent and Selective Dual CDK2/CDK4 Inhibitor, Demonstrates Robust Antitumor Activity and a Favorable Preclinical Tolerability Profile
 
Abstract: 234 | Poster: PB-129 | Session: Molecular Targeted Agents | November 19, 2026 | Exhibition Hall, CCIB| Presenter: Pete Olson, Iambic
Summary:
 
IAM-C1 inhibited CDK2/cyclin E1 and CDK4/cyclin D1 with biochemical IC50 values below 1 nM. Across a broad kinome panel at 30 nM, four non-mutant off-target kinases met the binding-hit criterion.
In cancer cell lines with distinct CDK dependencies (MCF-7, CDK4; OVCAR-3, CDK2; MFE-296, CDK2/CDK4), IAM-C1 suppressed RB phosphorylation (IC50 18–19 nM), inhibited proliferation (IC50 40–100 nM) and depleted S-phase cells.

In the ST4316B ER+/HER2- breast cancer patient-derived xenograft model, established from a patient previously treated with abemaciclib and palbociclib, IAM-C1 achieved

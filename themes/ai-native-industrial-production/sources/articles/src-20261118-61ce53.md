Benson Boone delivers vocal and instrumental mastery in concert of the year in Sydney&nbsp;&nbsp;Musicaltheatre.au

[page text]
Benson Boone has had a rapid rise to superstardom and after playing in Australia less than two years ago, he is back for his Wanted Man tour, this time in arenas. With three nights at Sydney’s Afterpay Arena, demand for the shows was high and the crowd was buzzing in anticipation.

Before Boone took to the stage, the crowd was treated to a set by Sydney-raised pop star Ruel, who was perhaps slightly off his game compared to some of his previous performances that we have reviewed. Eschewing some of his bigger hits, he seemed to lose some of his vocals in the midst of the venue and with a relatively sparse crowd watching him, the energy also felt a little low. It was a shame as he has given some fantastic performances in Sydney in recent years but this one felt just a little bit off.

The energy completely changed in the room when Boone took to the stage, exploding out amidst backflips and a sprint around the stage as he opened with “Sorry I’m Here for Someone Else”. While the comparisons to Freddie Mercury might seem lazy, it is clear that Boone has stylised himself around the late Queen frontman and there were obvious parallels which were particularly evident during “Mr Electric Blue”, which is one of the best songs on his latest album, allowing his powerful upper chest voice to shine.

Indeed, everything about the performance felt very ’80s-coded with a sound that felt very much like Lou Gramm at times and later, some piano playing that very much channelled Elton John’s “Bennie and the Jets” during “There She Goes.” A word needs to be said on Boone’s piano playing as well as usually when singers take to the piano, it is fairly perfunctory in terms of skill level but Boone showed off an incredible level of skill that really was quite impressive, especially as he had to sustain incredibly high vocals while seated and playing at the same time. The contrast between these more intimate musical moments and his extraordinary physicality on stage was one of the most impressive aspects of the performance.

The energy then picked up again as the whole crowd sang along to “Mystical Magical” before Boone flew around the stage for a heartfelt rendition of “In the Stars”. Boone’s style is very theatrical, almost feeling like musical theatre at times and his voice is one of the strongest that I have heard live. Throughout the entire show, not a single note was misplaced and each was hit with such extraordinary precision and skill that it almost defied belief. The show was carefully planned but still included some nice moments tailored to the Australian audience, like changing the lyrics to “Young American Heart” to “Young Australian Heart”. Another special moment was his ballad “Momma Song” which was made all the more special by the revelation that his parents were in the audience for the show.

As he moved through the set, it felt really clear and curated, with everything well considered and a healthy amount of backflips and skills alongside some great costumes and a fantastic band behind him. Given the amount of movement that Boone was doing on stage, I couldn’t believe that he wasn’t out of breath but he just kept going and continued to nail it throughout the whole show. To close out the night, Boone performed his biggest hit, “Beautiful Things” and it was an absolutely incredible way to end the night with the entire arena joining in.

Overall, this was a truly outstanding show from start to finish with Boone nailing everything from his staging to his presence, incredible vocals and instrumental skills. Even with only two albums under his belt, he has an ability to put on a show that lights up an audience and leaves them all smiling and wow that voice. After Robbie Williams took to the stage at Accor Stadium only a few days earlier, where 
I remarked
 that it was refreshing to see a performer who just gets it, Boone is another who truly gets it. If there was ever an heir apparent to the former Take That frontman, Boone seems to be the one to take the mantle. While I’ve been spoilt for choice this year, I would go as far as to say this was the best concert I’ve reviewed this year. Don’t miss the opportunity to see Boone while he’s still in Australia.

Rating: ★★★★★

Benson Boone – Afterpay Arena – 17 November 2026

This year has tested 
MP Materials
 (
NYSE:MP
) shareholders even as the company hit most of its 2026 operating milestones. MP Materials stock is down 27% year to date (YTD) to $36.67 in Wednesday afternoon trading. Back at the start of the year, the stock traded at $50.52.
 
 
 
 
Courtesy of Agnico Eagle Mines Limited
 
 
 
A rebound is underway, with MP Materials stock up 5% after Needham resumed coverage on November 18 with a Buy rating and a $48 price target. That target lies under the stock's 2026 starting level, so reaching it would only recover part of the slide. In Needham's view, MP Materials could become a major U.S. supplier of permanent magnets over the next three years, backed by government support and long-term supply agreements.
 
 
 
 
 
One of those agreements makes 
General Motors
 (
NYSE:GM
) the company's foundational magnet customer. Another covers magnet supply to a long-term MP Materials customer. Needham said MP Materials is in the "third or fourth inning of a multi-year transformation" into a fully integrated rare earth materials supplier and magnet manufacturer.
 
 
What Pulled MP Materials Lower in 2026
 
 
Heavy spending has weighed on MP Materials as its magnet business ramps. The company disclosed on November 5 that year-to-date capital expenditures reached $413.8 million. CEO James Litinsky added on that call, "I do expect the months and year ahead to be significantly challenging financially."

Third-quarter results still showed momentum. MP Materials reported Q3 2026 revenue of $147.7 million, up 176% year over year. Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) at MP Materials came in at $0.04, compared with an adjusted loss of $0.10 per share a year earlier. Both figures beat Fiscal.ai consensus estimates of $118.9 million in revenue and a loss of $0.01 per share.
 
 
 
 
MP Earnings Explorer — 24/7 Wall St.
 
 
 
A one-time $31.6 million SEG+ sale to a U.S. aerospace and defense customer lifted MP Materials' quarter. Without it, neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr) oxide and metal revenue of $100.96 million, up 227%, carried the results.
 
 
How MP Materials Holds Up Against Rare Earth Peers
 
 
Among rare earth and critical minerals stocks, MP Materials is holding up better than three smaller names this year. 
USA Rare Earth
 (
NASDAQ:USAR
) stock is down 42% YTD to $6.85. Meanwhile, shares of 
Critical Metals
 (
NASDAQ:CRML
) are down 46% YTD to $3.74. 
United States Antimony
 (
NYSEAMERICAN:UAMY
) stock has fallen 49% YTD to $2.56.
 
 
 
 
 
The business models differ sharply. MP Materials already mines, separates and makes magnets at its own U.S. sites. Through acquisitions, USA Rare Earth is putting together a chain across the U.S., Brazil and the United Kingdom.
 
 
Critical Metals remains pre-revenue, developing a rare earth deposit in Greenland and a lithium project in Austria. Antimony and zeolite drive sales at United States Antimony, which holds a sole source Defense Logistics Agency contract. Of the four, MP Materials is furthest along in providing magnets to a major automaker.
 
 
Can MP Materials Get Back to Needham's Target?
 
 
General Motors deliveries are the first milestone on that path. MP Materials completed magnet qualification for General Motors and expects saleable deliveries to begin in Q4 2026. Production under the second agreement should start contributing in the back half of 2027.
 
 
Earnings could take longer to follow. CFO Ryan Corbett said on the November 5 call that the adjusted earnings contribution from MP Materials' magnets should stay small in 2027, adding, "We expect the full earnings power of the segment to become visible in 2028."
 
 
 
 
 
Litinsky offered the bullish case, saying of the company's strategic opportunity, "I believe our current valuation materially understates it." Rates add pressure from the other direction for MP Materials, since New York Fed President John Williams stated on November 17 that rising artificial intelligence investment could point to further rate hikes next year.
 
 
Insider Buying and MP Materials' Road Back
 
 
MP Materials executives and a director bought more than 98,600 shares worth more than $3.5 million between November 10 and November 17, per securities filings. COO Michael Rosenthal purchased 32,000 shares for about $1.18 million. Director Randall Weisenburger added 55,000 shares worth $1.98 million.
 
 
Corbett's own purchases totaled 11,603 shares across three November transactions, worth around $418,700. That total includes 2,627 shares bought on November 17 at $34.86. Buying of this kind signals MP Materials leadership's confidence in the plan, though execution risk remains.
 
 
 
 
 
The next test for MP Materials is whether General Motors deliveries scale on schedule over the coming quarters. General Motors' magnet ramp could remain a key variable for MP Materials stock in the months ahead.

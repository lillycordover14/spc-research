Rare-earth stocks are clawing back ground after a two-day selloff, and the bigger question is whether the bounce can hold. 
USA Rare Earth
 (
NASDAQ:USAR
) stock is at $7.09, up 7%, in early trading Wednesday. Even with that gain, the stock is down 40% year to date (YTD), which shows how much ground the rebound has to cover.
 
 
 
 
mbaysan / iStock via Getty Images
 
 
 
MP Materials
 (
NYSE:MP
) stock is at $37.64, up 8%, the largest gain in the group. Down 25% YTD, MP Materials stock carries a smaller loss than USA Rare Earth but remains well below where it started the year.
 
 
What's Fueling the Rare-Earth Rebound
 
 
Traders are focused on U.S.-China negotiations over rare earths, with expectations that supply tensions could support domestic producers such as USA Rare Earth and MP Materials. China dominates global rare-earth processing, so conflict between the two countries tends to draw attention to companies selling non-China supply. USA Rare Earth markets its products to aerospace, defense, semiconductor and data center customers looking for exactly that kind of supply.
 
 
 
 
 
On November 16, William Blair downgraded USA Rare Earth over feedstock issues, and the whole group fell. A second leg of selling followed on November 17 ahead of a planned meeting between President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

Materials stocks as a group are down 0.8%, so the bounce for USA Rare Earth and its peers runs against the sector. A top New York Fed official said on November 17 that surging artificial intelligence (AI) investment could argue for more rate hikes next year.
 
 
 
 
 
How United States Antimony and Critical Metals Compare
 
 
United States Antimony
 (
NYSE:UAMY
) stock is at $2.65, up 7%, matching USA Rare Earth's rounded gain. Year to date, United States Antimony stock is down 47%, a steeper slide than either lead stock has posted.
 
 
Antimony and zeolite drive United States Antimony's business, and the company supplies military-grade antimony ingots. The company holds a $245 million sole-source contract with the Defense Logistics Agency (DLA). Falling antimony prices led United States Antimony to cut its full-year revenue outlook on August 17.
 
 
Critical Metals
 (
NASDAQ:CRML
) stock is at $3.92, up 2%, a more modest gain than the others. Over the past month, Critical Metals stock is down 44%. Still pre-revenue, the company is developing the Tanbreez rare-earth project in Greenland and has disclosed substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern.
 
 
USA Rare Earth's Mine-to-Magnet Model Versus MP Materials
 
 
USA Rare Earth is building a supply chain that covers the U.S., Brazil and the U.K. The company's Serra Verde operation in Brazil sells mixed rare-earth carbonate under a take-or-pay offtake to a U.S. government-backed buyer, while Less Common Metals produces metals and alloys in the U.K. and France. Downstream, a magnet plant in Stillwater, Oklahoma, began commercial sales in September, and a larger metal and magnet facility is planned for Blacksburg, South Carolina.
 
 
 
 
 
MP Materials runs a more concentrated domestic operation, mining and separating rare earths at Mountain Pass in California and making magnets at its Independence facility in Fort Worth, Texas. A price protection agreement with the U.S. Department of War supports the company's materials business. In Q3 2026, MP Materials completed a magnet qualification with a major automaker. Scale also separates the two companies, since MP Materials is already producing at volume while USA Rare Earth is still ramping its operations.
 
 
Can USA Rare Earth and MP Materials Hold Their Rebound?
 
 
USA Rare Earth faces a key question: whether the U.S.-China negotiations tighten non-China supply in a way that favors domestic producers. Government backing gives the bulls their case, including a $750 million Department of War investment in the offtake vehicle, a revolving facility of up to $500 million and a forward purchase of at least $300 million of product.
 
 
On the other side, the bears read the move differently. Some trader talk frames the bounce as an oversold rebound or short covering, with doubts that the gains in USA Rare Earth and MP Materials will stick. A short squeeze is possible, though short-interest data isn't available to confirm one.
 
 
 
 
 
For MP Materials, saleable magnet deliveries beginning in Q4 2026 give shareholders a company-specific marker beyond the trade headlines. Volatility across the group could stay elevated until the U.S.-China rare-earth standoff resolves.

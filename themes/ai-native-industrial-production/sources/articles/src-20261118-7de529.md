Best Buy is selling Blu-rays directly through its website and in physical stores again after exiting Blu-ray and DVD sales in 2023.

The Best Buy website’s movie section currently lists 15 Blu-rays (including one two-pack) that are “sold & shipped by Best Buy.” New titles available include 
The Odyssey
 (2026), 
Project Hail Mary
 (2026), 
Supergirl
 (2026), 
Super Mario Galaxy
 (2026), and 
Obsession
 (2025). Best Buy is also selling some older titles on Blu-ray, including 
It’s a Wonderful Life
 (1943), 
Saving Private Ryan
 (1998), and 
The Godfather
 (1972).

Most of the Blu-rays are available for purchase online and can be delivered to an address or picked up at a Best Buy store within a few days. As of this writing, 
The Odyssey
 appears to be in stock at Best Buy stores, as it’s currently listed as available for pickup within as little as an hour.

Ars Technica reached out to Best Buy to ask whether customers can expect the store to continue selling Blu-rays moving forward.

In October 2023, Best Buy announced that it would stop selling Blu-rays and DVDs online and in stores starting in 2024.

“To state the obvious, the way we watch movies and TV shows is much different today than it was decades ago. Making this change gives us more space and opportunity to bring customers new and innovative tech for them to explore, discover and enjoy,” a Best Buy spokesperson 
said
 in a statement issued at the time.

Last year, the Best Buy website started selling Blu-rays and DVDs from third parties 
via its online marketplace
.

Numerous other companies have reduced or ended their Blu-ray and/or 
DVD businesses
 in recent years. 
Target
, for instance, only sells the discs in stores during special occasions (like holidays or for big releases) now. 
Disney outsourced
 its DVD and Blu-ray business to Sony, which subsequently 
stopped selling recordable Blu-rays
 to people, and DVD rental company 
Redbox
 went 
out of business
.

That said, physical media, including Blu-rays and DVDs, still have a place in many people’s homes. Because digital copies of movies and shows can be 
plucked from online platforms
 and people’s libraries that were 
previously “purchased”
 (but truly only rented) without warning, enthusiasts continue relying on physical media. Enthusiasts also opt for physical copies of movies and shows for better picture and/or sound quality than what’s available digitally, as well as for 
bragging rights
.

Overall, the sales of Blu-rays and DVDs in the US have been steadily declining, and annual consumer spending dropped 9.3 percent from 2024 to 2025, 
according to the Digital Entertainment Group
 (DEG). During this same time, however, consumer spending on 4K Blu-ray discs specifically increased 12 percent year over year, per DEG.

A resurgence in Blu-ray sales has been associated with the growth of collectible SteelBooks, which are Blu-rays sold in collectible steel boxes, and Gen Z, which, in some cases, are even turning to video rental stores for Blu-rays and DVDs.

“We’re seeing a lot of evidence, including at the Criterion Mobile Closet, that more young people are thinking about physical media in a different way,” Peter Becker, president of Criterion, which sells Blu-rays and DVDs online, told 
the Los Angeles Times
 in February. “In an age where so much is available to us on demand, it becomes increasingly important to us.”

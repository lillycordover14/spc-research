Hyundai Motor Group (HMG) has acquired full ownership of Boston Dynamics, its US-based subsidiary specialising in AI-enabled mobile robots, as the South Korean automaker looks to accelerate the adoption of its Atlas humanoid robots.
 
 
According to a regulatory filing, HMG has acquired the outstanding 10% stake in Boston Dynamics held by SoftBank, after all put and call options on the shares were exercised and expired.
 
 
 
 
 
Boston Dynamics' current shareholders include Hyundai Motor Group, the company's executive chairman, Chung Euisun, and the group's transportation subsidiary, Hyundai Glovis, which holds a 12.5% stake after investing an additional US$ 35 million.
 
 
Bringing the company under full South Korean ownership is expected to speed up decision-making, including decisions on capital expenditures, R&D strategies, and collaboration with affiliated companies within the group, as the company looks to speed up the commercialisation of its humanoid robots.
 
 
In September, HMG opened a training and testing facility, the Robotics Metaplant Application Center (RMAC), for its Atlas humanoid robots at the Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America (HMGMA) plant in the US state of Georgia.
 
 
The company confirmed that the robots "are being trained in real-world environments, such as preparing the logistics and sequencing of automotive parts before placing them in the correct order for assembly. By 2030, applications will extend to component assembly. Over time, Atlas will take on new tasks involving repetitive motions and strenuous work, such as lifting heavy loads."
 
 
 
 
 
The automaker said it plans to step up the deployment of Atlas robots across its global manufacturing network, beginning with 25,000 units at Hyundai Motor and Kia's assembly plants globally over the next few years.
 
 
Boston Dynamics is building a new production facility in the US capable of producing up to 30,000 robots annually by 2028 to meet demand.

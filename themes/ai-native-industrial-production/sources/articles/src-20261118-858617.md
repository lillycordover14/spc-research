ActionSA
 leader 
Herman Mashaba
 has turned the tables on DA mayoral candidate Helen Zille, claiming that the DA leader was instrumental in setting in motion the process that eventually led to the appointment of former top cop Shadrack Sibiya, whom she now alleges was installed on Julius Malema’s instructions.
In a sworn affidavit dated November 16, Mashaba says it was Zille who recommended that he approach former prosecutor Glynnis Breytenbach for advice on a suitable candidate to head the City of Johannesburg’s Group Forensic and Investigation Service (GFIS), where Sibiya eventually landed up.
The counter-claim is contained in an affidavit filed in support of Malema’s application to stop Zille from repeating allegations that the EFF leader effectively controlled Johannesburg’s administration during Mashaba’s tenure as mayor.
Mashaba’s version is corroborated by Michael Beaumont, ActionSA’s national chairperson, who was Mashaba’s chief of staff at the time and says he personally approached Zille for advice on potential candidates after the position became vacant.
Beaumont says Zille directed him to Breytenbach, who subsequently recommended Sibiya because of his experience in law enforcement.
The affidavits directly challenge Zille’s claim that Malema instructed Mashaba to appoint Sibiya and that the appointment was the price the former mayor had to pay to retain the EFF’s political support.
In the interview that triggered the dispute, Zille alleged that Mashaba had allowed Malema to dictate major municipal decisions, capture City Power and influence the awarding of fleet tenders for the benefit of companies allegedly linked to the EFF leader.
She also claimed that Malema had instructed Mashaba to appoint Sibiya to head the city’s anti-corruption unit.
Zille said: “He even appointed Shadrack Sibiya as the head of the anti-corruption unit at Julius’s instruction and when I said to him we can’t do that and he said to me that is the price we pay to stay in power.”
But Mashaba has rejected that account, saying he had no prior relationship with Sibiya and that Malema played no role in bringing him to his attention.
In his affidavit, Mashaba says: “Before Mr Sibiya was brought to my attention in the circumstances I relay below, I did not know him. I had no prior relationship with him.”
He says the recommendation came through Breytenbach, following an approach initiated by Beaumont on Zille’s advice.
Beaumont’s affidavit provides further detail of how the process unfolded.
He says that after the vacancy arose in the city’s group security and investigations unit, he contacted Zille to seek her advice on potential candidates who could be encouraged to apply through the city’s ordinary recruitment processes.
Zille recommended that he approach Breytenbach, then a DA member of parliament and former senior prosecutor in the National Prosecuting Authority.
He subsequently approached Sibiya and encouraged him to apply once the position was advertised. Sibiya went through the city’s recruitment process and was ultimately appointed.
Beaumont further says Zille was aware that Sibiya was a candidate for the position because of Breytenbach’s recommendation.
The account is significant because it places Zille’s own involvement in the recruitment process at the centre of a dispute in which she has accused Malema of using his political influence to secure Sibiya’s appointment.
Mashaba also disputes Zille’s claims that Malema dictated municipal fleet tenders or facilitated the capture of City Power through his office.
He says he never discussed municipal tenders with Malema and did not receive instructions from him concerning procurement decisions.
In his affidavit, Mashaba says: “At no stage during my tenure as Mayor did I have any conversation with Mr Malema regarding the award, allocation, or determination of municipal tenders within the City.”
He specifically denies discussing the municipal fleet tender with Malema, whether in relation to prospective bidders, the evaluation of bids, the awarding of contracts or the allocation of benefits arising from those contracts.
Mashaba says Malema was not a member of any of the municipal institutions responsible for evaluating and adjudicating tenders.
He also rejects the suggestion that he allowed Malema to use the mayoral office to advance private financial interests in exchange for the EFF’s political support.
“To the extent that Ms Zille suggests I knowingly permitted the municipal processes to be used for Mr Malema’s personal benefit in exchange for his political support, I expressly deny this,” he says.
Mashaba further argues that the mayor did not have unilateral authority to appoint Sibiya or dictate tender awards, as these processes were governed by established municipal procedures and collective decision-making structures.
He says Zille had never opened a criminal case with the police against him or Malema over the alleged corruption during the period between August 2016 and October 2026.
The former mayor also takes issue with Zille’s claim that he told her Sibiya’s appointment was the price of remaining in power.
Mashaba denies having had such a conversation with her.
He says Zille was not part of the city’s municipal administration or the structures responsible for recruitment and that he never discussed Sibiya’s appointment with her.
The dispute also revisits the Public Protector’s investigation into Sibiya’s appointment.
Malema argues in his founding affidavit that Public Protector Report No 24 of 2020/21 made no adverse findings against him or the EFF in relation to the appointment.
He says neither he nor the EFF featured in the complaint that resulted in the investigation and that the report found Sibiya’s appointment and subsequent salary grading complied with the applicable legal framework.
Malema says the allegations are particularly damaging because they suggest he used political influence to secure private commercial benefits from municipal resources.
He argues that Zille’s reference to companies operating as his fronts implies that he concealed his financial interests behind corporate entities to benefit from procurement processes.
In his founding affidavit, Malema says the allegations portray him as a political leader who exploited public institutions for private gain.
He says he instructed his attorneys to send Zille a letter of demand on November 9, asking her to retract the statements and apologise.
He claims she refused to do so.
The dispute comes amid renewed coalition negotiations following the November 4 local government elections, with Malema arguing that the allegations were made at a politically sensitive time and could damage both his reputation and the EFF’s political standing.
Mashaba, meanwhile, says Zille’s allegations appear to be politically motivated and intended to undermine ActionSA’s position in Johannesburg’s coalition negotiations.
He says the EFF supported his administration as part of a coalition agreement built around service delivery and clean governance, despite the parties’ ideological differences.
Malema and the EFF are seeking an interim order prohibiting Zille from publishing, repeating or disseminating allegations that Malema captured City Power, manipulated municipal fleet tenders for his benefit, instructed Mashaba to appoint Sibiya or exploited the EFF’s political support to secure improper municipal decisions.
They want the interim relief to remain in place until the final determination of a contemplated defamation action.
Zille has formally notified the high court of her intention to oppose the application.

COLUMN: Carillon Flashback October 1, 1980 – The Dutch remember: Town plants Dutch gift of tulip bulbs
						

							By: Wes Keating

Steinbach town officials and members of the Steinbach Branch of the Royal Canadian Legion ceremoniously planted the Dutch government’s gift of several dozen tulip bulbs at city hall, marking the 35th anniversary of the liberation of Holland.

The bulbs, a royal orange variety specially developed by the Dutch in recognition of the country’s liberation from the Nazis, have been sent to Legion branches and local governments in various Canadian centres.

Some 1.5 million of the bulbs have been distributed over the past few weeks and requests have been received for many more.

CARILLON ARCHIVES 

Coun. George Neufeld and Legion President Fred Cross plant tulip bulbs, a gift from the Dutch government, at the Steinbach Civic Centre in a brief ceremony the last week in September. Looking on, are Legion and Legion Auxiliary members Walt and Tina Barkman, Ove Larsen, Henry Reimer, Tien Barkman, Neil Friesen, Peter Kehler, Fred Barkman, and Martha Wiebe. 

Speaking at a brief planting ceremony, Steinbach Legion President Fred Cross said the gift of bulbs from the people of the Netherlands was their way of expressing appreciation for their liberation during the Second World War that was brought about by Canadian forces.

“These people, even after 35 years, still greatly remember the almost 10,000 casualties suffered by the Canadians in this action.”

Coun. George Neufeld, on behalf of the Town of Steinbach, gratefully accepted this gesture of friendship by the Dutch people.

“Many of the citizens of Steinbach have their heritage rooted in Holland. One of Holland’s symbols, the windmill, is located in our Mennonite Heritage Village.”

The tulip bulbs are being planted around the civic building, at the windmill on the museum grounds, and at the K.R. Barkman Park on Main Street.

Neufeld said everyone was looking forward to the colourful display in spring.

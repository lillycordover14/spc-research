AI-Driven Sales Jump 103%; Samsung Electro-Mechanics Shares Gain 5.57%

Nvidia’s record quarterly revenue and expanded partnership with Samsung Electro-Mechanics highlight growing demand for AI semiconductors and advanced substrate production.

Nvidia has entered the era of more than $100 billion in quarterly revenue, driven by a surge in demand for artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductors. Despite the leadership setback caused by founder Jensen Huang’s resignation, the company delivered results that exceeded market expectations, demonstrating sustained growth. Particular attention has also turned to its expanding cooperation with South Korean component manufacturers, as Nvidia directly named Samsung Electro-Mechanics as a new semiconductor substrate supply partner during its earnings conference call.

On Nov. 17 (local time), Nvidia announced that its revenue for the third quarter of fiscal 2027 reached $115.84 billion, a 103% increase from the same period a year earlier. The figure surpassed the market consensus estimate of $109.1 billion by more than 6%. Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) came in at $2.68, exceeding the expected $2.48, while revenue from the data center segment, the company’s core business, reached $107.6 billion.

New partnerships aimed at expanding the supply chain were also disclosed. Colette Kress, Nvidia’s interim chief executive officer (CEO) and chief financial officer (CFO), named Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Taiwan-based Kinsus as new partners in the substrate category, and Taiwan-based SPIL as a partner in advanced packaging, during the conference call held after the earnings announcement. Kress said the agreements would strengthen the capacity and resilience of Nvidia’s global supply chain.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics will supply advanced package substrates for Nvidia’s accelerated computing platforms, next-generation GPUs and high-speed data center networking products. The partnership builds on a previously announced multi-year substrate manufacturing agreement. Nvidia and Samsung Electro-Mechanics have amended their existing supply agreement to improve operational capabilities and expand production capacity at existing facilities.

The scope of cooperation is also expected to expand from semiconductor package substrates to multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs). MLCCs are key components that stabilize voltage fluctuations and help ensure a steady power supply in electronic devices. As high-performance AI accelerators consume increasing amounts of power, the importance of highly reliable components for stable power delivery is also growing. Samsung Electro-Mechanics CEO Jang Duck-hyun said the company would combine its advanced substrate processing capabilities with its high-reliability MLCC technology to help ensure the timely supply of Nvidia’s AI platforms.

Expanding production facilities is another key part of the partnership. Nvidia and Samsung Electro-Mechanics are working to strengthen production capabilities at the South Korean company’s facilities in Sejong and Busan, which manufacture substrates for computing and high-speed networking products. Nvidia is also reviewing additional funding to expand production infrastructure and install advanced equipment at the two sites. Samsung Electro-Mechanics plans to establish a full-scale mass-production system starting in 2029.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics shares also rallied on expectations of benefits from the strengthened partnership. As of 9:23 a.m. on Nov. 18, the shares were trading at 796,000 won, or approximately $598, up 42,000 won, or 5.57%, from the previous session. They opened 5.17% higher at 793,000 won and climbed as much as 9.94% to 829,000 won in early trading. The buying interest was attributed to growing expectations that the company would benefit from Nvidia’s expanding AI semiconductor supply chain.

Nvidia’s supply-chain expansion is not limited to Samsung Electro-Mechanics. Under its agreement with Taiwan’s Kinsus, Nvidia plans to increase dedicated advanced ABF package substrate production capacity to more than 3.5 times its current level. It is also expanding advanced packaging capacity with SPIL. Nvidia’s total purchase commitments have grown to $390 billion, including $32 billion in commitments under agreements with neocloud companies as of the end of the third quarter.

The business outlook is also positive. Nvidia raised its projected revenue growth rate for fiscal 2028 from 70% to 75%. The increase comes as investment in AI infrastructure continues, with the total backlog across the cloud industry rising 25% from three months earlier to $2.5 trillion. With demand outpacing supply, renewal prices for Hopper GPUs have risen by as much as twofold, while B300 rental rates have increased by more than 60%. Nvidia also forecast fourth-quarter revenue of approximately $130 billion, above the market estimate of $125.1 billion.

However, the impact of U.S. export controls on China is expected to persist. Nvidia has halted shipments to China of Hopper data center products subject to export licensing requirements. The company said its fourth-quarter revenue outlook of approximately $130 billion does not include revenue from those products in China. Sales of the affected products in China totaled $2.4 billion in the third quarter.

Uncertainty over the company’s leadership remains. Former CEO Jensen Huang, who resigned amid a personal conduct controversy, said in a statement that he would be willing to return if requested by the board. The board, however, rejected the possibility. Board Chair Harvey Jones said the decision to accept Huang’s resignation was unanimous and that there were no plans for his return. Huang also acknowledged and apologized for his personal conduct and said he would cooperate with the board’s independent investigation.

Nvidia’s board has engaged global executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a successor. It plans to consider both internal and external candidates but will not rush the process before identifying a suitable candidate. Kress sought to reassure investors about the leadership transition, saying business was continuing as usual and that the company had world-class technical talent and a multi-year technology development roadmap.

Despite the management uncertainty, Nvidia shares rose more than 4% in after-hours trading on the strength of its results and growth outlook, moving above the $160 mark. As of 5:55 p.m. Eastern Time on Nov. 17, the shares were trading at $162.76, up 4.70% from the regular-session closing price.

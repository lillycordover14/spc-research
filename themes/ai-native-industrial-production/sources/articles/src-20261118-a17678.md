RTX taps Ursa Major and Avio USA to develop Tomahawk motors

Wednesday, 18 November 2026, 19:14

Photo: The USS Delbert D. Black fires a Tomahawk during Operation Epic Fury. Source: U.S. Navy/DVIDS.

*The work is expected to qualify two booster suppliers for a munition in high demand and widely used by the U.S. military.*

Ursa Major and Avio USA have secured a joint contract from RTX to design and develop solid-rocket motors for the Tomahawk cruise missile.

This is reported by 
Axios
.

The work is expected to produce two qualified booster vendors, strengthening supply chains for one of the most in-demand and relied-upon munitions in the U.S. inventory.

Tomahawk use

The Tomahawk is a mainstay of the U.S. Navy, launched from surface warships and submarines. The Army has also adopted it for its Typhon launcher, which has been tested in locations including the Philippines. Japan has bought hundreds and fired one from a destroyer for the first time this summer. Ukraine has also sought the missiles, with little luck.

More than 850 Tomahawks were expended in the first few weeks of Operation Epic Fury in Iran. That exceeded deliveries as well as historical usage during Iraqi Freedom, Desert Storm and Enduring Freedom.

What the companies said

“At the end of the day,” Ursa president Jason Meredith said in a statement to Axios, “this is about making sure” troops have “the critical munitions they need, when they need them.”

Avio chief executive James Syring said in a statement: “Our team has a deep understanding of the critical role the Tomahawk plays in America's defense and what it means to be entrusted with supporting this vital program.”

Syring said the award “also marks a significant step forward for our planned U.S. manufacturing facility.” In September, the company broke ground on a 900,000-square-foot plant in Hurt, Virginia.

The contract is valued at more than $80 million. RTX is expected to increase Tomahawk production to more than 1,000 a year under a seven-year arrangement with the Pentagon.

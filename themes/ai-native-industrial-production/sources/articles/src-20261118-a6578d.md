Fashion and technology companies are moving quickly to tighten safeguards after a series of cheating cases involving artificial intelligence smart glasses at national exams. The push follows criticism that designs indistinguishable from ordinary eyewear are fueling crime and privacy violations.
Gentle Monster recently reviewed the privacy and security policies for its Intelligent Eyewear smart glasses, set to launch on Dec. 10, according to industry sources on the 18th.
The company also shared security measures with related firms at a product unveiling in New York on the 9th, sources said.
Developed in partnership with Samsung Electronics and Google, the product runs on Google's Gemini AI. It will be offered in 16 versions based on two eyeglass frames and four sunglass frames. Users can access the AI assistant through simple voice commands.
Samsung Electronics said on Nov. 13 that the full lineup of its intelligent eyewear, developed in partnership with Google, Gentle Monster and Warby Parker, was unveiled in New York on Nov. 12 local time. The photo shows the Gentle Monster design of Samsung Electronics' intelligent eyewear. Courtesy of Samsung Electronics
Its defining feature is an appearance nearly impossible to tell apart from regular glasses. At about 41 grams, the weight is similar to that of conventional eyewear.
"The key point is that there must be no difference in design from ordinary glasses," a Gentle Monster official said. "We matched the weight and fit almost exactly to existing glasses so there is no unfamiliar feeling when wearing them."
Oakley, which earlier introduced AI glasses with Meta, said its frames are somewhat thicker because of the battery but that there is little design difference from existing sunglasses.
Smart glasses that have shed the look of electronic devices are selling briskly despite prices in the hundreds of thousands of won. Gentle Monster's product costs 650,000 to 700,000 won, or about $450 to $490, yet initial preorders sold out and the company is now taking a second round of reservations.
The 'Natural Design' Dilemma
That natural design, however, has emerged as a crime risk. Because the devices are hard to spot with the naked eye, concerns have grown that they could be used for exam cheating or illegal filming.
In October 2024, two Harvard University students drew wide attention by demonstrating I-XRAY, a system that linked facial recognition AI to Meta's smart glasses. Wearing what looked like ordinary glasses, they scanned the faces of passersby on the street, and within seconds their names, occupations and addresses appeared on a smartphone.
The industry has begun working on ways to prevent misuse while preserving the design. Meta recently introduced Ray-Ban Meta Audio, a voice-only model with the camera removed entirely. Gentle Monster designed its glasses so that an external LED indicator lights up during recording. The camera stops immediately if the indicator is covered or the glasses are removed. The company also lowered the risk of personal data leaks by automatically blurring faces when the lens captures other people.
"We are closely monitoring security, privacy and ethical issues, and we plan to keep addressing concerns as they arise," the Gentle Monster official said.

Contract Signings Slip Again, Agents to Buyers: ‘Here’s Your Chance’&nbsp;&nbsp;National Association of REALTORS®

[page text]
Buyers who can look beyond the gloomy housing headlines may find some of the best negotiating opportunities in years, real estate professionals say.

 

© RealPeopleGroup / E+ / Getty Images

Pending home sales dropped sharply in October, underscoring a sluggish fourth quarter for housing activity as elevated mortgage rates continue to sideline buyers.
The National Association of REALTORS®’ 
Pending Home Sales Index
—a forward-looking indicator of home sales based on contract signings—fell 4.7% in October and were down 16.7% compared to a year ago. All four major regions posted monthly and yearly declines, with the West and South recording the largest drops.
The slowdown also showed up in completed transactions last month. 
Existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October
 and were down 8.8% compared to a year earlier, NAR reported. Mortgage rates have been a major obstacle, climbing to their highest levels in three years. The 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged 7.33% in October compared to 6.25% a year earlier, according to Freddie Mac.
“October could mark the low point in contract signings for this housing cycle, as mortgage rates appear to have topped out,” says Lawrence Yun, NAR’s chief economist. “Two consecutive months of record-low pending contracts point to a very sluggish fourth-quarter home sales closings, and annual existing-home sales could be at the lowest since the mid-1990s.”
But the slow pace of home sales may be creating more opportunities for buyers who can afford to make a move. More homes are available, listings are lingering longer, and sellers are increasingly willing to negotiate.

 

‘There’s Incredible Opportunities’ for Buyers
"There's incredible opportunities," Lindsey Harn, a broker with the Lindsey Harn Group, affiliated with Christie’s International Real Estate Sereno, in San Luis Obispo County, Calif., recently told 
Real Estate Today
. “I think one of the most exciting things for home buyers is that sellers have some negotiability.”
That could mean offers below asking price, help with closing costs or mortgage rate buydowns to reduce monthly payments. “Sometimes that could even mean getting the washer dryer, the hot tub and the patio furniture,” Harn says. “There's just a ton of opportunity for buyers to take advantage of.”
Buyers also have more choices and time to negotiate. Existing-home inventories rose 4.6% annually in October, reaching a 5.1-month supply and hovering near its highest level since 2015, according to NAR. Homes spent a median of 35 days on the market, up from 33 days in September. Meanwhile, 20.5% of active listings had price reductions in October, the highest share for that month since 2022, according to Realtor.com.
The trend extends to new construction. Nearly half of builders (46%) cut prices in November—the highest share since the pandemic—with an average price reduction of 6%, according to the National Association of Home Builders. Further, 68% of builders offered sales incentives, such as mortgage rate buydowns and closing cost assistance—also a post-pandemic high.

Buyers may be waiting for those deals. A 
2026 Neighbors Bank survey
 of nearly 1,000 first-time buyers found that 47% are waiting for a price reduction before making an offer. Also, price cuts were their most sought-after seller concession, cited by 63%, followed by repairs (60%) and closing cost assistance (50%).
Harns says some buyers are finding additional savings by shopping around for lower mortgage rates at local credit unions or exploring adjustable-rate mortgages, which may offer lower initial rates. But she cautions to her buyers against assuming a slower market means prices are crashing. Some buyers are making offers 30% to 40% below asking price—far beyond what local market conditions support, she says.

 

Indeed, the slowdown may be masking significant pent-up demand. Yun notes that home sales have fallen to levels last seen 30 years ago, even as the U.S. population has grown by about 75 million. “Either sizable housing demand is building in the background, or the dream of homeownership is increasingly out of reach for many Americans,” Yun says.
A Closer Look at Pending Home Sales in October
Here’s a closer look at how pending home sales fared across the country last month, according to NAR’s latest report.
Northeast:
 down 0.8% month-over-month and a drop of 11.1% annually.
Midwest:
 fell 2.7% in October compared to September and down 14% annually.
South:
 fell 6.3% month-over-month and down 18.7% from a year ago.
West:
 down 6.9% in October compared to September and a 20.3% decrease from a year ago.

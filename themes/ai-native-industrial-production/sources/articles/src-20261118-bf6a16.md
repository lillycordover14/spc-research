UNC-Virginia Preview: Tar Heels Hoping to Continue Road Success, Earn Bowl Eligibility in Charlottesville&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
With 
its starting quarterback in limbo
, North Carolina (5-5, 2-4 ACC) is days away from another coin-flip road conference matchup at Virginia (5-5, 3-4 ACC) in another installment of the South’s Oldest Rivalry — in hopes of grabbing that sixth win and extending 2026 to bowl season. Here is everything you need to know before the Tar Heels and the Cavaliers square off.

How to Watch

Location: Scott Stadium — Charlottesville, Va.
Time: 12:30 p.m. ET
TV: The CW Network
Crew: Thom Brennaman, Jason Cabinda, Wes Bryant

Analyzing the Cavaliers

After an ACC Championship appearance last season, Virginia and fifth-year head coach 
Tony Elliott
 are not experiencing the same success in 2026. Ranked 11th in the conference, the Cavaliers’ five wins consist of two FCS victories and three conference wins — all at home — over teams that sit below them in the standings (NC State, California, Syracuse). Nonetheless, like the Tar Heels, Virginia sits one victory away from bowl eligibility after dominating the Golden Bears last weekend, 36-7.

ESPN’s SP+ moved the Cavaliers up seven spots over the weekend to 54th, uplifting a 79th-ranked offense with their 37th-ranked defense. Through 10 games, Virginia’s strength of schedule is 54th nationally. But in its four games against the current top-seven of the ACC, the Cavaliers have an average margin of defeat of 22 points.

“This is a team that we have a little bit of familiarity with from last year’s game,” 
Bill Belichick
 said on Monday. “They’ve got about half of those players that we played against.”

Elliott’s squad is below the FBS average in both passing (85th) and rushing (74th) yards per game. Virginia prioritizes its ground attack, though, with the second-most attempts in the conference — 38.8 per game. The Cavaliers’ middling averages have not coupled well with its 122nd-ranked red zone offense, grabbing points on 75.7 percent of its drives inside the 20-yard line.

Virginia’s defense has held every opponent but Wake Forest and Florida State under 28 points, boasting the nation’s 39th-best scoring defense. However, like many of UNC’s recent opponents, the Cavaliers also pose a strong disparity between stopping the run and the pass. In air yards allowed, Virginia sits at the top of the ACC (186.6 YPG). But opponents are tallying 148.6 yards per game on the ground, positioning them as the worst run defense North Carolina has seen this season.

Positions, Players to Watch

Quarterback 
Beau Pribula
 and running back 
Jekail Middlebrook
 shoulder the bulk of the Cavaliers’ work in the backfield, with each already tallying more than 100 carries this season. In nine games, Middlebrook, a Middle Tennessee State transfer, is up to 540 yards and four touchdowns, averaging 4.86 yards per carry. And while Pribula’s sack-adjusted numbers — 262 yards, two touchdowns and 2.54 YPC — are lagging behind, his layer to the attack shouldn’t be discounted. Last week against Cal, the fifth-year senior by way of Penn State and Missouri averaged 9.57 yards per carry.

Through the air, Pribula doesn’t put the football in harm’s way often, tossing 15 touchdowns compared to three interceptions in 10 starts. Averaging 172.7 yards per game, nearly 60 percent of Pribula’s attempts are under nine yards in depth. However, according to Pro Football Focus, the senior’s best grades come on throws of 20+ yards. In 33 such attempts this year, he’s recorded four scores and eight “big time throws” — PFF’s stat for a pass with excellent ball location and timing.

Da’Shawn Martin
 leads Virginia in receiving with 425 yards and seven touchdowns, doubling the next-highest pass-catcher in both categories. The 6-foot, 180-pound Kent State transfer has topped the Cavaliers in four of their last five outings. Elsewhere on offense, Virginia’s offensive line is allowing 2.8 sacks per game, ranked 117th nationally.

Cornerback 
Jacobie Henderson
‘s 47 tackles are a team high, but defensive end 
Matthew Fobbs-White 
is making the most of his stops, recording nine TFLs this season, tied for 11th in the ACC. A notable factor in the Cavaliers’ stingy pass defense is its 11 interceptions — and three pick-sixes — tied for 12th in the FBS. Safety 
Brandyn Hillman
 leads UVa with four takeaways despite playing in only seven games this season.

The Matchup

North Carolina has dealt with its fair share of stout defensive fronts and weak secondaries, hence its second-worst rushing numbers in the conference. The Tar Heels eclipsed the 150-yard mark at Clemson but have yet to replicate that success on the ground against a power-conference opponent since. Virginia’s rush defense, though, has already allowed outings of 309, 281, and 247 yards on the ground this season, presenting an opportunity for 
Benjamin Hall
 to build off his first-career multi-touchdown game and catalyze Carolina’s run game.

However, nothing looms larger than who will be under center for UNC in Charlottesville. After 
Billy Edwards Jr.
 suffered an injury against Louisville, freshman 
Travis Burgess
 stepped in at quarterback and offered a change of pace, notching more rush yards in eight attempts than Edwards added all season. As of Monday, Belichick had no update regarding Edwards’ health and the starter for Saturday. Despite the inexperience, North Carolina’s rushing attack may get an upgrade if the rookie is, in fact, QB1 in Scott Stadium.

Defensively, the Tar Heels are no stranger to two-headed backfields. After limiting run-heavy quarterbacks Kalieb Osborne and Lincoln Kienholz on the ground in the last two weeks, Pribula and Middlebrook are the next in line to challenge UNC. Without 
Greg Smith
 for the first half, 
Derek McDonald
 and 
Peyton Seelmann
 — Carolina’s two leading tacklers — will have to clean up the run game and assist in coverage. 

In line with its red zone woes, Virginia is also one of the worst third-down offenses in the country (No. 128), converting on 31.6 percent of its attempts. Insert the stoppable force versus movable object paradox, because the Tar Heels allow a conversion on 43.6 percent of opponents’ tries, the 120th-best rate in the country.

The Cavaliers are 1-1 in one-score games, both decided by a field goal. UNC is more familiar with those nail-biter-style contests, with seven of its 10 contests this season decided by eight points or fewer. Carolina is 3-4 in such games, but with the point spread inside a field goal once again, its experience may prove pivotal as the Tar Heels will take another shot at securing bowl eligibility Saturday.

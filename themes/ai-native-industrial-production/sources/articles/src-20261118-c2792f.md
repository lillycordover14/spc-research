MP Materials insiders bought about $3.6 million of the company’s stock over the past week, three Form 4 filings show, and on Wednesday the stock was up about 4%, at about $36.36 in early afternoon trading, after Needham resumed coverage with a Buy rating and a $48 target. The rare-earth miner (NYSE: MP) had lost 10% over Monday and Tuesday, closing at $34.86, as the whole group sold off ahead of Thursday’s meeting between President Trump and China’s Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.

The buying came from three people. Director Randall Weisenburger bought 55,000 shares at $36 on Nov. 10, about $1.98 million. Chief operating officer Michael Rosenthal bought 32,000 shares over Nov. 10 and 11 at an average of about $36.72, about $1.18 million. Chief financial officer Ryan Corbett bought three times: 5,476 shares at $36.25 on Nov. 10, 3,500 at $36.75 on Monday, and 2,627 at $34.86 on Tuesday, the day’s closing price, about $419,000 in all, which took his direct stake to 120,620 shares.

Three insiders put their own money into the stock within days of its Nov. 5 low, at prices from $34.86 to $37.30. Investors watch purchases like these because the buyers know the business best. The limit is size and history: the three bought about 99,000 shares between them, while chief executive James Litinsky sold about 1.2 million shares through his trust between April and June at prices in the $60s, part of it under a plan set up the previous September, and insider buying says nothing about where a stock that moves 3% or more on most days goes next month.

What moved it this week

The two-day drop started with an analyst note on a different company. On Monday, William Blair downgraded USA Rare Earth to Market Perform, citing delays in the feedstock for its magnet plant, and the whole group fell with it: USA Rare Earth lost 10% and MP Materials fell 6.9%, its worst day since Oct. 1. On Tuesday MP slipped another 3.4% as traders trimmed positions before the Shenzhen meeting.

The meeting matters to this stock because MP’s price carries a premium for being a supply of rare earths outside China. When China tightens its export controls, that premium grows. When the two countries move toward a truce, it shrinks. The current truce runs to Jan. 10, and the market reads any extension as a negative for the group and any breakdown as a positive, which is the reverse of how it treats most stocks.

Wednesday’s bounce had two pushes. Needham’s analyst Carter Goman wrote that MP is in “the third or fourth inning of a multi-year transformation” into a vertically integrated rare-earth and magnet supplier, with government support and anchor customers in General Motors and Apple. The $48 target is Needham’s number, 38% above Tuesday’s close, and it is well below the $81 target the same firm set when it first covered the stock in June and the $73 it cut to in July. Other recent targets run from $40 at Deutsche Bank to $70 at Canaccord Genuity, according to MarketBeat. Those targets sit $30 apart on a stock trading near $36.

The quarter behind the buying

The insider purchases followed the third-quarter report on Nov. 5. Revenue was $147.7 million, up 176% from a year earlier and well above the $117.5 million analysts expected. Adjusted earnings came to 4 cents a share against an expected 2 cent loss. The company produced 1,018 metric tons of NdPr, the rare-earth oxide that goes into magnets, up 41%, and sold 1,020 tons, up 94%. Its price protection agreement with the Department of War added $8.1 million of income in the quarter.

The magnet business, which MP is building toward, is still small. Magnetics revenue was $18.6 million, down 15% from a year earlier as the Fort Worth plant moved from precursor products to finished magnets. The company said it completed General Motors’ qualification process in the quarter, with saleable magnet deliveries and revenue starting in the fourth quarter and magnet earnings ramping gradually through 2027.

The swing from the May high to the Nov. 5 low was $44,900 on a $50,520 position, in six months. And even at Needham’s target the position would still be $2,520 under its January cost. Needham’s target, the most optimistic call of the day, would still leave a January buyer short of cost.

The swings are the point

Since Oct. 1, MP Materials has had 35 trading sessions and moved 3% or more in 19 of them. The biggest gain was 9.1% on Oct. 27 and the biggest loss was Monday’s 6.9%. At Wednesday’s price the stock is down 23% since Sept. 30, 53% below its May high and 14% above its Nov. 5 low. Much of that path followed headlines about export controls, trade talks and analyst targets.

For a holder, that volatility is the first fact to plan around. A position in a stock that can lose a third of its value in six weeks should be a size you can watch do that without changing anything else in your plan. If this position has grown into a large share of your portfolio, how much you hold matters as much as where the stock goes next.

A holder sitting on the January loss has one plain option that does not require a view on the stock: selling realizes a $15,660 capital loss that offsets gains elsewhere this year, with up to $3,000 a year against ordinary income and the rest carried forward, and buying back inside 30 days would disallow it under the wash-sale rule. A would-be buyer has a different reference point now: the insiders paid $34.86 to $37.30, and the stock has traded through that range in both directions within a single week.

The bottom line

Three insiders bought about $3.6 million of MP Materials stock in the week it touched its 52-week low, the CFO buying three times at prices down to $34.86. Needham called the stock a Buy with a $48 target, a target that is theirs and sits far below the firm’s own June number. The business reported a strong quarter and is starting magnet deliveries to GM. Against that, the stock is down 28% this year, has moved 3% or more in 19 of its last 35 sessions, and trades on the U.S.-China talks as much as on its own results. Size the position for the swings, treat the targets as opinions, and remember that a January loss can also be a tax decision.

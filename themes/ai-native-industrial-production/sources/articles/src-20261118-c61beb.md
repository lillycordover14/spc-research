Hadrian partners with Alabama, Auburn on 3-year Iron Bowl initiative to train students for advanced manufacturing careers&nbsp;&nbsp;Yellowhammer News

[page text]
Alabama and Auburn are teaming up with 
Hadrian
 in a three-year Iron Bowl partnership that will give students from both universities a chance to learn from engineers, visit factories and explore careers in advanced manufacturing.

Hadrian announced the agreement Wednesday at its Factory 4 in Cherokee. The company says it is the first to partner with both universities using the Iron Bowl name. The agreement begins in 2027 and covers matchups in several sports on both campuses.

Some of that learning will take place on the factory floor, where students will be able to talk with Hadrian employees and see how they use software and automation. Hadrian opened its Cherokee factory in March to expand production capacity for the U.S. Navy’s Virginia- and Columbia-class submarine programs.

Senior company leaders will mentor students and student-athletes one-on-one, sharing what they’ve learned in the industry and helping students build professional skills and explore career options.

“Few things bring Alabamians together quite like their pride in the storied rivalry of the Iron Bowl,” Hadrian founder and CEO Chris Power said. “We want to build on that passion and create the same excitement around engineering and advanced manufacturing.”

Hadrian’s technical experts will help both universities develop engineering coursework and advise their engineering programs, drawing on their experience in advanced manufacturing. A student ambassador program will introduce students to the company’s work and the kinds of careers available in the field.

“Our partnership with Hadrian opens doors to hands-on learning and meaningful careers in engineering and advanced manufacturing,” University of Alabama President Peter J. Mohler said.

Auburn University President Christopher B. Roberts said the agreement builds on work already being done at Auburn through research, industry partnerships and engineering education.

“Our partnership with Hadrian builds on that foundation and creates another opportunity to put Auburn’s expertise to work for Alabama and our nation,” Roberts said.

Learfield and Playfly Sports, the multimedia rights holders for Alabama and Auburn athletics, respectively, helped arrange the agreement. Both schools’ athletics directors pointed to what it could mean for students.

“The Iron Bowl represents one of the most iconic traditions in college athletics, and this partnership brings together the rivalry and rich history with Hadrian’s vision for advancing Alabama’s future,” Alabama Director of Athletics Greg Byrne said. “Equally exciting is Hadrian’s investment in engineering education and hands-on learning opportunities that will benefit our students.”

“We are grateful for Hadrian’s investment in Auburn Athletics and its commitment to supporting higher education in our state,” Auburn Athletics Director John Cohen said. “Beyond the field, this partnership will help connect students and workers with important career opportunities.”

Fans will see Hadrian at this year’s Iron Bowl on Nov. 28, before the three-year agreement begins. Build Submarines is the official sponsor of this year’s game.

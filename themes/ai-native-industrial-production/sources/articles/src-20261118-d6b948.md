Platinum could swing from a 468,000-oz. surplus this year to a 279,000-oz. deficit in 2027 as investors buy more bars and coins, exchange inventories rebuild and mine supply slips, the World Platinum Investment Council 
said Wednesday
 in its first forecast for next year.
The council expects investment demand to swing by 911,000 oz., more than the entire 747,000-oz. change in the market balance. It projects net investment of 513,000 oz. next year after outflows this year. Bar and coin purchases would climb 55% to 448,000 oz. and exchange warehouses would add 110,000 oz. Investors would still withdraw 150,000 oz. from exchange-traded funds, according to the quarterly outlook prepared for the council by Metals Focus.
“Platinum above ground stocks remain critically depleted with the forecast surplus in 2026 following three consecutive years of significant deficit,” council CEO Trevor Raymond said in a release.

Limited inventory

The forecast leaves little inventory to absorb a supply disruption if buying resumes. Above-ground stocks would fall to 1.81 million oz. by the end of next year, enough to cover just under three months of global demand. This year’s surplus, enlarged by 203,000 oz. from the previous forecast, would leave about three and a half months’ cover at year-end.
Industrial users offer the other source of growth. Use is forecast to rise 4% to a record 2.66 million oz. as gains in chemicals and petroleum outweigh declines in glass and electrical applications. Automakers are expected to use 5% less platinum, or 2.81 million oz., as battery-electric vehicles gain ground and some manufacturers switch back to palladium in catalytic converters.
Mine supply could fall 1% to 5.57 million oz., with growth in Zimbabwe offsetting only part of South Africa’s decline. Metals Focus expects recycling to rise 5% to 1.85 million oz., enough to lift total supply by 1%. Demand would rise 12%.

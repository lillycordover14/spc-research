What’s the story?

Boston Dynamics expands its collaboration with semiconductor manufacturer GlobalFoundries (GF) to deploy its Spot quadruped robot for autonomous enterprise asset management across GF’s global semiconductor fabs.

Why it matters

Routine inspections by Boston Dynamics’ Spot quadruped robot collect the data that lets GF shift from reactive to predictive maintenance to prevent downtime in its fabs.

The bigger picture

Pairing inspection quadrupeds such as Spot with humanoids such as Boston Dynamics’ Atlas robot (which GF plans to begin testing in early 2027) could lay the groundwork for fabs where robots fix the faults they find.

November 18, 2026 –
 
Boston Dynamics
, a developer of AI-enabled mobile robots, has this week announced an expansion of its collaboration with semiconductor manufacturer 
GlobalFoundries
 (GF) to deploy its Spot quadruped robot for autonomous enterprise asset management across GF’s global semiconductor fabs.

According to Boston Dynamics, its Spot robot can conduct industrial inspections and manage facility performance data. Under the agreement, Spot deployments will expand across GF’s manufacturing sites in Malta, New York; Burlington, Vermont; Dresden, Germany; and Singapore.

GF has previously worked with Boston Dynamics and deployed Spot at its Burlington, Vermont manufacturing site for more than four years, according to the announcement, which follows an earlier case study the companies released in 2022.

“GlobalFoundries was one of our earliest customers to adopt Spot, and we are excited to see the program continue scaling across the globe,” said Zack Jackowski, Chief Product & Technology Officer at Boston Dynamics. “Innovative companies like GF are realizing real operational value today while collecting the kind of data that will be extremely useful toward future manufacturing innovation in the next few years.”

Spot is Boston Dynamics’ quadruped robot, capable of carrying out routine industrial inspections and collecting facility performance data to support predictive maintenance.

The companies also stated that GF will be the first customer outside the automotive industry to begin testing Boston Dynamics’ Atlas humanoid as part of the company’s early adopter program and skills development workshop. The Atlas electric humanoid platform is currently in early production, according to Boston Dynamics.

The companies noted that they will begin testing Atlas onsite at GF’s facilities in early 2027. The goals of the skills development workshop will be to evaluate potential humanoid use cases, assess customer readiness and integration needs, and begin early testing and training for specific task execution, complementing GF’s global program to upskill employees in AI.

Boston Dynamics stated that, more recently, GF expanded the program to Singapore, where two Spot robots have traveled nearly 900 kilometers and completed more than 90,000 inspections in sub-fab areas since April, achieving a 99% mission success rate. GF also redeployed 875 routine inspections in two weeks with the latest hardware and software, and has an immediate roadmap to more than double that number using Spot’s visual inspection capabilities.

“Spot has helped us shift from reactive to predictive maintenance, collecting data across thousands of inspection points to prevent downtime and keep our fabs running more efficiently,” said Pradip Singh, Chief Manufacturing Officer at GF. “Scaling Spot and testing Atlas are the next steps in our journey to AI-enabled manufacturing, putting physical AI to work across our operations worldwide.”

For more information on Boston Dynamics and its AI-enabled mobile robots, please visit the company’s 
website

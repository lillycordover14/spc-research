    Twelve students were arrested after protest outside LSU Chancellor Jim Dalton’s office

                                                            By 
Laura Allen
, 
Reveille Editor in Chief
 

November 18, 2026

 

Alexis Persicke
 
Protestors march Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, outside the Hill Memorial Library in Baton Rouge, La.
 

The LSU Police Department arrested 12 members of LSU’s Students for a Democratic Society during a protest Wednesday outside of LSU Chancellor Jim Dalton’s office.

During the protest, SDS called for the restoration of 
LSU’s cultural centers
, full restoration of LSU’s Title IX Office and the expulsion of 
LSU football player Damien Shanklin
. This demonstration comes on the heels of multiple protests throughout the semester calling for university administration to meet these demands.

The students protested by laying on the floor inside Thomas Boyd Hall outside the Chancellor’s Office, reciting chants expressing their demands. Scott Sonnier, a general member of SDS who frequently acts as the group’s security, said that LSUPD officers approached him during the protest and explained that any members who remained inside would be arrested for trespassing shortly after the protest began.

“I addressed the officer whenever he came up,” said Sonnier, junior computer science major. “He kind of blew past me and tried to ignore me, and I engaged him again. He didn’t really want to give too much specifics, but he said that protest was not allowed inside the buildings, and if anyone stayed and didn’t leave, they’d be arrested.”

Sonnier said he relayed what the officer said to the other protestors. About half of the protestors chose to leave the building while 12 remained inside. Those 12 were arrested by LSU Police and led outside with their hands zip-tied behind their backs. They were brought to the LSU Police Department.

Emery Pipes, a sophomore majoring in communication disorders and early childhood education, was one of the students arrested. The SDS member said that she and the other students arrested cooperated with police while they were being escorted out of the building.

“I did not get up until a cop came and tapped my shoulder, and then I was like, OK, you know, wasn’t going to resist a cop or whatever,’” Pipes said. “But I don’t know, it’s really confusing because while we were walking with them, they praised us for not resisting. But while we were in there, I get charged for resisting.”

Pipes, along with the others who were arrested, has been charged with obstruction of education and resisting an officer, listed under 
LRS 14:328 
and 
LRS 14:108
, respectively.

LSU Chief Communications Officer Meg Sunstrom said in a statement following the incident, “On [Wednesday], the LSU Police Department was called to the Chancellor’s Office in regards to an active protest occurring inside of the building. The protestors were reported to be laying on the floor, preventing staff from being able to enter or leave the area. They were also loudly chanting and disrupting the staff’s ability to perform their roles at LSU.”

Sunstrom stated that officers observed 25 protestors in total, 13 of whom refused to disperse. This number contradicts the accounts of SDS members, who said only 12 were arrested. She also confirmed that the protestors who did not leave were physically removed from the building by LSUPD.

LSUPD has not yet released a statement on the incident or formally confirmed the charges.

Gabriela DeJean, SDS president, explained the organization’s reasons for escalating its protests.

“We had our big rally with multiple organizations, which just goes to show how big of a cause this is, how much people actually care about these things,” said DeJean, a senior political science major. “And then in the wake of that, nothing. Absolutely nothing came of that in terms of administration.”

She continued, “So today we had this die-in for that exact reason. We’re tired of being ignored.”

She went on to criticize LSU’s handling of the protestors and the message she believes it sends about the student body and their safety.

“I think that it was a madness of oversight and overstepping to arrest 12 students today for simply being at their breaking point with the fact that they are not appreciated, respected or, I mean, quite frankly, wanted on this campus,” DeJean said.

This is not the first time members of LSU’s SDS have been arrested. General member 
Gabriela Juárez was arrested
 in October 2025 during an LSU presidential search committee meeting, along with six others.

All 12 students who were arrested Wednesday have been charged, but court dates have not yet been set. They have also all been released from custody.

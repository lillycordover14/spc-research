Armenia’s nominal GDP projected to reach 13.6 trillion drams in 2027, twice 2021 level
4 minute read
Finance Minister Vahe Hovhannisyan presented the draft 2027 state budget to lawmakers in parliament, stressing that the Government projects economic growth of 5.8% in 2027 and average annual growth of 6% in the medium term.
Hovhannisyan said that Armenia’s average economic growth in 2021-2025 amounted to 7.9%, reflecting a breakthrough in economic development.
“Compared to 2021, budget expenditures are doubling, reaching 4 trillion drams. Capital expenditures are doubling compared to 2022. The increase in capital expenditures means not only more fixed assets and infrastructure, but also a doubling of the capacity of the public and private sectors to ensure the implementation of these capital expenditures,” the minister said.
He said that the 2027 budget is the first budget for the implementation of the Government’s five-year program, which should ensure adequate financing for the reforms of the previous period and create a solid foundation for the implementation of future programs.
“Nominal GDP will amount to around 13 trillion 614 billion drams in 2027, which is twice as much as in 2021. This growth includes inflation, which has amounted to just 23.8% cumulatively over these years. All these indicators demonstrate the confidence we have in Armenia’s future, the viability and prosperity of Real Armenia,” the minister said.
He noted that Armenia is becoming more attractive to international capital, attracting both foreign and portfolio investments. Investment inflows are also being recorded in the private sector, including in the real estate market.
According to the Finance Minister, financing the current account deficit has not been based on attracting external foreign currency debt. On the contrary, in recent years, the share of external and foreign currency liabilities in the structure of government debt has decreased significantly.
“Between 2010 and 2017, the current account deficit averaged 6.7% of GDP, between 2018 and 2021 it averaged 5.1%, and between 2021 and 2025 it was just 4.5%. We have improved our long-term current account position by significantly reducing dependence on external transfers. At the same time, the role of service exports and the services balance has increased. We are moving from an economy heavily dependent on external transfers toward one that relies more on its own production and export capacities,” Hovhannisyan noted.
The minister also presented figures on the increase in the number of active corporate taxpayers.
“If their number stood at 107,000 in 2021, it exceeded 148,000 in 2025. According to data for the first nine months of 2026, the number of active taxpayers has already reached more than 155,000,” the minister added.

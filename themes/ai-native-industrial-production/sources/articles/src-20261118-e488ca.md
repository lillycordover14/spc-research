Raytheon awards Ursa Major and Avio USA joint contract to develop and qualify a new booster for Navy’s Tomahawk missile

By Martin Chomsky (Defence Industry Europe)

                    Corporate                

|

                November 18, 2026            

Photo: U.S. Navy.

Raytheon has awarded Ursa Major and Avio USA a multiyear contract to develop a new solid rocket motor booster for the U.S. Navy’s Tomahawk cruise missile. The companies will jointly design and qualify the booster, work that could make both eligible for future production orders.

The award is intended to broaden the domestic supplier base for Tomahawk propulsion and give Raytheon greater flexibility in production. Adding qualified suppliers would also increase competition and help meet growing demand for munitions, the companies said.

“Expanding the industrial base for Tomahawk propulsion is essential to ensuring this critical capability remains ready for the fleet,” said Sam Deneke, president of Naval Power at Raytheon, an RTX business. “By dual-sourcing this critical component with both Avio USA and Ursa Major, we’re strengthening our supply chain, encouraging innovation, and building the capacity needed to meet the Navy’s mission requirements well into the future.”

The program combines Avio’s solid rocket propulsion and manufacturing experience with Ursa Major’s design and advanced manufacturing processes. It also builds on both companies’ investments in U.S. manufacturing capacity.

“Tomahawk is a critical capability for the warfighter, and meeting growing demand requires more capacity and a stronger, more resilient supply chain,” said Jason Meredith, president of Ursa Major. “Our partnership with Avio USA brings together complementary capabilities to create additional reliable sources of solid rocket motors for Raytheon and the U.S. Navy.”

Avio USA broke ground in September on a solid rocket motor manufacturing facility in southern Virginia. Ursa Major is also investing in manufacturing capacity and expanding its solid rocket motor portfolio across several tactical missile programs.

“Our team has a deep understanding of the critical role the Tomahawk plays in America’s defense and what it means to be entrusted with supporting this vital program,” said James Syring, chief executive of Avio USA. “This award also marks a significant step forward for our planned U.S. manufacturing facility, which we are building to become a vital asset in America’s defense industrial base for decades to come.”

Three things to watch in UNC basketball’s game against Marshall
How will UNC respond after their first loss of the season?
by
 
 
Thomas Blackington
Nov 18, 2026, 2:00 PM UTC
UNC had their first real test of the season last Friday, when they played their first team from a power conference. While Georgia is no one’s idea of a juggernaut this season, they’re the kind of team that will probably be on the bubble in a world with an expanded NCAA tournament. Unfortunately, the Heels failed that test, falling at home 78-70. While there were some good moments in the game — particularly the surge after halftime — UNC ultimately couldn’t survive their own poor shooting and late game decisions.
The good news is that the schedule lightens up a bit again, potentially allowing the Heels a chance to continue to work out the kinks. Up next on the schedule is Marshall; the Thundering Herd is 2-3 this season and coming off a 67-55 win against East Tennessee State. Marshall is ranked 148th by KenPom; while they fall firmly in the “tune-up game” category, I don’t think this UNC team has the luxury of overlooking anyone right now (for example, Western Carolina). Let’s look at some things to watch in this matchup:
Guarding the Guards
Marshall is a guard heavy team that relies on their perimeter players to carry the load on offense. Their top four scorers are guards*; they are particularly reliant on Noah Otshudi and Bryce Slay to score. The combo combines to score over half of Marshall’s points per game. Both shoot over 50% from the field and over 40% from three through five games, and both have taken giant leaps forward this year for Marshall (Slay in particular; he averaged just over two points per game last year).
Obviously, Otchudi and Slay having good games doesn’t necessarily preclude a Marshall win (as their record indicates); still, I assume most Carolina fans are like me and have vivid memories of a random mid-major guard suddenly having the game of his life in the Smith Center. Terrence Brown will have his hands full with Otchudi though; in addition to his scoring, the Marshall guard leads the team in assists and still finds the time to pull down over five rebounds per contest.
* Slay is officially listed as a guard, although it’s probably worth noting that he starts and spends the majority of his minutes at the small forward position.
Rebounding (again)
We’re only four games into the season, so it’s hard to believe a topic can be beaten to death already; yet it’s Groundhog Day, and we’re talking about the Heels’ rebounding deficiencies. UNC was outrebounded by Georgia by nine, marking the third time this season the Heels have lost the battle on the boards. According to KenPom, Carolina ranks 225th in offensive rebounding percentage and even worse on the defensive glass (287th). For a fanbase that has long been used to seeing their team dominate the glass, it’s been a jarring adjustment to watch the likes of Wyoming and Western Carolina routinely hoist second and third chance shots up while UNC players always seem to be in the wrong place at the wrong time.
There’s an old saying that you can’t teach size; the good news is, Michael Malone doesn’t have to. UNC is one of the top 20 teams in the nation in average height by KenPom’s metric. While height (very obviously) doesn’t mean a team will be a good rebounding team, this isn’t like the ‘24-’25 team that simply didn’t have the size to compete on the offensive glass (of note: that team was actually decent on the defensive glass). This team’s rebounding problems are a combination of poor positioning, lack of focus, and a bit of bad luck.
I’m actually cautiously optimistic that the team can and will improve in this area. Malone’s last three Nuggets squads all were among the NBA’s best at corralling rebounds. Having Nikola Jokic is certainly a part of that, but Malone was a part of his development. It won’t happen all at once, but if Malone is anywhere near the coach that won an NBA championship, I feel confident he can teach Sayon Keita where to position his 7’7” wingspan to maximize his chances of catching the odd carom off the rim.
Marshall presents a good opportunity for the Heels to work on this. They start 7’1” Khaman Maker at center, who averages seven rebounds a game; he’s not much of a threat on offense, but he will present a space-clogging rebounder on defense that Heels will have to contend with. He’s a big reason why Marshall is a top-40 team on the defensive glass, although they are far less effective at snagging offensive boards.
Who replaces Neo Avdalas?
Yesterday, the news broke that Neo Avdalas would 
be out for an extended period
 of time with a wrist injury. He sustained the injury late in the game against Georgia, which probably partially explains some of his poor play down the stretch in the Heels’ loss.
The big question is how Malone will replace Avdalas in the lineup. Much of UNC’s offense was built with the idea that he would serve as a point forward and the primary facilitator on offense, particularly in the half court. While I’d say the results of that have been mixed so far, Avdalas is UNC’s leader in assists per game, and the Heels don’t have an obvious replacement for his exact role. They could insert Isaiah Denis into the lineup, who has shown some ability to distribute the ball while also being a scoring threat; however, going to a three-guard lineup when UNC is already struggling to rebound probably isn’t the best idea.
It’s more likely that Maximo Adams joins the lineup. Adams has been an absolute spark plug off the bench, injecting both offense, rebounding and energy into the team when he’s in the game. He’s bound to have some freshman bumps this season, particularly as the competition eventually gets harder; still, a Sayon Keita/Jarin Stevenson/Adams/Matt Able/Terrence Brown lineup looks pretty solid on paper. Having Brown, Able, or Denis capably assume the role of facilitator will be critical for this to work.

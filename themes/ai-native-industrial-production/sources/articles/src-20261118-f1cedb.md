CHAMPAIGN, Ill. — Twelve researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign have been named to the 2026 Clarivate Analytics 
Highly Cited Researchers
 list. The list recognizes researchers and social scientists who have demonstrated exceptional influence, as reflected through their publication of multiple papers frequently cited by their peers during the last decade.

The highly cited Illinois researchers this year are:  
crop sciences
 and 
plant biology
 professor 
Lisa Ainsworth
; 
natural resources and environmental sciences
 professor 
Kaiyu Guan
; 
materials science and engineering
 professor 
Axel Hoffmann
; 
climate, meteorology and atmospheric sciences
 professor 
Atul Jain
; plant biology professor 
Tracy Lawson
; plant biology and crop sciences professor 
Andrew Leakey
; plant biology and crop sciences professor emeritus 
Donald Ort
; 
psychology
 professor 
Brent Roberts
; 
Siebel School of Computing and Data Science
 professor 
Jimeng Sun
; 
cell and developmental biology
 professor 
Boxuan Zhao
. Psychology professor emeritus Ed Diener and crop sciences professor Stephen Long, who are deceased, are also on this year’s list.

Lisa Ainsworth

Ainsworth, the interim director of the 
Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology
, studies crop responses to climate change and tests potential solutions for mitigation of climate change through agriculture. She was the Research Leader for the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s Agricultural Research Service Global Change and Photosynthesis Research Unit before joining the Illinois faculty in 2024. A key goal of her research is to maximize future crop production. She directs the 
Realizing Increased Photosynthetic Efficiency
 project and also is affiliated with the 
Center for Advanced Study
 and the 
National Center for Supercomputing Applications
.

Kaiyu Guan

Guan works to ensure sustainable food production and develop solutions to environmental challenges in agriculture, focusing on agroecosystem modeling, remote sensing, environmental forecasting and agricultural adaptation to climate change. He uses satellite data, computational models, fieldwork, supercomputing and machine-learning approaches to address how climate and human practices affect crop productivity, water resource availability and ecosystem functioning. Guan is the founding director of the 
Agroecosystem Sustainability Center
; a professor of computing and data science; a professor in the 
Institute for Sustainability, Energy, and Environment
; a 
Blue Waters
 professor at NCSA; and an affiliate of the 
Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology
.

Axel Hoffmann

Hoffmann’s research focuses on topics related to magnetism, such as spin transport, magnetization dynamics and biomedical applications. His work on spin Hall effects has contributed to developing spintronics, electronic devices that harness electron spin for faster and more efficient computing. He is a Founder Professor in Materials Science and Engineering, the director of the 
Illinois Materials Research Science and Engineering Center
, a member of the 
Materials Research Laboratory
, a professor of 
electrical and computer engineering
 and 
physics
 and is affiliated with the 
Holonyak Micro and Nanotechnology Lab
.

Atul Jain 

Jain’s research focuses on developing and applying advanced climate modeling systems to understand and predict how human activities influence the global climate system and how climate change, in turn, affects human activities and natural ecosystems. Internationally recognized for his scientific contributions, he has made significant contributions to major international climate assessments, including those of the United Nations 
Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change
.

Tracy Lawson

Lawson studies the influence of stomatal behavior on plant productivity and water-use efficiency, manipulating stomatal anatomy and function to produce crops that are more resilient to environmental changes, including higher temperatures and drought. She also focuses on developing new phenotyping tools to optimize plant growth in controlled environments, including vertical farms. She is an affiliate of the IGB.

Andrew Leakey 

Leakey is an international leader in transdisciplinary team bioscience approaches. His research group develops and applies AI-enabled tools to high-throughput phenotyping, physiological genetics, biodesign and global change biology, with a focus on photosynthesis, stomata and roots. His team has developed technologies to maintain crop production with less water and reduce yield losses to drought stress in multiple species. Leakey also is an affiliate of the IGB, ISEE and the 
Center for Digital Agriculture
.

Donald Ort

Ort’s research focuses on improving photosynthesis and addresses crop responses to global change including rising carbon dioxide levels, temperature stress and drought. He directed the open-air agricultural laboratory 
SoyFACE
, served as a research director of the 
Center for Advanced Bioenergy and Bioproducts Innovation
 and was deputy director of RIPE. He currently co-leads the Photosynthesis and Food Security theme at the IGB.

Brent Roberts

Roberts specializes in the field of personality psychology and studies continuity and change in personality throughout adulthood, with an emphasis on understanding the factors that influence change. His recent research focuses on assessing and building social, emotional and behavioral skills. He holds the Gutsgell Endowed Professorship, is a professor of biomedical and translational sciences in the 
Carle Illinois College of Medicine
, and an affiliate of the Center for Advanced Study, the 
Social and Behavioral Science Institute
 and the IGB.

Jimeng Sun

Sun uses artificial intelligence to improve healthcare, drug discovery, clinical trial optimization, computational phenotyping, clinical predictive modeling, treatment recommendations and health monitoring. He also is a professor in the 
Neuroscience Program
, the 
Coordinated Science Lab
 and in biomedical and translational sciences at CI MED.

Boxuan Zhao

Zhao’s laboratory develops and applies new sequencing technologies to map how cells connect and communicate with one another. His team charts how the mammalian brain is wired, how connections are formed and how they change. His team also explores communication between diverse cell types, including the interactions between cancer cells and neurons in brain tumors. His team incorporates transcriptomics and proteomics data into these maps to clarify the underlying molecular mechanisms causing these changes. He is also affiliated with the Neuroscience Program, the IGB, and the 
Cancer Center at Illinois
.

Ed Diener

Diener, who died in 2021, was a social psychologist and a leader in the field of positive psychology, which focuses on the factors promoting happiness and well-being. In the mid-1980s, he and colleagues developed the Satisfaction with Life Scale, and in 2009 the Flourishing Scale, both designed to gauge happiness — which he called “subjective well-being” — in a methodical, repeatable manner.

Stephen Long

Long, who died in 2025, used computational and bioengineering approaches to improve photosynthetic efficiency in crop plants and worked to address the effects of climate change on crop physiology and yields. He founded RIPE, a multinational project to increase crop production. He was the founding editor of the journal Global Change Biology and was invited to give a 
TED Talk about his work
 in 2023.He was a professor in the IGB, and an affiliate of the NCSA and the Center for Advanced Study at the U. of I.

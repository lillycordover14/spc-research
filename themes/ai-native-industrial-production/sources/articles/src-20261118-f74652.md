Cheertime has developed LENTO, a molded soilless growing medium designed to maintain air space and water retention in the root zone. The product combines a hydrophilic polymer matrix with natural organic matter and is intended for applications ranging from propagation to mature plant production. According to Elky Cheung of Cheertime, the development has involved six years of research and development. The company offers three formulations: polymer matrix with coco coir (100% peat-free), polymer matrix with peat, and a combination of the three materials.
 
"We combine a hydrophilic polymer matrix with natural organic matter to form a semi-rigid, three-dimensional micro-porous structure. Inside that structure, adjustable air pores maintain a stable gas-liquid-solid environment, giving roots room to breathe," Elky explains. Unlike loose substrates such as peat and coco coir, which can compact over successive crop cycles, LENTO uses a molded structure with pores that expand and contract in response to moisture levels.
© Cheertime
 
Water retention and aeration
The substrate is designed to retain moisture while maintaining air-filled porosity after irrigation. Cheertime reports 20–30% continuous air space, even when the medium is fully saturated. According to the company's laboratory measurements, LENTO has a water absorption capacity of 300–500%, a bulk density of 0.2 g/cm³ and an electrical conductivity below 800 μS/cm. The reported pH range is 5.6–6.6, with organic matter content above 30%.
 
Cheertime states that these physical and chemical properties have been tested by accredited third-party laboratories. Its comparisons include 52% higher aeration than peat and three times the water retention of coco coir, based on laboratory tests against commercial benchmarks.
© Cheertime
 
Applications in orchid and young plant production
Phalaenopsis cultivation was one of the main reference points during LENTO's development. The company focused on the requirements of aerial velamen roots, which need air availability and regular wet-dry cycles.
 
The modular cup formats cover the production cycle from 1.4-inch liners to 5.0-inch finished pots. Cheertime says the substrate's water retention, drainage and pH stability are intended to support root development and reduce the need for mid-cycle repotting.
 
The company has also conducted multi-season commercial trials in orchid greenhouses and foliage plant nurseries. According to Cheertime, these trials recorded approximately 20% faster rooting and more than 99% A-grade commercial uniformity. "Because LENTO encourages rapid root emergence, it helps support faster plant development across various crops - and in Phalaenopsis production in particular, vigorous early rooting can noticeably shorten the overall cultivation cycle and help plants reach marketable size earlier, depending on the cultivar and greenhouse conditions," Elky explains.
 
LENTO is also intended for post-tissue-culture cultivation, vegetative cuttings and young plant production. Its pre-formed structure holds the root system together during potting-up. A nested Parent-Child cup system allows young plugs to be placed directly into larger containers.
© Cheertime
Formats and greenhouse integration
The commercial range includes propagation plugs in 128-cell, 72-cell and 50-cell trays, as well as modular cups ranging from 1.4S to 5.0S. Solid rooting blocks and granular substrates are also available.
 
The pre-formed units are designed for automated potting and transplanting lines. Cheertime says their structural consistency allows handling by robotic grippers without crumbling or generating dust. Custom dimensions and adjustments to EC and pH are available.
 
Switching to LENTO may require changes to irrigation practices. Cheertime recommends shorter, more frequent pulse irrigations rather than larger volumes applied at longer intervals. The company provides irrigation and fertigation guidelines for different crops and production systems.
© Cheertime
 
Labor, energy and reuse
Cheertime identifies labor, water consumption and production cycles as areas where the substrate could affect operating costs.
 
Because the product arrives pre-formed and sterile, growers can avoid substrate mixing and sterilization. The company estimates potential labor and preparation time savings of up to 50%. Its nested cup system is also reported to reduce repotting time by approximately one-third compared with manual moss wrapping.
 
Cheertime estimates that a 20% shorter rooting and growing cycle could reduce electricity consumption for supplemental lighting and climate control by up to 30%. It also reports potential reductions of up to 40% in combined water, fertilizer and crop-related costs. "To keep the water and fertilizer savings figures completely grounded, we'd be happy to clarify that those reductions apply particularly when used with ebb-and-flow irrigation systems."
 
The company states that the polymer matrix can retain its structure for up to six years, allowing reuse across multiple crop cycles. The material is also designed for storage in dry conditions without deterioration.
© Cheertime
 
European market development
Cheertime is preparing a pilot trial program with selected European nurseries, particularly orchid growers. The program is intended to generate commercial cultivation data and develop irrigation and nutrition guidelines.
 
The company is also preparing trials involving young vegetable plants and controlled-environment crops, while exploring cooperation with European greenhouse automation manufacturers.
 
Alongside the professional growing range, Cheertime is developing separate products for retail consumers. A dedicated European warehouse is also under consideration as regional demand develops.

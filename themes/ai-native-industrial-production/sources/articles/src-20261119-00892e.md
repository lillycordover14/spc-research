Ursa Major and Avio USA Tomahawk booster contract. Credit: Ursa Major
Raytheon
, an RTX business, has awarded 
Ursa Major
 and 
Avio USA
 a joint, multi-year contract. It covers development and qualification of a new 
Tomahawk solid rocket motor
 booster for the Tomahawk® cruise missile. Once the booster qualifies, both companies could become approved U.S. suppliers for this U.S. Navy program.

Military demand for munitions is rising. Raytheon therefore wants more than one qualified source for Tomahawk propulsion. The company expects domestic suppliers to increase competition and production flexibility. It also expects a more resilient supply chain.

Raytheon’s Naval Power president 
Sam Deneke
 explains, “
Expanding the industrial base for Tomahawk propulsion is essential to ensuring this critical capability remains ready for the fleet. By dual-sourcing this critical component with both Avio USA and Ursa Major, we’re strengthening our supply chain, encouraging innovation, and building the capacity needed to meet the Navy’s mission requirements well into the future.
“

Who brings what to the booster?

The two companies are strategic partners on the program. According to the companies, Avio contributes its long heritage in solid rocket propulsion and its manufacturing expertise. It also brings investment in U.S. production capabilities. Ursa Major, meanwhile, contributes its design approach and 
advanced manufacturing processes
.

James Syring
 leads Avio USA, the Arlington, Virginia-based subsidiary of Avio S.p.A. He formerly directed the U.S. Missile Defense Agency and retired from the U.S. Navy as a Vice Admiral. In September, the company broke ground on a solid rocket motor manufacturing facility in southern Virginia. The award complements that project.

“
We are proud to have been selected alongside Ursa Major for this important work,
” Syring said. “
[…] This award also marks a significant step forward for our planned U.S. manufacturing facility, which we are building to become a vital asset in America’s defense industrial base for decades to come.
“

Ursa Major’s manufacturing investments

For Ursa Major, the contract builds on a growing solid rocket motor portfolio. It also follows investments in manufacturing capacity across multiple tactical missile programs. The company is based in Berthoud, Colorado, with manufacturing operations in Youngstown, Ohio. It says AM, adaptable design and flexible production methods support the speed and scale its missions require.

In 2024, Ursa Major received 
a $12.5 million joint investment contract in partnership with the US Navy
. The goal was to mature its Lynx solid rocket motor manufacturing process. Additive manufacturing was among the capabilities targeted for further development.

“
Tomahawk is a critical capability for the warfighter, and meeting growing demand requires more capacity and a stronger, more resilient supply chain,
” said 
Jason Meredith
, president of Ursa Major. “
Our partnership with Avio USA brings together complementary capabilities to create additional reliable sources of solid rocket motors for Raytheon and the U.S. Navy.
“

Next, the partners plan to qualify the booster they designed together. They also aim to show their eligibility for future production orders.

Ursa Major has built its production around AM. This contract brings it into the supply chain of an established U.S. Navy missile. It will work alongside a solid rocket motor specialist with a longstanding heritage in the field.

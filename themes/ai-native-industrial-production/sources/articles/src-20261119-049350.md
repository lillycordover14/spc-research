The Toyota Tacoma Lassen concept will look familiar underneath, but the sheet metal is another story. 
Machina Labs
 teamed up with British design firm 
CALLUM
 to rework the hood, front fenders, bedsides and tailgate. The company says it went from digital design to a customized body in about three weeks. That’s the bodywork, not the time it took to build a complete truck.

A Tacoma inspired by Lassen Volcanic National Park

Raised hood contours reflect the Lassen concept’s volcanic inspiration. (Photo: Machina Labs)

CALLUM, the design firm co-founded by Ian Callum, drew on Lassen’s volcanic landscape for the truck’s shape and surface details. The concept is headed to the LA Auto Show as a demonstration of Rebellis, Machina’s new dedicated automotive brand. The truck is based on a Tacoma, but this is a Machina and CALLUM project, not a new Toyota trim.

How the robots shape the metal

The finished Lassen concept sits in front of robotic equipment at Machina Labs. (Photo: Machina Labs)

A conventional stamping die makes sense when you need to turn out thousands of identical pieces. For a small run or a shape that keeps changing, paying for a dedicated die is harder to justify. Machina turns a digital design into a path the robot follows to shape the panel. It can add details to a factory-stamped panel, a process it calls restriking, or form a new panel from a flat sheet. The part still needs finishing before it goes on a truck.

“We have two robots that are manipulating a sheet of material,” Tim Dine said at a reveal event. For a fully formed replacement panel, the team scans the vehicle’s exterior surfaces, removes panels to scan the surfaces underneath and sends the resulting digital model to the designer. After forming the new metal panel, it scans that part and compares it with the model. The differences help guide the next iteration.

Machina Labs CEO and founder Ed Mehr explained that the Lassen’s hood and tailgate began as stock Tacoma panels. The company reshaped those skins with its restriking process. The front fenders and rear bedside panels, by contrast, were formed from flat sheets of metal.

Where this could matter for truck owners

The tailgate’s raised Machina lettering shows one way to personalize a metal panel. (Photo: Machina Labs)

A specialty shop could use the process for a short run of custom panels. An automaker could use it for prototypes, limited editions or replacement parts. That’s the opportunity here: a shop doesn’t have to sell parts at Tacoma production volumes to consider making a new panel in metal.

“Anywhere from tens to few thousand is where we are targeting,” Mehr said. He was describing the production range Machina wants to serve, not a proven output figure. He said traditional manufacturing makes more sense once a run reaches hundreds of thousands of vehicles. In between, he sees room for designers to offer limited editions of familiar truck platforms.

Can you buy the Lassen bodywork?

Machina has not announced pricing or an on-sale date for the Lassen bodywork. (Photo: Machina Labs)

Machina hasn’t announced a price or a firm on-sale date for the Lassen or a Tacoma body kit. Mehr said he hopes to get 10 trucks into customers’ hands in 2027 as a pilot. That is a company goal, not a confirmed consumer launch.

“I’m hoping we can get to a point where we can maybe put 10 trucks in the market next year to pilot this,” Mehr said. He also described work with automakers and other partners on software that could let customers customize designs, potentially at a dealership. Pricing, ordering details and a firm timetable remain unanswered.

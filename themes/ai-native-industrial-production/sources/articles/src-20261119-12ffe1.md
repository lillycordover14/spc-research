London-based drug-discovery AI company 
Boltz announced
 a $110 million Series A round on November 18, 2026, led by Bessemer Venture Partners. The new capital will fund deeper investment in compute and data, help meet demand from partners and support expansion of the company’s team.

Returning investors 
Andreessen Horowitz
, Amplify Partners, Zetta Venture Partners and Obvious Ventures participated, joined by new backers Greycroft, F-Prime Capital, Hummingbird and Benchling. Boltz said it has grown from a small group of MIT researchers to 40 scientists and engineers in just over a year.

Boltz reported partnerships with six of the world’s 20 largest pharmaceutical companies, including Pfizer, GSK, Takeda and Bristol Myers Squibb, alongside 50-fold growth in molecular-token usage among paying customers since June 2026. The company builds its business model around token usage for biomolecular design, making that increase an early indicator of commercial activity around technology that originated in open-source research.

Commercial Platform Builds on Research Adoption

Boltz launched Boltz Lab in January 2026, and thousands of scientists signed up within its first few weeks, according to the company. The platform now serves pharmaceutical, biotechnology and industrial companies, with scientists using it for work ranging from target validation to lead optimization. Boltz’s API, hosted inference, fine-tuning services and integrations with AI agents also allow researchers to run its models inside existing workflows.

The business emerged from research at MIT’s CSAIL and Jameel Clinic. Gabriele Corso is chief executive, Jeremy Wohlwend is chief technology officer and Saro Passaro is head of research. 
Bessemer said
 their open-source models have exceeded 5 million downloads and are used by more than 100,000 scientists across thousands of biotechnology companies and laboratories. That established user base helps explain why Boltz’s commercial launch could draw on researchers already familiar with its tools.

Series A Funds Proprietary Model Development

In June 2026, Boltz released the proprietary models BoltzMol-1 for small-molecule design and BoltzProt-1 for protein design. Boltz said it used the models to discover small-molecule hits and design protein binders for a majority of the challenging targets it pursued, at a fraction of the time and cost required by traditional methods or earlier models, and that its upcoming models are already showing substantial improvements in generated-molecule quality.

The company’s stated long-term objective is to enable a scientist to move from a therapeutic hypothesis to a human-ready molecule entirely through computation. Reaching that objective requires further model advances and substantial investment in compute and data, directly connecting the research program to the Series A’s planned spending.

The financing ties Boltz’s commercial expansion to its research agenda. Rising paying-customer usage is increasing the workload its platform must support, while improving molecular-design models demands more infrastructure and specialized staff. The company’s ability to expand both capacity and model quality will shape the next stage of its effort to make advanced drug-discovery tools broadly accessible.

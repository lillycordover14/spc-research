Dyndrite brings full toolpath control to Xact Metal LPBF 3D printers
 

 

3D printing software developer 
Dyndrite
 and metal additive manufacturing (AM) company 
Xact Metal
 have integrated direct scan-card control into Dyndrite LPBF Pro, allowing users to program native arcs and other non-linear toolpath on Xact Metal laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) systems.

The integration is available on the XM200G and XM200G µHD, with support for the XM300G planned to follow. By providing direct access to the printers’ scanning systems, the companies say users can exercise greater control over how the laser moves and delivers energy during the build process.

Conventional LPBF toolpaths typically approximate curves using multiple short straight-line vectors. According to Dyndrite, maintaining 1 µm geometric accuracy on a circle with a 10 mm radius requires around 222 such vectors, compared with a single command when using a native arc. Changes in direction between vectors can also require the scanning mirrors to decelerate, affecting local energy delivery.

“This is a hugely important step for LPBF,” said Harshil Goel, founder and CEO of Dyndrite. “For too long the industry has had to cope with inadequate toolpath definitions.”

Extending control over LPBF toolpaths

Beyond native arcs, the integration supports Bézier curves and wobble movements, as well as individual mark, jump and polyline delay settings. Users can also configure skywriting parameters and apply dynamic laser power settings and pulsing.

These commands are sent directly to the printer’s scan card, enabling engineers to adjust laser and scanner behavior according to the geometry being produced instead of relying exclusively on linear toolpath movements.

Dyndrite highlighted thin circular walls as one potential application. When a 1 mm-thick ring is filled using straight hatch lines, the short passes at its sides can cause the laser to return to neighboring areas before they have cooled. In the company’s illustrative example, 150 short lines are replaced by eight continuous arcs that follow the wall.

For turbine blade airfoils, Dyndrite LPBF Pro can use parametric hatching to follow the curvature of the component while reducing laser power as the trailing edge becomes narrower.

The company stresses that these examples demonstrate available capabilities rather than recommended production strategies. Any toolpath must still be validated against the required metallurgical and mechanical properties of the finished component.

Dyndrite and Xact Metal say the additional control could reduce build-file sizes and print times, limit wear on optical components, improve surface finish, and reduce porosity and defects. However, the companies have not released comparative build data demonstrating these improvements across specific materials or machine configurations.

Hatching a thin circular wall: raster vectors vs native arcs. Image via Dyndrite.

Expanding machine-level toolpath control

The Xact Metal integration follows Dyndrite’s previous work to extend LPBF Pro’s control over machine toolpaths. In 2025, Dyndrite integrated LPBF Pro with EOS Open Architecture⁠￼, using the EOS Toolpath API to give engineers vector-level control over laser speeds, feeds and exposure strategies.

That integration enabled exposure parameters to be adjusted for features including thin walls, overhangs and bulk regions within the same build. The Xact Metal integration extends Dyndrite’s machine-level toolpath capabilities through direct scan-card access, including native arcs, Bézier curves and other scan commands.

The development also adds to the process control available across Xact Metal’s LPBF portfolio. Its XM200G platform already allows experienced users to adjust process parameters including melting power and scan speed, while the new Dyndrite integration extends control to the toolpath and scanning system itself.

Dyndrite and Xact Metal are demonstrating the integration at Formnext 2026 in Frankfurt from 17–20 November at Xact Metal’s stand in Hall 12.0, Booth C89.

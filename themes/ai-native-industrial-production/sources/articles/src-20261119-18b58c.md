GlobalFoundries (GF) will begin on-site trials of Boston Dynamics' Atlas humanoid robot at its chip fabs in early 2027, making it the first chipmaker to test Atlas, the 
two companies announced Nov. 17
. The trial is a test, not a rollout: no 
Atlas task, fab or robot count
 has been named, so the best evidence so far comes from Spot, Boston Dynamics' four-legged inspection robot, whose reported results come from sub-fab areas, the utility space beneath the cleanroom floor.
Fab engineers, investors and chip customers now have a concrete test of whether humanoids can leave car plants for a far less forgiving factory. Readers can use the numbers below to judge what GF's Atlas pilot has to prove: which task, in which part of the fab, under which cleanliness and safety rules.
Spot Logged 90,000-Plus Singapore Inspections Since April, Company Figures Show
Two Spot robots at GF's Singapore site have walked nearly 900 kilometers (about 559 miles) through sub-fab areas and completed 
more than 90,000 inspections since April
, with a 99% mission success rate, according to the announcement. Those figures 
come from the companies
 and have not been independently verified. The release does not define what counted as a failed mission.
GF has run Spot at its Burlington, Vermont, site for 
more than four years
, and the new agreement adds fabs in Malta, New York; Dresden, Germany; and Singapore. After software and hardware updates, GF 
moved 875 routine inspections to Spot
 in two weeks and plans to more than double that number using Spot's visual inspection features. The release does not say what the updates were.
GF Chief Manufacturing Officer Pradip Singh says Spot has moved GF from 
reactive to predictive maintenance
 by collecting data across thousands of inspection points, with the goal of catching equipment problems before they cause downtime.

What Does Sub-Fab Space Hold, and Why Send Robot Dogs There?
A sub-fab is 
usually a large open space
 packed with vacuum pumps, gas abatement systems and other support equipment, and almost every cleanroom process tool depends on the vacuum and abatement services down there. Failures there are expensive. 
In one vendor case study
 of an unnamed foundry, harsher process chemistries cut pump maintenance intervals from every two to three years to roughly twice a week, and each event cost the process tool 4 to 8 hours of production. The foundry put its annual loss from unplanned sub-fab events at about $1.3 million, a vendor estimate that has not been independently verified.
GF's case for a mobile robot rests on economics. 
Its Burlington case study
 says putting sensors on thousands of inspection points is not economical, and that GF's equipment has a long life cycle and slow rate of change, which suits monitoring assets over time. GF senior factory-automation engineer Adrien Plouffe told Semiconductor Engineering in 2024 that 
one mobile sensor can replace
 data that would otherwise need many fixed sensors, and named integration across systems as the biggest challenge.
The mechanism has three layers. Spot carries the sensors to each asset. Computer-vision models from Boston Dynamics partner Levatas (now InFlightAI), 
built around gauges and motors
, teach Spot to read them. Boston Dynamics has also integrated 
Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6
 into its AIVI-Learning inspection software, which supports reading gauges and measuring sight glass levels. The tradeoff: Boston Dynamics says customers must share data with it to use the AIVI-Learning feature, and that the data goes only to Boston Dynamics. The announcement does not say whether GF's fleet uses that feature. For a reader judging predictive maintenance, the lesson is that mobile coverage trades continuous monitoring for periodic snapshots, and that snapshot data has limited value unless 
it reaches maintenance systems
.
Cleanroom Limits Could Test Atlas Where Spot's Sub-Fab Rounds Did Not
The announcement does not say where Atlas will work. If a task lands in the cleanroom, strict particle limits apply. Semiconductor facilities often run at 
ISO Class 7 or 5
 levels, and ISO Class 5 allows no more than 3,520 particles of 0.5 micrometers (about 20 millionths of an inch) or larger per cubic meter of air (about 100 per cubic foot). Equipment brought into a cleanroom must generate little contamination and be compatible with the processing fluids used there, so a humanoid's suitability has to be measured, not assumed.
Fraunhofer IPA has built a benchmark
 that tests humanoids for particle emission under ISO 14644-14, plus collision safety and cybersecurity. In its published test case, Unitree's G1 could be suitable for ISO Class 5 cleanrooms, but it also produced collision forces above 500 newtons (about 112 pounds of force) and had a Bluetooth flaw that allowed remote takeover, since fixed. That was a different robot. The announcement coverage reviewed gives no cleanroom class for Atlas.
Safety rules are also unsettled. 
A powered-off biped falls
, unlike a wheeled robot, which is why a separate draft standard, ISO 25785-1, covers industrial robots that need active control to stay upright. ISO lists the standard as 
still under development
 at committee-draft stage. Estimates for completion range from 
late 2026 or 2027
, with Fraunhofer IPA's coverage saying humanoid standards are 
not expected until 2028
. Humanoids Daily notes that fabs add 
cleanroom rules, chemical and gas handling
 and continuous operation of costly tools to the usual car-plant challenges.

Atlas Hand Gains Six Degrees of Freedom, but Fab Tasks Remain Unnamed
Boston Dynamics unveiled a new Atlas hand on Oct. 1 with four fingers and 
13 degrees of freedom
, up from seven, built for sim-to-real reinforcement learning. The thumb has four degrees of freedom and each remaining finger has three. Atlas itself offers 
56 degrees of freedom
 and a payload of up to 50 kilograms (110 pounds).
Dexterity matters because fab work is fine manipulation. A Samsung Austin Semiconductor engineer 
told Semiconductor Engineering in 2024
 that hand degrees of freedom were among the last problems to solve. STMicroelectronics showed video of a humanoid 
loading a wafer carrier
 into a machine, a step many modern fabs hand to front opening unified pods (FOUPs) and dedicated transport equipment. Whether GF targets a task like that, or something in the sub-fab, is the open question.
GlobalFoundries Is First With Atlas, Not First With Humanoids in Chip Fabs
Oversonic Robotics, an Italian firm, and STMicroelectronics 
announced a supply agreement
 in December 2025, with the first RoBee humanoid already installed at ST's advanced packaging and test facility in Malta, the Mediterranean island, according to Oversonic's release. In March, ST said it expects to 
deploy more than 100 humanoids
 across its sites over several years. GF's distinction is narrower: it is the first chipmaker, and the 
first non-automotive customer
, in Boston Dynamics' Atlas early-adopter program.
The program is young. Boston Dynamics 
committed all 2026 Atlas deployments
 to Hyundai's Robotics Metaplant Application Center and Google DeepMind, and said it would add customers in early 2027. Hyundai, which owns Boston Dynamics, has told investors it 
plans more than 25,000 Atlas robots
 in its own plants, while a Korean union says Atlas needs a labor agreement first. GF 
links the Atlas workshop
 to its company-wide effort to train employees in AI.
The pilot starts with a skills workshop to 
evaluate use cases and readiness
, 
assess integration requirements, pick tasks
 and begin early task training. 
No mass deployment has been decided
. Spot's results show a robot can inspect a sub-fab at scale; they do not show a humanoid can work to cleanroom standards. Watch for four disclosures: the first Atlas task, the fab zone, the cleanliness class and test data, and the safety standard GF applies.
Frequently Asked Questions
Will Atlas work inside the cleanroom at GlobalFoundries?
That has not been announced. The companies named 
no site, task or robot count
, and the reported Spot results come from sub-fab areas. Cleanroom suitability would need to be shown with 
particle-emission testing such as ISO 14644-14
, which Fraunhofer IPA's benchmark applies.
Does Spot's AI inspection require sharing fab data with Boston Dynamics?
For the AIVI-Learning feature, Boston Dynamics says 
data sharing is required
, and that the data is shared only with Boston Dynamics. The page does not specify the data types, retention or opt-out, and GF has not said whether it uses the feature.
When will Atlas testing at GlobalFoundries start?
Boston Dynamics and GF say on-site testing is set to 
begin in early 2027
. No exact date, fab or test duration has been given.
What safety standard covers humanoid robots in factories?
No finished standard yet covers them specifically. ISO 25785-1, for industrial mobile robots that need active control to stay upright, is 
still a committee draft
. Employers still have existing duties, such as 
OSHA's General Duty Clause
.

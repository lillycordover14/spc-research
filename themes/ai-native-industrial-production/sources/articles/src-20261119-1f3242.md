Raytheon awards Ursa Major and Avio USA contract for Tomahawk booster&nbsp;&nbsp;Naval Technology

[page text]
USS Spruance (DDG 111) fires a Tomahawk Land Attack Missile (TLAM) in support of Operation Epic Fury. Credit: Faizinraz/Shutterstock.com.

Raytheon, an RTX business, has awarded Ursa Major and Avio USA a joint contract to design, develop, and qualify a new solid rocket motor booster for the Tomahawk cruise missile, the companies announced.

The move establishes both firms as prospective US suppliers on the Tomahawk propulsion programme and aims to strengthen the domestic production base for this critical US Navy weapon.

Under the multi-year contract, both companies will work as strategic partners on the programme.

Avio USA will contribute its experience in solid rocket propulsion, established manufacturing capabilities, and commitment to developing advanced domestic production.

The contract also aligns with the company’s planned solid rocket motor manufacturing facility in southern Virginia, construction of which began in September.

Avio USA CEO James Syring said: “This award also marks a significant step forward for our planned US manufacturing facility, which we are building to become a vital asset in America’s defence industrial base for decades to come.”

Ursa Major will provide its engineering expertise and modern manufacturing methods to the programme.

This contract also builds on Ursa Major’s growing portfolio of solid rocket motors and its ongoing investment in production capacity, which includes supplying propulsion systems for various tactical missile programmes.

Together, the companies plan to qualify their jointly developed booster, aiming to establish eligibility for potential future production contracts.

Ursa Major president Jason Meredith said: “Our partnership with Avio USA brings together complementary capabilities to create additional reliable sources of solid rocket motors for Raytheon and the US Navy. 

“At the end of the day, this is about making sure the warfighter has the critical munitions they need, when they need them.”

For Raytheon, adding qualified domestic suppliers is intended to increase competition, provide greater production flexibility, and enhance supply chain resilience while supporting the ability to meet growing military demand for munitions.

In August this year, the US Department of War 
awarded Raytheon a $22.9bn contract
 to accelerate Tomahawk land attack missile (TLAM) production.

Additionally, the US Department of State recently 
approved a possible $300m Foreign Military Sale
 to Australia for Tomahawk capability development, signalling persistent international demand.

Raytheon Naval Power president Sam Deneke said: “Expanding the industrial base for Tomahawk propulsion is essential to ensuring this critical capability remains ready for the fleet.

“By dual-sourcing this critical component with both Avio USA and Ursa Major, we’re strengthening our supply chain, encouraging innovation, and building the capacity needed to meet the Navy’s mission requirements well into the future.”

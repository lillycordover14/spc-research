US eyes $2 billion financing for Philippines defense, West Philippine Sea deterrence
 

Pia Lee-Brago
 - The Philippine Star
November 20, 2026 | 12:00am

 
US President Donald Trump arrives in Manila on Tuesday, November 17, for the 49th ASEAN Summit.
Facebook  /  ASEAN 2026 PH
 

MANILA, Philippines — The United States yesterday announced multibillion-dollar deals with the Philippines, including Foreign Military Financing loan package of more than $2 billion and direct financing of $400 million, aimed at expanding its longtime treaty ally’s operational reach and deterrence capabilities in the South China Sea.

The White House said the “historic” defense and economic deals were secured during US President Donald Trump’s two-day state visit to Manila.

Trump concluded Wednesday his state visit to the Philippines including attending the Association of Southeast Asian Nations Summit, where he announced over $17 billion in commercial deals and strategic investments for US companies.

“President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. hosted President Trump for a historic State Visit in Manila, where the two leaders announced landmark deals that will strengthen the US-Philippine Alliance and support American companies and workers,” the White House said in a statement.

Trump said on Wednesday that the US and the Philippines agreed on the “biggest trade deal, fair deal” for both countries.

While he mentioned food and energy, the nature of the “biggest trade deal” remains unclear.

The leaders have also committed to eliminate the US trade deficit with the Philippines through increased Philippine imports of American energy and agriculture products, which is expected to support the jobs of tens of thousands of American engineers, technicians, and farmers.

The biggest item is a $12 billion sale of up to 20 Boeing 787 aircraft to Philippine Airlines.

Boeing finalized the aircraft sale to PAL along with another US firm GE Aerospace. The deal is expected to support 47,000 US jobs.

The agreement also includes a $40 million fuel depot in Subic Bay to support US military and commercial operations.

The Philippines, meanwhile, will modernize its Basco Airport in Batanes to improve US-Philippine operational access, disaster response, and commercial supply chains in the Philippines’ northernmost islands.

“President Trump’s America First Arms Transfer Strategy ensures that the surge in allies’ spending benefits the US defense industry, reindustrializing America and supporting American jobs while arming our partners with the best capabilities in the world,” the White House said.

The Department of National Defense has also announced a $37-million contract with a US firm for the provision of a Super Intelligence technology to modernize the Philippines defense cloud infrastructure through the US SI Exports Program. The arrangement is seen to support 140 US jobs.

The two countries have also committed to improve cooperation in critical technologies and announced exploration of an intergovernmental agreement on civil-nuclear technology which would open opportunities for US nuclear companies, marking the first US civil-nuclear intergovernmental agreement with an Indo-Pacific partner.

Astranis, a US-based company, announced it is offering connectivity services from its US manufactured AGILA satellite to the Philippines, a commercial opportunity worth up to $200 million. The deal is intended to provide independent and secure communications infrastructure for the Philippine government and the people.

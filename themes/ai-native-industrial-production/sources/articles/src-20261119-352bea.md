							By Brian Murphy, WRAL News
						

CHAPEL HILL —- North Carolina needs one win in its final two regular season games to become bowl eligible for the first time under head coach Bill Belichick — and the Tar Heels might have to turn to a freshman quarterback to get it done.

Starting quarterback Billy Edwards, Jr. is listed as questionable for the Tar Heels (5-5), who will be without several top defensive players for Saturday’s game at Virginia (5-5). Kickoff is at 12:30 p.m. on The CW.

Other WRAL Top Stories

Edwards, who left the Louisville game and didn’t return after a big hit, 
is listed as questionable
. Belichick said earlier in the week that Edwards was “getting better.” Edwards, in his sixth season in college football, has completed 65.5% of his passes. He has 18 touchdowns and six interceptions.

Backup Travis Burgess, a freshman, saw his first playing time replacing Edwards against Louisville. Burgess completed 6-of-11 passes for 59 yards and one interception and also rushed for 35 yards, when accounting for sacks. Though Edwards has shown the ability to run when necessary, Burgess’ running ability could be a feature of the Tar Heels’ offense.

“You always try to highlight what a quarterback, your starting quarterback, does well and maybe [there are] some things he’s not as comfortable with or needs more work on for whatever reason,” Belichick said.

Belichick pointed to his NFL success with Tom Brady at quarterback for the New England Patriots and how the team adjusted with backups Matt Cassel, Jimmy Garoppolo and Jacoby Brissett when Brady missed time.

“We have the same offense, but some plays are highlighted a little bit more with one guy than another,” he said.

Belichick and Brady won six Super Bowl titles together in New England. Brady, who split time as Michigan’s starter  in college, was a sixth-round pick in the NFL Draft and a fourth-string quarterback in New England. Brady became New England’s starter after an injury to Drew Bledsoe early in the 2001 season. He capped that season with a win in the Super Bowl.

“I played with an inexperienced quarterback one time in a Super Bowl, and he played pretty good,” Belichick said. “It’s not all about experience. It’s about, honestly, performance. I don’t think you have to be a 15-year veteran to win a Super Bowl. In fact, I know you don’t.”

Belichick said the biggest difference is simply handling the speed and intensity of the game. Quarterbacks don’t get hit in practice.

“It’s easy to go back there and chuck it on the mound when nobody’s coming at you,” Belichick said. “You throw 100 mph and let it go. A little bit different when they’re running at you.”

North Carolina defensive back Greg Smith III was suspended by the ACC for the first half of the Virginia game for his actions in a postgame fight with Louisville. He is listed as out for Saturday’s game. Smith leads the Tar Heels with three interceptions and has a forced fumble and a fumble recovery. He has 56 tackles, including three for loss, in 10 games.

Defensive end Melkart Abou Jaoude, who missed time earlier in the season with a broken bone in his right hand, is also out for Saturday’s game. Abou Jaoude has 21 tackles, including two sacks, in addition to a team-high nine quarterback hurries in eight games this season. He was a second-team All-ACC pick last year.

The Tar Heels remain without defensive back Tre Miller and linebacker Jonathan Agumadu, key contributors to the defense earlier in the season.

Belichick said that Smith and the Louisville player involved “both clearly threw punches” in the postgame fight and that he supported the penalties handed down by the ACC.

“We’re one of the least penalized teams in college football,” Belichick said. “We take a lot of pride in that. We’re not out there for anything other than to play football and win games and we have a lot of respect for this sport, this team, this institution and the way we play. Disappointed in what happened there, but that’s been addressed and hopefully won’t be an issue again.”

The Tar Heels went 4-8 in Belichick’s first season, but a victory over Virginia this week or NC State (4-6) in the regular-season finale will secure bowl eligibility. UNC played in six consecutive bowl games from the 2019 season through 2024, but lost their last five.

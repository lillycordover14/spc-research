Raytheon, the missile and defense unit of RTX (RTX), has awarded Ursa Major and Avio USA a joint contract to design, develop and qualify a new solid rocket motor booster for the Tomahawk cruise missile, a move that widens the supplier base for a critical Navy weapon and trims the long-term program share held by incumbent contractor L3Harris (LHX).

The multi-year award, announced Wednesday, does not hand either company a production order. Instead, it creates a qualification pathway: the two firms will jointly design the booster, then demonstrate that it meets requirements for future production contracts. Avio brings decades of solid rocket propulsion and manufacturing experience to the effort, while Colorado-based Ursa Major contributes propulsion design and advanced manufacturing methods.

Jefferies weighed in Thursday, telling clients that the dual-sourcing decision amounts to a "modest incremental negative" for L3Harris' long-term position in the Tomahawk program. The brokerage, however, does not anticipate any near-term revenue hit as Tomahawk output rises, according to The Fly. Jefferies kept a Hold rating and a $280 price target on L3Harris shares.

Trading was mixed. L3Harris stock rose about 1% while RTX slipped slightly. Year to date, L3Harris has lost roughly 12.2% and RTX has gained around 7.5%. Among the 23 analysts covering L3Harris, 14 rate the shares Buy or Strong Buy and nine rate them Hold, with 12-month targets spanning $252 to $402 and averaging $333.95.

Raytheon framed the arrangement as a supply-chain and capacity play. "By dual-sourcing this critical component with both Avio USA and Ursa Major, we're strengthening our supply chain, encouraging innovation, and building the capacity needed to meet the Navy's mission requirements well into the future," said Sam Deneke, president of Naval Power at Raytheon.

Both suppliers are investing in domestic infrastructure. Avio USA broke ground in September on a solid rocket motor plant in southern Virginia, and its chief executive, James Syring, called the award an important step for that facility and for the company's place in the U.S. defense supply chain. Ursa Major has been broadening its solid rocket motor portfolio and production capacity, work that spans tactical missiles, hypersonic weapons and space mobility systems. Its president, Jason Meredith, said the partnership would give Raytheon and the Navy additional reliable sources of solid rocket motors.

The Tomahawk is a long-range cruise missile the Navy uses to strike targets at extended distance, and its propulsion system is central to that reach. Adding qualified booster suppliers is intended to give Raytheon more production flexibility and reduce dependence on a small number of sources for a component that sits on a heavily used munitions line.

The announcement lands amid a broader push by the Pentagon to expand the U.S. defense industrial base as demand climbs for precision-guided munitions. For investors, the immediate read-through is limited: no production dollars have changed hands, and the qualification process must be completed before either newcomer can compete for orders. The longer-term question is how much of the Tomahawk booster work shifts away from L3Harris once that pathway closes, a share-of-program risk Jefferies described as modest rather than material.

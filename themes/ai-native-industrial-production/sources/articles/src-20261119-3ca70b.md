A computer generated image of Saronic's planned Port Alpha shipyard in Brownsville, Texas (Saronic Technologies)

Saronic has awarded a contract to Norwegian firm Syncrolift to design and deliver a large shiplift and transfer system for Port Alpha, the shipyard it is building in Brownsville, Texas, the company announced on 12 November.

The contract, worth more than USD100 million, covers a system with a 300 m (984 ft) platform and a lifting capacity of 67,000 tonnes, according to Saronic.

      “The shiplift's scale reflects the shipbuilding capacity we are bringing online at Port Alpha, which will initially be capable of producing vessels up to 850 ft (259 m) long,” a Saronic spokesperson told 
      
Janes

       on 19 November, adding that future site expansion could support production of vessels over 1,200 ft long.
    

Enabling capability

Launching can be a shipyard bottleneck because a vessel sits in a dry dock or on a slipway until it is put into the water, preventing construction from starting on a new vessel in that berth, the spokesperson explained.

The company has designed Port Alpha “around a software-defined, high-throughput production model in which final assembly is consolidated into a small number of large-block lifts with vessels moving between stations rather than being built in fixed docks”, the statement said.

The Syncrolift shiplift is enables “production to progress across the yard without requiring a separate fixed dock for each vessel”, the spokesperson added.

“The system pairs a rigid lifting platform with advanced condition-monitoring and digital-twin control technology that supports safe, closely monitored operation at every stage of a lift,” according to the statement.

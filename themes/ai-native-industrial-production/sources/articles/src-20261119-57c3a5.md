Tulip Siddiq weighs legal action against Yunus over corruption allegations&nbsp;&nbsp;The Business Standard

[page text]
Former UK minister says she was a victim of ‘malicious prosecution’ and that lawyers believe she has grounds to sue Bangladesh’s former interim chief adviser.    

TBS Report

      19 November, 2026, 02:05 pm    

      Last modified: 19 November, 2026, 02:06 pm    

 

          Former Chief Adviser Dr Muhammad Yunus and former Britain’s anti-corruption minister Tulip Siddiq. Photos: Collaged        

 

Former UK minister Tulip Siddiq is considering legal action against Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus over corruption allegations brought against her by Bangladesh's former interim government, which led to her resignation from the UK government last year.

Siddiq, the Labour MP for Hampstead and Highgate, said she was exploring her legal options with lawyers who believe she has grounds to sue Yunus, who led Bangladesh's interim government from 2024 to 2026, The Guardian reported.

Speaking on 'Politics Unleashed', a podcast hosted by Guto Harri and David Davis, Siddiq described the proceedings against her as a "malicious prosecution".

She said she was consulting a reputable lawyer and considering legal action over the allegations.

Yunus, approached for comment through two close associates, declined to comment.

Siddiq, a niece of former Bangladesh prime minister Sheikh Hasina, was accused by Bangladeshi authorities of benefiting from the former Awami League government through her aunt's influence.

In December 2025, a Bangladeshi court sentenced Siddiq to two years in prison in absentia after finding her guilty of influencing Hasina to secure a plot of land for her family on the outskirts of Dhaka. She was tried alongside 16 others.

Siddiq, who lives in London, has strongly denied the allegations and is unlikely to serve the sentence.

She said she had repeatedly sought details of the accusations against her, but the Bangladesh government had not responded to letters sent by her lawyers.

She also revealed that she had sent a handwritten letter to Yunus while he was staying at London's Dorchester Hotel, but he declined to meet her despite repeated attempts.

Describing her experience, Siddiq said she felt more baffled than stressed because she knew she was innocent.

She compared the trial to a "Kafkaesque nightmare", saying she had no idea what she was being convicted of until she woke up one morning to a Sky News alert reporting her conviction.

Siddiq also spoke about the support she received from former UK prime minister Keir Starmer during the controversy.

She said Starmer sent her a message while he was in the United States with Donald Trump after she had received a security threat.

However, she eventually decided to resign after Starmer was repeatedly questioned about her.

"I had been part of the project to get Keir into No 10. I had supported him from the beginning … and then here I am, being the obstacle," she said.

Siddiq described Starmer's departure from the premiership as "very painful" but praised his successor, Andy Burnham.

She said her priority remained securing another Labour government and that she would put aside her personal sadness if Burnham could help deliver that outcome.

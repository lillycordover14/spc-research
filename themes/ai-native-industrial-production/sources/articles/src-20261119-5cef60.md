At least one American woman qualified for each of the last four Grand Prix Finals, including Levito in 2022 and 2023. Olympic teammates Alysa Liu and Amber Glenn won the last two Finals but are sitting out this Grand Prix season. Levito is the last American woman with a chance to make this year’s Final.

Levito was third at her previous Grand Prix in Canada last month. The women who beat her there — Japan’s Mao Shimada and Estonia’s Niina Petrokina — are also in the Finland field, looking to qualify for the Final.
The Grand Prix Final is the most exclusive event in the sport, and the second-most prestigious annual competition after the World Championships held every March. The Final, in China from Dec. 10-13, features the top six per discipline from the six-event Grand Prix Series, where the world’s top skaters each compete twice.
Shimada, who is in her first senior season after going undefeated in four years as a junior, won Skate Canada with the world’s best score to that point in the season — 235.44 points. 
Countrywoman Ami Nakai supplanted her by tallying 236.81 last week at Saatva Skate America. How Shimada performs in Finland could determine the favorite going into the Final.
Shimada will also bid to give Japanese women five wins in five Grand Prix events so far this season. Only one nation has swept the Grand Prix titles in one year in a single discipline — the Russian women in 2019.
Also at Grand Prix Finland, Adam Siao Him Fa goes for a sweep of his Grand Prix regular season starts after winning his home event in France last month.
The man who likely would have been his top challenger — Yevgeny Semenenko — was withdrawn on Wednesday. All of the skaters from Russia who were in the field — Semenenko and three pairs’ teams — were unable to travel to the event due to Finland’s entry restrictions on Russian citizens.

The three pairs’ teams from Russia who were replaced are ranked Nos. 2-4 in the world this season by best total score, led by 2022 Olympic bronze medalists Anastasia Mishina and Aleksandr Galliamov.
In their absences, Canadians Lia Pereira and Trennt Michaud have an easier path to complete a sweep of their two Grand Prix starts.
In ice dance, Olympic gold medalists Laurence Fournier Beaudry and Guillaume Cizeron of France go for their second Grand Prix win this season.
Interested onlookers may be Americans Emilea Zingas and Vadym Kolesnik, who last week won their season debut at Skate America with the world’s second-best score this Grand Prix season (201.88) behind the French (207.57). Those two couples could face off for the first time this season at the Final.

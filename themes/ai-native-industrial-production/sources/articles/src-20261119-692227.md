PARIS — France and Sweden held their first joint exercise under the forward deterrence initiative proposed by French President Emmanuel Macron, with French nuclear-capable Rafale jets and an Airbus A330 MRTT tanker aircraft operating over Sweden alongside Swedish Gripen fighters.

The exercise, dubbed Excalibur, was held in northern Sweden on Nov. 16 to 18, with the aim of improving interoperability between the French and Swedish air forces, the two countries’ armed forces said in statements on Thursday. The exercise didn’t involve actual nuclear weapons and was not directed at any country, the defense ministries said.

The French-Swedish cooperation follows an exercise last month in which British Eurofighter Typhoon jets escorted French Rafale jets on a 
simulated nuclear-strike mission
. France has been working with several European allies to implement Macron’s proposal to 
bolster European deterrence
, in the face of nuclear saber-rattling by Russia and uncertainty about United States security guarantees.

France’s nuclear deterrent consists of an ocean-based component comprising strategic ballistic-missile submarines, as well as an airborne component consisting of Rafale jets capable of carrying nuclear-tipped cruise missiles developed by MBDA.

“Since the in-depth dialogue on ‘forward deterrence’ was initiated, France and Sweden are taking steps to implement their common roadmap for strategic cooperation,” the 
Swedish Armed Forces said
. The exercise “demonstrates our joint readiness and capability to act together and reflects the commitment of our two countries towards deterring threats and strengthening allied security.”

The deployment of the Rafale jets alongside Gripen fighters, with the support of an A330 MRTT, demonstrated the high level of interoperability between the two air forces in the areas of nuclear deterrence, in the service of Europe’s security, the French Armed Forces General Staff said in a 
post on the social network X
.

“This type of deployment not only allows to test the dispersion of French aircraft to improve their capability to survive an adversary disabling strike, but also opens the door for new modes of operation previously unimaginable, such as attacking targets deep within the Russian Arctic or planning much smaller raids without needing in-flight refueling,” said Etienne Marcuz, a senior analyst at the Fondation pour la Recherche Stratégique, 
in a post
 on X.

Sweden’s Gripen fighters are “excellent combat aircraft” specifically designed to penetrate Russian ground-to-air and air-to-air defense systems, and they could be valuable assets during a nuclear strike mission, while freeing up conventional Rafale jets for other missions, Marcuz said.

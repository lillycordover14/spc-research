When the leaders of powerful nations gather, they always give journalists plenty to dissect. But it’s pretty hard to focus on diplomatic pageantry or geopolitical stakes when Donald Trump keeps falling asleep on camera.

The setting for the president’s latest nap was the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, which took place this week in Shenzhen, China. As Chinese leader Xi Jinping delivered a speech, video captured Trump, 80, 
generally looking conked out
, sitting with his eyelids heavy, shoulders hunched, mouth askew, and head nodding. (On Thursday, the White House communications director heatedly denied that Trump had been caught dozing, claiming that the footage 
actually showed him studying documents
.)

In the president’s defense, international travel can be exhausting at any age. Yet Trump has repeatedly struggled to stay awake on this side of the globe too. An abbreviated list of places where he seems to have nodded off in recent years includes 
the Oval Office
, 
Cabinet meetings
, a 
9/11 memorial event
, and 
his own New York criminal trial
. There are other signs that our aging president may not be all there: 
flubbing words
, confusing 
names
 and 
places
, 
repeatedly visiting the doctor
, and 
more
.

•

This should be concerning to the White House—and the rest of us. But too often, it isn’t even remarked upon anymore. Write-ups of the summit in the 
New York Times
, the 
Wall Street Journal
, 
Washington Post
, 
Politico
, and 
Axios
 make no mention of Trump’s snooze. A one-sentence CNN live-blog entry does (“
Trump closes his eyes during Xi speech
”), but this particular faux-pazzz apparently didn’t rate the network’s 
list of summit takeaways
. So far, only a handful of U.S. outlets (including 
HuffPost
, the 
New Republic
, and the 
Daily Beast
) have dedicated stand-alone articles to it.

That lack of public scrutiny is striking given the media’s relentless focus on the risks of an aging president when that president was named Joe Biden. It’s true that 
medical privacy laws, the vagaries of aging, and outright deception
 can make it hard to ascertain the truth about a president’s health. And it’s not as if there’s been 
zero
 coverage of Trump’s; the 
Times
 and 
the Journal
 both published stories about it this year. Trump’s aging may also be getting less airtime because there’s no shortage of other things about him to cover critically.

But to be blunt, those are poor excuses. Having an octogenarian president in questionable health is a problem, or it’s not. Political journalists collectively embraced the former attitude during Biden’s term, and their coverage of Trump’s fitness hasn’t been proportionate either to its importance or to the scrutiny that was (and 
in some cases still is
) trained on his predecessor. Biden’s disastrous 2024 debate, which unfolded before the entire nation, uncorked an understandable media stampede to get to the bottom of his decline.

Trump falling asleep in front of the entire world should probably do something similar.

Tolga Akdoğan

A recent lawsuit has accused the company of “tomato fraud.” When food writer Danny Palumbo investigated, he discovered a tale full of spicy court documents, Italian warehouse raids, a mysterious outfit that sidesteps Italy’s official San Marzano police,

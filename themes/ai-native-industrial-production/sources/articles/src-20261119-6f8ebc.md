Raytheon has hired Ursa Major and Avio USA to develop a new Tomahawk cruise missile booster, opening a path for both to become qualified US suppliers.

Under the multi-year contract, the two companies will jointly design, develop, and qualify a new solid rocket motor booster for the US Navy missile, Ursa Major and Avio USA said in a 
joint statement
. They plan to qualify the booster and demonstrate eligibility for future production orders. The value of the contract was not disclosed.

The award adds domestic sources for a key Tomahawk component as Raytheon expands production. In August, the Pentagon awarded the RTX business a 
$22.9 billion, seven-year contract
 intended to lift output to more than 1,000 Tomahawks a year.

“By dual-sourcing this critical component with both Avio USA and Ursa Major, we’re strengthening our supply chain, encouraging innovation, and building the capacity needed to meet the Navy’s mission requirements well into the future,” said 
Sam Deneke
, president of Naval Power at Raytheon.

The companies said the work combines Avio’s experience in solid rocket propulsion with Ursa Major’s design approach and advanced manufacturing processes. Ursa Major, founded in 2015, is headquartered in Berthoud, Colorado, and manufactures in Youngstown, Ohio. It builds propulsion systems for several tactical missile programs.

Avio USA, the Arlington, Virginia-based subsidiary of Italy’s Avio, broke ground in September on a solid rocket motor plant in southern Virginia. Its chief executive, retired Navy Vice Admiral 
James Syring
, called the Tomahawk award “a significant step forward for our planned U.S. manufacturing facility.”

Raytheon and Avio signed a memorandum of understanding in November 2025 to establish that facility, with Raytheon getting preferred access to a share of its output, 
RTX said at the time
. That deal built on a July 2024 contract for preliminary engineering work on the Mk 104 rocket motor.

Avio is now the subject of a 
takeover bid from Finnish satellite operator ICEYE
. An Avio spokesman has said a deal would need approval from both the Italian and US governments.

The companies said establishing qualified domestic suppliers will allow Raytheon to expand competition and production flexibility. The next step for Ursa Major and Avio USA is qualification of the jointly designed booster, which would make them eligible to compete for production orders.

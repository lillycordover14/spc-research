MOTU Origins Shadow Beast Build-A-Figure Wave 1 In-Hand Images&nbsp;&nbsp;Toy Habits

[page text]
We first saw Mattel’s MOTU Origins Shadow Beast Build-A-Figure Wave 1 at SDCC 2026, and now we have an incredible collection of in-hand photographs of He-Man, Beast Man, and Battle Armor Man-At-Arms, courtesy of 
Mr. Home Arcade
! I want to give a 
HUGE shoutout to Mr. Home Arcade
 for taking the time to photograph these figures in such incredible detail and sharing everything with us. He went above and beyond with the packaging, new tooling, accessories, and comparisons with previous Origins releases. If you haven’t already, be sure to check out his 
YouTube channel
 and show him some love and be on the lookout for his YouTube review coming soon! 

Beast Man is easily my most anticipated figure of the three, especially after seeing how Mattel handled his vintage-inspired armor. He-Man might be the closest we’ve gotten to the original 1982 figure in Origins, and I’m really liking what I’m seeing so far.

We also finally know which Shadow Beast Build-A-Figure parts come with each release! Before we get into the individual figures, there’s some fantastic connected packaging artwork, a three-part storyline, and new leg tooling worth discussing.

Packaging Artwork and New Tooling

Mr. Home Arcade starts with the three Wave 1 packages together: Battle Armor Man-At-Arms, Beast Man, and He-Man. Mattel has kept the familiar Origins window-box presentation, with the Shadow Beast Build-A-Figure branding tying the assortment together.

The side panels connect into one illustration, with Man-At-Arms facing the Shadow Beasts, Beast Man at the center of the attack, and He-Man near Castle Grayskull. I love that Mattel is still using the packaging to tell a story, but it doesn’t replace the mini comics by any means and I’m really bummed these figures don’t include them.

There’s a genuine tooling update here, too. Mr. Home Arcade removed He-Man’s legs to show the new hip connections and the sockets inside the upper legs. It’s a useful detail for Origins collectors who are familiar with how the earlier bodies were assembled.

Mr. Home Arcade also removed an arm to document the shoulder connection inside Man-At-Arms torso. It’s a good look at the construction and happy MHA included these!

We also get our first close look at the Shadow Beast parts. He-Man includes the left leg, Battle Armor Man-At-Arms the right leg, and Beast Man the head. The purple fur, pale clawed feet, horn, and jagged teeth already give us a good sense of the creature’s design.

The reverse view reveals the connection sockets at the tops of the Shadow Beast’s legs, along with the sculpting around the knees and calves. Beast Man also includes the creature’s spiked weapon, while the remaining Build-A-Figure parts will come with later releases.

Mr. Home Arcade also demonstrates the new parts-swapping possibilities with these figures by attaching the Shadow Beast head to a different Origins body. This is awesome! I love seeing Mattel expand the interchangeability of the Origins line, especially with the new tooling introduced in this wave.

He-Man

Of the many He-Man figures Mattel has released in Origins, this might be the closest we’ve gotten to the original 1982 toy. Even through the packaging, the revised head sculpt, gray harness, and softer hair color have more of that vintage feel.

He-Man’s side illustration continues the battle against the Shadow Beasts, with Castle Grayskull in the background. Mattel has worked the story into the package artwork across the entire wave.

The opposite side carries the Shadow Beast Build-A-Figure graphics, including a reminder that this is only the first part of a larger assortment. 

The back of He-Man’s package shows Prince Adam taking cover as he transforms into He-Man, and joins the Heroic Warriors against the Shadow Beasts. It’s exactly the sort of story I wish we could have explored in a mini comic.

For anyone planning to hunt down He-Man at retail, Mr. Home Arcade included the UPC and assortment information from the bottom of the box. These details are handy to have when you’re checking inventory or trying to identify the right release.

Out of the package, the vintage feel comes through. The shape of the face, hair, and gray harness all feel closer to the He-Man I remember from the original line. Mattel has revisited him plenty of times in Origins, but this is a version I’ve really wanted to see.

The side profile gives us a better sense of the new head sculpt, especially the hair around the ears and back of the neck.

Mattel also changed the harness construction. The back has a broad rectangular section with straps over the shoulders, something we’ll be able to compare directly with an earlier Origins He-Man in a bit.

The other profile keeps the focus on the revised face and hair. Those might sound like small changes for a character we’ve gotten so many times, but they’re central to what separates this release from the earlier Origins versions.

The Power Sword can still be stored on the back of He-Man’s harness.

He-Man comes with his Power Sword and shield, both inspired by the original figure. More importantly, these in-hand photographs confirm that the axe displayed with He-Man at SDCC 2026 is not included with the retail release.

The softer hair color and facial proportions work especially well with the gray harness, and the shield keeps the familiar orange paint applications.

The figure’s vintage styling holds up across Mr. Home Arcade’s amazing shots. The revised face and armor are what make this particular He-Man interesting to me, and love how he looks raising his Power Sword.

The accessory close-up settles the contents of the release: the gray Power Sword and the gray-and-orange shield. There is no axe in the in-hand set, despite its appearance at SDCC 2026.

Side by side, the difference is even clearer. The earlier He-Man has bright yellow hair and a different face and harness; the new version has a softer hair color, revised facial features, and gray armor. Of the two, the new figure comes much closer to the original toy for me.

The earlier Origins He-Man has crossed straps across his back, while the new figure uses a much broader gray harness section. The hair sculpt and coloring have also changed.

Beast Man

Now we get to Beast Man, easily my most anticipated figure in this assortment. I’ve been waiting to see how Mattel would handle his vintage armor, and the oversized red shoulder pieces and blue chest emblem already look fantastic in the package.

Beast Man’s side illustration places him in the middle with the Shadow Beast in background. His connection to the creatures is more than an artistic choice; it’s the central idea behind the second chapter of the packaging story.

The other side continues the Build-A-Figure presentation used throughout Wave 1. 

The back of Beast Man’s box explains how he uses his telepathic connection with animals to take control of the Shadow Beasts and send them against the Heroic Warriors.

Mr. Home Arcade also photographed Beast Man’s UPC and assortment information. Collectors trying to find him in stores can use these identifiers when checking stock or confirming they’re looking at the correct figure.

Beast Man includes the Shadow Beast’s spiked weapon and head as his Build-A-Figure components. The weapon has a long shaft and a large spiked head.

Now that we’re looking at Beast Man out of the box, the armor is what really does this for me. The oversized red shoulder pieces, sculpted fur, and blue chest emblem feel much closer to the vintage cardback art than the earlier Origins version.

Beast Man also comes with his signature whip, with a black handle. I’m glad Mattel kept his familiar accessory alongside the Shadow Beast pieces.

The side view shows the thickness of the shoulder armor and how far the sculpted fur extends around the upper body. Mattel didn’t just change the colors; the shape of the armor is a major part of this redesign.

Here’s a closer look at the armor, easily my favorite part of this Beast Man. The red fur sculpting surrounds the blue chest emblem, and the large shoulder spikes follow the exaggerated styling of the original toy.

The other side reveals more of the layered sculpting in the shoulder armor. There’s a lot of character in this design, particularly compared with the simpler armor on the first Origins Beast Man.

The armor continues across Beast Man’s back as a broad section of sculpted red fur.

Seeing the two Beast Man figures together really puts the redesign into perspective. The new version has a different head sculpt, much larger red armor, and a blue chest emblem that stands out against the fur. This is the version I’ve been waiting for.

he earlier Origins Beast Man has a much smaller armor piece, while the new release carries the red fur sculpting across most of the upper back, which is one of the biggest changes between the figures.

The accessory photograph brings Beast Man’s whip together with the Shadow Beast’s spiked weapon. Both come with Beast Man, along with the Build-A-Figure head.

Battle Armor Man-At-Arms

Battle Armor Man-At-Arms is the one figure I’m least excited about in Wave 1. I like the vintage design, and I’m glad Mattel included the classic rolling-drum battle armor mechanism. I’ve just never been as enthusiastic about the battle-damage concept as I am about the other figures in this assortment.

Man-At-Arms gets his own section of the connected Shadow Beast illustration. The painted packaging artwork remains one of the things I enjoy most about Origins, even though I’m still disappointed about the missing mini comics.

The other side of Battle Armor Man-At-Arms’ packaging highlights the rolling-drum action feature, with illustrations of the three stages of battle damage.

The back of the package shows Man-At-Arms discovering Skeletor’s Shadow Beast threat during a mission for King Randor. The illustration includes Prince Adam, Man-At-Arms, and Man-E-Faces looking toward the Evil Warriors. Prince Adam is worth noting given 
our earlier reporting on possible Wave 6 figures
 as they are continuing to tease future releases.

For collectors planning to hunt down Battle Armor Man-At-Arms at retail, Mr. Home Arcade included the UPC and assortment information from the bottom of the box. It’s useful reference material when you’re checking listings or store inventory.

Out of the box, the familiar orange armor, green body, and blue helmet look good. The rolling-drum mechanism is a welcome vintage feature, even if the battle-damage concept isn’t my favorite.

From the other side, MHA shows Man-At-Arms’ familiar asymmetrical arm and leg armor.

The back armor covers much more of the torso than the earlier Origins Man-At-Arms.

Here we get a better look at Man-At-Arms’ helmet and the raised collar of his new Battle Armor. The orange chest armor extends up around his neck, giving this version a noticeably different profile from the earlier Origins release.

Here we get a look at the undamaged emblem on the rolling drum before the battle damage appears.

Rotating the drum reveals the first battle-damaged version of the emblem, with deep gouges across the center.

The final drum position shows the most heavily damaged version of the chest emblem.

Man-At-Arms includes his familiar blue mace, but the translucent shield shown at SDCC 2026 is not included with this release.

Mr. Home Arcade gives us another look at Man-At-Arms with his blue mace. I like the overall vintage styling, especially the familiar helmet and orange armor.

The mace retains its familiar rounded head, ridged detailing, and textured handle. The retail version does lack the paint application on the handle that we saw at SDCC 2026.

The front comparison with an earlier Origins Man-At-Arms highlights the larger chest armor and differences in the helmet and armor colors.

The new figure has a large orange back plate, while the earlier Origins Man-At-Arms leaves much more of the torso exposed with his traditional harness.

ABS and Radian Forge Test Whether Sensor Data Can Qualify 3D Printed Ship Parts
 

 

ABS
 and Portsmouth, Virginia-based
 Radian Forge
 have signed a joint development project (JDP). The project will evaluate whether digital manufacturing data can be used to qualify metal components built with wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM).

The agreement deals with a condition for wider use of additive manufacturing (AM) at sea. As the maritime industry looks at AM to improve component availability, confidence that printed parts perform consistently is critical. The partners will examine whether four types of data can together give a reliable technical basis for verifying and validating WAAM components: process parameters, real-time sensor data, inspection records and material test results.

“Digital qualification has an important role to play in the adoption of additive manufacturing across the marine and offshore sectors. We are proud to work with Radian Forge to strengthen confidence in additive manufacturing by improving how qualification evidence is generated, evaluated and traced,” said Michael Kei, ABS Vice President, Technology.

Splitting Production and Review

Under the JDP, Radian Forge will manufacture components for testing and validation, gather manufacturing and sensor data, and carry out applicable testing. ABS, which already publishes
 requirements for additive manufacturing
, will provide an independent technical review. It will evaluate the results and assess the proposed methodology and how it could be applied.

Radian Forge is developing on-demand manufacturing capacity for the U.S. maritime and defense industrial base. It combines industrial robotics, WAAM and digital process monitoring to offer an alternative production route for critical metal components.

“Traditional manufacturing timelines can create significant challenges when critical components are needed. Our goal is to build confidence in a manufacturing process that can respond to those needs. Partnering with ABS gives us an opportunity to rigorously evaluate how process data and material performance can support that objective,” said Jeff Yeager, CEO of Radian Forge.

Radian Forge Logo. Image via Radian Forge. 

Process Data as Qualification Evidence for Marine AM Parts

For WAAM parts to go into service at sea, the maritime industry needs confidence in their consistency and performance. The open issue in the ABS and Radian Forge JDP is where that confidence comes from: whether records generated while a part is built can support its verification and validation.

The U.S. Navy is pursuing a related approach through a program led by Senvol on metal wire directed energy deposition (DED). Senvol’s
 machine learning models link in-situ monitoring data to mechanical performance requirements.
 The goal is quantitative evidence for acceptance decisions that reduces reliance on extensive post-build qualification and testing. As in the ABS and Radian Forge JDP, data recorded during production is being evaluated as evidence that a part is acceptable.

ABS has also 
certified a WAAM marine part.
 Singapore’s Mencast Marine received certification from Lloyd’s Register and ABS for a bronze propeller, confirming that its WAAM process meets class standards for marine deployment. In both cases, ABS has evaluated WAAM for maritime use. The difference is the target: the Mencast certification covered one company’s propeller and process, while the JDP is studying the qualification method itself.

The ABS and Radian Forge JDP will test whether production data can serve as part of the evidence used to qualify WAAM parts.

State House Speaker Cameron Sexton has filed legislation to start discussions related to Tennessee's grocery tax. 
During the state's last legislative session, both a Republican and a Democratic version of the bill failed to pass through either chamber. Democrats wanted to tax corporations, while Republicans wanted to look for other give-and-take within the current confines of the existing budget. Neither side succeeded. 
As worded, the bill would eliminate the currently existing tax on milk, eggs and bread for human consumption. Sexton says this is just the beginning of the conversation.

Affordability was a major motivator among voters in 
the most recent election cycle
. In some states, that meant a change in who holds the power in government. While 
six state House seats flipped in Tennessee
, Republicans still retain a supermajority in the state House and Senate, and have the governor's seat. 
"This last election, if it taught us nothing else, is that what people want is something different," says Senate Minority Leader Raumesh Akbari (D-Memphis). She spoke about affordability at a recent panel on state policy held by the Sycamore Institute. "They don't want to go to the grocery store and spend all this money and still not be able to pay their bills. We can look more strategically at the grocery tax or temporary pause and ways to get back to the people." 
Gov.-Elect Marsha Blackburn has recently said she would like to work on affordability for Tennesseans. She has discussed creating further workforce housing for teachers, firefighters, nurses and police personnel. She has not directly addressed whether the grocery tax is a priority yet. 
The legislative session will start Jan. 12.

Direct3D-V3: Latent-Free Diffusion Transformers in the Native Voxel Domain&nbsp;&nbsp;alphaXiv

Abstract
Native 3D generation has largely been built around latent diffusion, with recent advances concentrating on stronger variational autoencoders (VAE) and increasingly elaborate latent representations, from vecset and triplanes to structured sparse voxels. Despite their architectural differences, these methods all share the same premise: geometry is first compressed into a latent space and only then modeled by the generator. We take a different route and propose Direct3D-V3, a latent-free diffusion transformer (DiT) framework that discards the VAE altogether and operates in the native voxel domain, directly modeling raw SDF voxel grids during both training and sampling. To make this direct voxel formulation geometrically reliable, we introduce an implicit manifold regularization over neighboring voxels 
to suppress non-watertight surfaces
,
[p
13
]
 and convert predicted SDF grids into meshes via Marching Cubes so that rendered normal losses can supervise the model directly from its predicted SDF. To improve alignment between the conditioning image and generated geometry, Direct3D-V3 also predicts the camera parameters of the conditioning image as an auxiliary task, encouraging the DiT to learn the mapping from image observations to 3D shape. This latent-free design removes the reconstruction bottleneck, brings training under an objective in the native voxel domain, and 
yields meshes with substantially richer local detail.
[p
11
]
 The predicted camera and native SDF voxel output further make Direct3D-V3 extensible to generative reconstruction, where diffusion is interleaved with normal-guided refinement to improve alignment with the observed view while the generative prior completes unseen regions. Project page: 
https://dreamtechai.github.io/Direct3D-V3
.

Turning one detailed reference image into a usable 3D asset is harder than it sounds: a mesh can capture the broad character of an object and still lose the fine hair, beard, or tread that made the image worth modeling. Direct3D-V3 targets this problem for settings like game and film asset creation, AR/VR authoring, and robotic simulation, where a high-fidelity mesh must preserve both detail and alignment to the observed view.
[p
2
]
 The authors argue that compressing a 3D object through a learned code can discard detail and leave the generator learning a distribution different from the geometry ultimately evaluated. That is their motivation rather than a separately measured comparison. Their move is to generate the shape's signed-distance values directly instead of a compressed code for a decoder.
[p
2
]
 The paper reports that hair and beard detail is better preserved in the first and fifth examples than in the compared methods.

Qualitative comparison: reference images at left, then gray mesh renders from five methods. Compare the same object across columns, especially hair and beard, tire tread, facial detail, and ship ropes. These are selected examples, not a guarantee for every input.

Generate in the Shape's Language

A signed distance field (SDF) stores, at each voxel, the signed distance to the nearest surface; the mesh is the zero-level surface of that field. Direct3D-V3 runs a diffusion transformer (DiT) directly on SDF values, which would be intractable on a dense grid, so it works on sparse patches near the surface.
[p
5
]
 At inference, a coarse first-stage model supplies the indices of these surface patches; they define where the high-resolution DiT operates, and sampling starts from Gaussian noise on those patches.
[p
4
]

Two transformers split the work. A patch-level DiT, conditioned on image tokens and a time-step signal, models broad structure. Its features condition a lightweight voxel-level DiT that refines SDF values inside each patch. Marching Cubes then extracts the mesh. The authors report scaling this approach to

Framework: a ground-truth mesh becomes an SDF grid and surface-patch SDF values; noise is added, image tokens and a time step feed the patch-level DiT, whose features condition the voxel-level DiT that predicts patch SDF values. A camera branch outputs camera parameters. At inference, the first-stage model supplies patch indices.

Two additions target reliability and alignment. Implicit-modulation regularization (IMR) penalizes neighboring predicted SDF values that jump by more than one voxel length, which reduces holes.

A Prior, Not a Fit

For single-view reconstruction, the model estimates a foreground mask and an input normal map, and calibrates the predicted camera against the mask. It interleaves SDF refinement with sampling: during early diffusion steps, the raw SDF is adjusted against rendered normals, then re-noised and returned to the sampler.
[p
9
]
 The learned prior can keep generating unseen geometry while visible geometry is pulled toward the observation.

On GSO, Direct3D-V3 reports the best Chamfer Distance (0.0131) and F1 scores (0.9600 and 0.6043), but its Volume IoU of 0.3815 trails TRELLIS2's 0.3884.
[p
11
]
 GSO consists of relatively simple scanned objects, and the paper itself cautions that these results do not show how well the framework handles complex geometry.
[p
11
]
 The same native SDF representation that avoids a decoder bottleneck is what lets normal observations adjust the visible surface directly, while the learned prior fills in what one image cannot show.

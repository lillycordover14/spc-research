Dive Brief:
 
 
 
Boston Dynamics and GlobalFoundries have expanded their collaboration to deploy Spot robots for autonomous inspection and data collection
 
across GlobalFoundries' global semiconductor fabs, 
according to a Nov. 17 news release
. 
 
 
 
Spot deployments will expand across GlobalFoundries' manufacturing sites in Malta, New York; Burlington, Vermont; Dresden, Germany; and Singapore, the companies said. 
 
 
 
GlobalFoundries also will be Boston Dynamics' first customer outside of the automotive industry to test humanoid robots as part of the the latter company's Atlas early adopter program and skills development workshop.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Dive Insight:
 
 
GlobalFoundries has been using the Spot robot at its Burlington manufacturing facility for more than four years, according to the press release. In April, GlobalFoundries began deploying Spot in its Singapore facility to conduct inspections in sub-fab areas, which house utilities, gas tanks and other support equipment.
 
 
"Spot has helped us shift from reactive to predictive maintenance, collecting data across thousands of inspection points to prevent downtime and keep our fabs running more efficiently," Pradip Singh, chief manufacturing officer at GlobalFoundries, said in a statement. "Scaling Spot and testing Atlas are the next steps in our journey to AI-enabled manufacturing, putting physical AI to work across our operations worldwide."
 
 
Also under their agreement, the companies will begin testing the Atlas humanoid robot at GlobalFoundries' facilities in early 2027. The goal of the testing will be to evaluate how the robot can best be used, determine whether customers are ready to use it and train it to perform specific tasks, according to the news release.
 
 
 
 
 
Boston Dynamics has also been collaborating with its parent company, Hyundai. In September, it officially 
opened its robotics center
 at Hyundai's electric vehicle production site outside of Savannah, Georgia, taking the next step to deploy humanoids and other robots across the automaker's operations.
 
 
Humanoids, in general, have been receiving 
a lot of attention from manufacturers
. However, at IMTS in Chicago this year, experts said a lot more progress is needed before they can be fully implemented on production floors due to scalability limitations, cost and other factors.
 
 
In addition to collaborating with Boston Dynamics, GlobalFoundries has reached agreements with a number of other companies to build its semiconductor business.

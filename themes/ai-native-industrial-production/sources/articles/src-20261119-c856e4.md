                        Iranian Sociological Association Condemns Domestic Murders of Women and Calls for Stronger Legal Deterrence
                    

November 19, 2026

                                                IranWire
                                            

                                            2 min read
                                        

The Iranian Sociological Association has condemned the “domestic murders” of women and girls, commonly referred to as “honor” killings, and described them as symptoms of structural problems and a direct threat to social security. The association urged policymakers to review relevant laws, introduce stronger deterrent penalties, and include women, sociologists, and subject-matter experts in the legislative process.

In its statement, the association noted that generational and cultural shifts within society have not been matched by reforms in legal, educational, and support mechanisms. According to the authors of the statement, clinging to authoritarian and patriarchal models turns the family into an arena of domination and ownership. Family security and cohesion, they said, depend on respecting the dignity and rights of all family members.

The Sociological Association also called for the public release of research data on social harms, warning that concealing or ignoring scientific findings leads to an accumulation of crises. The body further criticized the prolonged stalling of the “Bill on Preserving Dignity and Protecting Women Against Violence,” stressing that any potential revisions to the draft must preserve its protective provisions.

In another section of the statement, the association emphasized the importance of teaching life skills and peaceful conflict resolution in schools. It called for strengthening social work services in neighborhoods and schools, expanding safe houses under the supervision of the State Welfare Organization, and allocating greater funding to support institutions.

The death of Sogand Sayadi, a 16-year-old student from Kermanshah who fell from the fifth floor of a building, has sparked a public campaign to amend Article 301 of Iran’s Islamic Penal Code. Under this provision, fathers and paternal grandfathers are exempt from qisas (retribution in kind) if they kill their children or grandchildren, respectively. The exemption does not apply to mothers.

According to published accounts, Sogand’s father confronted her after school officials informed him of her romantic relationship. Reports state that her father beat her at the school and took her away with him. She died the following day. The Kermanshah prosecutor has announced the arrest of Sogand’s father on charges including murder and assault, as well as the detention of the school principal for negligence and dereliction of duty.

Speaking to the daily newspaper Shargh, attorney Shahla Orouji stated that Article 612 of the Islamic Penal Code can serve as the basis for a prison sentence of three to 10 years in such cases, provided that conditions such as a disturbance of public order or a risk of repeat offenses are established. She noted that the three-year minimum is disproportionate to the gravity of intentional murder. Shargh also reported that more than 125 women and children have been killed in domestic violence cases since the start of the current Persian calendar year in March 2026. The figure includes only cases reported in the media.

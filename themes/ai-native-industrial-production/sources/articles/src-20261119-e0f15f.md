AI server supply chain stocks have come under heavy selling pressure recently, with shares of ABF substrate and copper clad laminate (CCL) companies sliding from October highs to November lows, posting corrections of generally 40% to 50%. In an Asia-Pacific technology report, Bank of America argues the selloff has overreacted to market worries about a turning point in the AI server cycle and slowing capital expenditure at major technology companies, leaving relatively limited downside for the affected stocks. The bank simultaneously raised its 2027-2028 profit estimates for five companies — Unimicron Technology (3037.TW), Kinsus Interconnect Technology (3189.TW), Nan Ya PCB (8046.TW), Elite Material (2383.TW) and Taiwan Union Technology (6274.TW) — while keeping a "Buy" rating on all five.

Notably, although Bank of America raised its operating profit and earnings per share (EPS) estimates for these five stocks, it lowered their price targets instead. The main reason is that it cut the price-to-earnings multiple for the AI server supply chain by about 30% — a rational correction on the valuation side rather than a weakening of fundamentals.

Based on November 16 closing prices, Bank of America calculated that under a scenario in which 2028 AI-related revenue comes in 15% below its estimate, these stocks are currently trading at 7 to 13 times estimated 2028 earnings — already below the median of their valuation range before AI server demand took off in mid-2023. Backing out implied 2028 EPS from long-term anchor multiples, current share prices imply that 2028 AI-related revenue would be about 40% to 90% below Bank of America's estimates, suggesting the market has already priced in a fairly pessimistic scenario.

Bank of America noted that third-quarter 2026 gross margins were generally better than expected, and that price outlooks for ABF substrates, BT substrates and CCL remain bullish, prompting it to raise 2027-2028 operating profit estimates. However, as the AI server supply chain undergoes a sector-wide valuation de-rating, Bank of America still decided to lower its price-to-earnings multiples, which in turn led to adjustments in each stock's price target — producing the unusual combination of "higher earnings, lower targets."

On ABF substrates, Bank of America updated its global supply-demand model. Helped by upward revisions to the addressable market for AI accelerators and CPUs, it estimates supply-demand gaps of 7%, 18% and 20% for 2026, 2027 and 2028, respectively — tighter than its previous estimates of 7%, 14% and 19%. Bank of America estimates industry demand growth of 36% and 24% in 2027 and 2028, respectively, with AI accelerators accounting for an estimated 41%, 52% and 55% of demand in 2026-2028. Bank of America also cited data shared by Ibiden showing that demand for semi-additive process (SAP) capacity will grow 2.5-fold from 2026 to 2030, with server CPUs growing 1.5-fold and AI accelerators growing 4.4-fold.

On the CCL side, Bank of America is similarly positive on the overall supply-demand outlook, noting that lead times for key production equipment remain as long as 1.5 to 2 years, limiting supply increases. As AI server motherboards stack up to more than 30 layers, material specifications must also be upgraded to M8, M9 and even M10 grades. Among its Buy list, Bank of America favors Elite Material and Taiwan Union Technology: Elite Material is expected to derive at least 70% to 80% of its 2027-2028 revenue from M7-and-above grades, with an estimated 50% to 60% share of AI-related high-end CCL, and channel checks suggest it has the potential to win adoption of M10 in SpaceX AI server racks; Taiwan Union Technology is one of the few suppliers with relatively aggressive capacity expansion plans for 2026-2030, roughly tripling capacity during the period, with potential for an additional capacity expansion of about 2 million sheets per month in Thailand by 2030.

Regarding the impact of co-packaged optics (CPO) on CCL and PCBs, Bank of America's supply chain checks show that when pluggable optical modules are upgraded to OBO or NPO, many CCL materials will be upgraded to M9 grade. Because second-generation CPO with OOI is unlikely to ramp meaningfully before 2028, and with transmission speeds upgrading to 3.2T, motherboard CCL under non-CPO/OOI architectures may already have risen to M10 grade by then — meaning CPO/OOI adoption may only downgrade materials to M8 or M9 grade, without changing the long-term direction of material upgrades.

Measured by the size of the cuts, Elite Material's price target was slashed by NT$1,600 at once, a reduction of about 19.5% — the largest; Nan Ya PCB's target was cut by NT$390, also nearly 19.5%, putting the two stocks tied for the biggest correction in this round.

Still, the market is not without dissenting voices. Analyst Chi Wei-ming noted that while Unimicron, Nan Ya PCB, Kinsus, Elite Material and Taiwan Union Technology are benefiting from a widening supply-demand gap and simultaneous upward revisions to 2027-2028 EPS estimates, concerns about a turning point in the AI server cycle continue to weigh on valuations, and he recommends waiting for the broader market to stabilize before building positions. He also cautioned that the latest export control policy from the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) could slow the pace at which China-linked cloud service providers deploy GPUs and raise compliance costs for overseas AI infrastructure investment, and that long-term demand will depend on the details of policy implementation.

Overall, Bank of America believes this wave of panic selling has created an attractive valuation entry point for long-term investors, and that with operating growth and margin expansion over the next three years, the long-term investment value of the ABF substrate and CCL sectors has emerged. In the near term, however, noise around the AI server cycle and uncertainty over U.S. export controls remain the two major uncertainties weighing on valuations.

Saronic to Deliver Autonomous Resupply Vessel to US Marine Corps

 
The Defense Innovation Unit has awarded an Other Transaction prototype agreement to develop the Autonomous Low-Profile Vessel for contested logistics missions.

Ami Rojkes Dombe

                                | 

19/11/2026

					Sponsored

			

Photo: U.S. Marine Corps photo by Connor Taggart / Public domain / Wikimedia Commons

The U.S. Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) has awarded Saronic Technologies an Other Transaction (OT) prototype agreement for the development, construction, and delivery of the Autonomous Low-Profile Vessel (ALPV).

According to a report by 
Naval News
, addressing this critical operational requirement demands platforms that are not only fully autonomous in operation, but also cost-effective, easy to offload in the field, and capable of being produced at scale. The vessel serves as a long-range autonomous logistics connector designed to ensure resilient, efficient, and low-cost maritime resupply for widely dispersed U.S. Marine Corps units operating in complex environments.

The logistics challenge in contested maritime spaces has become one of the defining hurdles of modern naval operations, particularly the capability to safely deliver food, fuel, and other critical supplies to forces operating directly within enemy weapons engagement zones without endangering human crews.

Under the prototype effort, Saronic's ALPV will integrate advanced autonomy and passive sensing with a purpose-built hull engineered specifically for seakeeping, beaching capabilities, and serial production at scale.

The new vessel will directly leverage the company's proven autonomy system, developed and refined across its previous platforms and tested in over 350,000 nautical miles and more than 55,000 operational hours.

Thanks to this specialized design, the ALPV is engineered to transport required cargo in both ship-to-ship and ship-to-shore mission profiles.

Assured navigation, collaborative teaming, and advanced autonomous behaviors will enable the vessel to operate across a broad spectrum of maritime missions, while an open and modular systems architecture will allow rapid integration of mission payloads as required.

Rob Lehman, co-founder and Chief Commercial Officer at Saronic, stated that the award recognizes that winning in contested waters begins with sustaining the warfighter, adding that the ALPV provides Marines with a reliable, low-signature autonomous platform to deliver critical cargo across contested seas without putting human crews at risk.

The joint effort will also support government testing and strategic planning by the Marine Corps for integrating the platform into active service, including the concept of operations, staffing, training, and ongoing sustainment.

The company will work closely with the DIU and the Marine Corps to deliver an affordable capability aligned with warfighter needs.

This award builds upon Saronic's ongoing work with the U.S. Navy, which includes a production contract for the 24-foot Corsair autonomous surface vessel, a contract for the 180-foot Marauder autonomous ship, and the recent announcement of a Landing Craft Utility build at its Port Alpha shipyard in Brownsville, Texas.

Perseverance defines soccer captain’s Tar Heel journey&nbsp;&nbsp;The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

[page text]
Riley Berge went from hardly seeing the field to winning a conference championship, and he’s also closing in on his second Carolina degree, a master’s in accounting from UNC Kenan-Flagler Business School. (Courtesy of Riley Berge)
Riley Berge
 ’25 is in his fifth season with 
Carolina men’s soccer
, but the team captain only needs to go back to this past Sunday for his favorite Tar Heel memory.
As the final seconds ticked away in Carolina’s 3-0 win over No. 1 and previously unbeaten Southern Methodist University in the Atlantic Coast Conference title match, Berge fell backward, lay on his back and lifted his arms to the sky. The Tar Heels’ 15-year wait for a conference championship was over.
“I just couldn’t believe I was in that situation,” said Berge, a center back and key member of the Tar Heels’ backline.
Here’s why: He didn’t play in a single match his first two seasons. Berge finally saw the field as a junior — for four minutes.
But the defender’s breakthrough came as a senior in 2025 when he played in 17 matches and made 15 starts. His first career goal even earned him a spot on 
SportsCenter’s Top 10 Plays
, and he was named best breakthrough male athlete at the athletics department’s 2026 Rammy Awards.
With a fifth year of eligibility, he’s started in each of Carolina’s 19 matches this season, adding three goals and contributing to a Tar Heel defense that recorded seven shutouts.
Berge, a student in UNC Kenan-Flagler Business School’s 
online Master of Accounting program
, has gone from being able to count his game time with one hand to logging 3,144 minutes over the past two seasons.
“If you stick it out,” Berge told himself, “it’s going to work out.”
Couldn’t leave Carolina
Even when he wasn’t seeing the field, Berge didn’t complain. He had great friends on campus and enjoyed his undergraduate studies, as he earned a 
sports administration degree
 and a 
data science minor
.
He said he couldn’t be mad about not playing because “pretty much every guy that started in front of me is playing pro right now.”
Still, patience is a virtue that’s become less common in modern college athletics, where student-athletes can transfer without penalty. Heavy roster turnover year to year is common.
What kept Berge in Chapel Hill?
“I’m going to get a degree from a great place, and I still have all my friends here,” Berge thought at the time. “There’s no reason for me to leave to just go find some more playing time.”
Berge has started every game this year for the Tar Heels and has scored three goals. (Emma Newman/GoHeels)
Beyond that, he said he felt he “owed it” to the coaching staff who made him a Tar Heel and the University that’s molded him as a player and person. Berge cherishes the moment when, following the Tar Heels’ win at Clemson in the ACC Tournament, Tar Heel head coach Carlos Somoano approached him at his locker and said, “I’m so proud of you.”
Berge is also a multiyear participant in the Richard A. Baddour Carolina Leadership Academy, an opportunity that’s allowed him to mentor younger student-athletes and learn from leaders on other Carolina teams.
“It’s a great tool to get other perspectives, ones not just from your team,” Berge said.

Taking care of business
Always interested in business, Berge jumped at the opportunity to pursue the MAC program at UNC Kenan-Flagler when he knew he’d return for a fifth season. He’s on track to complete the program in March.
The quality and flexibility of the program are what he enjoys the most. As was the case during his undergraduate studies, his professors understand his soccer commitments and work with him, something “I love about this university,” Berge said. He applauded clinical assistant professor 
Tanja Snively
 and adjunct instructor Alexis Sexton for their great teaching and willingness to accommodate him when he has a game to play.
Berge has also learned plenty from his classmates, many of whom are older and full-time working professionals. “Some own grocery stores, some own advisory firms and things like that,” he said. “It’s super cool to hear from them.”
Berge is interested in commercial real estate careers and is applying to jobs. Days after the ACC title, he heard back from a company that wanted to talk to him about a junior accountant role.
The wins keep adding up.

Police in Shanghai, China have reportedly arrested 13 people in connection with an alleged criminal scheme selling counterfeit Final Fantasy 7 figures.
First reported by local Shanghai-based publication 
Shangguan News
 (translation via 
Automaton
), in May Shanghai police began receiving complaints about Final Fantasy 7 Tifa Lockhart figures purchased from an online store that didn't match-up with the official toys licensed by rights holder 
Square Enix
. Police reportedly verified with Square Enix that the seller had not been authorized to sell the figures, which appear to be nearly identical to an official Square Enix product, although seemingly without the articulation points that allow you to change the position of arms and legs. Here's a comparison via Dexerto's Twitter account:
Police have arrested 13 people accused of selling counterfeit Final Fantasy 7 Tifa Lockhart figures
The Chinese sellers reportedly charged higher prices by including extra faces, hands, and accessories not found with the official figures https://t.co/AE1IyS0wi9
November 19, 2026
According to Shangguan News, officially licensed figures typically don't include customization accessories because of the added copyright and production costs, but since counterfeiters don't have to worry about that stuff, they're able to add things like swappable face plates and other accessories and then sell the toys for more than their genuine counterparts. Police say the Tifa Lockhart operation in particular had been worth more than 2 million yuan, which is roughly $300,00, at the time of the arrests. The 13 suspects involved are said to be under investigation for alleged trademark infringement.

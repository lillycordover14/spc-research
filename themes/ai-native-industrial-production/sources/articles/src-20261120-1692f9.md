Defense Minister Nikos Dendias on Friday called for constant vigilance, credible deterrence and the modernization of Greece’s armed forces to meet 21st-century challenges, while stressing the need to improve support for military personnel and their families.

In a message marking Armed Forces Day on November 21, Dendias paid tribute to those serving in the military and to those who have died in the line of duty.

He said revisionism, challenges to international law, armed conflicts and rapid technological advances were creating an unprecedented security environment in which military readiness and deterrence could not be taken for granted.

The armed forces were essential to safeguarding national security, preserving peace and protecting Greece’s sovereign rights, he said.

Dendias said the government’s Agenda 2030 reform program sought to reshape the military through a modern force structure, joint and multi-domain operations, and the adoption of new technologies and innovation, strengthening the country’s deterrent capabilities and strategic autonomy.

He also stressed that personnel remained central to military effectiveness, saying even the most advanced weapons systems could not replace the knowledge, judgment, training and determination of those operating them.

He said the state’s responsibility extended beyond operational readiness to ensuring decent living conditions, family support, better training and meaningful career prospects for service members.

Armed Forces Day coincides with the Orthodox Christian feast of the Presentation of the Virgin Mary. Dendias said the occasion reflected the longstanding ties between Orthodox tradition and Greek history, noting that the Virgin Mary was traditionally regarded as the protector of the armed forces and the country.

He concluded by thanking military personnel and urging them to continue serving with professionalism, discipline and dedication to democracy, freedom and national independence.

The gathering
What to look forward to
ML and robotics engineers will explore the data, training, and simulation challenges behind physical AI, including talks from Ury Zhilinsky, founding technical member at Mind Robotics, and Nehil Jain, forward deployed engineer at Anyscale. Hosted by the team behind Ray, the evening will also feature networking and discussion of large-scale AI workloads.
Building foundation models for the physical world, whether VLAs or world models, means wrangling massive video and sensor datasets, running large-scale training and simulation, and iterating fast.
The more data these models see, the more capable they get.
Join us for an evening of technical talks and networking with fellow ML and robotics engineers and builders to see how they are tackling this new wave of physical AI innovation.

SESSION DETAILS
From Factory Floor to Training Run:
 What Works Today, What's Still Hard
Mind Robotics trains robot foundation models on egocentric video captured on live automotive production lines, plus teleop and third-party data, in a fast-growing corpus on a path to many petabytes. This talk walks through the pipeline end to end, from capture on the factory floor through QC and labeling to the training dataloader, covering what works in production today and the problems still ahead. Expect the patterns that worked and an honest list of what we haven't solved yet.
Speaker Bios
Ury Zhilinsky leads software and AI at Mind Robotics, which makes AI-powered mobile robotic platforms for industrial manufacturing, with Rivian as its first manufacturing partner. Before Mind, his work spanned Waymo, Nuro, and Physical Intelligence.
Mouli Sivapurapu leads data and applications engineering at Mind Robotics, building the infrastructure that carries video and teleop data from the factory floor through QC and labeling into model training. Prior to Mind, he worked at Scale AI, Apple and Samsung.
Hosted by
Anyscale
, the company behind the Ray open source project, is a fully managed AI platform for foundation model-scale workloads. AI teams use it to run data, training, sims, inference, and RL workloads at scale on any cloud or on-prem.

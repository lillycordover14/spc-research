Geno Smith nears return for Jets but two receivers ruled out vs. Chargers&nbsp;&nbsp;New York Daily News

[page text]
New York Jets quarterback Geno Smith (7) celebrates a touchdown with head coach Aaron Glenn during an NFL football game against the Las Vegas Raiders in East Rutherford, N.J., Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026. (AP Photo/Rusty Jones)		

By 
Antwan Staley
 | 
astaley@nydailynews.com
 | New York Daily News
PUBLISHED: 
November 20, 2026 at 11:47 AM EST
 
Geno Smith
 is on track to play Sunday.

The 
Jets
 just need enough healthy receivers for him to throw to.

Smith fully participated in Friday’s practice after 
injuring his ankle
 in last Sunday’s 
loss to the Bills
. Jets coach Aaron Glenn said the 36-year-old quarterback is trending toward playing against the Chargers at SoFi Stadium.

“He looks solid,” Glenn said Friday. “It’s very encouraging.”

Smith left the Buffalo game twice. He was first evaluated for a head injury and missed the opening series of the third quarter. He returned for one series before injuring his ankle when Bills safety Cole Bishop knocked him down. Rookie backup Cade Klubnik finished the game.

On Monday, Glenn dismissed the idea of starting Klubnik to get a longer look at the rookie before the season ends. Smith has thrown for 2,226 yards, 12 touchdowns and three interceptions. His 53.2 QBR ranks 19th in the NFL.

The Jets offense has gone nine straight quarters without a touchdown. The last time they scored a TD was during the third quarter of the 
Nov. 1 loss to the Raiders. 

“My job is to be coachable and be the player,” Smith said. “I respect Coach Glenn and everything that he says, and he makes the calls.
 

“So, I do as I’ve always done. You know, when I was the backup for years, I prepared like I was the starter and nothing changes for me, but I do appreciate coach going out and saying that. He doesn’t have to say that, but I do appreciate it.”

Smith was listed as limited for Wednesday’s walkthrough and Thursday’s practice. Glenn said earlier in the week that he wanted to see how Smith progressed before making a decision.

The Jets will try to snap a five-game losing streak without wide receivers Omar Cooper Jr. (ankle/hip) and Isaiah Williams (ribs), who missed all three practices this week and are out against the Chargers.

Williams also handles punt and kickoff returns. Running back Isaiah Davis could pick up some of that work after averaging 24.7 yards on three kickoff returns against Buffalo.
 

That leaves the Jets with four healthy receivers on the active roster: Garrett Wilson, Adonai Mitchell, Tim Patrick and rookie Malik McClain, who has played mostly on special teams. Sterling Shepard and Jamaal Pritchett are on the practice squad, and the Jets could elevate one of them for Sunday.

Glenn said he’ll see how left guard Dylan Parham (knee), defensive tackle Harrison Phillips (ankle), edge rusher Joseph Ossai (ankle) and linebacker Trevin Wallace (groin) progress before deciding whether they can play.

“We’ve got two days to see how these guys operate,” Glenn said. “It is looking promising for all these guys, so I feel good about that.”

The injuries at receiver could mean more work in the passing game for tight ends Mason Taylor and rookie Kenyon Sadiq.

“I think that’s the good thing about the tight end room,” Glenn said. “Those guys can actually end up playing some of those receiver spots that we have.

“Sadiq gives us a chance to have 12 personnel [one running back and two tight ends] on the field, but also to have 11 personnel [one running back, one tight end, and three wide receivers] concepts. The same with Mason, so that helps quite a bit.”

If the Jets are going to end their skid in Los Angeles, they may need their tight ends to do some of the receivers’ work.

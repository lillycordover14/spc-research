XPENG has locked in November 25 for its 2026 Tech Day in Guangzhou, themed "Origin." Headlining the event is the "Turing AI Co-Pilot," a system the automaker calls an industry first: it systematically extends intelligent driving capabilities into manual driving scenarios.
Image Source: XPENG
The feature's first application, "VLA Adaptive Deceleration," kicks in the moment a driver lifts off the accelerator. A background VLA model monitors road conditions in real time, smoothly adjusting deceleration force without requiring navigation assistance to be active. The aim is to cut the jerkiness often felt during coasting and braking, thereby reducing the likelihood of motion sickness.
On the efficiency front, XPENG data shows the function boosts actual usable range by 12.4% in stop-and-go city traffic compared to having regenerative braking off. That gain stems from the system's ability to predict road conditions, rather than simply ramping up regeneration intensity. The XPENG G9L and MONA L05 will be the first to feature it, with a broader rollout to follow.
Under the hood, the system runs on XPENG's in-house Turing AI chip and its VLA (Vision-Language-Action) model. As of August 2026, shipments of the Turing chip have surpassed 200,000 units. Strategically, XPENG has pegged 2026 as a pivotal year for the commercialization of physical AI, with the VLA model already being licensed to partners like Volkswagen for use in jointly developed vehicles.

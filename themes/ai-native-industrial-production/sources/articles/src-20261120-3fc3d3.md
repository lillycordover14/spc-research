UWEC Wind Symphony Orchestra Will Make Carnegie Hall Debut This Spring

university ensemble receives first-ever invitation to perform at the 25th New York Wind Band & Orchestra Festival

words & photos by 
Hattie Goethals
 | 
November 20, 2026

BLUGOLDS IN THE BIG APPLE. While the announcement that UW-Eau Claire's wind symphony was selected to perform at the historic Carnegie Hall in New York came out earlier this October, preparation for their showcase has only just begun. Students will perform at the New York Wind Band & Orchestra Festival on March 9, 2027.
 (Photo courtesy 
UW-Eau Claire Integrated Marketing and Communications
)

Following news of the UW-Eau Claire Blugold Marching Band 
performing
 at the 2027 Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, 
the Blugold band program has some more celebrations in order.

The local university's 
wind symphony orchestra
 recently announced they have been invited – for the very first time – to perform at the historic Carnegie Hall in New York City, N.Y. on March 9, 2027.

The ensemble will perform as the featured collegiate orchestra at the 25th annual New York Wind Band & Orchestra Festival. At this event, young musicians can experience musical growth while creating lasting memories, according to 
World Projects
.

John R. Stewart, the director of concert bands and conductor of the wind symphony orchestra, said he first set a goal to expand the Blugold name when he began at the university in 2013.

Come this spring, the ensemble will be the first from UW-Eau Claire to play at the acclaimed concert hall.

“It’s not something I ever pictured myself getting to do. But now it’s a reality,” Stewart said of the opportunity. “I don’t get to play, but I get to stand in front and make music with these wonderful young musicians we have here at the university.”

During the 2027 showcase, the group of 54 musicians will perform a 45-minute set and open its performance with a piece by 
Erika Svanoe
, a 1999 UW-Eau Claire alumna.

The ensemble will also perform a hallmark piece from its repertoire: “Lincolnshire Posy” by 
Percy Grainger
. While selecting music for the performance, Stewart has been mindful of the student outcome and learning moments that will happen during this experience.

“I have to make sure the music that we're picking (music) that gives them all the variety of things, and skills, and challenges that (students) need so that they can be successful at that next level when they are applying and preparing for graduate school,” Steward explained.

There are currently seven students in the ensemble who are not able to make the trip due to academic obligations, leaving an opportunity for other students to participate.

Auditions for open spots in the wind symphony orchestra will be conducted at the end of the fall semester.

“Their passion for music and making music is tremendous.”

John R. Stewart

director of concert bands at uw-eau claire

To help fund travel to New York, the 
Donald George Memorial Band Fund
 will be utilized for student travel fares, hotel stay and other expenses. On Dec. 1, 
Giving Tuesday
, Stewart will be serving as an 
ambassador
 for the symphony and any donations made on that day could support the effort even further.

This trip will also be a recruitment opportunity for UW-Eau Claire; the ensemble will visit high schools throughout the Brooklyn borough.

Miles Gleason is a tuba player in the Wind Symphony Orchestra and a music education major focusing on instrumentals. Both his parents are middle school band teachers and he was surrounded by music growing up, he said.

“I think it’s fun that we get to go and play at Carnegie Hall at this big festival and we’re not a giant world-renowned music conservatory, but (we are) just a (university) from Wisconsin,” Gleason said.

Macie Ness, a performance major and flutist in the program, said this experience will be helpful for someone, like herself, who wants to become a professional musician.

Carnegie Hall in the heart of New York City, pictured above. 
(Photos by Jeff Goldber)

During its 2027 showcase, 54 student musicians will perform a 45-minute set.

“To get that opportunity to play (and) to travel to New York, it really helps you start, at least even just thinking about envisioning (that) this could be my life,” Ness said.

“(When) we get to (perform) at a place that all these amazing professionals play at, makes it feel closer to the end goal that feels so far away,” she added.

Stewart is grateful for all the support and donations that the ensemble has received thus far since the announcement in October. Sponsors were recognized in the Wind Symphony Orchestra 
concert
 program on Nov. 18 at the 
Pablo Center at the Confluence
 (128 Graham Ave., Eau Claire).

The ensemble has also received local business sponsorships from 
Beyond the Notes Music Festival
 and 
Eckroth Music
.

“Their passion for music and making music is tremendous,” Stewart said. “I’m happy for them; I’m glad I get to share it with them. It’s really about them.”

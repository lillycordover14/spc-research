Russian figure skater brings Israeli Eurovision song to the ice
Lyubov Rubtsova chose ‘New Day Will Rise,’ Israel’s 2025 Eurovision entry performed by Yuval Raphael, for her free skate at a Russian Grand Prix event, bringing a message of hope and resilience to the ice
 Lia Ziskovich-Rahman

Russian figure skater Lyubov Rubtsova performed her free skate to "New Day Will Rise," the song with which Israeli singer Yuval Raphael represented Israel at the 2025 Eurovision Song Contest, bringing an unexpected Israeli connection to Russia's domestic figure skating circuit.
Rubtsova, 17, presented the program at the Russian Grand Prix event in Krasnoyarsk, held October 31-November 1. She earned 131.30 points for her free skate and 195.33 points overall, finishing seventh.
The performance came during a figure skating season that has also brought success for Israel. On November 14, Israeli figure skater Tamir Kuperman won bronze at Skate America in Everett, Washington, earning 248.55 points in his senior Grand Prix debut.
Russian figure skaters were barred from international competitions following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Although eligible athletes have since been allowed to return under neutral status, restrictions remain in place.
 "New Day Will Rise," a ballad about grief, healing and resilience, carries particular significance for Raphael, a survivor of the Hamas-led massacre at the Nova music festival on October 7, 2023.

In a May 2025 interview with Billboard, Raphael spoke about the song's message and the healing power of shared grief.
"Crying with someone else or someone that relates to your grief is something that is so healing," she said, expressing hope that the song would encourage listeners to support one another and find hope during difficult times.
 Rubtsova, who trains under two-time Olympic champion Evgeni Plushenko, acknowledged that her performance in Krasnoyarsk fell short of her expectations.

"To be honest, it wasn't my best performance. My jumps usually go better in training," Rubtsova said, according to the Russian sports outlet Sports Daily.
Despite her disappointment, she emphasized her determination to improve and take on greater challenges.
"Taking risks is always better than standing still and not progressing. But you have to execute those elements cleanly," she said.

Rubtsova said she continues to practice more difficult elements but intends to introduce them into her competitive routines only when she can perform them consistently.
Before the competition, Plushenko advised her to focus on executing her routine.
Rubtsova also expressed hope that she would compete in another Russian Grand Prix event this season and receive an invitation to the Russian Jumping Championships.

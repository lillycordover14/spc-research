    Rivalry Night fosters intercollegiate musical camaraderie

    Student bands from UNC, Duke and N.C. State performed at Cat’s Cradle on Tuesday night for an event hosted by Dugout Entertainment.

Margaret Lyon
, 
Escritora sénior
                 
             •  
            
November 20, 2026
 

 

Haydon Bode
 
Sarah Lorenzi, lead singer of UNC student Band Braless, holds up a heart to the crowd at Dugout Entertainment’s “Rivalry Night” at Cat’s Cradle in Carrboro on March 6th, 2025.
 

What do UNC, Duke and N.C. State students have in common? Not much, if you don’t count despising each other’s sports teams. However, Tuesday’s Rivalry Night at the Cat’s Cradle showed that even opposing schools can come together to enjoy a night of student bands. 

The Pull Aparts from Duke, Greenroom from N.C. State and The Buckets from UNC all rocked out in the venue’s back room for an event put together by Dugout Entertainment.

While named Rivalry Night, the event focused less on dueling guitars and more on appreciating each band’s unique sound and enjoying the different melodies. When they weren’t jamming out on the stage, band members hyped up each other’s sets.

Fans gathered at the venue to listen to the bands play. Event attendee and UNC freshman Dilni Kodippili said she had never been to Cat’s Cradle and wanted to experience a show.

“I like how everyone’s supporting each other,” Kodippili said.

The night began with The Pull Aparts, a three-person Duke band whose singer said it was the band’s third show ever. They played mostly original songs with haunting guitar melodies, crazy distortion and a lot of coordination.

Next, N.C. State’s Greenroom brought loads of energy and stage presence with its five-person
 
band. They made their identity clear from the start by coming on stage decked out in bright red gear and chanting “Wolfpack”. They had covers and original songs, all of which offered bluesy, groovy and sometimes ska-sounding beats to the room.

Last but definitely not least, The Buckets from UNC brought funk, rock and style. They played covers and three original songs that showcased the band’s flair and singer’s talent. From Paramore to Peach Pit, The Buckets brought the night to a satisfying close. 

When The Buckets concluded their set, the Greenroom band members could be heard yelling, “one more song,” which quickly turned into “10 more songs,” showcasing the camaraderie and friendship the event fostered between the participating bands.

UNC student Band Braless performs at Dugout entertainment’s “Rivalry Night” on March 6th, 2025, at Cat’s Cradle in Carrboro, North Carolina. (Haydon Bode)

River Summers, guitarist of The Buckets, said that the band was excited to play their new original songs for the first time and to play with the other bands.

“We’re super happy to be playing with Greenroom, the band that we’ve played with before, and we just learned that we’ve played with some of the members of Pull Aparts before, so super stoked to just be here,” Summers said. 

Summers said The Buckets played their first-ever live show with Dugout Entertainment, and they have been communicating and playing shows with them ever since.

“We know some people in Dugout, and the conversation came up eventually about playing at Cat’s, and we were like, ‘absolutely, first Cat’s show that we can play, let’s do it,’” Summers said.

Co-founder of Dugout Adam Castiller said the Dugout team comes up with set ideas like Rivalry Night or Indie Night and then contacts bands. He said that having a title event is great because people are drawn to the show itself even if they don’t know the specific bands that are playing.

Castiller said he chose to contact these bands for Rivalry Night because none of them had gotten the chance to play at the renowned local venue. 

“Chapel Hill is a great platform to get a start as a band because it allows you to make the mistakes that new bands can make, but also allows you to get in front of some pretty big acts and put on some pretty good shows in front of a crowd that loves live music,” Castiller said.

He said that the more the college band community grows, the more appealing Chapel Hill will be for bands to come and play.

“Venues are really supportive about having live music again, and people believe in the live music scene in Chapel Hill,” Castiller said.

Castiller said Dugout Entertainment tries to put on Rivalry Night once a semester. He said the performance is not only an opportunity for the participating student bands, but for the rivalry to thrive. 

Duke Blue and Wolfpack Red never fail to make UNC students cringe, but no matter what side you support, a little musical camaraderie never hurts anyone.

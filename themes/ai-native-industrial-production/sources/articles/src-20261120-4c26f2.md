Rare earth stocks are giving back their latest policy-fueled bounce, and 
Critical Metals
 (
NASDAQ:CRML
) is posting the steepest drop in the group. Critical Metals stock is at $3.21 in midday trading, down 8%. Over the past month, the stock has dropped 50%.

The group rallied on November 18 on hopes for U.S.-China rare earth talks, and that gain is already fading. Critical Metals is a pre-revenue developer that has disclosed substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern
[
1
]
, which puts it at the sharp end of the reversal. Why can’t rare earth stocks hold policy-driven rallies, and is the selling now about execution and supply rather than geopolitics?

Growth Stock Retreat Drags Critical Metals Lower

Declining risk appetite across the market is a major factor in the drop in Critical Metals stock. AI and high-growth stocks are selling off broadly, and technology is the weakest sector in the market. An AI sell-off is leading the retreat. Speculative critical minerals names tend to trade alongside that crowd when sentiment sours.

Critical Metals’ own financials leave it little cushion when that happens. For the six months ended December 31, 2025
[
1
]
, the company 
reported
 a net loss of $120.38 million and zero operating revenue, with an $80.14 million non-cash loss on warrants as the biggest driver. Cash climbed to $80.92 million
[
1
]
 after private placements and warrant exercises, though management expects capital and operating spending to rise significantly.

USA Rare Earth and MP Materials Fall Less Than Critical Metals

USA Rare Earth
 (
NASDAQ:USAR
) stock is at $6.51, down 5%. That decline adds to a 35% drop over the past month. William Blair analyst Neal Dingmann downgraded USA Rare Earth to Market Perform from Outperform on November 16, saying 
feedstock shortages
 add to a growing list of near-term problems for its Less Common Metals unit and, in turn, its Stillwater magnet plant. Dingmann still sees the company putting the pieces together for notable future upside, though he prefers companies with a longer processing track record for now.

MP Materials
 (
NYSE:MP
) stock is at $34.77, down 5%. Year to date (YTD), the stock has lost 31%. Three MP Materials insiders invested $3.6 million in shares near the 52-week low, with the purchases dated between November 10 and November 17.

Meanwhile, 
United States Antimony
 (
NYSE:UAMY
) stock is at $2.27, down 5%. Shares of the antimony producer are down 55% YTD. All three declines are smaller than the drop in Critical Metals stock.

How Critical Metals Differs From Its Critical Minerals Peers

MP Materials is the established U.S. rare earth miner and magnet maker, running the Mountain Pass mine in California and producing magnets at its Independence facility in Fort Worth, Texas. USA Rare Earth is assembling an integrated magnet supply chain that runs from the Serra Verde mine in Brazil through metals processing to its magnet plant in Stillwater, Oklahoma.

Critical Metals sits at an earlier stage, developing the Tanbreez rare earths project in Greenland and the Wolfsberg lithium project in Austria. That leaves the company reliant on outside capital until production starts, a gap that weighs more heavily when markets turn cautious. United States Antimony produces antimony and zeolite, and its sole-source contract with the Defense Logistics Agency ties it to military demand.

Can Rare Earth Stocks Hold Their Next Policy Rally?

The bulls can point to deep government backing across the group. USA Rare Earth has a $750 million Department of War investment in its offtake vehicle, while MP Materials earns price protection income under its own Department of War partnership. Recent insider purchases at MP Materials add a vote of confidence from the people closest to the business.

On the other side, the bears could argue that execution keeps tripping up the group. Feedstock shortages at USA Rare Earth, the going-concern warning at Critical Metals and the repeated failure to hold policy-driven gains suggest the selling now centers on delivery more than geopolitics. The next test for Critical Metals is whether it completes its Tanbreez definitive feasibility study by the end of 2026
[
1
]
, as the company has targeted.

Until Critical Metals and its peers convert policy support into steady output, rallies built on headlines may keep fading. Traders may prefer to keep their exposure smaller until the group’s supply question resolves.

Bitcoin
's latest sell-off is reviving doubts that it can behave as the "digital gold" market hedge that 
crypto
 backers have long envisioned.
Coming into 2026, experts said that showing bitcoin could serve as a market hedge rather than a risk-on asset that moves up and down with growth stocks was an important test for the cryptocurrency.
Bitcoin was trading under $62,000 on Friday morning, down from an all-time high of more than $126,000 in early October last year. Its downward spiral in recent months and weeks has been precipitated by a confluence of factors. The heavy midterm elections losses for the Republicans, the Binance hack, and a world on edge about the security of all things technology — FAA ground stop, the outage of the National Security Agency's highly classified internal communications system, several online banking outages — have not helped the mood when it comes to putting trust in the digital world.
But the market itself may be the biggest factor. Investors are currently in sell everything mode, including tech stocks and 
gold
 — the 
Dow Jones Industrial Average
 and 
S&P 500
 
tumbled into correction territory
 on Friday, according to CNBC data. Bitcoin's recent price action has also 
tracked the move in the biggest tech IPOs
, SpaceX and Anthropic. Taken together, the cryptocurrency is failing the test again when it comes to showing its worth as a market hedge during times of investor anxiety. 
In the past month alone, bitcoin had dropped roughly $20,000, as of Friday morning, according to CNBC data.
Bitcoin's three-month performance compared with the Nasdaq 100 Index.
Investors and financial management firms aren't viewing it as safe an investment as in the past, said Ryan Kirkley, co-founder and chief executive of Global Settlement Network, a Miami-based fintech that provides infrastructure for currency settlement, including crypto. "There's a bit more caution than there was a year ago."
What is behind bitcoin's fall, and what is in store for its future? Here is what experts say.
Bitcoin and the AI sell-off
There have been many theories about how bitcoin would react based on different market and economic conditions. But historically, bitcoin has done best in low-rate environments and when lending opportunities are plentiful, said Juan Xavier Sanchez, head of wealth strategy at Activest Wealth Management in Aventura, Florida.
When rates are down and treasuries aren't paying good yields, people tend to be more risk-seeking for higher returns. "Bitcoin for many investors is in that bucket," Sanchez said.
Experts have noted that gold, which is now down over 35% from its early 2026 peak, is less attractive when cash accounts are paying 5%. Investments like gold and bitcoin offer no yield. 
Bitcoin's rising correlation with the 
Nasdaq-100
 since late September suggests the two are increasingly moving in tandem, according to data from Fairlead Strategies, a technical research firm. That's consistent with an environment of higher volatility in which correlations across risk assets tend to increase. However, the correlation remains below its early-2026 highs, the data show.

Squawk on the Street
How bitcoin is like gold, but also like the S&P 500
  
Bitcoin has certain characteristics of digital gold, in that it has a limited supply, no single owner and no CEO or board that can decide to issue more, said Anthony Georgiades, founder and general partner at Innovating Capital in New York, which invests in early-stage crypto, blockchain, digital infrastructure and deep tech companies. 
But it's also been caught up in monetary policy since its inception. The last decade or so of monetary policy has been extremely rewarding to speculative risk-on assets, Georgiades said. However, in periods of volatility, those types of assets have experienced the most significant drawdowns and have been the most heavily punished, Georgiades said. That means that a shock on one side of the board that might be completely independent of bitcoin could trigger a large volatility event "given how intertwined digital assets have become in the overall financial system," he said.
Notably, bitcoin hasn't acted much like digital gold in more recent years, said Abe Sheikh, chief investment officer of Cordoba Advisory Partners in New York. In fact, it has fallen substantially during periods when the stock market has been falling. For example, from October 6 through November 18, the S&P 500 fell about 6.3%, and bitcoin fell around 27%. From January 21, 2025 through April 8, 2025, the S&P dropped around 17.4%, while bitcoin slid 27.8%, he said.
Bitcoin's all-time price performance.
Did Democrats' midterms blue wave matter?
Democrats gaining control over both houses is viewed as 
a negative for crypto
 from a market perspective. The crypto industry 
spent more than $200 million
 to support crypto-friendly candidates, but its role had less of an impact on overall results than in the last election cycle. However, bitcoin's downward trend was already in the works, said Cyrus Amini, chief investment officer at Hyphen Wealth Management in Lafayette, California. 
In September, the Clarity Act failed in the U.S. Senate, and while 
its outlook is now dim
, according to many political and market experts, some form of crypto legislation is still considered a priority for many Democrats and Republicans, while the Trump-appointed heads of the Commodity Future Trading Commission and Securities and Exchange Commission continue to favor crypto in rulemaking. 
This fall, investors also started to question the security of encryption technology, leading to more concerns over the security of their bitcoin holdings, said Kirkley of Global Settlement Network. The 
Binance hack
 in October muddied the waters further. And the recent stock market jitters and economic concerns piled on doubts, Kirkley said. The last few weeks have been especially rough with data security concerns from multiple fronts, he added.
Wall Street's role in bitcoin selling as ETF assets rise
Wall Street's embrace of bitcoin has been a big positive for the cryptocurrency. Kirkley noted that 20% of bitcoin's market price is coming from institutional investors, and that's a conservative estimate. 
In addition, 
new research
 from digital assets company Galaxy shows that a record 40.5% of all 13F-reported spot bitcoin exchange-traded product holdings, and 11.2% of all the bitcoin in the funds, came from advisory and wealth planning firms, such as RIAs and brokerage platforms, as of the third quarter 13F filings with the SEC, which are required of firms with over $100 million in assets to report their holdings. 
Much of this investment is through bitcoin spot ETFs like the iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF 
(IBIT)
 that track its price. These ETFs, first approved by the SEC in January 2024, were supposed to provide a reliable new source of institutional demand. But ETF ownership also makes bitcoin especially easy to sell. 
Outflows from several of these funds have been substantial in recent months. IBIT, the largest of its peers, had six-month net outflows of $1.65 billion through Nov. 18 and one-month outflows of $1.37 billion, according to ETF Database. The second biggest, Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (
FBTC
) had one-month net outflows of $322.65 million and six-month net outflows of $771.86 million, according to ETF Database. Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust ETF (
BTC
) had one-month net outflows of $66.35 million, though it had inflows of $590.04 million over six months.
A problem with corporate bitcoin treasury strategies
As of September 2026, public companies collectively hold close to 1.29 million bitcoins, which is more than 6% of the 21 million coins in existence, according to 
bitcoin.com
. 
Most public companies invest their corporate treasury portfolios in low-risk, highly liquid instruments such as money market funds, Treasury bonds and Treasury bills. That said, there are some well-known companies that have invested in bitcoin. SpaceX, for example, purchased 18,712 bitcoins for a total cost of $661 million, at an average price of $35,324 per coin. The company began accumulating bitcoin in early 2021 and held its position unchanged from mid-2024 until its IPO 
filing
.
Digital Asset Treasury companies, known as DATs or DATCOs,
 have also played a role in bitcoin's volatility
. DATs are publicly listed firms whose primary business strategy involves holding significant quantities of digital assets, such as bitcoin or 
ethereum
, on their corporate balance sheets to drive shareholder value. Companies in this space include 
Strategy
, Strive, and Twenty One Capital.
"DATs have exacerbated the problem," said Sheikh of Cordoba Advisory Partners. When bitcoin is rising, these companies borrow money in the debt market or raise capital to buy more bitcoin, which magnifies the upswings. They have also sold bitcoin to make their bond coupon payments. "It leads to enormous volatility on the upside and downside," Sheikh said.
As of mid-November, Strategy, the largest corporate holder of bitcoin, holds 850,849 BTC, according to a regulatory 
filing
. That represents roughly 4% of bitcoin's total supply. The company acquired these holdings at a total cost of approximately $64.2 billion, with an average purchase price of roughly $75,452 per coin, according to the filing.
Where could bitcoin's price be heading?
Bitcoin has been trending lower since the peak in October 2025. A trading range has taken hold and the bottom of the range is just below 60,000, said Katie Stockton, founder and managing partner of Fairlead Strategies. "It appears to be trying to find a footing," she said.
Oversold conditions on the heels of recent lows in June and July, in addition to long-term momentum, suggest bitcoin can support an upturn, she said. But that depends on whether the crypto can hold at a current range around $60,000, she said. "At the end of the day, bitcoin is exceptionally volatile. It's still an area that people might go to sell when they think that risk is higher in other areas."
By mid-morning Friday, the price of bitcoin had slipped below $60,000, its lowest mark since July. It was on pace for its seventh-straight weekly loss for the first time since May 2022, according to CNBC data.

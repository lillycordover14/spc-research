Geno Auriemma blasts NIL payouts to player that ‘hasn’t dribbled the ball one time in college’&nbsp;&nbsp;New York Post

[page text]
After landing just one of the top-10-ranked recruits in the country, UConn women’s basketball head coach Geno Auriemma questioned the idea of “paying a kid a million dollars” right out of high school. 

Auriemma was addressing reporters after the early national signing period for the class of 2027 recruits wrapped up and UConn had only signed No. 9-ranked Sydney Savoury. 

One of the biggest changes in college sports continues to be the introduction of NIL money, which has led to student-athletes earning up to millions of dollars just to play for a certain program, which seemed to frustrate Auriemma.

“We just signed the No. 9 player in the country. We don’t have cachet with guys that are getting paid a million dollars to go play some place,” 
he told reporters
. “They’re getting the cachet. I just don’t see the value of paying a high school kid a million dollars to come out of high school that hasn’t dribbled the ball one time in college.

“I don’t get the idea of paying a kid a million dollars coming out of high school. That’s more than [about] 90 percent of best players in the world in the WNBA. As long as that’s the case, we’re going to get the guys we’re gonna get, but we’re not going to get the guys we’re not supposed to get.”

Continuing to address the ever-evolving landscape of college recruiting, Auriemma noted how money has become such a dominant factor in where players choose to go. 

Advertisement

And he called out players and their families who have said academics and the school being the “right fit” were factors in their decision. 

“I don’t have those conversations with the parents,” Auriemma said. “We have an agent that talks to their agent because the kids and the parents lie to you. ‘It’s not about the money. It’s about academics. It’s about finding the right fit. It’s about the right experience.’ No it isn’t. Just say it’s about the money, and we can all live happily ever after.”

UConn has gotten off to a 3-2 record to start the season, which has included losses to Michigan and Louisville.

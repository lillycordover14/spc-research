Geno on UConn's recruiting: 'I don't see the value of paying a high school kid a million dollars'
The Huskies only have one 2027 prospected committed for next season.
Daniel Connolly
Nov 20, 2026
In partnership with
UConn’s 2027 recruiting class currently consists of just a single player: Sydney Savoury, a 5-11 guard out of Belleville, Michigan rated by ESPN as the No. 9 prospect in the country. If that holds, it’ll be the first time the Huskies only bring in one freshman since Saniya Chong in 2013.
If UConn wants to add any other first-years next season, it’ll almost certainly have to look internationally with all 100 players on ESPN’s 2027 recruiting rankings now committed somewhere.
This week, all the Huskies’ remaining targets in the class went elsewhere. On Thursday, Ivanna Wilson Manyacka (No. 2) picked UCLA over the Huskies, Duke, Maryland and LSU. Earlier, Kaleena Smith (No. 1) left UConn off her list of three finalists, Haylen Ayers (No. 8) picked Vanderbilt and Kinga Josepovits (a 6-5 center from Hungary) decided on Duke.
On Thursday, Geno Auriemma pushed back on the notion that the Huskies whiffed on the recruiting trail.
“
We just signed the number nine player in the country. We don't have cachet with guys that are getting a million dollars to go play someplace. They're getting the cachet,” he said. “I just don't see the value of paying a high school kid a million dollars that hasn't dribbled the ball one time in college. I don't get the idea of paying a kid a million dollars to come out of high school. That's more than 90 percent of the best players in the world [are making] in the WNBA. So as long as that's the case, we're going to get the guys we're going to get but we're not going to get the guys that we're not supposed to get.”
Specifically, Auriemma criticized recruits (and their families) who are only looking for the highest dollar amount.
“I don’t have that 
conversation (about money) with the parents. We have an agent that talks to their agent because the kids and the parents lie to you. ‘It's not about the money. It's about academics. It's about finding the right fit. It's about the right experience.’ No, it isn’t. Just say it's about the money and we can all live happily ever after.”
This isn’t anything new from the head coach. Auriemma has said on numerous occasions that he has no problem with college players getting paid and believes UConn provides some of the best NIL opportunities in the country. The Huskies just prefer to reward those already on their roster.
“You can compensate the players that really impact winning on your team, which is what we like to do,” he said in May.
UConn doesn’t want players who are only interested in the money, either. Auriemma articulated the program’s NIL-era recruiting philosophy a bit better back in 2024.
“You have to get kids that are really buying into all this. The hardest part right now is finding those kids,” he said at the time. “We’re really fortunate here. Our sophomores (KK Arnold and Ashlynn Shade) that were terrific last year and our freshmen (Sarah Strong, Morgan Cheli and Allie Ziebell) that I think are going to be terrific, for the most part, the NIL stuff never came up (during the recruiting process). It wasn’t like, ‘Here’s my offer sheet.’ It was, obviously, ‘We’re going to take care of you.’ But if that’s going to be the first thing you talk to me about, then that’s a nonstarter.”
Whether or not that approach can survive in the modern world is another question all together. In both 2023 and 2024, the Huskies had three freshmen from the United States in each class. They’ve signed a combined three domestic prospects since.
Instead, UConn has been successful in the international market, landing Blanca Quiñonez, Gandy Malou-Mamel, Jovana Popovic and Marine Dursus over the last two years.
As the saying goes: Once is an accident, twice is a coincidence, three times is a pattern. This will be the third straight year the Huskies only add one top-ranked freshman from the US. Given Auriemma’s comments, it seems like that trend will continue.

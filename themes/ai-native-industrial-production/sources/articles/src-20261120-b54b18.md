By DAMIEN FISHER, InDepthNH.org

RYE — He may never get a gold watch from the governor, but former New Hampshire Ports and Harbors Director Geno Marconi was honored for his 30-year career anyway.

The Propeller Club of Portsmouth gave Marconi a lifetime achievement award Thursday night during its annual meeting held at the Atlantic Grill in Rye.

“Throughout his tenure, Geno worked tirelessly to support and strengthen New Hampshire’s working waterfront,” Club President Sean Clancy said.

Along with the many infrastructure projects Marconi oversaw during his 30 years, he also served as a strong advocate for commercial fishermen and others who earn their living on the sea, Clancy said.

“Geno’s legacy extends well beyond the projects completed, and the years he served. It is reflected in the strength of the maritime community he has worked so hard to support,” Clancy said. 

Left unsaid was the scandal that forced Marconi to retire last year, the scandal that upended state government, crippled the New Hampshire Supreme Court, but curiously ended with plea deals that allowed Marconi to walk away with fines and a suspended sentence when prosecutors blinked.

The whole, convoluted saga, which is still partly playing out, began with a conflict between Marconi and Pease Development Authority Board Vice Chair Neil Levesque.

 But it soon became a contest between Marconi and the establishment as he and his wife, retired New Hampshire Supreme Court Associate Justice Anna Barbara Hantz Marconi, were pitted against the whole PDA, the New Hampshire Attorney General’s Office, former Gov. Chris Sununu, Gov. Kelly Ayotte, the Executive Council, and former Supreme Court Chief Justice Gordon MacDonald. 

Marconi was suspended from his job as Ports Director in early 2024 and placed under criminal investigation by the Attorney General’s Public Integrity Unit based on allegations of corruption from Levesque. The grand jury never handed up any charges of actual corruption, but instead Marconi and Brad Cook, a commercial fisherman and then chair of a harbor advisory committee, were indicted for violating the Drivers Privacy Act for allegedly sharing copies of vehicle registrations Levesque submitted to Marconi’s office as part of a pier permit application. 

By the time Marconi was indicted on felonies in October of 2024, Hantz Marconi had also become a target of Attorney General John Formella’s crusade. Formella broke with DOJ protocol and acted as sole investigator into Hantz Marconi’s conversations with Sununu and PDA Chairman Steve Duprey before handing it off to the Public Integrity Unit.

Hantz Marconi was indicted on felonies the same week as her husband for allegedly trying to get Sununu and PDA Chair Duprey to intervene in the original investigation of Geno Marconi. Both Sununu and Duprey told Formella and subsequent investigators they did not believe Hantz Marconi crossed any legal lines when she spoke to them in June of 2024.

Hantz Marconi and Marconi pushed to force Formella to be a witness during their trials. Hantz Marconi also planned to call MacDonald and Sununu as witnesses. Days before the trials were set to begin, during which Formella and MacDonald might be forced to testify under oath, prosecutors had a sudden change of heart.

Hantz Marconi, Marconi, and Cook all got plea deals that dropped the felony charges and allowed all three to walk away with misdemeanors punishable by fines. Hantz Marconi pleaded no contest and maintains she did not break the law.

It’s since come to light that before Hantz Marconi was suspended in 2024, she had been tasked with managing the problems caused by director of the Administrative Office of the Courts Dianne Martin, a long-time MacDonald confidante. 

Martin was moved to a new, higher paying job with the Office of Bar Admissions in early 2025, and took a $50,000 payout of earned accrued time for a two day layoff between jobs for her troubles thanks to MacDonald’s intervention, according to Judicial Conduct Committee records. 

The JCC records also reveal MacDonald was angry with Geno and Anna Barbara Hantz Marconi, blaming them for spreading rumors that he was having an affair with Martin. MacDonald denied any inappropriate relationship with Martin.

MacDonald resigned Sept. 15 after admitting to ethical violations of the Code of Judicial Conduct. One of those violations was issuing a false statement about Martin’s job change. Associate Justices Patrick Donovan, Melissa Countway, and Bryan Gould all signed off on that statement along with MacDonald. It’s currently unconfirmed if those three sitting Supreme Court justices are being investigated by the JCC.

Geno Marconi is fighting the state in court over legal fees. He claims he should be covered by state attorneys in a civil lawsuit connected to his time as Ports Director. Hantz Marconi is currently pushing back on the JCC’s investigation into her case, and she accuses the oversight committee of getting the facts wrong.

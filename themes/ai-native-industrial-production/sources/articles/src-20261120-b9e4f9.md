Durham Violence Reduction Plan proposes focused deterrence model

City and County leaders will soon decide on funding for the new Violence Reduction Plan, after a violent week in Durham.

							By Monica Casey, WRAL Durham Reporter
						

City and County leaders will soon decide on funding for the new Violence
Reduction Plan.

Wednesday’s presentation on the plan
 came in the middle of a violent week,
with 
seven total shootings.

Other WRAL Top Stories

Parts of the plan are already underway, including a weekly review by police
of every shooting to determine those at high risk.

Other parts of the plan need more funding.

The team working on the plan has requested about $447,000 through next
June, and another $4.6 million in Fiscal Year 2028.

“I think the cost is absolutely worth it when we're talking about saving
people's lives,” Durham County Commission Vice Chair Nida Allam said. “Hopefully,
saving people's lives who are, you know, the victims of the violent crime, but
also saving the people's lives who are maybe committing it.”

Allam is hopeful partnerships will make a difference. “Even if it's a small population that is maybe committing the crimes, it is
the broader community that's being impacted,” Allam said. “We want to connect
people to faith-based organizations, civic groups, where they can feel and
build a community with, so that they're not driven to commit violent crime, that
they're not driven to these states of desperation.”

The plan proposes to use outreach workers to engage people and partner them
with life coaches.

It also includes a place-based strategy, working in communities with the
highest rate of violence.

Four of the five most violent spots are Durham Housing Authority
properties.

"This is not necessarily DHA residents, but happens on DHA properties,”
City Council Member Carl Rist said.

Rist is the City liaison for DHA.

“Residents in those communities have been begging us, as well as staff at
Durham Housing Authority, have been begging for more support from the police
department to address violent crime there,” Rist said.

He believes the focused deterrence model will be a success.

“We know that that this kind of work will take investment on the
part of the city and county, and so we've just got to do the hard work of looking
carefully at the budgets and numbers, but then also making some tough decisions
about how do we find the resources to make this happen,” Rist said.

The next step for City and County
leaders will be to look at current budgets and whether they can fund the FY27
ask.

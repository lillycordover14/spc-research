Rafale fighters from the French Strategic Air Forces (FAS) were deployed to Sweden from November 16 to 18, 2026, as part of Exercise Excalibur, the first joint exercise under the two countries’ forward deterrence framework, the 
Swedish Armed Forces announced
 on November 19, 2026. 

The French aircraft operated alongside Swedish Air Force Gripen fighters in northern Sweden, supported by an Airbus A330 MRTT tanker, according to a 
separate statement from the French Ministry of the Armed Forces
. The exercise aimed to improve interoperability between the two air forces and demonstrate their ability to collaborate. 

Photographs released by the French military show at least three two-seat Rafale B fighters and two Swedish Gripen C fighters. Although neither defense ministry identified the bases used during Excalibur, photographs released by the Swedish Armed Forces show French and Swedish aircrew at Kallax Air Base near Luleå, while flight-tracking data confirms that a French A330 MRTT tanker landed at Luleå-Kallax during the exercise.

The exercise aimed to improve interoperability between the two air forces and demonstrate their ability to operate together. Both countries stressed that no nuclear weapons were involved and that the exercise was not directed at any country.

From Macron’s proposal to joint operations 

A Swedish Gripen fighter flies in formation with French Rafales during Exercise Excalibur. (Credit: État-major des armées)

The FAS operates the airborne component of France’s nuclear deterrent. French President Emmanuel Macron introduced the concept of 
forward deterrence
 at the Île Longue naval base on March 2, 2026. 

The approach allows European partners to participate in French deterrence exercises and could involve temporarily deploying elements of France’s strategic forces on allied territory. However, France retains sole authority over any decision to use its nuclear weapons. 

Sweden and Denmark agreed to enter discussions with France on the same day. In a joint statement, Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson and Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen welcomed the initiative but stressed that it would not change either country’s nuclear policy. 

French and Swedish fighter pilots walk through the snow at Kallax Air Base during Exercise Excalibur. (Credit: Swedish Armed Forces)

France and Sweden held their first nuclear steering group meeting in Paris on April 23, 2026. Co-chaired by the Élysée and the Swedish Prime Minister’s Office, the meeting produced a 
roadmap for cooperation
. The Swedish Armed Forces described Excalibur as a step towards implementing that roadmap. 

The initiative has since expanded to other European partners. Finland formally joined on September 14, 2026, agreeing to establish its own nuclear steering group with France. 

Excalibur follows UK-France nuclear deterrence exercise 

A French Air and Space Force Rafale B and a Royal Air Force Eurofighter Typhoon take off during joint nuclear deterrence exercise RUMMY in October 2026. (Credit: Catherine Vautrin / X)

Excalibur comes less than a month after Exercise RUMMY, held on October 20 and 21, 2026, when 
Royal Air Force (RAF) Typhoons escorted French Rafales
 during a simulated nuclear strike mission over the Mediterranean. 

RUMMY marked the first British participation in a French nuclear deterrence exercise. French aircraft operated from Saint-Dizier Air Base in northeastern France, with the British fighters providing conventional escort and protection. 

Excalibur took a different approach, deploying FAS aircraft onto Swedish territory for joint operations with Gripen fighters. Neither the French nor Swedish authorities disclosed the specific operational scenario that had been rehearsed. 

French Rafales had previously operated in Sweden during the 
Pégase 25 deployment
 in April 2025.

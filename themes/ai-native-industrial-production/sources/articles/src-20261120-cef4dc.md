Aaron Collins said making his international debut for Wales had "put a smile on my family's face" after the death of his grandfather.
The 29-year-old forward, who plays club football for League One MK Dons, came off the bench to feature in Tuesday's 
3-2 Nations League defeat to Portugal
 in Cardiff.
Collins was called into Craig Bellamy's squad following 
Kieffer Moore's withdrawal due to injury
 and said the achievement was made more emotional following the loss of his grandfather Graham Mitchell last month.
"He loved watching me play," Collins told BBC Look East. "The first thing my nan said to me when I got my cap was that he was watching over me. It does hit differently."
He said: "It's been hard. It's the first kind of passing I've been had in my family. I'm grateful I got spent 29 years with him and that's the main thing I look back at.
"But as well, there have been there's been positives since his passing and happiness where I've been able to put a smile on my mum's face, my nan's face, my family's face and bring that bit of happiness when it's been such a sad time.
"That's all I can ask for."

Collins' path to the international stage had been far from conventional.
As a youth team player he took a job at a McDonald's restaurant to afford to fuel his moped to travel to Newport County training prior to landing his first professional deal as a 17-year-old in 2014.
While at the Exiles, he made one appearance for Wales U19s side in 2015 before spells at clubs including Morecambe, Forest Green Rovers, Bristol Rovers and Bolton Wanderers.
But it has been Collins' form at MK Dons that has finally earned him a spot in the Welsh senior setup.
The forward has scored 17 goals in 16 games across all competitions for the League One side this season, including four goals in a game against Doncaster last month, and was subsequently named League One's player of the month for October.
Having sat out Saturday's defeat to Norway, Collins featured against the reigning Nations League holders as a 63rd minute substitute for David Brooks.
"It was when the ball turned over and it fell to players like Bruno [Fernandes], Vitinha, [Joao] Cancelo, that's when I was a bit like, 'Oh'. 
"I realised where I was and that made me realise I'm on the big stage here and playing against some serious players.
"It's just the intensity. You've got the pressure which you maybe you don't expect to get. When they're on the ball you need to cut off every angle because they're able to make a pass from anywhere."

Despite finishing bottom of Group A4, Wales avoided immediate relegation to League B and will instead fight for their place in next March's play-offs.
Having returned from international duty, Collins will resume MK's third-tier campaign with Saturday's trip to Mansfield Town, with Paul Warne's side seeking a fifth consecutive League One win.

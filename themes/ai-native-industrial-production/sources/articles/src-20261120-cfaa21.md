Can You Figure Out What These Spring-Loaded Contacts On The De Tomaso Guara Do?
 

 

By

										Matt Hardigree					

 

										November 20, 2026					

 

										2:45 pm					

 

										27 Comments					

 

I’m at 
Retromobile NYC
 this week, and there are often two kinds of people you meet at shows like this: People who delight in pretending to know everything, and people who delight in learning a bunch of new things. I try not to be the former, and one lovely De Tomaso provided me an opportunity to engage my curiosity.

The car in question actually provided me with 
two
 opportunities be the unknowledgeable type. The first one was when I remarked to a friend I was walking with that the Isdera Imperator he pointed out looked great. Upon closer inspection, it was not an Isdera, but a low-mileage example of the De Tomaso Guara.

This specific example was in the Hagerty booth, because the vehicle is 
currently for sale on Hagerty Marketplace
. Here’s what the listing has to say about the 2,700-mile time capsule:

Introduced at the 1993 Geneva Motor Show, the De Tomaso Guarà was the last car developed with input from company founder Alejandro de Tomaso. Based on the 1991 Maserati Barchetta Stradale prototype and styled by Carlo Gaino of Synthesis Design, it entered production in 1994 and was built in small numbers through 2004, with roughly 50 examples produced across coupe, spyder, and barchetta body styles. Early cars used a mid-mounted BMW-sourced 4.0L V8 and a six-speed manual transaxle, while later cars switched to Ford V8 power.

This 1995 Guarà coupe is finished in silver over a blue leather interior and is powered by its BMW-sourced 4.0L M60 V8 paired with a six-speed manual transmission. The car was imported to the U.S. circa 2020, and its metric odometer shows approximately 2,700 kilometers.

This is one of only 50 cars built, which makes it rare. It also has the early ’90s pop-up headlight styling that makes me love the same generation Lotus Elan. What the description somehow fails to mention is whatever is going on here:

Photo: Matt Hardigree

Those are spring-loaded contacts on the, I think, massive carbon-kevlar composite hood. The Hagerty folks let me push them in a few times and they felt great. There’s plenty of life left in them, if that’s what might be stopping you from spending the money. But what do they do? Here’s a wider photo of the rear of the car.

Photo: Matt Hardigree

The BMW motor is really the star of the show back here, and probably what I should have been focusing on. Instead, my companion and I spent a good chunk of the time trying to track the path of those contacts. Here’s where they connect at the taillight, and this probably provides the biggest clue as to what they do:

Photo: Matt Hardigree

Everything on this car is clean, and I’d have believed it if you told me it only had 270 miles instead of 2,700, though I think it’s been driven just enough that you wouldn’t have to feel guilty about putting some miles on it.

Ok, let’s go onto the interior to see if there are any clues:

Photo: Matt Hardigree

Great steering wheel. These pre-airbag steering wheels have a real clean look that we’re probably never going to see again. Have you figured it out yet? If you haven’t, 
here’s probably the biggest clue
.

Leave your guesses in the comments!

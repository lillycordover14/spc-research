North Carolina (3-1) vs. Marshall (2-3)
Chapel Hill, N.C. — Dean E. Smith Center
Friday, Nov. 20 at 9:00 p.m. ET
TV: ACC Network (Eric Rothman, Jason Capel)

And whether it’s a missed free-throw box-out, whether it’s a missed box-out, whether it’s a turnover, whether it’s not running off a shooter from the three-point line, we just had too many breakdowns at the end.” — Head coach 
Michael Malone 
after UNC’s loss vs. Georgia

“I told our guys my message to them was, ‘We have to learn how to win.’ We can’t get so caught up in the record at this point because Sun Belt play is yet to start, but we have to learn how to win. Tonight was the third game we’ve been up 11 at halftime. We talked about that at halftime. Obviously, that talk didn’t work, because they came back. But I was proud of the way we were able to answer.” — Marshall head coach Cornelius Jackson after its win vs. East Tennessee State.

Pregame Notes

Last Time Out:
 North Carolina is amid a week off after suffering its first loss of the season to Georgia last Friday, 78-70. After leading by nine midway through the second half, the Tar Heels went cold in the final stretch, shooting 9-for-38 from 3-point range, a program record in attempts. 
Terrence Brown
 led UNC with 21 points. 
Neo Avdalas,
 who added 14 points against the Bulldogs, 
suffered a wrist injury
 and will be sidelined for multiple months.

Coming off three straight losses, Marshall downed ETSU, 67-55, on Tuesday. The Thundering Herd were uplifted by 20 points and six 3-pointers from sixth-man Fazl Oshodi, while top scorer Noah Otshudi added 10 points, four rebounds and seven assists. Marshall outrebounded the Buccaneers 48-31.

Analyzing the Thundering Herd:
 The third season for head coach Cornelius Jackson is off to a rollercoaster start, ranging from a competitive, 10-point loss at Ohio State to falling five days later to KenPom’s 207th-ranked Youngstown State. In addition to its win this week, Marshall opened its season with a 92-65 victory over Buffalo. 

The Herd had their fair share of turnover courtesy of the transfer portal, with its starting lineup now featuring Emmanuel Manyuon from Pacific (0.5 ppg), Luke Rasmussen from Tulane (2.6 ppg) and Khaman Maker from DePaul (1.3 ppg). The aforementioned Oshodi, who averaged 8.9 points at Southern last season, is Marshall’s third leading scorer despite coming off the bench. He’s attempted a team-high 38 3-pointers at a 36.8 percent clip through five games.

The early star in 2026 for Jackson, though, is Otshudi, a preseason First-Team All-Sun Belt selection. An addition from the University of Western Ontario in Canada before last season, he has ballooned his scoring average to 20.6 ppg this season. Don’t be fooled by Otshudi’s 6-foot-4 frame and three games with 22 points; the senior has attempted 12 3-pointers this year, doing most of his damage around the rim.

Charlotte native Bryce Slay is the Herd’s second-leading scorer, averaging 18.0 points per game and drilling 44 percent of his triples. Marshall does not get much of its offense from its frontcourt, though the 7-foot-1 Maker and his backup, 6-foot-10 Erich Harding, both average more than 5.5 boards per game.

By the Numbers:
 The Thundering Herd are projected to finish fifth in the Sun Belt preseason poll. Ranked 146th in Evan Miya, 154th in KenPom and 208th in Bart Torvik, Marshall is already on track to improve from its 2025-26 campaign. Albeit in a small sample size, the Herd’s defensive efficiency has jumped from 297th to 96th since last season, holding each of its opponents to under 76 points. 

According to KenPom, Jackson’s defense is top-12 in the country in defending 3-pointers, preventing offensive rebounds and keeping opponents off the free-throw line. Marshall forces 10.6 turnovers per game, though, ranking 321st in defensive turnover percentage. 

The Herd is averaging 76.0 points per game, 43.8 percent from the field and 32.3 percent from deep. Bart Torvik rewards Marshall with the 12th-best defensive rebounding metrics in the country; however, its numbers on the defensive boards are more run-of-the-mill compared to the national average. Its offensive metrics lag well behind its top-100 defense, partially because the Herd turn the ball over 15 times per game, ranking 312th in Division I.

The Matchup:
 Based solely on efficiency, Marshall possesses the worst offense North Carolina will have seen thus far under Malone (244th). The Tar Heels are holding opponents to 35.7 percent from the field this season, but their defensive prowess has been masked by an inability to finish possessions with a rebound. As Malone has alluded to, defense, rebounding and fast-break points springboard off each other, and the Thundering Herd’s scoring woes offer UNC another opportunity to improve in all three of those phases.

Opponents are making 4.8 3-pointers per game and shooting 23.1 percent from deep against Marshall — ranked 12th in Division I. After its record-breaking 38 attempts last week, Carolina may be inclined to attack similarly to how it did against Wofford and Wyoming, prioritizing shots around the rim and improving its success on layups and dunks. The Tar Heels were 11-of-19 on such attempts versus the Bulldogs.   

UNC’s ability or inability to generate transition offense appears to be a barometer for its overall success through four games. The Herd represents the 24th-fastest pace in the country, creating an intriguing matchup for North Carolina, which has played one team in KenPom’s top 150 in tempo.   

Potentially atop the Tar Heels’ to-do list is adapting to the loss of Avdalas. While 
Maximo Adams
 has played starter-like minutes so far, Friday will mark the first data point of how UNC rotates and operates without a 9.0 PPG, 3.5 RPG and 3.8 APG key contributor. 
Terrence Brown
 will presumably inherit some of what Avdalas did structurally, but replacing a 6-foot-9 assist leader isn’t as cut and dry — making North Carolina’s two upcoming lower-tiered matchups, beginning with the Herd, critical before another marquee tilt with West Virginia next week in Charlotte.

Welcome, Cameron Fens: 
Based on the Orange County Superior Court’s ruling on Thursday afternoon, South Dakota transfer Cameron Fens is eligible to suit up for the Tar Heels, effective immediately. The emergency motion for a temporary restraining order fell in Fens’ favor, making the 7-footer available against Marshall, immediately bolstering UNC’s frontcourt.

Fens led the Summit League in rebounds and blocks in 2025-26, averaging 8.7 and 1.5 per game, respectively. The center added 14.4 points per game as well, scoring 20 or more points eight times. According to Malone, the Iowa native has continued to practice with the team despite his eligibility hurdles, and Carolina likely won’t hesitate to insert Fens into the rotation, considering its distinct limitations down low through four games. 

Last Meeting:
 UNC is 3-0 all-time against Marshall, with the last matchup coming on Dec. 22, 2009.

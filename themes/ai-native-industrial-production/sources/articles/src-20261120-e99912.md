Ursa Major uses metal Additive Manufacturing to develop and manufacture its rocket engines (Courtesy Ursa Major)

Ursa Major, based in Berthoud, Colorado, USA, has secured a $10.9 million contract option exercised by the US Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) to continue the development of the Draper liquid rocket engine.

The Draper engine is a storable liquid rocket engine developed for hypersonic applications. Ursa Major uses metal Additive Manufacturing in the production of its rocket engines.

“Draper is at the heart of our hypersonics work, and AFRL and the United States Air Force have been partners from day one,” said Jason Meredith, President, Ursa Major. “This award keeps that work progressing, and it moves us closer to putting real capability in the hands of the warfighter.”

The decision to exercise this option comes following the reportedly successful flights of the Affordable Rapid Missile Demonstrator (ARMD), part of the same contract between AFRL and Ursa Major to develop new rocket propulsion technologies.

The ARMD was 
first tested at supersonic speed earlier this year
 and is powered by the Draper liquid rocket engine.

Founded in 2015, Ursa Major also produces the Hadley liquid rocket engine, which powers the US Department of War’s hypersonic test bed. Its hypersonics portfolio also includes HAVOC, a hypersonic and long-range precision strike missile system.

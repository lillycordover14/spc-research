Boston Dynamics, the robotics arm of Hyundai Motor Group, and semiconductor manufacturer GlobalFoundries have announced an expanded partnership, according to Gasgoo Embodied Intelligence.
Spot, the company's quadruped robot, is set to be deployed in more of GlobalFoundries' wafer fabs across the U.S., Europe, and Asia. Meanwhile, GlobalFoundries will become the first customer outside the automotive sector to test Boston Dynamics' humanoid robot, Atlas—and the first semiconductor maker to validate a humanoid on-site.
The partnership between Boston Dynamics and GlobalFoundries isn't starting from scratch.
GlobalFoundries has already been using Spot for over four years at its Burlington, Vermont plant. The decision to expand follows operational data from Singapore, where two units have logged nearly 900 kilometers in the sub-fab level since April. They completed more than 90,000 inspections with a 99% success rate.
In just two weeks, GlobalFoundries migrated 875 routine inspection tasks to the robot. It now plans to more than double that scope by leveraging visual inspection capabilities.
The setup addresses a specific cost challenge. With thousands of inspection points inside a wafer fab, outfitting every piece of equipment with fixed sensors isn't economical. Instead, Spot acts as a mobile sensor suite, reading temperature and gauge data from pumps, motors, and compressed gas systems on demand.
Pradip Singh, GlobalFoundries' chief manufacturing officer, noted that Spot helps the facility shift from reactive repairs to data-driven predictive maintenance. Under the agreement, Spot will be deployed to plants in Malta, New York; Burlington, Vermont; Dresden, Germany; and Singapore.
Image Source: Hyundai Motor
Unlike Spot, the all-electric Atlas differentiates itself with dexterous dual-arm manipulation—though specific tasks have yet to be defined. The companies plan to assess use cases and integration needs through skill development workshops first, with on-site testing slated to begin in early 2027.
Semiconductor manufacturing demands extreme levels of cleanliness, precision, and safety. Whether humanoid robots can operate reliably on a live production line remains to be proven through testing.
Broadly speaking, the potential customer base for Atlas is expanding beyond Hyundai's own auto factories to GlobalFoundries' wafer fabs. Boston Dynamics is opting to deploy the proven Spot first, then cautiously validate Atlas through early pilots—a strategy of proving value before scaling investment.

HELSINKI, Finland (AP) — Mao Shimada of Japan won the Finlandia Trophy Grand Prix on Saturday, making it two-of-two for the 18-year-old in her first senior-level 
figure staking
 season.
Shimada entered the campaign after winning four consecutive junior world titles and has kept up her winning streak in skating’s big league. She 
won Skate Canada International
 this month and followed that up with another dominant performance in Helsinki.
Already owning a comfortable lead after her short program on Friday, Shimada ran away with it by shaking off an early fall on a quad toeloop early in her free skate to “Waloyo Yamoni” by Christopher Tin. That gave her a total of 229.49 points for the two-day meet.
Niina Petrokina of Estonia, the reigning European champion, was second with 214.06 total points. American Isabeau Levito was third with 207.19, completing a repeat of the podium at Skate Canada.
Japanese women have won all five Grand Prix events so far this season. Olympic bronze medalist Ami Nakai won the season-opener in France and Skate America last week, while 
Mone Chiba won the Cup of China
 earlier this month.
The regular season ends with NHK Trophy in Tokyo next week.
The top six performers in the rankings for each discipline will then qualify for the Grand Prix Final in China from December 10-12. Shimada and Nakai are leading the women’s standings, followed by Alexandra Ignatova, Petrokina and Anastasiia Gubanova. Levito is clinging to sixth and the final berth for the final.

Mihhail Selevko of Estonia won the men’s event after topping both the free skate and the short program to finish with 254.99 points. Sweden’s Andreas Nordeback was second with 240.94, and Japan’s Kazuki Tomono was third with 237.75.
In pairs, Germany’s Annika Hocke and Robert Kunkel won with the highest total score of 196.40 points after finishing both the free skate and the short program in second place.
They edged Canada’s Lia Pereira and Trennt Michaud, who finished with 195.28 points, and Americans Audrey Shin and Spencer Akira Howe, with 193.18.
Seven Russian skaters were withdrawn from the meet early this week after the Finnish government 
barred Russian athletes
 from entering the country due to the war in Ukraine.

FBI reportedly hacked again, this time targeting nationwide police database

                            By 
Lawrence Abrams

November 21, 2026

09:26 AM

0

FBI systems have reportedly been breached for the second time in two months, this time targeting the agency's National Crime Information Center (NCIC), which is used to share information between local, state, and federal law enforcement agencies.

The National Crime Information Center is an FBI-operated database used by law enforcement agencies across the United States to share information about wanted and missing persons, outstanding arrest warrants, stolen vehicles and firearms, protection orders, and other criminal justice records.

Police officers routinely use the system during traffic stops, criminal investigations, and background checks.

According to 
CNN's reporting
, the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the NCIC system, citing two people briefed on the investigation.

The intrusion was reportedly discovered in recent days, although it is unclear when the breach occurred, how the attackers gained access, or whether any information was stolen.

The FBI has not publicly confirmed the incident and did not respond to CNN's request for comment.

The reported breach comes just two months after the ShinyHunters extortion group 
claimed to have breached the FBI's systems
 by exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability.

The earlier attack targeted a third-party platform associated with the FBI's recruitment website, FBIJobs.gov, with ShinyHunters claiming to have stolen sensitive information from the agency's systems.

The FBI subsequently launched an investigation that led to the 
arrests of suspected ShinyHunters members
, including a 
Canadian cybersecurity executive
 reportedly linked to the attacks.

There is currently no indication that the latest NCIC incident is connected to ShinyHunters or the earlier breach.

It remains unclear whether the attackers accessed sensitive law enforcement records stored in NCIC or whether the intrusion affected the system's availability to law enforcement agencies.

BleepingComputer contacted the FBI on Friday evening with questions about the reported breach but has not received a response.

‘Like our security guard’: Cleared at last, Fens gives the Tar Heels new presence&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

CHAPEL HILL — Terrence Brown jumped on Cameron Fens’ back mid-practice Thursday, and Fens walked around the court with Brown on his back.

“He was walking normal,” Brown said. “I don’t know how.”

The Tar Heels had just found out their 7–0, 255-pound center was finally cleared to play. On Friday night, Fens checked in 2½ minutes into North Carolina’s 88–72 win over Marshall to loud cheers from the Smith Center crowd. He had nine points and a team-high eight rebounds in 18 minutes.

His teammates described the player they’ve practiced with since August, and none of them started with his size.

“He just cares about everybody in the locker room as much as he cares about himself,” Brown said. “He’ll run through a brick wall for us if he had to.”

Fens, a graduate student who transferred from South Dakota, had been held out of every game this season because the NCAA considered him ineligible. The NCAA denied his waiver request in October. Fens sued on Wednesday, and Orange County Superior Court Judge Alyson Grine granted him a temporary restraining order Thursday, the day before the Marshall game.

Matt Able said Malone shared the news the same way he does most things in practice, without much ceremony. Malone waited for a dead ball and then stopped everything.

“Coach stopped it for a second and just said Fens is cleared, and we were super hyped,” Able said. “I love Cam. I’m so happy he’s back. He brings a whole other dimension, and I think his impact was definitely felt today.”

Malone said integrating Fens over the past few months meant staying patient. He could practice every day, but none of it could show up in a game. On Friday, Malone was more interested in what the moment meant to Fens than in what it meant for his rotation.

“It was just great to have him available tonight,” Malone said. “I was just happy for him. Everything he’s gone through has not been easy.”

Fens averaged 14.4 points and 8.7 rebounds in his final season at South Dakota and made the All-Summit League first team. His lawyers argue that a misdiagnosed foot injury cut short one of his seasons there, and that he deserves that year back.

Malone said Fens had “been biding his time, working” while he waited.

“Getting him out there tonight was just really fun to see him be able to put on a uniform for him and his family,” Malone said.

Fens scored his first basket on a follow-up shot 2½ minutes after he entered the game. Later he hit a baseline hook shot, and Jarin Stevenson said his teammates had already seen that one plenty of times.

“He hit that hook shot. He’d been hitting that hook shot in practice all the time,” Stevenson said. “He’s been putting in the work. I feel really happy for him to get out there and play.”

UNC needs that work around the basket, but it needs his rebounding even more. Marshall outrebounded the Tar Heels 41–40; the third straight game UNC lost the battle on the boards. When Fens or Sayon Keita was on the floor, the rebounding was noticeably better.

Malone called Fens’ eight rebounds in 18 minutes “incredible” for a player in his first game. He also told his team that Fens can’t fix the problem by himself.

Brown said Fens’ value shows up in plays that don’t make the box score, and that his teammates notice his protective streak even off the court.

“It feels like when we go out places, and he’s with us, it’s like he’s our security guard,” Brown said. “He cares so much about all of us and all our well-being, and that’s how he plays out there on the floor. Whether it’s going and getting rebounds, setting screens for us, setting hard screens, and you need a guy like that who’s gonna get chippy when you need it in big games.”

Able said that energy is contagious, whether Fens scores or not.

“He plays so hard, and he’s such a big dude,” Able said. “So his impact is felt no matter what, whether he scores, whether he doesn’t score.”

Stevenson said the wait was hard on the whole team, especially since nobody knew how it would end.

“It’s definitely a little bit of uncertainty, but again I don’t know too much about it,” Stevenson said. “We’re just here to support him, and I’m just happy he’s able to play.”

That uncertainty isn’t over yet. The restraining order keeps Fens eligible while his case continues, and a preliminary injunction hearing is scheduled for Nov. 30. Malone said he still needs to develop the 18-year-old Keita. He called Fens “the name du jour.”

For one night, though, the Tar Heels were happy just to have him on the floor.

“He adds a lot to us,” Able said. “And I feel like the sky’s the limit as we all just continue to grow with him.”

Cameron Fens timeline

Background

— 2022: Fens graduates from high school as a member of the class of 2022. The NCAA’s new five-year eligibility model does not apply to that class. Fens plays two seasons at Illinois-Chicago, then two at South Dakota.
— 2024–25 season (South Dakota): Fens feels foot pain in November 2024 but continues playing and practicing. He aggravates the injury in January 2025 and doesn’t play again. His attorneys later argue that the injury was a stress fracture misdiagnosed as a muscle or tendon injury, and that no X-ray was taken. They say a correct diagnosis would have qualified him for a redshirt waiver.
— 2025–26 season (South Dakota): Fens is named First Team All-Summit League and to the Summit League All-Defensive Team. He averages 14.4 points, 8.7 rebounds and 1.5 blocks per game. His 12 double-doubles rank 29th in Division I.

This year

— February: South Dakota seeks a medical waiver for Fens.
— April 6: Fens enters the transfer portal and joins a lawsuit against the NCAA in Tennessee seeking a fifth season.
— June: The NCAA passes its “five-for-five” rule, which gives athletes five seasons in five years. The rule doesn’t apply retroactively, leaving the high school class of 2022, including Fens, under the old eligibility rules.
— July 31: A nationwide injunction is granted in 
Wisne v. NCAA
, a separate federal class action covering athletes from the high school class of 2022.
— Early August: A Davidson County, Tennessee, Chancery Court grants a temporary injunction in 
Washington et al. v. NCAA
, a case headlined by former Tar Heel Jalen Washington. The order covers 19 basketball plaintiffs, including Fens, and directs the NCAA to “recognize Plaintiffs as eligible for the 2026–2027 basketball season.”
— Aug. 7: Fens commits to UNC, filling Michael Malone’s final roster spot. He’s UNC’s sixth transfer portal addition and 11th newcomer overall.
— Week of Aug. 17: The Tennessee court denies the NCAA’s request to stay the injunction, so Fens remains eligible.
— Aug. 21: The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit stays the 
Wisne
 injunction.
— Wednesday, Aug. 26: The Tennessee Court of Appeals stays the Chancery Court injunction while the NCAA appeals. Fens can practice but is ineligible for games unless another ruling intervenes. The court sets no timetable for the appeal.
— Oct. 10 The NCAA denies Fens’ medical waiver, saying he had been medically cleared to compete at South Dakota.
— Late October: The difference in eligibility rulings across states draws attention. Former Tar Heel Seth Trimble, now at Louisville, is temporarily eligible through a Kentucky lawsuit. Fens, who sued in Tennessee, isn’t.
— Nov. 6: The Tenth Circuit reverses and vacates the 
Wisne
 injunction, ruling that the plaintiffs failed to define the market for players. The ruling removes Fens’ broadest remaining path to eligibility. That night, he misses UNC’s 109–70 win over Wofford.
— Wednesday: 
Fens sues the NCAA in Orange County Superior Court and asks for a temporary restraining order and a preliminary injunction. 
The new suit doesn’t rely on the class of 2022 argument. Instead, it seeks to recover the season shortened by his 2024–25 injury. The complaint says his January 2025 injury “was a progression” of the fracture he suffered in November 2024.
— Thursday: Judge Alyson Grine holds a virtual hearing with Fens’ lawyers from Womble Bond Dickinson and the NCAA’s legal team. Hours later, she grants a temporary restraining order (below) that temporarily clears Fens to play while the case proceeds. The order bars the NCAA from treating him as ineligible because of the 2024–25 season. Grine finds that his case shows a “likelihood of success” on multiple fronts. In an affidavit, Malone says Fens “belongs to this team, and he should be allowed to play for it.”

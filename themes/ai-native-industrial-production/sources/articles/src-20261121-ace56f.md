Three Takeaways from UNC’s win over Marshall
Some good, some bad as the even-newer-look Tar Heels picked up a win.
by
 
 
Matt Bowling
Nov 21, 2026, 4:00 PM UTC
There were some sloppy stretches, but Carolina ultimately took care of business in a bounce-back win over Marshall on Friday, beating the Thundering Herd 88-72 a week after Georgia handed the Tar Heels their first loss of the season.
Here are my three biggest takeaways.
Cameron Fens is, in fact, a difference maker
A bit more than 24 hours after 
a court granted him eligibility
, Cameron Fens made his Carolina debut and nearly posted a double-double, logging nine points, a team-high eight rebounds, and three blocks in 18 minutes off the bench. He didn’t get up and down the court very well, but that’s not completely shocking for a guy who weighs in at 255 pounds, and the physical presence he brings to the paint is worth the tradeoff.
(Yes, I am aware that Fens’ addition couldn’t keep the Tar Heels from getting outrebounded yet again–but imagine how much worse the 41-40 margin would have been without him).
No Neo, no problem
With Neo Avdalas 
out for the next two months
 after injuring his wrist against Georgia, one of the big questions heading into this game was how well the offense would run without him.
The answer: very well, actually.
Terrence Brown took over as the primary point guard and dished out five assists, matching the most by any Tar Heel this season (Isaiah Denis had five against Wofford). Meanwhile, Matt Able scored a career-high 23 points, including 11 of Carolina’s first 15. As a team, UNC matched its season low with only eight turnovers, and 88 points represents its second-highest scoring output of the year.
By the way, Brown also posted his third 20-point game of the season, so it certainly looks like he can serve as a distributor while still getting buckets himself.
This team’s mentality still needs work
Carolina got off to a blistering start, taking a 17-point lead in just over eight minutes. Before the end of the first half, Marshall had cut it to three.
It’s a bit discouraging to see the same issue that cost the Tar Heels the game against Georgia–namely, failing to close out the half strong–show up again in their very next outing. On a more micro level, there were multiple defensive breakdowns leading to easy baskets for the Thundering Herd. Those lapses in intensity and focus simply can’t happen, and that’s something Michael Malone needs to drill down hard on before UNC’s run of tune-up games ends.

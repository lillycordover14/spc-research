Tulip Siddiq tells podcast she's planning to stay on as Hampstead and Highgate MP

She says she got a standing ovation from members after speaking about her experiences

Saturday, 21st November — By 
Richard Osley

Tulip Siddiq, the MP for Hampstead and Highgate appearing on Politics Unleashed

Hampstead and Highgate MP Tulip Siddiq has sent out a message to Labour hopefuls eyeing a possible opening in her constituency by declaring during a podcast interview that she “definitely” wants to stay in her seat.

The former City minister was speaking to two Tories – Guto Harri and David Davis – on 
Politics Unleashed
.

Her comments come after a strained two years in which she was, in her absence, convicted and sentenced in Bangladesh on corruption charges linked to her aunt, the country’s former leader Sheikh Hasina – all of which she flatly denies. This followed her resignation as the government’s City minister in the Treasury after media reports around how she acquired properties in King’s Cross and Hampstead. Again she denied any wrongdoing, and a standards investigation found “no identified evidence of improprieties”.

She told the podcast that the hurdles had not made her think about stepping down from her seat at the next general election.

“Bear in mind, the reason I got into politics is because I wanted to help the people of Hampstead and Highgate,” she said. “I wanted to represent them and I had such support in the constituency.”

Later she said: “It’s an obsession to represent north London, Hampstead and Highgate… Those things sort of never go away because you want to make your constituency better and try to help people, and I know it sounds a bit cheesy, but it’s really true. It is a passion – so I definitely want to stay as the MP for Hampstead and Highgate.”

The former Camden councillor recalled her first constituency Labour Party meeting after allegations about her family in Bangladesh were reported.

“I’ll never forget the first meeting I had with my CLP after all of this came out, you know, the constituency Labour Party. And when I finished, I mean, I explained things and then when I finished, they gave me the standing ovation. And it was just one of those moments where I was like, oh, they’re reporting all this in the press, and here are my people, my members who hold in their hands the right to deselect me if they want.”

Asked whether she would like to return to the Treasury, Ms Siddiq said: “I would like to help the Labour government. You know, if they want me to be back there, I would take a job.”

Ms Siddiq also described seeing Keir Starmer, the former prime minister and until recently the MP for neighbouring Holborn and St Pancras.

“When I saw him a few weeks ago, he looked quite relieved, actually,” she said. “Keir is 20 years older than me. He’s a lot older than he looks. So I’m 44, he’s 64, and I was looking at him when we went for drinks at their place and I was thinking you could be close to retirement, but I don’t think he’s doing that.”

She said she had begged him not to stand down as an MP but he had wanted to leave the House of Commons and do something on the world stage again.

On her own resignation from his government, she said it had got to the point where “Keir, who is a good friend of mine, was standing up talking about his policies, and every other question would be, is Tulip Siddiq going to resign, is Tulip Siddiq going to resign? And I just thought, you know, when you’ve heard the Prime Minister say for the 17th time ‘I have full confidence in Tulip Siddiq’, you just think, am I becoming the story?

“I had worked so hard to get us into government, and I had been part of the project to get Keir into Number 10. I had supported him from the beginning. I helped him become the MP for Holborn and St Pancras, and then here I am being the obstacle.”

The podcast also turned to the means-testing of the winter fuel payment after Labour took office. Ms Siddiq said: “I didn’t write that policy because I was City minister,” adding that the policy “was presented to us as a necessity that needed to be done.”

Did it make sense economically? “Yes, probably,” she said. Politically? “No.”

The interview returned to the Bangladesh allegations. “I knew I hadn’t done anything wrong,” she said. “None of this is true.”

She described a “Kafkaesque nightmare where I was being convicted in a foreign state,” adding: “I had no idea what I was being convicted for. The only way I found out I had been convicted is when I woke up in the morning and saw an alert from Sky News saying Tulip Siddiq has been convicted.”

“They have never contacted me even to this day,” she said. “It’s been two years of accusations one after another, and I have never been contacted by anyone in the Bangladeshi authorities, not for want of trying.”

Hobbled Hughson not enough for D-IV top-ranked Twelve Bridges in semifinals&nbsp;&nbsp;Turlock Journal

[page text]
Hughson standout Blake Hobbs runs the ball during Friday’s D-IV semifinal against the Raging Rhinos of Twelve Bridges (JORDAN GEORGESON/Gold Country Media).
                        
                    

Hughson head football coach Shaun King knew his team needed to slay a giant to reach another Sac-Joaquin Section title game.
The Huskies, seeded fourth in the SJS Division IV bracket, made the two-hour trip north to Lincoln to take on No. 1 seed Twelve Bridges, a team that quickly established its place atop the bracket in just its fifth year with a varsity football team.
The Raging Rhinos are the definition of a heavyweight in the division and showed it in a 45-7 win on Friday. They entered the semifinal with two losses, the most they’ve had in the past three seasons. They had three straight undefeated regular seasons prior to 2026 and this season finished perfect in Foothill Valley League play for the fourth straight year.

To make matters worse, the Huskies suited up without their super sophomore quarterback Hudson Baldwin.
The southpaw with three Division I scholarship offers left Hughson’s 26-21 win over Roseville in the first half after a hard hit on a touchdown run. He got up holding his hip and got it worked on in a chair on the sideline but did not return to game action. Baldwin’s score made it a close game and Blake Hobbs rushed for a score while Titus Beers rushed for a touchdown and added a pick-six to put the game out of reach.
The Huskies learned this week that Baldwin’s injury was season-ending, a fractured hip that will need multiple weeks to heal. So, they made gameplan adjustments during the week, installing wildcat packages with Beers receiving snaps and prepping freshman Ethan Barnes, who came on in relief last week, for more reps at signal caller.
The Huskies went into the game with confidence in the game plan. The goal was to make sure the best player on the field touched the ball every single possession. And if it wasn’t Beers, it would be Hobbs, who overcame a pair of fumbles last week to rush for 163 yards and a score.

                Hughson's Titus Beers makes a catch in Friday’s D-IV semifinal against the Raging Rhinos of Twelve Bridges (JORDAN GEORGESON/Gold Country Media).
                
            

But Twelve Bridges is the division’s No. 1 team for a reason, boasting a tough-to-contain offense and countering with a physical defense. After the first possession, the Huskies went from the wildcat to a traditional offense with Barnes at quarterback. The Rhinos defense forced him into four interceptions

The highlight of the game for the Huskies came on a 10-play, 78-yard drive on its final possession in the fourth quarter. Barnes completed multiple passes and scrambled to set up a 15-yard rushing touchdown by Caden Bowerman.
The two sides met for the first time in the 2022 Division VI quarterfinals. Hughson rallied to earn a 35-34 win en route to section and state titles.
Friday was their first matchup since then and Twelve Bridges wasted no time announcing its presence. The Rhinos scored on each of their five first half possessions. Four different players reached the end zone on two four-yard rushes, one of 40 yards and one of 11 yards. They ended the half on a 19-yard field goal to take a 31-0 lead into the intermission.
The Huskies offense never quite got going. They nearly put together a scoring drive on their second possession that started on their own 20-yard line but had a 32-yard field goal attempt blocked.
The second half was more of the same. Hughson had turnovers on its first two possessions while Twelve Bridges scored to make it a 38-0 game after forcing a turnover. The Rhinos opened up a 45-0 lead before Hughson’s scoring drive at the end of the game.
The Huskies finish their season 8-4 overall, 4-3 in the toughest small-school football league in California and with a lot of promise.
Friday night was, though, the final game in the successful career of Beers, who joined the Huskies last season as a transfer from Big Valley Christian. He entered Friday’s semifinal as the team’s leader in receiving yards (1,092), receiving touchdowns (13), total tackles (127), tackles for loss (22) and sacks (9.5) and was second in rushing yards and touchdowns.
In just the second season of the Hudson Baldwin era, Hughson has had little drop off as one of the top contenders in its league and the Sac-Joaquin Section. Baldwin will be a junior next season and it is anticipated that the Huskies will bring back Hobbs, their leading rusher, Bowerman, two-way player Izaiah Alkhas and multiple other contributors up and down the offense and defense as they chase another section title game appearance under King.

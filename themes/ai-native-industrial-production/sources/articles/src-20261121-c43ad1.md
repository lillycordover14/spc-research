"I've been in football 37 years and I've never experienced anything like that," said a hugely dispirited Steven Pressley after Dundee were demolished 8-0 at home by Hearts on Saturday.
The jubilant victors can top that span of time by quite a distance given they are celebrating their biggest away win since they began playing league football in the 1890s, a result which also happens to be Dundee's record home defeat.
While there have been higher scores, it was just the third 8-0 Scottish top-flight game since 1988, with Hamilton Academical hammered by Celtic and Rangers.
The eight at Dens Park came from 14 efforts on target and an expected goals (xG) of just 3.5.
Ruthless Hearts put eight past ramshackle 10-man Dundee
There were some fine finishes, a deft first-time effort from Sabah Kerjota being the pick of the bunch, a lot of shoddy defending, and a red card for the hosts before the hour with the score at 4-0.
Just the tonic for Hearts after three defeats in a week prior to the international break, including a League Cup semi-final derby and a shellacking from Falkirk which ended their 19-month unbeaten league streak at Tynecastle.
"They really looked like a team again," enthused former club hero John Robertson on Sportsound. "The rotations were good, the passing was good. The guys coming off the bench wanted to be part of this and they just kept going and going."
Another ex-Hearts player, Michael Stewart, called it the "perfect evening".
"To come here and win was the most important thing, but to do it in such a fashion is as good a reset as you're ever going to get," he said on Premier Sports.
'Clean sheet maybe most important thing'
Hearts are the Premiership's top scorers with 34 from 13 outings. 
Add goals against and you have a whopping tally of 56, with five conceded at home to Falkirk last time and seven shipped at Celtic Park last month.
This was just a third league blank for Wouter Vrancken's defence.
"It sounds crazy after scoring eight, but the clean sheet is maybe the most important thing," added Robertson. "They just haven't had enough of them."
Reflecting on the biggest win of his career, the Belgian appeared to agree, telling BBC Sport Scotland: "What actually pleased me the most was at six or seven when Dundee were countering, nine players were sprinting back to fight for a clean sheet. 
"These are the things we want to see."

Hearts returned from the previous international break with a 5-0 win over St Mirren before the heavy loss to Celtic and Vrancken is certainly not getting carried away with nine more games coming over the next 30 days, beginning at home to Monaco in the Conference League on Thursday.
"We had talks before our days off, they were really honest and open," he revealed.
"It was good to hear but you have to take ownership of it on the pitch. You can talk as much as you want but if you can't show it on the pitch it doesn't mean anything.
"When you do it like this it's the first step. But it's only the first step, only one game and we have to try to bring more.
"It was ruthless but the schedule is also ruthless â but we got a good boost from tonight."
Pressley appalled and apologetic
Having gone into the international break on the back off those three successive losses, Hearts fans may have been apprehensive.
After all, Dundee had lost just once at Dens Park all season, back in August.
However, the hosts were abject, allowing Hearts so much room.
"Every aspect of our performance was below par," bemoaned Pressley. "We looked unmotivated and lacking edge.
Figure caption, 
Pressley says 8-0 defeat is 'on him'
"Of course the sending off made the second half very difficult, but we didn't do the things that are normally associated with our performance. 
"We weren't gritty enough in our defending, we didn't fill the spaces we normally do. We normally defend in a particularly strong manner, but we didn't do that.
"I can only apologise. My thoughts are that it's on myself. It's my responsibility to make sure you see a motivated Dundee team, it is my responsibility to make sure we are tactically sound, it is my responsibility to make sure they show grit and determination and not go under like we did.
"I had a little sense we haven't been quite on it over the last couple of weeks but I never expected that and I take ownership."

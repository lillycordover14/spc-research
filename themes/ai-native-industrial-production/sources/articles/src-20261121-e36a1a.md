Able’s career night, Brown’s steady hand lift UNC past Marshall&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

CHAPEL HILL — With a different rotation and a clear lead point guard, North Carolina smoothly rebounded from its first loss.

There were a few lulls and surprising defensive lapses, especially in the second half. But the Tar Heels had impressive stretches and Cameron Fens with rebounding in an 88–72 victory Friday at the Smith Center over Marshall.

With Neo Avdalas out, Terrence Brown had the ball in his hands for more minutes than he had all season. He scored 20 points and dished out a season-high five assists. Matt Able scored a career-high 23 points and made five 3-pointers, after combining for seven from beyond the arc in the first four games.

Brown said the move to point guard didn’t feel foreign, since he had played the position for most of his career. His priority Friday was getting his teammates involved before looking for his own shot.

“It’s not really hard getting used to, just trying to help get everybody else shots because obviously I’m a scorer,” Brown said. “I was trying to get Matt going early, getting all those other guys going early. Then it would open up for me to get more shots.”

After opponents frequently got second and third shots in earlier games, the Tar Heels (4–1) allowed Marshall only four offensive rebounds in the first half. That number rose to 12 after halftime and Marshall (2–4) still outrebounded UNC 41–40. It was the third straight game, and the fourth in five, that the Tar Heels lost the rebounding battle.

Fens pulled down a team-high eight rebounds and finished with nine points in 18 minutes. As long as Sayon Keita or Fens was in the game, rebounding improved, though the Heels still struggled to box out at times, with Alex Samodurov among the biggest offenders.

UNC coach Michael Malone said his players were excited going into the game because they knew their new big man would help on the glass. He also warned them that one player couldn’t fix the problem alone.

“One thing I told our players, just because he is back, it can’t be all on Cameron Fens. Everybody else has to do their job,” Malone said.

Fens checked in 2½ minutes into the game to loud cheers and scored his first basket on a follow-up shot 2½ minutes later. He also showed off a nice baseline hook shot.

“It was just great to have him available tonight,” coach Michael Malone said. “I was just happy for him. Everything he’s gone through has not been easy.”

The 7-foot center had waited months to be cleared to play, and his teammates found out mid-practice this week. Able said Malone stopped practice after a dead ball to share the news.

“Coach stopped it for a second and just said Fens is cleared, and we were super hyped,” said Able, who reached double figures for the 19th time in his career and made a career-high eight field goals. “I think TB ended up jumping on him, and Cam’s a big dude, so Cam was like walking with TB on his back.”

Brown confirmed the story. He said Fens carried him around the court without any trouble, then talked about what the big man means to the locker room.

“He just cares about everybody in the locker room as much as he cares about himself,” Brown said. “It feels like when we go out places, and he’s with us, it’s like he’s our security guard. He cares so much about all of us and all our well-being, and that’s how he plays out there on the floor.”

Malone said that Fens offers a different kind of presence than the Tar Heels have had, though he reminded everyone that he still needs to develop the 18-year-old Keita.

“He’s 255 pounds. He’s 7 feet tall. He’s big. He’s strong. He’s athletic. He’s skilled, and so when you have him available, he gives you just a different presence,” Malone said. “So I know right now, Cam is the name du jour, but I have to help each and every guy on our team because that’s my job.”

Jarin Stevenson scored a season-high 13 points and made two 3-pointers, his first game this season with multiple 3s. The senior forward hadn’t had a big offensive night since the exhibition win over Southern Cal in October, and he credited a small change in his shot.

“Staying confident in my shot, I was able to extend a little bit more on my shot,” Stevenson said. “I feel like that’s part of the reason why I was more consistent today.”

Freshman forward Maxi Adams took the spot in the starting lineup that Avdalas vacated after fracturing his left wrist in last week’s loss to Georgia. Malone said he learned about the injury Sunday, and then he had to call Avdalas to tell him what the X-ray showed.

“That’s not news that you want to deliver,” Malone said. “But while he’s out, that’s an opportunity for other guys to step up, and we’ll have to just man the fort while he’s down.”

Adams had his least productive scoring game of the season, finishing with two points on 1-of-7 shooting, though he added seven rebounds. Malone said he chose Adams over other options for his rebounding. Starting him also let UNC play a big lineup with Adams at small forward, Stevenson at power forward and Keita at center.

“Maxi is a guy that we rely upon to defend, to rebound, to run the floor, and he’s not worried about going 1 for 7, only scoring two points,” Malone said. “And we’re not worried about it because we know come Monday night we’ll get a much better effort from him.”

Able scored 11 of UNC’s first 18 points. The Heels led 23–6 after a Jaydon Young 3-pointer capped a 16–2 run, and they had no turnovers in the first eight minutes. Able said Malone called several plays early to get him open, and once the first shot dropped, he settled in.

“Coach had some really good plays to start the game just to get me open,” Able said. “I felt good, obviously, that the shots were going in, but there’s other parts of the game that I need to still keep working at, get better at, and I think that goes for the whole team as well.”

Marshall answered with an 18–7 run. Alex Charlotin’s steal and dunk, followed by a free throw, cut the deficit to three with 1:08 left in the first half, and UNC led 42–37 at halftime.

Malone didn’t like how his team handled the big lead. He gave Marshall credit for never letting up, but he said his players lost their focus once things got easy.

“I felt we did not show maturity when we built that lead. I thought we relaxed,” Malone said. “I felt that we forgot what helped us get the lead in the first place. We got a little bit bored with success, and then next thing you know, we got rattled.”

The Tar Heels quickly rebuilt the lead to 13 when a Stevenson three-point play with 16:11 left capped a 9–2 run that included six Brown points. Malone said he liked how his team came out of halftime, and that it showed some maturity and poise.

Brown leaned on Able early before taking over after halftime

“Obviously I started off really slow in the first half, so that’s what was really big for me, is just if I’m starting slow, I gotta trust in other people, and that’s Matt,” Brown said. “And then when it opened up for me to get downhill and get my shots off, I just took advantage of [it].”

Marshall pulled within six with a 9–2 run with 12:15 left, but UNC responded with nine straight points, and a dunk and jumper from Keita pushed the lead back to 15.

Malone said Brown’s first start at point guard wasn’t perfect. He wants Brown to be more vocal and to be on the same page with him more often. Still, he liked what he saw from a player he said will never take a backward step.

“He’s just coming at you for 40 minutes, and sometimes that’s a good thing, and sometimes it gets him into trouble,” Malone said. “But I like the fact that he has an attack mentality, and I thought he was really instrumental in that second half. I thought Terrence had a stretch in that second half where he led the charge and put the team on his back a little bit, and we fed off of that.”

The defense held up better early than in recent games. UNC limited Marshall’s best 3-point shooters, sent the Herd to the free-throw line only 14 times and scored 20 fast-break points. Malone said the Tar Heels should have had even more.

Able said the faster pace was partly a response to losing Avdalas. Without their primary playmaker, the Tar Heels needed other ways to create easy baskets.

“I think we’re feeling the loss of Neo. He’s a great point guard. He makes offense a lot easier for us,” Able said. “So, we had to find other ways, like getting out in transition, just to be able to create those easier buckets.”

The second half still exposed the two problems Malone sees most. Marshall finished with 16 offensive rebounds, which led to 12 points, and the Herd kept scoring right after UNC baskets.

“It happened in the Georgia game as well. We’re scoring baskets. We’re celebrating … and they’re getting it in, and they’re running the ball up the court, and they’re scoring,” Malone said. “After we score, that has to stop.”

Malone also pointed out that Able had only two rebounds, even as he praised his all-around game. Able, who had three assists and one turnover, agreed with his coach.

“There are times when I might box out, but I just need to make that effort to go ahead and grab the ball instead of hoping someone else gets it,” Able said. “Especially me as one of the leaders, I feel like I need to step up as well.”

Brown said communication and focus will be important as the team works to improve its defense and transition defense. He also said the week’s physical practices after the loss to Georgia challenged the Tar Heels to compete for rebounds and play harder.

“The minor mistakes that we were making, we’ve got to fix those,” Brown said of the Georgia loss. “The film and the game really helped us look at how good we could be if we just fix those small mistakes and put them out on the floor.”

UNC hosts Monmouth, coached by former Tar Heels point guard King Rice, at 8 p.m. Monday. Malone said the rebounding must improve before then, and that he is looking forward to welcoming Rice back to the Smith Center.

“I think it’s going to be really great to have King back, such a storied player in this tradition, this great program,” Malone said. “So I’m thankful that King is coming back, and hopefully we can play better as we welcome him back on Monday evening.”

Notes

— Monmouth (1–4), which lost 75–64 Friday night at Princeton, gave Wake Forest a good game on the road Tuesday before losing 81–76. Rice’s staff includes Assistant Coach Brian Reese and Assistant Coach for Player Development Shammond Williams.
— UNC has won all four meetings with Marshall, all in Chapel Hill, but it was the first meeting since a 98–61 win in the Smith Center on Dec. 22, 2009.
— Able’s previous scoring high was 19 twice last season with N.C. State: against Florida State and UNC.
— Carolina held Marshall to 38.4% from the floor, the fifth time in as many games this season UNC has held opponents below 40%.
— Marshall made six 3-pointers, fewest by an opponent this season.
— Carolina turned the ball over eight times, the fourth time in five games UNC has committed fewer than 10 turnovers.

MONTICELLO, Ill. (WAND) – Urbana residents Jo and Doug Kibbee made a six-figure gift to establish a named endowment supporting the natural areas at Allerton Park and Retreat Center in Monticello.
The endowment will support environmental research and habitat improvement to restore and sustain native species.
The couple visited Allerton nearly 40 years ago with their children. Allerton’s recent development of programming and facility improvements caught their attention, and they thought about the impact they wanted to have. 
“We thought, hey — what about the natural area?” Jo said. “That’s what really brought us out here. What about really taking care of that, expanding it or preserving it?”
The Kibbees worked with Allerton staff for a couple of years to figure out how their gift could best be used. They eventually settled on habitat restoration and research. 
Alex Lourash and Jo Kibbee examine the unique characteristics of Orange Jewelweed.
(Allerton Park and Retreat Center)
“They showed us the [trail] cameras that showed all the different kinds of wildlife that came to different parts of the Park,” Doug said. “And making sure that wildlife would thrive was an important part of what we were looking at.”
Jo said their intention is to help bring the natural area back to a similar ecological balance to what it once had.
“How can you restore and preserve a natural area to maximize the natural life in it?” she said. “Getting rid of invasives is a big part of it.”
The endowment will help provide park staff with funding to continue assisting researchers studying wildlife, habitats and other ecological issues at Allerton.

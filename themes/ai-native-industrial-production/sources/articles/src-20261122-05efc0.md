THE Trinidad and Tobago Police Service (TTPS) says it utilises multiple benchmarks when assessing crime trends and public safety outcomes.
This includes year-on-year and monthly comparisons, long-term analysis and comparisons with significant periods in the nation’s crime history.
In a statement yesterday, the TTPS explained its approach to reporting and analysing homicide statistics following public discussion over the use of historical comparisons in crime reporting.
“The TTPS does not rely on a single benchmark. Rather, the Service adopts a comprehensive and transparent analytical approach designed to provide the public with a complete understanding of crime trends over time,” it stated.
The statement comes on the heels of Arouca/Lopinot MP Marvin Gonzales calling on the Police Service Commission (PolSC) to conduct an independent audit of TTPS crime statistics as he questioned the use of 2024 instead of 2025 as the comparison year and the accuracy of its reported 46.2% reduction in murders.
According to Gonzales, the TTPS’ decision to compare the 296 murders recorded between January 1 and November 16 this year with 550 murders recorded during the corresponding period in 2024 raised questions about the way crime trends were being presented.
He said the more appropriate comparison was with 2025, when 325 murders were recorded during the corresponding period. That would mean a reduction of 29 murders, or 8.9%, rather than the 46.2% reduction highlighted by the TTPS, he maintained.
Appropriate and necessary
In its statement yesterday, the TTPS defended its use of 2024 as a reference point, saying that year recorded the highest annual homicide figure in Trinidad and Tobago’s history.
“As a result, comparisons against 2024 provide an important measure of how far the country has progressed from the most severe levels of violence ever experienced,” it said.
The TTPS said it remained committed to ensuring the country never returned to those levels of violence and therefore considered it “both appropriate and necessary” to assess current performance against that historical peak.
It stressed, however, that comparisons with the corresponding period of the previous year remained an important tool routinely used to measure immediate changes in crime levels.
It explained that such comparisons establish whether crime is increasing or decreasing relative to the same period a year earlier.
Historical comparisons serve a different purpose, allowing the police and the public to assess progress against the most challenging periods in the nation’s history and determine whether long-term reductions are being achieved and sustained, it added.
The TTPS said its long-term analysis draws on homicide data dating back to 2001, when comprehensive and granular records became available. This enables the Service to examine both short-term changes and broader historical patterns over a 25-year period.
“The TTPS believes that presenting both year-on-year data and long-term historical trends provides the most accurate, transparent and balanced assessment of public safety outcomes,” it stated.
The TTPS reaffirmed its commitment to evidence-based policing, data-driven decision-making and transparent crime reporting.
It said the public could expect the continued publication of monthly, annual and historical crime analyses to help place progress, challenges and emerging trends in their proper context.
The TTPS also pledged to continue working with all stakeholders to reduce violent crime, improve public safety and build safer communities throughout Trinidad and Tobago.

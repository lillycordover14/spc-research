FARGO — In the end, the 22 players in their last home appearance (maybe) at Gate City Bank Field at the Fargodome got a victory. North Dakota State held on for a 31-25 win over winless Northern Illinois, a game that the Bison had to sweat out late in the fourth quarter.
Yes, it prevented some of the stars from being taken out to some fanfare. Most of the 22 players, 15 to be exact, spent five years with the program.

ADVERTISEMENT

That in this day and age is worthy of a standing ovation alone.
True, NDSU is in its first year of NCAA Division I FBS, but there is a clear distinction on what a real program looks like compared to one that is struggling. Through three quarters, this was Example A but give the 0-11 Huskies credit for making a game of it.
NDSU finished its Mountain West Conference at 6-2 and is 9-2 overall, which is a sound body of work for a team that was FCS when the move up was made.
The Bison are a good team.
Not a perfect team. There were some head-scratching, non-running play calls in the fourth quarter against a team that came in 134th of 138 FBS teams in rushing defense. But a win is a win. They’re all enjoyable.
“Most definitely,” said Bison receiver Mekhi Collins. “Especially us seniors. We’ll probably have a little get-together, eat some food and just hang out. It’s just nice to be part of something so special.”
There is an avenue for NDSU to return to the dome. But if Saturday was it, the Bison seniors did a laudable job in Fargo in the school’s first FBS season. A win at San Jose State next week and NDSU’s 10-2 record would supplant Jacksonville State and James Madison, who had nine wins in their first FBS seasons, as best debut of an FCS program.

ADVERTISEMENT

“It’s huge, it’s a testament to the dudes that came before us,” Collins said. “I can’t do anything but be thankful. That goes for everybody. I know all these seniors, it’s a surreal moment and you put so much into this program. I think we’re all going to be happy and enjoy this.”
Collins’s fifth year has been his best. He put behind injury issues in previous years. His 35-yard touchdown reception in the second quarter gave the Bison a 14-3 lead, a play where Collins landed on top of a defensive back around the 1-yard line and rolled into the end zone.
“Felt like I was surfing a bit, felt like I was back in Hawaii,” Collins said. “That was my first reaction. I pointed at the ref and said, ‘Give it me, I know I was in there. We had an air attack today so my mindset was when the ball is in the air, it has to be mine. Just go up and get it. It also helps having this guy throwing the rock to me.”

North Dakota State's Mekhi Collins is honored at senior night before the game against Northern Illinois on Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026, at the Fargodome.  
Lexi Hills / The Forum

That would be quarterback Nathan Hayes, who was sitting next to Collins in the post-game press conference. Hayes finished 15 of 23 passing with four touchdowns.
The Bison also welcomed the return of a running game. DJ Scott averaged 7.6 yards per carry and Nyeoti Punni 7.5.
“We knew if we could run the ball it would open up some of the air attack,” Hayes said.
If there was any question why NIU was winless coming in, it was answered late in the first half. Trailing 21-3 with three time outs, the Huskies got the ball with 2:28 remaining. A 32-yard run by Elijah Porter reached the Bison 40-yard line and they were within striking distance.

ADVERTISEMENT

No huddle offense? Like, try to hurry a little bit? Nope. The Huskies took their time, including a huddle on one play, and didn’t use their first time out until 24 seconds were left. A false start penalty forced a fourth-and-8 and an incomplete pass halted the scoring chance.
NDSU still had 19 seconds to do something. A 28-yard pass to a wide open Isiah St. Romain reached the 33 at :11. Only a missed pass interference call on Collins prevented a touchdown on the next play.

North Dakota State's Nathan Hayes looks up the field for an open receiver against Northern Illinois on Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026, at the Fargodome.
Lexi Hills / The Forum

As it was, a 51-yard field goal by Drew Klein gave the Bison a 24-3 lead at halftime. One team was trying to score a TD in the last two minutes; the other, well, I’m not sure what the plan was.
Anyway, it was a fitting finale for the seniors who took the baton in the first year of FBS ball. Hayes has been near the top in the Mountain West all season in passing touchdowns. Collins is near the top in touchdown receptions.
“That was my whole mindset today, get the ‘dub,” Collins said. “There was to be no losses in my mind. Go up and get every ball thrown my way and go get a block for the running backs if they get a hole. I’m so happy I can be a part of this.”

Lewis Hamilton hopes Ferrari can resolve its driver dilemma after yet another incident with team-mate Charles Leclerc at the Las Vegas Grand Prix.
							

Hamilton was spun around by Leclerc as the pair fought over sixth place on Lap 38 of 50 in Sin City, for which the latter was given a 10-second time penalty.
							

The seven-time F1 drivers' champion put the onus on the team to fix the problems the pair have experienced with wheel-to-wheel racing of late.
							

At the Mexico City Grand Prix, their first-lap battle cost them a likely victory from a strong 1-2 position.
							

That led to harsh words from team principal Fred Vasseur, who has reiterated that stance now, and Hamilton did not hold back, either, with his comments in the weeks that followed.
							

However, after talks at the previous round in São Paulo, both the British driver and Leclerc were keen to put the tensions behind them and work together moving forward.
							

That was short-lived. But unlike last time, Hamilton was unwilling to remark on the latest incident.
							

"I'm not going to get into the scenario today," he told 
Sky Sports F1
 after recovering to seventh in Nevada. "That's something for you to speak to Ferrari about."

Hamilton: team orders for Ferrari 'to decide'

Instead, Hamilton focused on his evening under the lights of the Las Vegas Strip Circuit. He highlighted the difficulties the SF-26 endured with its underpowered power unit and the 10-second time penalty he earned for leaving the track and gaining an advantage during an early-race battle with Lando Norris and Leclerc.
							

"But we knew that we were faced with a massive challenge this weekend," he added. "We didn't realise that there would be such a limitation on these straights, and the qualifying obviously was difficult.
							

"In the race, I felt great, and I really was progressing well. I really ultimately wanted to try and make sure we finish ahead of the McLarens...
							

"And then avoiding collision with one of the McLarens got me a 10-second penalty, which was, I think, really harsh, which was frustrating.
							

"But I just got my head back down and just kept chasing. Managed to get back in the fight, and then again got knocked back again.
							

"I never gave up, and I really was just trying to get the best results. I really think we could have had a podium today with the pace we had."
							

Given the apparent inability of Hamilton and Leclerc, for which most blame has been placed at the feet of the latter, to live harmoniously on track and fight for the betterment of the Scuderia, the former was asked whether team orders and rules of engagement must now be implemented.
							

"That's for Ferrari to decide," Hamilton replied. "Obviously, I've done all I can, and I've said everything and more... it's been difficult.
							

"So all I can do is just show up and give it everything I can, and hopefully in the background they eventually figure it out."

By R.L. Bynum

Ven-Allen Lubin had the biggest night of his career in the biggest game of N.C. State’s young season, but it wasn’t enough on a weekend that Jonathan Powell also had a career-best game and Elliot Cadeau had a tough game.

Lubin scored a career-high 27 points and grabbed eight rebounds Friday night in N.C. State’s 87–81 overtime loss to Seton Hall in the Baha Mar Championship final in Nassau. He went 8-of–12 from the floor and made all 11 of his free throws. After managing two points in the first half, he scored 23 after halftime, making all seven shots and all nine free-throw attempts.

Lubin carried the Wolfpack late. He scored 12 straight N.C. State points during one second-half stretch and, with seven seconds left in regulation, he scored two free throws to tie it at 72 and force overtime. N.C. State managed one field goal in the extra period, and it was its first loss of the Justin Gainey era.

Lubin’s breakout came after Gainey moved him to center in the second half. Kyle Evans tweaked his knee, and Gainey saw a chance to get Lubin the ball closer to the basket.

“We saw a matchup that we wanted to take advantage of,” Gainey said, 
according to a Pack Insider
 transcript
. “We also wanted to get him closer to the basket.”

Lubin said the switch put him back in spots where he’s most comfortable.

“I just think it created more of a mismatch, getting more of a position where I’m more comfortable,” he said, 
according to a 
Pack Insider
 transcript
. “I can attack from the perimeter, but I think at the five, you hang around the dunker spot, the post and the mid-post.”

The career high came a night after a rough shooting game. Lubin scored six points on 3–10 shooting in Thursday’s 93–83 semifinal win over Murray State, a game in which teammate Paul McNeil Jr. scored 33 points with nine 3-pointers.

N.C. State (5–1) went 4–22 from 3-point range against Seton Hall and committed 16 turnovers.

Powell scored 24 points on 8-of–11 shooting, 5-of–7 from 3-point range, in a 104–82 home win Friday over Central Michigan.

Powell scored 10 points during an early 13–3 run and reached double figures in the first five minutes. He had 16 points at halftime, then made two more 3-pointers early in the second half as Pitt pulled away. He also went 3-of–4 at the line.

Powell, Baye Ndongo and Naithan George were the first Pitt trio to score at least 20 points in the same game since a 2021 win over Syracuse. 

Kyan Evans has quickly become the engine of an unbeaten Minnesota team. The guard had 15 points and 10 assists, tying his career-high in assists, in Friday’s 85–70 win over Oklahoma State at the Charleston Classic. That gave him a double-double — his third in his last four games. He also had four steals and made 8 of 9 free throws.

Evans is back with Niko Medved, who coached him for two seasons at Colorado State before he spent last season at UNC. Medved, 
according to the Star Tribune
, said he calls Evans an “irrational confidence guy,” referring to his tendency to mix highlight-reel plays with reckless ones.

“I thought we outwilled them [Friday] with our style,” Medved said. “It’s fundamentals we do every day.”

Evans’ former UNC teammate Luka Bogavac was on the other side. He scored four points in 12 minutes off the bench for Oklahoma State (3–2).

Cadeau and No. 18 Michigan lost 79–73 against Villanova on Saturday night at Xfinity Mobile Arena in Philadelphia. Cadeau finished with 11 points, six rebounds and five assists, but he shot 3-of-12, committed five turnovers and fouled out.

He missed his first eight shots. Cadeau capped a 14–3 spurt with back-to-back layups that made it 66–60 with 4:17 to go, part of a rally from 17 points down that fell short.

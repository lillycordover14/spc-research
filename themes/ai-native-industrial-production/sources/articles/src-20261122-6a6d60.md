Dominick Barlow’s impact was felt immediately in his season debut
Dominick Barlow still needs to shake off some rust, but his season debut gave a glimpse of how he can help the Sixers.
by
 
 
Josh Grieb
Nov 22, 2026, 2:35 PM UTC
The Sixers’ latest win was another nail-biter. They barely escaped by a one-win Milwaukee Bucks team 132-129, catching multiple breaks in the last minute of the game to squeak out a victory. While the starters are still looking to put together that first laugher of a win this season, they did welcome back very important depth piece to the lineup in the win Saturday.
After missing the first 16 games with a back injury, Dominick Barlow made a productive season debut. He scored 12 points in 11:28 minutes of action, shooting 5-of-6 from the field with four rebounds, three being on the offensive end of the floor. His only miss was a corner three that he airballed in the first half. After the game, Barlow joked that he had no legs on that one. He buried his next chance at a corner three in the second half.
“It was a lot of fun,” said Barlow after the game. “That was what I’ve been looking forward [to].”
Barlow earned himself a reputation for being a heady cutter in his first year with the Sixers, and it didn’t take long for that to be on display with their new elite passer. Three of Barlow’s five field goals of the night were assisted by LeBron James.
“If he’s passing the ball in a situation, you’re probably open,” Barlow said, “so you kind of have to be aggressive in that situation.”
After having zero time to get to play with James in training camp, Barlow was figuring out where to be on the fly. For his first time out there, he was mostly able to figure it out.
“When that level of IQ is on the court, move around, find space, he’s gonna find you. Run in transition, he’s gonna throw it,” he said. “Just playing with energy, stuff that I do well, I think complement the things that make him an all-time great.”
While Barlow and James are still new to each other, Barlow’s knack for finding space to cut is something the team has been waiting for to return. They know that skill will translate very well when sharing the court with as much star talent as the Sixers have.
“I think he’s gonna be a recipient of some of the cutting and stuff from passes from LeBron and [Joel Embiid],” said head coach Nick Nurse. “That’s kind of a need, I think, that we got a cutter who can finish.”
Everything Barlow can do to help the team offensively is a big boost, because he just may be the best backup center option currently on the roster. That proved to the be case in the second round of the playoffs last year against the New York Knicks. While not being a traditional center, Barlow is slightly taller, longer and has more athleticism than Jabari Walker. Thanks to how Walker’s shot the ball this year, Nurse was able to play both of them together against the Bucks, a look that should be sustainable against other teams.
“We miss him. You can see by him being on the court, playing both positions, four and five, what he can do with the switchability on defense,” Joel Embiid said. “I thought [he was] fantastic for his first game, especially because he hasn’t gotten a lot of time on the court.”
That wasn’t all quite on display against the Bucks, but Barlow is hopeful that will come with time. He definitely felt like he had to adjust more on that end of the court in his return.
“Defensively is honestly probably where I felt some more rust,” he said. “Just guarding a lot of bodies, I haven’t been able to guard guys playing at that speed. A lot of stuff vs. coaches, and our coaches do a tremendous job of being in shape. But it’s a little bit different when Tyler Herro was trying to come down and score 50.”
Herro’s 50-ball nearly put a damper on what should have been an easy afternoon win while acclimating a rotation piece into a new lineup. The Sixers have some cleaning up to do to make sure games like that don’t slip away from them in the future, but contuining to get healthier should help that cause.

Three takeaways from UNC’s faceplant against Virginia
The QB kid was alright. The defense, not so much.
by
 
 
David Starnes
Nov 22, 2026, 2:00 PM UTC
UNC went to Charlottesville and got whipped. What did we learn in the process?
Travis Burgess flashed a lot of talent in a game in which the QB wouldn’t have mattered
Starting a true freshman quarterback on the road in a conference road game often goes sideways. It doesn’t even have to be errors by the quarterback. On UNC’s first two third downs, false starts turned a 3rd and 6 into a 3rd and 11 and then a 3rd and 7 into a 3rd and 12. 3rd and 12 turned into an interception on an underthrown pass to the sideline. Add two errant Burgess throws to Shipp on those two opening drives. It was a messy beginning that found UNC in a 10-0 hole early.
After those initial hiccups, Travis went 23-40 for 345 yards, one touchdown, and one interception. He ran for 106 more yards, totaling 480 yards of offense himself. The most yardage gained by an entire UNC offense under Bill Belichick prior to this game: 427 against East Tennessee State. It wasn’t perfect, but it’s clear that Travis Burgess can play QB for a P4 team at a high level with continued reps and better timing.
While the two interceptions weren’t what anyone would want to see, they were the only “pick worthy” throws Burgess made all game. In that respect, two picks were in some ways bad luck. For the most part, Burgess kept the ball out of harm’s way and showed good field vision as both a runner and passer. It’s a small thing, but his scramble on the final play of the first half demonstrated great situational awareness, getting as many yards as he could to shorten the field goal attempt but getting down a couple of seconds before the half expired.
Burgess clearly has the makings to be an exciting QB1 for a P4 team next season. Hopefully that’s UNC.
UNC’s defense was abysmal, which is why another QB wouldn’t have mattered.
In three losses to ACC teams ranked behind the Heels in the major efficiency metrics, three struggling QBs erupted with season-best performances. Beau Pribula, like Tait Reynolds and Steve Angeli before him, turned in his best performance of the year against Bill Belichick’s defense. Pribula threw for 297 yards and four touchdowns with zero interceptions. His gest game prior to Saturday was against Delaware: 279 yards and four touchdowns. Against ACC competition, his best game was against Wake Forest, with 212 yards, one touchdown, and one pick. It should be noted that Wake Forest won that game 51-7, allowing a lot of those yards to Virginia long past the point the game was decided.
The damage Saturday was done primarily by running backs isolated against UNC linebackers in coverage. Jekail Middlebrook and Xavier Brown out of the backfield amassed seven catches for 
179 yards
 and two touchdowns. Worse, UNC never made any adjustments to counter Virginia’s clear intent; the strategy worked all game long, even after veteran safety Greg Smith returned to the game after halftime. The final dagger was again a wheel route that Smith looked confused on where he was supposed to be and who he was supposed to be covering. This was not a new wrinkle for Virginia or something they’d never shown before. UNC simply wasn’t prepared to defend it successfully.
Virginia for the game averaged 7.9 yards per play after mustering only 4.9 yards per play against conference competition this season. It’s a complete inversion of last week, when the UNC defense held Louisville to three yards per play below its season average. This sort of erratic week to week performance is now a clear trend within the Belichick program. One week a unit shows out, the next it collapses. It’s baffling.
Bill Belichick and his staff cannot build on success
With every step forward comes a step back, maybe two. Bill Belichick is now 5-14 against P4 programs (plus Notre Dame) at UNC, and UNC has now lost four P4 games in a row after beating Duke at Duke. A winning record for this season is now off the table. UNC must beat NC State at home both to reach a bowl game and to prevent another losing season.
Yesterday’s game was a bad day for the coaching department across the board. After being one of the least penalized teams in the conference, UNC racked up 11 flags for 82 yards. On two crucial fourth and shorts when the game was still competitive, UNC went full “manball,” sending June straight into the teeth of the Virginia defense with no motion or use of a mobile QB. The decisions were even more frustrating given that UNC’s interior line was struggling to handle Virginia’s defensive tackles. Both efforts were stuffed, and soon afterwards Virginia put the game out of reach with the aforementioned wheel route to Brown.
Special teams had a field goal blocked and missed another.
The defense, as noted, had no answer for Virginia’s running backs in the passing game.
Worse, this now means another season for Bill Belichick without a winning record. Going back to his days at New England, his last five years look like this:
8-9
4-13
Unemployed
4-8
5-6
Would UNC move on from Bill Belichick with a loss at home to NC State and a second losing season in a row? That seemed implausible after beating Duke in Durham, when UNC stood at 4-2 with four very winnable games left on the schedule. Now, it’s an open question. Even if Belichick does return for season three, will 5-7 or 6-6 generate enough excitement for donors and potential portal adds for the program to take steps forward next season?

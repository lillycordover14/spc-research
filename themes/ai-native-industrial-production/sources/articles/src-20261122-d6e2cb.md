NCAA will drop pregame handshake rule at women's tourney following Geno Auriemma-Dawn Staley incident&nbsp;&nbsp;New York Post

[page text]
The NCAA women’s basketball tournament is set to scrap the rule of pregame handshakes between head coaches, 
per multiple reports Saturday
 that cited a memo sent by the Division I women’s basketball committee to all D-I programs. 

Per the memo, the committee reportedly “encourages” coaches to still shake hands informally ahead of team introductions at the tournament. However, it will no longer be a requirement. 

The change comes after the ruling blew up during the women’s Final Four a year ago when Geno Auriemma 
infamously had a fiery confrontation with Dawn Staley
 in a postgame handshake line following the Gamecocks’ semifinal victory over the Huskies.

The coaches met at center court along the sidelines to shake hands when suddenly the two started jawing at one another and as things escalated, game officials and team staff got in between the two. 

In the immediate aftermath, Staley said, “Ask Geno,” when asked about the incident.

The 12-time national champion head coach went on to explain he took issue with Staley not showing up for their pregame handshake at center court during the coach introductions following the starting lineups. However, photographs later confirmed that they shook hands before the game.

“I waited there for like three minutes,” he said after the game. “So, it is what it is.”

Less than 12 hours later, Auriemma 
released an apology
, stating there was “no excuse” for his outburst.

“It’s unlike what I do and what our standard is here at Connecticut,” he said in a statement. “I want to apologize to the staff and the team at South Carolina. It was uncalled for in how I reacted.”

“The story should be how well South Carolina played and I don’t want my actions to detract from that,” Auriemma added. “I’ve had a great relationship with their staff, and I sincerely want to apologize to them.”

Staley and her Gamecocks went on to lose the final to UCLA, 79-51.

The NCAA’s memo also detailed that game clocks are required to display tenths of a second on shot clocks for the 2027 opening round, first and second rounds of the tournament.

That has already been a requirement at regional games and for the Final Four. By 2028, it will be a requirement for each round. 

Additionally, there will no longer be locker room media access or mandatory press conferences between the Sweet 16 and Elite Eight at the regional locations. Pregame interviews with coaches at those events have also been discarded.

The NCAA men’s and women’s tournaments are expanding to 76 teams in 2027. The top 16 seeds will be revealed on March 13, the day before Selection Sunday.

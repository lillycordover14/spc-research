NATO lawmakers: resilience, innovation and industrial preparedness are key to deterrence and defence

 22 November 2026 
 

Berlin – On Sunday, lawmakers at the Annual Session of the NATO Parliamentary Assembly pressed for stronger legislation and more funding to boost resilience, innovation and industrial preparedness as key enablers of effective defence in dangerous geopolitical times.

“The NATO PA recognises resilience as an essential pillar of credible deterrence and defence,” said a draft resolution slated for adoption at the Assembly’s Annual Session in Berlin. “It urges parliaments to take the legislative, oversight, and budgetary measures necessary to strengthen national resilience, address critical vulnerabilities, and promote a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach to preparedness,” added the resolution drafted by Greek lawmaker 
Dimitris Kairidis
.

The urgency of strengthening resilience was a cross-cutting key theme of the three-day session that drew over 270 legislators from NATO and partner countries to the German capital. 

The session is due to wrap up on Monday with a plenary sitting to be attended by NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul and other key speakers. 

“Russia’s war of aggression against Ukraine has demonstrated the importance of integrating military preparedness with societal resilience,” Kairidis said. “Strengthening Allied resilience … contributes to deterrence by reducing adversaries’ ability to achieve objectives through disruption and coercion.”

Pointing to the heightened need to protect assets as adversaries seek to exploit vulnerabilities through cyberattacks, sabotage, political interference, information manipulation, energy coercion and other hybrid methods, the resolution urged parliaments to ensure NATO nations have the funding, oversight and legislative tools to meet the Alliance’s resilience targets.

Discussions on the second day also focused on the role of innovation, ranging from parliaments’ role in fast-tracking defence innovation and mobilising private capital, to integrating laser weapons into the Alliance's force mix. 

US Congressman 
Rick Larsen
 said NATO had to learn the lessons of Ukraine’s fast-paced defence innovation under fire. He urged parliaments to provide funding and appropriate legislation to develop industrial partnerships with Ukraine. 

“Ukraine’s battlefield experience offers lessons on the importance of rapid technological innovation and adoption,” Larsen said in a draft report, adding that deepening defence innovation cooperation with Kyiv was “an important tool to fast-track progress in NATO countries.”

The Assembly highlighted the importance of unlocking capital to sustain the innovative industrial base vital for a “quantum leap in deterrence and defence,” said a draft resolution authored by Luxembourg lawmaker 
Sven Clement
.

He urged parliaments to enable governments to “rapidly issue contracts to non-traditional firms and mobilise private capital for the defence, security, and resilience sector.” Such new players drive a “diverse and resilient defence industrial base … responsive to the rapidly evolving threats and challenges as well as to the changing character of warfare.”

Legislators in Berlin advocated action – including greater public-private cooperation to protect critical undersea infrastructure. They called for legislation to ease critical raw material dependencies, including by boosting cooperation among Allies to strengthen supply chain resilience.

Protecting research and innovation against efforts by hostile players to acquire key technologies was another priority raised in Berlin. 

“To protect the Alliance’s knowledge base, parliamentarians should enhance their national research security policies by providing resources, such as funding to offset the cost of research security activities … implement strong research security policies,” said a report drafted by British member 
Stuart Anderson
. 

Such measures had to strike a balance between security and the openness needed to spur innovation, he concluded.

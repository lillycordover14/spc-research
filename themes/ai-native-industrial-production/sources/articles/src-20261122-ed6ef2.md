[page text]
Ben Shapiro
, the conservative commentator and movie maker, is not happy with today’s Republican Party and quite disappointed with where Donald Trump’s decisions have taken our country. He has not been reluctant to express his views. But at the end of a recent NPR interview, he was asked what advice he had for Democrats and what advice for Republicans. His answer, he said, was the same for both parties: the American people want normal. My guess is he means normal by the standards of the American people, not elected officials who won or survived an earthquake of an election.

Here’s what I think normal looks like: less chaos. We used to call this business climate. Tariffs have a place in modern economies, but during the past two years Trump’s tariff policy has had the stability of a teenager crush. Trade agreements exist to give predictability for businesses, not to be ignored in some moment of anger or bewilderment. Confusion about America’s world leadership, the importance of NATO, and the threat of Russia to Europe and the world contributes to instability internationally. 

Second, the American people want a Congress that goes to work and works for them. 
Rep Jim Jordan
’s apparent belligerent intent as the new minority leader misses the potentially important role that minority members can play in shaping legislation. And 
Rep. Robert Garcia
’s repeated school-ground threat to his oversight targets to “lawyer up” misses several realities. The Trump family was born lawyered up, and does anyone doubt the likelihood that Trump’s pardons prior to leaving office will be broad? The American people want oversight that makes us a better country by improving law, improving transparency, and stopping corruption. Criminal referrals are important in congressional oversight, but Americans don’t want shows, particularly if they seem to be connected with campaign fundraising. I am tired of Rep Garcia’s fundraising requests. 

 

 

Third, how about a list of priorities that warrant oversight and change? Here’s a few. Tens of thousands of Americans are still recovering from natural disasters. Disasters are always in our future. What is the role of FEMA now and what will it be going forward? Can we return to a FEMA that has the confidence of the American people like we did with Arkansas’ James Lee Witt? 

For many areas of the country businesses depend on a strong tourist economy connected to our national parks and public lands. What is happening to the National Park System? Where is the funding going? Why is staffing inadequate? Have funds been diverted to weird projects in Washington, D.C.?

With Democrats now in control, let’s find out the status of medical research, research that improves our health and drives a chunk of our economy. What have we lost and how much can be recovered from DOGE amateur-hour cuts? Have researchers been so intimidated they have been reluctant to talk candidly about the impact of funding cuts and what it means for the research infrastructure and the health of Americans? 

 

 

Why are health insurance rates increasing, and what can be done about it? Americans need financial stability for themselves, their children, and their small businesses. A strong rural America depends on strong rural hospitals with financial predictability. 

Normal governing should not fill every day with breaking news. It is work. I am confident there are many representatives and senators, new and old, many not in the news, feeling their own excitement at the prospect of productivity for America. And in America, productivity is normal. Let’s get to it.

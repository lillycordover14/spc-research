ST. LOUIS, Mo. (First Alert 4) - A controversial plan to detonate unstable bomb-making chemicals in southeast Iowa has been delayed once again, raising continued concerns about potential impacts in Missouri.
The U.S. Army had planned to carry out the controlled detonation Monday after an earlier delay. However, that operation has now been postponed again.
The planned explosion is about 200 miles from the St. Louis metropolitan area. While the distance initially eased concerns locally, those concerns grew over the weekend after Missouri Gov. Mike Kehoe said state agencies would closely monitor air and water conditions for potential impacts.
The detonation is expected to release a plume of potentially toxic, lead-contaminated dust into the air, prompting concerns across parts of Missouri, including the St. Louis area.
For some local residents, the situation brings back painful memories of the fight over radioactive waste contamination near Coldwater Creek in north St. Louis County.
Founders of Just Moms STL, a group that has spent years advocating for families affected by the Coldwater Creek contamination, say they’ve seen more questions and concerns since the governor’s comments.
“I think that that indicates that he’s concerned downwind, dust particles. Just putting Missouri citizens on alert because really this is kind of an unknown situation here,” said Karen Nickel, co-founder of Just Moms STL.
Dawn Chapman, another co-founder of the group, said residents are particularly concerned about how to protect themselves if dust particles reach their homes and neighborhoods.
“The number one concern is how can we protect ourselves from dust particles in the event this does reach their home, their neighborhoods?” Chapman said.
The Army has said a controlled detonation is the safest option, and that safety measures will be in place.
However, Nickel and Chapman say assurances from government officials may not be enough for residents who have spent years fighting for answers about the Coldwater Creek contamination.
They say years of government denials and concerns about the potential health effects have created a deep distrust that continues today.

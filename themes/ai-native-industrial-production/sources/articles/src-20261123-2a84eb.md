PORTAGE, Mich. — 
A Portage food pantry is calling for help after a spike in families seeking food assistance.
Twelve Baskets says the line at its weekly food distribution is more than 10 times longer today than it was during the pandemic.
Its distribution center along Portage Road holds everything needed for a balanced meal, including meat, cheese, bread and vegetables, along with shelves and shelves of boxes and cans.

Even with a supply to feed thousands of people, Executive Director Kathlen Fett says it may not be enough for long.
Fett says Twelve Baskets served around 60 local families a week during the pandemic.
Six years later that number sits around 750, a big increase even compared to earlier in the year.
From losing SNAP benefits to inadequate fixed incomes, she says there are many reasons people need food pantries to get through the week.
"It's just been, you know, public awareness that we're here," she said. "But also, the need in this area just continues to grow."
Twelve Baskets receives some produce through donations from area grocery stores.
Much of their most-needed foods include non-perishables like cereal, soup and pasta sauce, items that can sit on the shelf until they’re needed to meet the increasing demand.
They’re also asking for financial help as a purely donation-based food bank.
Fett says a pallet of green beans might cost $25 at a store.

Through the hunger relief organization Feeding America, they can sometimes buy it for as little as $4, feeding that many more families in need.
"We're just handing out more and more food and meat and obviously our expenses increased too with gas and utilities and that kind of thing," she said."
News Channel 3 asked if Twelve Baskets would be able to provide meals for everyone who requests them this winter.
Fett says donations are currently keeping pace, helped by fundraisers from individuals and Portage companies like Stryker and Pfizer.
But she stresses they can only give what they have to offer.
"Some will use it a couple times. Some people have been coming for years," she said. "So, we're just happy to provide for anyone in need."

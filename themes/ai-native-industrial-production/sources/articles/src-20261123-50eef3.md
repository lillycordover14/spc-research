Photophore 2, a 100-oscillator iOS/AUv3 flock synth twelve years in the making

              Peter Kirn - November 23, 2026            

Add comment

Photophore renders a swarm of one hundred oscillators as a flock navigating a three-dimensional habitat. 
First seen on CDM in 2014
, version 2 of Taika Systems’ instrument adds AUv3 support on iOS — and parameters modeling predators, hunger, and fear. The result is lush, nuanced layers of sound, pads to leads, that feels uniquely alive.

Hold on while I put on my Attenborough voice…

The veldt is swarming with software. An algae bloom of vibe-coded apps has colored the watering hole a dark, murky color, making it impossible to clearly see what swims within. Only the quickest, best-sounding gazelles of software will survive this freshly competitive landscape…

Oh, yes. Photophore 2 is worth your time. The layered pads shimmer and glow. The effect is trippy, hypnotic — not 
quite
 like modulation with other instruments, since you get subtle variations across all 100 oscillators. It really is like hearing a deeper sonic spectrum. As the developer puts it:

“As they drift, they naturally detune and spread across the stereo field, creating rich timbres that are perfect for massive basses, complex pads and swarming leads.”

But don’t take my word for it; check the sound demos. On their site, 
Bop
 has produced some gems demonstrating the full range of sounds. I had some good fun, as well, if in a particular direction; here’s my jam:

New in this version:

AUv3 Audio Unit support, which makes it a natural for the likes of GarageBand, Cubasis, Loopy Pro, AUM, and (on iPad) Logic Pro

iPhone support — and that is really satisfying, enough so that I was working happily on iPhone exclusively

Predators that “hunt” the flock, disturbing tuning and space in lush ways

Touch response with Fear and Recovery (changing the dynamics of how the flock responds when you touch it — think attack and decay on a more conventional effect)

Stereo width (ooh, it’s rich)

Enhanced stereo delay — rebuilt with tape-style changes to delay time

Redesigned UI

Host tempo sync (nice as the arpeggiator is really a lot of fun)

Preset import and import, including Photophore 1 import support

It’s really the ideal sort of app for iOS and AUv3, because you can mess with your fingers with all the gusto of a child — remember the “finger painting” discussions back when the iPhone and iPad were new? It’s a chance to rid yourself of some of the more precious knob twiddling and animate the sound as you hear it in your head — tapping to add a predator and send the oscillators scurrying onscreen with satisfying detuned cloud as a result. But unlike those early apps, you have an AUv3 plug-in you can use to take those spontaneous textures and give them homes in finished tracks, if you choose.

In fact, the current firmware craze just seems the latest iteration of a simple concept: digital audio and software can be whatever you like. The trick is to find the structure that can be engaging — and Photophore is tough to top. It’s about time that touchscreen came to life with models and sounds and didn’t just keep scrolling in the same direction.

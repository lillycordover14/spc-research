The U.S. is investigating Alice Huang and her former company, Megaspeed, Director of National Intelligence 
Jay Clayton
 said, as a report identified one of her associates as a key decision-maker at the Singapore-based company.
Clayton said the investigation was "ongoing" on Sunday, the day before 
Nvidia
 announced it had reinstated 
Jensen Huang
 as CEO, a position he resigned from in part because of his relationship with Alice Huang.
Alice Huang was a key figure in Megaspeed International, a cloud computing firm that operates data centers across Southeast Asia. U.S. intelligence reportedly believes she is a Chinese spy.
"There is an investigation ongoing into her firm and into her," Clayton told ABC on Sunday, adding that, "Nvidia chips, they are the jewel. They are the absolute top that everybody wants, that's why we're focusing on this."
Nvidia's board announced Jensen Huang's return before the opening bell on Monday, saying it had "a series of discussions and meetings" with him.
It added that it received input from senior levels of the United States government" and is confident that his reinstatement "poses no national security risk."

Alice Huang's associate, Chinese national Li Ming, who is now out on bail in Singapore, was "deeply involved" in Megaspeed's activities and took part in major decisions, The Wall Street Journal reported earlier on Saturday, citing people familiar with the matter. 
The Singapore charges against Li concern alleged false representations to Super Micro Computer, a U.S.-based firm with known links to chipmaker Nvidia, though Megaspeed is not named in those charges.
Last week
, the Commerce and Justice departments said that the investigation into Megaspeed began in 2024 over potential export-control violations. Their 
joint statement
 said Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security and Singapore law enforcement interviewed Huang during an on-site check in June 2025. 
The Justice Department later took the lead in the criminal investigation.
Treasury Secretary 
Scott Bessent
 told CBS on Sunday that the administration was seeking answers from Nvidia about its chip sales to China, including who oversaw compliance and which levels of the company knew about the activity.

Separately, The New York Times 
reported Sunday
 that Alice Huang was in contact with an officer of a top Chinese intelligence agency from spring 2025 through fall 2026, citing people briefed on U.S. intelligence reports.
Bloomberg 
reported
 last week that the Justice Department is investigating whether entities linked to Alice Huang helped smuggle Super Micro products to China.
Li 
faces five charges
 in Singapore, including two counts of fraud by false representation, along with allegations involving the operation of a company he controlled, unauthorized bank-account access and another company director's duties. 
Prosecutors allege he falsely told Super Micro that the company would hold and lease the servers it purchased. 
The Journal reported that Li is out on bail and that his case is ongoing. He was offered bail of S$1 million in March 2025. The Singapore charges do not name Megaspeed.
Earlier this month, Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs said that police had found insufficient evidence to establish that Alice Huang committed offenses related to the possible inaccurate declarations under investigation. 
The ministry said she was issued a stern warning for providing information that police assessed as knowingly inaccurate and for downplaying her role at Megaspeed. It also said Singapore had not investigated her for espionage.
Megaspeed has said it is a "legitimate and lawful business" and that Huang left the company in August 2025.
Multiple smoking guns
The scrutiny is set to intensify in Washington. Jensen Huang and the company's general counsel, Tim Teter, have agreed to closed-door, transcribed interviews with three House committees on Dec. 1 and Dec. 2, respectively.

CNBC's Squawk Box
 on Sunday U.S. time, Ryan Fedasiuk, a former advisor for U.S.-China Bilateral Affairs at the State Department, said that there was "still a lot we don't know" about the case.

Ghalibaf: Sustained Deterrence Is the Way Out of Iran’s Neither-War-Nor-Peace Situation

23 November 2026

11:14 PM

Foreign Policy
 
WANA News
 

 

 

WANA (Nov 23) –
 Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of Iran’s Parliament, said that achieving sustained deterrence through strengthening the country’s military, economic, political and social capabilities is the way to counter efforts by its adversaries to impose a prolonged state of neither war nor peace on Iran.

 

Speaking in Tehran on Monday, November 23, Ghalibaf said the continuation of the current situation was not viable and that Iran needed to strengthen its national capabilities to bring it to an end.

 

He stressed that sustained deterrence was not limited to military power, but also depended on economic strength, effective diplomacy, defense capabilities, media capacity and social cohesion.

 

Iran’s military command structure remains intact despite the loss of commanders

Referring to attacks targeting senior Iranian military commanders during recent conflicts, the parliament speaker said Iran’s adversaries believed that eliminating senior commanders would undermine the country’s military and defense capabilities.

 

However, he stressed that Iran’s military command structures were sufficiently established and resilient to ensure that the loss of commanders would not mean a collapse of operational capabilities.

 

Referring to the killing of senior commanders, including Hossein Salami and Ali Tangsiri, Ghalibaf said the institutional and command structures of Iran’s armed forces remained intact, allowing them to continue their operations and maintain command capabilities.

 

He described these established structures as one of the foundations of Iran’s deterrence, emphasizing that the country’s military strength did not depend solely on individuals but rested on a broader network of institutions and established capabilities.

 

Diplomacy and military power as complementary tools of deterrence

Ghalibaf said the ultimate goal of strengthening national power was to remove the threat of war, adding that neither diplomacy alone nor military power by itself could achieve this objective. Both, he said, must be employed together to establish effective deterrence.

 

Referring to developments in Iran’s defense industry, particularly its aerospace sector, he said the sector’s progress demonstrated how wartime experience and defense requirements could translate into domestic technological and industrial capabilities.

 

Assessing recent conflicts, the parliament speaker also said the nature of warfare had changed compared with the Iran-Iraq War. He argued that had Iran entered recent confrontations using the same methods and level of military preparedness as in the past, it would not have been able to withstand the new conditions.

 

Social cohesion among Iran’s main sources of strength

In another part of his remarks, Ghalibaf identified social cohesion as one of Iran’s most important sources of strength in confronting external pressure and stressed the need to preserve and reinforce it.

 

He also called on the media to play a more active role in covering military and diplomatic developments and preventing public pessimism. He said the media should explain Iran’s achievements in the military and diplomatic arenas to the public.

 

Ghalibaf concluded that strengthening national power across different sectors was essential to ending the neither-war-nor-peace situation and preventing Iran’s adversaries from imposing a prolonged war of attrition on the country.

Hadrian’s $300M Pa. Foundry Expansion Boosts US Naval Steel Castings&nbsp;&nbsp;Thomasnet

[page text]
Advanced manufacturing company Hadrian has acquired Kutztown Foundry & Machine in Pennsylvania and plans to invest 
$300 million to expand production
 of naval-grade steel castings used in United States Navy submarine programs.

Modernizing Pennsylvania Foundry Operations

The acquired facility, which has more than 150 years of manufacturing history, will operate as Hadrian’s Factory 6. Planned upgrades include advanced manufacturing software, automation, and expanded production capabilities, with the goal of establishing a Factory of the Future at the site.

The investment is expected to increase domestic output of naval-grade steel castings and support hundreds of manufacturing jobs. These components are essential to submarine construction and other defense applications, but limited domestic capacity and lengthy production lead times have created supply chain constraints.

Supporting Submarine Manufacturing

Factory 6 will support production for the Navy’s Columbia- and Virginia-class submarine programs. The expansion is part of a broader effort to increase domestic component production, ease bottlenecks, and reduce pressure on traditional shipyards.

Hadrian is also expanding submarine-related manufacturing at its 2.25-million-square-foot Factory 4 in Cherokee, Alabama. The facility is designed to produce critical submarine hardware at scale and is backed by $2.4 billion in public and private investment.

Growing Domestic Industrial Capacity

The Kutztown acquisition follows several other expansion milestones for Hadrian, including the opening of Factory 3 in Mesa, Arizona, and a $1.37 billion Series D funding round in August.

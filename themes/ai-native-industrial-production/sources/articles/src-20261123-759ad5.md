Tar Heels say outsiders can’t see their growth, and State game chance to show it&nbsp;&nbsp;Tar Heel Tribune

[page text]
By R.L. Bynum

CHAPEL HILL — Jordan Shipp hadn’t been keeping count.

Told Monday that North Carolina had lost four of its last five games, the senior wide receiver said the number hadn’t registered with him, and he made it clear he didn’t plan to start tracking it now. The Tar Heels close the regular season at noon Saturday against N.C. State at Kenan Stadium, and a win over their in-state rival would make them bowl eligible in Bill Belichick’s second season.

“We just think day by day, week by week, and just one game at a time,” Shipp said Monday at the Kenan Football Center.

The stretch has been hard on a team that felt it was building something through the first half of the season. UNC lost 31–30 in overtime to Syracuse, 38–35 to then-No. 1 Miami and 16–14 to Louisville, with a 38–21 win at UConn the only break in the run. Then came Saturday’s loss at Virginia, the first game this season in which the Tar Heels didn’t look like they belonged on the same field.

Shipp sees the close losses as proof of how near UNC has come to a very different season, not as a reason for frustration. He went through them one by one, including the earlier loss to Notre Dame.

“We’re in all of those big games. We’re a handful of plays away from winning those games,” Shipp said.

Those few plays, he said, are the difference between scrambling for a sixth win in the final week and sitting at eight wins with a bowl trip already secured. Shipp said that kind of swing is what separates this team from the one that went through the 2025 season, when he felt the Tar Heels were rarely in games at all.

“We weren’t in any of those games last year. We were just getting blown out. Just call it what it is,” Shipp said. “We were losing, and the vibe around the building didn’t feel the same.”

The problem, as Shipp and his teammates acknowledge, is that progress without results is hard to see from outside the Kenan Football Center.

Derek McDonald, the graduate linebacker who transferred from Syracuse, said he isn’t tired of talking about growth instead of wins. He knows the program still has to prove it to everyone else.

“I think we see what we can be, and obviously we come up a little bit short in a couple areas,” McDonald said. “But there’s definitely been growth, and obviously we want to show it to the people that aren’t in this program. They don’t see it every day, so we got to get that done.”

McDonald pointed to how thin the margins are in the ACC, where so many of UNC’s games have been decided late. That, he said, is why finishing the season the right way matters as much as the rivalry or the bowl game.

“It’s hard to win in this conference. It’s really competitive,” McDonald said. “A lot of games come down to the last possession. So, I think finishing the season the right way and finishing this game will be super important for us.”

Asked where the program has grown most since this time last year, Coach Bill Belichick didn’t name a position group or a statistic. He focused on how the team goes about its work.

“The culture, the players, the way they prepare, practice, compete, the chemistry of the team, all those things,” Belichick said.

Belichick also didn’t pretend the Virginia loss was close. UNC had nearly 600 yards of offense but failed on two fourth-down tries, missed two makeable field goals, had a touchdown called back and committed six pre-snap penalties on offense. The defense gave up several explosive pass plays.

He said the game still left the Tar Heels something to work with.

“It wasn’t like every play was a bad play out there, but there were too many of them to win,” Belichick said. “When you look past that and some other plays, there are some things to build on. So, it’s like every game, you build on the positives and correct the negatives.”

Shipp said the responsibility for turning close losses into wins starts with the team’s veterans.

“Of course the people outside the facility can’t see it, but the people that are in here, we know that we’re right there. We just got to get over the hump,” Shipp said. “You can’t come in on Sunday saying, ‘My bad, my bad.’ And, of course, that’s got to start with me and start with the leaders and the captains.”

Facing N.C. State adds urgency since no one on UNC’s current roster has beaten the Wolfpack. Belichick admitted that last year’s meeting got away from his team, even if parts of it were competitive, and that he expects more this time.

“We need to do a lot more than we did last year to change the results,” Belichick said.

Shipp said a bowl bid and a rivalry win would add to what he believes the team has carried all season. They don’t change it.

“Bowl eligibility, yeah, rivalry, yeah, that adds on top of it, too,” Shipp said. “But we’re bringing the same motivation that we had from week zero all the way until the last week.”

He also said that the jump from Belichick’s first season to his second is real, even if the record doesn’t show it yet, and he has no doubt about who can take the Tar Heels the rest of the way.

“I don’t think it’s no coincidence that the jump from year one to year two has been so big, and not too many coaches can do that,” Shipp said. “Our record definitely doesn’t show it, but I feel like the brand of football that we’re playing shows we’re trending in the right way. There’s no doubt in my mind that Coach B and every coach we have in this building can help us get over that hump.”

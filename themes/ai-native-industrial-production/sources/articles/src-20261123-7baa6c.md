UNC-Monmouth Preview: Tar Heels Welcome Back Familiar Faces for Final Tune-Up&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
North Carolina (4-1) vs. Monmouth (1-4)
Chapel Hill, N.C. — Dean E. Smith Center
Monday, Nov. 23 at 8 p.m. ET
TV: ACC Network (Anish Shroff, Jason Capel)

Quotables

“I’m excited to welcome King Rice back. I think it’s going to be really great to have King back, such a storied player in this tradition of this great program. So I’m thankful that King is coming back, and hopefully we can play better as we welcome him back on Monday evening.” — 
Michael Malone
 after UNC’s win vs. Marshall

“This one means a lot because I haven’t been down there to play in a long time. Unfortunately, I didn’t go down the five years that my former teammate was the coach. Some hard feelings, my feelings got hurt on a couple of things, so I would not go down there. That wasn’t their fault. That was me being immature. (The current staff) called me this year, and we started talking. And I’m excited about Michael Malone cause I’ve known him a long time. I’m so thankful to the Carolina family for helping me grow up when I was a kid.” — King Rice about the matchup with UNC

Pregame Notes

Last Time Out:
 North Carolina downed Marshall, 88-72, on Friday behind a career-high 23 points from 
Matt Able
. In 
Cameron Fens
‘ first game since being ruled eligible, the 7-footer played 18 minutes, scored nine points and grabbed a team-high nine rebounds. Still, the Tar Heels were outrebounded 41-40.

Monmouth fell to Princeton, 75-64, on Friday, tumbling to 1-4. The Hawks shot 52.6 percent from the field but a season-low 17.6 percent from 3-point range. Guard Ashton Walker led with 27 points and 11 rebounds, though Monmouth’s bench mustered 15 points from three players.

Analyzing the Hawks:
 In King Rice’s 16th season at the helm, the Hawks are off to a shaky start after a winning season in 2025-26. The matchup with UNC will mark the finale of a six-game road trip that included tilts with St. John’s and Wake Forest. Monmouth held its own, falling to the Red Storm by 15 and the Demon Deacons by five, but losses to Rhode Island and Princeton spoiled its difficult stretch.

Sophomore duo Ashton Walker and Stefanos Spartalis were named Preseason All-CAA Honorable Mentions, and the guard-forward tandem is pacing the Hawks thus far. Walker, a La Salle transfer, wears a do-it-all hat for Rice, as shown by his recent outburst against the Tigers and season averages of 13 points and five boards per game. The 6-foot-9 Spartalis, a returnee from a year ago, collects nearly 10 rebounds per game and has recorded 13 or more points in three of Monmouth’s last four contests. 

UNC Wilmington transfer Madison Durr is the Hawks’ leading scorer with 13.2 points per game. The 6-foot-9 guard is a true three-level scorer, shooting 38.1 percent from deep and over 50 percent from the field. But the graduate loves to get downhill with his size, tallying 30 free throws through five games.

By the Numbers:
 Monmouth is the 194th-ranked squad in KenPom. Its defensive efficiency (167th) grades ahead of its offensive efficiency (235th), scoring below 75 points in four of its five games. The Hawks shoot 42.8 percent from the field and 31.5 percent from 3-point range, making 5.8 from deep per game. Projected to finish fifth in the CAA preseason poll, the notable metric sites consider the Hawks a top-200 team. 

Even while recording seven or more steals in two of the last three games, Monmouth still has a negative turnover margin, ranked 128th and 280th, respectively, in offensive and defensive turnover percentage. In addition, according to Bart Torvik, the Hawks’ rebounding numbers are worse than North Carolina’s. Of 365 Division I teams, Monmouth’s rebounding metrics rank 320th on the offensive glass and 216th on the defensive.

The Matchup:
 With Fens in the mix and the Hawks’ aforementioned rebounding woes, the Tar Heels have no excuse but to dominate in that department on Monday. While Walker and Stefanos are proven contributors on the glass, Monmouth has one other player with double-digit boards this season. And in its two prior high-major games combined, Rice’s unit notched 14 offensive boards and was outrebounded by 20.

The Tar Heels’ final mid-major matchup before facing West Virginia on Friday will offer another opportunity to turn stops into fast-break points. Malone hasn’t stopped mentioning that tandem in postgame press conferences, making it a segment to watch for in every outing. But especially against the Hawks, who present one of the weakest offenses UNC will see all season, North Carolina’s improvement should be glaring, though it did have 20 transition points against the Thundering Herd.

Homecoming for Familiar Faces on Monmouth Staff:
 Rice and assistant coaches Brian Reese and Shammond Williams are no strangers to Chapel Hill and the Dean E. Smith Center, returning three decades after their respective playing careers to coach against the Tar Heels on Monday.

Rice, a captain of the 1991 Final Four squad, scored 861 points during his career at North Carolina from 1987-91. Many will remember Reese from his starting role on the 1993 National Championship squad, eclipsing 1,000 points in his stint from 1990-94. And in UNC history, Williams ranks fourth in all-time made 3-pointers, third in 3-point percentage and second in free-throw percentage. He was added to Monmouth’s coaching staff in September. 
Last Meeting:
 North Carolina is 2-0 all-time against the Hawks. The last meeting came in the 2016-17 season, resulting in a 102-74 Tar Heels win. Both of the previous meetings were with Rice as the head coach.

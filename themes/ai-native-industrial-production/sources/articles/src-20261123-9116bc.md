Five-Star Forward CJ Rosser Chooses Home-State Tar Heels, Commits to Carolina&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
CJ Rosser
 is heading home. A native of Rocky Mount, N.C., the class of 2027 forward spent his final two years of high school at Southeastern Prep in Orlando, Fla. On Monday, he announced his commitment to North Carolina, meaning he’ll play for Michael Malone and the Tar Heels a little more than an hour from his hometown.

The 6-foot-10 Rosser, ranked the No. 3 prospect in the country by the Rivals Industry, is UNC’s second five-star commitment within the last month, joining guard/wing 
DeMarcus Henry
. The duo represents the first time Carolina has signed two, top five players in the same class since Roy Williams’ famed 2006 group of Ty Lawson, Brandan Wright, and Wayne Ellington.

“He has unbelievable upside,” said Southeastern Prep head coach David Peavy. “That kid has a chance to be super special. The sky is the limit for him … Just consistently playing at a really high level with a really high motor (is how he can improve). Getting him confident in who he is and being able to just dominate every time he steps on the court. He’s got that kind of ability.”

As a sophomore, Rosser unofficially visited Chapel Hill for the Tar Heels’ win over Miami and received an offer from former head coach Hubert Davis immediately following the game. It was his second unofficial visit to UNC. Prior to Davis’ firing, former assistant Marcus Paige was spearheading Rosser’s recruitment for Carolina.

“Coach Malone is a really good person to talk with; being able to get to know him a little bit more has been good,” Rosser told On3 over the summer. “He comes from an NBA background so he has been there before. He likes that I am really versatile, and that I look to play defense and am a good communicator. The facilities there are great. When I went to a game, everyone was loud and it was a big place, it fits a lot of people.”

Malone, associate head coach Chuck Martin, and assistant coach Sean May closely tracked Rosser with his Team United AAU squad on Nike’s EYBL circuit. They watched him win a gold medal with USA Basketball in Istanbul during the FIBA U17 Men’s World Cup. They took countless trips to Southeastern Prep, hosted him for an unofficial visit in late July, and again for an official visit in early September.

And what they saw was a player whose production was beginning to catch up to his immense potential. On Nike’s EYBL circuit, Rosser averaged 16.6 points (38.3% FG, 17.7% 3pt), 7.6 rebounds, and 2.4 blocks per game. His efficiency improved dramatically for USA Basketball, where he played more of a complementary role to other highly-rated 17U prospects. Rosser started all seven of the United States’ games, averaging 16.4 points (51.3% FG, 36.7% 3pt), 3.7 rebounds, 1.7 assists and 1.0 block per game.

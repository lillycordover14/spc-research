Three things to watch in UNC’s matchup against Monmouth
The Heels have their easiest rebounding battle of the season, but will they come out on top?
by
 
 
Brandon Anderson
Nov 23, 2026, 1:00 PM UTC
UNC has had some interesting non-conference matchups this season, mostly because the mid-majors they’ve been facing have also been able to put up a decent fight against other Power Four opponents. Monmouth is no different — they lost to #13 St. John’s by just 15 points, and only lost to Wake Forest by five. Is it likely that the Heels will still win this game? Yes. Will fans be really loud and irritated if the Heels only win by 10 or so points even though the Hawks didn’t even get smoked by a ranked opponent? Also yes.
But with that out of the way, let’s discuss three keys to tonight’s game against Monmouth.
The rebounding battle has to go in UNC’s favor
It’s likely that a number of you are reading this key to the game thinking, “What else is new? Why is this worth bringing up when this is going to be a problem all season long? The reason is two-fold: 1.) Cameron Fens had a promising outing on the boards against Marshall, and the hope is that once he is fully acclimated to actual games outside of practice, things will smooth out at least a little bit in that department for the Heels. The second thing is that Monmouth is really, really bad on the boards, and it would be a major problem if UNC lost the battle tonight.
Right now Monmouth is averaging 31.43 rebounds per game, which is good enough for 334th in the country 
per NCAA.com
. By comparison, UNC averages 40.20 rebounds per game, which is good enough for 91st. When zeroing in on offensive rebounding, the Hawks rank 349th in the country, averaging 8.2 per game. There’s no reason that the Tar Heels shouldn’t have a huge day on the boards against a team that rebounds this poorly, but in a world where they still lose the battle on the boards, it will probably be time to hit the panic button, because it won’t get any easier than this.
Can Matt Able repeat his excellent performance from the Marshall game?
We have all been waiting for NC State transfer Matt Able to have a 20-point game in Carolina blue, and we finally got it against Marshall. Able finished his night with 23 points, two rebounds, three assists, and one turnover. He shot 57% from the field, and he also shot 55% from three-point territory. It was easily his best game at UNC so far, and now the question is whether or not he can do it again.
Able seems like he’s getting more comfortable with Michael Malone’s system with how he was operating within the offense. We saw a bit more movement from him, he was taking defenders off the dribble, and he even got into the lanes a little bit more. It felt like it was only a matter of time before he started putting everything together, and while he may not score 20+ every game, he has the potential to be a consistent offensive threat for the Tar Heels. Let’s hope that we get more of what we saw against The Herd against Monmouth, because this offense can really hum when him and Terrence Brown are playing well off each other.
Seeking a rebound game from Maximo Adams
One player who had a bad shooting day against Marshall was Maximo Adams, who was only able to score two points off one made field goal, but he did pull down seven rebounds and logged two assists. While he was able to contribute in other aspects of the game, Adams really couldn’t find any rhythm against Marshall, and the hope is that he can bounce back against Monmouth.
One possible culprit for his poor shooting performance could’ve been the nerves of starting for the first time, but the problem is that he also had the same issue against Georgia. It’s been a bizarre stretch because it isn’t as though he’s taking bad shots, but it just seems like they haven’t been going in. Hopefully Malone figures out a way to get him easier looks at the basket to get him in rhythm, because this team is a lot more fun with Adams is cooking.

Obituary

Gloria Christina Zapata of Lubbock passed away on Monday, November 23, 2026, at the age of 47. She was born February 28, 1979, in Lubbock to the late Mario and Toni Zapata. Christina graduated from Lubbock High School in 1997 and went on to attend Texas Tech University.
Christina worked in the finance department at Covenant Medical Center for many years before joining The Salvation Army Church as a Soldier, where she served as a Christmas Administrative Assistant and sound technician for worship services. Her work with The Salvation Army Church was a meaningful opportunity to combine her desire to serve others with her love for the Lord.
Christina had a strong and unwavering faith. She loved Jesus and found great joy in sharing the Word of God with others. Although she was very shy growing up, Christina blossomed over the years, finding “her voice” and becoming confident in expressing her faith. She had a beautiful ability to see the best in people and looked for the goodness in those around her.
In her spare time, Christina enjoyed diamond painting, working puzzles, and getting lost in a good book. She also had a love for musicals, with Grease being her favorite. Above all, Christina treasured her family and the relationships she shared with those she loved. She will be remembered for her kind heart, encouraging spirit, and devotion to her faith.
Those left to cherish her memory include her son, Benjamin; her brother, Mario Zapata and wife Erika of Lubbock; as well as numerous aunts, uncles, extended family members, and friends who loved her dearly.
The family will receive friends from 10:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m. on Friday, November 27, 2026, at Combest Family Memorial Chapel. Visitation will continue until 8:00 p.m. for those unable to attend during the morning hours. Services are scheduled for 2:00 p.m. on Saturday, November 28, 2026, at The Salvation Army, 1111 16th Street, Lubbock. Honorary pallbearers will be: Lee Madrid, Jorge Juarez, Alex Castro, Francisco Ortega, Albert Zapata, JJ Zapata, Rudy Zapata, Jr., and George Zapata.

Hate Week Arrives: Florida Sets Out to Spoil FSU’s Bowl Hopes … Again

By 
Lily Perkins
November 23, 2026
 

Share
 
 

Rivalry week has arrived in Florida, and the Gators have just one final task to close out the regular season: taking down Florida State. 

Coming off a 34-27 win against Vanderbilt
, Florida (7-4, 5-4 SEC) secured their first winning conference record to end the season since 2020.
 With a chance to finish the season 8-4 amid a year of upsets, Sumrall’s first season has been a relatively successful one. His potential eight-win season would put him in line with several successful Florida head coaches, including Urban Meyer, who finished his first season with a slightly better 9-3 record in 2005.

Florida currently sits sixth in the SEC standings, behind Georgia, Texas, Tennessee, Oklahoma and Alabama. While the result is nothing to overlook, Sumrall isn’t ready to celebrate just yet.

“I’m glad that there’s a lot of things that we’ve accomplished. We’ve missed a lot of opportunities as well,” he said Monday. “I probably am such a realist that I don’t really feel real excited about winning seven games. I also understand the good of that too, but I’m not like, ‘Man, we’ve accomplished so much.'”

Sumrall has another opportunity this Friday. Affectionately known among Florida and Florida State fans as “hate week,” the annual contest brings an added level of anticipation to the final stretch of the season, and the Gators can deny the Seminoles bowl eligibility for the second straight season.

With much of Florida’s roster experiencing the matchup for the first time, Sumrall has made an effort to show his players the history between the two programs.

“I think you have to educate your team on what those games mean because it may naturally not mean to them what it means to our fan base,” Sumrall said.

The first-year coach has spent time showing players footage and discussing recent results between the programs. 
Rather than simply using the rivalry as motivation, he wants the team to understand why 
Friday’s matchup
 carries so much weight for Florida fans.

“Anybody that says it’s just another game, I don’t buy into that,” Sumrall said. “This is one that means something to so many people … It is a bragging rights in-state game, which does matter.”

The Gators have won their last two meetings against the Seminoles, both by more than two scores. Florida State, meanwhile, enters the game at 5-6 after parting ways with coach Mike Norvell just three weeks ago. Interim head coach Rick Stockstill will lead the Seminoles into the rivalry game as they look to become bowl eligible and avoid another loss.

Florida’s last loss to Florida State came in 2023
, when the Seminoles defeated the Gators 24-15 in Gainesville. The loss came during the Billy Napier era and ended a disappointing 4-8, 2-6 SEC year for Florida. Following the game, Florida State players celebrated on Florida’s field, taking patches of grass from The Swamp.

Although arguably a part of regular rivalry fare, similar to the flag-planting trend that has taken over rivalry week in recent years, 
the action added fuel to the fire for Florida
 – one that still burns now.
 

Running back Jadan Baugh
, who is just 19 yards shy of becoming the first Florida player to record back-to-back 1,000-yard rushing seasons, could reach the milestone against FSU.
 On Monday, Baugh made his feelings about the rivalry clear. 

“I don’t like them,” he said. “I never have.”

Having beaten the Seminoles in both of his previous meetings, Baugh has no interest in seeing that streak end. Although not on the roster at the time, the memory of FSU’s celebration in Gainesville remains fresh in his mind, and the running back emphasized his goal to make sure the Gators leave Tallahassee with bragging rights in tow. 

For him, that win will be sealed through their approach. 

“We will go out there with more effort,” Baugh said. “We’re going to play harder. We’re going to play faster. We’re going to play more physical. And I refuse to see anything less from my team.”

How a Vail teen went from winning her downhill debut to making the US Ski Team

Mari Renick
 came into her first-year FIS season never having raced a “real” downhill, as she puts it. The 17-year-old ended it by securing World Cup starts in the event for the following winter and 
earning a spot on the U.S. Ski Team roster. 

“I was through the roof — so excited,” the Ski & Snowboard Club Vail product said of her feelings after finishing second in the final NorAm downhill of the year in Aspen on March 19. When Renick got to the bottom that day, her coach radioed down and simply said, “You did it.”

“I was so stoked with how I skied,” Renick said.  

Did she expect such a breakout campaign?

Mari Renick trains with the U.S. Ski Team during its media days last week at Copper Mountain Resort.
Dustin Satloff/U.S. Ski Team

“Not even close,” the Vail Ski & Snowboard Academy senior admitted. “I was expecting to score some NorAm points, maybe get a top 10 — and I just exceeded my goals substantially.”

Renick’s season included representing her country at the FIS Junior World Alpine Ski Championships in Norway; a 
fourth-place finish in Vail at the U.S. national championships super-G
 on March 28; and four NorAm podiums — including a win at Whiteface Mountain on Jan. 4.

“That was my first-ever downhill,” Renick said, prompting an understandable double take from this Vail Daily correspondent.

“Not ever, but in FIS. A ‘real’ downhill race,” Renick continued, clarifying and confirming with a subtle smile. “I had no expectations. So it was good to have my mind completely clear. It was fun.”

Renick scorched the New York course in 1 minute and 13.97 seconds, 0.38 second ahead of Canadian Stefanie Fleckenstein — who is 12 years her senior — and 0.80 second ahead of third-place finisher Katie Quinn. Junior worlds were among the few races Renick wished she could have back last season, making Whiteface Mountain’s selection as the 2027 host welcome news. The globe’s top junior Alpine racers will convene in the Adirondacks on March 2-12, marking the first time in 33 years that the FIS Junior World Ski Championships will be in the United States.

“Thirty-three years ago, I stood on a junior world championships podium in Lake Placid and I’ve never forgotten what that home crowd felt like,” Chip Knight, U.S. Ski & Snowboard Alpine development director, stated in a news release. “Bringing this event back to Whiteface gives our next generation of racers that same chance to compete for a world title in front of family, friends and a community that’s been part of this sport’s history for decades.”

At the 2027 junior worlds, each discipline winner will receive a start at the World Cup Finals a few weeks later in Sun Valley, Idaho. Of course, Renick already has World Cup starts. The question is, will she use them? 

“It’s undecided,” Renick said before adding, “I won’t do all of them. This year I’m focusing on the NorAm Cup, trying to get that title. And then we’ll go through some of the Europa Cup series and then potentially race some World Cups.”

One reason Renick isn’t jumping right into the deep end of the pool is that she’s working back from an injury. On July 30, she fractured her humerus during a giant slalom session in Argentina. The U.S. Ski Team’s November training camp at Copper Mountain was Renick’s first time back on snow in several months. While she put in some good strength sessions in Utah over the summer — Renick said she might be stronger now than she was at the same time last winter — the teenage sensation wants to make sure she’s fully ready to race before going up against the big dogs.

Still, Renick would be lying if she said she wasn’t tempted by the chance to compete at Beaver Creek in December. Asked if she’d like to race on home snow in front of a local crowd on Dec. 11 or 12, she replied, “Absolutely.” Considering her track record in debuts, someone better find Renick a bib. 

But getting to this point required Renick to overcome an initial aversion to the sport. When her family moved from Crested Butte to Vail so her older brother, Mason, could start at the club, Renick — then only 10 — didn’t want to have anything to do with Alpine racing. Up to that point, she’d been a freeskier.

Ski & Snowboard Club Vail’s Mari Renick finished second in the super-G at the Federation of European Ski & Snowboard Associations (FESA) Games in Les Menuires, France, on March 6, 2025.
Ski & Snowboard Club Vail/Courtesy photo

“I just never wanted to ski race, but I got kind of dragged into it,” she said. Renick’s love for the sport didn’t diminish when she started doing gates, though. 

“It grew even bigger,” she said. “Because there was something to reach for, a huge goal I had every season. It was an awesome feeling to always have something to look forward to. And the community around me was so supportive that it made it easy to perform my best.”

Renick first represented her country at the 
FESA Games in 2025
, where she finished second in the super-G. Although she started at SSCV around the age of 10, it wasn’t until she was 14 that Renick’s augmenting aspirations grew to World Cup proportions. 

“I really started following ski racing and it just astonished me how fast they could go and how fearless they were,” Renick explained. “And I just wanted to be like that.”

Mari Renick with her SSCV coach, Lisa Perricone, at the Federation of European Ski & Snowboard Associations (FESA) Games in Les Menuires, France, in 2025.
Ski & Snowboard Club Vail/Courtesy photo

Her idol was and still is another Ski & Snowboard Club Vail skier who first made the U.S. team as a teenager: Lindsey Vonn. Though Vonn is still recovering from injuries sustained at the Milano Cortina Olympic Games, the 
42-year-old was named to the 2026-27 U.S. Ski Team
 roster — right along with Renick.

“She’s just so fearless,” said Renick, who got to talk to Vonn last winter when the two were training at Copper Mountain. “She’s really nice.”

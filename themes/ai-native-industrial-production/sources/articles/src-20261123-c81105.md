Stream / Buy / Download the album on Bandcamp
The music keeps moving. The mind steps aside.
Lausanne-based instrumental quartet What Aleph Said have released their third studio album, ‘Nowness’.
The record grew from those rare moments on stage when conscious thought seems to disappear. The hands continue moving, the music keeps unfolding, yet the musicians briefly feel as though they are observing the performance from somewhere outside themselves.
That interval between action and awareness shapes the entire album.
Contemplative post-rock passages develop through repetition and instinct before acquiring darker progressive movement and post-metal weight. Keyboards introduce greater depth and colour to the band’s established guitar, bass and drums framework, allowing stillness, atmosphere and physical intensity to occupy the same space.
The result is not music designed as an intellectual puzzle. Every transition and dynamic shift matters, but the objective is something more immediate: to capture the instant when projection stops, attention becomes absolute and everything briefly makes sense.
A band named after eternity
Formed in Lausanne, What Aleph Said take their name from the Aleph — an idea of eternity through which distant histories, forgotten worlds and buried fragments might eventually become visible again.
Their instrumental music attempts to transmit traces of those imagined whispers through patient construction, cathartic release and occasional passages of darkness. Without vocals determining the narrative, repetition changes perception, restraint increases tension and the quietest moments often make the eventual weight feel considerably more physical.
The quartet released its debut album, ‘AEONIA’, through Fluttery Records in 2021, followed by ‘Aulasy’ via Sixteentimes Music in 2024. With ‘Nowness’, its musical language becomes simultaneously more atmospheric and heavier, while the introduction of keyboards expands what can happen between those two extremes.
For listeners of God Is an Astronaut, Russian Circles, If These Trees Could Talk, Toundra, Pelican and Cult of Luna, What Aleph Said offer instrumental music built less around technical demonstration than attention, dynamics and collective instinct.

New musical 'Ceilidh' to make Off-Broadway debut in summer 2027
Set at the traditional Scottish dance event of the title, the show will have its NYC premiere at PAC NYC under the direction of Tony winner Sam Pinkleton.
Summary
The new musical Ceilidh will run off Broadway from June 19 to August 8 at the Perelman Performing Arts Center (PAC NYC)
The show is set at a traditional Scottish dance party called a ceilidh, which unfolds with the audience
The show is written by Scott Gilmour and Claire McKenzie and directed by Tony Award winner Sam Pinkleton
Alan Cumming is among the producers
Gillian Russo
November 23, 2026, 05:00
Get ready to dance! The acclaimed new musical 
Ceilidh
 will make its NYC debut next summer at the 
Perelman Performing Arts Center (PAC NYC)
, running from June 19 to August 8 only. Opening night is June 24.

Ceilidh
 (pronounced kay-lee) is a traditional Scottish call-and-response dance party. The musical is set at one of these events, where the audience follows the story of one family across the room — and maybe even gets to participate in the festivities. The show has a book and score by Scottish writers Scott Gilmour and Claire McKenzie, and Tony Award winner Sam Pinkleton (
Oh, Mary!
, 
The Rocky Horror Show
) directs.

The show also has scenic design by Rachel Hauck, costume design by Sarah Laux, lighting design by Isabella Byrd, sound design by Danny Erdberg and Ursula Kwong-Brown, and music supervision by Kris Kukul. Additionally, the producing team includes 
Alan Cumming
 alongside PAC NYC, Grove Entertainment, and Barbara Whitman.

Off-Broadway casting for 
Ceilidh
 has yet to be announced.

“I believe quite fiercely that all theatre should be an engine for joy, but there is no joy quite like the joy of 
Ceilidh
. In my time with this show, I’ve learned that 
Ceilidh
 — whether you can pronounce it or not — is not just a musical, it is oxygen,” Pinkleton said in a statement. “
Ceilidh
 has given me life and thawed me out. I hope it can do the same for even the most frozen of New Yorkers.”

“We began writing 
Ceilidh
 during the pandemic. At the time, our oldest Scottish tradition — gathering folk to dance and have a lovely time together — felt distant, and something we might not experience in the same way again," Gilmour and McKenzie said in a statement. "We also wondered if we’d perhaps had the worst idea for a musical of all time.

"But as the show began to grow [...] it’s been a reminder that, despite all the complications the mad little world we share offers, people do want to be together.”

Ceilidh
 previously ran to acclaim in Glasgow, Scotland, where it made its world premiere, and Baltimore, Maryland, where it made its North American premiere. It is also currently running in London.

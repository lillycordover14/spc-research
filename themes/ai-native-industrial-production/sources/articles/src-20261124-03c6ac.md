Ibiden Stocks Soar 14.98% as AI Substrate Maker Leads Tokyo's Chip Rebound
Less than 1 min read
Ibiden (IBIDF) shares jumped 14.98% to 5,372 yen at the November 24 regular-session close in Tokyo, the best performer in the Nikkei 225, as Japan's first session after Jensen Huang's return revived AI hardware names. Ibiden makes the high-end IC package substrates that sit under AI processors. The Nikkei closed 4.11% higher.
The company has bet big on that demand. Ibiden's board approved about 151 billion yen of new capacity, mainly for AI server substrates, and lifted its full-year operating profit forecast to 133 billion yen from 127 billion yen on October 29. The shares fell 15.2% the next day. First-half sales rose 34.3% to 262.5 billion yen and operating profit 84.6% to 60.1 billion yen; the full-year net profit forecast of 89 billion yen includes about 34.5 billion yen of one-off gains from stock sales.
Strip those gains out and the 2.64 trillion yen market value is roughly 48 times recurring profit. The GuruFocus chart shows a GF Score of 69/100, a middling grade: momentum is top-tier, but profitability and the balance sheet are only average. The close sits about 128% above the GF Value of 2,351 yen, which GuruFocus calls significantly overvalued, so the shares are pricing the 2030 targets of more than 1 trillion yen in sales. Execution on the new lines is the test.

Trump administration officials were internally debating whether the US military should target senior Iranian leaders to remove hard-liners and bring the nine-month Iran war to an end, 
NBC News
 reported on Tuesday.

Citing a US official and a person familiar with the discussions, the report said supporters argue such operations could demonstrate Washington's ability to locate and kill Iranian leaders at will, giving US President Donald Trump an off-ramp to conclude the war.

However, opponents within the administration and military warned that leadership strikes could cause remaining Iranian officials to dig in, damage US credibility in future peace talks and expose American personnel to heightened retaliation.

Qatari premier meets US vice president in Washington, backs diplomatic solution to achieve lasting peace

Qatari Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met with US Vice President JD Vance in Washington and reaffirmed Doha's support for diplomatic efforts to achieve lasting peace across the region, the Foreign Ministry said on Tuesday.

The meeting took place as part of Sheikh Mohammed's visit to the US, during which he also met with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

The Qatari premier reaffirmed Doha's "support for all diplomatic efforts and initiatives seeking to reach a solution that guarantees freedom of maritime navigation and paves the way for a comprehensive agreement that achieves lasting peace in the region," the ministry said in a statement.

Iran says 4th oil tanker hits naval mine, catches fire in Strait of Hormuz

Iran said on Tuesday that a fourth oil tanker exploded and caught fire after striking a naval mine in restricted waters east of the Strait of Hormuz as regional tensions continue to escalate.

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC)’s Navy said the tanker suffered a severe explosion and fire after hitting the mine early Tuesday, according to the semi-official Tasnim news agency.

It came shortly after the navy said two oil tankers also caught fire after striking naval mines east of the waterway.

In a statement carried by state broadcaster IRIB, the navy's public relations department said the two tankers entered “a prohibited area” after switching off their identification and navigation systems.

Both vessels struck naval mines, caught fire and were immobilised, the statement said.

The navy did not identify the tankers or provide details about their crews, possible casualties or the extent of the damage.

It called on tanker owners to coordinate with Iran's armed forces to ensure safe passage through the strategic waterway.

The latest incidents come amid continued disruptions to maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, a vital route for global oil and gas shipments.

Iranian parliament speaker says US seeks ‘no war, no peace’ stalemate with Iran, calls for deterrence

Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammed Bagher Ghalibaf said on Monday that the US wants to impose a "no war, no peace" stalemate on Iran, arguing that "sustainable deterrence" alongside or instead of negotiations is the way to end the threat of conflict.

"Sustainable deterrence means strengthening the country," Ghalibaf said in remarks carried by Iran's semi-official Tasnim news agency.

Diplomacy and war are both instruments for achieving deterrence, he added.

The "neither war nor peace" situation cannot continue, Ghalibaf said, adding that it "must be resolved."

“The question now is how, by whom, and with what actions should we resolve this situation," he added.

Regional tensions have escalated since the US and Israel launched a war against Iran in February. Tehran responded by attacking Israel and US military facilities across the region.

The US and Iran signed a framework agreement in June to end hostilities and began negotiations, which later stalled amid disagreements over how to ensure safe and free navigation through the Strait of Hormuz.

Fighting between the parties has flared intermittently since July amid continuing efforts to end the war.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy says mine blasts set 3 oil tankers ablaze in Strait of Hormuz

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy (IRGC Navy) said late on Monday that three oil tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

It said two of the tankers struck naval mines in the eastern part of the strait and that powerful explosions triggered fires aboard all three vessels.

It said US military air cover had failed to prevent the incidents and that it would release footage of the explosions and fires.

It also warned vessels against taking “unauthorised routes” or ignoring maritime warnings in the strait.

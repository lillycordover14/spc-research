Bengaluru's Antropi Robotics builds autonomous CNC factory that uses robots to produce precision metal parts
          
Bengaluru-based robotics startup Antropi Robotics has launched its first autonomous Computer Numerical Control factory, which uses robotic arms, cameras, and proprietary software to automate the production of precision metal components.

Bengaluru-based robotics startup Antropi Robotics has launched its first autonomous Computer Numerical Control (CNC) factory, which uses robotic arms, cameras, and proprietary software to automate the manufacturing of precision metal components.
The startup is already fulfilling commercial manufacturing orders and aims to reduce its manufacturing and delivery timelines to 24 hours.
About the autonomous CNC factory developed by Antropi Robotics
Antropi Robotics emerged from stealth in October 2026 and announced that its first autonomous microfactory in Bengaluru was operational and accepting manufacturing orders.
In conventional CNC manufacturing facilities, several operations, including production planning, material handling, machine preparation, and quality inspection, require human intervention.

To automate these operations, Antropi Robotics has integrated industrial robotic arms and camera-based inspection systems with its proprietary software called Factory OS.
The software coordinates different stages of manufacturing, from generating machining instructions to managing robotic operations and quality inspection.
Unlike companies that develop and supply industrial robots to manufacturers, Antropi Robotics operates its own manufacturing facility and directly fulfils orders for customised metal components.
How does the autonomous CNC factory work?
Computer Numerical Control (CNC) machining is a manufacturing process that uses computer-controlled machines to cut, drill, and shape raw materials into components of specified dimensions.
At Antropi Robotics, the manufacturing process begins when customers submit their Computer-Aided Design (CAD) files and manufacturing requirements.
The startup's software generates machining instructions that determine the movement of cutting tools during production.
These instructions undergo simulation and engineering approval before being executed by CNC machines.
During production, robotic arms load and unload components from the machines, reducing the need for operators to manually handle materials between machining operations.
The factory also incorporates cameras and automated inspection systems to examine manufactured components.
The inspection data is fed back into the production system to identify deviations and determine whether adjustments are required.
Customers receive the finished components along with inspection reports.

Automation capabilities and current limitations
The manufacturing facility is designed to continue production for extended periods, including overnight, without requiring continuous human supervision.
By automating material handling and coordinating production through a common software system, the facility can reduce the time CNC machines remain idle between operations.
However, the factory is not entirely independent of human intervention.
Engineers are still required to prepare fixtures for new components, approve machining instructions, complete initial machine setups, and address unexpected situations during production.
Once these initial procedures are completed, several subsequent manufacturing operations can be carried out autonomously.
According to co-founder Naman Gupta, most of the startup's US orders are reaching customers in under seven days, including approximately four days of transit.
The company aims to reduce its turnaround time to 24 hours. However, this remains a target rather than its current standard delivery timeline.4

About Antropi Robotics and its founders
Naman Gupta and Shubh Agarwal have been working together on hardware projects since their college days in 2017, when they were involved in developing Mars rovers.
While working on these projects, the duo experienced difficulties in sourcing customised components, which affected their prototyping and product development timelines.
Gupta later spent six months managing his father's CNC machining business, where he gained experience in conventional manufacturing operations and the challenges associated with them.
According to Gupta, introducing software into the existing manufacturing processes helped nearly double the factory's production.
Meanwhile, Agarwal pursued a master's degree in robotics at the University of Pennsylvania.
The duo subsequently established Antropi Robotics to integrate robotics and software into precision manufacturing. Gupta serves as the company's CEO, while Agarwal serves as its CTO.

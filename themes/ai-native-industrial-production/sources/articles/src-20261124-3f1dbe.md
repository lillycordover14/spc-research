Larkin leaves Canadiens debut 'excited' despite loss to Kings&nbsp;&nbsp;Sportsnet.ca

[page text]
Darcy Kuemper made 29 saves and Trevor Moore's goal held up as the winner as the Los Angeles Kings spoiled Dylan Larkin's season debut with a 2-1 win over the Montreal Canadiens.

MONTREAL — Leave it to the Los Angeles Kings to get in the way.

They live to suck excitement out of a game and the energy out of a building, and they succeeded in doing that Monday.

This was Kings hockey at its awful best, employing the antithesis of entertainment—a 1-3-1 system that ideally would’ve been retired when Jacques Lemaire stepped off the New Jersey Devils’ bench for the last time in 2011—to slow-burn their way to a 2-1 win over the 
Montreal Canadiens
.

The Kings were in the way in the neutral zone for 59 minutes of this game before getting in the way of five consecutive Canadiens shot attempts in the final minute of play. 

Down four-on-six, Los Angeles defenceman Brian Dumoulin dove to the ice and blocked 
Dylan Larkin
’s shot at a wide-open net and, in the process, blocked the one story everyone suffering through that painfully boring and seemingly endless game was hoping for—a dramatic tying goal from the newest member of the Canadiens.

At least the Kings didn’t steal away Larkin’s excitement on what he termed “a very emotional night.”

The 30-year-old’s first game as a Canadien came after what had to have felt like the longest 26 days of his life as a Red Wing, and he’d lived through miserable ones through 10 playoff-less campaigns over his 11 years in Detroit. 

Larkin spent these last days away from the franchise that drafted him 15th overall in 2014, holding out for a trade after 824 games with that winged wheel on his chest, watching as the hole in his wallet grew bigger and bigger with each passing day of his team-imposed suspension. And then everything changed dramatically with one phone call.

Larkin’s bags had been packed for some time, but it was only after he got news that he was traded to the Canadiens Sunday for a haul of picks, expiring contracts and one premium prospect in Michael Hage that he could finally grab them and move on from the most forgettable chapter of his hockey career. 

The turn towards the most exciting one was so immediate.

One morning skate, one lengthy press conference later, and one surely wasted afternoon nap later, Larkin stood at centre ice, helmetless in his first warm-up with the CH on his chest, soaking up every cheer that came his way.

He saluted Canadiens fans who chanted, “We got Larkin,” and then he returned for the opening face-off at left wing, with Nick Suzuki and Juraj Slafkovsky.

Larkin then notched a secondary assist on the goal Alex Newhook scored. And after having that late chance stolen away by Dumoulin’s posterior, he talked about what he’d just lived.

“(It) was like nothing I’ve experienced in my career,” Larkin said, and he wasn’t talking about the misery of playing a team that sat with all its players on its own side of centre for most of the night.

“I’m excited leaving the rink tonight because of the way we play,” Larkin added. “Yeah, the Kings played well defensively, and they locked it down in the third period, but the puck possession, the way our D activate, it was a lot of fun, and we get a couple of bounces, and we get a couple greasy ones around the net, I think it’s a different outcome. I know the guys weren’t happy with how the result was and how they felt they played, but for me, not being around it and not being in it, I’m leaving excited about the future.”

He talked for just over seven minutes and, at one point, transitioned from saying “they” to saying “we,” ultimately reinforcing just how comfortable he had appeared in his new environment.

The signs during the game were obvious enough to Martin St. Louis, who had a great view from the Canadiens’ bench.

“I thought he just played the game that’s in front of him with poise and calm and smarts and pace, and I felt like he was in control when he was on the ice on both sides of the puck, offensively and defensively,” St. Louis said. “It’s rare that he wasn’t in the right place based on what the game was asking him to do, so I love that.”

The coach said he also liked Larkin’s engagement, which demonstrated the extent to which the player found proper balance in assuming his place with the Canadiens and just going out and taking it.

Larkin was instructive to linemates Alex Newhook and Alex Texier on face-off plays. He even took control of the most important one—planning it when the Canadiens called timeout with one minute left and then executing what he planned thereafter.

“We have the possibility to take it on either side, and we were talking about that because he lost a couple on the PP on that (left) side,” St. Louis said. “But he pretty much said, ‘No, I got it.’”

This was Larkin’s first game following his first-ever trade to a city he’s only passed through for short stretches, and here he was dictating how to make the pivotal play in extending it.

“We were just talking about the face-off and where I felt I could win it,” Larkin later explained. “Just put Suzy (to the right), stacked him on the inside and had him sweep above the dot to pick it up because I knew (Quinton) Byfield was doing well on face-offs and I knew it would be a 50-50 or some kind of garbage puck around the dot.”

Larkin lunged into Byfield and the Canadiens recovered that 50-50 puck, and then the Kings blocked shot after shot.

They got in the way of almost everything on this night.

Almost.

“I think I had a lot of fun playing with these guys, not really even knowing them and just feeling the way they treated me today and just being around it,” said Larkin.

He was excited, and so were they.

“It was a huge addition,” said Suzuki. “He’s just going to continue to get better with us.”

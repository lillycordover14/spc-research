The Colorado Supreme Court rejected an incarcerated defendant’s petition seeking release for the second time this year, while acknowledging on Monday that another trial judge incorrectly required him to prepay a filing fee for his case.

David J. Gottorff is serving a prison sentence following his 2023 convictions in Ouray County. Although his challenge to his convictions is pending in the state’s Court of Appeals, Gottorff separately filed “
habeas corpus
” petitions with multiple courts seeking his release on various grounds.

In June, the Supreme Court addressed two of 
Gottorff’s petitions from Chaffee County
, where he is incarcerated. The justices upheld the petitions’ dismissal, but they agreed that the two trial judges who handled Gottorff’s cases incorrectly required him to pay a $235 filing fee that is inapplicable to habeas cases.

While the Supreme Court was considering the Chaffee County cases, Gottorff filed another habeas petition in Pitkin County. The court there again ordered Gottorff to pay the $235 fee and issued a notice to various state officials and asked them to acknowledge receipt.

ADVERTISEMENT

The state moved to transfer the case from Pitkin County — which had no apparent connection to Gottorff’s underlying proceedings or his confinement — to El Paso County, where the Colorado Department of Corrections has its headquarters. A judge quickly granted the motion.

Gottorff, representing himself, moved to dismiss the case, arguing the El Paso County court had no jurisdiction to hear his petition. However, the day after the Supreme Court rejected Gottorff’s petitions out of Chaffee County, 
District Court Judge Samuel Evig
 dismissed the petition in El Paso County.

Evig noted that it “does not matter” whether he or a Pitkin County judge handled Gottorff’s petition because “the end result has to be the same. Any court must deny the petition for anyone of several reasons.”

He elaborated that Gottorff had a pending appeal, had already litigated other habeas petitions and had also filed multiple petitions for postconviction relief. Evig dismissed the petition.
ADVERTISEMENT
ADVERTISEMENT

In an unusual unsigned opinion, the Supreme Court largely rejected Gottorff’s appeal again.

First, the court noted that the state defendants had no obligation to respond to the petition because Evig was authorized to dismiss it if Gottorff was clearly not entitled to relief. Further, the initial notice to the defendants from the Pitkin County court was not a directive to respond.

“A notice, after all, is just a notice,” the court wrote 
in the Nov. 23 opinion.

Second, judges in either Pitkin or El Paso counties were authorized to hear Gottorff’s petition and changing the location only required “good cause.”
ADVERTISEMENT
ADVERTISEMENT

Based on “the circumstances of this case — namely, that neither the conduct, the witnesses, nor the parties were located in Pitkin County — we can’t conclude that the Pitkin court abused its discretion by granting defendants’ request to transfer the case to El Paso County, the only proposed venue where any of the parties, conduct, or witnesses were located,” the court wrote.

Finally, the justices agreed that Gottorff was wrongly ordered to pay the $235 filing fee, as they previously found in his Chaffee County cases. The Supreme Court overturned that portion of the orders.

The case is 
Gottorff v. Executive Director of the Colorado Department of Corrections et al.

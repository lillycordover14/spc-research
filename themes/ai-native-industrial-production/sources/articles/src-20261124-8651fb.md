Kinsus Q3 Gross Margin Jumps to 34%, Up 8 Percentage Points QoQ; Capacity Utilization Tops 95%

Kinsus Interconnect Technology (3189.TW) reported at its November 24, 2026 earnings call that third-quarter gross margin reached 34%, up sharply by 8 percentage points from 26% in the second quarter, driven mainly by ABF substrate capacity utilization climbing above 95%, volume shipments of high-margin core products, and a weaker New Taiwan dollar. Management also expects fourth-quarter and 2027 revenue and gross margin to rise quarter by quarter, though at a slower pace than in the first three quarters, with another leg up in gross margin possible when new capacity at K6 Building B comes online in mid-2027.

Cumulative revenue for the first ten months of 2026 reached NT$43.51 billion (approx. $1.3 billion), with significant year-on-year growth. Monthly revenue climbed steadily from NT$3.96 billion (approx. $120.3 million) in January to NT$5.37 billion (approx. $163.1 million) in October, up 49.0% year on year, showing a month-by-month upward trend.

Strong ABF Substrate Demand, AI Applications the Main Driver

In Kinsus's product mix, ABF substrates account for 46.7% of total revenue, the largest product line, mainly used for high-layer-count substrates in AI and HPC; BT substrates account for 38.3%, with subsidiary Pegavision at about 5.5%. By end application, handsets account for 35%, high-performance computing 23%, memory 21%, contact lenses 16%, and base stations 5%.

Management noted that ABF substrate demand is driven mainly by AI and HPC applications, including high-layer-count substrates for CPUs, GPUs, and ASICs. Recent market chatter about an AI bubble has dissipated, reducing short-term uncertainty. In the long term, however, U.S. AI restrictions and anti-AI sentiment could lead to a downward revision in supply-chain valuations after 2028, with U.S. and Chinese supply chains gradually diverging. Management said bluntly: "Most analysts will probably take this opportunity to trim their previously overly optimistic views, because those views really were too optimistic."

On BT substrates, demand growth for memory substrates, expansion of the optical module business, and spillover orders from peers have significantly lifted capacity utilization. Management said BT substrate growth will be lower because there is no completely different product driving the industry, and the company typically takes a conservative approach.

Capacity Layout: K6 Plant Nearing Full Load, New K7 Plant to Take Over in 2029

ABF capacity utilization has reached above 95%, close to full load, with limited room for overtime expansion, making new capacity urgently needed. Kinsus continues to expand high-layer-count ABF substrate capacity. The sixth plant (K6) has four buildings in total; currently only Building A is in mass production, Building B is expected to come online in mid-2027, and Buildings C and D will follow in subsequent years, with the K6 campus expected to be at full load before 2030.

The company announced last month the purchase of land for the seventh plant (K7) in Xinwu, expected to be completed in 2029, to take on capacity demand after 2029, with initial planning for monthly capacity of 150,000 to 200,000 units. Management stressed: "As long as AI demand doesn't stop, we'll keep going."

Three Engines Behind the Gross Margin Jump; Subsequent Gains to Moderate

Management explained that the sharp third-quarter gross margin improvement came from three forces: first, rising capacity utilization, most notably in September; second, volume shipments of core products after hybrid-type material certification was completed, with large volumes of a single part number making actual gross margin better than quoted estimates; and third, a weaker New Taiwan dollar.

Management further explained that core products began production in June, but materials were insufficient at the time amid a severe Tg glass shortage; after material certification was completed in July and August, shipments ramped up quickly. "In manufacturing, we love it when a single part number has a big volume — it generates a much bigger gross margin than estimated at the time of quoting."

Looking ahead, management expects gross margin gains to flatten out and no longer jump by 5 percentage points in a single quarter as in the first three quarters. "If you added 5 percentage points a quarter, you'd quickly exceed 100%, and that probably won't happen." But when new capacity at K6 Building B comes online in mid-2027, utilization will jump again and gross margin could gain another leg. Management suggested using 13% as a safe baseline estimate for the expense ratio.

Customer Investment and Flexibility in Capacity Transfer

NVIDIA invested cash in Kinsus and also invested in several peers, showing customer confidence in AI substrate demand. Management disclosed that other companies are participating in NVIDIA's investment alongside Kinsus. Responding to market concerns about the risk of a single customer's demand declining, management said: "If NVIDIA falters, that means other customers are stepping up, not that AI is over. This is a game of market-share shifts."

Management stressed that if customer demand declines, it will discuss with customers transferring reserved capacity to other customers — a matter of market-share ebb and flow, not the end of AI demand. As AI moves from the learning stage to the application stage, the customer base will naturally rise and fall.

Advanced Packaging Trend Keeps Raising Substrate Value

Management noted that advanced packaging structures will continue to evolve over the next five years, with ABF substrate areas growing larger and core layers thicker, and glass or ceramic core materials possibly introduced after 2029. In the transition period, the company will respond with multi-layer cores and embedded passive components (resistors, capacitors, IPD), increasing process complexity and cost, while substrate ASP and value continue to rise.

"Although substrate ASP keeps increasing, a large part of the value has in fact moved upstream," management said, noting that interposer layer counts and complexity are increasing, with some value shifting upstream, but the remaining demand growth for substrates is still very fast.

Two Major Expansion Challenges: Materials and Equipment Lead Times

Management acknowledged two major challenges in expanding capacity. The first is materials supply, with open questions over whether copper foil, Tg glass, and other materials will be sufficient over the long term. Recently, however, the Tg glass shortage has eased, mainly by certifying Taiwanese suppliers and ramping volumes, persuading customers to switch to E-glass, and Japanese suppliers expanding capacity by 30%. Taiwanese materials have ramped up this year and can be used for mid- to low-end products, relieving pressure on high-end products; Chinese materials are also in progress, but U.S. customers will not use Chinese materials, and under tighter U.S. regulation, Chinese materials are mainly used for mid- to low-end products for non-U.S. customers.

The second is equipment lead times, with key Japanese equipment lead times stretching beyond 18 months. The company has placed orders in advance, even pre-ordering for 2028 and 2029, and dispatches staff to Japan monthly to monitor factories and ensure on-time equipment delivery. "Japanese suppliers won't take the opportunity to raise prices — a promise is a promise — but we're still not at ease, and the boss wants us there watching."

Optimistic Operating Outlook: AI Journey "Only Half Done"

Management is optimistic about the outlook, expecting fourth-quarter 2026 and 2027 revenue and gross margin to rise quarter by quarter. The company stressed that AI development has only reached the "learning and compute build-out" stage, with physical applications such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and factory automation yet to materialize, leaving enormous room for future substrate demand growth. "The AI road is only half traveled; whether it speeds up or slows down later, the demand is there."

On expansion strategy, Kinsus maintains a balanced product mix, not cutting BT because ABF is hot, but building new plants focused on ABF — in contrast to peers concentrating on high-end products. Management said: "Every company has a different strategy; whether concentration or diversification is better is not certain."

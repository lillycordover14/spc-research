Raymond Russell Sr (1939-2026): maverick union figure
			

Jonathan Bell, Chief Obituary Writer

Created: Nov 24, 2026 07:57 AM

Raymond Russell, a former Bermuda Industrial Union member who became a strident critic of the union (File photograph)
A former trade unionist and ex-member of the Progressive Labour Party turned into a gadfly critic of both and spoke up frequently on what he felt were the island’s shortcomings on civil rights.
Raymond Russell Sr, a former hotel worker, characterised himself “a community activist since the age of five” in 2004 at the time of his falling out with the ruling party over financing at the Bermuda Industrial Union, where he was a former hotel shop steward.
Never one to shy from a confrontation, Mr Russell was head bellman at the old Sonesta Beach Hotel 
in 1994 
when he fought for changes to the administration of the Hotel Pension Plan, which he maintained was too difficult for elderly or unwell workers to access.
Mr Russell also proved outspoken in letters to 
The Royal Gazette
, occasionally provoking the ire of BIU officials.
Derrick Burgess, a former BIU president, said that while the two often had cause to disagree, Mr Russell nonetheless “meant well”.
He added: “He was also very kind in raising funds for people who were in need. I can say that for him.”
Raymond Russell Sr (File photograph)
Mr Russell was a champion for hotel workers that he insisted were left unable to afford retirement by the administration of the Hotel Pension Plan.
He told the 
Gazette
 in 1991: “The present administration needs to be scrapped and replaced by people who are more compassionate to the plight of Black hotel workers who must work until they die.”
Mr Russell’s push for changes to the Hotel Pension Plan constitution resulted in staff over age 65 and still working being able to access the plan.
In 1998, he did not back down from taking on his own employer over the reinstatement of a fired worker that he felt was unfairly dismissed.
Mr Russell was put on leave that year, and ultimately cleared of wrongdoing, after getting into a dispute on the Sonesta property.
When the Club Med resort in St George’s made several workers redundant in 1997, Mr Russell took on the Government as well as the hotel over their treatment.
Immigration was another topic where Mr Russell, who served on the island’s immigration board in the late 1990s, felt strongly.
He spoke out frequently over what he viewed as the unfair treatment of guest workers.
Raymond Russell Sr defies the Bermuda Industrial Union in its 2012 stand-off with Kentucky Fried Chicken (File photograph)
In 2005, Mr Russell formed the Civil Rights Coalition of Bermuda, aimed at representing ordinary citizens on topics ranging from immigration to employment.
As chairman of the group, Mr Russell
 declared in 2008
 that the PLP government had lost his vote over what he believed was the unfair treatment of foreign workers.
“We have Bermudians working in other countries, we have Bermudians in schools overseas and I just hope to God that they don’t get treated the same way that we treat some of our guest workers who simply may have a different view of things from ourselves,” he said.
When the BIU launched a boycott of Kentucky Fried Chicken in 2012 in the wake of a dispute over a collective bargaining agreement, Mr Russell was in characteristic form.
He called on the public 
to ignore it
, stating: “When I feel like eating nice chicken, I’ll eat here, and I’m not going to change.”
Newlyweds: Raymond Russell and Dianne on their wedding day in 1963 (File photograph)
Mr Russell married the late Dianne Simons in 1963. She died last year, aged 83.
Their union produced four children: the Reverend Maria Seaman, of Shekinah Worship Ministries, Raymond Russell Jr, Allison Russell and the late Sharon Russell.
Raymond Quinton Russell Sr, an activist and longstanding trade unionist, was born on November 4, 1939. He died in November 2026, aged 87

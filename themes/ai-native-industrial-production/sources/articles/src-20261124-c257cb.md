LAREDO, Tex. (KGNS) - Webb County Sheriff Martin Cuellar said his office needs more personnel and technology to monitor the border and respond to calls.
Cuellar shared his concerns during a Nov. 17 roundtable with Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa and other border sheriffs. He said his priorities include funding for technology and expanding his team.
“A lot of sheriffs were asking about personnel. And they were asking about equipment. And they were asking about, you know, having, having more people, more boots on the ground,” Cuellar said.
Drones and cameras
Cuellar said his office needs drones and fixed cameras in certain locations. The office uses both hidden and visible cameras, Cuellar said.
The sheriff’s office currently has two drones based at its headquarters. Cuellar hopes to expand drone capacity to areas including El Cenizo and Rio Bravo to reduce flight time and improve efficiency.
Staffing needs
Cuellar said the office has cameras and other technology to monitor the community but not enough personnel to do so.
He said the border security conference room could need as many as eight people, though the number could be higher or lower depending on staffing gaps.
Zapata County request
Zapata County Sheriff Ramon Montes, who also attended the roundtable, told KGNS he emphasized the need for additional resources at Falcon Lake.
Montes said the resources are important as border wall construction continues in neighboring areas and portions of the international boundary along the lake remain without physical barriers.
Cuellar said no promises were made to Webb County at the roundtable. He said Hinojosa was there to listen and document the sheriffs’ needs for later review with her staff.
Cuellar invited Hinojosa to visit the county’s command center to see its current capabilities and potential improvements.

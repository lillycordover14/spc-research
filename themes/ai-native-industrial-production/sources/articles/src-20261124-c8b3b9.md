But before going into what we can expect, it's worth quickly revisiting how we got here.
 
WILL THE COLLEGE FOOTBALL PLAYOFF SELECTION COMMITTEE EMBARRASS ITSELF AGAIN WITH NEW RANKINGS?
 
 
Alabama Crimson Tide head coach Kalen DeBoer reacts during the first half against the Tennessee Volunteers at Neyland Stadium in Knoxville, Tenn., on Oct. 17, 2026.
 
(IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect)
 
In week one, the committee's poll consistently overrated SEC teams, with virtually all of them sitting several spots, at least, ahead of where more unbiased, objective metrics-based system placed them. That unsurprising trend continued in week two, and despite the outrage and complaints from fans and analysts about week one's poll, the committee doubled and tripled down.
 
The Indiana Hoosiers dropped five spots, despite sitting out with a bye, and 
Yurachek's explanation
 for it was disastrous at best, and disqualifying and insulting at worst.
"Every team that Indiana had played to date in the Big Ten all lost and that affected their 
strength of schedule rank
 and the committee's review of Indiana," he said. "The quarterback play in general and the offensive play in general is kind of a discussion point of Indiana ... They've won games because of their defense and not their offense."
 
Ignoring everything else, the important note was that Indiana's Big Ten opponents all lost in the same week, hurting their strength of schedule. Then, between week two and week three, the exact same thing happened to the 
Alabama Crimson Tide
. South Carolina lost to an awful Arkansas team by 28 points. Texas A&M lost at home, again. Mississippi State lost its fifth game in a row with yet another defensive disaster class. Kentucky lost its second in a row, as its offense continued to struggle, then LSU lost at home again too.
Yurachek and the committee didn't 
drop the Tide a single spot
. Then when asked about it, gave a thoroughly discrediting answer.
 
"Alabama faced a similar circumstance where five of their SEC opponents all lost, but it didn't have the same impact on their strength of schedule that it had on 
Indiana's strength of schedule
," he said. "Actually, it is very minimal for Alabama because of the quality and the strength of schedule of those five teams that lost."
 
 
There are only a few certainties in life, death, taxes, and the selection committee worshipping at the feet of the Crimson Tide.

The good news for fans who want to see some objectivity? The committee has another opportunity this week, thanks to another rough Saturday for 
the Tide's strength of schedule
, to show that they're willing to be unbiased and not play favorites.
Again, the stated justification for Indiana dropping five spots was that all the Big Ten teams they played lost in the same week. When that exact scenario played out for Alabama, they did not drop at all. This weekend? It got worse.
 
 
The Tide's "signature" win thus far was the Kentucky Wildcats. In conference, they also beat LSU, Texas A&M, 
Vanderbilt
, Mississippi State without Kamario Taylor, and South Carolina. Out of conference, they beat Florida State, East Carolina, and Chattanooga.
Here's how their primary wins did this past weekend:
LSU, lost 27-13 at 
Tennessee
Texas A&M, lost 28-7 at Oklahoma
Kentucky, lost 28-14 at Missouri
South Carolina, lost 34-6 to Georgia
Vanderbilt, lost 34-27 at Florida
Mississippi State, beat Tennessee Tech 34-16
Florida State, lost 28-10 to NC State
HERE'S HOW BAD THE COLLEGE FOOTBALL PLAYOFF'S SELECTION COMMITTEE RANKINGS ACTUALLY ARE
 
Every single team that provided a "big" win for Alabama and played a competitive program lost. Kentucky's fallen off a cliff, losing three straight games and scoring just 34 combined points. South Carolina showed why their ranking was always based on unjustified assumptions of SEC strength. Vanderbilt and Texas A&M continued to be two of the worst Power 4 teams in the country, LSU was LSU, and Tennessee Tech provided the free SEC November bye week, allowing the Bulldogs to end their five-game losing streak.
 
 
With these results, here are the records of Alabama's signature wins this year:
LSU 6-5 (3-5)
Texas A&M 4-7 (1-7)
Kentucky 7-4 (5-4)
South Carolina 6-5 (4-5)
Vanderbilt 4-7 (1-7)
Mississippi State 5-6 (2-6)
Florida State 5-6 (3-5)
The combined records of those teams, including FCS wins, is just 37-40. Meanwhile, Alabama lost to the only two elite teams they played, Georgia and Tennessee.

Is that enough? Is that finally enough for the committee to admit they might have made a mistake by assuming that every SEC team deserved to be ranked because they're in the SEC? And that in doing so, they provide undeserved strength of schedule boosts to teams like Alabama? Do they acknowledge that basing their discussions on "ranked wins" is insane, considering how flawed their rankings are? Or that margin of victory in the SEC is a nonsensical "standard" that begs the question?

None of this is to pick on Alabama. The Tide aren't doing this themselves, they're a very good team that at this point clearly deserves a spot in the CFB Playoff. The issue is that their treatment by the committee exemplifies the ridiculous double standards being employed here, in order to engineer their preferred outcomes.
One of the most aggrieved parties is Tennessee, who beat the Tide on the field, has a similar resume and identical win-loss record, yet sits well behind the Tide.
 
Funnily enough, with all the hand-wringing over Indiana's supposedly "easy" schedule, per 
ESPN's SP+ metric
, which has a "strength of schedule" and "strength of resume" component, Indiana's slate is the 38th toughest in the country? The Georgia Bulldogs? Just 44th. USC's played a harder schedule than Tennessee, yet is buried with little hope of reaching the playoff, even should they beat a top-10 UCLA team on Saturday.

CuspAI delight as board welcomes Prof Susumu Kitagawa, 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry winner
 
 
 
 
 
By 
 
 Mike Scialom
 
 
-
 
mike.scialom@iliffemedia.co.uk
 
 
 
 Published:
 17:12, 24 November 2026 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
CuspAI has welcomed Prof Susumu Kitagawa, winner of the 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, as CuspAI’s latest advisor.
Prof Kitagawa, of Kyoto University, is one of the pioneers of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs): crystalline materials full of tiny, precisely shaped pores that can capture, store and separate gases. He shared the Nobel Prize with Profs Richard Robson and Omar M Yaghi for discoveries that opened up an entirely new field of materials science.
Professor Susumu Kitagawa, who has joined CuspAI's board. Picture: Kyoto University iCeMS
This sits very well with CuspAI, the made-in-Cambridge AI company on Station Road that uses generative AI and molecular simulation to design 
novel materials on demand
.
“For decades, discovering a new material has meant years of patient work in the lab: designing, synthesising, testing, and very often starting again,”said Prof Kitagawa. “That process taught me how vast the space of possible materials really is, and how little of it we have explored. AI is changing that.
“CuspAI’s platform allows us to explore more of that space faster, allowing scientists like me to focus on the most promising candidates from the start. I chose to join CuspAI’s advisory board because I believe this approach can find the materials we urgently need for clean energy, carbon capture and many more important applications. I am delighted to be part of this effort and excited about what we will discover together.”
Prof Kitagawa joins 
Geoffrey Hinton
, Nobel laureate, and Yann LeCun, Turing Award laureate, former chief AI scientist at Meta on the CuspAI board, alongside others including Prof Kristin Persson of UC Berkeley, director of the Materials Project, and Martin van den Brink, former president and CTO of ASML.
 
“We’re delighted,” said 
CuspAI 
in a statement. “When we founded CuspAI, we set out to build a new kind of materials discovery company: one combining frontier AI, world-class scientific expertise and the ability to turn discoveries into real-world impact.
Nobel Prize winner and CuspAI advisor Prof Geoffrey Hinton. Picture: University of Cambridge
“We also wanted to surround ourselves with people who have already changed their fields, and who will expand and challenge our thinking.
“Professor Kitagawa joins Geoffrey Hinton as our second advisor who is a Nobel laureate. Hinton was recognised for the neural networks behind modern AI, Professor Kitagawa for the materials that AI is now helping us discover. Between them, they reflect the two parts CuspAI brings together: AI and materials.”
“Welcome to CuspAI, Professor Kitagawa!”

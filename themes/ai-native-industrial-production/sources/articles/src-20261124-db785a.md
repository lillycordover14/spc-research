Three takeaways from UNC’s blowout win against Monmouth
The Hawks didn’t stand a chance on either side of the ball last night.
by
 
 
Brandon Anderson
Nov 24, 2026, 2:00 PM UTC
Roy Williams used to say that everything looks better when the ball goes into the basket, but the inverse can be true when discussing opponents. Monmouth was cold as ice from the field in the first half of last night’s game, and while they were able to get things going a bit in the second half, it was far too late. UNC was able to pummel the Hawks on their way to an 85-45 win to improve to 5-1.
Carolina fans finally got the blowout win that they have been looking for from this group, but more importantly, the Heels won a rebounding battle against a team that is awful on the boards. It’s fair to split hairs and point out that they only won the battle by six boards, but honestly? You have to take what you can get. Besides, there was a specific reason why it wasn’t better than it was, which we will discuss a bit later.
But for now, let’s discuss three takeaways from last night’s win over the Hawks.
Alex Samodurov earns his first double-double of the season
The biggest surprise from last night’s game was Alex Samodurov, who finished his night with 10 points, 11 rebounds, three assists, and two blocks. He was a huge positive off the bench in his 25 minutes of action, especially after Sayon Keita and Cameron Fens got into foul trouble.
Samodurov had a very efficient night against the Hawks — he shot 50% from the field, and went 2-3 from three-point land. It felt like he was letting the game come to him for the most part, but he also had some impressive moves in the paint at times. For at least one night it was fun seeing the Greek player get into his bag, and it does seem like there’s potential there for him to be able to contribute during the tougher part of the schedule. At this point Michael Malone may need him, because Keita and Fens both had four fouls each, which not only hamstrings the Heels’ ability on the boards, but it creates some problems on offense as well. Perhaps Samodurov is capable of making those issues less glaring.
Terrence Brown running point guard has been fascinating to watch
There was some very real concerns about Terrence Brown being the starting point guard after Neo Avdalas went out with his injury. That isn’t to say that the team had a choice — looking at the options, there was no other way this was realistically going to go — but the expectation was that Brown would be an OK facilitator while also taking and missing a bunch of shots at the rim. But much to my surprise, Brown has actually been really dangerous with the ball in his hands, but he did keep the ball in his hands a lot last night.
Brown finished his night with a team-high 20 points, four rebounds, three assists, two steals, and four turnovers. Coughing up the ball that many times is something that would’ve had fans screaming at Avdalas, but the difference is that Brown was able to get to the rim anytime he wanted. Monmouth didn’t have any answers for Brown as he mercilessly blew by defenders and scored repeatedly. When the Hawks were able to clog the paint, Brown was usually able to kick the ball out, but that was by and large when we’d see him find someone else that could score on the perimeter.
It probably seems like the implication is that Brown is a ball hog, but the truth is that he just knows when he has the upper hand against opposing defenses and will attack relentlessly until they make him stop. He also draws so much attention in the paint that he’s been able to find a wide-open shooter on the perimeter that can knock down the three. That is an element that Avdalas doesn’t have to his game, so it’s a welcome change when looking at UNC’s offense.
UNC’s defensive capabilities were on full display
Monmouth had an awful night in Chapel Hill, and it’s fair to say that a lot of that was because of the Tar Heels. They finished their night only making 26% of their shots from the field, and a dreadful 9% of their three-pointers. UNC’s defense was relentless for the entire 40 minutes of action — as a team, they recorded nine blocks, 10 steals, and scored 17 points off turnovers. They probably would’ve scored more points off turnovers if it weren’t for a little slop at times, but why split hairs when they did everything else so well?
UNC’s defense has honestly been good this season, but the problem has been their ability to rebound. Stopping teams is one thing, but being able to get the ball back and score on the other end has been a whole other thing. Thankfully the Heels had 33 defensive rebounds and limited the Hawks to just six second-chance points. Credit to Michael Malone for getting this team to buy in on the defensive side of the ball, and hopefully it will be enough to take down West Virginia on Friday.

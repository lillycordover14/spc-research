    Redzone inefficiency hinders UNC football against the Cavaliers

    The Tar Heels scored on only four of seven trips in the 41-24 loss against Virginia, leaving them to fall to 5-6.

Sarah Stephens
, 
Senior Writer
                 
             •  
            
November 24, 2026
 

 

River Harlan
 
UNC junior running back Demon June (0) rushes for a first down during the game against Virginia at Scott Stadium on Nov. 21, 2026.
 

CHARLOTTESVILLE, Va. — UNC football moved the ball against Virginia on Saturday. 

Getting into a scoring position wasn’t the problem.

Scoring was. 

Despite gaining 581 yards of offense, the Tar Heels fell 41-24 against the Cavaliers. The 17-point difference was North Carolina’s worst loss of the season. North Carolina reached the red zone seven times, but only scored on four of those trips. Meanwhile, Virginia scored on all of its three trips. 

The difference shows how much the Tar Heels struggled to finish drives, even when the offense found success with moving down the field.  

First-year quarterback Travis Burgess threw for 354 yards — and rushed for another 106 — all in his first career start. 

“There’s something special about him,” graduate offensive lineman Christo Kelly said. “And we just gotta do a better job.”

But none of those numbers reflected the final score.

North Carolina scored on only four of its 11 possessions, compared with UVA’s seven of 11, and converted on just 1 of 4 fourth down attempts. 

Two of UNC’s failed fourth down conversions came in the third quarter while the Tar Heels were trailing 20-10. 

On consecutive possessions, UNC faced fourth-and-2 and gave the ball to sophomore running back Demon June. But neither attempt gained enough yards for a first down. The first ended a 60-yard drive at Virginia’s 15-yard line. The second gave the Cavaliers possession near midfield, leading to yet another Virginia touchdown. 

Those plays added to an ongoing problem: UNC’s offense could move the ball, but whenever they needed just a few more yards, they crumbled. 

Penalties also played a part in the Tar Heels missing second chances. 

In the second quarter, UNC reached Virginia’s 5-yard line and appeared to score. But an illegal shift penalty erased the touchdown. 

A holding penalty followed, pushing UNC further from the end zone. The drive ended with a blocked 29-yard field goal attempt.

The problems persisted in the fourth quarter as North Carolina reached the 13-yard line before a false start, then three incomplete passes and another missed field goal. 

“We had more problems today than we’ve had all year,” head coach Bill Belichick said. 

Virginia responded with an 80-yard touchdown drive. The score was 34-10. 

UNC finished with 11 penalties for 82 yards, all while Virginia committed just three for 40. The Tar Heels recorded 28 first downs compared to Virginia’s 23, and held possession for nearly six minutes longer. 

Still, the Cavaliers were more successful at turning their opportunities into actual points.

North Carolina’s issues were not a matter of being unable to gain. The offense showed it could move, but penalties, missed kicks and failed fourth-down attempts proved the defense did not have the offense’s back. 

However, the conversation on the sidelines was a different story. 

“Don’t worry, I got you,” sophomore defensive lineman Xavier Lewis recalled saying. 

Falling to 5-6, the Tar Heels have one last chance in the regular season. One last chance to become bowl eligible. 

“We’re gonna watch the film and it’s gonna be our best week of practice,” Kelly said.  “We’re gonna be ready to go on Saturday and give it everything that we got.”

It’s not about positioning. It’s about finishing what you start. 

“We’re capable of more than this,” Belichick said. “So hopefully we can do that.”

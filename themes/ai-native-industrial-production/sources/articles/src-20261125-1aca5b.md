CLOBOT Unveils Boston Dynamics Spot Live Demo Room in South Korea, Expands Industrial Robotics Business&nbsp;&nbsp;로봇신문

[page text]

닫기

- Nine autonomous industrial tasks demonstrated, including rough terrain navigation, air leak detection and AI-powered gauge reading

- CLOBOT expects to sell 30 Spot robots in 2026, targeting industrial inspection, security patrol and public safety markets

South Korean robotics service provider CLOBOT Co., led by CEO Chang-gu Kim, is expanding its industrial inspection and security robotics business based on Spot, the quadruped robot developed by Boston Dynamics.

▲ A view of the newly unveiled Spot Live Demo Room on the ground floor of CLOBOT's headquarters in Seongnam, Gyeonggi Province, South Korea. (Photo: The Robot News)

On November 25, CLOBOT unveiled its new “Spot Live Demo Room” to members of the Korean media at its headquarters in Seongnam, Gyeonggi Province, South Korea.

The event was attended by CLOBOT CEO Chang-gu Kim and Jin-seok Choi, director of the company's Field Robotics Division, who presented the company's robotics business strategy, Spot's advanced capabilities, industrial deployment cases and future growth plans.

Unlike conventional robot showrooms that primarily demonstrate mobility and locomotion, CLOBOT's new facility is designed to replicate real-world industrial environments and showcase practical applications in manufacturing plants, power generation facilities and construction sites.

The demonstration highlighted how Spot combines artificial intelligence (AI) with advanced sensing technologies, including thermal imaging, acoustic imaging and AI-powered visual inspection, to detect equipment anomalies and support predictive maintenance.

▲ CLOBOT CEO Chang-gu Kim speaks during the unveiling of the Spot Live Demo Room. (Photo: The Robot News)

Kim emphasized that developing advanced robotic hardware and successfully deploying robots in real-world industrial environments are two distinct challenges. CLOBOT aims to address customers' operational needs by combining world-class robotic hardware with its own software, system integration and service capabilities.

Nine Autonomous Industrial Tasks in Seven Minutes

The Spot Live Demo Room features simulated industrial environments, including rough terrain, a pedestrian crossing, stairs, piping systems, analog pressure gauges, an electric heater, motors and an air compressor.

Starting from its docking station, Spot follows a predefined autonomous route, performs nine inspection and navigation tasks in approximately seven minutes, and returns to the docking station for automatic recharging.

▲ Boston Dynamics' quadruped robot Spot performs nine demonstration tasks at CLOBOT's Spot Live Demo Room. (Photo: The Robot News)

The nine demonstration tasks include:

Rough Terrain Navigation:
 Spot maintains its balance while traversing uneven surfaces consisting of gravel and inclined blocks, demonstrating its ability to operate in environments that are difficult for wheeled robots to access.

Crosswalk Navigation:
 The robot stops before entering a designated crossing area and checks for moving obstacles, such as pedestrians and forklifts, before proceeding.

Visual Alarm Inspection:
 Using AI-powered visual inspection (AIVI), Spot examines elevated visual alarm devices for physical damage or other abnormalities.

Emergency Exit Sign Inspection:
 Spot captures images of emergency exit signs and uses AI to determine whether they are properly illuminated.

Thermal Imaging Inspection:
 A thermal camera measures the temperature of equipment from a distance and can trigger alerts when predefined temperature thresholds are exceeded.

Stair Navigation:
 Spot checks whether the stairs are clear before autonomously ascending them.

▲ Boston Dynamics' quadruped robot Spot demonstrates compressed air leak detection in an industrial piping system. (Photo: The Robot News)

Compressed Air Leak Detection:
 An acoustic imager detects ultrasonic signals generated by compressed air leaks and identifies their precise locations, helping industrial operators reduce energy losses and improve operational safety.

Analog Gauge Reading:
 AI interprets analog pressure gauge readings, converts them into numerical data and provides supporting evidence for the readings.

▲ Boston Dynamics' quadruped robot Spot demonstrates abnormal motor vibration detection. (Photo: The Robot News)

Abnormal Motor Vibration Detection:
 Visual, thermal and acoustic data are combined to identify potential equipment abnormalities, including signs of bearing failure in compressor motors.

Throughout the demonstration, a large display shows Spot's movement, current tasks and inspection results in real time.

The collected data is stored and managed through Orbit, Boston Dynamics' robot fleet management and inspection software, allowing operators to review inspection results and monitor equipment conditions over time.

▲ A large display in the demo room shows the location of a compressed air leak detected by Spot. (Photo: The Robot News)

After completing its mission, Spot autonomously returns to its docking station to recharge.

CLOBOT has also installed four surveillance cameras inside the demonstration facility, enabling its sales representatives to conduct live remote demonstrations for prospective customers without requiring them to visit the company's headquarters.

The facility will serve as a permanent platform for customer demonstrations, technical validation and industrial robotics business development.

Beyond Security Patrol: Predictive Maintenance Takes Center Stage

Commercially launched in 2020, Boston Dynamics' Spot has become one of the most established quadruped robots in real-world industrial applications.

▲ Jin Choi, director of CLOBOT's Field Robotics Division, explains the capabilities of Boston Dynamics' Spot robot. (Photo: The Robot News)

During the media briefing, Choi identified industrial equipment inspection and predictive maintenance as Spot's most significant commercial applications, extending well beyond conventional security and surveillance tasks.

By regularly monitoring equipment temperature, vibration, abnormal sounds and gas leaks, Spot can help industrial operators identify early signs of equipment failure, reduce unplanned downtime and improve operational efficiency.

Spot supports a payload of approximately 14 kilograms and provides around 90 minutes of operation on a single charge. With an IP54-rated enclosure, the robot can autonomously navigate challenging terrain, slopes and stairs.

The robot demonstrated at CLOBOT's facility was equipped with Boston Dynamics' Enterprise Asset Management (EAM) kit, incorporating a thermal pan-tilt-zoom (PTZ) camera, an acoustic imager, LiDAR and an edge computing device.

The thermal PTZ camera enables remote inspection of equipment and detection of abnormal temperatures, while the acoustic imager identifies ultrasonic signals associated with compressed air leaks and abnormal equipment noise.

CLOBOT also integrates third-party sensors, including hazardous gas detection devices, depending on specific customer requirements.

The company conducts on-site technical assessments and recommends appropriate combinations of sensors, software and robotic hardware tailored to each industrial application.

In South Korea, Spot has already been deployed at major industrial and power generation facilities operated by companies such as POSCO, Korea Zinc and Korea Western Power.

Internationally, companies including AB InBev, Michelin and National Grid have adopted Spot for industrial inspection and asset management applications.

Spot's applications are also expanding into public safety and security patrol. CLOBOT has supplied Spot robots to Incheon International Airport and implemented a robotic patrol solution at Ieung Bridge in Sejong City, where the robot can inspect infrastructure and identify people who may require assistance.

In construction and industrial plant environments, Spot can also support 3D scanning, digital twin development, construction progress monitoring, hazardous gas detection and thermal anomaly inspection.

Google Gemini Enhances AI-Powered Visual Inspection

Boston Dynamics has recently enhanced Orbit by integrating Google's Gemini generative AI technology, expanding Spot's AI-powered visual inspection capabilities.

While conventional robotic inspection often requires human operators to manually review captured images and sensor data, AI-powered visual inspection enables automated image analysis based on predefined prompts.

Operators can configure inspection tasks by entering questions such as whether a door is open or closed, what value an analog gauge indicates, whether water has accumulated on the floor, or whether a person has collapsed.

The AI system analyzes images captured by Spot and provides corresponding inspection results.

Recent software enhancements also enable AI-powered video inspection using clips ranging from five to 30 seconds. This allows operators to identify dynamic conditions, such as flashing warning lights or water dripping from pipes, which cannot be reliably assessed using still images alone.

Additional capabilities include 360-degree panoramic inspections to identify unauthorized individuals or misplaced objects, as well as visual vibration monitoring to detect abnormal equipment movement.

Choi explained that although the current technology does not constitute a fully autonomous AI agent capable of independently planning and executing every task, it significantly expands the robot's ability to interpret industrial environments based on predefined inspection missions.

CLOBOT is further enhancing the usability of industrial robotic inspection by offering customized services, including mobile alerts through text messages and KakaoTalk, automated inspection reports and intuitive operational monitoring interfaces.

The company is also exploring ways to standardize these customized services to reduce implementation costs and shorten deployment times for industrial customers.

CLOBOT Expects Record Spot Sales in 2026

CLOBOT is both an authorized reseller and a certified integration partner of Boston Dynamics in South Korea.

According to the company, CLOBOT became the first among Boston Dynamics' global partners to obtain its system integration certification in 2024. The company expects to maintain its certification through 2027.

Beyond hardware distribution, CLOBOT provides comprehensive services covering technical consulting, sensor integration, system installation, on-site deployment, customer training, customized software development and maintenance.

One of its core competencies is robot mission programming, commonly referred to as teaching, which involves configuring navigation routes and defining specific inspection tasks at designated locations within industrial facilities.

Choi stressed that demonstrating a robot on a one-time basis is fundamentally different from deploying it for continuous operation in demanding industrial environments.

CLOBOT's extensive deployment experience and system integration expertise are key competitive advantages in addressing these challenges, he said.

Including the business previously conducted by its subsidiary ROAS, CLOBOT has supplied approximately 100 Spot robots and completed more than 140 related projects involving robot deployment and system development.

The company expects to sell approximately 30 Spot robots in 2026, marking its highest annual sales volume to date.

Notably, most of this year's demand has come from manufacturing companies and public institutions seeking practical robotic solutions rather than from academic or research organizations.

CLOBOT also expects revenue from its Spot business to nearly double in 2026 compared with the previous year, although final results will depend on the timing of revenue recognition for year-end projects.

CEO Kim said the company conservatively estimates the compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of the quadruped robot market at approximately 18 percent.

CLOBOT is targeting growth of more than 20 percent in its Spot business in 2027, aiming to outperform the broader market.

The company plans to further expand Spot deployments across industrial inspection, public safety, security patrol, construction and industrial plant applications.

CLOBOT Expands into Logistics Automation and Physical AI

Founded in 2017 and listed on South Korea's KOSDAQ market in 2024, CLOBOT is a robotics software and service provider specializing in autonomous navigation, multi-robot fleet management and integrated robotic solutions.

Its flagship software products include CHAMELEON, a versatile autonomous navigation solution, and CROMS, a fleet management platform designed to coordinate heterogeneous robots.

The company serves more than 130 customers across service robotics, logistics robotics and field robotics.

CLOBOT recently acquired Doosan Logistics Solutions (DLS), expanding its business into large-scale warehouse and distribution center automation.

The company is also pursuing new opportunities in Physical AI and humanoid robotics. It plans to conduct proof-of-concept (PoC) projects in 2027 involving humanoid robots performing repetitive tasks, such as item picking, in logistics warehouses.

By combining advanced robotic hardware with proprietary software, system integration and application-specific services, CLOBOT aims to accelerate the commercial adoption of robotics across a wide range of industries.

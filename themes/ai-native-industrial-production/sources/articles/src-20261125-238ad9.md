Plus, Derek McDonald reflects on his year as a Tar Heel, a look at Travis Burgess’ preparation and two Tar Heels embrace the Thanksgiving spirit.
 By: 
Brennan Doherty
Bill Belichick
 was asked at his weekly press conference on Monday whether college football rivalries have become a relic of what they used to be in an era where lots of student-athletes play for multiple schools during their careers. 

The UNC head coach agreed that there might be something to that premise. But in answering, Belichick made another point: This is the Triangle, a place with the most unique dynamics in college sports. 

"We're right across the street from Duke. Right across the street from NC State," Belichick said. "It's no mystery how close those schools are and how competitive things are between those schools in every sport. It doesn't matter who you talk to here, whether it's somebody on another team or another coach. It's kind of the same for all of us at North Carolina." 

All that to say: Don't expect a diluted rivalry game when the Tar Heels host NC State at noon on Saturday. In addition to playing for bowl eligibility, UNC is attempting to snap a losing streak in a series it leads 68-41-6. 

"This game, it's a little bit different," Belichick said. "I mean, the game's still the game, but it's a little bit different, and we'll approach it that way." 

And while the Tar Heels, like most college teams, have their fair share of newcomers and freshmen who haven't experienced the NC State-UNC rivalry, junior wide receiver 
Jordan Shipp
 is convinced all his teammates will be ready to go. As was the case before UNC's win at Duke in October, the importance of the matchup with the Wolfpack will be echoed amongst the team this week, Shipp said.  

He noted the Tar Heels have plenty of players who "understand the importance" of rivalries, even if they haven't been in Chapel Hill as long as he has. Shipp even pointed out that he has two teammates, 
Benjamin Hall
 and 
Jelani Thurman
, who have played in the Michigan-Ohio State rivalry. 

"At the end of the day, everyone knows what the last week of college football is," said Shipp, a Charlotte native. "Everybody knows what rivalry week means to everyone." 

Carolina Quotable 

"We're process-oriented. The results are the results, and we all understand that. But if you have a good process, you'll have good results, and if you don't have a good process, I don't think you'll have good results. They may or may not coincide, but our process is the most important thing, and as long as that's good, then we're all confident the results will follow." — Head coach 
Bill Belichick
 

Three and out 

A trio of additional notes from this week in Tar Heel football 

1. McDonald grateful for his year as a Tar Heel 

Derek McDonald's
 Tar Heel experience is proof that you don't have to spend multiple years in a place to have a meaningful experience there. 

"This year has meant a lot to me," said McDonald, who transferred to UNC this year after five years at Syracuse. "My mom went here. This is somewhere I always dreamed of being." 

He said he's honored to be a part of the Tar Heel tradition and has made many friends here. 

The captain is also pleased with how he's developed as a player during his time in Chapel Hill. He's been named the ACC Linebacker of the Week twice, and his 108 tackles lead the conference and are ninth nationally. 

Playing for Belichick and former Pro Bowler 
Jamie Collins
, UNC's inside linebackers coach, has been a big boost to McDonald's game and his leadership. 

"I thought I knew a lot about football, but the knowledge they have for football is unparalleled, especially coach (Belichick)," McDonald said. "And Jamie has so much inside experience from playing linebacker for so long. He taught me a lot of things that I had never really thought about or techniques that I hadn't really used before that have helped me a lot." 

2. Burgess 'handled himself like a vet' 

The stats from 
Travis Burgess'
 first collegiate start last week speak for themselves. With his 460 yards of total offense against Virginia, Burgess became just the fourth quarterback this season to throw for 300-plus yards and run for 100-plus yards in a game. 

Beyond the numbers, Burgess received a compliment from 
Jordan Shipp
 that spoke to the high level of his performance. 

"It felt like it was just another week out there," Shipp said. "Much respect for Trav and everything he's done to get to this point in preparation. He carries himself well." 

Shipp added that Burgess "handled himself like he was a vet," mentioning his poise in the pocket and the huddle. 

While Burgess replaced the injured 
Billy Edwards Jr.
 the week prior against Louisville, Belichick said it's a different process for a quarterback throughout the week when he knows there's a good chance he could be starting. 

The Tar Heel head coach thought his young quarterback prepared well for the most part, though Belichick noted there's always room for growth — especially after doing something for the first time. 

"Overall, I thought he did a pretty good job of working through the week because it's a process," Belichick said. "We don't put in the whole game plan on Tuesday. It's early downs, then we go to third down, then we go to two-minute (offense), red area, four-minute (offense), other situational plays. It all kind of piles up day after day." 

3. Getting in on the Thanksgiving spirit 

The same day that 
Jelani Thurman
 
learned he was a finalist for the Mackey Award
, the Tar Heel tight end had other business to tend to. Matching his jersey number, No. 45 
gave out 45 turkeys
 to the local Orange County community on Tuesday. Thurman's number is a nod to his late mother Kara Braxton, a two-time WNBA champion who died earlier this year. 

"Carolina fed into me a lot this year, so I would like to give back," said Thurman, who has eight touchdowns this season, tied for the most in a season by a Tar Heel tight end. His 51 catches and 610 receiving yards are each the sixth most among tight ends nationally. 

Running back 
Demon June
, who leads UNC with eight rushing TDs, also gave back to his local Onslow County community by 
giving away 100-plus turkeys and 200-plus canned goods
 in his local Jacksonville, North Carolina.

Laminar Open-Sources Reinforcement Learning Framework for LLMs
Researchers from The University of Hong Kong and ByteDance Seed released the Apache-2.0 licensed framework for post-training large language models and long-horizon agents.

Researchers from The University of Hong Kong and ByteDance Seed have open-sourced Laminar, a reinforcement learning post-training framework for large language models and long-horizon agents. The framework is built on verl and Uni-Agent and is released under the Apache-2.0 license.
Why it matters
Laminar decouples rollout, buffering, training and weight transfer into independent loops, a design the researchers say improves efficiency in training AI systems. Its reported gains on a coding benchmark suggest the approach could lower the cost of improving AI models for software tasks.
Laminar was tested on 64 H800 GPUs and up to 1,024 H800 GPUs, with its inference backend limited to vLLM. The team reports the benchmark gain and efficiency figures, but how the framework performs on other hardware or inference backends has not been reported.
The players
The University of Hong Kong
A public research university in Hong Kong with a large computer science and artificial intelligence research community.
ByteDance Seed
The research arm of ByteDance, the Chinese technology company that operates TikTok and Douyin, focused on foundational AI models.
The details
Laminar's Weight Relay stages model weights into per-node shared CPU memory, and replicas pull updates on their own schedule. A version-consistent rollout mode finishes each trajectory under one policy version, and faulty replicas are fenced, restarted and resumed from their generated token prefix.
Timeline
November 24, 2026: Laminar is open-sourced, according to a MarkTechPost report.
Tech Race
Laminar extends the verl reinforcement learning framework by separating rollout, buffering, training and weight transfer into independent loops. The open-source release adds to a field where teams are competing to make post-training of large language models cheaper and faster.
For developers and researchers building AI coding agents, Laminar offers an open-source option for reinforcement learning post-training under an Apache-2.0 license. Its efficiency claims could affect how much compute is needed to improve a model's software-engineering performance.
The takeaway
Efficiency gains in training pipelines can matter as much as model architecture, since they determine how many experiments a team can afford to run. Readers following AI tooling can watch whether Laminar's reported results hold up under independent testing.

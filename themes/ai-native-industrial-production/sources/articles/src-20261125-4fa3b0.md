In the preview pages, we see that J. Jonah Jameson is in trouble with the maniac known as Itsy-Bitsy, who is obsessed with Spider-Man, and did not appreciate the fact that Jameson spoke out against all superheroes following the Russian disaster. You see, Jameson pointedly didn't just speak out against superheroes in GENERAL, but he even had to admit that Spider-Man deserved to be lumped in with the rest of them (this is tough for Jameson to admit since he now knows Peter Parker is Spider-Man, and he has become a friend to Spider-Man). So Itsy-Bitsy has kidnapped him, and plans to torture him while live-streaming it on social media.
 
So Spider-Man assembles his new Avengers team to help Jameson, and that is where Spider-Man sees his first problem with his new crew. It is not that they don't think that it is important to rescue Jameson, they just don't see it as being an AVENGERS-level threat. This is an important thing about why Spider-Man has rarely been an Avenger over the years, as he has always felt that the Avengers were operating on a much higher level than him, in terms of the types of crimes that they specialize in.

Spider-Man is a street level superhero, and the Avengers have generally NOT been a street level superhero team (although there have obviously been exceptions over the years). So the other heroes are actually kind of irked at Spider-Man for, in effect, wasting their time. 
 
There is also a classic bit that is already going viral between Daredevil and Captain Marvel where he notes he can hear her rolling her eyes at him, and she tells him that she can hear the STD he has. Hilarious. 
 
If you want to find out if the Avengers will still come through for Spidey, pick up a copy of
 Amazing Spider-Man 
#1004 this week!

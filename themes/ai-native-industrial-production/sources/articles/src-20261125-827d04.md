The UConn women's basketball team is off to one of its roughest starts in nearly 40 years after a 65-56 loss to No. 1 South Carolina dropped the Huskies to 4-3. But despite the early struggles, fans and a mental performance expert believe the team still has plenty of potential.

The loss to South Carolina marks UConn's third defeat against a top-ranked opponent this season, following losses to Louisville and Michigan. The challenging schedule has left head coach Geno Auriemma frustrated and searching for answers.

“It’s incredibly frustrating and just at a loss, I really am; usually I have a pretty good idea, but…” Auriemma said during a postgame press conference.

“I am having a really hard time coaching this team and I gotta figure it out and it would be nice if I got a little help,” said the coach.

Auriemma also acknowledged the difficulty of the team's early schedule.

“no one has ever played a schedule like this in the history of the NCAA, so maybe I overstated what kind of team we had.”

Despite the frustration surrounding the program, some UConn fans in Storrs aren't ready to give up on the season.

“I don’t think it’s a lost cause at all but how do we move forward in this next generation while keeping history of the stars, that drive alive,” fan Esme Allopenna said.

Others believe Auriemma could take a different approach to help his players navigate the early challenges.

“It’s a learning curve, I feel like Geno can do more to lift the confidence of the team instead of tear them down,” one student said.

While fans recognize that UConn isn't dominating opponents the way it once did, some say there's still plenty of time for the team to improve.

“It’s just the beginning of the season so I am excited about what they do next,” another student said.

Why UConn shouldn't panic

Andrew Toce, a certified mental performance consultant, believes the Huskies' early adversity could ultimately help them develop into a stronger team.

“I don’t think it’s time to hit the panic button at all,” Toce said.

Toce believes Auriemma's demanding schedule and tough coaching style could be part of a larger strategy to prepare the team for championship-level competition.

“We have come to know Geno as someone who doesn’t do anything without an intention behind it so he definitely, I think there is a method to the madness he is sharing.”

Toce also pointed to the team's youth, noting that many of the players are underclassmen who are still developing their identities and learning how to compete together.

“This team is still in the forming and storming stage, where they are figuring out who they are before they start norming and performing.”

For Auriemma and the Huskies, there isn't much time to dwell on the latest setback. Another ranked opponent, Duke, is next on the schedule.

“We have a game Saturday, so we don’t have a lot of time to wallow in it, we have to go to practice and we have to get ready for Saturday,” Auriemma said.

With a difficult stretch of games already behind them and more challenges ahead, the Huskies will have to find a way to turn their early-season struggles into progress.

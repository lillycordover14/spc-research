Boston Dynamics and GlobalFoundries take humanoid robots into semiconductor manufacturing

Bill Tanner
 | 
25 November, 2026

Boston Dynamics is taking humanoid robotics into semiconductor manufacturing through an expanded partnership with GlobalFoundries that will accelerate the deployment of autonomous inspection robots across three continents.

GlobalFoundries will become the first semiconductor manufacturer and the first customer outside the automotive industry to test Boston Dynamics’ Atlas humanoid under its early adopter program.

Testing will begin in early 2027, focusing on identifying manufacturing applications, assessing integration requirements and training robots to execute specific tasks.

Meanwhile, GlobalFoundries will expand deployments of Boston Dynamics’ four-legged Spot robot across semiconductor manufacturing facilities in Malta, New York; Burlington, Vermont; Dresden, Germany; and Singapore.

The robots will undertake autonomous asset inspections, collecting operational data to identify potential equipment failures before they disrupt production.

GlobalFoundries has deployed Spot in Burlington for more than four years. In Singapore, two robots have travelled nearly 900 kilometers and completed more than 90,000 inspections in areas beneath semiconductor production facilities since April, achieving a 99% mission success rate.

The manufacturer also redeployed 875 routine inspections within two weeks using upgraded hardware and software, with plans to more than double that figure through advanced visual inspection capabilities.

“Spot has helped us shift from reactive to predictive maintenance, collecting data across thousands of inspection points to prevent downtime and keep our fabs running more efficiently,” said Pradip Singh, Chief Manufacturing Officer, GlobalFoundries.

The Atlas trials will complement an initiative to train GlobalFoundries employees in AI, extending the company’s automation strategy beyond autonomous inspections into physical AI and more complex manufacturing tasks.

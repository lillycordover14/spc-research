Tallon from Castle Rock, CO
There are good players, there are great players, and then there are freak athletes. We have talked about how 
Jordan Love
 has carried this team, as an incredible player playing at the most valuable position on offense. The defense has been great, but we have been missing our superstar, one of the best defensive players on the planet. He's back. Enjoy the show.
As the great Jim Ross once said, business is about to pick up.
Jonathan from Osceola Mills, PA
With the extended rest for the Rams game and the following game against the Saints, should we see a more energized team or expect some rust?
I would hope to see the Packers' best form. Green Bay is as healthy as it's probably going to be the rest of the year and getting its big dawg back in 
Micah Parsons
. Make it count.
Michael from Las Vegas, NV
For Week 12, Thanksgiving Eve, will/can the Pack protect Jordan Love from the Rams' defense? How will the Pack win the turnover battle? And/or shut down Kyren Williams and Blake Corum?
Protecting Jordan Love is a must. If Love stays upright, the Packers have a great chance of winning this game. It would be great to finally get a look at 
Kevin Zeitler
 at right guard and see if he can settle the right side like 
Laken Tomlinson
 settled the left. This also would be an ideal time to get 
Zaire Franklin
 back in the middle. This game will be won in the trenches, and the Packers need an answer for the Rams' multifaceted ground game and their army of tight ends.
Asher from Las Vegas, NV
Hello Mike/Wes, I heard that Puka Nacua, Davante Adams, and Terrance Ferguson are all out and not playing but they were not listed in the Rams' injury report that was on the Packers app. Is it true? And if it is, shouldn't we be focusing on stopping the run game? GPG
All three are ruled out. The Rams' injury report came out later because they practiced later on the West Coast. The Packers still gotta respect Tutu Atwell and L.A.'s wideouts, but stopping Williams and Corum is the primary objective. Those two can hurt you both on the ground and through the air. So yeah, it probably would behoove Green Bay to drop a safety in the box tonight and let the corners work their sides.
Mike from Oklahoma City, OK
Gentlemen, regardless of how Micah Parsons finishes the season, how fortunate we are to have such a decent player? His comments about 
his long recuperation and return were uplifting
.
I think so much of the noise surrounding Parsons is misconception. The guy wants to be great, and he wants his team to be great. When you consistently perform at an All-Pro level, you want to be paid like it. I'm not nearly that valuable, but I remember a time in my own career when I learned I was well underpaid relative to my coworker and you know what? I wasn't very happy about it. Thankfully, there were no cameras on me marching into my boss's office. People can believe whatever narrative they want but know this: Micah Parsons is valued inside this building, both the player and the person.
Caleb from Knoxville, TN
As Micah (hopefully) returns against the Rams, what to you would constitute a successful debut for him?
One key pressure at a critical time. That's it.
Paul from Glen Rock, N.J.
I'm truly looking forward to Micah's return on Wednesday night. It should add yet another dimension to an already excellent defense. But that doesn't help some of the other problems the Pack has been facing, and most of them still boil down to the O-line. Both the running game and Love's protection are still major concerns. Hopefully getting people back off IR will make a difference, as well. Every game is a mountain from here on in.
Sean Rhyan
's importance at center also cannot be overstated. He's been Green Bay's most consistent and durable offensive lineman in addition to serving as the key communicator up front. Rhyan's injury, and the immediate loss of backup 
Jacob Monk
, was the worst-case scenario for Green Bay. Credit 
John Williams
 for playing out of position and aiding a couple wins, but the Packers getting their starting center back would be a huge deal.

Will we ever see a ref throwing a hanky because Micah Parsons is being held, simply because it happens EVERY SINGLE TIME?
Those odds are about as good as Jordan Love drawing a roughing the passer penalty for the second straight week.
Ryan from Sun Prairie, WI
Is the Rams game a must-win for the Packers to get into the playoffs?
No, but it sure would help.
Paul from Winnebago, IL
We are still very much in the playoff race, but is the Rams game a turning point game, of sorts?
This game is about the Packers deciding what team they want to be this season. This was the Super Bowl favorite in the NFC at the beginning of the year and most of those defensive pieces are in place.
Ken from Boynton Beach, FL
If the magic number of wins to make a wild-card spot in the NFC is 11, the Packers will need to win five of the last seven games... Which five in your opinion do they win? Thanks for all the cool readings and writings here and Happy Thanksgiving to you both Mike and Wes.
A win over the Rams helps, but there is still so much football left to be played. A win creates breathing room, but Green Bay still needs to take care of business against New Orleans and Miami. Beyond that, I think the Packers need to win three of four against Buffalo, Chicago, Houston and Detroit to feel good about its playoff chances. Blink one time too many and then you're asking for help.
Kevin from Indianapolis, IN
I remember the buzz around the Matt LaFleur hiring was that he is an innovative offensive mind similar to Kyle Shanahan and Sean McVay. The fact that I am just a fan and not a professional notwithstanding, I haven't seen much innovation lately in the same way that I do when I watch the 49ers and the Rams. What am I not seeing that proves me wrong?
I don't think your point could be any more misguided. If you need an obvious example, the Packers ran a pitch pass against Minnesota where 
Tucker Kraft
 went under center and tossed the ball to Jordan Love, who then completed a pass for a first down. There are plenty of unseen examples of what the Packers do to throw a defense off-balance, as well. Creativity isn't the problem. What the Packers must do better is the boring, fundamental stuff â blocking, finding gaps and playing well situationally.
Ray from Phoenix, AZ
I would like to see Brian Gutekunst and Russ Ball figure a way to extend 
Lukas Van Ness
 right now. He is having a great year without Parsons. As Parsons gets back to more plays, Van Ness should have an even greater impact on the success of the defense. He could then command an even larger price tag and cap hit at the end of his contract. Do you agree with my thinking?
Van Ness is off to a great start and I'm excited to see what he can do alongside Parsons, but he's now signed through 2027 after the team exercised his fifth-year option. Green Bay still could extend him now or after the season, but it may have more pressing needs at the moment. We shall see.
Chris from Portland, OR
Sometimes I feel like we don't run enough quick passing plays. With the way our line is playing, I feel we should be focusing more on short passes to beat the blitz. Is this something we should be doing more or am I missing something?
Not at all. The Packers must unlock the screen game, an area in which they've been so proficient over the years. A lot went wrong for Minnesota in Mexico City, but there was a play where the Vikings dialed up a third-and-7 screen for Aaron Jones and asked their running back to make a play. He did and the drive kept moving. Scheme is fun. It can get you on SportsCenter's top plays. But simple execution is what makes the football world go 'round.
Matt from Kula, HI
Perhaps the 49ers have unlocked the way to attack the Vikings' defense. It will be interesting to see if other teams can emulate this without SF's offensive personnel. I saw an OL that worked as a cohesive unit and passing scheme that quickly found the areas that the Vikings left exposed by their blitzes. What did you see the 49ers do that the Packers and other teams failed to do?
Made the Vikings pay for their aggression just like Detroit did, albeit the Lions did it mainly through Jahmyr Gibbs. Minnesota's front can be challenging when it mugs up seven or eight on the line of scrimmage but also puts defenders at a brief disadvantage of needing to get back in position once the ball is snapped. The 49ers' offensive line held its ground against pressure and Brock Purdy went to work against scrambling Vikings. San Fran had a solution to every problem Minnesota presented, particularly in the middle of the field.
Nhoj from Waterloo, WI
I would add to Mike's closing words that the last wild-card team in the NFC most likely holds the tiebreaker over another 11-win team to get in. So just beat the Rams. And I'm rooting against Chicago on Thursday out of pure disdain. Interested to hear others' thoughts on that game.
It is an interesting paradox because I feel like the Lions are the more dangerous team and wouldn't be too interested in facing that offense again at Ford Field. A Bears win could help knock Detroit down a peg. Conversely, a Detroit win sets up a tough final stretch for Chicago. So, I guess I don't know how to feel about it.

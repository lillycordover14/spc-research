Here are 7 prospects who figure to benefit from recent Hot Stove moves&nbsp;&nbsp;MLB.com

[page text]
The wheels of the offseason have continued to turn in recent days and weeks with the Rule 5 protection deadline (an especially notable one on the calendar for prospects), the non-tender deadline and -- in the biggest revelation of all -- the Tarik Skubal agreement with the Mets. The latter could add some grease to the transactions page for the next week, but even as it stands now, we’ve already seen some moves that will affect prospects and how we view them for 2027.

For this exercise, we’re going to set aside players added to 40-man rosters last week. That’s obviously a big step in their careers, and that new roster status makes it all the easier to call them up for an MLB debut at some point in 2027. We’re also not going to list any Mets farmhands who could be affected by the Skubal addition. It’s widely acknowledged across the industry that New York needed starting pitching help, and their biggest arms on the farm (specifically Jonathan Santucci) could use more seasoning before they’re seriously considered for an MLB rotation spot.

But in general, MLB roster moves are not isolated transactions. They have downstream effects that will alter how we view the club’s prospects, specifically when it comes to potential playing time in The Show in 2027. Here are a few notable prospects who look like they’ll be affected by recent moves by their parent clubs:

Rainiel Rodriguez, C/1B, Cardinals (
STL No. 1
/
MLB No. 10
)

Rodriguez already had one of the most productive 2026 seasons of any Minor League hitter with a .324/.413/.574 line and 27 homers across 115 games at High-A and Double-A in his age-19 season. It’s why there’s a lot of enthusiasm around the right-handed slugger’s profile both in St. Louis and beyond. 

As Rodriguez closes in on a potential 2027 debut, the Cardinals have been open about their willingness to move him beyond catcher (where he started 66 games this year) and first base (24), specifically with some potential time at third base. On Friday, the organization non-tendered Nolan Gorman, who moved back to the hot corner in 2025 after previous dalliances at second base. The Cards still have Thomas Saggese and Blaze Jordan as third-base options going into ‘27, and no one is suggesting a 20-year-old Rodriguez would be ready to compete for an Opening Day spot at a position where he has zero professional experience. But with the Cardinals having a much deeper logjam at catcher right now (including fellow prospect Leo Bernal), removing a hurdle from Rodriguez’s path to the bigs at another position is notable.

Ryan Sloan, RHP, Mariners (
SEA No. 2
/
MLB No. 11
)

Seattle dipped into its enviable rotation depth by sending Bryce Miller to Kansas City as part of the return for Maikel Garcia last week. That sets up Logan Gilbert, Bryan Woo, George Kirby, Kade Anderson and Emerson Hancock as the rotation going into 2027. The next man up? That looks like it’ll be Sloan, who turns only 21 in January. 

The 6-foot-5 righty spent all of 2026 at Double-A Arkansas and finished with a 3.73 ERA, 128 strikeouts and only 16 walks in 101 ⅓ innings; his 27.1 K-BB% was second-best in the Minors (min. 100 IP). Sloan certainly has the arsenal to help immediately with a 95-98 mph four-seamer and sinker (touching 100), a big mid-80s sweeping slider, an 89-91 mph kick changeup and a low-90s cutter, and a big reason he’s able to keep the walks so low is the way he elicits batters to chase outside of the zone with his late-moving stuff. 

It wouldn’t shock if he heads back to the Texas League to begin ‘27, only because the Mariners don’t love sending top pitching prospects to the hitter-friendly Pacific Coast League, but Sloan, like Anderson before him, could jump two levels whenever that rotation depth needs additional reinforcement next year.

Walker Jenkins, OF, Twins (
MIN No. 1
/
MLB No. 13
)

Minnesota was active last Friday, and among its moves were a pair of non-tendered outfielders in Trevor Larnach and Matt Wallner. The former was a little more of a surprise considering he had a 123 wRC+ over 433 plate appearances, but after finishing the year on the IL due to wrist surgery, the Twins decided to cut the 29-year-old, knowing they were loaded with other options on the grass. 

Among them is Jenkins, who now officially sits in the driver’s seat for a starting spot on Opening Day. The 2023 fifth overall pick debuted on Aug. 28 and got hot late in the bigs, going 15-for-39 (.385) with three homers in his final 10 games of 2026. Jenkins, who has battled his own constant injuries throughout his career, only played center in the Majors while Byron Buxton was out with a hip issue, but he got some looks in the corners at Triple-A St. Paul before his ascent. Expect that to continue on days when Buxton doesn’t DH, but Larnach’s departure also offers Buxton more opportunities to avoid the grass in his own attempt to stay healthy.

Caden Bodine, C, Rays (
TB No. 3
/
MLB No. 46
)

The American League champs traded backup catcher Nick Fortes to the Red Sox last week, and that should draw eyes immediately to Bodine. 

Acquired from the Orioles in last offseason’s Shane Baz blockbuster, the former Coastal Carolina backstop put together a strong, well-rounded 2026 across all four of the Rays’ full-season affiliates with a .342 average that ranked second among full-season qualifiers and a 6.3 percent strikeout rate that was lowest on that leaderboard. The switch-hitter was more than a contact maven though with 17 homers in his 117 games (16 of which came from the left side), and on top of that, he was named a Minor League Gold Glove winner behind the plate. 

The Rays kept Bodine on the taxi squad for the postseason run in case of emergencies, but now with Fortes out, he should compete with Kenny Piper for the Opening Day backup spot in St. Petersburg and perhaps even win the starting spot over Liam Hicks at some point next summer should his now plus-plus hit tool translate quickly.

Jake Bauers didn’t accept the qualifying offer and chose to sign a four-year, $58 million deal with the Red Sox instead. That leaves the World Series champions searching for a left-handed hitter complement to Andrew Vaughn at first base. They could look for that on the free-agent market – Nathaniel Lowe is an example – but it’s worth noting that they have a few options in the upper Minors. 

Fischer is coming off his historic 48-homer campaign at High-A and Double-A in his first full season of the Minors, and with his combination of exit velocities and angles off the bat, he has the highest ceiling, though his contact concerns point to him needing at least some Triple-A experience first. Burke also bats lefty with significant pop after going deep 34 times last year, but he hit just .193 with a .635 OPS in 31 games for Nashville. Adams has the 40-man spot after getting Rule 5 protection, and his combination of power and patience could play quickly in the bigs. But alas, he’s right-handed like Vaughn. 

It could be a crowded corner infield situation in Nashville next year, but it comes with a terrific opportunity for this trio, barring other Crew moves.

Patti Smith Makes NPR Tiny Desk Concert Debut: Watch&nbsp;&nbsp;Consequence of Sound

[page text]
Patti Smith
 recently stopped by the NPR offices for a 
Tiny Desk Concert
 featuring spoken word poetry and a handful of selections from her solo catalog. Watch a replay of her four-song performance below.

Backed by her son Jackson Smith (guitar), Tony Shanahan (keyboards, bass), and Seb Rochford (drums), the Godmother of Punk began with a mostly improvised spoken-word tale about an old sea captain wistfully longing for one last journey. Then she moved into a wandering, wistful rendition of “Nine” from her latest solo studio album, 2012’s 
Banga
, before picking up the pace with a melancholy performance of “Fireflies” from 1996’s 
Gone Again
.

After that, Smith dedicated “Peaceable Kingdom” off 2004’s Trampin’ (which she co-wrote with Shanahan) to “all of the children globally who suffer the consequences of war, famine, and disease.” She added, “We wish them all an unsullied childhood. That their dreams stay intact and that their kites fly high.” She wrapped up the set with “People Have the Power,” a song of resistance she co-wrote with late husband and MC5 guitarist Fred “Sonic” Smith as the lead single from 1988’s 
Dream of Life
.

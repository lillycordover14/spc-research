Tar Heels Are Preaching Progress, Saturday Offers Final Chance to Prove It&nbsp;&nbsp;On3

[page text]
CHAPEL HILL, N.C. — The back half of the 2026 season has not been kind to North Carolina. After winning four of their first six games, the Tar Heels have since dropped four of their last five contests, and sit at 5-6 entering Saturday’s regular season finale against NC State. 

Halfway through October, it appeared UNC had turned a corner in 
Bill Belichick’s
 second year at the helm. Now, as Carolina looks to salvage some semblance of success from a once-promising season and secure bowl eligibility on Saturday, the Tar Heels assert their record doesn’t tell the full story. 

“I think we for sure have made tremendous strides and progress, and we’ve shown it week in and week out,” 
Jordan Shipp
 said Monday. 

As UNC prepares to close out the regular season against the Wolfpack, however, the Tar Heels find themselves in a position similar to the one they found themselves in entering last season’s rivalry matchup — amid a disappointing finish to the season and riding a two-game losing streak. 

To Shipp’s point, however, North Carolina does have one more win to its name this time around, and unlike last season, the Tar Heels still have bowl eligibility in their sights. 

“I think we see what we can be, and obviously we’ve come up a little bit short in a couple areas,” 
Derek McDonald
 said. “But there’s definitely been growth, and I think, obviously we want to show it to the people that aren’t in this program. They don’t see it every day, so we gotta go get that done.”

En route to a 4-8 finish a season ago, North Carolina’s average margin of defeat was 17.1 points, suffering five double-digit defeats, three of which came by 28 points or more. 

Even after last weekend’s 17-point loss to Virginia — its largest of the season — UNC’s average margin of defeat has shrunk to seven points, as the Tar Heels, at the very least, have put a more competitive product on the field in 2026.

“We weren’t in any of those games last year,” Shipp said. “We were just getting blown out. Just call it what it is. We were losing, and the vibe around the building didn’t feel the same. But I mean, it’s just a lot different than where it was last year.”

Of Carolina’s six losses this season, four have come by one possession or less, with the Tar Heels having a chance to tie the game or take the lead in the fourth quarter of each before ultimately falling a handful of plays short.  

“You can’t come in on Sunday saying, ‘My bad, my bad,’” Shipp said. “And of course, that’s got to start with me and start with the leaders and the captains. Just go do what we gotta do to get over that hump, and we know we’re right there. We’ve just got to put a little extra in, and get over it.”

Still, North Carolina sits with a combined 9-14 record, including 4-11 in ACC play in its two seasons under Belichick. And as the Tar Heels gear up to close out their second regular season of the Belichick era, they’ll have one final opportunity to transform the aforementioned progress into a tangible result on Saturday. 

“In terms of progress and the progress that we’ve made, of course the people outside the facility can’t see it,” Shipp said. “But the people that are in here, we know that we’re right there. We’ve just got to get over the hump.”

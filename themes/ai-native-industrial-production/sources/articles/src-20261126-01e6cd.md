China’s lithium hydroxide futures tumble in debut as demand looks weak

The launch is part of China's push to gain more global pricing power in the electric vehicle battery sector.

 Reuters    

    |
    
November 26, 2026 - 3:35 pm

Categories:

China

Lithium

Markets

 

Stock image.

China’s lithium hydroxide futures contracts slid in their Thursday trading debut at the Guangzhou Futures Exchange, as prospects for demand for the battery metal remain gloomy.

The launch follows the listing of lithium carbonate futures in July 2023, and is part of China’s push to expand hedging tools for its vast electric vehicle battery supply chain and gain more global pricing power in the sector.

The most traded June 2027 contract, which also has the earliest maturity date, closed daytime trade at 69,600 yuan ($10,445), down 7.2% from a base price of 75,000 yuan per metric ton set by the exchange.

The first batch of tradable contracts is to be delivered over the June-to-November 2027 period.

“The launch of the new contract came as demand prospects for battery metal worsened and the macroeconomic sentiment was weak,” said Zhang Weixin, an analyst at China Futures.

Supply and demand fundamentals for lithium hydroxide are weaker than those of lithium carbonate, analysts at broker Everbright Futures said in a note.

Lithium carbonate futures prices slipped more than 3% to their lowest in more than one year to 80,580 yuan a ton on Thursday. They have slumped 62% from a 33-month high of 209,880 yuan touched in May.

Prices of lithium carbonate are on average 10,000 yuan higher than those of lithium hydroxide so far this year, according to Nick Wang, an analyst at information provider Zljsteel New Energy.

“Demand for the type of battery using lithium hydroxide was subdued; moreover, export demand may take a hit from the protectionism measures taken by the European Union for China’s electric vehicles,” said Wang.

The EU has imposed temporary limits on imports of hybrid vehicles from China, Japan, Turkey, Britain and other countries in a move aimed at protecting EU automakers, effective from December 1.

($1 = 6.6629 Chinese yuan)

Geno struggling for answers after latest loss: 'I'm having a really hard time coaching this team'
Will the Huskies finally learn from their mistakes after falling to South Carolina?
Daniel Connolly
Nov 25, 2026
In partnership with
As Geno Auriemma processed 
UConn’s 65-56 loss to South Carolina
 in his postgame press conference on Tuesday, he revealed a truth that had been evident for some time.
“
I'm having a really hard time coaching this team,” he said. “You might be able to tell that. I'm having a really hard time coaching this team and I gotta figure it out.”
If Auriemma tried to hide his frustration through the first month of the season, he didn’t do a good job. After the Huskies’ loss at Michigan, 
he called it the most uncharacteristic performance from his team in over two decades
. As they were 
getting blown out at Louisville
, Auriemma declined to use any of his own timeouts, reasoning, ‘The timeouts were very unproductive, so I just felt like, why bother?’” 
Strictly looking at what happened on the court, UConn can take away plenty of positives from Tuesday in spite of the defeat. The Huskies were tough, winning the rebound battle while also getting to the free-throw line 24 times. Even without their top two point guards, they only lost by nine to the undefeated No. 1 team in the nation.
UConn outplayed South Carolina at times. 
It just couldn’t overcome a disastrous end to the first half
.
Auriemma acknowledged that much, 
saying the Huskies “lost the game” in the last two minutes of the second quarter
. Still, his mood on the podium more closely resembled that of a coach who had just been beaten by 30, not single digits. He seemed defeated — not by the result but by his own team.
“You get a quarter like we had in the first quarter… If it never happened, then you could say, ‘Well, they just don't have it in them to make it happen.’ But when you see it happen, and then you see it go completely the other way… It's incredibly frustrating and I’m at a loss. I really am. Usually, I have a pretty good idea,” Auriemma said.

The coach had no trouble articulating what needs to be fixed. He pointed to UConn’s 8-27 showing on layups and 22 fouls against the Gamecocks.
“I've never had a team at the University of Connecticut miss as many layups as this team misses, or commit as many stupid fouls as this team does,” Auriemma said.
The latter isn’t hyperbole. 
According to Rebecca Lobo
, UConn’s 19.1 fouls per game would be its most in a season since at least 1999-2000. Since 2009, the Huskies have committed 20+ fouls in a single contest on 26 occasions. They’ve done so three times this year alone.
That problem starts at the top. Sarah Strong picked up three fouls in the first half and added a fourth with 3:01 left in the third quarter. That sent her to the bench for nearly five minutes while UConn tried to mount a comeback bid.
Strong has now dealt with foul trouble in three of the Huskies’ five ranked matchups.
“How many games now in a row has Sarah been on the bench with fouls? You can't have your best player, in every big game that we’ve played, sitting on the bench with fouls,” Auriemma said.
That illustrates a clear pattern with this UConn team: It makes the same mistakes over and over again. After each loss, the Huskies have arrived at a crossroads. Instead of making the clearly-marked correct turn, they seem intent on proving they know better than the GPS and continually drive themselves into a ditch.
“We can't just keep trying to do it our way. Obviously it doesn't work,” Strong said. 
I'm tired of having the same conversation.”
“It's just decisions that we're making out on the floor,” Auriemma said. “I don't understand why someone, a team, not one individual, will keep doing the same things over and over again in some of these games. I don't understand,” Auriemma said.
One explanation?
“We're really immature and I said that at the beginning of the season. It's just a very, very, very immature team basketball-wise — and maybe in general. I don't know,” Auriemma said.
So it’s not as if UConn can’t figure out what’s wrong. It just hasn’t committed itself to change. While the coach has taken some of the blame for not getting his message across, Strong indicated the problem lies with the players.
“When he says something or calls a play and we don't run it, it's just little things. Whatever he says on the court, we need to do. Whatever any coach says, we need to do it. We can't just do our own thing out there,” she revealed.
“There has to be a different intensity about us,” Strong said. “Coach was talking about how we don't put enough pressure on ourselves in practice. So when we miss layups, it should be a much bigger deal in practice and we don't put enough pressure on ourselves. So I think it needs to start there,” she added.
Auriemma isn’t just trying to light a fire under his team by calling them out, as he’s done so many times in the past. He seemed genuinely unsure of how to proceed — helpless, almost.
“As a coaching staff, we're shaking our head, trying to figure out what new strategies we can come up with,” he said. “I want to be able to give them some answers to why we're so up and down… I feel like every time you try to fix one thing, something else breaks, and then you try to fix that thing, and something else breaks.”
UConn had never lost three games prior to Dec. 3. Now, it’s fallen three times in November alone. It’s not just that the Huskies are losing, either. It’s that they don’t display many — if any — of the program’s most identifiable hallmarks. 
“It's just so out of character for anybody that's watched UConn play for 30-some years. I don't even recognize it,” Auriemma said.
Granted, the Huskies have lost to the No. 1, No. 2 and No. 3 teams in the country. South Carolina, Michigan and Louisville could very well all end in the Final Four. UConn has been tested — and exposed — more than anyone else. That, in theory, should pay dividends down the road.
Auriemma isn’t so sure, though.
“Maybe I did this team a disservice (with the schedule). Maybe they're just not mentally ready for something like this. Maybe it's just too much for them,” he said. “Maybe I overstated what kind of team we had mentally, and what I thought would be a great thing for them to experience — maybe not so much.”

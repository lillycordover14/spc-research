Rally1 Rewind final: Sesks’ Poland debut or Evans’ Sardinia title?
Sixteen moments have become two. Mārtiņš Sesks’ stunning Poland debut faces Elfyn Evans’ first world title in the Rally1 Rewind final. Voting opens today and closes on Monday morning.
Written by WRC
2 min read
Published on 
26.11.2026 · 09:39 UTC
Mārtiņš Sesks’ first full-length Rally1 stage and Elfyn Evans’ dramatic Sardinia title decider are the last two moments standing as fans choose their greatest memory of the first Rally1 generation.
Sesks reached the final with 59% against Craig Breen’s ‘Mayor of Brattby’ interview. Evans took 64% against Kalle Rovanperä’s record-breaking first world title in New Zealand.
The deciding vote opens today, Thursday 26 November, and runs until Monday morning, 30 November. One final choice will settle the competition, which began with 16 nominations spanning the 2022–2026 seasons.
Sesks announces himself in Poland
Sesks and co-driver Renārs Francis had barely begun their Rally1 debut when they produced the performance that earned their nomination.
After Thursday’s short opening super special at Rally Poland in 2024, Friday morning brought the 29.40km Stańczyki test. It was their first full-length stage in the M-Sport Ford Puma Rally1, which was running without hybrid assistance.
 
© WRC
They finished just 0.3sec behind stage winner Andreas Mikkelsen and 7.3sec ahead of reigning world champion Kalle Rovanperä. The time lifted them to second overall. Sesks’ disbelief on hearing the result captured how unexpected that position was.
The Latvians held second through the morning and finished fifth on their debut. Their nominated moment has since beaten Takamoto Katsuta’s Safari victory, Sami Pajari’s historic hat-trick and Breen’s Brattby joy to reach this final.
Evans’ wait ends in Sardinia
Evans and Scott Martin’s nomination came at the opposite end of a long pursuit: the final corners of a season that finally made them world champions.
A puncture on Sardinia’s penultimate stage in 2026 forced them to stop and change a wheel. They retained fifth overall but lost their chance of Super Sunday points, leaving the championship to be settled on the Wolf Power Stage against Oliver Solberg and Elliott Edmondson.
 
© WRC
Evans completed his run first. Solberg was ahead on the splits, but slid into the sand near the finish. He recovered to win the rally, while his stage time fell 8.9sec short of Evans’.
After five runner-up seasons, Evans and Martin secured their first world titles by five points.
That finish has now come through votes against Esapekka Lappi’s Sweden comeback, Ott Tänak’s Finland victory and Rovanperä’s New Zealand crown.

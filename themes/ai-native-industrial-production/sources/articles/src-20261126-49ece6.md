The World Cup begins: Lidl-Trek to debut cyclocross team in Ostrava and Tabor&nbsp;&nbsp;Trek Race Shop

[page text]
With the opening round of the cyclocross World Cup in Ostrava, Czech Republic, a new chapter in Lidl-Trek’s history begins.

Lidl-Trek’s new cyclocross team will make its full debut at the opening World Cup weekend, racing in Ostrava on Friday and Tábor on Sunday. Blanka Vas gave the team’s blue, yellow and red colours their first outing last weekend, racing twice in Slovakia.

Vas will be joined by Fleur Moors for the World Cup opener. The pair will be supported by four mechanics, one soigneur and new sports director Tom De Kort.

Cyclocross is a natural next step for Lidl-Trek. Several riders already race across multiple disciplines, and a dedicated cyclocross programme gives them the same level of support off-road as they have on the road. Trek and SRAM will provide the equipment for the new programme.

Grischa Niermann, Head of Sport at Lidl-Trek, explains:

Cycling is more than just a summer sport, and cyclocross is a fantastic discipline in its own right. We want to seize the opportunity to showcase our colours all year round. With so much talent across multiple disciplines within our squad, expanding our presence into the winter season is a natural next step. That’s why we’ve decided to establish our own cyclocross team – growing Lidl-Trek even more while providing Fleur Moors and Blanka Vas with the best possible support to achieve their off-road ambitions.

Team manager Andy Schleck first contacted Tom De Kort over the summer as Lidl-Trek put its cyclocross programme together. The 52-year-old brings several years of experience as a sports director, most recently with De Ceuster Bouwkranen, following a spell at Pauwels Sauzen-Bingoal.

De Kort and Fleur Moors spent two seasons working together at Pauwels Sauzen-Bingoal, but their connection goes back to when Moors was 16 and first getting into cycling.

Moors played handball as a teenager in Belgium. When the COVID-19 pandemic put team training on hold, she tried riding with a local cycling group. She quickly took an interest in cyclocross and showed enough promise to be put in touch with De Kort, who helped her take her first steps in the sport.

Fleur Moors - Photo by Luc Claessen/Getty Images

“You saw immediately she has talent,” De Kort remembers. “I have worked in cyclocross for 15 years and I never saw someone as talented as her.”

De Kort and Moors are both relishing the chance to work together again at Lidl-Trek. The sports director is also looking forward to working with Blanka Vas. The 25-year-old Hungarian has two World Cup wins to her name and finished fourth at last winter’s World Championships.

“I’ve known her for a long time and we get along very well, also with her husband, who joins us as a mechanic, so we will have a very good environment,” De Kort said.

“We are very excited to get this up and running now,” said De Kort before travelling to Czechia. He also explained why cyclocross can be such a valuable stepping stone for riders looking to develop on the road: “The handling of the bike, but also the specific athletic skill like explosiveness. Especially in the classics you need skills from cyclocross.”

For Moors and Vas, the cyclocross campaign will also form part of their preparations for the road season.

“At the moment the main goal for us is to start this project. We of course hope and fight for good results in cyclocross, where both are big talents, but it’s also part of the preparation for the road season where the really big goals are the Classics.”

Moors and Vas will take two weeks away from cyclocross in mid-December to join Lidl-Trek’s training camp in Spain. Both are set to race between 10 and 15 times this winter, with the World Cup making up most of their schedules and selected rounds of other major series pencilled in over Christmas and New Year.

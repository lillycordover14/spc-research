Could European conventional deterrence make nuclear weapons obsolete?&nbsp;&nbsp;PubAffairs Bruxelles

[page text]
In short

In the short term, nuclear weapons remain necessary to deter nuclear-armed adversaries.

In the longer term, conventional deterrence could gradually reduce the role of nuclear weapons in international relations.

It is crucial that (a coalition of) European states will cooperate closely and prioritise their investments in conventional deterrence capabilities.

While the credibility of the US nuclear umbrella over NATO territory is slowly decreasing, European NATO states are investing a great deal of money in conventional weapons to deter potential adversaries from waging war. Could this kind of conventional arms in the longer term make nuclear weapons obsolete? In other words: could coordinated European military investments ultimately boost global nuclear disarmament? 

This Alert explores three parallel efforts to increase European deterrence to prevent blackmail from nuclear-armed adversaries. It also analyses the possibility that conventional deterrence capabilities in the longer term could offer a credible alternative for nuclear weapons. For this, a comparison is made with US President Barack Obama’s ‘Conventional Prompt Global Strike’ initiative and with chemical weapons disarmament.

 after UNC’s win vs. Monmouth on Monday

Ross Hodge
 after WVU’s loss vs. Florida at the Players Era 8 on Nov. 19

Pregame Notes

Last Time Out:
 North Carolina posted its best defensive effort of the season on Monday, holding Monmouth to 29 percent from the floor and nine percent from three in its 85-45 win. 
Terrence Brown
 led the Tar Heels in scoring with 20 points, and 
Alex Samodurov
 pitched in a double-double off the bench. 

West Virginia had a busy three days in Las Vegas last week, dueling with Auburn, Kansas and Florida over a 72-hour stretch. The Mountaineers started with a 76-70 win over the Tigers before stumbling to the Jayhawks and Gators by four and 13 points, respectively. The early-season tournament served as a national introduction to four-star freshman 
Miles Sadler
, who notched 70 points in the trio of games, highlighted by 34 against Kansas.

Analyzing the Mountaineers:
 Head coach Ross Hodge is in his second season at the helm for West Virginia after a 21-14 record a year ago. WVU missed the NCAA tournament in 2025-26 but won the second-annual College Basketball Crown. Outside of the three Players Era games, the rest of the Mountaineers’ games have been against sub-Top 200 teams, and they’ve won those by an average of 32.3 points.

As West Virginia heads to Charlotte after an eight-day break, its starting lineup projects to feature the marquee freshman, one returner and three transfers. Sadler, a 6-foot guard, will likely be atop the scouting report for UNC, showcasing a skillset rooted in quickness with the ball and craftiness around the rim, averaging over 20 points per game. He can shoot the three, too, despite making 23.8 percent early in his collegiate career.

Butler transfer and former All-Big East selection 
Finley Bizjack
 is Sadler’s counterpart in the backcourt. The senior has attempted more 3-pointers than 2-pointers in every season of his four-year career, but like Sadler, he is struggling thus far at 27.3 percent. While the Mountaineers go smaller in their first five, they lean on 6-foot-10 
Mouhamed Sylla
 and his backup, 7-foot-1 
Amadou Seini
, to uplift the frontcourt. Both average over five rebounds per game, while neither is above eight points per game in the scoring department.

By the Numbers:
 Ranked 37th in KenPom, West Virginia leans on its stout defense under Hodge — 18th nationally in 2025-26 and 31st nationally so far in 2026. Opponents are shooting 44.5 percent from the field and 30.4 percent from deep against the Mountaineers. They also boast the 33rd-best turnover percentage in Division I, creating an interesting clash with the Tar Heels, who are top-10 nationally in taking care of the basketball.

West Virginia averages 36.5 rebounds per game and has a positive margin on the season, though the Mountaineers were outrebounded by 12 against Florida and by two against Kansas. All notable metrics grade its rebounding efficiency — offensive and defensive — around the 100th-ranked mark in the country, while UNC still lags in the mid-200s. 

Offensively, according to KenPom, Hodge’s squad plays at one of the slowest tempos at the power conference level despite averaging 81.2 points per game. West Virginia’s 45.1 percent from the field and 32.9 percent from distance nearly mimic North Carolina’s splits, but the Mountaineers have not shot above 31.8 percent from 3-point range since Nov. 6 versus Stetson.

To round out its profile, West Virginia averages 12.3 second-chance points, 33.0 points in the paint and 6.8 fast-break points per game. The Mountaineers were picked to finish in a tie for eighth in the Big 12 preseason poll.

Last Meeting:
 West Virginia is one of two teams to hold an undefeated multi-game record against the Tar Heels, entering Friday at 5-0 all-time — the most wins against UNC without a loss of any program. The last meeting came on Dec. 31, 1965 in Reynolds Coliseum in Raleigh, a 102-97 Mountaineer victory.

German Real Wages Rise 1.3% in Q3 2026 as Nominal Pay Outpaces Inflation

Real wages for employees
 in Germany were 1.3% higher in the third quarter of 2026 than in the same quarter a year earlier, according to the Federal Statistical Office (Destatis). The office reported that nominal wages rose 4.3% over the same period while 
consumer prices
 increased 2.9%.
Nominal wage growth was above average in several economic sectors during the third quarter of 2026. Energy supply recorded an increase of 6.7% year on year, financial and insurance services rose 6.3%, and other economic services climbed 5.6%. By contrast, nominal wage growth was comparatively modest in freelance, scientific and technical services at 3.2%, other services at 3.1%, and mining and quarrying at 3.1%.
Lower Earners See Strongest Gains
Among full-time employees grouped by earnings class, the fifth with the lowest earnings recorded average nominal wage growth of 6.6% in the third quarter of 2026, again the strongest increase compared with the same quarter a year earlier. Earnings for full-time workers overall rose 4.3%, matching the pace of nominal wages across the whole economy. For the top fifth of full-time earners, nominal wage growth was 3.4%, below the overall economic trend.
Trainees and marginally employed workers posted above-average nominal wage growth of 5.6% in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the prior-year quarter.
Methodological Notes
Destatis said the nominal wage index tracks the development of gross monthly earnings including special payments for all employees, while the real wage index compares earnings development with consumer price development and thus offers indications on the purchasing power of employees in Germany.
The office noted methodological challenges in the public administration, defense and social security sector that are understood to be linked to structural changes in that branch. It said the informative value of results for this sector is currently limited and the corresponding figures will not be published until further notice, with the collective bargaining index available as an alternative source of information for the sector.

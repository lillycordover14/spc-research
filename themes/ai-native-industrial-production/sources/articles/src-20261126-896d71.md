The parade was postponed due to concerns over high winds and rain Thanksgiving day.

The University of Connecticut marching band is adjusting its travel plans after the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed, but the roughly 300-member band is still expected to make its debut in the iconic New York City event on Saturday.

The postponement comes as the UConn marching band celebrates its 130th anniversary, making its first appearance in the parade a significant milestone.

Band members had already traveled to New York City, where they performed in Central Park alongside a local high school band.

UConn Director of Athletic Bands Jessica Dickerson said the band will return to campus Thursday morning before heading back to New York City late Friday evening for Saturday's parade.

The change in plans also gives band members who live in New York and New Jersey an opportunity to spend time with their families for Thanksgiving.

Dickerson said UConn has a logistically easier time compared to some other participating bands because of its proximity to New York City, making the last-minute travel adjustments easier to manage.

“A day of rest and a return to the city in a pattern that we're very familiar with at UConn with our Huskies playing in MSG, Storrs South, all the time, you know, this is business as usual for us in UConn athletic bands, and so just a few more buses on this trip back over,” Dickerson said.

Other college marching bands, including Alabama's, could face additional logistical challenges if the postponement requires them to extend their stays in New York City.

Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade executive producer Will Coss said organizers are communicating with participating bands to work through the scheduling and travel complications.

“We are optimistic that we're going to be able to maintain the parade that we set out to produce and to deliver to New York City on Thanksgiving. There's still some logistics to be worked out, but everyone understands what we're up against,” Coss said.

Despite the unexpected changes, UConn officials said the band remains on track to perform Saturday thanks in part to generous donors who helped make the trip possible.

Dickerson says the marching band raised more than its original fundraising goal, providing additional financial support to help cover unexpected expenses associated with the schedule change.

Men’s Basketball: UConn guard Solo Ball makes season debut against Duke 

By
 
Thaddeus Sawyer

November 26, 2026

UConn shooting guard Solo Ball celebrates a 3-pointer in a contest against Duke University at T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas, Nev. on Nov. 25, 2026. Ball finished 2-of-9 from the field and 1-of-6 from behind the arc. 

Photo by Madison Hendricks, Photo Editor/The Daily Campus

LAS VEGAS — Though No. 10 UConn fell to No. 16 Duke Wednesday, the Huskies still found at least one thing to be thankful for this Thanksgiving. 

Shooting guard Solo Ball made his season debut after undergoing offseason wrist surgery and missing UConn’s first five contests of the season. 

“I love UConn with all my heart,” Ball said postgame. “This was a big game coming back for me, but I wish it went into a different direction — us winning.” 

Ball himself was certainly thankful to walk into the Huskies’ locker room at T-Mobile Arena and find his jersey hanging in his locker. 

“It was surreal,” Ball said. “When I first walked into the locker room and I saw my jersey in the locker, I really just went in the stall and just bawled out crying, just because I haven’t really seen my jersey there in a while. That feeling is just a certain kind of rush you get from putting on the UConn jersey, and I’ll never forget it.” 

Ball, who was originally set to take a medical redshirt this season prior to the NCAA’s adoption of an age-based eligibility model, said that he was originally told he wouldn’t be shooting in front of a rim until January. Instead, the senior found himself in the thick of a ranked matchup between a pair of blue bloods, hoping to give the Huskies a jolt off the bench. 

“This is a game I really wanted to be a part of,” Ball said. “I just wanted to be with my brothers. This team has really motivated me the most to just come back and play just because of how close we are off the court.” 

UConn Head Coach Dan Hurley said postgame that Ball choosing to be available for a contest, especially one of this magnitude, after just over a week of practice following the team’s announcement that he had been 
cleared for full-contact practice on Nov. 16
 was telling of Ball’s character. 

“For this to be his first game since playing in 
the national championship game
, I mean, that guy could have picked another one to come back for,” Hurley said. “This guy picked this one — that tells you about that guy. I think he’s going to help solve a lot of offensive woes that we’re having in terms of finding scoring at some other spots.” 

Hurley added that Ball’s minutes will increase every game. 

The shooting guard played 14 minutes Wednesday, shaking off some obvious rust while going 2-of-9 from the field and 1-of-6 from 3-point range. 

Ball first subbed in for guard Braylon Mullins at the 12:50 mark in the first half, giving the sophomore a high five as they passed. He stayed in for just over three minutes in his first shift, one which was a mixed bag. While Ball swiped away a steal from Sebastian Wilkins near midcourt, drawing a foul in the process, and added a defensive rebound, he also missed two 3-point attempts and left the contest with a team-low -8 rating after committing a shooting foul on Wilkins. 

Ball began to find his footing midway through the second half, checking in at 13:54 with UConn down seven for his biggest minutes of the contest thus far. After dishing a pass from the paint toward forward Jayden Ross in the left corner, who missed off the front of the rim, Ball found himself near the top of the key. Center Na’jai Hines pulled down an offensive rebound and found Ball, who knocked down his first 3-pointer and field goal of the season. 

A possession later, taking a handoff from Hines, Ball drew a foul on Dame Sarr on driving a layup attempt to the left side of the rim and sank a pair of free throws on his first two attempts of the season. 

The 3-pointer was the 200th of his four-year career at UConn, making him the tenth player in program history to reach that mark.  

“Just having that confidence to keep shooting and not getting in my own head,” Ball said. “I know I have a healthy wrist now, so it’s just getting back in the flow of the game.” 

Ball finished with seven points, adding an assist and two rebounds.  

Senior point guard Silas Demary Jr. said postgame that Ball’s return will add veteran leadership on the court. 

“I think it just gives us more confidence to be the team that we need to be,” Demary Jr. said. “A guy that’s been here four years; a guy that knows what the standard is and what it takes to win here. I think it gives us another leader and it should propel us.”

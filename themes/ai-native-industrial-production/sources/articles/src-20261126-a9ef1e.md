Pecco Bagnaia has revealed he suffered a small haematoma on his brain during the Qatar accident, prematurely ending his Ducati MotoGP career.
The Italian underwent surgery after fracturing two metatarsal bones in his right foot when he was taken out by Fermin Aldeguer early in the Lusail Sprint on 7 November.
Although ruled out of Portimao, the double
 MotoGP
 champion was expected to be fit for a final Ducati appearance at this weekend’s Valencia finale.
However, 
Ducati
 announced on Wednesday that “final medical checks… did not give the green light to get back on the bike.”

No further details were given. However, speaking alongside Ducati Corse general manager Gigi Dall’Igna at an emotional ‘Ducati farewell’ press conference in Valencia, 
Bagnaia
 confirmed he suffered a concussion in Qatar and is quoted by Sky Italia as saying: 
"I'm not racing because there is still some blood, a small hematoma, in the head [brain].
 
"We are not afraid because it's very little, but there is some possibility of a bigger consequence, so we cannot share our last moment together. It's a shame for everybody."
Aprilia debut: "At the moment, the doctors have said 'no' "
It’s not yet clear if Bagnaia will be able to make his Aprilia MotoGP debut in Tuesday’s test.
"At the moment, the doctors have said 'no' to Tuesday's test, too," Bagnaia said.

"In any case, if I could take the test, I should be very calm."

Reigning world champion Marc Marquez's new team-mate, 
Pedro Acosta

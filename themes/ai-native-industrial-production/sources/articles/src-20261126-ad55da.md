If your 
tulip bulbs
 are still in their bag, now’s a good time to plant them. Garden designer Pollyanna Wilkinson takes the first hard frosts as her cue, but there’s no urgent need to rush out this weekend. 
“I often don’t get round to doing mine until Christmas,” she writes in the caption to her tulip-planting video.
The RHS says tulips can grow well 
when planted from September to December
, though it’s best to plant them soon after buying them so they don’t deteriorate in storage. And if squirrels tend to get to your bulbs before spring does, Pollyanna has a trick that starts in your spice cupboard.
Deb Perry
//
Getty Images
How to plant tulip bulbs for spring
“With tulips, just remember to plant them pointy side up and you want to plant them about three times the depth of the bulb,” says Pollyanna. 
The 
RHS recommends a depth of two to three times the bulb’s height
, whether you’re planting in pots or borders. So for a bulb that’s 5cm tall, dig a hole 10–15cm deep and sit the bulb at the bottom.
Pollyanna waits for the soil to cool because of tulip fire, a fungal disease that can leave leaves brown-spotted and twisted. Frost alone won't stop it. The 
RHS advises checking bulbs for signs of decay
 before planting and keeping tulips out of any bed where the disease has struck for at least three years.
Trudie Davidson
//

How to stop squirrels digging up your tulips
“And if you have a lot of squirrels like I do, then I tend to put mine in a bucket with loads of chilli powder and toss it about, and that seems to work at keeping them away,” says Pollyanna.
She says chilli powder helped in her own garden. For pots and raised beds, Pollyanna has another suggestion: "You can just put some chicken wire or a cloche over the top to stop the squirrels getting at them." The RHS also recommends wire netting for bulbs in pots. Pull it taut and check it now and then, so no birds or other wildlife get tangled. Once the shoots poke through, you can usually take it off.
If you’d rather use what’s already in the garden, Pollyanna mentions laying thorny rose prunings over the soil. 
One social media user suggested bay sprigs as another way to 
deter squirrels from digging in pots
 “as they smell so powerful”.“Excellent tip,” Pollyanna replied. 
Country Living's 
editor Vicky Carlisle tried a similar approach when she 
tucked holly sprigs into the compost to protect her spring bulbs
.
As for shaking your fists and chasing the squirrels away? Pollyanna reckons it “doesn’t have much effect but may make you feel better.”

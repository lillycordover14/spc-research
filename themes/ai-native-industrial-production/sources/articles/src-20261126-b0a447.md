Here Comes the Rain Again&nbsp;&nbsp;North Coast Journal

[page text]
Having done this beat for nearly a decade, I know this week is generally a quiet one, but this year’s edition is a real outsider. Two venues, both alike in dignity, in fair Arcata where we lay our scene … but no ancient grudges or new mutinies. Just a couple of shows in the front-half of the weekend after a kinda cruel and contentious holiday. I don’t want to talk about that holiday, so instead we’re going to take a spin around some things I’m thinking about while writing on the day of this Supermoon. Obviously, it’s flooding everywhere, which sucks for the people near the water. You have my sympathy. “Crime doesn’t climb” has been the mantra regarding civic planning since before the Romans, and that the wealthy are still insulated from the effects of their largesse and corrupt policies is still a huge problem for society thousands of years later. Here’s a thought: Maybe the rest of us should do something about that.

On a more personal note, Liz and I found a juvenile bald eagle a couple of days ago while hitting the old birding spots that used to be just for me before I found a better option with a super cool lady. The big critter looked like a golden eagle at first but was hanging out in the fields that a nesting pair — probably its parents — used to frequent a couple of years ago. They would hang out on telephone poles above the cows, and the lady bird took the higher spot, as it should be. She is the largest bald eagle I’ve seen south of Canada, and her much smaller mate seemed absolutely stoked to hang out on the lower cross section of the “t” of the service pole. I appreciate the feeling, even though in my life it’s not about relative size, but a certain aura.

I took a dear friend to see the Spark Trio play Beethoven’s Piano Trio opus 1, No.2 at the Lutheran Church of Arcata in the redwoods by the university last weekend and it was a fucking blast. The pianist, Eric Zivian, played on a fortepiano, and the accompanying string players used guts on their instruments. What a fantastic and rare treat. I look forward to writing about more of the Eureka Chamber Music Series gigs early next year.

 

 

Today also marks 35 years since Freddie Mercury died. Which is a long time to be missing that voice. Oh my, what a voice. I recently read a line about how “the eyes we use to look for God are the eyes God uses to find us.” And that’s probably true about the voice I hear on Queen records.

And finally, it’s the time of year when I usually watch a lot of horror films and listen to extreme noise and metal as a means of radiating myself with some comforting waves that will (hopefully) sustain me into the springtime. Lots of early Norwegian black metal and very bleak movies from here until the lilacs and lupine return. I had a dream the other night that made everything so clear and obvious: It involved vampires and big cats (mostly leopards and jaguars) to drive home how feral and satisfying it would be to fucking kill something or someone using just your fucking face. 

Consider this a gift to ponder for those of you who — unlike myself — are going to inflict yourselves upon this holiday with gusto.

Have a great week.

 

 

Friday
Big Mood
, the big ol’ queer dance party at the Miniplex every fourth Friday of the month, is still rolling on for those who celebrate. And thank God for that because there’s something quite special about 
DJ Pandemonium Jones
 holding court over jeweled beats from the vinyl throne for every creature great and small. The fun starts at 9 p.m., and it’s only $5 to get in the door, which is a mere pocketful of pennies tossed aside for an evening of fun.

Saturday
How about an indie folk show at the Outer Space tonight at 7 p.m.? Regular readers of this column will surely recall the excitement I had for the recently released music of 
Luna Sofia
, who will be joined this evening with 
Hank Van. K
 and 
Kasper Herbst
, whose music I haven’t had the pleasure of hearing yet but whose stage names hit a sort of harmony on the handbill. There’s a suggested donation of $5-$10 to check it out, which hardly seems like much for what might be a lovely acoustic cycle into the coming winter.

Collin Yeo (he/him) is trying to be quiet in the middle of the storm.

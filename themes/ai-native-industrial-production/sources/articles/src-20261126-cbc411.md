South Korea's Clobot Nears 10 Billion Won in Annual Revenue from Boston Dynamics' Spot Business as Industrial Deployments Expand
Add preferred source
South Korea's Clobot expects revenue from its Boston Dynamics quadruped robot "Spot" business to exceed 10 billion won (approximately $7.4 million) this year. Sales volume is projected at about 30 units this year, with more than 90 units supplied cumulatively over the past four years and over 140 projects completed. Facility inspection and predictive maintenance at industrial sites operated by POSCO, Korea Zinc and Korea Western Power form the core market, while the company is widening applications into public safety, including patrols at Sejong City's Ieung Bridge and remote bomb checks at Incheon International Airport. Next year, Clobot plans to commercialize anomaly alerts, inspection reports and monitoring functions as standard software, and to use Doosan Logistics Solutions' capabilities to conduct humanoid technology validation in distribution and logistics warehouses.

Clobot, a South Korean robot service and solutions company, expects revenue from its business involving Boston Dynamics' quadruped robot "Spot" to exceed 10 billion won (approximately $7.4 million) this year. Clobot unveiled a live demo room at its headquarters in Seongnam, Gyeonggi Province, on the 26th and said it will expand the scope of its industrial quadruped robot business from facility inspection to power plants and public safety.

Clobot is a sales distributor and systems integration (SI) partner for Boston Dynamics. In 2024, it became the first among global partners to receive SI certification, and renewal has been confirmed for next year. It handles sensor configuration for each customer, design of movement routes and inspection scenarios, integration with existing systems, and maintenance.

Spot sales volume this year is expected at about 30 units, with related revenue of more than 10 billion won. According to the company, this is its largest performance on record. Including the business period of its subsidiary Roas, more than 90 Spot units have been supplied over the past four years, and more than 140 projects have been carried out.

Spot is equipped with thermal imaging cameras, acoustic imaging sensors and lidar to detect equipment overheating, gas leaks and abnormal noise. The price of the body including key equipment reaches the hundreds of millions of won. Choi Jin-seok, a director at Clobot, explained that it is comparable to the price of a Rolls-Royce.

Despite the high price, demand is growing quickly. The largest market is facility inspection and predictive maintenance at industrial sites. At POSCO's Gwangyang steel mill, it monitors heat and gas and coolant leaks from equipment around blast furnaces, while at Korea Zinc's Onsan smelter it patrols high-risk areas at night and on weekends. At Korea Western Power's Gimpo Power Generation Headquarters, it collects video and thermal, acoustic and gas data to inspect gas turbine facilities around the clock. At Sejong City's Ieung Bridge, it conducts autonomous day and night patrols over a 1.5-kilometer section and, when a person collapses or a fire is detected, sends on-site video to the city's integrated information center to support 112 and 119 responses. Incheon International Airport Corporation adopted it to remotely verify suspected explosive objects.

Clobot combines its own solutions with Boston Dynamics' monitoring software "Orbit" to provide anomaly alerts, automatic generation of inspection reports, and visualization of robot location and work status. Photos and video captured by Spot are read by "AIVI," a video inspection function that uses Google's robotics AI. If check items for each inspection point are entered in advance in question form, the AI produces answers and the basis for its judgment, and the person in charge compares this with previous records to decide whether an on-site inspection is needed.

The share of customized development is also not small. According to Director Choi, 20-30% of Spot deliveries involve one-off development at customer request. Typical examples include sending alerts through existing monitoring systems or organizing inspection records to match in-house report formats. Clobot is considering bundling functions commonly requested by multiple customers and launching them as a standard software product next year.

Kim Chang-gu, CEO of Clobot, said, "Demonstrating a robot once and installing it at an industrial site for round-the-clock operation are completely different matters," adding, "Until now we have done business mainly centered on factories, but we will expand applications to power plants and the public safety sector." He added, "Having hardware alone does not make patrols possible," and "You must decide where it will go and what it will check, and you also need a monitoring system to check the robot's status and work results."

Clobot has also established a humanoid business division. It is currently carrying out government projects centered on R&D personnel, and next year it plans to use the logistics automation capabilities of acquired Doosan Logistics Solutions (DLS) to conduct proof-of-concept (PoC) validation in distribution and logistics warehouses. Kim explained that because manufacturing plants involve high task difficulty, the company will first verify the possibility of performing simple repetitive tasks in distribution and logistics warehouses. DLS is a company that builds integrated logistics center automation equipment and operating systems.

In its cleaning, guidance and logistics robot businesses, Clobot has supplied externally procured hardware combined with its own autonomous driving software "Chameleon" and its heterogeneous robot integrated monitoring software "Croms." It plans to apply the same model to the humanoid business, selecting robots suited to on-site work and verifying whether they can be linked with existing logistics equipment and operating systems.

Quadruped robots still carry high unit prices and adoption records are concentrated in a small number of industrial facilities, so Clobot's task is to drive repeat adoption. The company expects to secure new customers while also seeing additional adoption by existing customers and expansion to other sites. Some observers say that if standard software products are commercialized, the burden of customized development will ease, which could also help improve profitability.

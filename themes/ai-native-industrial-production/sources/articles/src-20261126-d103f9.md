[page text]
Schematic overview of the formally verified harness setup.

Zapata Quantum
 (OTCQB: ZPTA) and the 
University of Maryland
 (
Joint Center for Quantum Information and Computer Science
) have launched 
ForShor.ai
, an open research challenge combining formal theorem proving with artificial intelligence to optimize quantum factoring subroutines. Developed with early grant support from the Unitary Foundation and scientific advisor Peter Shor, the initiative invites human researchers and AI agents to submit recursive arithmetic schedules to a public leaderboard, where an automated Lean 4 harness verifies mathematical correctness and asymptotic gate complexity before exporting circuits to Qualtran for logical resource estimation.

Serves as a proving ground for formal verification capabilities in Zapata’s Quantum Pilot software platform.

Rather than treating AI-generated quantum circuits as opaque black boxes, ForShor exposes a constrained policy-search interface governing how recursive Toom-Cook multiplication splits bit registers and schedules reversible additions, shifts, and negations. Any submitted policy that passes four kernel-checked conditions automatically inherits machine-checked proofs of Shor order-finding success probability and subcubic gate scaling, avoiding the need to re-prove surrounding modular exponentiation or continued-fraction logic.

By pairing machine-checked correctness with interactive proof maps, open documentation, and a community Discord, the partners aim to ensure that AI-discovered optimizations remain inspectable and reusable across broader fault-tolerant software stacks, including Zapata’s Quantum Pilot platform.

Three Things to Watch as UNC Hosts NC State
The Tar Heels will try to punch their ticket to a bowl game and end the Wolfpack’s season.
by
 
 
Matt Bowling
Nov 26, 2026, 2:00 PM UTC
Thanksgiving is here, which means the college football regular season has reached its end.
It’s been a wild ride for Carolina, with plenty of surprises on and off the field, and the Tar Heels have a chance to keep it going if they can attain bowl eligibility with a win over NC State on Saturday. UNC has lost its last five games to the Wolfpack; a sixth would make it the longest losing streak for the Tar Heels in the rivalry’s 132-year history.
Here are three things to watch in this year’s matchup.
Can the defense bounce back?
Carolina turned in its worst defensive performance of the season last week at Virginia, allowing nearly eight yards per play and getting the Cavaliers to third down just 10 times – half of which they converted. Virginia had eight plays of 20 yards or more, four of which went for touchdowns.
Obviously, UNC will need to have a much better day on that side of the ball to contain an NC State offense that seems to have righted the ship after a disastrous showing against Duke on November 7th. The Wolfpack managed a mere 217 yards in a 41-3 loss to the Blue Devils, but put up a respective 413 and 464 in their next two games, a 34-31 loss to Syracuse and a 28-10 win at Florida State. Third-year starting quarterback CJ Bailey beat the Tar Heels in Chapel Hill as a true freshman and has thrown one interception across his last six outings, so it’s likely going to take a strong effort to get him off his game.
Who gets the start at quarterback?
Two weeks ago, we saw Travis Burgess get thrown into his first college game off the bench when Billy Edwards Jr. went down with an injury against Louisville. Last week, he made his first college start with Edwards listed as a gametime decision (Edwards dressed out and went through warmups but did not play) and, all things considered, 
had a really good performance
.
Edwards was not listed on the injury report for this week’s game, so he’s back to full strength and could resume his starting role if Bill Belichick and co. see fit. As of Wednesday night, the team has not announced who will be QB1 against NC State, and I wouldn’t expect them to before Saturday. It sure feels like an easy decision to keep rolling with Burgess, especially now that he’s gotten his first start – and presumably the nerves that came with it – out of the way. With this coaching staff, though, you can never be too sure.
Can the Tar Heels keep their cool?
You expect emotions to run high in any rivalry matchup, especially at the end of the season, but this one in particular feels like it’s destined to get chippy. Not only is it effectively a playoff game for bowl eligibility with both teams sitting at 5-6, but it also marks the first time former Carolina running back Davion Gause will face his former squad. Hopefully the Tar Heels got something out of their system through their brawl with Louisville two weeks ago, because it’s going to be important for them to avoid getting caught up in extracurriculars on Saturday.

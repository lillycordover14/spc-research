It's that time of year again!
One of our favorite annual traditions here at Keeping It Heel is putting together our Carolina-themed Thanksgiving dinner guest list.
Each year, we extend hypothetical invitations to some notable Tar Heels who have given us plenty to talk about over the past calendar year. Whether it's to celebrate their accomplishments, welcome them to the Carolina family, or simply enjoy some good conversation over a Thanksgiving meal, there's always a reason to pull up another chair.
Before we get started, let's make one thing abundantly clear.
Roy and Wanda Williams have a standing invitation to our Thanksgiving dinner. They don't need a yearly invitation, as there will always be two seats reserved for them at our table.
Now that we've gotten that out of the way, let's reveal who else will be joining us for Thanksgiving dinner in 2026.
Our 2026 Carolina Thanksgiving dinner guest list
Michael Malone and his family
Let's start by extending an invitation to the newest head coach of the UNC basketball program and his family.
While Bridget Malone was already part of the Carolina family, it's the entire Malone family's first Thanksgiving as members, and there's certainly plenty for them to be thankful for.
Not only is 
Malone 
acclimating to life in Chapel Hill, but he's also generating plenty of excitement about the program, especially on the recruiting trail.
We'd love to hear about how the family is settling in, what Malone thinks of the college basketball world, and, of course, some stories from his time in the NBA.
Given how busy he's been on the recruiting trail lately, we might have to make a rule that basketball recruiting-related phone calls are prohibited at the dinner table (well, maybe we'll just let it slide!).
CJ Rosser and DeMarcus Henry
Speaking of recruiting...
How could we possibly put together this year's guest list without including the two prospects who have sent the entire UNC basketball world into a frenzy?
Rosser and Henry have given Tar Heel fans plenty to be excited about, as both talented North Carolina natives have elected to return to their home state to play college basketball. 
Considering the 
magnitude of their respective commitments
, we might have to give these two the biggest plates at the dinner table. We'd also love to hear more about their decision to team up in Chapel Hill and what they hope to accomplish together as Tar Heels.
Cameron Fens
If anyone deserves to sit down, relax and enjoy a Thanksgiving meal, it's 
Cameron Fens.
The big man has had quite the journey to get on the basketball court for the Tar Heels, dealing with an eligibility battle that kept him sidelined at the beginning of the season.
Now that we've finally gotten an opportunity to see him in Carolina blue, there's plenty of reason to be excited about what he brings to this team.
For at least one day, we don't want Fens to have to hear the word eligibility.
In case you were wondering, no one from the NCAA's eligibility department will receive an invitation to our Thanksgiving dinner.
Kate Harpring
The UNC women's basketball program has a special talent in 
Kate Harpring.
The highly touted freshman hasn't taken long to showcase why there was so much excitement surrounding her arrival in Chapel Hill.
As someone from a basketball family, I would be interested in hearing more about her journey to North Carolina, her experiences adjusting to the college game, and her goals for herself and the program moving forward.
If her family joins us for the festivities, I hope her father, Matt Harpring, doesn't get offended when I tell him I watched him play in the NBA as a kid!
Hubert Davis and his family
Just because Hubert Davis isn't working at UNC anymore doesn't mean that he and his family are no longer part of the Carolina family.
Hubert Davis has given so much to the University of North Carolina, whether as a player, an assistant coach, or the program's head coach of the program.
Regardless of how his tenure as head coach ended, Davis' love for the university was never in question.
From leading the Tar Heels to the national championship game in his first season as head coach to the countless relationships he built throughout his time in Chapel Hill, Davis deserves to be appreciated for his contributions to the program.
We'd love to catch up with him, hear about his transition into broadcasting, and reminisce about some of his favorite Carolina memories.
There will always be a place for Hubert Davis within the Carolina family and definitely at our table.
Jelani Thurman
Perhaps no one on this year's guest list better embodies the spirit of Thanksgiving than 
Jelani Thurman.
The standout tight end has put together a tremendous season, earning recognition as a finalist for the John Mackey Award, which honors the nation's top collegiate tight end. Only two other tight ends in UNC football history have ever been named finalists for this award, showing how rare the feat is.
However, it's what Thurman recently did away from the field that makes this invitation especially meaningful.
In honor of his late mother, Kara Braxton, Thurman distributed 45 turkeys to families in the local community ahead of Thanksgiving. The number corresponds to his UNC jersey number, which he wears in tribute to his mother. 
In a year of tremendous personal loss, Thurman has found a way to give back to others and make their holiday a little brighter.
After spending time helping other families enjoy their Thanksgiving dinners, Thurman certainly deserves to have someone prepare a nice meal for him.
Damon Nahas and Carlos Somoano
Who's bringing the hardware to Thanksgiving dinner?
That responsibility belongs to Damon Nahas and Carlos Somoano, who recently led their respective soccer programs to 
ACC Tournament championships. 
It was a historic day for Carolina athletics, as both programs captured conference titles on the very same day.
While basketball and football typically dominate the headlines, the success of UNC's soccer programs deserves plenty of attention and appreciation.
We'd love to hear both coaches discuss their championship journeys and what it meant to accomplish something so special alongside another program representing the same university.
Of course, we'll happily make some room on the dinner table for both ACC championship trophies.
As always, putting together our annual Thanksgiving dinner guest list is a difficult task, as there are so many deserving members of the Carolina family we'd love to spend the holiday with.
If we had enough seats, we'd invite them all!
However, the beauty of being a Tar Heel is that no matter where you're celebrating Thanksgiving, you're always part of a much larger family.

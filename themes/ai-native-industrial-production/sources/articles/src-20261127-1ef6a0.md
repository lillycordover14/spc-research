By R.L. Bynum

Seth Trimble and Ian Jackson spent the 2024–25 season sharing a backcourt in Chapel Hill. On Thanksgiving night in Las Vegas, they shared the spotlight in the wildest game of the young college basketball season.

Trimble poured in a career-high 33 points in 31 minutes off the bench, and No. 25 Louisville built a 24-point lead before holding off Jackson and No. 13 St. John’s 93–90 in the Players Era 16 semifinals at Michelob ULTRA Arena. Jackson led St. John’s with 18 points, including 12 in the first half, and went 10-for-11 from the line. 

The game wasn’t decided until Trimble drove into the lane on the final possession and passed to Adrian Wooley, who hit a corner 3-pointer at the buzzer.

Trimble was nearly unstoppable. He made 12 of 19 shots from the floor and 8 of 11 free throws, adding four rebounds and five assists with no turnovers. He had 16 points by halftime, when Louisville led 57–39.

It was a fitting encore for Trimble, who scored 24 in Tuesday’s upset of No. 5 Texas Tech in his first game back from a minor injury. That gives him 57 points in two games in Las Vegas, both off the bench, and he’s averaging 19.4 points in his final college season.

“It don’t change nothing for us; we know the team we are,” 
Trimble said
, according to WLKY.

Trimble matched the 33 points he scored for UNC in an 84–76 exhibition victory over Memphis on Oct. 15, 2024. It topped his previous career high of 30 points for Carolina last season when the No. 18 Tar Heels beat No. 24 Louisville 77–74 on Feb. 23.

As the Red Storm rallied from a 68–44 deficit with 14:59 left, Jackson was at the center of the chaos in the final seconds.

With Louisville out of timeouts, officials ruled that guard Jackson Shelstad had called one anyway, drawing a technical foul. Jackson calmly made both free throws with 13 seconds left to tie it at 90. Under a rule that left both coaches confused, though, Louisville kept possession, and Trimble found Wooley for the winner.

Louisville coach Pat Kelsey said Wooley “hit a shot that he’s going to tell his grandkids about,” 
according to the Louisville athletics website
.

Jackson, averaging 17.7 points and shooting 53.7% from the floor, suffered his first loss of the season. He and St. John’s (6–1) play Iowa State in the third-place game at 1 p.m. Friday on ESPN2. Trimble and Louisville (5–1) face No. 9 Tennessee at 9 p.m. Friday on ESPN in the championship game.

The loss came in St. John’s coach Rick Pitino’s first game against Louisville since the school fired him in 2017. The 57 first-half points were the most a Pitino team at St. John’s has allowed in a half, but he said the matchup with his former school didn’t stir strong emotions.

“I didn’t have any, to be honest with you,” 
Pitino said
, according to CBS Sports.

Rough Vegas trip for Cadeau, Michigan

A year ago, Elliot Cadeau and Michigan owned Las Vegas. The Wolverines won the Players Era by a combined 110 points over three games, then went 37–3 and won the national championship, with Cadeau named the Final Four’s Most Outstanding Player.

This year’s trip has gone the other way. No. 17 Miami beat Michigan 97–70 in a consolation game on Thursday, two days after Creighton routed the Wolverines 99–73. Michigan has given up 196 points in two games in Las Vegas, lost three straight and fallen to 3–4, its worst start since the 2007–08 season.

Cadeau scored 15 points and had a game-high eight assists, seven of them before halftime, as Michigan built an eight-point first-half lead.

Foul trouble changed that. Cadeau drew his third foul right after halftime and his fourth with 16:52 left, as Miami opened the second half on a 12–2 run. He finished 5-for-20 from the floor, including 1-for-9 beyond the arc, and committed five turnovers.

Michigan also lost starting guard Roddy Gayle Jr. to an apparent ankle injury in the second half, and he left the arena on crutches.

“Our defense just went completely away [in the second half],” 
Michigan coach Mike Boynton said
, according to CBS Sports.

Cadeau is averaging 11.3 points and 6.4 assists, but he’s shooting 32.9% from the floor and 20.0% on 3-pointers, with 4.3 turnovers per game.

Boynton said the Wolverines are still searching for a leader and have a long list of problems to fix.

“It’s a buffet, on Thanksgiving, if you will, of things that need to be addressed,” 
Boynton said
, according to the Detroit Free Press.

Michigan plays Kansas State for seventh place at 5:30 p.m. Friday on ESPNU.

Dixon, Arizona win Maui title

Derek Dixon and No. 8 Arizona (7–1) won the Maui Invitational on Wednesday, beating Ole Miss 84–73 in the championship game. Dixon started and scored seven points in 33 minutes, a day after scoring 12 with three 3-pointers in a semifinal win over Providence.

Lubin posts double-double

Ven-Allen Lubin registered his fourth double-double of the season, with 13 points and 10 rebounds, as N.C. State beat Norfolk State 80–49 on Wednesday. He went 5-for-6 from the floor, including his only 3-point attempt, and added two blocks in 33 minutes. Lubin is averaging 15.3 points and 8.4 rebounds and shooting 61.5% for the 6–1 Wolfpack.

Powell, Pitt unbeaten

Jonathan Powell and Pittsburgh stayed unbeaten, beating DePaul 71–64 on Thursday to win the Beach Division of the Fort Myers Tip-Off. Powell scored seven points in 39 minutes and hit a 3-pointer with 37 seconds left in the first half, giving Pitt a 34–31 halftime lead. The Panthers are 8–0 for the first time in Jeff Capel’s nine seasons.

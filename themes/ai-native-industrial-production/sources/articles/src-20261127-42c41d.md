Romania's 2027 budget based on 1.7-1.8 pct economic growth, nominal GDP of RON 2,227 bln

                                    Category: 
English

Date: 2026-11-27 20:48:07

Foto: 
Cristian NISTOR / AGERPRES Photo

Romania's 2027 state budget must ensure the continuation of deficit reduction while supporting economic recovery and public investment, according to the Report on the State of Public Finances published on Friday by the Finance Ministry.


According to the report, the 2027 budget framework is based on real economic growth of 1.7%-1.8% and a nominal gross domestic product (GDP) of approximately RON 2,227 billion, in line with forecasts by the National Strategy and Forecast Commission (CNSP).


'The construction of the 2027 budget is based on real economic growth of 1.7%-1.8% and a nominal GDP of approximately RON 2,227 billion. The recovery of economic activity, after three years in which growth remained below the European Union average, creates favorable conditions for continuing fiscal consolidation,' the report states.


The Ministry noted that average annual inflation is expected to slow to 4.4%-4.6% in 2027. While lower inflation should help restore purchasing power, it is also expected to moderate the nominal growth of budget revenues compared with 2025-2026, when higher inflation expanded the tax base.


'In this context, improving tax collection efficiency and tax compliance will be decisive for achieving fiscal and budgetary objectives,' the report said.


The Finance Ministry identified maintaining high levels of public investment after the completion of Romania's National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) as one of the main challenges for 2027.


This will require accelerating the absorption of European funds from alternative financing instruments, while prioritising nationally financed projects and aligning them with available budgetary resources.


The report stressed the importance of avoiding a repeat of the situation seen in 2024, when the end of the eligibility period for the 2014-2020 EU multiannual financial framework was followed by a significant contraction in investment spending.


'Avoiding a scenario similar to that of 2024, when the end of the eligibility period of the 2014-2020 multiannual financial framework was followed by a significant decline in investment, is essential for supporting the projected economic growth,' the Ministry said.


The report also pointed to persisting external imbalances, noting that the current account deficit is projected at 7%-7.7% of GDP in 2026 and above 7% of GDP in 2027, signalling continued macroeconomic vulnerabilities.


According to the Ministry, Romania's twin deficits remain a key element in assessments conducted by international rating agencies, while budget deficit reduction remains one of the main tools for correcting these imbalances and reducing reliance on external financing.


'As a consequence, the 2027 budget must ensure the continuation of deficit reduction in a context where macroeconomic factors contribute less to revenue growth and where sustaining public investment requires stricter coordination of national and European resources,' the report states.


The Ministry said policy priorities should focus on improving revenue collection, maintaining fiscal discipline, enhancing the quality of public spending and prioritising investments so that fiscal consolidation remains compatible with economic recovery.

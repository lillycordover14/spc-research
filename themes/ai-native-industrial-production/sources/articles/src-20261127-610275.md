Viktoria Zaytseva’s World Cup Debut Had Roots in Edwards, Colorado
Peter Lange
Nov 27, 2026

Viktoria “Vika” Zaytseva  / GEPA pictures

Viktoria “Vika” Zaytseva 🇺🇸 USA made her World Cup debut at 19 in the Nov. 21, 2026, slalom in Gurgl, Austria. She shared the start list with two 
Stifel
 U.S. Ski Team teammates she’d known since childhood. Twins Liv and Kjersti Moritz grew up in the same Edwards, Colorado, neighborhood as Zaytseva. She had looked up to them when they skied together at 
Ski & Snowboard Club Vail
.

The 
Atomic
 racer is in her second season as a named member of the Stifel U.S. Ski Team’s alpine C Team, a development squad. She earned her Gurgl start in a time trial in Italy. Then her World Cup teammates welcomed her into their training group for a week before the race.

Viktoria Zaytseva USSS

Two Runs Counted for a World Cup Start

The Stifel U.S. Ski Team C was nearing the end of a weeklong camp in Val Senales, also known as Schnalstal, in Italy’s South Tyrol. The skiers had been working on slalom on a steep slope in the lower part of the ski area. On the second-to-last day of camp, the coaches used that slope to decide who would race in Gurgl.

Two skiers took three runs apiece. The team added each skier’s two fastest times. Zaytseva had the lower combined time and earned the spot.

The Teammates Who Settled Her Nerves

Zaytseva’s family stayed home in Colorado. In Gurgl, she leaned on the Moritz twins. Both had raced on the World Cup before and shared how they approached it. Their advice helped settle her nerves. They treated it like any other race.

“Growing up, I remember I always looked up to them because they were older than me,” Zaytseva told Ski Racing Media.

She also talked through the course with teammates she had trained with throughout the previous winter. Some had earned World Cup starts through their results on the NorAm Cup, the North American circuit below the World Cup. Between those familiar faces and the veterans who welcomed her into training, she had plenty of teammates to turn to.

Zaytseva said she’s grateful to everyone who helped make her World Cup debut possible.

Zaytseva felt the adrenaline as she pushed out of the start gate in bib 60. At the finish, what stuck with her was the sound.

“When I went through the finish, everyone was cheering,” she said.

She clocked 55.47 seconds and placed 55th in the first run. Only the 30 fastest skiers advanced to the second run. Liv wore bib 43 and finished 23rd

Karen Ghent and the Mornings That Made Her a Skier

Zaytseva was born in Vail and grew up in Edwards, farther down Colorado’s Vail Valley. Her parents took up skiing as adults. She started skiing with them at Beaver Creek and tried racing after a friend in kindergarten got involved.

Karen Ghent, who also lives in Edwards, coached Zaytseva in the under-10 program at Ski & Snowboard Club Vail. Zaytseva remembers mornings skiing moguls, powder and trees around Vail’s Chair 11. They practiced in gates after lunch. Ghent remembers the Moritz twins also skiing in an earlier U10 group.

A young Viktoria Zaytseva, in pink at the center, poses with her U10 Ski & Snowboard Club Vail teammate, March  2017

“I think a lot of my love for this sport came from Karen Ghent,” Zaytseva said.

Ghent raced for the U.S. Ski Team from 1980 to 1985. She finished fourth in the 1982 World Cup giant slalom in Aspen, Colorado. The FIS record lists her as 
Karen Lancaster
.

Ghent remembers Vika as always game and quick to put a coaching cue into practice.

“I could get kind of technical with her and she would still keep up with me,” Ghent told Ski Racing Media.

From Vail to APEX2100 and the U.S. Team

Zaytseva attended 
Vail Ski & Snowboard Academy
. She then joined 
APEX2100
 in Tignes, France, to train on different hills and in different snow conditions.

She was with APEX2100 when a super-G crash interrupted her season in February 2025. That winter, she had already reached NorAm podiums in both speed events. She took second in super-G at Panorama and third in downhill at Kimberley, both in British Columbia.

The Stifel U.S. Ski Team named her to the C Team for 2025–26. During her comeback, she focused on slalom and giant slalom. She finished third in a NorAm slalom at Owl’s Head, Quebec, and placed ninth in the season’s NorAm slalom standings. In August 2026, she won a South American Cup slalom at Cerro Castor in Ushuaia, Argentina.

What Gurgl Changed and Where She Races Next

Zaytseva spent much of last season rebuilding her confidence after a crash. Racing in Gurgl showed her how far she’d come. She said her biggest takeaway was the motivation to keep improving until racing against the world’s best feels like a normal weekend.

Her priority this winter is the NorAm Cup. She’ll also race on the Europa Cup, Europe’s circuit below the World Cup, when the schedules allow. Her next planned starts are two Europa Cup giant slaloms on Nov. 30 and Dec. 1 in Zinal, Switzerland.

She’s focusing on slalom and giant slalom this season and wants to return to speed racing in the future. She’s eligible for the Junior World Championships this winter and next.

Her focus when she races again is to trust the skills she’s built.

“I know that I have the technical and tactical skills to ski well,” she said. “And so that last part is just having, you know, the confidence to go and execute that.”

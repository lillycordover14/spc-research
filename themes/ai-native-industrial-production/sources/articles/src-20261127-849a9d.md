            A fresh flare up in tensions in the Middle East is putting the brakes on oil’s recent slide.
    

            Front-month Brent crude futures for January delivery have climbed this week to trade around $96 a barrel Friday, after settling Thursday at their highest point since the week of the midterm elections
.
 The global oil benchmark is on track for its first weekly gain this month, rising around 3.5% for the week.
    

            Prices remain far off the highs of near $120 a barrel seen at points throughout the war with Iran. But they’ve bounced back after dipping below $90 earlier this week.
    

            For weeks, oil prices have been falling – in part because US President Donald Trump held back from escalating military action in the Middle East after the midterms. Flows from the region have also picked up, despite attacks on tankers, and global oil consumption has fallen dramatically.

            But a fresh wave of attacks in the Middle East has sent prices climbing again in recent days. US-traded West Texas Intermediate oil futures also climbed Friday, rising 1% to trade around $84.50.
    

            On Thursday, a suspected Iranian drone attack on a Kuwaiti military site 
killed two Italian nationals,
 according to Italy’s foreign minister Antonio Tajani. The individuals were technicians employed by defense firm Rheinmetall Italia. A Kuwaiti national and a Pakistani national were also wounded, Kuwait’s defense ministry said. Iran denied carrying out the attack.
    

            Meanwhile, Yemen’s Houthi rebels 
struck
 two Saudi Arabian oil refineries back-to-back this week – first on Wednesday, when the group claimed to have struck the nation’s biggest oil refinery at Ras Tanura. The following day, a projectile struck an oil refinery in Saudi Arabia’s southwestern Jazan region, killing a Nepalese worker. The strike sparked a fire and 
temporarily suspended some operations at the facility
, according to the Saudi Ministry of Energy.
    

            A skyscraper that houses the headquarters of Saudi Arabia’s trillion-dollar sovereign wealth fund in the capital, Riyadh, was also hit for the second time in a matter of days this week, wounding 11 people, according to an eyewitness and Saudi authorities.
    

            Iran has continued to strike tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Around two dozen attacks have been reported in November, according to a CNN analysis of data from the UK Maritime Trade Operations Centre, nearing the record 30 attacks set in October.
    

Satellite view of thick black smoke rising from a burning Navig8 oil products tanker in the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Oman's Musandam Peninsula on November 8, 2026.

            Oil flows through the strait were at 55% of their pre-war level over the week to November 25, preliminary data from Kpler showed Friday, indicating that the attacks are having an effect on transits.
    

            “These attacks remind us of the inherent instability of the status quo, as a lasting peace deal between the US and Iran remains elusive,” Kathleen Brooks, research director at online broker XTB wrote in a note Friday.
    

            “From a market perspective, a sustained rise in the price of oil… could take the shine off an AI rally later today,” she continued. US stock futures are rising Friday morning, with Nasdaq-100 contracts up more than 2%, following a stretch of 
volatile trading for tech stocks
 earlier in the week.
    

            The front-month Brent futures contract differs from the also closely watched continuous Brent contract, which traded around $92 Friday. The difference reflects the fact that the front-month contract is more closely tied to the physical oil market, and its higher price points to stronger demand for near-term supplies of crude as refiners work to secure barrels.
    

            The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and its allies, known as OPEC+, will meet Sunday. The group, which meets monthly to set production quotas for members, has held oil output steady since September following six consecutive increases before that.
    

            “The group is expected to consider keeping current production quotas unchanged through January, although some members have been pushing for higher quotas,” ING analysts 
said
.
    

            – 
CNN’s Billy Stockwell and Nadeen Ebrahim contributed reporting.

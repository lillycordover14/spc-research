Unseen Twelve Tales: Conker 64 footage and dev materials revealed by Rare co-founder&nbsp;&nbsp;Nintendo Wire

[page text]
Tim Stamper, co-founder of legendary British developer Rare, has revealed a ton of never-before-seen gameplay footage, concept art, and design documents for cancelled platformer Twelve Tales: Conker 64.

The game was the precursor to what would become 2001’s Conker’s Bad Fur Day, a Nintendo 64 title specifically designed for mature audiences — breaking the mold for cutesy platformers of its era with unapologetically adult depictions of violence, profanity, alcohol use, and dark humor.

Stamper’s latest video, which is the first episode of his new “In Tim’s World” series, has him going through a folder full of artwork and in-game renders for Conker 64, mostly dated 1998.

Full of concepts for adorable characters and creatures, the project, at this stage, seemed to lack the adult humor that would later become a core theme of the final game.

 

 

Also shown is footage from a 1998 build of Conker 64 running from a development cartridge, which Stamper says was likely taken with him to an event to showcase the gameplay.

Tim Stamper and his brother Chris were known for being extremely secretive during their days at Rare — a reputation that Tim is now putting to rest with his new series. He intends to explore more old folders and cover other Rare projects from around the same time.

Summary

The conversation covered the structure and challenges of the aerospace and defense supply chain for high precision CNC parts, supplier selection criteria, current pain points, and the relevance of decentralized contract manufacturing networks for critical and non-critical components.

EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Head of Supply Chain and Quality Management at a major aerospace and defense company, with experience in civil and military aircraft, UAVs, ground systems, launchers, and satellites, specializing in procurement and quality control of high precision mechanical parts.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Believes that the traditional, centralized manufacturing and procurement model will remain dominant for critical aerospace and defense components due to stringent qualification and traceability requirements.
- Views decentralized, software-driven contract manufacturing networks as currently more suitable for rapid prototyping, short series, and non-critical parts, with limited penetration in large OEMs.

TAKEAWAYS

- Most high precision CNC parts are procured from suppliers using a build-to-print model, with strict adherence to technical specifications and quality standards.
- Quality, tolerances, supplier capability, cost, lead time, geographical presence, and certifications (such as AS9100, EN 9100, NADCAP) are the most critical supplier selection criteria.
- Current pain points include long lead times, capacity constraints, raw material scarcity (especially titanium), rising labor costs, and difficulty finding skilled workforce.
- The supply chain is moving towards fewer, larger, and more strategic suppliers, with a focus on continuity and loyalty, making it difficult for new entrants to penetrate the market for critical parts.
- Decentralized manufacturing networks like Isembard, Xometry, Protolabs, and Fictiv are seen as more applicable for prototyping and non-critical parts, with limited current relevance for large-scale, flight-critical production.

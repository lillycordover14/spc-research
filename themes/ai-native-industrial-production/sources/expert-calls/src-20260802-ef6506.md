The interview covers the evaluation and use of new contract manufacturers in aerospace, focusing on prototyping and supplier selection criteria. EXPERT BACKGROUND

- Head of strategy in the CTO office at a major aerospace and defense OEM, responsible for technology roadmaps, digital engineering, supply chain transformation, and supplier evaluation, with direct experience in selecting and managing relationships with contract manufacturers for prototyping and R&D.

EXPERT SENTIMENT

- Sees new contract manufacturers like Hadrian as promising future suppliers due to their vertical integration and speed, but currently views them as supplemental rather than critical suppliers.
- Believes that for a supplier to become strategic, they must demonstrate long-term reliability, reproducibility, and regulatory acceptance, which takes years of proven performance.
- Is open to switching suppliers if technical consistency or pricing becomes unfavorable, but technical capability and repeatability are the primary decision factors.

TAKEAWAYS

- Current engagement with Hadrian is limited to prototyping and non-critical parts, with spend representing 2-3% of precision machining and expected to grow only slightly in the next year.
- Hadrian is differentiated by its vertically integrated, end-to-end factory model, which offers faster turnaround (2-3x faster than traditional shops) and potential for future scalability.
- Selection criteria for suppliers focus on technical performance (tolerance, finish, repeatability), supply chain logistics (lead time, cost), and potential for digital integration.
- Hadrian's main limitation is its narrow focus on CNC machining; broader manufacturing capabilities (e.g., sheet metal, additive, finishing) are desired to consolidate suppliers.
- Incumbents remain dominant due to regulatory approval and long-term trust; new entrants must build credibility and reliability over time to be considered for critical program-level supply.

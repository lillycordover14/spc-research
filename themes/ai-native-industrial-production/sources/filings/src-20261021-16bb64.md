Management Commentary
“Reliance delivered an exceptional third quarter with record quarterly net sales and our third-highest quarterly earnings per share. We capitalized on a favorable market environment, characterized by continued healthy demand and tight supply, leading to increased price levels for most carbon steel products. Our long-term strategy of supporting both our valued customers and key domestic mill partners positioned us particularly well to benefit from these dynamics,” said Karla Lewis, President and Chief Executive Officer of Reliance. “Our scale, product diversification, expansive value-added processing capabilities, and ability to source the inventory our customers need, led by the strong execution of our teams in the field, drove our exceptional results. The U.S. Department of Homeland Security border wall contract we were awarded earlier this year continued to ramp and delivered another meaningful contribution to third quarter earnings. Our smart, profitable growth strategy once again produced record quarterly tons shipped, contributing to non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $8.02, up 120% year-over-year.”
Mrs. Lewis continued, “Our strong balance sheet and ample liquidity continue to provide us the flexibility to concurrently invest in our businesses, pursue strategic growth opportunities, and return capital to our stockholders. Looking ahead, we believe Reliance remains exceptionally well positioned as customer optimism remains high and strengthens across infrastructure, semiconductor, general manufacturing, and aerospace. Together, our proven business model, outstanding team, and current market dynamics give us confidence in our ability to gain market share and invest through cycles to continue driving profitable growth and strong stockholder returns.”
Third Quarter 2026 Financial Highlights 
Earnings per share were $7.84 and non‑GAAP earnings per diluted share were $8.02, significantly exceeding management’s guidance of $6.40 to $6.60 and up 120% year-over-year. Results also include $2.74 per share of LIFO expense compared to management’s expectation of $1.10, representing an incremental $1.64 per share negative impact, primarily due to higher-than-anticipated carbon steel product costs. In addition, third quarter earnings include a $0.74 per share contribution from the U.S. border wall project, exceeding management’s expectation of approximately $0.60 per share.
Quarterly tons sold increased 4.3% sequentially, including a 3.7% contribution from the U.S. border wall project. Excluding the border wall project, tons sold increased 0.6% sequentially, exceeding management’s expectation of a 2% to 4% decline. Reliance’s third quarter year-over-year growth in tons sold of 15.6% includes a 9.4% contribution from the border wall project and exceeds the industry-wide increase of 10.1% reported by the Metals Service Center Institute (“MSCI”).
Average selling price per ton sold increased 8.2% sequentially, which includes a 1.7% product mix-related reduction attributable to the U.S. border wall project, exceeding management’s expectation of flat to up 2.0% due to stronger-than-anticipated carbon steel pricing.
Gross profit of $1.45 billion represented the second-highest quarterly result in the Company’s history, as gross profit dollars per ton continued to increase in the third quarter across carbon and stainless steel products and remained historically strong for aluminum products. Gross profit margin of 27.9% decreased 20 basis points sequentially due to increased LIFO expense associated with carbon steel costs rising significantly more than anticipated and product mix impact associated with the U.S. border wall project. Non‑GAAP FIFO gross profit margin, which represents management’s ongoing assessment of operating performance, increased 110 basis points sequentially to 31.6% despite a 60 basis point year-over-year reduction attributable to the U.S. border wall project. The project’s below-average operating costs per ton contributed 40 basis points of year-over-year pretax income margin expansion during the third quarter, despite its dilutive impact on gross profit margin.
Higher shipments and improved gross profit per ton, supported by pricing discipline and continued market share gains, together with the contribution from the U.S. border wall project, drove a 116% year-over-year increase in non-GAAP pretax income to $544.2 million.
End Market Commentary
Non-residential construction 
demand (including infrastructure), Reliance’s largest end market by tons, improved compared to the third quarter of 2025. Strong demand from data centers, energy infrastructure and public infrastructure projects continued to offset softness in various non-residential construction sectors. The Company expects non-residential construction demand levels to remain solid in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to normal seasonality and potential headwinds from supply availability.
Demand across the 
broader manufacturing
 end market Reliance serves improved compared to the third quarter of 2025, supported by continued strength in industrial machinery, shipbuilding, military, and construction machinery sectors. The Company anticipates demand for its products across the broader manufacturing sector will remain healthy in the fourth quarter of 2026 subject to the customary seasonal slowdown.
Demand in 
aerospace 
improved compared to the third quarter of 2025. Reliance anticipates commercial aerospace demand to modestly improve in the fourth quarter of 2026, with gradual build-rate increases and growing backlogs supporting continued improvement. Demand in the defense and space-related portions of Reliance’s aerospace business is expected to remain robust in the fourth quarter of 2026.
Demand for the 
toll processing
 services Reliance provides to the automotive market improved from the third quarter of 2025. The Company expects demand for automotive toll processing to remain at healthy levels in the fourth quarter of 2026. Reliance’s toll processing operations remain flexible and able to quickly adapt to the variable demands of the automotive market.
Demand for certain products Reliance sells into the 
semiconductor
 market meaningfully improved compared to the third quarter of 2025, supported by increasing data center activity. The Company anticipates demand for its semiconductor products will continue to improve in the fourth quarter of 2026.
Balance Sheet, Cash Flow and Stockholder Returns
As of September 30, 2026, Reliance had $246.0 million of cash and cash equivalents and total debt of $1.6 billion, including $495 million outstanding under its $1.5 billion revolving credit facility. Cash flow from operations was $212.0 million in the third quarter of 2026, despite a significant investment in working capital driven by robust shipment volumes and higher metals prices.
The Company returned approximately $109 million to its stockholders in the third quarter of 2026 paying dividends of $64 million and repurchasing $45 million of its shares at an average price of approximately $395 per share. Reliance’s 2026 share repurchases have reduced outstanding common shares by 3% year-over-year. On October 20, 2026, Reliance’s Board of Directors approved an amendment to Reliance’s existing share repurchase plan, increasing the repurchase authorization to $2.5 billion without a set expiration date. Since 2021, Reliance has repurchased $3.4 billion of its common stock at an average price of approximately $236 per share, reducing issued and outstanding common shares by 23%.
On October 20, 2026, the Board of Directors declared a quarterly cash dividend of $1.25 per share of common stock, payable on December 4, 2026 to stockholders of record as of November 20, 2026.
Fourth Quarter 2026 Business Outlook
Reliance anticipates fourth quarter 2026 demand to remain at healthy levels across its key products and end markets, despite ongoing domestic and international trade policy uncertainty and the continued conflicts in Iran and Ukraine, which could pose supply availability and macroeconomic risks.
Excluding the impact of the border wall project, the Company estimates tons sold in the fourth quarter of 2026 will be up 4.0% to 6.0% compared to the fourth quarter of 2025, and, consistent with seasonal trends, down 5.5% to 7.5% compared to the third quarter of 2026. Including an estimated 9.5% year-over-year contribution from the U.S. border wall project, we anticipate tons sold will be up 13.5% to 15.5% compared to the fourth quarter of 2025.
Reliance expects its average selling price per ton sold for the fourth quarter of 2026 will be up 1.0% to 3.0% compared to the third quarter of 2026.
Based on these assumptions, and inclusive of LIFO expense of $112.5 million, or $1.64 per diluted share, the Company anticipates non‑GAAP earnings per diluted share in the range of $7.40 to $7.60 for the fourth quarter of 2026, up over 200% year‑over‑year. This outlook includes approximately $0.70 of earnings per share from shipments associated with the U.S. border wall project at pretax income margin levels above the Company average.

About Reliance, Inc.
With over 85 years of operating experience, Reliance, Inc. (NYSE: RS) is a leading global diversified metal solutions provider and the largest metals service center company in North America. Through a network of approximately 310 locations in 41 states and 10 countries outside of the United States, Reliance provides value-added metals processing services and distributes a full line of over 100,000 metal products to more than 125,000 customers in a broad range of industries. Reliance focuses on small orders with quick turnaround and value-added processing services. In 2025, Reliance’s average order size was $3,120, approximately 49% of orders included value-added processing, and approximately 40% of orders were delivered within 24 hours. Reliance’s press releases and additional information are available on the Company’s website at reliance.com.

Reliance presents certain non‑GAAP measures, including non‑GAAP gross profit, pretax income, net income and earnings per share, to provide meaningful period‑to‑period comparisons of its operating performance. These non‑GAAP measures reflect adjustments for certain items, including impairment and restructuring charges related to the closure or reorganization of certain locations, non-recurring settlement charges, including the resolution of certain customs duty matters, non-recurring debt financing expenses related to a term loan agreement, and gains on the sale of non‑core property, plant and equipment, which can reduce the comparability of GAAP results across periods. 
Reliance uses first‑in, first‑out (“FIFO”) gross profit, FIFO gross profit margin, and other FIFO‑based non-GAAP performance measures to assess its ongoing operating performance and provide a basis for comparison with competitors that do not use the last‑in, first‑out (“LIFO”) inventory accounting method. See footnote 1 for additional information regarding the Company’s gross profit and gross profit margin. In addition, Reliance presents net debt‑to‑EBITDA and total debt‑to‑EBITDA as measures of leverage used by management to monitor debt levels relative to operating performance, for which EBITDA is used as a proxy. Free cash flow is presented as a measure of cash generated by operations that may be used to repay scheduled debt maturities, fund additional growth initiatives, or be returned to stockholders.

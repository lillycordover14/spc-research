Gross profit (calculated as net sales less cost of sales) and gross profit margin (calculated as gross profit divided by net sales) are non-GAAP financial measures as they exclude depreciation and amortization expense associated with the corresponding sales. About half of our orders are basic distribution with no processing services performed. For the remainder of our sales orders, we perform “first-stage” processing, which is generally not labor intensive as we are simply cutting the metal to size. Because of this, the amount of related labor and overhead, including depreciation and amortization, is not significant and is excluded from cost of sales. Therefore, our cost of sales is substantially comprised of the cost of the material we sell. We use gross profit and gross profit margin as shown above as measures of operating performance. Gross profit and gross profit margin are important operating and financial measures as their fluctuations can have a significant impact on our earnings. Gross profit and gross profit margin, as presented, are not necessarily comparable with similarly titled measures for other companies.

Overview
​
Record tons sold and higher average selling prices drove record net sales of $5.21 billion in the third quarter and $13.87 billion for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, up 43% and 28%, respectively, year-over-year. For the third quarter of 2026, tons sold increased 16% and average selling price per ton sold increased 24% year-over-year, reflecting strong underlying demand across most end markets. 
​
In the first quarter of 2026, we were awarded a multi-year contract by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security to provide steel and steel logistics support services for the construction of the border wall along the Southwest U.S. border (the “U.S. border wall project”). Deliveries under the contract began in the second quarter of 2026 and contributed 9.4 percentage points and 4.9 percentage points to year-over-year tons sold growth in the third quarter and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively. 
​
Continuing a multi-year trend of market share gains, our third quarter of 2026 year-over-year growth in tons sold exceeded the industry shipment increase of approximately 10% reported by the Metals Service Center Institute by more than five percentage points. We believe our scale, diversified business model, and customer service capabilities, including delivery and value-added processing, supported these market share gains.
​
Carbon steel prices have benefited from tight supply conditions, solid underlying demand and supportive trade policy, including the 50% Section 232 tariff. Aluminum prices also remain at historically elevated levels, supported in part by the 50% Section 232 tariff implemented in June 2025.
​
Gross profit of $1.45 billion in the third quarter of 2026 was the second-highest quarterly gross profit in our history. Gross profit margin under our LIFO method was 27.9% for the third quarter and 28.3% for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared to 28.3% and 29.2% for the same 2025 periods. Higher carbon steel and aluminum product costs significantly increased our LIFO expense year-over-year. On a FIFO basis, which excludes the impact of LIFO accounting and is how management evaluates operating performance, gross profit margin improved 260 basis points and 80 basis points in the third quarter and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively, compared to the same 2025 periods, due to higher gross profit margins on carbon steel products. Shipments of carbon steel plate and tubing under the U.S. border wall project reduced FIFO gross profit margin by 60 basis points and 40 basis points in the third quarter and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively. Despite its dilutive impact on gross profit margin, the U.S. border wall project was accretive to pretax income margin due to our ability to leverage existing infrastructure and incur lower incremental operating costs.
​
The increases in SG&A expense were primarily due to variable warehousing and delivery costs associated with higher tons sold, increased incentive compensation resulting from higher profitability, higher transportation costs from elevated fuel prices, and wage and benefit inflation. On a per ton basis, SG&A expense increased moderately by 2.4% and 2.3% in the third quarter and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively. The U.S. border wall project generated favorable operating leverage due to its below-average SG&A expense per ton, contributing 40 basis points and 30 basis points to our pretax income margin in the third quarter of 2026 and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively. 
​
Driven by higher selling prices, improved operating leverage, and U.S. border wall project EPS contributions of $0.74 and $1.14 in the third quarter and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively, earnings per diluted share increased to $7.84 (the third-highest quarterly EPS in our history) and $19.21, respectively, up 118% and 64% compared to the same 2025 periods.
​
Operating cash flow in the nine months ended September 30, 2026 decreased $29.7 million to $525.6 million, as a significant working capital increase related to strong shipment volume and higher metals pricing more than offset higher profitability.

Income Tax Rate
​
Our effective income tax rates for the third quarter and nine months ended September 30, 2026 were 24.2% and 24.3%, respectively, compared to 23.3% for the same 2025 periods. The differences between our effective income tax rates and the U.S. federal statutory rate of 21.0% were mainly due to state income taxes partially offset by the net effects of company-owned life insurance policies.

Financial Condition
​
As of September 30, 2026, we had $246.0 million in cash and cash equivalents, and our net debt-to-total capital ratio was 15.3%, compared to 14.4% at December 31, 2025, reflecting increased borrowings under our revolving credit facility. 
​
Cash Flows
​
Net cash provided by operating activities in the nine months ended September 30, 2026 decreased $29.7 million year-over-year to $525.6 million, primarily due to higher working capital requirements associated with stronger shipment volumes and higher metals pricing, which more than offset a significant increase in profitability.
​
Net cash used in investing activities was $215.9 million in the nine months ended September 30, 2026, a decrease of $16.8 million from $232.7 million in 2025. The decrease was primarily attributable to a $19.1 million reduction in capital expenditures. Capital expenditures in both periods included significant investments in growth initiatives.
​
Net cash used in financing activities was $280.7 million in the nine months ended September 30, 2026, a decrease of $106.5 million from $387.2 million in 2025. The decrease primarily resulted from $115.1 million lower share repurchases, partially offset by a $20.0 million decrease in net borrowings under our revolving credit facility. Despite a 4.2% increase in our quarterly dividend rate, effective in the first quarter of 2026, total dividend payments were $194.2 million in the nine months ended September 30, 2026, only slightly higher year-over-year due to the offsetting impact of share repurchase activity.
​
Income taxes paid were $213.6 million in the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to $115.7 million in 2025. The increase was mainly due to higher estimated tax payments as a result of increased pretax income.
​
Liquidity and Capital Resources
​
We believe our primary sources of liquidity, including funds generated from operations, cash and cash equivalents and our $1.5 billion unsecured revolving credit facility, will be sufficient to satisfy our cash requirements and stockholder return activities over the next 12 months and for the foreseeable future. 
​
As of September 30, 2026, we had $400.0 million outstanding under our Term Loan, which matures prior to the September 10, 2029 maturity of our revolving credit facility.

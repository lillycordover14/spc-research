Ryerson Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
Exceeded shipment, sales, and earnings guidance and eclipsed first-year annual run-rate synergy target ahead of schedule
while generating market share gains, productivity gains, and higher book value per share
alongside excellent net working capital management
CHICAGO – October 28, 2026 – Ryerson Holding Corporation (NYSE: RYZ), 
a leading value-added processor and distributor of industrial metals, today reported results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.
Highlights:

Excluding Olympic Steel, which generated $602 million of revenue during the quarter, same-store revenue was $1.58 billion, with sequential average selling prices increasing by 7.3% and tons shipped increasing by 1.8% to reach their highest quarterly level in over five years.

Eddie Lehner, Ryerson’s Chief Executive Officer & Director, said, “Ryerson delivered an excellent third quarter, generating results above our guidance while eclipsing our first-year synergy run-rate target ahead of schedule. These simultaneous achievements reflect the resourcefulness, collaborative efforts, and passion of our teammates as our investments, commercial execution, and integration efforts came together to produce our best quarter of 2026. The strength we saw in daily shipment volumes and invoice counts defied normal industry seasonality, with AI infrastructure and power generation investment elevating overall demand through the quarter. I want to thank and recognize all of my RYZ colleagues for acting with shared purpose and empowering one another to create great customer experiences while translating the strengths of our combined enterprise into growth with greater earnings power. Two full quarters into the merger, our combined positive momentum continues to build further toward the formidable potential we believe our enterprise can realize in the quarters and years ahead.”
Rick Marabito, Ryerson’s President, Chief Operating Officer & Director added, “Resilient customer demand and strong execution across our expanded network drove our highest same-store quarterly shipments in more than five years. We are also aligning customer programs with the facilities best equipped to serve them, which is supporting higher asset utilization and a lower cost to serve while creating capacity for additional transactional business. Commercial synergies continue to exceed our expectations, and progress across our four synergy pillars reinforces our confidence in achieving our two-year target of $120 million in annual run-rate synergies. Third Quarter Results
In the third quarter of 2026, Ryerson generated net sales of $2.18 billion, an increase of 8.6% compared to the previous quarter with total Company tons shipped 1.2% higher and average selling prices 7.2% higher quarter-over-quarter. Excluding Olympic Steel, which generated $602 million of revenue during the quarter, third quarter same-store net sales were $1.58 billion, an increase of 9.3% quarter-over-quarter with average selling prices 7.3% higher and tons sold 1.8% higher. Compared to the third quarter of 2025, same-store net sales increased by 35.7% with tons shipped 14.2% higher and average selling prices 18.7% higher. Third quarter demand was stronger than anticipated while carbon prices continued to rally at multi-year highs supported by trade policy, tight domestic supply, extended lead times, and multi-year lows in service center inventories. Prices for stainless products and aluminum products, in contrast, faced downward pressure due to the easing of nickel supply restrictions, leading to LME nickel price reversion, while aluminum prices remained volatile and trended lower as future supply expectations began to normalize later in the quarter.
Third quarter gross margin contracted by 50 basis points to 17.2%, compared to 17.7% in the prior quarter, as higher material costs flowed through ahead of corresponding price adjustments for program customers. Third quarter results also included a $4.0 million inventory purchase accounting charge to cost of materials sold, a decrease compared to the $15.7 million charge recorded in the second quarter, as we continued to sell through the inventory acquired in the Olympic Steel merger. Reflecting the rising price environment, third quarter LIFO expense was $30.0 million, compared to $17.0 million in the second quarter. Excluding both LIFO expense and purchase accounting adjustments, gross margin contracted by 60 basis points to 18.7%, compared to 19.3% in the second quarter of 2026.
Third quarter total Company warehousing, delivery, selling, general, and administrative expenses were $326.1 million, an increase of 1.6% compared to $320.9 million in the prior quarter, driven by variable compensation and producer inflationary cost pressures. Warehousing, selling, general, and administrative expenses as a percent of sales decreased to 15.0% from 16.0% in the second quarter and 17.3% in the third quarter of last year. On a per ton basis, these expenses were $401 in the third quarter compared with $399 in the second quarter and $413 in the year ago period.
Net income attributable to Ryerson Holding Corporation for the third quarter of 2026 was $32.0 million, or $0.61 per diluted share, compared to net income of $15.5 million, or $0.30 per diluted share, for the previous quarter and net loss of $14.8 million, or $0.46 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2025. After removing the impact of inventory purchase accounting adjustments, an insurance settlement gain, a litigation settlement gain, advisory service fees, and the related income tax effects of these items, Ryerson's third quarter Adjusted Net Income was $32.4 million, or $0.61 per diluted share. Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO was $115.0 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared to $101.0 million in the second quarter of 2026 and $40.3 million in the year-ago period. On a same-store basis, excluding Olympic Steel's contributions, Ryerson generated Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO of $84.8 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $77.5 million in the prior quarter.
Olympic Steel Integration & Financial Results
In the third quarter, Ryerson realized approximately $13 million of synergy benefits through procurement, efficiency, network optimization, and commercial enhancement actions, in-line with management's expectations. This represents synergy attainment of
approximately $52 million on an annualized run-rate basis, substantially exceeding the Company’s $40 million first-year target well ahead of its February 2027 merger anniversary. In the fourth quarter, management expects to achieve synergy benefits in the range of  $14 million to $15 million, which would bring the Company's cumulative realization to approximately $33 million to $34 million in 2026. One-time costs to achieve these synergies have totaled $2.6 million through the third quarter of this year. Given the early attainment of the Company's first-year synergy target and the momentum building across all four of its synergy pillars, management remains confident in achieving its $120 million two-year annualized run-rate synergy target.
Olympic Steel contributed $602.1 million of revenue and $30.2 million of Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO to Ryerson’s third quarter results, exceeding management’s expectations of $21 million to $23 million in Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO.
Liquidity & Debt Management
Ryerson used $18.2 million in cash from operations in the third quarter as net income generation was offset by higher working capital requirements. This compares to a use of cash from operating activities of $5.6 million in the second quarter of 2026. The Company ended the third quarter of 2026 with debt of $1.01 billion and net debt of $966 million, an increase of $51 million and $53 million, respectively, compared to the second quarter of 2026, driven by higher working capital requirements in support of revenue growth.
Global liquidity, composed of cash and cash equivalents and availability on its revolving credit facilities, increased to $865 million as of September 30, 2026 compared to $757 million as of June 30, 2026, reflective of the Company's increased borrowing base from higher working capital.
Stockholder Return Activity
Dividends. 
On October 28, 2026, the Board of Directors declared a quarterly cash dividend of $0.1875 per share of common stock, payable on December 17, 2026, to stockholders of record as of December 3, 2026. During the third quarter of 2026, Ryerson’s quarterly dividend was of the same amount and represented a total cash return to stockholders of $9.8 million.
Share Repurchases and Authorization.
 Ryerson returned $3.8 million to stockholders during the third quarter through the opportunistic repurchase of approximately 159,000 shares in the open market at an average price of $23.85 per share. Following these repurchases, the Company has $96.2 million remaining under its share repurchase authorization, which expires on April 30, 2028.
Outlook Commentary
In the fourth quarter of 2026, the Company expects that shipments will decline sequentially by 6% to 8% from third quarter levels, reflecting three fewer business days and holiday-related seasonal softness. Average selling prices are expected to be flat to up by 2% quarter-over-quarter as higher carbon pricing is expected to slightly offset relative softening in stainless and aluminum pricing. Net sales are therefore expected to be in the range of $1.96 billion to $2.04 billion. Net income generation for the fourth quarter of 2026 is expected to be in the range of $16 to $20 million, or $0.30 to $0.38 per diluted share, with LIFO expense between $28 and $32 million. Fourth quarter Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO is expected to be in the range of $100 to $104 million, inclusive of Olympic Steel's expected contribution of $28 to $30 million.

Note: EBITDA represents net income (loss) before interest and other expense on debt, provision (benefit) for income taxes, depreciation, and amortization. Adjusted EBITDA gives further effect to, among other things, gain on insurance settlement, gain on litigation settlement, reorganization expenses, impairment charges on assets, advisory service fees, foreign currency transaction gains and losses, and purchase consideration and other transaction costs. We believe that the presentation of EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, and Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense, provides useful information to investors regarding our operational performance because they enhance an investor’s overall understanding of our core financial performance and provide a basis of comparison of results between current, past, and future periods. We also disclose the metric Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense, to provide a means of comparison amongst our competitors who may not use the same basis of accounting for inventories. EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, and Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense, are three of the primary metrics management uses for planning and forecasting in future periods, including trending and analyzing the core operating performance of our business without the effect of U.S. generally accepted accounting principles, or GAAP, expenses, revenues, and gains (losses) that are unrelated to the day to day performance of our business. We also establish compensation programs for our executive management and regional employees that are based upon the achievement of pre-established EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, and Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense, targets. We also use EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, and Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense, to benchmark our operating performance to that of our competitors. EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, and Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense, do not represent, and should not be used as a substitute for, net income (loss) or cash flows provided by (used in) operations as determined in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, and neither EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, and Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense, is necessarily an indication of whether cash flow will be sufficient to fund our cash requirements. This release also presents gross margin, excluding LIFO expense, which is calculated as gross profit minus LIFO expense, divided by net sales and gross margin, excluding LIFO expense (income), net and purchase accounting inventory adjustments, which is calculated as gross profit plus LIFO expense (or minus LIFO income) and purchase accounting inventory adjustments divided by net sales. We have excluded LIFO expense (income), net and purchase accounting inventory adjustments from gross margin and Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of net sales metrics in order to provide a means of comparison amongst our competitors who may not use the same basis of accounting for inventories as we do as well as remove the effect of non-cash purchase accounting adjustments. Our definitions of EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense (income), net, gross margin, excluding LIFO expense (income), net, gross margin excluding LIFO expense (income), net and purchase accounting inventory adjustments, Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense (income), net, as a percentage of sales, and Adjusted EBITDA, excluding LIFO expense (income), net, and purchase accounting inventory adjustments as a percentage of sales may differ from that of other companies.

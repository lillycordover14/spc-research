- Report: Deloitte and The Manufacturing Institute workforce study, April 2024 (Deloitte slug: supporting-us-manufacturing-growth-amid-workforce-challenges). Article by Kate Magill, Manufacturing Dive, April 16, 2024.
- Net need: 3.8M new manufacturing workers 2024-2033; ~1.9M could go unfilled if labor gaps persist.
- NAM reproduction of the same study: 2.8M openings from retirements, 760K from industry growth, 230K from recent legislation and regulation.
- Baseline: US manufacturing employment 13M (BLS, Jan 2024); establishments 393K (+11% Q1 2019 to Q2 2023); 234K jobs created so far from BIL/IRA/CHIPS projects.
- Method: five years of job-posting data plus a company survey (size not stated); growth projections 2022-2032; net need 2024-2033.
- Skills: simulation software skill demand +75%; technical roles integrating connected devices grow fastest; industrial machinery maintenance technicians 270K (2022), +16% by 2032; highly technical production roles (semiconductor processing techs, electromechanical assemblers) fastest growing.
- Education: associate degree awards stagnant for a decade; certificate awards grew more than 4x faster 2011-2022.
- Retention: nearly half of respondents said flexible shifts helped retention; competitive benefits ranked most effective lever; nearly all respondents partner with at least one community or education org.
- Quotes: Carolyn Lee (President, The Manufacturing Institute): companies need to "broaden the aperture"; "How do you bring people who are on the sidelines back in and what are the pathways?"

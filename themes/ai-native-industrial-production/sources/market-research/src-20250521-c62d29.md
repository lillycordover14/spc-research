- Report: Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025, IEA, released May 2025 (news release 'Diversification is the cornerstone of energy security, yet critical minerals are moving in the opposite direction').
- Concentration: average top-three producer share for copper, lithium, nickel, cobalt, graphite, rare earths = 86% in 2024 vs ~82% in 2020; nearly all growth from one top supplier (Indonesia nickel, China others). Projected marginal decline over the next decade back to ~2020 levels.
- Refining: China leading refiner for 19 of 20 energy-related strategic minerals; average refining share ~70%.
- Export controls: 55% of covered minerals under some export control. China controls key LFP and sodium-ion inputs (manganese sulphate, phosphoric acid).
- Demand/supply: lithium demand +~30% in 2024; battery-metal supply growth since 2020 twice the late-2010s rate.
- Investment: +5% in 2024 (from +14% in 2023); exploration plateaued in 2024.
- Deficit: copper 30% short by 2035 on current pipeline. 15 of 20 minerals more volatile than oil.
- Scope: 20 energy-related strategic minerals (new); 6 core minerals. IEA critical minerals work since 2021; ministerial mandate March 2022; annual outlook since 2023.
- Quotes: Fatih Birol (IEA Executive Director): 'critical minerals have emerged as a frontline issue in safeguarding global energy and economic security'; 'Even in a well-supplied market, critical mineral supply chains can be highly vulnerable to supply shocks'.

- Report: Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, USGS, Feb 6, 2026; 90 nonfuel mineral commodities; DOI 10.3133/mcs2026. Reliance figures are 2025; import sources are 2021-24.
- Counts: 100% net import reliant for 16 nonfuel commodities; imports > half of apparent consumption for 54. Of the 60 commodities on the Final 2025 Critical Minerals List, 100% reliant for 13 and 50-99% for 20 more (incl. 14 lanthanides).
- 100% list: arsenic, asbestos, cesium, fluorspar, gallium, graphite (natural), indium, manganese, mica (sheet), niobium, rubidium, scandium, strontium, tantalum, titanium (sponge), yttrium.
- 50-99%: gemstones 99; xenon 98; bismuth 92; potash 92; antimony 91; platinum 89; titanium mineral concentrates 88; chromium 79; cobalt 79; silver 77; refined tin 77; rhenium 75; bauxite >75; magnesium metal >75; refined zinc 73; alumina 71; rare earths (compounds and metals) 67; aluminum 60; refined copper 57; palladium 57; germanium >50; lithium >50; silicon >50; tungsten >50.
- Sources: rare earths China, Malaysia, Estonia, Japan; refined copper Chile, Canada, Peru, Mexico; lithium Chile, Argentina; nickel (41%) Canada, Norway, Australia, Brazil; cobalt Norway, Finland, Canada, Japan; graphite China, Canada, Mozambique, Mexico; gallium Canada, Japan, China, Germany; titanium sponge Japan, Saudi Arabia, Kazakhstan; tungsten China, Germany, Bolivia, Vietnam; antimony China, Belgium, Thailand, India.
- China: leading import source for 8 critical minerals with >50% reliance, major supplier for 6 others; leading producer of 20 of 60 critical minerals; 2025 export controls on bismuth, indium, molybdenum, tellurium, tungsten, rare earths; late Oct 2025 suspended controls on five REEs for one year. US reliant on China as a major source for 14 of 33 most import-dependent critical minerals.
- Production: US nonfuel mineral production $112B in 2025 (+5.6% from $106B); metals $38.1B; industrial minerals $73.7B. Mineral-reliant industries $4.09T. Net imports of processed metals and materials $185B (2025) vs $77B (2024). Nevada top state; Arizona second (copper, moly, gold, silver). World lithium production +31%, consumption +20%.
- Quote: Ned Mamula (USGS Director): 'Critical minerals have become central in U.S. Government policymaking and international relations.'

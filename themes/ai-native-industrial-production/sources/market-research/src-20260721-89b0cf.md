- Article: 'IEA warns critical mineral supply risks have become immediate economic threats, 2026 analysis', Skillings Mining Review, Charles Pitts, July 21, 2026 (updated July 22), summarising IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026.
- Refining concentration (early 2026): China ~72% of the global refined energy minerals market; 60-90% of refining capacity for lithium, cobalt and REEs; >90% of rare earth refining. Planned non-China REE refining reaches only two-thirds of required 2035 output.
- Export controls: Chinese controls cover ~3x the mineral tariff codes of 2023; gallium, germanium, high-purity graphite controls implemented 2025; rare earth magnet controls, if fully enacted, put ~$6.5T of annual downstream production outside China at risk. New controls also from DRC, Zimbabwe, Mozambique.
- Investment: total critical mineral project investment -9% in 2025 (lithium and nickel price swings 2024-25).
- Costs/policy: tripling REE prices raises EV cost ~0.1%; stockpiling 11 highest-risk materials costs < $900M/yr; recycling ~20% of supply by 2040 (10% today). Terms: 'Mineral Security Premium', 'supply cliff' late 2020s.
- 2035 table: Copper 42% (China) top-refiner share, 30% shortfall (grids, AI data centers; declining Chile/Peru grades); Lithium 65%, 15% shortfall; Rare earths 90%, 33% refining gap (defense, robotics, wind); Nickel 35% (Indonesia/China), 10% surplus (low grade); Graphite 90%, 25% shortfall.
- Quotes (attributed to the report): 'The risk is no longer just about where the rocks are in the ground'; 'It is about the specialized industrial facilities that turn those rocks into magnets, battery chemicals, and semiconductors'; 'Compared to the potential loss of $6.5 trillion in industrial output' a low-cost mineral security policy is an economic imperative.

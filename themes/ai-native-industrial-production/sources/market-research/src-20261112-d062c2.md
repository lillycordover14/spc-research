summary: 'The 57-page ATI Investor Day deck, which last week''s record lacked. The 2029 revenue bridge from ~$5.2B in 2026E adds base growth plus four announced capacity projects: zirconium sponge +$100M (early 2028), titanium SQ/PQ +$300M (early 2028), nickel remelt and VIM melt +$350M (mid 2028) and nickel powder +$200M (late 2028), for a 10-12% CAGR. Capex runs 5% of sales in 2023-2024, 6% in 2025, ~7% in 2027E-2028E and ~6% in 2029E, and customer-funded capital remains part of the strategy. ATI says customers fund capacity development before ATI qualifies and supplies, and that this funding comes on top of LTA pricing. It claims 5 of 7 sole-source positions in nickel superalloys, 1 of 2 qualified Western producers in PQ titanium, 1 of 3 in hafnium and zirconium, and 1 of 2 integrated US isothermal forgers, the only one supplying all three engine OEMs. 2026E adjusted EBITDA is $1.195B and adjusted FCF $575M, rising to ~$1.25B in 2029E.'

takeaways:
- ATI gets customers to pay for new capacity on top of long-term pricing. That is the financing template a startup forger or melter would have to beat, and it shows how scarce qualified capacity is (Q5, Q9)
- The supplier counts (1 of 2, 1 of 3, 5 of 7 sole-source) put a number on the qualification moat, which is a barrier for entrants and also the gap they aim at (Q9, Q11)
- Capex stays at ~6-7% of sales even through a capacity step-up, so the incumbent adds capacity slowly; lead times stay long and new entrants keep room (Q6, Q14)

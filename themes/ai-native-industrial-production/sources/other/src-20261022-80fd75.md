ENERGY & THE AI POWER BOTTLENECK
AI data centers are making electricity the binding constraint on AI growth, with US data center demand projected to rise from 192 TWh in 2024 to about 650 TWh by 2030. The grid cannot supply this on normal timelines, because
new connections often take years and large transformers take 2.5+ years, so developers need faster power and more flexible load. This creates investable opportunities across the electricity chain: making firm power and its fuel,
moving it with better equipment and software, storing it, and selling flexible capacity. Durable companies may pair hardware with software, because the scarce inputs are physical equipment and grid access, and controlling them
delivers speed that software-only vendors cannot.

INFERENCE & THE ENTERPRISE AI PRODUCTION STACK
Inference - the process of running a trained AI model on new input to produce an output, now makes up about two-thirds of AI computing and recurs with every request, unlike one-time training. Open-weight models, which
companies can run and retrain themselves, move the work of operating models from a few labs to enterprises and their vendors. Large enterprises therefore now need cheaper, faster serving, memory systems that keep context
close to the chip, and models tuned to their workflows, creating investable opportunities across chips, networking, memory, serving software, and customization. Durable companies may own several of these layers, including
hardware, because inference performance depends on how chips, memory, networks, and software work together.

AI NATIVE INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTION
US manufacturers face a labor gap of up to 3.8 million workers by 2033 and supply chains where the top refining country holds a 72% average share of key energy minerals, while defense and AI buildout raise demand. This creates
investable opportunities in producers that own the plant across metals supply, fabrication, precision parts, and materials discovery. Durable companies may be vertically integrated with hardware, because owning the full loop of
sensing, software, and production captures data and speed that software sold into other companies’ factories may not.

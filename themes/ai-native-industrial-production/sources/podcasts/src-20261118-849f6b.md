summary: A 65-minute interview with Ed Mehr. He says Machina moved from selling technology to running its own factories, becoming the supply chain and selling finished parts to customers such as Toyota and Lockheed Martin, because selling the final product is an easier conversation than selling the system. Customers care about price and speed, not AI. Factory equipment is debt-financed and bought only against a customer contract. Equity goes into RoboCraftsman capability (new markets, robot speed), and he says running robots twice as fast halves cost. A part goes through 6-7 months of one-offs and sometimes 2 years of qualification before recurring revenue, then becomes sticky because a prime like Lockheed may spend $20M on its side to qualify a part. He says entrants have to be about 10x better to displace an incumbent supplier. He frames the market as mid-volume products where a dedicated factory costs $200-300M.

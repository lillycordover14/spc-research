The conversation covered the impact of data center growth on U.S. utilities, grid costs, and policy, with a focus on how utilities and data centers are adapting to new demands and the evolving regulatory landscape. EXPERT SENTIMENT

- The expert sees the current U.S. utility and data center landscape as highly dynamic, uncertain, and risk-laden, with utilities struggling to adapt to rapid changes and new demands.
- They believe that policy will increasingly require data centers to bear the full cost of their grid impact, and that collaborative, market-led solutions between data centers and utilities are preferable to top-down mandates.
- The expert expects natural gas to be the primary power source for new data centers in the near term, with significant short-term inflationary pressures on natural gas prices.

TAKEAWAYS

- U.S. utilities operate on a regulated asset base model, historically designed for slow, predictable growth, but are now facing rapid, unpredictable demand from data centers and electrification trends.
- Data centers are driving up grid costs, which can lead to higher utility bills for all consumers; this is influencing both federal and state policy discussions.
- There is no uniform national policy yet; states are taking varied approaches, with some welcoming data centers and others imposing moratoriums or requiring 'bring your own generation' (BYOG) solutions.
- BYOG (behind-the-meter generation) is about six times more expensive than grid power, but is being pursued for speed and reliability, especially by large tech companies; however, supply chain and technical challenges are significant.
- The expert expects most new data center power to come from natural gas over the next 5-10 years, with short-term price spikes likely due to global events, and sees the biggest beneficiaries as integrators who can deliver turnkey power solutions (combining turbines, batteries, and controls) to data centers.

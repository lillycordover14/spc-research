TAKEAWAYS

- Gimlet's approach as a software orchestration layer for heterogeneous AI hardware is interesting, but faces tough competition from established players, especially as companies like NVIDIA expand their stack with initiatives like Vera Rubin.
- NVIDIA's strategy is to control as much of the AI stack as possible, acquiring or partnering with companies to fill gaps, which could make it difficult for independent orchestration platforms to differentiate unless they partner with emerging hardware players.
- The value of disaggregating inference workloads (e.g., splitting prefill and decode across different chips) is significant for customers if it leads to substantial cost savings, but the differentiation must be meaningful (e.g., 50%+ savings) to drive adoption.
- Convincing customers to switch from incumbents like NVIDIA is extremely difficult, even with superior technology, due to entrenched relationships and risk aversion.
- The expert expects only four to five major chip companies to become credible players in the market, with many others likely to be acquired for IP or fail to scale, making the orchestration opportunity dependent on the success and proliferation of these new entrants.

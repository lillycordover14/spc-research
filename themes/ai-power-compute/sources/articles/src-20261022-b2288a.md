Fireworks AI takes 105K sf in San Mateo as Peninsula booms&nbsp;&nbsp;The Real Deal

[page text]
Another artificial intelligence company has 
inked a lease in Silicon Valley
 as the AI boom continues to gobble up office space across the Bay Area. 
Fireworks AI leased roughly 105,000 square feet at 450 Concar Drive in San Mateo, expanding its current office space by nearly five times, the Silicon Valley Business Journal 
reported
. The 210,000-square-foot building was previously 
occupied by cloud data company Snowflake
 before it relocated to a 
larger Menlo Park campus
. 
Fireworks’ headquarters currently span about 21,800 square feet at 900 Concar Drive, about half a mile west of 450 Concar. The lease deal lands as AI and robotics companies drive the strongest office leasing market in the Peninsula since 2018, according to JLL data cited by the Business Times. Newer offices near Caltrain stations, such as 450 Concar, 
have become especially hot commodities
 as demand for space spikes. 
In San Francisco alone, tenant requirements are up to about 9.7 million square feet, up 50 percent from a year ago, according to Newmark data cited by the Business Times. Tech firms make up roughly half of that demand, with AI companies alone comprising roughly a quarter. Silicon Valley, meanwhile, just 
notched its second-busiest quarter
 for office leasing activity ever, with leasing reaching 3 million square feet in the third quarter. Office leasing activity in San Francisco 
approached a 30-year high
 over the summer with 6.4 million square feet leased in the first half of the year. 
Both
 OpenAI
 and
 Anthropic
 surpassed 1 million square feet of office space in San Francisco earlier this spring, and smaller AI companies such as
 Together
,
 Flux, Zyphra
,
 Harvey
 and
 Reflection
 have leased tens of thousands of square feet across the city, usually growing their footprints by significant amounts each time. In Silicon Valley, AI companies like Databricks have been in growth mode, 
leasing up 635,000 square feet
 in Sunnyvale in less than a year. 
— 
Chris Malone Méndez

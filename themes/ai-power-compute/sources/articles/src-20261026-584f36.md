Together AI’s Vipul Ved Prakash is building an AI native cloud for open-source models, with US$1.15bn in bookings and over a million developers
Clients of 
Together AI
 report that they save between six and 60 times by running open-source models rather than closed alternatives. 
Driven by this significant financial advantage, the firm has pushed its annual bookings past US$1.15bn in just four years.
Established in 2022, the company describes itself as an AI-native cloud. It is designed specifically for teams seeking to train, fine-tune and serve open models at scale, supporting architectures such as DeepSeek, Kimi and Nemotron.
Our mission is to ensure that intelligence is abundant, not expensive
Vipul Ved Prakash, Co-Founder and Chief Executive Officer of Together AI
Vipul Ved Prakash, Co-Founder and Chief Executive Officer of Together AI, established the enterprise alongside Ce Zhang and Percy Liang. 

The founders built the organisation on the premise that open models would eventually close the quality gap with proprietary systems, believing businesses would ultimately seek more adaptable and affordable tools to deploy them.

Vipul says: “Our mission is to ensure that intelligence is abundant, not expensive.” This strategic bet has paid off as models from MiniMax, DeepSeek, Kimi and NVIDIA have steadily advanced open-weight capabilities towards the technical frontier.

Supporting inference performance

To cater to these evolving workloads, the platform supports the entire lifecycle of an AI model. 

Software engineers can access hosted models using an API, fine-tune systems on proprietary company data or lease dedicated GPU clusters for large-scale training workloads.

Through this comprehensive offering, Together AI now supports more than one million developers alongside thousands of paying commercial accounts. 

Behind the scenes, its internal research team focuses heavily on inference speed, recognising that low latency is critical for running voice agents, code assistants and other real-time software.

Practical deployment data demonstrates the financial and operational benefits of this performance-led approach. For instance, Decagon reduced its cost per turn sixfold compared with a closed alternative while operating sub-second voice agents.

Similarly, Cursor runs real-time inference with low latency on systems powered by 72 NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 GPUs. 

Salesforce AI Research cut system latency by 50% and lowered operational costs by approximately one-third, while Zomato doubled customer satisfaction scores after deploying a support bot capable of managing more than 1,000 messages each minute.

Global data centre expansion plans
Because infrastructure capacity remains the primary operational bottleneck, Together AI is moving decisively to secure additional power and hardware globally. 
The firm already controls 200MW of power across North America, which includes dedicated data centres in the state of Maryland, US, as well as facilities in Sweden.
Expanding beyond these initial sites, a formal agreement with Humain in Saudi Arabia provides access to 250MW of power and 120,000 computing chips. Humain will deliver the initial 7,000 chips under this initiative by November 2026.
At the same time, European partners Hypertec and 5C Group will supply up to 100,000 NVIDIA Blackwell and next-generation GPUs through 2028.
To finance this aggressive infrastructure growth, Together AI raised US$800m during July 2026 in a Series C funding round. 
Led by Aramco Ventures with capital participation from chipmaker 
NVIDIA
, the transaction valued the business at US$8.3bn – representing an increase of more than 100% across a 17-month period. 
Supported by this fresh capital, Together AI intends to expand its total computing capacity around 50 times over the next five years.
Hyperscaler competition and enterprise adoption
Armed with expanding infrastructure, Together AI is moving beyond competing against fellow technology startups to challenge established hyperscalers for enterprise workloads. 
Its growing commercial portfolio already includes traditional enterprises such as Salesforce and The Washington Post.
In addition to legacy corporations, the business serves fast-growing generative AI firms including Krea, Pika and Hedra. Hedra highlights that Together AI easily absorbs sharp spikes in traffic whenever its creative tools go viral online.
Vineet Khosla
, Chief Technology Officer at The Washington Post, says: “Together AI offers optimised performance at scale and at a lower cost than closed-source providers.”
Vineet Khosla, Chief Technology Officer at The Washington Post. Credit: Vineet Khosla/LinkedIn
By addressing these core enterprise needs, Together AI has turned the broader shift toward open models into a profitable and rapidly expanding infrastructure operation. 

Crucially, the vendor provides enterprise clients with genuine choice over model selection alongside substantially reduced computing bills.

As the market matures, successfully deploying its planned capacity across North America, Europe and Saudi Arabia will ultimately determine whether Together AI can stay ahead of legacy cloud hyperscalers.

I use Google Assistant when I’m driving, planning my day, or trying to remember something before it slips my mind. I can ask it to message someone, find an answer, or remind me about a task without breaking my flow.

So when Google started pushing Gemini as its replacement, I was curious but a little hesitant. The Assistant and I had a good thing going, and I wasn’t sure I needed a change.
 
Still, I decided to ditch Assistant and give Gemini a proper try.
 
I expected smarter answers. What I didn’t expect was how differently I’d end up using my phone’s assistant — and the few things that made me miss the old one.
 

                        I stopped giving robotic commands
               

            Gemini actually lets me talk things through
    

 Google Assistant has always been good at following commands, but I had to be pretty specific. I’d ask it to play a song, call someone, or set an alarm. If I wanted to do 
multiple tasks at once
, I would’ve had to think twice.
 
Gemini feels different because I can explain what I mean instead of sticking to short commands. I can describe a dish I tried at a restaurant and ask Gemini to help me make it at home.
 
If I ask for weekend outing ideas, I can narrow them down by asking for kid-friendly places or options that don’t require a long drive. It feels more natural than treating every request as a separate task.
 
Another thing I appreciate is being able to ask Gemini to explain something in simpler terms. If I come across a technical topic while researching an article, I can ask for an explanation, then request an example to help me understand it better.
 
The biggest change is that I no longer think only in terms of commands. I can start with a half-formed idea and work through it with Gemini, which makes using a voice assistant feel much more useful than I expected.
 

                        Gemini goes beyond voice commands
               

            My apps are part of the conversation

The biggest surprise was discovering that Gemini can do more than answer questions. When I connect with supported Google apps, Gemini can help me work with information I already have instead of making me jump between apps.
 
I use Google Keep to save article ideas and grocery lists. With Keep connected, I can ask Gemini to add an item to an existing list without opening the app myself.
 
It’s also useful when I’m planning a trip. I can ask Gemini to suggest places to visit, group them by area, and build a day-by-day itinerary, so I’m not driving across town between every stop. I can then open the suggested locations in Google Maps to check distances and plan my route.
 
I noticed this difference in Android Auto, too. Google Assistant could already play music, navigate to places, make calls, and send messages. But 
Gemini lets me make more detailed requests
, ask for coffee spots along my route, and follow up on a message without starting over. I can even ask it to search Gmail for my hotel’s address and navigate there, all through voice commands.
 
That’s what makes Gemini feel like a bigger upgrade to me.
 

                        But there’s a catch
               

            Being smarter doesn’t always mean being better
    

 As much as I enjoy Gemini, I understand why some people aren’t ready to give up Google Assistant. The Assistant was great at handling quick commands, and I knew exactly what to expect from it.
 
I also miss how easily I could rely on familiar voice commands. When you’ve used the same assistant for years, you don’t want to relearn 
simple tasks just to get similar results
.
 
Then there’s the smart-home side of things. With Google Assistant, I could say “Hey Google, good morning” and trigger a routine that turns on my lights, gives me the weather and calendar updates, and plays the news. Gemini supports many smart-home controls, but some familiar routine actions aren’t supported, so I’d need to check which parts of my setup still work.

Gemini shines when I want to brainstorm, ask follow-up questions, or explore an idea. But not every request needs an AI conversation.
 
That’s the trade-off: Gemini feels more capable, but Google Assistant’s simplicity still has its place.

            Why should I have to choose?
    

After switching to Gemini, I can see why Google wants it to replace Assistant. I enjoy having a smarter assistant that can help me think through things, but I don’t want to lose the convenience I’ve come to expect from my phone.
 
I don’t think one assistant has to win at everything. I want Gemini when I’m exploring ideas and solving problems, and I want the simplicity of Google Assistant for everyday tasks that don’t need much thought.
 
Ditching Assistant was easy. Replacing it wasn’t.

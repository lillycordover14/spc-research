Simplismart’s $14M Series B
Simplismart raised $14 million in Series B funding led by Dallas Venture Capital, with participation from NVIDIA, Accel, Shastra VC and Micromax, to expand its AI inference platform. 
Amritanshu Jain, co-founder and chief executive officer of Simplismart, said, “The advantage comes from how you run them: what each interaction costs, how quickly the application responds, and whether it stays reliable as demand grows,” as the company positions its software layer between AI models and GPU infrastructure. 
Simplismart said its modular inference engine lets businesses configure how AI models run based on speed, cost and performance, while its orchestration platform manages deployment and scaling across cloud and on-premises infrastructure.

Nvidia’s bet on efficiency
Nvidia backed Simplismart in the $14 million round, and TechCrunch coverage described the deal as an all-primary Series B with Accel, Shastra VC and Micromax joining Dallas Venture Capital. 
Amritanshu Jain told TechCrunch, “Nobody actually needs the fastest or the cheapest inference,” arguing that businesses instead need AI models to run at speeds and costs suited to their specific applications. 
Simplismart said it can optimize how quickly a model responds, how many requests it handles, and how much computing power it consumes, while letting companies deploy through its platform or within their existing cloud and on-premises infrastructure.
Simplismart said it helped AI video-generation platform InVideo cut its GPU serving costs by 56%, and it also works with HeyGen and Higgsfield, as well as enterprise AI startup Ema and Indian cloud provider Yotta. 
TechCrunch reported that Nvidia worked with Simplismart on technical integrations around its Dynamo inference software, with Simplismart engineers and Nvidia solutions architects collaborating on the effort.

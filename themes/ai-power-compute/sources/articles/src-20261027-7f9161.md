Simplismart Raises $14 Mn In Series B Funding Led By Dallas Venture Capital

AI inference infrastructure startup to use funds to expand enterprise platform, cloud-provider partnerships and global operations
Bengaluru-based Simplismart, an AI inference infrastructure startup, has raised USD 14 million in a Series B funding round led by Dallas Venture Capital, with participation from Nvidia, Accel, Shastra VC and Micromax, as it looks to expand its platform and strengthen its presence in global markets.
The company will use the proceeds to develop its AI inference software, expand partnerships with cloud providers and sovereign AI infrastructure players, and grow its business across North America and other international markets.
Simplismart provides software that helps enterprises run AI models on graphics processing units (GPUs) more efficiently. Its platform enables businesses to optimise inference workloads for speed, cost and performance, while allowing cloud providers to offer managed AI services using their existing GPU infrastructure.
The company said its annualised revenue run rate increased 14-fold over the past six months. Its customers include HeyGen, Higgsfield, InVideo, Shiprocket, Apna and Ema, which use its platform for production workloads spanning video, voice, multimodal and enterprise AI applications.
“As frontier-class open models become readily available, access alone is no longer enough to set a business apart. The advantage comes from how you run them: what each interaction costs, how quickly the application responds, and whether it stays reliable as demand grows,” said Amritanshu Jain, Co-founder and CEO at Simplismart.
The company has spent four years developing software to manage the deployment and operation of AI models on GPU infrastructure. The new capital will help it take the platform to more enterprises and cloud providers, Jain said.
Simplismart is targeting the cost and performance challenges associated with running AI applications at scale, as enterprises increasingly deploy models in customer-facing and operational systems.
Simplismart's business model differs from that of inference providers that purchase or reserve GPU capacity and resell computing services. Instead, it supplies the software layer that enables third-party GPU infrastructure to run production AI workloads, positioning itself as an enabler for data centre and cloud operators.
The company works with Nvidia on its cloud-provider offering, which allows infrastructure operators to deploy and manage AI services without having to build their own software stack.
The platform is already being used by Yotta, where it powers the serverless inference layer of Shakti Studio. The service allows customers to run AI models without directly managing the underlying inference infrastructure.
“Inferencing is emerging as one of the biggest game changers in AI economics,” said Dayakar Puskoor, Managing Partner at Dallas Venture Capital.
Simplismart is addressing the need for enterprises to run AI workloads more efficiently while helping cloud providers convert GPU capacity into scalable services, Puskoor said.
The startup also plans to develop a dynamic GPU scheduling layer that can match AI workloads with available computing capacity across regions and time zones, potentially improving utilisation of infrastructure operated by cloud providers.

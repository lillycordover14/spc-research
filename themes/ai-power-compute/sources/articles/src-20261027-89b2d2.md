Nvidia backs Indian AI startup Simplismart in $14 million round

Tuesday, 27 October 2026, 13:00

Simplismart raised $14 million in a Series B led by Dallas Venture Capital, with Nvidia among the investors. The startup’s software helps companies deploy and run AI models more efficiently on their existing computing infrastructure.

The all-primary round also included Accel, Shastra VC and Micromax. It values the four-year-old startup at about $100 million, including the new funding. Companies are spending heavily on GPUs for AI applications, but running models reliably and economically at scale remains difficult, with different workloads requiring trade-offs among speed, cost and performance.

This is reported by 
TechCrunch
.

Simplismart’s software sits between AI models and the infrastructure they run on, adjusting deployment and management to meet customer requirements. The Bengaluru-based company says it can optimize response times, request volumes and computing use, helping businesses get more from their existing GPU capacity. Customers can run models through Simplismart’s platform or on their own cloud and on-premises infrastructure. The company also offers tools for fine-tuning models, scaling resources as demand changes and monitoring performance. Cloud providers can use its software to build and operate AI inference services on GPU infrastructure.

“Nobody actually needs the fastest or the cheapest inference,”

 – Amritanshu Jain

Jain, Simplismart’s co-founder and CEO, told TechCrunch that businesses instead need AI models to run at speeds and costs suited to their applications. He said the company helped AI video-generation platform InVideo reduce GPU serving costs by 56%. Simplismart also works with AI video companies HeyGen and Higgsfield, enterprise AI startup Ema and Indian cloud provider Yotta, which uses its technology for the serverless AI inference service in its Shakti Studio platform.

Simplismart’s annualized revenue run rate has increased 14-fold over the past six months to an eight-figure sum, Jain told TechCrunch. The company said the figure reflects recent customer usage, not contracted recurring revenue, and excludes revenue passed through to infrastructure providers. Jain did not disclose the exact amount. More than half of revenue comes from the U.S., despite the startup having more customers in India, he said.

Nvidia has also worked with Simplismart on technical integrations, including around its Dynamo inference software. The collaboration involves Simplismart engineers and Nvidia solutions architects; Nvidia has also introduced the startup to potential customers and partners, Jain told TechCrunch. Nvidia is the dominant supplier of AI chips, and its investment in software that can help companies run some workloads with fewer GPUs presents an apparent paradox. Jain argued that more efficient inference could make Nvidia’s hardware more attractive and encourage wider adoption of AI applications.

“Where we tend to win is where people have to be more stringent on their SLAs [service-level agreements] and TCO [total cost of ownership],”

 – Amritanshu Jain

Simplismart operates in a crowded AI inference software market that includes Nvidia, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure, alongside startups developing tools to make models faster and cheaper to run. Jain said Simplismart seeks a balance tailored to each customer rather than preset options for faster or cheaper inference.

Jain founded Simplismart in 2022 with Devansh Ghatak, a former Google engineer; Jain previously worked at Oracle. The pair met while studying at BITS Pilani. The startup now has 58 employees, Jain said. It plans to use the new funding to expand in the U.S., grow its team and develop its inference software further.

The funding follows a report by Indian newspaper The Economic Times that Simplismart was in talks to raise $40 million to $50 million at a valuation of $300 million to $400 million. Asked about the report, Jain declined to comment and said the startup was not actively fundraising. He added that the Series B had closed and would not be extended.

A model of high-bandwidth flash (HBF), a next-generation artificial intelligence (AI) memory technology, is on display at SK hynix. /Courtesy of News1
SK hynix said demand for NAND flash is expected to grow as a method of storing and reusing the large volumes of data generated during artificial intelligence (AI) inference on solid-state drives (SSD) spreads. Some AI systems already apply data storage technology using SSDs, and the scope of use is expected to expand by allowing multiple servers and graphics processing units (GPU) to share data, the analysis said.
SK hynix said on the 28th during its earnings conference call for the third quarter, "With the spread of agentic AI and long context, the size of the KV cache is increasing rapidly, and there are capacity and expense limits to storing all of it in HBM and DRAM."
A KV cache stores data that AI has previously processed so it can be reused in subsequent computations. The longer AI performs complex tasks or the more large-scale data it handles, the more the KV cache capacity that needs to be stored increases.
SK hynix said, "A tiered memory structure that stores data across HBM, DRAM, and SSD according to access frequency is becoming more important," adding, "We expect this will create new demand opportunities for NAND to keep and reuse the context needed for AI inference."
It added, "SSDs are already being used for KV cache offload (data transfer) in some AI systems, and we expect their role to expand into a context memory tier in which multiple servers and GPUs share large KV caches."
SK hynix is developing software technology that optimizes data placement among HBM, DRAM, and SSD by judging the reuse value of the KV cache and storage expense to respond to this demand. It is also developing a Compute Express Link (CXL)-based hybrid memory technology that stores the entire KV cache on SSDs while predicting data likely to be needed later and prefetching it into DRAM.
SK hynix has recently been defining the application scope and required performance of SSDs through system demonstrations based on real AI work environments. Based on this, it plans to prepare so that related technologies can be adopted in the AI system designs of customers and platform partners.
In the mid to long term, it plans to respond to a new memory tier between HBM and SSD through high-bandwidth flash (HBF), which combines the large capacity and expense efficiency of NAND with high-bandwidth technology.

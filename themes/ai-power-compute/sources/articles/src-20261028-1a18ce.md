Simplismart Raises $14 Mn In Series B Led By Dallas Venture Capital

AI infrastructure startup Simplismart has raised USD 14 million, equivalent to around Rs 133 crore, in a Series B funding round led by Dallas Venture Capital. The round also saw participation from NVIDIA, Accel, Shastra VC, Micromax and angel investors, as per a media report.
Dallas Venture Capital invested close to USD 5 million in the round. Simplismart plans to use the fresh capital to strengthen its AI inference platform, expand partnerships with cloud providers and sovereign AI players, and enter more international markets, particularly North America.
Founded in 2022 by Amritanshu Jain and Devansh Ghatak, Simplismart develops software that connects AI models with GPU infrastructure. Its inference engine enables businesses to configure model deployment based on speed, cost and performance requirements, while its orchestration platform manages deployment and scaling across cloud and on-premises infrastructure.
The funding will also support the development of a dynamic GPU scheduling layer to match AI workloads with available computing capacity across regions and time zones, co-founder and CTO Devansh Ghatak said.
Simplismart’s Business Model
Co-founder and CEO Amritanshu Jain said the startup offers three deployment and pricing models: shared endpoints, dedicated endpoints and bring-your-own-compute deployments.
Under the shared endpoint model, customers pay based on token consumption without a minimum fee. Dedicated endpoints allow customers to rent GPU infrastructure alongside Simplismart’s optimisation software, with charges linked to GPU usage and runtime.
The bring-your-own-compute model caters to businesses that prefer running workloads on their own infrastructure to meet data security and regulatory requirements. This offering is particularly relevant to sectors such as banking, financial services and insurance.
Simplismart also works with NVIDIA to help cloud providers deploy and manage AI infrastructure at scale. The startup does not own computing infrastructure but provides software that allows cloud providers to offer managed AI services under their own brands using existing GPUs.
Its platform is being used by Yotta to power the serverless inference layer of its Shakti Studio platform, enabling customers to run AI models without managing the underlying inference infrastructure.
Around 55 per cent of Simplismart’s revenue comes from the US, with the remainder generated in India, Jain said. Its customers include HeyGen, Higgsfield, InVideo, Shiprocket, Apna and Ema, which use the platform for video, voice, multimodal and enterprise AI workloads.
The startup said its annualised revenue run rate grew 14-fold over the past six months.
Simplismart had previously raised USD 7 million in a Series A funding round led by Accel in October 2024.
The latest fundraise comes amid increasing investment in India’s AI infrastructure ecosystem. Tata Communications recently announced plans to invest between USD 400 million and USD 450 million in AI-ready connectivity infrastructure spanning 10,000 km across India. The proposed network is expected to connect major data centres and AI hubs to support demand for high-speed, low-latency connectivity.

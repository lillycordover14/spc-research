Simplismart Raises $14M to Expand AI Inference Infrastructure&nbsp;&nbsp;citybiz

[page text]
Simplismart CEO Amritanshu Jain.

San Francisco-based 
Simplismart
 
has 
raised $14 million in a Series B round led by Dallas Venture Capital,
 with participation from 
Nvidia, Accel, Shastra VC, and Micromax
 as the AI inference infrastructure company seeks to reduce the cost of running artificial intelligence models in production, shifting attention from model development toward the computing infrastructure required to deliver AI applications reliably at scale.

Simplismart develops software that sits between AI models and graphics processing units (GPUs), handling model deployment, performance optimization and ongoing operations. Its inference engine evaluates configurations against requirements such as latency, throughput and cost, while its orchestration platform manages workloads across cloud providers, geographic regions and on-premises infrastructure.

“The industry has spent years pushing the limits of what AI models can do. With frontier-class open models now readily available, access alone is no longer enough to set a business apart. The advantage comes from how you run them: what each interaction costs, how quickly the application responds, and whether it stays reliable as demand grows,” said 
Amritanshu Jain
,
 co-founder and CEO of Simplismart.

Jain is an Indian technology entrepreneur. He earned a Bachelor of Engineering degree from Birla Institute of Technology and Science (BITS), Pilani. Before launching Simplismart, Jain worked as a machine learning engineer at Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, developing cloud and machine learning systems, and at Capillary Technologies, where he contributed to machine learning applications and production-scale data infrastructure.

As companies adopt open-weight models and build applications around generative AI, operating costs can become a significant constraint. Simplismart said its software is designed to help enterprises and cloud providers manage GPU resources, balance response times against operating costs, and scale deployments without independently configuring every environment.

The company serves customers in video generation, voice, multimodal applications and enterprise software, including HeyGen, Higgsfield, InVideo, Shiprocket, Apna and Ema.

Simplismart also helps cloud infrastructure companies seeking to monetize computing capacity without building their own inference software. Its model differs from businesses that purchase or reserve GPU capacity for resale because it provides the software layer that lets third-party cloud operators offer managed AI services under their own brands.

Dallas Venture Capital (DVC) is a venture capital firm with offices in Dallas and Hyderabad that invests in software-as-a-service, cloud infrastructure, cybersecurity, data, DevOps, and artificial intelligence. Since 2021, DVC has operated its second fund alongside the DVC India Fund. The firm reports $121 million in cumulative capital deployed and 24 investments, with its investment strategy spanning technology companies serving enterprise markets.

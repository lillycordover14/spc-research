Bengaluru-based AI inference infrastructure startup Simplismart has raised $14 million in a Series B round led by Dallas Venture Capital. Nvidia, Accel, Shastra VC and Micromax also participated. TechCrunch reported that the round was all-primary and values the four-year-old company at about $100 million, including the new funding.
Simplismart builds software that sits between AI models and the computing infrastructure they run on. It tunes how models are deployed so companies can balance response speed, request volume and compute use. It also provides tools to fine-tune models, scale compute automatically and monitor performance. Unlike inference providers that buy and resell GPU capacity, Simplismart does not own its own compute.
 

The company said its annualised revenue run rate has grown 14-fold over the past six months to an eight-figure sum. More than half of its revenue comes from the US, though it has more customers in India.
“Nobody actually needs the fastest or the cheapest inference,” said co-founder and CEO Amritanshu Jain, adding that customers need speeds and costs that make sense for their own applications.
Simplismart’s customers include InVideo, HeyGen, Higgsfield and Ema and Indian cloud provider Yotta uses its technology for the serverless AI inference service on its Shakti Studio platform. The startup says it helped InVideo cut GPU serving costs by 56%,and communications platform Exotel reported a cost per minute reduction of up to 57%.
Nvidia is also working with Simplismart on technical integrations, including around its Dynamo inference software and has helped introduce the startup to potential customers and partners.
Dayakar Puskoor, Managing Partner at Dallas Venture Capital, said inferencing is emerging as one of the biggest game changers in AI economics.
Simplismart plans to use the funds to expand in the US, grow its team of 58 and invest further in its software. It was founded in 2022 by Jain, formerly of Oracle and Devansh Ghatak, formerly of Google.

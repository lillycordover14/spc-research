Christine Lagarde

A.I. split screen 
Judging by the market on Thursday, Microsoft is demonstrating how to excel at the artificial intelligence game.
At the same time, investors are punishing other hyperscalers, especially Alphabet’s Google, as fears grow that their huge bets on A.I. won’t pay off. The contrasting performances could upend the tech-stock rally, analysts warn.
The latest:
Shares in Microsoft were up roughly 1.6 percent as the tech giant disclosed quarterly profit and revenue numbers that surpassed analysts’ expectations. It also forecast 
robust growth for Azure
, its high-margin cloud business that has become a key A.I. revenue driver for the company.
Shares in Alphabet have plunged by nearly 11 percent in premarket trading on Thursday after the search giant reported 
disappointing revenues
 and told analysts that it would continue to spend lavishly on the A.I. buildout next year.
What went wrong at Google?
 Revenue in the tech giant’s core search business increased by 12 percent, to about $63.3 billion. That was the smallest increase in five quarters, Deutsche Bank analysts said, and it hurt the company’s top-line growth.
The company is making strides in A.I. Its Gemini chatbot recently notched more than 
1
 
billion monthly active users
. And its cloud business is growing fast, bolstered by sales of its A.I. chips. But it is finding that its A.I. search offerings are 
generating fewer advertising dollars
 so far.
The size of Google’s A.I. infrastructure buildout also spooked investors, as it pushed the company’s free cash flow deeper into the red, to a $9.1 billion loss, far below analyst expectations.
Shares in Meta, which has been on a 
borrowing and fund-raising binge
 to finance its A.I. ambitions, have also been under pressure.
What went right at Microsoft? 
Unlike Google, its finances are 
looking rosy
 even as it splashes out on A.I. infrastructure. It booked $18.6 billion in free cash flow last quarter, which easily surpassed analysts’ estimates. During the same period, it spent $45.2 billion on capital expenditures, which includes spending on data centers.
“After Meta’s and Alphabet’s numbers revived A.I.-spending anxiety, this is the counterweight,” Jesse Cohen, an analyst at Investing.com, told Reuters.

HERE’S WHAT’S HAPPENING 
Oil dips on hopes for a diplomatic breakthrough.
 Brent crude, the international benchmark for oil, fell below $115 a barrel after The Times and The Wall Street Journal reported, citing anonymous sources, that Vice President 
JD Vance
 had 
contacted a top Iranian political and military official
 to propose the resumption of comprehensive peace talks. The letter, 
reportedly sent
 at 
President Trump
’s direction, underscores his administration’s efforts to end the war, but comes after the White House reportedly warned Gulf states that Washington could attack Iran after U.S. midterm elections.
Weil, Gotshal & Manges prepares to shut down. 
The prominent Wall Street law firm said that its senior leaders had 
recommended dissolving the partnership
 and that its partners would vote on the matter in the coming days. The decision to wind down the firm became increasingly necessary amid an 
exodus of its top-earning partners
 and a failed deal to merge with its rival Willkie Farr & Gallagher.
A key A.I. acquisition for Nvidia reportedly faces Justice Department scrutiny. 
The agency has made inquiries about the effect Nvidia’s roughly $13 billion agreement to buy Hugging Face — a platform that hosts artificial intelligence models from many companies — would 
have on competition in A.I.
, Reuters reports, citing unnamed sources. The department has reportedly also examined Nvidia’s other acquisitions, like that of Groq, an A.I. chip start-up, for signs of anticompetitive behavior, even as Trump has embraced the company and its C.E.O., 
Jensen Huang
, as one of the country’s leading A.I. champions.
DoorDash will buy Instacart. 
DoorDash said that it had agreed to buy its food delivery rival for 
$17 billion in cash and stock
, building a potential juggernaut in the fast-growing market. The offer price for Instacart would be an 11 percent premium over the company’s closing share price on Wednesday.

Warsh’s October surprise 
The Fed gave many investors what they wanted on Wednesday when it left its benchmark 
lending rate unchanged
.
But 
Kevin Warsh
, the Fed chairman who has vowed to bring “
regime change
” to the central bank, threw a curveball a few minutes into his news conference. The central bank, he said, soon planned to 
alter its meeting schedule
. The move would upend decades of tradition and potentially result in fewer policy votes.
It’s not a huge change, but it already has Fed watchers buzzing. Investors on Wednesday seemed to view it as a dovish move, as the S&P 500 ticked higher after Warsh announced the move.
What’s new? 
The
 
Fed will stick with its eight-meeting schedule. But beginning next fall, one of those meetings will be dedicated to discussing strategy. In that meeting, Warsh anticipates, no policy decisions will be made. In 2028, he said, there is likely to be six policy meetings and two strategy meetings.
Jason Furman
 and 
Mohamed El-Erian
, economists who’ve also criticized the Fed for being slow to fight inflation, 
welcomed
 
the change
 in social media posts.
But it has also set off a guessing game as to which meeting next year would be a no-vote affair.
The early consensus is the October meeting
, which comes just before Election Day.
 
At the very least, such an arrangement would potentially spare the Fed chair from having to field questions about politics being a factor in a decision to move or hold on rates.
Warsh got that very question on Wednesday from reporters. 
“Politics,” he responded, “played no role in the decision.” 
The other bit of intrigue came in Wednesday’s vote — a 10-to-2 decision.
 (That left the benchmark rate at a range of 3.75 percent to 4 percent.) The two dissenters were 
Beth Hammack
, the Cleveland Fed president, and 
Lisa Cook
, a Fed governor whom Trump has tried to fire.
Hammack is a vocal inflation hawk. Cook, though, is thought to be one of the more dovish members of the rate-setting committee. That’s left some Fed watchers wondering if Cook’s vote was a political statement, or whether she thinks the Fed is behind the curve.
“Cook’s dissent was a surprise,”
 Ruchir Sharma, an economist at Nomura, wrote in a research note. But, he added, it potentially reflected her “concerns about A.I.-driven inflationary pressures highlighted in the latest C.P.I. report.”
Traders seem to be aligned with Cook’s thinking.
 They give a roughly 80 percent odds that the Fed will raise rates at its next meeting in December.

Scoop: Kalshi lands a $40 billion valuation 
If you were betting on Kalshi securing another $1 billion in venture capital before the end of October, you can cash in your winning contract.
The prediction market announced on Thursday that it had closed a new round of funding that valued the company at $40 billion, Lauren McCarthy 
is first to report
.
This round was led by Sequoia Capital and Wellington Management. Baillie Gifford, Morgan Stanley Investment Management, Coatue Management and Andreessen Horowitz also participated.
Riding high:
 The $40 billion valuation solidifies Kalshi’s position as a dominant player in the booming prediction market business.
“What it means to us is we get the resources and the capital to build all the things that are on our product road map that our customers, both retail and institutional, want us to build,” 
Tarek Mansour
, a founder of Kalshi and its C.E.O., told DealBook. “Or the things that we have vision for.”
An I.P.O. could be next. 
The financing round is widely expected to be the start-up’s last before an initial public offering, but Mansour declined to comment on when or if that might happen, beyond noting that “there’s been discussions.”
Kalshi’s value is now nearly double that of its main rival.
 The 
company’s closest competitor
, Polymarket, was negotiating a fund-raising round 
led by 1789 Capital
, 
Donald Trump Jr.
’s venture capital firm, The Times reported, that gave it a valuation of $21 billion. (The president’s son is also a paid adviser to Kalshi.)
It’s finding growth in new areas.
 Analysts say Kalshi’s current valuation reflects investors’ perception of potential growth beyond sports betting, which at present is around 80 percent of its volume.
“Our revenue, outside of sports, is now over a billion dollars,” Mansour said, adding: “Crypto has been growing faster than sports. Commodities have been growing faster than crypto.”
But there are legal headwinds. 
Opponents of prediction markets — including dozens of state attorneys general — say that they are indistinguishable from gambling and should be subject to existing gambling laws.
While more than a dozen states have gone to court to argue that prediction markets 
should be subject to state laws
, Kalshi maintains that they fall under the regulatory purview of federal authorities
Was Nvidia the target of a spy plot? 
The high-stakes battle between the U.S. and China to dominate artificial intelligence has taken a new plot twist.
The Times reports that Washington has been 
surveilling a top executive
 at a Singapore-based data center company. That action ultimately revealed that the executive had developed ties to 
Jensen Huang
, the C.E.O. of Nvidia, the chipmaker at the heart of America’s A.I. ambitions.
The executive is 
Alice Huang
 or 
Huang Le
, a Chinese citizen who U.S. officials have concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset passing information to Beijing.
More from The Times:
For years, Washington has worked to deny China the technology needed to match or surpass the United States in the intense global competition for superiority in artificial intelligence. The discovery that Beijing was getting reports from someone with direct access to Mr. Huang, one of the most powerful figures in the A.I. revolution as well as an influential ally of President Trump’s, set off alarms at the White House.
Roughly a year ago, Mr. Trump was briefed about the intelligence findings, which have not been previously reported, and chose not to do anything with the information. Mr. Trump has been wary of disrupting Nvidia’s chip sales, which are helping to drive the economy and the stock market, and of further complicating the trading relationship with China.
Huang, the Chinese businesswoman, did not respond to The Times’s requests for comment.
Nvidia described the ties as a normal business relationship. John Rizzo, a Nvidia spokesman, told The Times that “Jensen’s interactions with Ms. Huang are not unusual or unexpected.”

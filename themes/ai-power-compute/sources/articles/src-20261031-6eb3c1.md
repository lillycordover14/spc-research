Google Scores Big Win in Quest to Put Data Centers in Space&nbsp;&nbsp;PCMag

[page text]
Google has just hit 
a big milestone
 in its “moonshot” project to put functional solar-powered data centers in space. The tech giant has now received its first-ever AI response from one of its 
Tensor Processing Unit
 (TPU) chips launched into low Earth orbit.
During a ground-station pass, Google researchers prompted the company’s 
Gemma 4
 open-source AI model, asking it to generate a haiku about being in space. As the satellite passed by Svalbard, Norway's Arctic archipelago, it was able to beam back a short poem: 
“Blue marble below
 Data streams in silent grace
 Starlight feeds my core.”

The news comes after the first prototype satellite for 
Project Suncatcher
 launched on Oct. 1, in collaboration with satellite imaging company Planet, using SpaceX's rideshare service.
Google says that proving its TPUs “can reliably execute workloads in space” paves the way for the next step in its plan of having twin interconnected satellites operational in 2027. 
Google has been open about the 
immense technical challenges
 of putting AI data centers in space. For example, things like cosmic radiation and solar storms can wreak havoc on sensitive electronic devices, not to mention issues like cooling data centers without normal airflow and having data centers in orbit communicate with each other effectively.
The tech giant has acknowledged that scalable AI compute in space “won’t happen all at once” and that these early launches are about “seeing what works, identifying points of failure, and applying those findings to future missions.”

Google isn’t the only tech giant eyeing space as the next frontier for AI compute; SpaceX has made some exceptionally bold claims about the future of satellite data centers. In January 2026, SpaceX sought FCC approval 
to launch up to one million satellites
 as part of 
plans
 to create a network of orbiting data centers around Earth to produce “unprecedented computing capacity to power advanced artificial intelligence.” Meanwhile, Amazon and Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos is also predicting massive "gigawatt" data centers in space 
within the next two decades.
Google also has bold plans ahead, 
telling
 the 
New York Times
 last month that it has designs for fleets of more than 80 satellites that will fly in close formation and communicate with one another. Google is also reportedly meeting with designers to potentially create a custom satellite “the length of a soccer field.”

What Rock Matrix Released

The card describes EdgeRock as Rock Matrix’s “fixed-memory 
model
 family” and EdgeRock-4B as “the first open-source release”. The 
model
 reads text and images and generates text for reasoning, coding and agent workflows.

It converts the eight full-attention layers of the Qwen3.5-4B backbone and keeps the 24 Gated DeltaNet layers. Recurrent state summarises the context, a local window of 1,024 positions handles recent tokens, and what the card calls Rock Memory Anchors keep up to 1,024 selected distant records per KV group.

On reasoning, the card reports a mean of 82.9% on AIME 2024 to 2026, against 86.1% for Qwen3.5-4B, which it describes as retaining 96.3% of the backbone’s score. Among other bounded-memory models it tested, the best AIME mean was 18.8%, for RWKV-7 G1j, which the card places in the 14B class.

What the Fixed Cache Buys on One GPU

On one Hopper 80GB GPU, the card reports EdgeRock-4B serving 512 requests at once with 32K-token inputs and outputs, at 12,124 output tokens per second. Qwen3.5-4B’s best measured batch was 64 requests, at 1,889 tokens per second. That is the basis of the card’s 6.42 times throughput claim.

For a single request with 128K tokens in and 128K out, the card reports 233.7 decode tokens per second for EdgeRock-4B and 151.3 for Qwen3.5-4B.

A Licence That Reserves the Chip

The weights carry the EdgeRock Model License 1.0, which permits commercial use, paid APIs and fine-tuning with no 
revenue
 or user threshold. Its “Sole additional restriction” is that users may not, without written authorisation, “physically hardwire” the weights into a chip’s non-reprogrammable 
structure
.

That is the route Rock Matrix describes for itself. Its card lists a SiliconRock line that would “Build main weights into circuits and keep fixed-capacity state in on-chip SRAM”, targeting one million tokens per second for a single user. The runtime code is released under Apache-2.0.

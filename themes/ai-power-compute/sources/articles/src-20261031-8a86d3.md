Hermantown City Council passes all 6 resolutions on Google’s proposed data center project

By WDIO

After 4 hours of public comment, and around an hour of discussion, including conversations with representatives from Google and Minnesota Power, the Hermantown City Council unanimously passed 6 resolutions surrounding Google’s proposed Data Center project on Friday.
This is the third time the meeting has been scheduled, as it was canceled on Monday after being deemed an unlawful assembly by Police Chief Jim Crace.

The resolutions on the meeting agenda included:
Citizen Petition For An Environmental Assessment Worksheet (EAW) For the Harmony Group Data Center Project
Special Use Permit For Construction Of A Communication Services Facility (Data Center) In A Business And Light Manufacturing Zoning District And Imposing Conditions Thereon
Preliminary And Final Plat Of Hermantown Industrial Addition And Imposing Conditions On The Final Plat
A Development Agreement And Memorandum Of Understanding With Harmony Group LLC For The Hermantown Data Center Project
Authorizing And Directing The Mayor And City Clerk To Execute And Deliver A Cooperative Agreement Between St. Louis County And The City Of Hermantown For Roadway And Utility Improvements 
Approving Reuse And Decommissioning Agreement With Harmony Group LLC And Google LLC.
Public comment started around 6:30p.m. and went until around 10:30p.m. Only 85 people were allowed inside the building and were able to speak during the meeting’s public comment period. Others were able to attend the meeting virtually, and protestors also gathered outside of City Hall in the designated safe pedestrian zone.
According to Hermantown officials, the council chambers allow 100 people inside to comply with fire code. The 85 people in attendance do not include the city councilors, media, or law enforcement. 
Many of the people who spoke during the public comment period were against the project, including local legislators. 
“I just know that we cannot sit back in our hands and allow these broligarchs to come in to make decisions that are going to reshape our economy, our entire workforce, our environment, our democracy, and our shared future. In Minnesota, our air, our water, our kids, our democracy are not for sale. They belong to the people. This whole process has been shrouded in secrecy in NDA after NDA,” said Representative for House District 8B Liish Kozlowski.
“I am worried about the risk that you are signing us up for. It is against your constituents’ wishes. It comes really down to a fundamental question. Is government authoritarian? Do you know better or are you here to represent? I have hope for Hermantown. But I realize today that my hope does not come from any one of you. It’s coming from after you’re done,” said Hermantown City Council candidate Candi Mock.
Other legislators and political candidates, including Trina Swanson (Candidate for U.S. House District 8) and Mary Carlson (candidate for Minnesota Senate District 11), were in attendance both inside the council chambers and outside in the pedestrian area. 
There were also multiple speakers who supported the project. 
“This agreement demonstrates to me personally that Google wants to be a responsible and good neighbor. Related investments and jobs are here for the long term. This is in addition to the economic benefits and increased tax revenue to help support the city and school. Reducing pressure on existing taxpayers while creating a stronger and diverse tax base for the city and the region,” said Hermantown resident Eric Palmer. 
“What we’ve done tonight, it’s not about AI, it’s not about big tech, it’s not about software, it’s not about acts of violence, it’s not about pine island projects. It’s about a project here in Hermantown, a land use application and agreements there are associated with that. We’ve done an awful lot of work to get to this point, so it has gone on with a lot of responsibility, and I thank the rest of the council for sticking in there with it,” said Hermantown City Councilor John Geissler
Hermantown City Council will meet again on 
Monday November 2nd at 6:30p.m.

Google is completely turning off Search in schools that reject its AI features, the company 
told 
The New York Times
. The decision was also shared in 
a broader education-related update
 from Google on Tuesday, saying that access to Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search “will be entirely off” for K-12 student accounts unless “administrators explicitly turn them on.”
Administrators will not be given the option to toggle Gemini-powered features off without disabling Search, however. Chris Phillips, Google’s vice president of education, told the 
NYT
, “Search and AI are deeply integrated,” adding that schools that disable Google’s AI features “will not have access to Search, and they’ll see a message from their school that indicates it’s not available.” Google didn’t immediately respond to 
The Verge
’s request for comment.

The announcement comes just months after Google 
turned its Gemini AI assistant
 on by default inside the Google Classroom app, which is used by 
more than 150 million
 teachers and students globally. The move added to skepticism surrounding Google’s introduction of AI in schools amid reports of 
declining test scores
 and other impacts on 
students’ learning abilities
. Last month, 
lawmakers pressed Google
 on the “potential harms” of putting its AI products in schools. Although Google is no longer turning on AI features in schools by default, this choice comes at a cost.
The company is also attempting to address concerns about students using its AI features to find “shortcuts” instead of solving problems themselves. Google is embedding its Guided Learning feature, 
which is supposed to help students work through problems
, into its AI Overviews in Search. Google will begin testing this feature in select US schools this month.

 run rate,” the company wrote. Kleiner Perkins, Lux Capital, NVIDIA, Blackstone, Shopify Ventures and HubSpot Ventures are among the other investors it lists in the round.

Applied Compute’s reported valuation at each round: $100 million at its $20 million seed in June 2025 (Upstarts, citing sources), $1.3 billion post-money in April 2026 (company, via Latham & Watkins) and $3.25 billion in November 2026 (company post). The Business Behind the Run Rate

Applied Compute builds custom models for companies on top of open-weight models. “We started Applied Compute because we believed companies would want to own and improve the AI powering their products and operations,” the post says.

It gives two customer results. For Harvey, it says it trained a 
model
 that improved answer and citation 
quality
 “at less than half the inference 
cost
 of a frontier alternative”. For Clay, it says a custom router cut production inference costs “by up to 47% without sacrificing 
quality
”.

The company says customers have run “more than 1,700 training runs” on its AC2 
platform 
since July, that its inference service serves more than 1 trillion tokens a day, and that 40% of customers use it for both training and inference. Its team has tripled since January, and 85% are researchers and engineers, it says.

From $100 Million to $3.25 Billion

In June 2025, Upstarts reported, citing sources, that Applied Compute had raised $20 million in a round led by Benchmark partner Victor Lazarte, valuing it at $100 million. It named the founders as Rhythm Garg, Linden Li and Yash Patil, all three recent Stanford students who worked on technical projects at 
OpenAI
.

In April 2026, Applied Compute announced $80 million at a $1.3 billion post-money valuation, led by Kleiner Perkins with Elad Gil among the participants, according to Latham & Watkins, which represented it. The 2 November post puts the new valuation at $3.25 billion; it does not say whether that is pre- or post-money.

By our arithmetic, the new round of $350 million is 3.5 times the $100 million run rate, and the valuation rose 2.5 times in about seven months.

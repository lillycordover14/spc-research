OpenRouter Pricing: 5.5% Fee vs Together, Fireworks [2026]

 

Tom Vance

Updated Nov 4, 2026 · 24 min read

 

 

OpenRouter pricing works on a pass-through model. The platform bills each request at the underlying provider’s list price, then collects its fee when you buy credits: 5.5% on the Standard plan and 8% on Business, with a $0.80 minimum per purchase. Together AI and Fireworks AI skip that middle layer and sell tokens straight from their own GPU fleets. On the open models both clouds host, the standard rates match to the decimal. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash costs $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens at either one.

That overlap changes the buying question. 
OpenRouter’s documentation prices every model per token, per request or per unit
, so the true cost of the routing layer is the credit fee plus whatever the router does with your traffic. Together AI and Fireworks AI compete on the edges instead: speed tiers, batch discounts, dedicated GPUs, fine-tuning, and where your data gets processed.

The timing matters. Meta’s 
Muse Spark Open
 landed on Together AI on October 15 and on Fireworks AI on October 20, and OpenRouter rebuilt its default provider routing on October 28. Each event changed what a million tokens costs in practice. The gap between sticker price and invoice is where these three platforms differ most.

OpenRouter Pricing: Credits, the 5.5% Fee and BYOK

OpenRouter runs on prepaid credits. You buy dollar-denominated credits, manually or through auto top-up, and each request deducts its cost from the balance. The company states that inference is billed at the provider’s list price on every plan. Its fee lands when you buy credits, never on individual requests, which makes the fee easy to model but easy to overlook on a first read of the pricing page.

The Standard plan charges 5.5% on credit purchases, and Business charges 8%. Crypto payments carry a flat 5%. A $0.80 minimum applies to the card fee, which means small purchases pay more than 5.5% in effect. Teams that buy credits in small, frequent amounts quietly pay a higher effective rate than the headline fee suggests.

Token prices are identical on both paid plans. OpenRouter’s FAQ names the credit fee as the only billing difference between Standard and Business, so any reason to pick Business has to come from account controls rather than cheaper tokens. On a $1,000 monthly credit purchase, the gap between the two plans is $25.

How Per-Model Prices Appear in the API

The model endpoint returns prices as per-token strings. A prompt price of 0.0000025 and a completion price of 0.00001 mean $2.50 and $10 per million tokens, so multiply by 1,000,000 before you compare anything against a Together or Fireworks page. The same object carries cache-read and cache-write fields, and the docs describe tiered pricing where a model charges $2.50 per million input tokens normally and $5 beyond 200,000 prompt tokens. Read the tier thresholds before you send long prompts.

Bring Your Own Key

On Standard and Business, the first $25,000 per month of list-price inference through your own provider keys carries no OpenRouter fee. Usage above that line costs 5% of the underlying model and provider cost, and only the overage is charged. Users can also opt in to prompt and completion logging in exchange for a 1% discount, a trade many security teams will decline.

Routing Changes That Move the Bill

On October 28, 2026, OpenRouter 
rebuilt its provider routing
. The new default algorithm evaluates endpoints continuously and balances cost, speed and reliability instead of mostly picking the cheapest provider. Secondary coverage reportedly puts the resulting cost increase near 6%, though that figure does not come from OpenRouter and the sources checked could not confirm it. For tool-calling requests, Auto Exacto re-evaluates providers every 5 minutes across throughput, tool-call telemetry and benchmark scores, and it is on by default.

On October 30 the company added Private Endpoints for Enterprise customers, along with organization-level limits that separate workspace defaults from company-wide budgets. If cost predictability matters, pin a specific provider in your request rather than accepting the default. The 5.5% fee is a known quantity. An unpinned router is not. For a sense of the savings you are routing toward, 
DeepSeek’s $0.30 input price against Claude’s rates
 shows how wide the open-versus-closed gap has become.

Together AI Pricing: Serverless, Dedicated and Post-Training

Together AI’s serverless API bills by usage, with no minimums and no provisioning cost. Chat, language, embedding and rerank models all charge by input and output tokens. 
Together’s model catalog
 lists Kimi K3 at $3.00 input and $15.00 output per million tokens, and OpenAI’s GPT-OSS 120B at $0.15 and $0.60. Meta’s Muse Spark Open sits at $1.25 input, $0.15 cached input and $4.25 output. DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 costs $1.32, $0.13 and $3.96 on the same three lines.

Smaller models run far cheaper. NVIDIA’s Nemotron 3.5 Super VL charges $0.15 input, $0.02 cached and $0.50 output, and Qwen3.5 9B charges $0.17 input and $0.25 output. Together says Muse Spark Open carries a 1-million-token context window on both serverless and dedicated infrastructure, which makes long-context jobs a natural fit for the cached-input line.

Regional processing costs extra. Point your client at the US endpoint, us-api.together.ai/v1, and every input, cached input and output rate multiplies by 1.25. That is a 25% premium, and it applies to the models Together lists for US-only inference, including Kimi K3, GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. The company has also documented the OpenAI Responses API at /v1/responses, with billing that matches Chat Completions for serverless inference.

Dedicated Model Inference bills by deployment runtime instead of tokens or requests. An H100 80GB runs $5.49 per hour, 
according to an analysis of Together’s business
, and a B200 is reportedly $8.99. Compare the hourly rate against your token volume: at DeepSeek V4.1 Flash’s $1.20 per million output tokens on serverless, a dedicated GPU only pays off when it stays busy.

Post-Training, launched October 19, bills reserved GPU time rather than tokens. Together’s own example uses four trainer GPUs and four generator GPUs on B200-SXM at $11 per GPU-hour, which comes to $88 per hour. A promotional rate, reportedly $9 per B200 GPU-hour for the first eight weeks, stays valid until December 14, 2026. Supervised fine-tuning prices also dropped, reportedly to $0.34 per million tokens for Qwen3.5-9B and $1.05 for Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma 4 31B.

One more product deserves a flag. TogetherLink lets coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode run on Together’s open models, and Together claims it cuts model spend by over 50%. That is a vendor number, not an audited one. For the licence side of those models, the guide to 
open-weight licence terms across six families
 tells you what you may deploy commercially.

Fireworks AI Pricing: Standard, Priority, Spot and Batch

Fireworks advertises pay-per-token usage from $0.05 per million input tokens, with no commitment. The base rates track Together’s closely, but Fireworks stacks several tiers on top. Standard is the default. Priority raises the price in exchange for faster, more reliable capacity. Spot cuts 30% off Standard on best-effort capacity for select models. Batch cuts 50% off input and output for jobs that do not need an immediate answer.

The Priority premium is not a flat markup. Working from Fireworks’ published rates, it adds 25% on Ember-1, Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, 20% on GPT-OSS 120B, and 50% on Muse Spark Open and Qwen 3.8 Max. Spot works the other way. The page warns that throughput can drop to zero, so retry or fall back to Standard. Kimi K3 under Spot lands at $2.10 input and $10.50 output, and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash lands at $0.21 and $0.84.

Caching is where Fireworks pushes hardest. Its pricing page says cached input costs half or less than uncached input, and session affinity keeps each agent on the server that already holds its context, with long runs reaching cache hit rates of up to 99.6%. On DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, cached input is $0.006 against $0.30 uncached, a 98% discount. If your agents resend the same long prefix every turn, that line item dominates the invoice.

Regional rules follow Together’s pattern with a bigger premium. US-only serverless on select models carries a 50% premium, and Fireworks waives it on Muse Spark Open. Fireworks also trains: fine-tuning starts at $0.50 per million training tokens, and the Serverless Training API bills per million tokens you prefill, sample and train, with repeated prefixes billed 80% off. Fireworks documentation reportedly bills LoRA adapter inference at the base model’s serverless price, with no separate charge for loading or idle time.

For raw GPUs, On-Demand pricing is reportedly $13 per hour for a B200 180GB and $15 for a B300 288GB. That is well above Together’s reported $8.99 for a B200, though the two companies may bundle different software, so do not read it as a like-for-like gap. Anyone choosing between hosted Qwen and Meta’s model can start from the 
Muse Spark Open versus Qwen3.8 agent benchmark
 before pricing either one here.

Six Real-World Cases From the Last Eight Weeks

Pricing pages describe intent. These events show how the three platforms behave when something changes.

Day-zero hosting of Muse Spark Open.
 Together announced the model live on October 15 with a 1-million-token context window. Fireworks followed on October 20 on US serverless capacity and waived its 50% US-only premium for the model. A buyer who needed US processing paid 25% more on Together and nothing extra on Fireworks.

OpenRouter’s routing rebuild.
 On October 28 the router moved from price-first selection to a balance of price, latency, reliability and retry cost. Teams that never pinned a provider saw their effective rates shift without a pricing-page change. Secondary coverage reportedly put the increase near 6%, unconfirmed by OpenRouter.

Together pruning its serverless list.
 A PricingSaaS tracker reported that by October 26 Together had dropped four serverless models: Gemma 4 31B, DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731, Llama 3.3 70B and MiniMax M2.7. Anyone hard-coding a model ID needs a fallback plan, because catalog entries disappear on a few weeks’ notice.

Together’s Post-Training launch.
 On October 19 Together introduced reinforcement learning and supervised fine-tuning for open-weight models including Muse Spark Open, GLM 5.3, Kimi K3 and Qwen 3.8-27B, with a promotional GPU rate valid through December 14.

Fireworks’ Forge announcements.
 On November 3 Fireworks reportedly introduced an Ultrafast inference preview, multi-day prompt caching, a background mode, higher rate limits and added European capacity on NVIDIA Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell platforms. Multi-day caching, if priced like today’s cached input, would stretch the discount the table above shows.

ElevenLabs on OpenRouter.
 On October 7 OpenRouter added nine text-to-speech models and two speech-to-text models from ElevenLabs, with a 50%-off introductory promotion that ran through October 19. That promotion has ended, so current pricing is the standard rate. The move shows the router expanding past text.

One more case sits upstream of all of these. DeepSeek recently opened its own inference stack, a move that pressures every third-party host on price, as 
the DeepInfer coverage
 explains. If the original model maker undercuts hosts on its own weights, expect the platform price floor to keep sliding.

Reliability, Regional Processing and Vendor Risk

A cheaper token means little if the endpoint falls over. The three companies publish different assurances, and none of them is directly comparable. Together describes its reserved inference capacity as committed, with token-based pricing, reserved throughput and a 99% uptime SLA. Fireworks lists throughput SLA and 99.9% uptime terms. OpenRouter’s pitch is failover across providers, which spreads risk but adds a hop. OpenRouter also published a post about outages on February 17 and 19, 2026, a reminder that a routing layer can fail even when the providers behind it stay up.

Ownership is the second risk. The New York Times reported on August 19, 2026 that 
Stripe agreed to buy OpenRouter for a reported $7.5 billion
, noting that the companies had not disclosed the price. A secondary account said the deal had not closed by October 19, and the sources checked show no completion announcement. If you rely on OpenRouter, watch for changes to fees and terms after any closing. Nothing in the official pages reviewed confirms a rename, a shutdown or a new fee schedule.

Use-Case Recommendations

Match the platform to the shape of the traffic, not to the brand. Here are six common profiles and the platform that fits each.

Prototyping across many models.
 Pick OpenRouter. One key and one balance reach 400+ models, and the 5.5% fee on a $200 experiment budget is $11. Switching models costs a string change, not a new vendor contract.

High-volume production on one open model.
 Go direct to Together or Fireworks. On DeepSeek V4.1 Flash the standard rates are identical, so the 5.5% OpenRouter fee has no offsetting benefit once you know which model you run. At $5,000 a month, that fee is $275.

Agents with long, repeated prefixes.
 Choose Fireworks. Session affinity, cache hit rates up to 99.6% and a cached-input price of $0.006 on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash make it the cheapest place to resend context, and the Forge caching changes may extend that edge.

Nightly and bulk jobs.
 Use Fireworks Batch at 50% off or Spot at 30% off. The Kimi K3 example drops from $1,200 to $600 on Batch. Add retries for Spot, because throughput can fall to zero.

Custom models and reinforcement learning.
 Look at Together Post-Training for GPU-time billing, with the promotional window open until December 14, 2026, or Fireworks fine-tuning from $0.50 per million training tokens. Pick Together if you want to rent B200 hours, and Fireworks if token-based training pricing fits your dataset size better.

Strict US processing.
 Pick Fireworks if your model is Muse Spark Open, since the premium is waived. For other models, Together’s 25% premium is smaller than Fireworks’ 50%. Check the per-model US rates before you sign, because both vendors list them by model.

Migration Guide: Moving Between the Three

All three platforms expose OpenAI-compatible chat endpoints, so most migrations touch a base URL, an API key and a model ID. The traps sit in model naming, caching behavior and rate limits.

Inventory your model IDs.
 OpenRouter uses a provider/model format. Together uses IDs such as openai/gpt-oss-120b. Fireworks uses its own naming, which you should copy from its catalog instead of guessing.

Pull current prices into code.
 Keep a small price table with input, cached input and output rates per million tokens, dated and sourced, and compute cost from each response’s usage object.

Check regional settings.
 If you need US-only processing on Together, change the base URL to the US endpoint and expect the 1.25x rate. On Fireworks, confirm whether your model is among the select US-only models.

Replay a sample.

import os
from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI(
    base_url=os.environ["LLM_BASE_URL"],
    api_key=os.environ["LLM_API_KEY"],
)

# USD per 1M tokens: input, cached input, output (copy from the vendor page)
PRICE = (0.30, 0.006, 1.20)

def cost_usd(usage):
    details = getattr(usage, "prompt_tokens_details", None)
    cached = getattr(details, "cached_tokens", 0) or 0
    fresh = usage.prompt_tokens - cached
    return (
        fresh * PRICE[0] + cached * PRICE[1] + usage.completion_tokens * PRICE[2]
    ) / 1_000_000

resp = client.chat.completions.create(
    model=os.environ["LLM_MODEL"],
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "Summarize this ticket in two lines."}],
)
print(resp.choices[0].message.content)
print(f"cost: ${cost_usd(resp.usage):.6f}")

Verdict: Which One to Pay in 2026

The data points to a split decision with a clear default. If you already know the model you will run, go direct. Together AI and Fireworks AI charge identical Standard rates on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash ($0.30 in, $1.20 out), Muse Spark Open ($1.25 in, $4.25 out), Kimi K3 ($3.00 in, $15.00 out) and GPT-OSS 120B ($0.15 in, $0.60 out). OpenRouter would add 5.5% to the same tokens, or $23.10 on a $420 month and $66 on a $1,200 month.

Between the two clouds, the winner depends on traffic shape. Choose Fireworks when you run agents with long repeated prefixes, need Batch at 50% off or Spot at 30% off, or process Muse Spark Open in the US. Choose Together when you want dedicated H100 time at $5.49 an hour, plan post-training before December 14, or need the smaller 25% US-only premium on other models. Where a model appears on only one cloud, that fact settles it.

OpenRouter earns its fee in one situation: when switching models is the product. If your team compares ten models a week, the single key and balance save engineering time worth more than $11 per $200 of spend. Once usage stabilizes on one or two models, move the steady traffic to a direct contract and keep OpenRouter as the experimentation and failover lane. That hybrid carries the lowest regret across the scenarios priced above.

One caution applies to everyone. Prices here come from vendor pages dated late October and early November, and new open releases and the Forge announcements are already shifting the floor. Re-check the cached-input line and the regional premium before you sign a commitment, since those two lines move the invoice more than the headline rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does OpenRouter mark up token prices?

No. OpenRouter says inference is billed at the provider’s list price on every plan. Its revenue comes from a fee on credit purchases, 5.5% on Standard and 8% on Business, with a $0.80 minimum.

Is Together AI cheaper than Fireworks AI?

On shared models, no. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Muse Spark Open and GPT-OSS 120B carry the same Standard rates on both. Differences appear in tiers, caching, regional premiums and GPU pricing.

How much does Fireworks Priority cost over Standard?

It depends on the model. From the published rates, Priority adds 25% on Kimi K3, Ember-1 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, 20% on GPT-OSS 120B, and 50% on Muse Spark Open and Qwen 3.8 Max.

What does bring your own key cost on OpenRouter?

Nothing for the first $25,000 per month of list-price inference on Standard and Business. Above that, OpenRouter charges 5% of the underlying model and provider cost.

Which platform is best for US-only data processing?

For Muse Spark Open, Fireworks, because it waives its premium on that model. For other models, Together’s 1.25x multiplier undercuts Fireworks’ 50% premium, though both vendors list US rates per model, so verify the exact line.

Can I use Batch and Spot together?

The sources checked do not say whether the discounts stack, so assume they do not. Price each separately: Batch at 50% off and Spot at 30% off Standard.

Does Together AI charge extra for US-only inference?

Yes. Pointing a client at the US endpoint, us-api.together.ai/v1, multiplies the standard serverless rate by 1.25 for input, cached input and output tokens. That is a 25% premium, and the per-model US rates sit on Together’s documentation.

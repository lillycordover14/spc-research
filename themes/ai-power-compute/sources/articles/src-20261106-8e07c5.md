Google announces $17bn data center campus in Cheyenne, Wyoming

Set to be powered via fuel cells and gas generation, coupled with CCS

November 06, 2026

By

Zachary Skidmore

         
            Have your say
        
    

Google has formally announced its first data center in Wyoming.
The search giant revealed plans for the data center in Cheyenne, Laramie County, as part of a $17 billion commitment in the Cowboy State. While the company did not disclose the data center's capacity, earlier reports suggest that it could total up to 2.7GW.

– Getty Images

As part of the investment, the company has partnered with Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power (AEP), and Related Digital to develop the facility.
The data center will be powered by a 702MW fuel cell project with carbon capture and storage (CCS), under a Power Purchase Agreement signed with AEP 
last month
. The fuel cells will run on natural gas, and the CCS technology will seek to capture the associated CO2, which will be sequestered and stored at Tallgrass’s nearby Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub. Bloom Energy will supply the solid oxide fuel cells, which will be owned and operated by AEP.
The data center will also offtake power from gas generators operated by Tallgrass Energy and Black Hills Energy. Google said that it is currently conducting a technical front-end engineering and design (FEED) study to evaluate fitting Tallgrass’s combined cycle turbines with CCS technology. The company has committed to paying for 100 percent of the power it uses for the data center.
“Together, these efforts represent a novel approach to powering data centers efficiently, accelerating the commercialization of CCS, and creating pathways to reduce the carbon emissions associated with natural gas generation,” said Steve Walter, VP, Global Infrastructure and Energy.
The data center is expected to utilize an air-cooled system to “minimize strain on local water resources,” added Walter.
Google was revealed to be the company behind the planned campus in 
July
. The campus will be developed on a 716-acre parcel located eight miles south of Cheyenne in the Switchgrass Industrial Park.
The application suggests that four data halls will be built, along with an office hub, logistics, and network buildings. Construction is slated to be completed by 2031.
Project Jade was previously being developed by Crusoe. At the time, it was said to be a 1.8GW campus that
 could scale to 10GW
, but in June, Crusoe said it had 
paused work
 on the site "at the request of our customer."
According to local reports at the time, the campus is now being developed by Google's subsidiary Jupiter Star Holdings, LLC, replacing Crusoe.
Google claimed that the development will provide significant benefits to the local community, with the company recently hosting a community meeting in downtown Cheyenne. Google said it expects the project to generate hundreds of millions of dollars in local property taxes for Laramie County and School District 1.
Cheyenne is the capital city of Wyoming and is home to several large projects. Microsoft broke ground on a campus in Cheyenne in
 August 2025
, and is seeking to purchase a further
 3,200 acres of land
. 
Meta
 also has a large-scale data center campus in the area. In October 2025, Related Digital broke ground on a 
$1.2bn
 data center project for CoreWeave.

Applied Compute Raises $350M at $3.25B as Run Rate Hits $100M in Under 18 Months&nbsp;&nbsp;Wowtale

[page text]
Companies that rent AI models from OpenAI or Anthropic watch costs pile up, and the knowledge only their business has never makes it into the model. So more are turning to open-weight models, whose underlying weights are published for anyone to download, and retraining them on their own data to build AI that is theirs. The hard part is that training the model, serving it in production, measuring it and improving it over time is a lot of work. A startup that does that job for companies has passed a $100 million annualized revenue run rate in under 18 months.

Applied Compute
 has 
raised $350 million
 at a $3.25 billion valuation. Elad Gil led the round, with participation from Kleiner Perkins, Lux Capital, Hanabi Capital, NVIDIA, Blackstone, Shopify Ventures, HubSpot Ventures, Diffusion, Definition, Timeless, Layer Global, Neo, Abstract, Nova, Quantum Light, First Harmonic and Swish.

Run rate is the company’s current monthly revenue multiplied by 12. It describes the pace of revenue today, not revenue booked over a year. Applied Compute says it went from zero to $100 million in less than 18 months, and that the number of enterprises it serves has more than doubled since its last raise. These are the company’s own figures, not audited revenue.

Applied Compute retrains open-weight models on a company’s data and ways of working, an offering it calls specific intelligence. Reinforcement learning, in which a model is scored on how well it does a task and tuned accordingly, is at the core. The company embeds engineers with customers to build the models and then runs them on its own cloud, working from open-weight models such as Alibaba’s Qwen and Moonshot’s Kimi. CEO 
Yash Patil
 has claimed its models can be up to 10 times cheaper than relying on OpenAI or Anthropic in some cases.

It shared customer results. For legal AI company 
Harvey
, it 
trained a model
 for the Review Table product that improved answer and citation quality at less than half the inference cost of a frontier alternative. Inference is the work a finished model does when it produces an answer. For 
Clay
, it 
trained and deployed a custom router
, which sends each request to a suitable model, cutting production inference costs by up to 47% without sacrificing quality. Neither result has been independently verified.

Microsoft, Shopify, Mercor, Harvey, DoorDash, Cognition, Clay and Decagon use Applied Compute, and its customers now span life sciences, cybersecurity, hardware design, legal, financial services, logistics and more. NVIDIA is reported to have also hired it to post-train the chipmaker’s open Nemotron models.

Training a better model is only part of the problem, the company says, since customers also have to deploy it, evaluate it and keep improving it. Three months ago it introduced AC2, the Applied Compute Agent Cloud, to bring training, inference and continual improvement into one platform. Customers have run more than 1,700 training runs on AC2 since July. AC Inference serves more than 1 trillion tokens a day, tokens being the units of text an AI processes, and 40% of customers now use Applied Compute for both training and inference. Training, inference and routing are the first pieces of a larger stack, the company says, covering the models and the systems that run, monitor and secure them.

All three founders came from OpenAI. Patil worked on Codex. 
Rhythm Garg
 was a core contributor to the o1 reasoning model, and 
Linden Li
 worked on ML infrastructure for reinforcement learning training. Forbes reported they met at Stanford and left OpenAI together about five months after DeepSeek’s R1. The team has tripled since January, and 85% are researchers and engineers.

Funding has come fast. The company raised a 
$20 million seed
 led by Benchmark, then 
$80 million in October 2025
 from investors including Benchmark, Sequoia and Lux as it came out of stealth. In April it 
raised another $80 million
 at a $1.3 billion post-money valuation, led by Kleiner Perkins with Elad Gil, Lux, Greenoaks, Neo, Hanabi and others. The new valuation is about 2.5 times that. The money will go toward compute capacity, hiring and faster enterprise deployment, and Forbes reports most of it will buy chips to grow the company’s cloud.

Competition: turning open models into company assets

The market for customizing and serving open-weight models for enterprises is crowded with platforms that cover both training and serving. 
Fireworks AI
 raised a $1.5 billion Series D in July at a $17.5 billion valuation and has passed $1 billion in annualized revenue, letting customers train and serve custom models on their own data. 
Together AI
 is an AI-native cloud built around open-source models, and raised $800 million in a Series C at an $8.3 billion valuation.

Model developers are going after the same ground. Former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati’s 
Thinking Machines Lab
 offers a customization platform, Tinker, that bills customers for the compute they use to adapt models on their own data, and is reported to have passed a $100 million annualized run rate. 
Reflection AI
 unveiled an open-weight model, Beam, aiming at the Western open-source frontier. Applied Compute stands apart by selling custom training and embedded engineers as a single package.

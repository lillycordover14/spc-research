[page text]
Xiaomi’s LLM-Core team says a new attention 
design
, HySparse2, cuts the KV-cache memory a 1M-token context 
needs
 to 2.69 GB, against 12.09 GB for the Hybrid SWA 
design
 used in its MiMo-V2 series. That is a 4.5x reduction by our arithmetic. The figures are in a paper the team posted on arXiv on September 22 (
arXiv:2609.26368
).

model
 writes anything. The research firm SemiAnalysis drew attention to the paper on X on November 7 and said Xiaomi MiMo is moving to the 
design
 for its next-gen models; the paper itself does not say that.

What the Paper Measures

The tests use 80B-A3B mixture-of-experts models, meaning 80 billion parameters with about 3 billion active per token. Each 
model
 has 49 Transformer layers, and the models differ only in their attention designs, the paper says.

The paper compares three designs: HySparse2, its predecessor HySparse, and “Hybrid SWA used in MiMo-V2 Series”. Measured with FP8 KV-cache storage at 1M tokens, “The KV cache of HySparse2 occupies only 2.69 GB, compared with 6.72 GB for HySparse and 12.09 GB for Hybrid SWA”.

On prefill compute at 1M tokens, the paper reports reductions of 2.92x against HySparse and 5.02x against Hybrid SWA. On 
quality
, it says HySparse2 improves mean MRCR-v2 and RULER-v2 scores over HySparse by 11.30 and 19.81 percentage points.

The research firm SemiAnalysis posted on November 7 that, after DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Xiaomi MiMo is moving to a causal encoder-decoder for its next-gen models, citing HySparse2. The post gives 25 of 49 layers, 5x less prefill compute and a 4.5x smaller KV cache at 1M context versus MiMo-V2; this piece checks those figures against the paper, which does not itself say which future MiMo 
model
 will use the 
design
.

How It Skips Half the Model

The 
design
 splits the 
model
 into a self-decoder and a cross-decoder, and builds the cross-decoder’s KV caches from the self-decoder’s hidden states. “Prefill can therefore exit after the self-decoder, skipping all cross-decoder layers”, the paper says.

In a serving setup that splits prompt reading from text generation, the paper says, the prompt-reading node 
needs
 only part of the 
model
. For the 49-layer 
model
, the paper says, “this requires deploying only the first 25 layers, cutting the memory requirement of the prefill node by nearly half”.

The paper also tested the bridging step at a larger 290B-A8B 
scale
, trained on about 1.8T tokens at 32k context, and reports that “Models with and without KV Bridging achieve broadly comparable 
quality
”.

Why Memory Is the Cost Line

The paper frames the problem around agents: “Long-horizon and multi-turn agents typically generate short actions and process long observations from tools and environments”. Every token of that context sits in the KV cache while the 
model
 works, which ties up accelerator memory per conversation.

By our arithmetic from the paper’s figures, HySparse2 saves 9.40 GB per 1M-token context against Hybrid SWA, and the memory one Hybrid SWA cache occupies would hold four HySparse2 caches with room to spare. That is a storage comparison only; the paper does not give serving prices or throughput in dollars.

The Structural Read

As we read the paper, the gain comes from not running half the 
model
 while reading a prompt: the cross-decoder’s caches are built from the self-decoder, so prefill stops after the first 25 of 49 layers.

As we read it, the lever is memory per conversation: the paper frames the problem around agents that generate short actions and process long observations, and every token of that context sits in the KV cache.

As we read the evidence, this is a research result on 80B-A3B test models; the paper does not say which 
product
 will use it, and the next-gen claim is SemiAnalysis’s.

Fireworks AI’s Second Breach

The attackers did not break into Fireworks AI head-on. They came through a third-party cloud provider that hosted the company’s research machines. In a 
notice to customers on November 9
, the AI inference platform said the October 24 intrusion reached billing records and, for a limited set of accounts, stored secrets, datasets and incomplete model weights.

Image credit: Freepik, free license

Key Takeaways

A cloud provider hosting Fireworks’ research machines was breached on October 23, and a day later the attacker used that foothold to reach Fireworks development machines.

Exposed data includes account and billing details, plus stored secrets, datasets and partial model weights for some customers; payment card numbers were not stored or affected.

The intrusion follows an earlier incident, identified on October 6, in which misused internal credentials exposed customer API keys.

How the Attack Unfolded

Fireworks says the cloud provider suffered a security incident on October 23. On October 24, the intruder moved from there to a Fireworks development machine, ran an unauthorized workload and escalated permissions through several hops until it could act as a cloud service account.

The company detected the activity the same day. It has not named the provider or said how many customers were affected. Fireworks believes the attackers used AI to move at high speed.

What Was Exposed

The affected internal application gave access to account metadata and billing information. For a limited number of accounts, the attacker also reached stored secrets, datasets and a subset of incomplete model weights.

Fireworks says its Inference, Training SDK and Nexus products show no impact, and all three run with zero data retention by default. It advises customers to rotate API keys and stored secrets, since deleting a stored secret does not cancel the credential behind it.

Two Incidents in One Month

The first breach surfaced on October 6, when Fireworks found that someone had used internal credentials to read environment variables in some customer accounts. API keys saved as secrets were probably captured. On October 10, the company said it had found no sign that customer models or datasets had been touched.

Fireworks presents the October 24 activity as part of its update on the October incident, while others count it as a second hack. The Nvidia-backed startup was valued at $17.5 billion in July. Reporting on the notice says Fireworks linked the attack to a financially motivated group that steals and resells access to AI models, a practice known as LLMjacking.

A Month of Attacks on AI Platforms

Fireworks was not alone. At least nine AI developer platforms disclosed incidents in October, and researchers 
described a wave of agent-driven attacks aimed at AI provider keys
. In the same weeks, a 
tampered Claude Code extension reached up to 5,000 Cursor users
, and a 
Cloudflare Workers AI bug sent responses containing other customers’ data
.

LLMjacking has grown from a nuisance into a business. Stolen keys let criminals run expensive models on someone else’s bill, and Google’s threat researchers warned this month that such abuse can rack up more than $100,000 in charges within minutes. 
Security analysts now describe stolen AI compute as infrastructure for further attacks
, and automated tools keep lowering the cost: 
one hacker’s AI agents broke into more than 100 company websites
 for about $5,000. Platforms that hold customer keys, data and weights rank high on that target list.

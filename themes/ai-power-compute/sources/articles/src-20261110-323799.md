Adobe
 and Google are announcing an AI-focused strategic partnership on Tuesday that will deepen the integration of the two companies' products.
 
The two companies plan to bring Adobe tools into more Google apps and also incorporate 
Google
 DeepMind's AI models into more Adobe apps. For example, Acrobat will be available in Google Drive and Gmail, while Google's latest imaging tools will be in Photoshop, Premiere and Firefly. The announcement comes as part of Adobe MAX, the world's largest creativity conference that began Tuesday in Miami Beach.
Ely Greenfield, chief technology officer for creativity business at Adobe, told FOX Business that a major theme for the company in the past few years has been "pushing the bounds of how we can use AI to accelerate that and to make people even more capable and more powerful through the use of creative AI."

"It used to be that you had to bring that into a desktop tool like Photoshop to really be able to get access to those capabilities. Now we can deliver that to them whether they're doing that through a web app, through our desktop flagship tools, or through any of these new AI portals like Gemini where customers are really coming to start and do more and more of their work," he said.

"We get to pair that together with Google's deep 
expertise in AI
, really pushing the bounds with DeepMind and what they're doing in bringing those capabilities to the bleeding edge, and also, obviously, their world-class capability to reach a mass of users around the world," he added.
Matt Renner, president and chief revenue officer for 
Google Cloud

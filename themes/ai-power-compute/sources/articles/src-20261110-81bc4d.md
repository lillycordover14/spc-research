Fireworks AI reports second breach as Oct. 24 cloud attack exposed customer files
Kalbir Talwar
Published 
Nov 10, 2026
 
 
 
Share:

Summary: 

Fireworks AI told customers Monday that an outside provider hosting its machines was breached on Oct. 24, exposing customer data in some cases.

The company said a separate early October incident used internal credentials to access customer accounts, later confirming effects on account and billing data and, for some users, "secrets, datasets and incomplete models."

Fireworks says it is "actively working with our customers to manage the incident, including Notion, Cursor and others," and that it engaged Palo Alto Networks Inc. and contacted law enforcement.

What happened

Fireworks AI, a startup that helps businesses run AI models, alerted customers Monday that an unauthorized party compromised an external cloud host on Oct. 24. A person familiar with the matter said the intrusion involved tens of thousands of files, and some contained customer information.

This comes on the heels of a different issue in early October, when the company said internal 
Fireworks
 credentials were misused to reach a number of customer accounts. In a Monday update, 
Fireworks
 said the earlier breach affected account and billing records and, for a limited group, touched "secrets, datasets and incomplete models." The company said evidence ties a cybercriminal threat group to that early October incident. It has not identified who carried out the Oct. 24 breach.

Customers and the company response

Fireworks lists more than 10,000 customers on its site, including Harvey, Notion and Cursor. A spokesperson said the team is "actively working with our customers to manage the incident, including Notion, Cursor and others."

Upon detecting unauthorized access on certain systems, Fireworks brought in Palo Alto Networks Inc. to assist the investigation and alerted law enforcement. The company said it had also engaged a cybersecurity firm earlier in October "to assist with the incident," and added, "We take this matter very seriously and are connecting directly with customers to provide appropriate support."

What it means for investors

Fireworks' investors include Nvidia Corp. and Advanced Micro Devices Inc. In July, the startup carried a $17.5 billion valuation, and people familiar with the discussions say it is now seeking new financing that might value it between $30 billion and $40 billion.

Why this matters: security posture is now a line item in AI infrastructure investing. Repeated incidents not only create operational risk, they can influence customer churn and valuation narratives. If you hold chipmakers or late-stage private tech exposure in your portfolio, keep an eye on how Fireworks manages remediation and disclosure, and whether customers like Notion and Cursor stay put or shift providers.

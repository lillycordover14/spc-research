Google-backed Tapestry completes deployment of HyperQ AI platform for PJM's Cycle 1 interconnection review

Claims AI platform completed the process 60x faster than would take manually

November 11, 2026

By

Zachary Skidmore

         
            Have your say
        
    

Google-backed energy software company Tapestry has completed the first deployment of its HyperQ AI platform for PJM Interconnection’s Cycle 1 window, the grid operator's reformed interconnection queue cycle.

– Tapestry

According to the company, its platform successfully reviewed site control documentation for 220GW of proposed generation in less than a day. Tapestry claimed that the same work would usually take two months to complete manually, with the HyperQ platform completing it 60 times faster.
The AI platform produced readiness reports for 811 applications, with a median runtime of six minutes and 15 seconds per application, according to Tapestry. The company said 202GW of the screened capacity advanced into Phase 1 of the study process.
Cycle 1 is the first application window under PJM’s reformed interconnection process, which handles new generation requests in large, concurrent batches. The site control process provides the legal proof grid operators require that a developer has the right to build on a specific piece of land. Tapestry said every HyperQ assessment links to the page, clause, or coordinate in the source document, giving reviewers an audit trail. The firm also said it delivered the reports overnight, rather than over multiple months as would have happened in a manual review.
The HyperQ platform uses multimodal AI to analyze PDFs, maps, and legal documents, returning page-level citations to PJM engineers who make the final compliance determinations. Tapestry contends the citation system has reduced the time engineers spend locating supporting documentation.
Reflecting on the results, PJM said: “HyperQ’s preliminary results demonstrate a repeatable model for how AI can operate reliably under strict human governance. By automating intake while keeping our PJM subject matter experts in absolute control, HyperQ provides a promising basis for the foundational velocity needed to hit PJM’s target 12-month interconnection timeline.”
Tapestry said the figures covered only initial site control evaluations, not downstream technical or engineering studies.
After completing the studies, the company said it plans to extend HyperQ across three areas. The first is technical data validation, using AI to audit engineering files such as single-line diagrams, interconnection model files, and power-flow models for errors before cluster studies begin. The second is a pre-submission interface that helps developers find and fix compliance gaps before they enter the queue. The third is unified model management, replacing static grid planning files with a synchronized, interactive network model that stakeholders share.

Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a lower-cost model said to outscore Argon

																																	by								
Duncan Riley

Google LLC today released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a lower-cost model in its Gemini 4 artificial intelligence family that it says outscores its frontier 
Gemini 4 Argon
 model on an independent intelligence ranking.

Measured on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default high thinking level, Hydrogen scored 51 to Argon’s 50, according to Google. A fourth thinking level called xhigh, new with Hydrogen, takes the score to 54.

The rollout is different, too. Argon spent nearly two weeks with members of Google’s Fairwind Program, a group of vetted cybersecurity defenders, before paying developers 
could get at it
 on Oct. 13. Hydrogen skipped that stage. Anyone can use it from today, including people on the free version of the Gemini app.

Koray Kavukcuoglu, who became head of Google DeepMind 
in August
 when Demis Hassabis moved into a chairman role, wrote in 
the announcement
 that Hydrogen is “built for rapid execution.”

Orchestrator, which Google added to its Antigravity coding tool today, picks between Hydrogen and 
Gemini 4 Carbon
 as a session goes along. Google’s runs of the Terminal-Bench 4.0 coding test found Orchestrator getting close to Carbon’s success rate on an average of 24% less quota.

Gemini 3.8 Flash
, which came out in September, is the yardstick for most of the other numbers in the announcement. Against it, Hydrogen finished tasks 2.5 times faster at the default setting. On Terminal-Bench 4.1, which tests work done at a command line, Hydrogen more than tripled the older model’s 18.2% with a score of 57%.

Among the early testers Google quoted, Andrew Carr, a former OpenAI scientist who is now chief scientist at 3D animation startup Cartwheel Inc., said the model caught two problems with an approach his team had been stuck on for 10 days. Discovery Loop Inc., the startup former Google Chief Scientist Jeff Dean co-founded after leaving in August, was another.

Kavukcuoglu said in late September that Google wanted Gemini 4 out “
much earlier
” than the end of the year. Three Gemini 4 models have now shipped in just over six weeks. Hydrogen arrived two days after Carbon, which also started out with Fairwind members, opened up to paying developers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.

Hydrogen is available today in the Gemini API, Google AI Studio and Google Search, and paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can also use it in Antigravity. It costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across the full 1 million-token context window. Argon costs $2 and $10 until its introductory pricing ends Nov. 30, then moves to $4 and $20.

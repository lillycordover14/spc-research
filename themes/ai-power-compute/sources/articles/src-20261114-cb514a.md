Hackers took Uber drivers' license data through AI vendor Fireworks AI&nbsp;&nbsp;Startup Fortune

[page text]
Uber's own systems weren't the way in. If you hand driver or employee records to a vendor, a stranger's stolen password can still empty your files.

Uber has told drivers that hackers stole their driver's license details after breaking into an outside provider, Fireworks AI, according to state breach filings reported on November 13. Gizmodo counted at least 7,870 people across three states: 3,430 in South Carolina, 2,567 in Massachusetts and 1,873 in Rhode Island. Uber hasn't said how many drivers are affected nationwide, so that figure is a floor.

The dates matter, because the notice took a month to reach drivers. Per the reporting, Fireworks AI told Uber on October 9 that an unauthorized party had compromised its internal credentials. On October 13, Uber's investigation concluded the attacker had used those credentials to reach the Uber account the vendor held, where the driver records sat. Notification letters are dated November 10.

Fireworks AI is a startup that helps developers access and run open AI models. An Uber spokesperson confirmed the vendor's involvement to TechCrunch. It isn't clear why Uber held license details in a third-party AI account, and Uber hasn't published the full list of data types taken.

Uber says it has found no misuse of the stolen information so far. It's offering affected drivers free credit monitoring and fraud assistance through Cyberscout, and drivers have 90 days from the date on their letter to sign up. It also recommends fraud alerts or credit freezes.

Uber has been here before. Bitdefender wrote up an earlier incident in which a threat actor posted stolen Uber and Uber Eats data after breaking into Teqtivity, an IT asset-tracking vendor, exposing email addresses and Active Directory data on more than 77,000 employees. The same coverage described a separate case where Genova Burns, a law firm representing Uber, lost driver data affecting over 77,000 Uber and Uber Eats drivers. Two earlier leaks and now a third, all from outside Uber's walls.

Frankly, that pattern is the story. A company can spend millions on its own perimeter and still lose its workforce's identity documents through a contractor's laptop, a law firm's server or an AI startup's admin login. The attacker here didn't need to beat Uber. They needed one set of credentials at a smaller company that was holding Uber's data.

What a founder should do before the next vendor signs

If you run a gig platform, a marketplace or any startup that pays contractors, you almost certainly keep license scans, tax forms or bank details somewhere you don't control. The Uber case suggests where to look first.

Start with what you send. Ask whether the vendor needs a driver's license image, or whether a verification result would do. Data that was never shared can't be stolen, and a pass or fail flag is worth far less to a thief than a scan. Then ask how long the vendor keeps it, and make deletion a clause in the contract, not a favor.

Check how the vendor protects the account your data lives in. Credentials were the weak point at Fireworks, so you want to know whether staff logins need multi-factor authentication, whether your data sits in an account separate from other customers, and who can reach it. A security questionnaire that never asks about internal credentials misses the most common way in.

Write the disclosure clock into the contract too. Uber learned of the compromise on October 9 and drivers were mailed on November 10. You can't always speed up your own investigation, but you can require the vendor to tell you within days, in writing, and to name the data involved. Your own notification duties under state law start when you know, not when your vendor gets around to it.

Finally, keep an inventory. Few founders can list every service holding their people's data, and you can't review a vendor you've forgotten you use. AI tools are the newest additions to that list, bought quickly by teams who want a feature live this quarter.

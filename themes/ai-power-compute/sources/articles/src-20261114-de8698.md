Fireworks AI: 2 Breaches in 18 Days Reach Notion Chats

 

Ryan Cole

Updated Nov 14, 2026 · 16 min read

 

Image: Fireworks AI
 
Fireworks AI
 has now disclosed two separate security incidents in 18 days, and the second one pulled customer chat data into the blast radius. 
Notion said its investigation found Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature in a dataset Fireworks provided on November 8, 2026
, which turns the Fireworks AI breach into a live test of how far one AI vendor’s problem can travel into the products built on top of it.

The first incident surfaced on October 6, 2026, when Fireworks identified unauthorized use of internal credentials. The second began when a third-party cloud provider hosting Fireworks R&D machines was compromised on October 23. A day later, an unauthorized party used that access to reach Fireworks development machines and run a workload nobody had approved. CX Today and MediaNews4U both framed the story as a warning about the supply chains behind enterprise AI.

Security teams will recognize the pattern. Credentials leak, a vendor sits one hop away from the data, and customers learn the scope in installments. Our earlier coverage of a

 covers the credential-theft side of the same problem. This analysis sticks to what Fireworks, Notion and named outlets have confirmed, flags what remains unproven, and lays out what enterprise buyers should do before the next disclosure lands.

Incident One: Internal Credentials and Evaluation Job Variables

On October 6, 2026, Fireworks identified a security incident involving unauthorized use of internal Fireworks credentials. An unauthorized third party accessed environment variables used in evaluation jobs in a limited number of customer accounts. Fireworks drew a boundary around its initial finding. It said the investigation turned up no evidence of unauthorized access to customer models, datasets, training data, personal information or inference traffic within the Fireworks security perimeter or control.

Why environment variables are a sensitive target

Evaluation jobs run a model against a test set and score the output. To do that, they often need access to other systems, which means their configuration can carry connection details. Environment variables are a common place to park those details because they keep secrets out of code. That convenience cuts both ways. Anyone who reads the variables of a running job reads whatever the job was trusted with.

Fireworks has not published a list of what those variables held in each account, so this article does not claim to know. The point for buyers is structural. A platform that runs customer workloads will hold customer-supplied settings, and those settings deserve the same protection as production keys.

What the perimeter language really says

The phrase “within the Fireworks security perimeter or control” in the first investigation’s finding is narrow by design. It describes what Fireworks could see inside its own boundary. It says nothing about systems that sat outside that boundary, such as machines hosted by a third-party cloud provider. Two weeks later, that gap became the second incident.

Incident Two: A Cloud Provider Compromise Reaches R&D Machines

The second event is the one that widened the story. Fireworks said a third-party cloud provider that hosted its R&D machines experienced a security incident on October 23. On October 24, an unauthorized third party used that access to reach Fireworks development machines and run an unauthorized workload. The company has not named the cloud provider in the material reviewed for this article.

The Register ran the story under a headline saying Fireworks AI got burned twice as customer data exposure widened
, and the framing fits the facts. Development and research machines tend to hold copies of data that production systems guard more tightly. Engineers pull samples, fine-tuning sets and billing exports onto them to debug problems quickly. When attackers land on those machines, they land near the interesting material.

Running an unauthorized workload also tells defenders something. An attacker who executes code on a development machine has more than read access. Fireworks has not said what the workload did, and this article does not guess. What Fireworks did say is that the investigation later confirmed access to specific categories of data, which the next section lays out.

Fireworks’ update lists two clear categories and one narrower one. Resource metadata and billing information fall in the first group. A subset of incomplete model weights and datasets, for certain customers only, falls in the second. Payment card numbers sit outside all of it because Fireworks did not store them.

Billing data deserves more attention than it usually gets. Addresses, invoice history and credit usage let an attacker map who a company is, how much it spends on AI inference and when it spends it. That profile feeds targeted phishing and invoice fraud. A message that quotes a real invoice date and amount looks routine to an accounts-payable clerk.

Metadata carries a similar risk. Display names and descriptions often reflect internal project names, and project names reveal product plans. A resource called something obvious tells an outsider which teams are building which features.

Notion AI Chat Transcripts: The Downstream Impact

Notion’s account is the clearest view into how a vendor incident becomes a customer incident. The company said the affected dataset included Notion AI synthetic testing data and that no Notion AI customer data was involved in that part. Then came the harder finding. Notion’s investigation found Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature in the dataset Fireworks provided on November 8, 2026.

Notion added that for the vast majority of impacted workspaces, the transcripts were unattributable. That means a reader holding the transcripts could not easily tie them to a specific customer or workspace. It reduces the harm, though it does not erase it. A transcript can still reveal what employees ask an AI assistant to do with company documents.

TechCrunch reported on November 12 that the cyberattack on Fireworks AI allowed hackers to access Notion customers’ chat transcripts
, which pushed the story beyond security trade press. Notion’s own timeline shows it acted before the final picture was clear. It disconnected its systems from Fireworks on November 3, deleted its data in the Fireworks system on November 4 and contacted impacted customers on November 9.

That sequence is the model to study. Notion cut the connection first, removed its data second and notified customers after it had facts. Plenty of companies reverse that order and spend days arguing about wording while the data sits in someone else’s environment.

How Fireworks Says It Contained the Attack

Fireworks described a standard but wide set of remediation steps. It revoked and replaced credentials and service accounts, restricted unauthorized network pathways, isolated affected systems and tightened access to data-storage and analytics systems. It also worked with Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 to remediate the attack.

Each step maps to a failure the incidents exposed. Replacing credentials answers the October 6 misuse. Restricting network pathways answers the way the October 24 intruder moved from a cloud provider’s environment to Fireworks machines. Tightening access to storage and analytics systems answers the data categories listed in the update. The list reads like a post-incident checklist, and that is a good sign, though a checklist is not proof of outcome.

Engaging an outside firm like Unit 42 also matters for credibility. Customers and insurers tend to trust an independent forensic team more than a vendor’s self-assessment. Fireworks has not published the forensic report, and no outlet reviewed for this article reports that it will.

What Remains Unconfirmed

Discipline matters most where the claims get loud. A person familiar with the matter described the breach as involving “tens of thousands of files.” That figure did not come from an identified Fireworks statement, so treat it as unverified. Fireworks’ own wording refers to a subset of incomplete model weights and datasets for certain customers, which is a different and narrower claim.

The same caution applies to any suggestion that the incident exposed all customer models. Nothing on the confirmed record supports that. Fireworks said access to incomplete model weights and datasets applied to certain customers, and its first investigation found no evidence of access to customer models, datasets, training data, personal information or inference traffic within its own perimeter.

Other open questions remain. Fireworks has not said how many customers fall into each category, which cloud provider was compromised or what the unauthorized workload did. It has not said whether the attacker is a known group. Until it does, any claim about attribution, motive or the total number of affected files belongs in the rumor column.

Why This Is an Enterprise AI Supply-Chain Problem

Most enterprises now consume AI through layers. A software product calls a model, the model runs on a platform, and the platform runs on a cloud provider. The Fireworks AI security incident touched three of those layers: the platform’s own credentials, a hosting provider and a downstream application. A Notion customer who never heard of Fireworks could still have had chat content in the dataset.

Traditional vendor reviews struggle with this shape. A questionnaire asks whether the vendor encrypts data at rest and whether it holds a compliance certificate. It rarely asks where the vendor’s research machines live, who else administers them or whether production-adjacent data gets copied there. Those questions decided the outcome here.

A related worry involves what AI tools send out in the first place. Our report on

Market Impact: Vendor Risk, Insurance and Procurement

No outlet reviewed for this article reports lost contracts or a stock move tied to the Fireworks incidents, and this piece does not invent one. The commercial effect shows up elsewhere. Insurance Journal covered the story under a headline calling Fireworks AI Nvidia-backed and noting it had notified customers of its second hack in weeks. Coverage in an insurance trade outlet signals that underwriters are paying attention to AI vendor incidents.

Expect procurement teams to react in three ways. First, they will ask AI platform vendors to disclose where research and development environments run. Second, they will push for contractual notice windows that start when the vendor learns of an incident, not when it finishes its analysis. Third, they will ask for the right to delete their data on demand, which is the exact power Notion used on November 4.

Historical Context: Developer Platforms as Repeat Targets

Attackers have long targeted the systems developers use because those systems hold keys to everything else. Build servers, code repositories and evaluation harnesses collect secrets over time. The 2025 and 2026 run of incidents on this site, from CI stealers to vendor leaks, points the same direction. The cheapest way into many companies runs through a tool those companies trust.

AI platforms add two features that raise the stakes. They process data that people would not post publicly, such as internal documents and support transcripts. They also store artifacts with real commercial value, including fine-tuned weights and curated datasets. A breach of a conventional SaaS tool exposes records. A breach of an AI platform can expose the work product that took months to build.

Finally, the speed of adoption works against careful vetting. Teams wire up a new inference provider in an afternoon. Security review follows weeks later, if it follows at all. By then the provider already holds credentials, test data and chat logs.

Five Steps Security Teams Can Take This Week

Start with an inventory. List every AI platform that holds your credentials, data or chat logs, and record which of your own products depend on each. If you cannot produce the list in an hour, that is your first finding.

Next, rotate anything an evaluation or batch job could read. Environment variables in jobs run by third parties should hold scoped, short-lived tokens, never a master key. Our walkthrough on 
secret scanning with push protection, Gitleaks and TruffleHog
 shows how to find the leftovers before an attacker does. A quick local check looks like this:

gitleaks detect --source . --report-format json --report-path gitleaks-report.json
env | grep -i -E 'key|token|secret|password'

Third, confirm your deletion rights. Notion deleted its data in the Fireworks system on November 4, which was only possible because it could reach that data. Check whether your contracts and your technical setup give you the same power.

Fourth, tighten secrets handling inside your own clusters. Our guide to 
Kubernetes Secrets with encryption at rest and RBAC
 covers the access-control side. Fifth, ask each AI vendor three questions in writing: where do research machines run, who administers them and how fast do you notify customers after you detect an incident. Browse the

Predictions: What Comes Next for AI Vendor Security

These are analyst judgments, not reported facts. Each carries uncertainty, and none comes from a named source.

More disclosure detail from Fireworks.
 Notion’s advisory named a data category that Fireworks’ headline summary did not. Pressure from customers and outlets will likely push Fireworks to say more about customer counts and the cloud provider involved.
Hosting transparency becomes a contract clause.
 Expect enterprise buyers to demand disclosure of where research and development machines run, with notice duties that cover subcontractors.
Chat transcripts get reclassified.
 Companies that treated AI chat logs as low-risk telemetry will start handling them like customer records, with retention limits and deletion workflows.
Insurers add AI-vendor questions.
 Cyber underwriters already read incident coverage, so questionnaires will probably start asking which AI platforms a company depends on.
More disconnect-first playbooks.
 Notion’s early cutoff gives other teams a template. Incident plans for AI dependencies will list the disconnect step before the investigation step.

The takeaway: the Fireworks AI breach shows that the weakest link in an AI stack may sit two vendors away from the product a customer actually uses. Security reviews that stop at the first vendor miss that link.

FAQ: Fireworks AI Breach and Enterprise Security

 Fireworks disclosed two incidents. On October 6, 2026, it identified unauthorized use of internal credentials, and an unauthorized party accessed environment variables used in evaluation jobs in a limited number of customer accounts. A third-party cloud provider hosting its R&D machines was then compromised on October 23, and on October 24 an unauthorized party reached Fireworks development machines and ran an unauthorized workload.

 No. Fireworks said it did not store payment card numbers, so they were not impacted.

Did the breach involve tens of thousands of files?

Who helped Fireworks respond?

What should enterprise customers do now?

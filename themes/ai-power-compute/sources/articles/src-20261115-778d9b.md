Google is planning to launch drone deliveries to households in the UK for the first time in a challenge to 
Amazon’s rival Prime Air service
.
Wing, which is owned by the US search engine giant, is preparing to trial an airborne “last-mile delivery service” near Birmingham next year.
The drone company has already launched drone services in London, carrying blood samples for the NHS between hospitals in the capital.
However, its latest trial will deliver groceries and other household goods. If approved, Wing’s new drone trials will be introduced in May and last up to three years.
Documents filed with the Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) show Wing is targeting an area spanning Dudley, West Bromwich and Wolverhampton for its initial scheme.
A Wing spokesman said: “We are excited to explore expanding our service to more areas in the UK and extending our service to include a trial of residential drone delivery services.
“As an early step in that process, Wing has applied for an airspace change proposal with the CAA in the UK.”
The trial, if approved, will allow Wing’s four-bladed drones to “deliver groceries and everyday essentials to customers’ doorsteps”, the spokesman added.
It will also “provide valuable data to the CAA on operating in airspace alongside other airspace users, conducting multiple simultaneous operations, and routine automated beyond visual line of sight operations”.
Wing already operates in multiple cities across the US, promising to deliver groceries in as little as 30 minutes. The company has a partnership with retail giant Walmart.
Its UK launch would put Wing into direct competition with Amazon, which has been expanding its own drone network in Britain.
Amazon launched its Prime Air service in May, serving around 600 homes around its Darlington warehouse. It has since 
expanded the scheme
 to cover around 8,000 households, delivering products weighing up to 2.2kg.
Drone deliveries have been slow to take off in the UK, with the CAA only approving limited trials that rely on bespoke airspace agreements.
It has been preparing to allow more widespread drone operations that will see aircraft fly beyond the horizon, sharing the sky with planes and other drones, with “routine” operations planned in 2027.
Previous drone trials by Wing in other markets have not always gone without a hitch.
In 2022, a Wing drone crashed into a power line in Australia, causing 2,000 households to lose power. In another incident, its operations were temporarily suspended after its drones were attacked by ravens.
Amazon was forced to suspend drone deliveries in 2025 after two of its aircraft crashed in the US.

Google inks 50MW geothermal PPA with Quaise in Oregon

PPA to support Google's data centers in The Dalles

November 17, 2026

Google has signed a 50MW Power Purchase Agreement (PAA) with geothermal firm Quaise Energy in Oregon.
The PPA will see Google offtake power from Project Obsidian, an under-development enhanced geothermal system (EGS) project located close to Bend, Oregon.

Under the terms of the agreement, Google is expected to begin receiving power from the project in 2030, when it reaches commercial operation. 
The power will support operations at its data centers in The Dalles, Oregon, where the company currently operates three data center campuses in the city, with its first opening in 2006. It announced plans to upgrade that data center 
last year
.
“Google data centers are long-term investments in the communities we call home,” said Lucia Tian, head of Advanced Energy Technologies at Google. “This collaboration with Quaise on superhot geothermal will help fuel local economic growth while helping to build a more robust and affordable energy future for Oregon.”
The power delivered to Google will come from the project's first phase, which Quaise said will utilize conventional drilling technology to access superhot geothermal conditions. The second phase will incorporate the company’s millimeter-wave drilling to reach deeper rock layers, increasing the project's capacity to 250MW. A potential third phase could see the project expand to more than 1GW.
“We started Quaise to make the Earth’s most abundant energy source universally accessible — and this agreement marks a critical next step, not just for Quaise but for the entire clean energy sector,” said Carlos Araque, CEO and president of Quaise Energy. “Together with Google, we are building the commercial foundation for superhot geothermal and cementing the United States as the global leader in geothermal power.”
Unlike conventional geothermal systems, which rely on naturally occurring hot water reservoirs, EGS borrows technology and techniques from hydraulic fracking to create permeability in reservoirs that would otherwise be inaccessible to produce commercially viable quantities of geothermal brine. As a result, EGS can access significantly more geothermal potential than conventional practices and achieve much higher capacity factors.
The PPA is Google’s second this year with an EGS firm, after signing a 
396MW PPA
 with Fervo Energy in Utah. This year, it also signed a 150MW conventional geothermal PPA with 
Ormat Technologies
 in Nevada.
Google has also signed a geothermal PPA in Taiwan, inking a 10MW deal with Baseload Capital
 last April
.
Google isn't the only hyperscaler to back geothermal energy. Meta has also signed offtake agreements with two EGS developers in the US. 
Last year
, it signed a deal with Sage Geosystems for 150MW of power from a project under development “east of the Rocky Mountains.” Following this, in 
June
, it signed a 150MW agreement with XGS Energy for a project under development in New Mexico.
Microsoft has also gotten in on the act. In 2023, the tech giant signed a ten-year
 PPA
 with Contact Energy for 51MW of power in New Zealand. The company also
 partnered with G42
 to construct a data center in Kenya that will be run exclusively on geothermal energy.

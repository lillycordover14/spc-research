PJM asks FERC to halve data center grid studies to 12 months&nbsp;&nbsp;Startup Fortune

[page text]
Waiting two years just to learn what a grid connection will involve is now the slowest step for many AI data centers. PJM wants it down to one, and wants states to see exactly who pays for the upgrades.

PJM Interconnection filed a proposal with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission at the end of the day on Monday, November 16, that would cut the time it takes to study new data center connections to its 13-state grid from 24 months to 12, according to Bloomberg. The plan, which PJM calls fast energization, would study power plants, transmission upgrades and large loads like data centers together instead of in separate queues.

PJM has asked FERC for a 60-day comment period and a ruling within 120 days of filing, which points to a decision around mid-March 2027, according to MLQ.ai's summary of the filing. Bloomberg says the filing runs 1,067 pages.

Under PJM's own description, the process also moves financial and readiness requirements earlier, so speculative projects drop out before they clog the study. Quick screening rounds would hand developers usable information sooner, and a new transmission service would be created for data centers and other large customers. The process also makes room for loads that bring their own generation and for flexible loads that can cut their draw, which lowers what the grid has to build for them.

That last part follows earlier PJM proposals that gave priority to data centers willing to supply their own power or curtail in extreme conditions. If you're a developer who can do either, the new track is aimed at you.
 

AI data centers have pushed America's largest power grid to its breaking point

PJM Interconnection, the US grid serving 67 million people, missed its power supply reliability target in July 2026 as AI data center demand drove capacity costs up more than tenfold. The cost side matters just as much. PJM says states would get transparent access to the transmission costs driven by data centers, so they can bill large loads for the upgrades those loads cause. Ratepayer bills have been the political sore spot in PJM's region, and this is the first piece of the plan that speaks to it directly.

Not everyone in the industry likes the combined study

The Data Center Coalition told PJM's board in October that merging the processes could complicate interconnection and lead to longer delays, Bloomberg reported. That's a fair worry. A single combined study means a late or failed power plant project can hold up a data center sitting next to it in the queue.

Frankly, PJM doesn't have much room to get this wrong. FERC has pressed the grid operator for faster, clearer large-load interconnection rules, and this filing is part of PJM's answer.

The reliability filing running alongside it

PJM made a second filing on November 16, this one on ride-through rules. It would require loads of 50 MW or more, defined as Large Computational Loads, to stay connected during voltage and frequency disturbances. MLQ.ai points to a July 22 incident in which close to 4 GW of data centers switched to backup generation during a transmission disturbance. New loads would have to comply when they connect, and existing ones would get a year from the January 16, 2027 effective date.

Both filings sit against a demand forecast that explains the urgency. PJM serves roughly 10 GW of data center load today and expects up to 30 GW more by 2030, per MLQ.ai. Speeding studies doesn't create any power, though. PJM is separately working on a reliability backstop procurement for a 6,831 MW capacity shortfall, with a proposed start of March 1, 2027.

So the timeline math for AI capacity is simple. If FERC approves in March, a halved study time still only gets a developer to the point of knowing what they can plug into and what it costs. Whether the plants, lines and transformers exist by then is a separate question. PJM hasn't said when the first projects would go through the new process, and Bloomberg's report doesn't say either.

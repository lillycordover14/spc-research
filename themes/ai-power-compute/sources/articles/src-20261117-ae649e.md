Key points

PJM filed its Fast Energization proposal with FERC on Nov. 16 to cut projected study timelines for new generation and large-load transmission service from two years to one 
[1]
.

The plan studies generation and load together, applies stricter readiness and financial requirements earlier, and creates new transmission services for data centers, including those with their own generation or flexible load 
[1]
.

PJM says states would get transparent access to the transmission costs driven by data centers, so they can bill large loads for them 
[1]
.

PJM asked FERC for a 60-day comment period and an order within 120 days 
[1]
.

A separate filing would require data centers of 50 MW or more to ride through voltage and frequency disturbances from Jan. 16, 2027, after 3.9 GW of data centers dropped to backup power on July 22 
[2]
.

PJM Interconnection asked federal regulators on Monday, Nov. 16, to approve a new process that would halve the time it takes to study power plants and grid service for data centers and other large electricity users, from a projected two years to one 
[1]
. PJM, which runs the largest US grid, said neither generation nor transmission is coming online fast enough to keep up with demand growth driven mainly by data centers, and that the shortfall in new supply is driving up electricity prices 
[1]
.
The proposal, which PJM calls Fast Energization, also sets up new transmission services for large loads and is meant to give states a clear view of the grid upgrade costs data centers cause, "empowering states to bill Large Loads for those costs," PJM said 
[1]
.

How Fast Energization would work

Today, PJM studies new generation and transmission service for large loads in separate processes, which PJM said makes it hard for states to see all the costs that data center development creates 
[1]
. Fast Energization would study generation and load together, removing duplicate work 
[1]
.
Projects would go through quick rounds of initial screening so developers and customers get usable information early, before deciding whether to post readiness requirements, which PJM said are now stricter and applied earlier. The goal is to limit the queue to projects that are ready to build 
[1]
. PJM said the plan keeps the "first-ready, first-served" principle of its reformed interconnection process 
[1]
.
The filing also creates transmission services for large loads, including those that bring their own generation and flexible loads that can cut their demand 
[1]
. PJM said FERC had found that new services should reflect that some large loads are willing to limit their use of the grid, including an interim service while network upgrades are built 
[1]
. The proposal tracks FERC directives in two dockets on large loads and co-located loads, where generation sits next to a data center 
[1]
.

Data centers would have to ride through disturbances

In a second filing on Monday, PJM proposed voltage and frequency ride-through requirements for large computational loads, defined as facilities of 50 MW or more 
[2]
. On July 22, nearly 3.9 GW of data centers unexpectedly switched from utility power to their own onsite generators during a routine transmission disturbance, the largest such load transfer in PJM's history, PJM said 
[2]
. The event affected voltages, frequency and power flows in the Dominion zone and across the Eastern Interconnection 
[2]
.
New large computational loads would have to show compliance as a condition of connecting, and existing ones would have one year from the Jan. 16, 2027 effective date, or from commercial operation if later 
[2]
. PJM serves about 10 GW of such load today and forecasts up to 30 GW more by 2030 
[2]
.

Part of a wider push on data center costs

PJM has a separate plan before FERC, the Reliability Backstop Procurement, to buy up to 15 years of new capacity against a 6,831 MW shortfall from its 2028/2029 capacity auction and charge it to the large loads driving demand; it filed an amended version on Oct. 29 aiming to start the procurement on March 1, 2027 
[3]
. In Congress, the Senate's permitting bill carries a rule that would make data centers pay for their grid upgrades (
our story
), and Exelon has asked FERC to scrap PJM's three-year-ahead capacity auctions (
our story
). PJM has not published reactions from states, consumer advocates or data center developers to Monday's filings.

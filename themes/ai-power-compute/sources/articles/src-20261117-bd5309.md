Apple said it was experiencing tech issues Tuesday that were affecting several of its popular consumer-facing services.

As of 2 p.m. ET, Apple’s own 
system status page showed
 that Apple Business, Apple School Manager, AppleCare, iCloud Mail and Web Apps, and SchoolWork were each experiencing outages.

Earlier, the page showed the App Store and Apple Music experiencied outages.

“Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning,” an Apple spokesperson told NBC News. “We’re working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible.”

								UCLA adjunct engineering professor emeritus Peter Reiher says the complexity of what the internet does will make outages a common occurrence that is unlikely to change.
							

At Apple stores in New York and Chicago, employees told NBC News earlier in the day that their internal software had stopped working and they had been unable to complete purchases or make appointments.

Marriott International, the largest U.S. hotel operator, was also having technology issues that guests reported had impacted reservations and check-ins, among other problems, according to users posting on Reddit. Downdetector.com also reported that Marriott was experiencing an outage.

Marriott, which has 10,200 properties and more than 1.8 million rooms under its corporate umbrella, did not immediately respond to a request for comment from the Associated Press.

Gmail also appeared to experience an outage briefly Tuesday, with some users reporting issues accessing their email, according to Downdetector. The issue did not appear to be widespread and the company posted on X that they weren't "currently seeing evidence of a broad Gmail service disruption."

								If there is an internet service outage, does that mean it is time to change your password? UCLA adjunct engineering professor emeritus Peter Reiher weighs in.
							

The outages come on the heels of a string of major disruptions impacting U.S. services over the past several days — including Monday's 
Chase, Bank of America and Wells Fargo banking disruptions 
and 
United Airlines departing-flight system glitch
, following 
Friday's nationwide FAA ground stop
 and 
Thursday's Microsoft cloud outage.

President Donald Trump brushed off the recent outages that wreaked havoc on air travel, banks and emails, saying Tuesday, “it’s life.”

“It’s life, it’s life,” he told reporters during his trip to the Philippines when asked about the outages. “When you’re big and powerful and you have all the money, things like that happen. It’s fine.”

While none of the issues appear to be related, they’ve exposed America’s reliance on older infrastructure and revealed the extent to which companies and governments are being forced to upgrade systems in the AI age. The incidents have also stoked unfounded paranoia that something larger may be afoot.

Cerebras paired GPU prefill with Cerebras decode to deliver five times more tokens per megawatt in production while token generation speed held steady. The company plans a broader rollout by the end of 2027 and expanded partner access.
Cerebras launched disaggregated inference on Nov. 17, 2026, pairing GPUs for prompt processing with Cerebras systems for token generation, Atharva Talpade wrote in a company blog post. The setup runs in production and delivers five times more tokens per megawatt at Cerebras speeds. Cerebras can now serve more ultrafast tokens through Cerebras Inference Cloud, and infrastructure partners can run the same setup in their data centers.

Agentic applications such as Muse, Copilot and Codex scale fast and need interactive inference to keep users engaged. Goldman Sachs Research forecasts AI inference demand will reach 24 times 2026 levels by 2030, while data center capacity grows 2.2 times over the same period. That gap pushes providers to raise tokens per megawatt without slowing response times.

An inference request has two stages. Prefill processes the input and produces intermediate model state. Decode uses that state to generate output tokens one at a time. Decode reads model weights from memory for each token, so memory bandwidth governs speed.

Cerebras keeps weights in on-chip SRAM across a wafer-scale chip, with tens of gigabytes of SRAM and tens of petabytes per second of memory bandwidth. That is 350 times the SRAM capacity and 5,400 times the memory bandwidth of a typical GB300 GPU, the company said. The design fits more concurrent requests and model layers on chip, which lets CS-3, CS-4 and CS-5 reach ultrafast speeds at attractive throughput. Other splits such as draft-target or attention-FFN exist, yet separating prefill from decode suits interactive inference.

In a production deployment on Cerebras Inference Cloud, AMD Instinct GPUs process prompts and Cerebras CS-3 systems generate responses. Moving prompt processing to GPUs frees Cerebras systems to focus on response generation. The combination delivered five times the throughput per megawatt of a CS-3-only configuration, with no drop in token generation speed, in a Nov. 14 measurement of a shared GPT-OSS-120B endpoint. Throughput per megawatt rose from a 1x baseline to 5x after disaggregation. Cerebras plans to deploy the approach across hundreds of megawatts in its cloud and in partner deployments by the end of 2027.

Infrastructure partners can deploy clusters from a Cerebras reference architecture that co-locates chosen prefill accelerators and Cerebras systems in one network. Partners choose between two software paths.

The end-to-end disaggregated stack suits teams that want fast time to market. It includes a framework that routes requests between prefill and decode pools and manages replica capacity and health; a prefill engine that uses open-source engines such as vLLM or SGLang; a Cerebras decode engine; and KV Portal software that moves key-value caches between engines over RDMA.

The decode-only SDK suits teams that want control. It lets partners bring their own inference framework and prefill engine, and includes a mock Cerebras decode replica plus a reference integration to an open-source disaggregated framework and prefill engine.

Early-access partners include Amazon Web Services, Fireworks AI, Gimlet Labs and General Compute. Cerebras is expanding access to more partners. The company tied the launch to its CS-4 rollout, scaled manufacturing and planned 700 megawatts of data center capacity, and said those pieces will support more ultrafast tokens in 2027 and beyond.

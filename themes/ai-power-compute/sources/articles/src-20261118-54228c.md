Elementl Power partners with Google Cloud on AI suite to speed up nuclear siting and licensing

Builds on existing partnership with Google

November 18, 2026

By

Zachary Skidmore

         
            Have your say
        
    

US nuclear project developer Elementl Power has partnered with Google Cloud to develop AI-powered tools to support the development of its portfolio of nuclear power projects.

– Southern Nuclear

As part of the tie-up, Google Cloud will provide its AI expertise to support Elementl’s integrated suite of project delivery tools, dubbed Elementl Lattice. According to the companies, the system will be built on Google Cloud’s Gemini for Business and BigQuery. The system aims to address challenges in the preconstruction phase of nuclear projects, including siting requirements, technology selection, regulatory approvals, and rigorous technical planning, which they claim lead to prolonged development timelines and schedule uncertainty.
“Through our collaboration with Google Cloud, we’re turning our nuclear development expertise into practical tools built on Gemini for business that help us move faster and with greater confidence,” said Elementl chairman and CEO Chris Colbert. “These tools will allow us to advance more projects, reduce repetitive work and carry what we learn from one project into the next, enhancing our platform’s ability to deliver a growing portfolio of new nuclear plants.”
The system will address these challenges through several systems. The most notable is Atlas, which Elementl claims is designed to identify viable sites for nuclear power projects. It works by assessing hundreds of site characteristics against the unique geographic, environmental, and infrastructure criteria required for nuclear projects. According to the companies, the tool is able to evaluate several potential locations simultaneously, examining thousands of square miles while accounting for the needs of multiple reactor designs and technologies.
The partners claim this lets them compress months of preliminary desktop research and analysis into days or weeks.
They added that several other capabilities and tools are currently under development that seek to create a single, controlled record of a nuclear plant’s design, licensing requirements, and approved changes.
“A major preconstruction bottleneck for nuclear energy stems from an intelligence and data orchestration problem, which Elementl is using Gemini for business to tackle head-on,” said Raiford Smith, global director of power and energy at Google Cloud. “This collaboration is helping move the nuclear industry from fragmented desktop studies to real-time, predictive project delivery that accelerates the arrival of efficient baseload power to the grid.”
The companies first partnered in 
May of last year
, signing a strategic agreement to develop three nuclear energy projects in the US. As part of the agreement, Google committed early-stage development capital to advance the three projects. Each project is expected to generate at least 600MW of capacity, with Google providing the option for commercial offtake when the projects reach operational status. Further details on the proposed projects have not been revealed.
Elementl is headquartered in Washington, DC, and is backed by Energy Impact Partners, a global investment firm, with more than $4 billion in assets under management.

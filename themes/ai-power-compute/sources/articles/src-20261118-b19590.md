Government makes request to tech firm in wake of 51-year-old’s prison message, according to Sky News

The British government has reportedly asked Google to deactivate 
Russell Brand
’s YouTube account after he was able to publish a message claiming his innocence following his conviction for rape and sexual assault.
A justice minister met representatives of the technology company to make the request after social media firm Meta disabled the former comedian’s profiles on Instagram and Facebook, 
according to Sky News
.
Earlier this week, the Ministry of Justice launched an investigation into whether Brand, 51, broke prison rules after he 
shared a message claiming he was not guilty
 days after his conviction at Southwark crown court.
He was 
sentenced to 10-and-a-half-years in prison
 for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.
His wife, Laura Brand, read a message from her husband on Sunday in a video uploaded to their 
YouTube
 channel.
Prisoners are allowed to protest their innocence, meaning that no criminal offence was committed in the release of the video. However, convicts are not permitted to make statements from jail that cause distress to victims, and heavy restrictions apply to speaking to the media.

Laura Brand read out a message from her imprisoned husband on the couple’s YouTube channel on Sunday.

If he is found to have broken the rules, Brand could face sanctions ranging from an extension of his sentence to loss of privileges such as access to a phone for a limited period.
It is understood a prisoner will be deemed to have broken the rules if they provide comments to a family member that they know will be published.
Responding to the video, the prime minister’s spokesperson called the comments “clearly appalling”.
The education secretary, Lucy Powell, also criticised broadcasters on Wednesday for “amplifying” Brand’s words by airing a clip of Laura Brand reading the message.
She told LBC radio: “I was appalled at that, and actually I think mainstream media shouldn’t have transmitted that either.”
Asked why she wanted to censor the message, Powell said: “It’s a platform I don’t think … should have been amplified, to be honest.”
The Ministry of Justice and 
Google
 were contacted for comment.
Google 
stopped Brand from profiting from YouTube content
 in 2023 after a media investigation revealed allegations of rape and sexual assault. At the time, the video platform said the decision was made “to protect” its users.

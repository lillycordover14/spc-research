BEND, Ore. -- Quaise Energy has signed a 50-megawatt power purchase agreement with Google, marking the first major customer announcement for a proposed geothermal energy project near La Pine that could begin generating power by 2030, the company announced Tuesday.
A sizable portion of geothermal energy from Deschutes County will power Google's data center in The Dalles.
The first phase of the project will use conventional drilling to access what is known as super-hot geothermal energy. This is natural heat from volcanic activity at the Newberry Caldera.

At least one local leader is raising concerns about the power purchase agreement with Google. Deschutes County Commissioner-elect Jamie Collins said the announcement lacks specifics. He said Central Oregon needs clean power to meet the needs of a growing population.
Below is the full statement from Collins, followed by the announcement from Quaise Energy.
Commissioner-elect Jamie Collins
BEND, Oregon — Deschutes County Commissioner-elect Jamie Collins, a climate scientist and former environmental emergency response official, offered the following statement on Quaise Energy’s 50 megawatt geothermal energy offtake agreement with Google LLC:
“Deschutes County is home to a geothermal resource that could power millions of American homes with stable, carbonfree, always-on energy. The 46 square miles of public land that would provide it, outside the Newberry National Volcanic Monument, belong to the American people. This land is a recreation resource for thousands and, most importantly, sacred cultural property to several recognized tribal nations.
“I’m confident Deschutes County can contribute to the clean energy transition while reducing costs for residents and maintaining the integrity of our environment. But that balancing act requires earnest, transparent and deep engagement with our community. Quaise’s Nov. 17 announcement contains very few specifics: whether any additional clean power will remain here, what the company’s plan for public outreach is, or what it has done to engage with tribal nations and community organizations.

“Those are questions to which this community deserves straightforward, public answers. I’m excited that Central Oregon could become a net exporter of clean energy, but right now our county doesn’t have enough energy of any kind — or enough of the basic infrastructure like power lines — to meet our own needs over the next five years.
“I welcome the jobs and other community benefits that Quaise and the other companies investing in this lease block have said they will bring, and I look forward to working with any company that has a specific plan to help secure a better future for our county. I hope these companies, backed by investors who stand to make hundreds of millions of dollars if these projects succeed, will not take our community for granted.”
Quaise Energy announcement
BEND, Ore. — November 17, 2026 — Quaise Energy, a leading developer of utility-scale superhot geothermal energy, today announced that Google has signed a power purchase agreement (PPA) for 50 megawatts (MW) of superhot geothermal power from 
Project Obsidian
, Quaise's flagship project in Central Oregon. Once online, this always-on, carbon-free energy is slated to support Google's existing data center operations in The Dalles, Ore. and the region’s communities. The agreement will help catalyze the commercial development of superhot geothermal energy, providing benefits for ratepayers and helping protect the environment.
Beginning as early as 2030, Google will purchase 50 MW of power from Project Obsidian. The initial phase of development will use conventional drilling technology to access superhot geothermal conditions at commercial scale. 
Superhot geothermal
 is a virtually limitless energy source that uses the Earth’s natural heat to generate up to 10x more clean power per well than traditional geothermal systems. The second phase of 
development at Project Obsidian
 is expected to be the first in the world to use millimeter wave drilling, a technology designed to uniquely access superhot geothermal conditions worldwide. 
“We started Quaise to make the Earth's most abundant energy source universally accessible — and this agreement marks a critical next step, not just for Quaise but for the entire clean energy sector," said Carlos Araque, CEO and President of Quaise Energy. “Together with Google, we are building the commercial foundation for superhot geothermal and cementing the United States as the global leader in geothermal power.”
Unlike traditional geothermal energy, which is limited to regions with naturally occurring hot water reservoirs rarely exceeding 600°F, Quaise develops Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS) at superhot conditions, which are expected to enable utility-scale projects to compete with today’s most efficient power plants. Quaise is simultaneously advancing the development of 
millimeter wave drilling
, a technology that can plug into existing drilling rigs to economically reach superhot geothermal conditions.
The agreement builds on Google’s clean energy portfolio and pioneering support of geothermal energy. From 2010 to 2025, Google signed more than 240 agreements to purchase nearly 35 GW of new clean energy, including more than 29 GW in North America across 148 projects. As demand for electricity continues to grow across the country, Google remains committed to ensuring that energy is affordable, reliable, and accessible for families and businesses.
Google pays for 100% of the power its data centers use, and as the company builds data centers, Google covers the infrastructure needs directly driven by its growth. In Oregon, the company’s three data center campuses have already achieved 
83% carbon-free energy
, with residents of Northern Wasco County People’s Utility District’s service territory having the 
third-lowest energy rates in the state
. Today’s partnership further extends Google's clean energy and geothermal leadership, while accelerating the development of superhot geothermal as a cost-effective, clean energy resource for Oregon residents and businesses.
“Google data centers are long-term investments in the communities we call home,” said Lucia Tian, Head of Advanced Energy Technologies at Google. “This collaboration with Quaise on superhot geothermal will help fuel local economic growth while helping to build a more robust and affordable energy future for Oregon.”
This new investment extends Google’s track record as a leader powering economic growth in Oregon. Google has proudly called Oregon home since 2006, when it built its first owned-and-operated data center in The Dalles. In the last 20 years, Google has invested more than $7 billion in the state, ranks as the largest taxpayer in Wasco County in recent years, and in 2025, the company helped provide $9.3 billion of economic activity for tens of thousands of Oregon businesses, nonprofits, publishers, creators, and developers.
Quaise’s agreement with Google is the latest milestone in a period of significant momentum for the company. In August 2026, it closed a 
$180 million Series B
 financing round, including a $35 million investment from Nabors Industries. 
Project Obsidian represents a significant economic opportunity for Central Oregon. The project is expected to support 200 to 400 jobs during construction, more than 50 permanent high-skilled jobs in engineering and operations, and establish the region as a hub for superhot geothermal development. Grid upgrades associated with the project will strengthen reliability for local consumers, and royalties from electricity sales will provide lasting economic benefits to the region. 
Technology proven at Obsidian is expected to reduce development costs for future superhot geothermal projects across the United States, where abundant subsurface heat remains inaccessible with conventional methods.

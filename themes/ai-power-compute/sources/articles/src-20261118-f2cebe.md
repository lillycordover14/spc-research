Palo Alto Networks bets $4.5B on AI startup Applied Compute as earnings top forecasts

																																	by								
Duncan Riley

Cybersecurity company Palo Alto Networks Inc. today 
announced
 that it has agreed to acquire artificial intelligence model training and inference startup Applied Compute Inc. in an all-stock deal valued at $4.5 billion.

The news came alongside 
fiscal first-quarter earnings
 and revenue that were well ahead of Wall Street expectations. Shares of Palo Alto Networks rose more than 8% in late trading, adding to a gain of nearly 10% during the regular session.

The acquisition, subject to regulatory clearance, is expected to close in Palo Alto Networks’ fiscal third quarter. Once the deal closes, Applied Compute co-founder and Chief Executive Yash Patil will become co-chief AI officer at Palo Alto Networks, with the startup’s research team also joining the company.

Founded in 2025
 by former OpenAI Group PBC researchers Patil, Rhythm Garg and Linden Li, Applied Compute offers a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight models. The platform is designed to make it easier for companies to train, serve and improve their own models, with an autoscaling inference engine and a model router called Shift-1 also included.

Applied Compute 
raised $350 million
 at a $3.25 billion valuation earlier this month in a round led by Elad Gil, with Nvidia Corp., Shopify Ventures and HubSpot Ventures also participating. At the time, the company said that its annualized recurring revenue had topped $100 million. Notable customers include Microsoft Corp. and DoorDash Inc. Coming into its acquisition, Applied Compute had raised more than $500 million in total.

Palo Alto Networks is pitching the deal as a bet that enterprise AI will be hybrid, as a large share of companies run open-weight models tuned to their own data. According to the company, no large provider is currently delivering those models securely. With Applied Compute, enterprises will be able to train and deploy open-weight models with security built in, either in the public cloud or in sovereign environments.

“Every enterprise is going to run a mix of open and closed models, and every one of those models needs to be secured,” Chairman and Chief Executive Nikesh Arora said. “Applied Compute has built the best post-training and inference platform we’ve seen, and its research team is exceptional.”

The deal came on the same day Palo Alto Networks reported what Arora called a record first quarter. For the quarter that ended on Oct. 31, the company reported adjusted earnings per share of $1.05, up from 93 cents in the same quarter of the previous year, on revenue of $3.44 billion, up 39% year-over-year. 
Analysts were expecting
 earnings of 97 cents per share on revenue of $3.31 billion.

Next-generation security annual recurring revenue grew 66% from a year earlier, to $9.73 billion. Remaining performance obligations rose 35%, to $21 billion. Helped by a tax benefit of $457 million, net income on an unadjusted basis came to $482 million, up from $334 million in the same quarter a year ago.

Chief Financial Officer Dipak Golechha said that given the current threat environment and the demand ahead, the company is “materially raising” its full-year guidance.

For its fiscal second quarter, Palo Alto Networks expects adjusted earnings per share of $1.01 to $1.03 on revenue of $3.53 billion to $3.55 billion. Full-year revenue is now forecast at $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion, up from the $14.10 billion to $14.20 billion 
guided in September
, with adjusted earnings of $4.26 to $4.30 per share ahead of the $4.18 expected by analysts polled by 
FactSet
.

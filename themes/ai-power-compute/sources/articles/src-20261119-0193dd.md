Google is disrupting itself with its embrace of artificial intelligence&nbsp;&nbsp;The Seattle Times

[page text]
By 

Kate Conger

The New York Times
 

SAN FRANCISCO — Marshall Evans, a marketer who works with small businesses, has helped home repair companies, fitness studios and other mom-and-pop companies buy Google search ads for more than a decade.
The ads — which are tailored to show up when people search on Google for a related term — have been effective in helping a business appear at the top of search results, increasing the chances that someone will click on the ad.
But now those traditional search ads often appear much lower, as Google highlights artificial intelligence-written responses to user queries above its usual list of links and ads.
“AI takes over such a large amount of the search results now,” Evans said.
Evans’ experience is an example of how AI has disrupted Google’s core business of search advertising. As the company plows hundreds of billions of dollars into developing AI and incorporating the technology across its services, some longtime advertisers are having second thoughts.

Over the past six months, Evans, who is based in North Carolina, has moved ad dollars away from Google search ads. For one company that sells trophies, he said, he is allocating $500 a month to the search ads, down from $2,000 a month about a year ago, when Google introduced a feature called AI Mode. And he is putting more of the money toward streaming services such as Tubi, other search engines like Bing and social media sites like Reddit and Nextdoor. 

Advertising

 

Last month, Google reported for the first time that search advertising had fallen to less than 50% of its $128 billion in quarterly revenue. Search ad revenue rose 12%, falling short of the 15% that Wall Street expected.
In effect, AI has become a double-edged sword for even the biggest companies. While Google stands to benefit enormously from the technology, including in its booming cloud computing business, AI is also taking a toll as the behemoth adjusts to the technology’s effects. 
“Google is trying to take a business that was the best business in the world for 10 years and transform it into a different business that doesn’t have the same advantages,” Thomas Monteiro, a senior analyst at Investing.com, said of the move from search to AI ads. “Making that transition is a lot harder than Google’s management thought it would be.” 
Google’s executives have said the company wanted to make AI search features appealing to users and would think about advertising later. At the top of its search results page, users often now see AI Overviews, an AI-written answer to the user’s query. The bottom of the summary also invites users to interact with a chatbot through the AI Mode feature. Advertising in its AI answers and chatbots has not yet taken off. 
“We are focused on building the best search user experience, followed by monetization,” Google CEO Sundar Pichai said during the earnings call.
Google said that users were flocking to its AI search tools and that the number of search queries was growing faster than it had in the past five years.

Advertising

 

“AI is driving an expansionary moment for Search, with our AI features leading to increased usage and overall query growth, including commercial queries,” said Thomas Schoenfelder, a Google spokesperson. “Our ads experiments have shown promising results, and we’re confident about our trajectory.” 
Advertisers can pay more for the chance to feature their ads in AI Mode, but there is no guarantee that the promotion will show up. When ads appear in AI results, some advertisers say, people are getting caught up in back-and-forth discussions with chatbots instead of clicking their ad and visiting their websites. 
“What we’re seeing is arguably the biggest change in searcher behavior,” said Valerie Schlosser, a marketing executive at VML, a global creative agency in New York. Whether people are talking to Google’s chatbot or other chatbots, she said, they are spending more time in conversation and less time surfing the web. 
Advertisers aren’t abandoning Google search, she added, but are trying to figure out how to survive in the new system. “Clients are very interested, and they want to test new things,” Schlosser said. “They are interested in ChatGPT and Perplexity, but they are also not totally pulling back from Google.” 
Max Anderson, CEO of OnlyFinder, a service that helps users find creators on adult entertainment site OnlyFans, said Google search ads had become more expensive in recent years. He has been running search ads for OnlyFinder on Google since 2022 and ran ads there for his previous company, a software startup, starting in 2014. 
“Our ads won’t show on the page at all, or it will show down the page, below an AI Overview,” he said. “Our costs have probably tripled or quadrupled in the last three years.”

Advertising

 

The solution was to spend less, he said, adding that he was “gradually and mathematically” pulling back as costs rise and was now devoting about half as much spending to Google ads as he did in the early days of the business. Anderson declined to discuss specific spending, saying the numbers were proprietary. 
Anderson isn’t planning to abandon Google but is flummoxed by how to get his business mentioned in AI Overviews. Google doesn’t offer advertisers a way to buy guaranteed placement in AI Overviews.
“We’d love to have a way as a business to predictably play there, but we have not seen one yet,” Anderson said.

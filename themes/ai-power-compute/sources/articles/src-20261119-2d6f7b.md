Cybersecurity firm Palo Alto Networks raised its annual revenue forecast and agreed to acquire AI startup Applied Compute for $4.5 billion. Shares of Palo Alto Networks jumped about 9% in after-hours trading on Wednesday to $368.70, driving gains among its rivals.

Palo Alto Networks has been positioning itself as an integrated cybersecurity provider through a series of recent acquisitions. With the acquisition of Applied Compute, the company aims to offer a technology platform that helps customers securely train and deploy open-weight AI models as they expand their use of artificial intelligence.

Palo Alto Networks said it believes the future of enterprise AI will be hybrid, with a large share of enterprises running open-source and open-weight models tailored to their data, cost and control requirements.

The acquisition will enable Palo Alto Networks to help enterprises train, adapt and deploy open-weight AI models with security built in, whether in public cloud environments or sovereign infrastructure. According to the company, this will help organizations strengthen compliance and security. Applied Compute will continue serving AI-native companies, digital-native businesses and large enterprises, while Palo Alto Networks will bring its platform to its global customer base.

Palo Alto Networks 
said
 Applied Compute has one of the industry’s strongest AI research teams. Its team will join Palo Alto Networks, with Applied Compute co-founder and CEO Yash Patil set to become Palo Alto Networks’ co-chief AI officer following the close of the 
transaction
.

“Applied Compute extends Palo Alto’s push beyond traditional cybersecurity into enterprise AI,” TD Cowen analyst Shaul Eyal said.

The deal is expected to close in the third quarter of fiscal 2027.

Palo Alto Networks now expects fiscal 2027 revenue to reach between $14.52 billion and $14.57 billion, compared with its previous forecast of $14.10 billion to $14.20 billion.

The company’s first-quarter revenue reached $3.45 billion, up 39% from a year earlier and above analysts’ estimates of $3.31 billion, according to data compiled by LSEG.

Palo Alto Networks is also in talks to 
acquire
 Clutch Security for $500 million in cash, along with additional milestone-based payments, according to a person familiar with the matter.

Clutch is a three-year-old startup that was last valued at $85 million when it raised a $20 million Series A funding round in January 2025, led by Lightspeed and SignalFire.

The company is currently approaching $10 million in annual recurring revenue (ARR). If completed, the acquisition would value Clutch at approximately 50 times its ARR, a common metric used to value software companies.

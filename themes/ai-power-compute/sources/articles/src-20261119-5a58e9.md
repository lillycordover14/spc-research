Applied Compute CEO Yash Patil says every company “should own its intelligence” as it joins Palo Alto Networks in a US$4.5bn deal
Palo Alto Networks
 has agreed to acquire 
Applied Compute
 in an all-stock transaction valued at US$4.5bn, strengthening its position in the enterprise AI security market and sending its stock soaring high in pre-market trading. 
The deal will bring Applied Compute’s post-training and inference platform for open-weight AI models, along with its research team, into 
Palo Alto Networks’ growing AI security
 portfolio.
The move reflects Palo Alto Networks’ belief that enterprises will increasingly rely on a combination of open-source, open-weight and closed AI models, or as it says: “the future of enterprise AI is hybrid”.
Why did Palo Alto Networks stock rise after the Applied Compute deal?
Since the news of the acquisition, Palo Alto Networks’ stock (NASDAQ: PANW) has risen 9.47% at the time of writing, trading at US$372.12 in the 19 November 2026 pre-market session, up from previous close of US$339.92, as per data from Trading 212. 
Every enterprise is going to run a mix of open and closed models and every one of those models needs to be secured
Nikesh Arora, Chairman and CEO of Palo Alto Networks
The stock bump can also be analysed as a result of the company’s stellar financial results for the fiscal first quarter of 2027, as company performance beat analyst expectations. 
The expected earnings per share (EPS) was US$0.97, though the adjusted EPS landed at US$1.05. 
Revenue reached US$3.44bn, up 39% year on year. 
Palo Alto Networks’ next-generation security
 annual recurring revenue (NGS ARR) for the fiscal first quarter of 2027 grew by 66% year on year to US$9.73bn.
“Proud to report record Palo Alto Networks Q1 results, which exceeded guidance across all metrics,” writes Dipak Golechha, Chief Financial Officer at Palo Alto Networks, on LinkedIn. 
Dipak Golechha, Chief Financial Officer at Palo Alto Networks | Credit: Dipak Golechha/LinkedIn (modified)
“Accelerating growth on each of our three segments - Network & AI Security, Security Operations and Identity drove the addition of over US$600m in Net New NGS ARR in Q1 alone. 
“Prisma AIRS, our AI Security platform, reached ~US$170m in ARR, continuing its pace as the fastest-growing product in customer history as we continue to be the partner of choice to securely enable AI adoption in the enterprise.”
Applied Compute’s AI research team to strengthen Palo Alto Networks’ capabilities
Even though the future may be hybrid, Palo Alto Networks maintains that businesses currently lack a large-scale provider capable of delivering open-weight AI models with 
comprehensive security
 built in.
Through the acquisition, the company aims to address this gap by enabling enterprises to customise and deploy open-weight models while maintaining security and compliance standards. 
Rhythm, Linden and I founded Applied Compute with a belief that was unusual at the time: that every company should own its intelligence
Yash Patil, Co-Founder and CEO of Applied Compute
The combined offering will support deployments across public cloud platforms and sovereign environments, giving organisations greater flexibility over where and how their AI systems operate.
“Every enterprise is going to run a mix of 
open and closed models
 and every one of those models needs to be secured,” says Nikesh Arora, Chairman and CEO of Palo Alto Networks.
“Applied Compute has built the best post-training and inference platform we’ve seen, and its research team is exceptional.
“We saw that firsthand as a prospective customer and our 
Unit 42
 Frontier AI Assessment confirmed their strong security foundations. 
Nikesh Arora, Chairman and CEO of Palo Alto Networks | Credit: Palo Alto Networks (modified)
“Together, we will be a leading provider, helping customers train, adapt and deploy open models securely, whether in the public cloud or in their own sovereign environments."
Following the completion of the transaction, Applied Compute’s Co-Founder and CEO, 
Yash Patil
, will become Palo Alto Networks’ Co-Chief AI Officer. 
Yash says the acquisition would accelerate the company's reach while placing security at the heart of its technology.
“Rhythm, Linden and I founded Applied Compute with a belief that was unusual at the time: that every company should own its intelligence,” he notes on LinkedIn. 
“We think training, serving, and improving proprietary models will become a normal part of running a company.
“To do useful work, a model needs to access sensitive data and systems, comply with policies, and remain explainable, verifiable and auditable. 
“This is the reason we’ve always prioritised security and why joining Palo Alto Networks will help us go further faster. 
“Customers will still own and 
control their models
, and I’m excited for all the ways our combined capabilities can serve them even better.”
The all-stock acquisition is valued at US$4.5bn, subject to adjustments. 
Completion remains dependent on customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance, though Palo Alto Networks expects the transaction to close in its fiscal third quarter of 2027.

Palo Alto Networks to snap up AI model startup Applied Compute for $4.5B

Nvidia, Blackstone-backed upstart marks PAN acquisition number eight for the year

November 19, 2026

By

Sam Woodcock
, iland
                    

         
            Have your say
        
    

– Getty Images

Palo Alto Networks has unveiled plans for yet another acquisition, striking an agreement to snap up open-weight AI model firm Applied Compute.
In what marks its eighth deal of the year, the cybersecurity giant looks set to acquire a platform serving enterprises with post-training and inference means for open models.
In an all-stock deal worth $4.5 billion, Palo Alto would gain Applied’s platform and research team, tapping its tech to allow users to train and deploy models securely either in public cloud or on-prem environments.
Expected to close in the vendor's fiscal third quarter of 2027 (covering February to April), Yash Patil, Applied Compute's co-founder and CEO, will become Palo Alto Networks' co-chief AI officer upon closing, although the title is not currently shared by anyone at the firm.
“We built Applied Compute with an exceptional team of AI researchers and engineers to make it easy for any company to train, serve, and improve proprietary models. Joining Palo Alto Networks will put security at the center of all our products and accelerate our traction with the world's largest organizations,” Patil said in a statement.
Applied Compute was only founded in 2025 but was 
valued at $3.25 billion
 following its $350 million funding raise in early November. Nvidia, Blackstone, and Lux Capital count among its backers with companies using its offerings including Microsoft, Shopify, and DoorDash.
The startup claimed that its team size tripled from January, 85% of whom are researchers and engineers. They’ll now join a company rapidly expanding through acquisitions, with the likes of 
Clutch Security
, 
Koi Security
, 
Portkey
, 
Console
, and 
Embrace
 among the brands to have joined or set to join Palo Alto Networks.
“Every enterprise is going to run a mix of open and closed models, and every one of those models needs to be secured. Applied Compute has built the best post-training and inference platform we've seen, and its research team is exceptional. We saw that firsthand as a prospective customer, and our Unit 42 Frontier AI Assessment confirmed their strong security foundations,” PAN CEO and chair Nikesh Arora said in a statement.
“Together, we will be a leading provider, helping customers train, adapt and deploy open models securely, whether in the public cloud or in their own sovereign environments.”

Palo Alto Networks
 has agreed to acquire Applied Compute, an artificial intelligence startup founded by three former OpenAI researchers, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 bn.

The company has surpassed $100 mn in annualized revenue despite operating for approximately 18 months. Its founders, Yash Patil, Rhythm Garg and Linden Li, are all 25 or younger.

Palo Alto Networks to acquire Applied Compute in a $4.5 bn all-stock transaction, approximately 18 months after the startup was founded.

Applied Compute was established by three former OpenAI researchers aged 25 or younger and has exceeded $100 mn in annualized revenue.

The company develops specialized AI models using proprietary enterprise data, with Microsoft, DoorDash and Harvey among its customers.

Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora told analysts that the acquisition could create a business larger than the company’s existing cybersecurity operations.

Applied Compute develops AI models using proprietary enterprise data

Applied Compute’s founders developed their business concept during a January 2025 ski trip to Lake Tahoe, shortly after Chinese AI developer DeepSeek released a powerful open-source model.

Patil told Forbes that his perspective on AI development differed from that of many colleagues at OpenAI. The three researchers left approximately five months later.

Their company takes open-source language models, adapts them using customers’ internal information and operates the resulting systems for individual businesses.

Applied Compute calls this approach “specific intelligence”, emphasizing models optimized for particular organizations rather than general-purpose applications. Patil argues that large corporations have strong incentives to retain control of their proprietary information. Banks such as JPMorgan, he said, are “never going to give that data away.”

Microsoft, DoorDash and Harvey use Applied Compute technology

Applied Compute’s customers include Microsoft, DoorDash and legal AI company Harvey. DoorDash used its technology to develop a model that helps restaurants generate accurate menus during onboarding.

Harvey deployed an AI agent to review large volumes of litigation documents and contracts. Its head of applied research told Forbes that development took less than two months, compared with conventional timelines ranging from several months to years.

Patil said some of its specialized models can operate at one-tenth the cost of frontier models from leading AI developers. Harvey described the improvement achieved through its implementation as “an order of magnitude difference.” These results concern specific applications rather than a general performance comparison across all AI workloads.

How businesses can apply specialized AI

Applied Compute’s approach relies on information unavailable to general-purpose AI systems, including internal records, customer communications and operational experience.

Smaller businesses can apply a similar principle without developing their own language models. Customer emails, sales conversations, reviews and cancellation records contain information about purchasing decisions, recurring questions and service expectations.

Organizing these records alongside pricing and company policies gives existing AI tools a more relevant basis for generating communications.

For example, a childcare provider that has conducted 200 facility tours may know which questions parents ask most frequently and which factors influence enrollment. That information can improve automated follow-up emails.

The approach can also be applied to sales inquiries, quotation requests and marketing materials. Starting with one recurring task allows a business to assess whether company-specific information improves the results.

Palo Alto Networks adds enterprise credibility to Applied Compute

Applied Compute’s rapid revenue growth and high-profile customer base have not eliminated the challenges associated with selling enterprise software as a relatively new company.

Large organizations require assurances about security and reliability before allowing external providers to work with sensitive internal information.

During the earnings call, Arora said another CEO had contacted him to express relief that Palo Alto Networks’ involvement would help address concerns about trusting enterprise AI to a small team.

Patil said joining the cybersecurity company would “accelerate our traction with the world’s largest organizations.”

Palo Alto Networks’ established enterprise relationships and security reputation could help Applied Compute expand its customer base after the acquisition closes.

For smaller providers facing similar trust barriers, recognized certifications, established customer references, partnerships and contractual guarantees can help reduce buyers’ concerns.

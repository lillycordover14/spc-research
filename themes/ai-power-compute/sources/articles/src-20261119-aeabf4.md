Andy Burnham has piled pressure on 
Google
, urging them to do the “right thing” and take down a YouTube recording in which convicted rapist 
Russell Brand
 claims he is innocent. 
The prime minister’s official spokesman said ultimately the decision lay with the platform, while 
The Independent
 understands Mr Burnham would like the disgraced comedian’s entire channel taken down. 
No10 added: “Victims have shown incredible bravery, and they now deserve peace from him… the prime minister thinks that the public has heard enough from Russell Brand now and the focus should be on his victims.”
Brand’s wife Laura Gallacher
 shared the message from the jailed sex offender in which he insisted he was not guilty of the offences for which he is serving a 10-and-a-half year term.
According to the 
BBC
, Google denied the request because it determined its rules had not been broken.

Downing Street described his comments as “clearly appalling”.
A spokesperson for Google said: “We’re aware of the new content and our teams are investigating. We suspended Russell Brand’s channel from the YouTube Partner Program in September 2023. The channel has not been able to monetise on YouTube since then.”
The Ministry of Justice (MoJ) submitted an official request to 
Google
, which owns YouTube, asking for the video to be removed.
An MoJ spokesperson said: “It is only right that the Government asks for content that is distressing for victims to be taken down, and we strongly believe YouTube should remove this content.

“Removal ultimately depends on the platform’s own terms.”
Housing minister Matthew Pennycook was asked by 
Sky News
 if it was correct the Ministry of Justice had made a removal request to the tech company, and told the broadcaster: “It is correct. I don’t know if you’ve seen the video. It’s quite a disturbing video, actually, but abhorrent in the message it’s conveying.
“I mean, imagine if you’re one of the victims of Brand’s heinous crimes and you see that video. Prisoners are already banned from making statements to the media. 
“We think this is an exceptional case where MoJ ministers have asked Google to remove this specific content and we hope they’ll do so.”
Asked if it was the YouTube channel or the video content the Government wanted to see removed, he replied: “Well, the content is what we are focused on because of the particular message that has been conveyed. They have taken action in the past on this, in 2023 I think, they restricted the channel on the basis of some of Brand’s behaviour having harmful effects.
“So, it’s within their gift to do so, but it’s based on their terms and conditions. We have made that request to them in this instance because we think it is right that it comes down.

“I go back to who are the victims in this. If you are the victims seeing that message, absolutely no remorse about the crimes that were committed. I think that’s deeply harmful, so does the Government. So, we hope Google listens and removes that specific video.”
Meta disabled Brand’s Instagram and Facebook accounts after his conviction.
During Sunday’s episode of the couple’s YouTube podcast, Ms Gallacher read out the message from Brand, in which he spoke of his life behind bars. 
The statement has prompted an official investigation into whether Brand has breached prison rules. 
Labour’s deputy leader Lucy Powell earlier condemned media organisations for playing the message, criticising broadcasters for “amplifying” Brand’s words.
She told LBC Radio: “I was appalled at that and actually I think mainstream media shouldn’t have transmitted that either.”
She said Brand was “someone who’s not showing a jot of remorse, a jot of any sensitivity at all to their victims”.
“This is somebody who’s been convicted and found guilty of very, very serious offences and rape.”
Asked why she wanted to censor that message, she said: “It’s a platform I don’t think… should have been amplified, to be honest.”

Palo Alto Networks Plans $4.5 Billion Acquisition of AI Startup Applied Compute

Palo Alto Networks
 plans to acquire 
Applied Compute
, a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight models, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, the company said in a Wednesday (Nov. 18) 
press release
.

 

The planned acquisition will boost Palo Alto Network’s ability to bring secure open-weight artificial intelligence to enterprises, according to the release.

With the acquisition, Palo Alto Networks, a global AI security firm, will add Applied Compute’s platform and AI research team to help enterprises adopt AI securely, whether they choose open-weight models, closed frontier models or both, the release said.

Palo Alto Networks expects the transaction to close during the company’s fiscal third quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory clearance and other customary closing conditions, per the release.

“Every enterprise is going to run a mix of open and closed models, and every one of those models needs to be secured,” Palo Alto Networks Chairman and CEO 
Nikesh Arora
 said in the release. “Applied Compute has built the best post-training and inference platform we’ve seen, and its research team is exceptional.”

Applied Compute’s AI research team will join Palo Alto Networks and will help the company embed AI across all its offerings. Applied Compute’s co-founder and CEO, 
Yash Patil
, will become Palo Alto Networks’ co-chief AI officer after the close of the transaction, according to the release.

“We built Applied Compute with an exceptional team of AI researchers and engineers to make it easy for any company to train, serve and improve proprietary models,” Patil said in the release. “Joining Palo Alto Networks will put security at the center of all our products and accelerate our traction with the world’s largest organizations.”

Applied Compute said in a Nov. 2 blog post that the company was founded less than 18 months ago and has grown from zero to $100 million in 
annualized revenue run rate
 during that time.

PYMNTS reported Tuesday (Nov. 17) that Applied Compute is one of the training partners Nvidia relies on to help enterprises tailor 
open models
 with their own data, which feeds the demand for Nvidia compute.

The Information reported Nov. 4 that Palo Alto Networks had made four 
acquisitions
 this year and was in talks to acquire 
Clutch Securit
y for around $500 million.

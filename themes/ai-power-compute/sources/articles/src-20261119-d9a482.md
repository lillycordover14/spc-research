As AI has become a thorn in the side of the security industry, Palo Alto Networks (PAN) has reached an agreement to acquire Applied Compute, an AI model training platform.
"We believe many of the major disruptions of the last few weeks, including outages across major enterprises and governments, were partially due to unintended consequences of patching and configuration changes, as IT teams simply cannot patch these vulnerabilities fast enough without compromising their production environments," 
said
 Nikesh Arora, chairman and CEO of PAN, during the company's 
fiscal Q1 2027 earnings call
 on Wednesday. "The second effect is the rise of AI-assisted cyberattacks."
So in the spirit of "hair of the dog that bit me," PAN is ingesting Applied Compute to make open-weight AI more secure for corporate customers. The answer to insecurity brought on by AI is buying security with AI.

REG AD

In its 
announcement
 of the tie-up, PAN declares "the future of enterprise AI is hybrid, and that a large share of enterprises will run on open-source and open-weight models tuned to their own data, cost, and control needs."

REG AD

That view has been clouded a bit by Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang's fall from grace: Back in July, he came to the defense of open-weight models as a defense against the hegemony of purveyors of closed-weight commercial models like Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI. His resignation as CEO of Nvidia has led to worries that the open-weight ecosystem has lost some lobbying power at a time when the US is wary about the uptake of models made in China.
Nonetheless, PAN clearly sees a future for open-weight models.
"Every enterprise is going to run a mix of open and closed models, and every one of those models needs to be secured," said Nikesh Arora, chairman and CEO of PAN, in a statement. "Applied Compute has built the best post-training and inference platform we've seen, and its research team is exceptional."
The company expects the $4.5 billion all-stock transaction to add more than $1 billion to the company's Next Generation Security business by FY 2029. 
Arora, on the company's earnings call, explained the pricey bet as recognition of the corporate desire to avoid vendor lock-in and to retain control of data.
"We're already seeing the desire for sovereignty and control compel enterprises to adopt open-weight and open-source models," he said. "Early signs of that shift are clear, with open-weight models growing from under 5 percent of token traffic to more than 60 percent over the past year."
Beyond the sticker price, PAN is ready to up its infrastructure ante. Arora said the biz expects to build infrastructure supporting more than 100 megawatts of inference capacity, a project that will take "a modest CapEx commitment of less than $3 billion through fiscal 2030." ®

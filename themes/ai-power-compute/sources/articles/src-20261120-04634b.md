Hundreds of Google employees are urging the tech giant to curb its aggressive approach to rolling out artificial intelligence in K-12 schools.
In a petition published on Thursday, workers called on the company to let schools use Google Search without the AI features that are increasingly prominent in query results. More than 540 Google employees and contractors signed the letter, according to a spokesperson for the Alphabet Workers Union, which organized the effort. 
“Any opportunity to drive positive change with AI in education will rely on educators having the freedom to choose how to implement it in their curriculum,” workers wrote in the letter, which is addressed to Alphabet Inc. Chief Executive Officer Sundar Pichai, vice president and head of search Elizabeth Reid and Chris Phillips, vice president and general manager of education. “Cutting off access to Search for schools that don’t wish to use AI will not help persuade our users, and will more likely result in them shutting out Google products altogether.”

Google said in a statement that it was working closely with schools to ensure its products benefitted students and teachers.“We hear parents and teachers’ concerns about students using AI to shortcut learning, and we share the same goal: help students learn,” the company said. 
Amid a torrent of criticism from parents and schools, Google has said it plans to turn off AI tools, including its search engine, by default for K-12 student accounts. School administrators that switch on Google Search will have to accept the AI features that come with it.
Google has said that its moves are intended to give schools more control over AI in the classroom. But the announcement sparked fierce objections, including from Senator Ed Markey, a Massachusetts Democrat who urged Google to change course.

Google’s Gemini lets kids ask questions to get help with homework, create study guides and access classroom materials. AI is increasingly embedded in search, and the company is adding such learning features as interactive explanations.
The search giant’s products are a gateway to AI for many American children. Google’s Chrome operating system ran on 58% of the laptops and tablets shipped last year to K-12 schools, according to Futuresource Consulting, a research firm. As its rivalry with startups Anthropic PBC and OpenAI heats up, Google has brought more and more AI to those devices, reaching students as they form tech habits that may last for life.
In an interview, a Google employee who works on education said colleagues have been hearing from friends and family who are concerned about the company’s approach to AI in the classroom. Google’s announcement to schools this month came as a surprise to many on the education team, said the worker, who requested anonymity for fear of retaliation.
Over the past year, company leadership has justified bringing AI to students by saying that if Google doesn’t do so, other firms will – potentially with a less responsible approach, the employee said. Google personnel are increasingly pushing back on that air of inevitability, this person said. He added that he doubted it would be technically infeasible to offer a version of the search engine without the generative AI features, as schools have requested.
Earlier this month, a North Carolina family sued Google alleging that their daughter spent months discussing suicidal thoughts with the company’s search AI tools on a school-issued device. Google said it’s reviewing the claims and strives to connect people in crisis with appropriate resources.
For schools that want to shield students from AI, it’s not so simple to walk away from Google Search, said Dan Carroll, the co-founder of ed tech startup Clever. Chromebooks are designed to run seamlessly with Google software, search included, and training teachers and students to use a new search engine would take time and resources, he said. Carroll said he suspects Google is reluctant to have students use a version of its search engine that feels frozen in time as the general public uses AI more and more.

“Either Google listens to schools and gives students a version of search that’s already feeling outdated compared to the market, or they alienate schools by requiring they use the new version,” Carroll said. “It’s a real strategic pickle.”
Employees themselves expressed concern about alienating schools in their letter to the company’s leadership. 
“It is our job as Googlers to respect the user, and listen to them when they have concerns about our products,” they wrote.

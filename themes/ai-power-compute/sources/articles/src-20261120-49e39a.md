                        Make new notes open as text notes by default
               

            Skip the extra step
    

Close

Google Keep captures all kinds of information, from handwritten sketches and photos to audio recordings and checklists.
 
However, if you primarily use it to jot down thoughts, ideas, and quick reminders, having to choose a note type every time you tap the 
+ button 
feels like an unnecessary extra step.
 
Thankfully, Google added a setting that brings back one-tap text note creation.
 
To enable it, open Google Keep on your Android phone, tap the 
menu icon
 in the upper-left corner, and select 
Settings
. Under 
Note creation
, enable 
Create text notes by default
.
 
Now, tapping the floating 
+ button
 opens a blank text note with the keyboard ready. You can still long-press the 
+ button
 to display options for creating lists, drawings, image notes, and audio notes.
 

                        Turn off rich link previews to keep notes tidy
               

            Save URLs without the bulky preview cards
    

 I often paste website links into Google Keep when saving research, articles to read later, or useful reference material. However, Keep automatically turns these URLs into rich link previews, complete with thumbnails and snippets from the linked pages.
 
While this can be handy when I want to revisit a website, it also makes notes unnecessarily bulky. If I save several links in one note, the previews take up considerable space and make it harder to scan the information I've written.
 
I prefer keeping my notes compact, with URLs appearing as simple text rather than large preview cards.
 
To change this, open Google Keep, tap the 
menu icon
 in the upper-left corner, and go to 
Settings
. Find
 Display rich link previews
 and turn it off. From then on, Keep retains the URLs you paste without generating the accompanying preview cards.
 
This setting is useful if you use Keep as a research repository or frequently save multiple links in the same note. You still have the original URLs, so you can tap them when you want to visit the web pages.
 

                        I made Keep accessible from my home screen
               

            Create new notes without opening Google Keep
    

 I don't always want to open an app, wait for it to load, and then create a note. Sometimes I just need to jot down an idea before I forget it.
 
Google Keep has a Quick capture widget that solves this problem. It lets me create notes and lists from my Android home screen.
 
To add it, long-press an empty area of your home screen and tap 
Widgets
. Scroll down to Google Keep, expand its widget options, and find 
Quick capture
. Touch and hold it, then drag it to your preferred spot on the home screen. You can also resize the widget to fit your layout.
 
The widget provides shortcuts to create different types of notes without opening Keep. I can start a text note, create a checklist, record an audio note, make a drawing, or capture a photo.
 

                        Switch to List view for easier browsing
               

            Make your notes easier to scan
    

Close

Google Keep displays notes in a grid by default, placing them in separate cards across multiple columns. While this layout is useful when you want to see several notes at once, I find it can get a little cluttered when I'm juggling lots of saved information.
 
Switching to List view gives my notes a cleaner, more linear layout, making it easier to scan them and find what I'm looking for.
 
To change the layout, open Google Keep and look for the 
List view icon
 near the top of the screen, next to your 
profile picture
 on Android.
 
Tap it, and Keep arranges your notes in a single column instead of a grid. Tap the same icon again to return to Grid view when you prefer.
 
I like List view when I'm using Keep on my phone because the wider note cards make individual entries easier to read. It's also useful when I have several notes with longer titles or descriptions and want to get a better preview without opening each one.
 
Grid view still makes sense if you prefer a compact overview and want to see more notes simultaneously, so there's no universally better option.
 

                        Pin Google Keep to Android's Share menu
               

            Make capturing information from other apps easier
    

Close

I often come across interesting articles, useful website links, quotes, or bits of information while browsing the web or scrolling through other apps.

Instead of copying everything, switching to Keep, creating a new note, and pasting the content manually, I use Android's Share menu to send information to Keep.
 
To do this, open the web page, select the text or link you want to save, and tap 
Share
. From the Android sharing panel, select 
Google Keep
. Keep lets you create a new note with the shared content or add it to an existing note. You can then add other details before saving it.
 
Android sometimes rearranges the apps in the sharing panel based on your usage, which means Keep might not always appear in the same place. However, you can pin it to make it easier to find.
 
To pin Google Keep, open the 
Share
 menu from any app, find Keep in the list of available apps, long-press its icon, and tap 
Pin
. Keep moves to the front of the app-sharing list.
 
The next time you want to save an article, image, or snippet of text, you can select Keep without scrolling through the entire list.

At just 23, Indian American entrepreneur Yash Patil has turned an 18-month-old artificial intelligence startup into a $4.5 billion acquisition deal. His company, Applied Compute, built by a team of just a few dozen people, grew from zero revenue to more than $100 million in annualized revenue run-rate in just over 18 months. The remarkable growth caught the attention of cybersecurity giant Palo Alto Networks, which has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire the startup in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion.

Patil’s journey from Stanford University to OpenAI and eventually building a multibillion-dollar AI company has quickly become a remarkable entrepreneurial story in Silicon Valley.

Patil co-founded Applied Compute with fellow Stanford alumni Rhythm Garg and Linden Li. The company develops a platform that helps businesses customize, deploy and operate open-weight AI models, allowing enterprises to build and control AI systems suited to their specific requirements.

The acquisition will bring Applied Compute’s platform and AI research team into Palo Alto Networks, strengthening the cybersecurity company’s efforts to help enterprises adopt artificial intelligence securely.

Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora described the acquisition as part of the company’s broader ambition to establish itself at the center of the expanding AI industry. According to The Wall Street Journal, Arora said, “We are trying to become central to the AI economy.”

From Stanford to OpenAI

Patil’s journey into artificial intelligence began during his time at Stanford University, where he studied computer science and explored entrepreneurship alongside his academic work.

Born to Indian immigrant parents, Patil grew up in Austin, Texas, and attended the Liberal Arts and Science Academy. During his student years, he worked on several technology projects, including Armadillo Labs, which focused on developing machine learning tools for e-commerce websites. He also co-directed TreeHacks, Stanford’s annual hackathon.

An encounter with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman at a dinner hosted for Stanford engineering students became an important moment in his career.

Following the launch of ChatGPT in 2022, Patil emailed Altman expressing his interest in working on the chatbot. Altman subsequently encouraged him to apply for OpenAI’s residency program.

Patil eventually joined OpenAI as a member of its technical staff. It was there that he met Garg and Li, who would later become his co-founders at Applied Compute.

Building a billion-dollar AI business in 18 months

The idea behind Applied Compute began taking shape in January 2025. Its founders focused on a growing demand among businesses to develop and operate their own AI models, particularly open-weight models that can be customized for specific organizational needs.

The company quickly attracted attention from major Silicon Valley investors. By April 2026, Applied Compute had reached unicorn status after securing $80 million in funding from Kleiner Perkins at a valuation of $1.3 billion.

The proposed $4.5 billion acquisition marks another significant jump in its valuation and highlights the growing interest in AI infrastructure and enterprise-focused artificial intelligence.

For Patil, the deal represents a remarkable milestone at an age when many technology entrepreneurs are still working to establish their first ventures. His journey from Stanford and OpenAI to building a company that attracted a multibillion-dollar acquisition agreement in less than two years highlights the pace at which opportunities are emerging in the AI industry.

At the Slush conference in Helsinki on November 19, 
Chenyu Zhao
, co-founder of 
Fireworks AI
, argued that many businesses can use smaller, lightweight models rather than relying solely on more expensive general-purpose systems. His position is about when a cutting-edge model is needed and when a more specialized solution is sufficient, according to the 
interview published by TechCrunch
.
Fireworks AI provides infrastructure for running models and adapting them to organizations’ data, as described on its 
official enterprise services page
. TechCrunch notes that the open-source models can be downloaded and customized. Fireworks presents its platform as a model selection tool based on quality, speed, and cost.
Zhao contrasted the general-purpose models being developed by OpenAI and Anthropic with smaller models that can be tailored to specific needs. He argued that this specialization can help a company differentiate its services, rather than using exactly the same generic solution as its competitors.
The Fireworks AI co-founder also claimed that the gap between open-weight models and leading models has narrowed. In his estimation, the best open options are about 3 months behind the leading systems, while the gap can be as much as 6 months. This is his own estimate, not an independent measurement presented in the interview.

What Chenyu Zhao says about the cost of AI models
According to Chenyu Zhao, companies often initially choose a known state-of-the-art model because it reduces uncertainty while developing a new product. But when a service gains a large number of users and using models becomes a significant operational expense, he believes it is worth considering alternatives.
As an example, he cited businesses with more than 10,000 or 100,000 users spending $10,000 to $100,000 per month on model usage. This is his estimate, not a universal rule. The actual cost depends on the requests, workload, frequency of use, and required quality.
Chenyu Zhao did not propose eliminating cutting-edge models, but rather coexisting with smaller models. The former, in his view, are useful for complex design, while the latter can serve simpler tasks. The comparison, he said, should be about the cost of completing a task, not just the per-use charge.
Query routing sends each job to the model that seems most appropriate. Fireworks describes relevant tools, but its statements about quality, speed, and cost remain the company's claims, not independent benchmarking.

What businesses should check before switching
Moving to a smaller model does not guarantee lower overall costs. Organizations need to factor in the required compute infrastructure, model tuning, quality monitoring, and the time required to correct incorrect responses. Fireworks offers different deployment options, such as shared execution and dedicated installations, with different features.
It is equally important to consider where the data is stored and how long it is retained. The official Fireworks page lists options related to infrastructure isolation and data control, but each business should confirm the specific terms of the service they will use. Commercial descriptions are not a substitute for reviewing the contract, technical documentation, and data protection policy.

A meaningful evaluation requires testing with tasks that the organization actually performs. The team can compare accuracy, latency, consistency, and total cost on identical requests, against controlled data. If the model fails in critical cases, a lower cost per use is not enough to justify the change.
What does Fireworks AI's proposal mean for Greece?
For businesses in Greece, the question is whether an open model works reliably in their own linguistic and professional conditions. The test should include Greek texts, technical terms and real industry demands, not just examples in English.
SecNews' technical team recommends testing with representative workloads before moving sensitive functions. Managers should review the use of personal data, access to records, and the ability to verify responses. Zhao's position is a reason for comparison, not proof that the same solution fits every business.

Fireworks AI’s proposal is to treat AI as a set of tools, not as a single option. For a business, the next step is to measure whether a more specialized model meets the same need with acceptable quality, security, and cost. The final decision should be based on testing and usage conditions, not just the founder’s general comparison.

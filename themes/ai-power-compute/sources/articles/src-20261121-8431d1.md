The News:
 Together AI launched Qwen3 TTS on its serverless inference platform on November 20, 2026 
[1]
, making the open text-to-speech model available on both Together Serverless Inference and dedicated endpoints 
[1]
. Priced at $20 per 1M characters 
[1]
, the model offers nine preset voices spanning English, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean native speakers 
[1]
, each supporting 10 languages including German, French, Russian, Portuguese, Spanish, and Italian 
[1]
. Together AI's serving stack delivers first audio in as little as 97ms 
[1]
, with both HTTP streaming and a WebSocket API for real-time voice agent applications 
[1]
. A companion VoiceFinder tool at voicefinder.together.ai lets developers preview voices on custom text before committing to a preset 
[1]
. The underlying model is Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B, developed by the Qwen team at Alibaba Cloud 
[1]
.

Together AI Brings Real-Time Voice to Its Open Inference Stack

Analysis:
 This launch is more than a catalog addition. By combining sub-100ms latency, multilingual coverage, and plain-language delivery control in a single open model, Together AI closes a meaningful gap in its stack and positions itself as a credible full-stack inference provider for voice-enabled agentic applications 
[1]
. The timing aligns directly with where enterprise AI spending is heading.

Architecture That Enables Real-Time Agents, Not Just Batch Synthesis

The design choice that makes Qwen3 TTS production-relevant is its separation of speaker identity from delivery style 
[1]
. Developers choose one of nine preset voices, then control tone through plain-language instructions such as 'excited and upbeat' or 'sad and disappointed,' without altering the chosen voice. This decoupling matters for agentic deployments where a consistent brand voice must adapt emotionally across interactions. Paired with HTTP streaming and a WebSocket API for text that arrives in pieces 
[1]
, and first audio in as little as 97ms 
[1]
, the model crosses the latency threshold required for real-time conversation. Together AI's inference team explicitly tuned the serving stack for this benchmark, signaling that production voice performance was a deliberate engineering priority, not an incidental outcome. Latency metrics like time-to-first-token are a key production monitoring priority for 41.8% of enterprise decision-makers 
[2]
, making the 97ms figure a meaningful differentiator in procurement conversations.

Voice Lands at the Center of Enterprise AI Priorities

The use case alignment here is direct. Customer support and experience automation, covering autonomous chatbots, virtual assistants, and service automation, is the leading generative AI application among enterprise decision-makers at 56.5% 
[2]
. Customer experience, defined as automated service, personalized engagement, and support triage, ranks among the top areas where organizations plan to deploy agentic AI in the next 18 months at 48.6% 
[2]
. Both use cases depend on voice as a delivery layer when interactions move beyond text interfaces. Multilingual capability amplifies this value: every Qwen3 TTS preset voice supports all 10 languages 
[1]
, allowing a product to maintain a consistent voice identity when it enters a new market. For enterprises managing global customer operations, that consistency reduces the overhead of maintaining separate voice assets per region.

Open Inference as a Full-Stack Alternative

Together AI's strategic positioning rests on offering developers flexibility that proprietary ecosystems do not. Competing frameworks remain primarily optimized for and tightly integrated with OpenAI's proprietary model ecosystem, enforcing model lock-in rather than providing adapters for third-party foundational models 
[3]
. Together AI's approach, which now spans language, code, and voice, lets development teams compose their stack from best-available open models without committing to a single vendor's roadmap. This resonates with how most enterprises actually operate: 51% of enterprise decision-makers favor a balanced mix of in-house and vendor solutions 
[2]
, a preference that maps directly to the flexibility Together AI's serverless inference provides. Adding a production-ready TTS capability reinforces the platform's credibility as a complete alternative rather than a partial substitute. The VoiceFinder tool 
[1]
 further reduces adoption friction by letting developers evaluate voices on their own content before writing a single line of production code.

Market Scale Provides the Commercial Backdrop

The AI platforms market is on a base-case trajectory to reach $1.46 trillion in 2026, with a 52.5% CAGR projected through 2030 
[4]
. At that growth rate, the competitive question is not whether developer spend will materialize, but which platforms will capture it. Together AI's rapid expansion of its model library, now covering language, code, and voice, positions it to serve developers who want a single inference provider across modalities. Qwen3 TTS, built on the Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B model from Alibaba Cloud's Qwen team 
[1]
, brings a capable open-weight foundation to that library without requiring Together AI to develop the underlying model itself. That asset-light approach to model acquisition, combined with proprietary serving optimization for latency, reflects a platform strategy focused on inference quality and breadth rather than model ownership.

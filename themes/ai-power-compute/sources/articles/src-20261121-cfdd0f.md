PHOENIX (AZFamily)
 — Arizonans went online to search for “
Grand Theft Auto VI
” as the long-awaited game launched, and a Phoenix internet hub set a traffic record when pre-downloads began.
Google Trends data
 shows Arizona tied for 21st among states for search interest in the week around the launch, with the Yuma-El Centro area leading the state’s TV markets.
How Arizona stacked up
Google Trends data from Nov. 14 through Nov. 21 shows Arizona tied with New Hampshire, New Mexico and Oklahoma for 21st among the 50 states for search interest in the game, relative to overall search volume.
Kentucky ranked first, followed by West Virginia and Florida. Nevada tied for 9th, California tied for 40th, and Utah ranked 48th.
Within Arizona, the Yuma-El Centro area had the highest search interest, followed by the Phoenix and Tucson areas. It’s worth noting that Google groups its metro areas by TV market, and the Yuma market also includes El Centro, California.

Search interest was low in the days before the Nov. 19 launch, then began to surge the evening of Nov. 17. It peaked in the early morning hours of Nov. 19, according to Google Trends. Since then, searches have followed a late-night pattern, rising in the evening and dropping off in the morning.
For most of the past year, searches for the game in Arizona were nearly nonexistent.
Interest stayed close to zero through spring, with only brief bumps in June and August, aligning with its preorder opening June 25 and the Aug. 27 release of “
Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look
.”
It climbed through October and surged in November, with the search interest the week of Nov. 15 roughly five times as high as the week before.
The launch broke internet records
The launch was big enough to 
set internet traffic records
. 
DE-CIX, a company that runs internet exchanges around the world, said global traffic across its exchanges hit 33.03 terabits per second on Nov. 18, and traffic around the launch was about 25% higher than a typical week at the beginning of the year.
Its Phoenix exchange also set a record on Nov. 10, when pre-downloads began, at 196.45 gigabits per second, according to DE-CIX. That’s the peak traffic at that one exchange, not the total amount of data Arizonans downloaded.

Anchor Number: Roughly $30 Billion for Rented AI Power
 
 
Under a new agreement, Alphabet
 (
NASDAQ:GOOGL
) will have Google pay Elon Musk's 
SpaceX
 (
NASDAQ:SPCX
) $920 million per month for AI computing capacity. Over the life of the deal, that comes to approximately $30.4 billion across 33 months. SpaceX's June 5 SEC filing shows the agreement running from October 2026 through June 2029. The contracted commitment is based on the monthly rate in SpaceX's filing. It does not appear in Alphabet's reported earnings.
 
 
 
 
Tulane Public Relations/Wikimedia Commons
 
 
 
On an annual basis, Google is paying the equivalent of $11.04 billion for access to approximately 110,000 
NVIDIA
 (
NASDAQ:NVDA
) GPUs and related infrastructure. CNBC's Jim Cramer summed it up on X: "Google is paying $920 million per month for access to SpaceX's Nvidia GPUs."
 
 
 
 
GOOGL Price Target — 24/7 Wall St.
 
 
 
What It Means for Google's AI Buildout
 
 
Google runs one of the world's largest clouds and designs its own TPU chips, yet it is still leasing GPUs from a rocket company. Demand explains the decision. Google Cloud revenue reached $30.709 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 103% year over year. Growth has sped up every quarter: 48% in Q4 2025, 63% in Q1 2026, 82% in Q2, and now triple digits. CEO Sundar Pichai said: "We're also seeing strong demand for our TPU systems across a wide range of customers."

Google's own expansion is already running hot. Capital expenditures rose to $51.355 billion in the quarter, up 114.4%. First-party model APIs now process nearly 32 billion tokens per minute, more than four times the level of a year earlier. The Gemini app has reached 1 billion users. Leasing SpaceX capacity puts more GPUs to work right away while Google's own data centers are still being built.
 
 
 
 
 
Cramer compared this with Google's long relationship with 
Apple
 (
NASDAQ:AAPL
). The Justice Department documented about $20 billion in annual payments from Google to Apple for default search position. Separately, Apple agreed in January 2026 to use Google's Gemini models, with reported compensation estimates around $1 billion annually. For years, Google paid mainly for distribution. Now it is also paying for raw computing power.

Market Reaction: Shares Slide Despite the Demand Signal
 
 
Investors have not paid back the expansion yet. Alphabet closed at $265.29 on November 20, down 10.31% over the past week and 20.75% over the past month. The stock fell 2.7% in the most recent session. Shares traded at $319.68 when Q3 results were filed.
 
 
 
 
GOOGL Price Scenario — 24/7 Wall St.
 
 
 
The selloff has hit AI stocks across the board. NVIDIA fell 14.01% on November 20, the same day Reuters reported that NVIDIA's accounting is a focus of an SEC investigation (per sources). Alphabet held up better and passed NVIDIA in market value, driving the chipmaker to fourth most valuable US company.
 
 
 
 
 
Bull Case: Alphabet Is Paying for Speed
 
 
The SpaceX contract shows investors that Google's demand is greater than the capacity it can build on its own. The reported numbers back that up. Google Cloud operating income rose to $11.121 billion from $3.594 billion a year earlier. Alphabet's total operating income grew 29.93% to $40.576 billion, and its operating margin stands at 32.03%.
 
 
The deal also shifts risk away from Google. SpaceX has to finance and maintain the expensive hardware, and the contract lets Google terminate or reduce payments under specified delivery conditions. Google gets more capacity without carrying the equipment on its own books.
 
 
Q3 GAAP EPS of $2.12 missed the $2.97 consensus. The miss traces to an $8.7 billion loss on equity securities, which cut diluted EPS by $0.55. It had little to do with how the core business performed. At a P/E of about 12, investors are paying a modest price for that growth. The risks are real, though. Free cash flow was negative $9.065 billion, and Alphabet raised $28.7 billion through senior notes to help fund the expansion.
 
 
 
 
 
Bottom Line for Long-Term Holders
 
 
The next date on the calendar for income-focused holders is the $0.22 quarterly dividend, with a record date of December 7 and payment on December 14, 2026. The about $30 billion contract makes SpaceX a major AI infrastructure supplier, but SpaceX still has to turn that contracted revenue into profit. For Alphabet shareholders, the deal shows that Google's demand for computing power is already larger than what it can build.

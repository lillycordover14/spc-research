Key Takeaways

Stocks had their worst week of 2026, and angel investor Jason Calacanis said he’ll put any free cash he has into SpaceX, Tesla, Uber, Shopify and Google.

He says big tech trades at its lowest P/Es since the dot-com era. That holds up much better for Alphabet and Uber than for Tesla.

Tesla trades at 162x forward earnings, well above its five-year average, while Uber’s 16x is its lowest in three years.

SpaceX looks cheap only against 2027 earnings, so Uber is the strongest bargain on the list.

The S&P 500 fell 9% last week, its worst week since April 2025, and the Nasdaq dropped 10%. On this weekend’s episode of This Week in Startups, angel investor Jason Calacanis said people are “capitulating totally on big tech companies.” He added that those companies now trade at their lowest P/Es since the dot-com era.

His response?

“I am going to catch every knife in the stock market I can. Any amount of free cash I have, I’m buying SpaceX, Tesla, Uber, Shopify, Google…”

(He also admitted, “I haven’t bought any yet, but I will.”)

A day earlier, he 
told bitcoin holders on X
 to sell at least half their position if they’re up and move it into the same names, plus Amazon, Apple and Meta.

But those knives are falling from very different heights.

Tesla’s multiple is still sky-high

Tesla’s forward P/E has come down a long way from its peak, but it’s still far above its own average:

At 162x forward earnings, Tesla’s multiple is down by a third from its 243x peak last December. That still leaves it well above the 108x it averaged over the past five years. Next year’s estimates don’t help much either. In an 
X exchange
 about a possible Tesla-SpaceX merger, Future Fund’s Gary Black put Tesla at 168x 2027 earnings. Whatever Tesla is, it isn’t trading anywhere near a dot-com-era low.

Alphabet and Uber look like real bargains

Calacanis’s pitch holds up much better for Alphabet, whose forward P/E has dropped sharply since the spring:

At 19x, Alphabet trades below its 22x five-year average and far below the 33x it reached at the end of April. That makes it a stock the selloff really did mark down, even though it’s still above the 16x it hit in April 2025. For scale, Tesla’s multiple is more than 8x Alphabet’s.

Uber fits his pitch best of all. Its forward P/E sits at its lowest point in three years:

At 16x, Uber trades well under its 25x average since late 2023 and at less than half its 40x peak in February 2024.

SpaceX is cheap only on next year’s earnings

Black’s other figure, 31x 2027 earnings for SpaceX, makes it look like the bargain next to Tesla. That multiple rests on earnings SpaceX doesn’t have yet. Analysts expect its normalized EPS to swing from a loss of $0.30 this year to $2.50 in 2027 as its compute rentals ramp up. Building that capacity costs a lot. Management guides fourth-quarter capex to $35 billion to $40 billion, and analysts expect negative free cash flow of $140.7 billion in 2027. So 31x is a bet that the compute ramp arrives on schedule.

A basket can carry one expensive name

Calacanis is buying a basket of companies he expects AI to make far more powerful, and a basket doesn’t need every name to be cheap. Tesla investors have paid up for years for what the company might become. His “generational buy time” call also looks a decade out. Still, his reason for buying now is that multiples are low, and that’s only true for part of his list.

If I could catch only one knife this week, it’d be Uber. No other stock on his list trades at its lowest multiple in three years. I suspect part of that discount is worry that robotaxi fleets won’t need Uber, and that’s the risk to watch.

SpaceX’s fourth-quarter report early next year is the first real test of the 2027 earnings behind its multiple. It will show whether December revenue reached the $100 billion annualized run rate management is aiming for. That takes about $8.3 billion in the month.

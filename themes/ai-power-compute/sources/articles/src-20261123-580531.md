Will PANW's Applied Compute Acquisition Boost Its AI Security Growth?
3 min read
PANW
Palo Alto Networks, Inc. 
PANW is strengthening its AI security business through the planned acquisition of Applied Compute, a platform specializing in post-training and inference of open-weight AI models. The $4.5 billion all-stock deal is aimed at helping enterprises train, adapt and deploy AI models securely across public and sovereign cloud environments. The acquisition will also bring Applied Compute's AI research team to Palo Alto Networks, supporting its efforts to integrate AI capabilities across its existing security offerings.
Applied Compute has already generated more than $100 million in annualized revenues in the last 18 months of its founding. PANW plans to leverage its global customer base and enterprise relationships to expand the acquired business. Management expects Applied Compute to contribute more than $1 billion to next-generation security annual recurring revenue by fiscal 2029. The company also expects the acquisition to support its broader AI security strategy by combining AI model training with security capabilities.
The acquisition complements PANW's existing AI security offerings, including Prisma AIRS, which reached nearly $170 million in ARR within five quarters of its general availability. As enterprises increasingly adopt AI agents and open-weight models, the need to secure AI applications, data and infrastructure is expected to rise. Applied Compute could help PANW address this demand by providing secure AI training and inference capabilities alongside its cybersecurity platforms.
However, the acquisition will involve additional costs and could put pressure on profitability in the near term. PANW expects the deal to reduce operating margin by up to 100 basis points in fiscal 2027 and 200 basis points in fiscal 2028. The company also anticipates less than $3 billion in cumulative capital expenditures through fiscal 2030 to support its compute requirements. While Applied Compute could strengthen PANW's position in enterprise AI, successful integration and the ability to manage additional costs will be important to realizing the expected benefits.
How Competitors Fare Against PANW
Competitors like 
CrowdStrike Holdings, Inc.
 CRWD and 
Fortinet, Inc.
 FTNT are also gaining ground through platform expansion and AI innovation through acquisitions.
CrowdStrike is expanding its AI security portfolio through two planned acquisitions, Fiddler AI and Dreadnode. Both deals are aimed at helping enterprises secure artificial intelligence (AI) models and agents as they move from development to production. Dreadnode specializes in automated AI security testing, while Fiddler AI provides AI agent observability and explainability. These additions are expected to extend the Falcon platform's capabilities and help enterprises identify security risks before deployment and monitor AI agent behavior after deployment.
Fortinet is also strengthening its AI security portfolio through its August 2026 acquisition of Virtue AI, a provider of AI runtime protection and automated AI validation. The technology enables enterprises to test autonomous AI agents for security weaknesses through simulated attacks, including prompt injection. It also provides visibility into AI agents, monitors their behavior and blocks malicious tool calls before they are executed. In addition, Virtue AI offers continuous testing and real-time security controls across AI models, applications and agents. These capabilities will extend Fortinet's AI protection from development to runtime.

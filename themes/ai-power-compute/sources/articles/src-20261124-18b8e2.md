Palo Alto Networks to Acquire AI Startup Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion
Om Singh
| November 24 | 
Business Stories
, 
Industry & Events
Share

Palo Alto Networks
 has agreed to acquire artificial intelligence startup 
Applied Compute
 in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, marking a major expansion of its efforts to secure enterprise AI infrastructure. The deal comes shortly after Applied Compute reached a valuation of $3.25 billion in a funding round.

The acquisition will bring Applied Compute’s AI infrastructure technology and research team into Palo Alto Networks, allowing the cybersecurity company to strengthen its capabilities in helping businesses build, customise and deploy AI models securely.

The transaction remains subject to regulatory approval, with completion expected in the third quarter of Palo Alto Networks’ fiscal 2027.
    

Why Palo Alto Networks Is Acquiring Applied Compute

Applied Compute specialises in post-training and inference infrastructure for open-weight AI models. Its technology allows businesses to adapt existing models and develop proprietary AI systems while maintaining greater control over their deployment.

The acquisition addresses a growing challenge for enterprises adopting artificial intelligence: securing AI systems throughout their development and deployment.

By combining Applied Compute’s infrastructure with its existing cybersecurity business, Palo Alto Networks aims to extend its security capabilities into the AI ecosystem.

CEO 
Nikesh Arora
 highlighted Applied Compute’s research team and its security foundations as important factors behind the acquisition. He also positioned the deal as part of the company’s broader ambition to establish a central role in the expanding AI economy.

Yash Patil’s Journey From OpenAI to a $4.5 Billion Acquisition

A key figure in the deal is 23-year-old 
Yash Patil
, Applied Compute’s co-founder, who will become co-chief AI officer at Palo Alto Networks following the acquisition.

Born to Indian immigrant parents in Austin, Texas, Patil studied computer science at Stanford University before dropping out to join OpenAI at the age of 20.

During his time at OpenAI, he worked on Codex and post-training infrastructure. He also worked alongside future Applied Compute co-founders Rhythm Garg and Linden Li.

The three launched Applied Compute in May 2025, with a focus on helping businesses develop and operate proprietary AI models.

The startup reportedly grew from zero to $100 million in annualised revenue within 18 months, attracting significant attention in the enterprise AI infrastructure market.

Applied Compute to Retain Existing Customers

Following the acquisition, Applied Compute will continue serving its existing customers, which include Microsoft, Nvidia and DoorDash, while becoming part of Palo Alto Networks’ broader global customer base.

Patil will also help lead the cybersecurity company’s AI strategy as the two businesses combine their technological capabilities.

Palo Alto Networks has outlined plans to expand its computing capacity to more than 100 megawatts over the next five years, reflecting the infrastructure requirements of its growing AI ambitions.

The acquisition could also have implications for enterprise customers as Applied Compute becomes part of a larger cybersecurity platform. Businesses using its technology will need clarity on data ownership, operational control and contractual exit provisions as the integration progresses.

With the $4.5 billion transaction, Palo Alto Networks is making a significant investment in the infrastructure behind enterprise AI, rather than limiting its focus to securing conventional digital systems.

ROCHESTER — You might not be aware of it, but Google has been on a charm offensive lately.
It has been donating large sums of money across the region.

ADVERTISEMENT

The tech behemoth has, within the last month, donated $250,000 to Rochester Community and Technical College to strengthen skilled trades training. It also gave a $25,000 game-night donation for the Pine Island Panthers homecoming football game to fund an array of food trucks.
Last week, Google gave Family Promise Rochester, a nonprofit dedicated to ending family homelessness, $500,000 to support families facing eviction.
And this comes on top of $25 million pledged by Google to fund Pine Island Schools in a multi-decade partnership as it pursues efforts to build a data center in the 3,700-person community.

Advocates see Google pursuing a good neighbor policy by investing in workforce development and vulnerable families and showing what a force for good the company can be in the region.
Cynics view the donations as an attempt to win acceptance of a Pine Island data center that has divided the community at a time when public opinion holds such facilities in low regard.
John Wade, president of Rochester Area Economic Development, Inc., which views data centers as engines of economic growth, noted that Google is making these community investments when Google’s data center hasn’t yet been built and remains on the drawing board.
“That money has been spent, and the project has not begun,” Wade said. “I don’t believe I have ever seen a corporate steward support the community at the level that they have prior to a final project being built.”

ADVERTISEMENT

Wade spoke out last week against a one-year moratorium on new data centers that the Rochester City Council recently passed.
The action reflected public skepticism toward data centers even though no such facilities are being proposed for Rochester. Wade said he feared that the moratorium could harm Rochester’s reputation as a community that welcomes innovation and private investment.
Google began to be an aggressive philanthropist in the area after a judge last year blocked construction of a data center in Pine Island as part of a development project called Project Skyway.

John Wade, president of RAEDI, speaks during RAEDI's annual meeting Monday, March 10, 2025, at the Rochester Event Center.
Joe Ahlquist / Post Bulletin file photo

Google’s goodwill campaign started last summer with a Pine Island community event featuring food trucks, bounce castles and live music. Google has since agreed to a more stringent environmental review, ending a lawsuit by the Minnesota Center for Environmental Advocacy, an environmental group.
Google did not respond to calls seeking comment. 
Nate Stoltman, an RCTC spokesperson, said the money will strengthen the school’s cybersecurity program as well as its skilled trades program.
When the cybersecurity program was first launched, it drew nearly 60 students, far more than it expected. He said such private financial support for in-demand programs is especially appreciated given the financial pressure higher education is under.

ADVERTISEMENT

Google benefits, too.
“They have many needs across the board,” Stoltman said. “They need cybersecurity folks, they need data folks. Google has been working with two-year colleges across the country, because they are a direct-to-employment pipeline. You don’t necessarily need a four-year degree to get into the job market.”
Stoltman declined to speculate whether Google’s generosity was related to the Pine Island controversy.
“I can’t say. I’m not in Google,” he said.
Yet Pine Island data center opponents see Google’s largesse as an attempt to buy back community trust that cannot be restored with monetary gifts.
“It is a sign of desperation from their end, because they know that public sentiment is firmly against the project, and so they’re trying to do something to change,” said David Derksen, a Pine Island resident opposed to the proposed data center.
“As a resident, you can throw money all around if you like, but that’s not going to repair what was destroyed through lying and deception.”

ADVERTISEMENT

Opponents maintain that city officials and Project Skyway leaders knew that Google was the primary client for the entire 482-acre technology and data center all along, even though they claimed they didn’t.
Carrie Moscho, Family Promise Rochester’s executive director, said her organization was “incredibly grateful” for the donation. The organization, she said, spends on average $1,300 to help a struggling family avoid eviction. The donation, spread across hundreds of families, means that “a lot of kids will get to stay sleeping in their own beds.”
“I can't speak to Google’s plans or intentions, but what I can speak to is that this money is going to help a whole lot of families stay housed and kids get connected to services,” Moscho said.

Together AI links prompt leak to AI coding agent&nbsp;&nbsp;TechInformed

[page text]
Together AI, an AI inference provider, has revealed an AI coding agent committed a credential to a public GitHub repository, allowing an unauthorized party to download stored samples of customer prompts. The intruder then searched those samples for credentials for other services, according to a 
security disclosure
.

The samples came from requests to MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 through Together’s serverless inference service, primarily on June 24 and July 4, 8 and 14, 2026. Together said it collected the prompt samples for routine software testing, was authorized to use them for that purpose and stored them in a private Hugging Face repository.

Together said an AI coding agent exposed the credential granting access to the private repository while working on xorl, its public GitHub project. Automated scanning detected the exposure, and the company reverted the commit, but the intruder obtained the credential during that window.

According to Together, the intruder tested credentials found in the downloaded prompts, including five decoy credentials, known as canaries, tied to a Dragos security experiment.

Dragos, an industrial cybersecurity company, 
submitted about 2 million prompts containing traceable credentials and URLs to AI services
 between July and November 2026. The researchers found evidence of data exposure involving at least three AI providers but did not publicly identify them.

Scope of the leak remains unclear

The disclosure does not identify the coding agent or affected customers, specify how long the credential was exposed or give the number of prompts or customers affected. Nor does it say whether the intruder used credentials found in the data to access any other service.

Together said it has found no evidence of additional prompt leaks and has no reason to believe the samples were shared further. It also said it understands that the downloaded data has since been deleted. The company has worked with an external forensic firm and law enforcement to investigate.

Together is still reviewing the prompt samples for sensitive personal information or other customer data and said it will contact affected customers directly as needed.

Retention changes follow the incident

Together said it now checks all pull requests for credentials and has updated its processes for AI coding agents. It also uses scoped or short-lived credentials and keyless access where supported.

The company said it has also made zero data retention the default for new accounts and advised customers handling sensitive prompts to enable the setting.

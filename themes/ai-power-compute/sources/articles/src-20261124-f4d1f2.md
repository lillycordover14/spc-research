Preemptible nodes cost a flat 50% of on-demand rates without any bidding or spot market pricing.
Kubernetes workloads can use the full drain window for checkpointing while Slurm jobs need requeue to survive reclamation.
The discount only pays off when workloads checkpoint and retry because reclamation can kill work at any moment.
 

Together AI
 made preemptible GPU compute generally available on Together GPU Clusters on November 24, 2026, covering Kubernetes and Slurm deployments in all regions. The interruptible nodes use the same NVIDIA accelerated compute as standard nodes, at a flat 50% of the on-demand rate.
Customers can add preemptible capacity when creating a cluster or to one already running. The nodes draw from unused supply and can be reclaimed when that capacity is needed elsewhere. Standard nodes remain the default: they are provisioned synchronously and are never preempted, whereas preemptible nodes fill in asynchronously as spare capacity becomes available.
The price does not move with a spot market or require a bid. Usage is metered every one to two minutes and billed sub-hourly, so customers pay approximately for the minutes a node runs rather than a full hour. The fixed discount makes the rate predictable even though the requested capacity is not guaranteed.
Capacity Targets and Automatic Refill
The 
Together AI documentation
 distinguishes 
desired_preemptible_gpus
 from 
allocated_preemptible_gpus
, so customers can see when live capacity falls short of the target. Targets must be set in multiples of eight GPUs. After a reclamation, the cluster retains its target and automatically provisions replacements as spare capacity returns, without another capacity request.
Reclamation starts with a drain window of at most five minutes and ends with node removal, regardless of whether work has finished. There is no minimum node lifetime. Those terms put recovery design at the center of the cost decision: a workload needs to tolerate losing a worker at any point, then resume when replacement capacity arrives.
Kubernetes and Slurm Controls Differ
On Kubernetes, reclamation cordons the node, emits a 
TogetherPreemptionNotified
 warning and sends pods a SIGTERM shutdown signal. Workloads can set 
terminationGracePeriodSeconds
 to use up to the full drain window for checkpointing and exit; pods without that setting receive the Kubernetes default of 30 seconds. A 
together.ai/compute-class
 node label lets interruptible workers target preemptible nodes while coordinators, login pods and user-facing serving replicas remain on standard nodes.
On Slurm, preemptible and standard nodes share partitions and the same scheduling feature, so the scheduler cannot target preemptible nodes or keep a job off them. Jobs still running when a node is removed are killed, and only jobs submitted with 
--requeue
 are resubmitted. A requeued job can resume from its last checkpoint, but any work completed after that checkpoint is lost.
Suitable Workloads and Storage Limits
Together AI recommends the capacity for checkpointed or retryable work, including short experiments, configuration sweeps, fine-tuning, evaluations, distillation, batch inference and temporary inference bursts. For a sweep or internal inference spike, teams can raise the target and lower it afterward while keeping the cluster running, instead of holding standard capacity sized for peak demand. Multi-day runs without checkpoints, user-facing serving without fallback and workloads with strict service-level objectives are not suitable.
The company said it already uses preemptible clusters for internal research, development and testing, and non-critical production workloads, spreading spare capacity across product teams.
A cluster can consist entirely of preemptible nodes, but 
Together
 can reclaim all of them. Data on shared volumes persists, yet shared volumes and the Slurm control plane remain reachable while no nodes are running only in some regions and on a best-effort basis. Customers needing continuously reachable shared storage therefore need at least one standard node. Nodes cannot be converted between standard and preemptible types in place.
The launch turns Together’s spare GPU supply into a fixed-discount option for AI infrastructure teams. Its value depends on workload design: checkpoints, retry logic and standard-node fallback determine whether reclamation is a recoverable interruption or results in lost progress and unavailable service.

GREEN BAY (WLUK) -- When searching for something on Google, an AI overview appears and an "AI Mode" is available. However, students in Green Bay schools no longer have that option.
"If students attempt to use AI mode or they try to click on the AI overview option when they're in a Google search, we've set it up where our network will reroute the students back to a generic Google search," said GBAPS Technology Integrator, Elizabeth Chavez.
Green Bay schools block Google AI Overviews and AI Mode on internal network (WLUK)
Chavez says the district roadblocked the features through its internal filtering system.
She said, "It's disabled because our first priority at Green Bay Area Public Schools is to teach our students how to use our high-quality instructional materials to find the answers."
The change
 follows a Google announcement from earlier this month.
 Soon, programs that use AI like Gemini or Google Search will be turned off unless enabled by school administration. Schools that don't want students using AI will have to go without those features entirely. 
Chavez says the district doesn't allow students to use any AI tools,
 other than an internal AI chatbot.
"It's not going to give them academic answers. It's not going to generate anything for them, and it's not going to allow them to have hurtful or harmful conversations," said Chavez. 
"It really doesn't let us search that much things as we did. We sometimes have trouble finding something," said Green Bay East High School Junior, Eileen Galeano. 
Galeano says she uses AI technology to help in a pinch.
She said, "If I don't know anything, I'll just ask AI to help me do certain tasks."
Research done by College Board in 2025 says 
84% of surveyed students reported using generative AI for schoolwork.
 That doesn't necessarily mean students are cheating. About half said it's for brainstorming ideas, revising essays and finding sources.
The district's change is part of a balance to teach proper research and analysis while using a quickly evolving technology.

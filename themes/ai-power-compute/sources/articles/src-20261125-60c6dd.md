Google plans to spend $10.8 million on advanced transmission technology, and hopes other tech giants will adopt its model.
The post Google’s grid deal in Ohio tests a faster path to data center power appeared first on Latitude Media.

[page text]
Google and Ohio Power plan to test a new way for the hyperscaler to pay for advanced transmission upgrades, potentially unlocking faster access to power and shielding other customers from the costs.

Under a proposal filed last week 
with the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio
, Google would pay $10.8 million for the utility to install advanced conductors on about 15 miles of power lines in Columbus — lines Ohio Power was already planning to rebuild. As a result, the hyperscaler said it would get access to 24 megawatts of capacity two years early as it expands a data center in the city.

Those advanced conductors are expected to unlock 700 MW of additional headroom on the grid compared to a standard rebuild by 2031. That boost depends on 
a separate transmission project
 underway by Ohio Power and other partners coming online by 2031. Some 300-miles of new lines would bring more power into central Ohio, and advanced conductors can carry between 40% to 70% more electricity than conventional wires. This’ll allow Ohio Power to squeeze more power out of an existing corridor.

The first-of-its-kind approach comes as hyperscalers are under pressure to pay for their own energy costs and more, especially after so many candidates who campaigned on the issue 
won their races in the midterms
.

“We want to demonstrate that this kind of investment can work, not just for our data center pipeline but to enable a significant amount of more headroom for other customers in a utility’s service territory,” Ellen Zuckerman, Google’s head of energy market development for North and South America, told 
Latitude Media.
 “This is a path others can hopefully follow to unlock capacity that exists on the grid today if we deploy more innovative technologies.”

Higher capacity conductors are among a suite of advanced transmission technologies that utilities could deploy on the grid. ATTs can be faster and cheaper than building new lines, saving ratepayers money over the long term and helping unlock the speed-to-power data center developers demand. 

Advanced conductors can cost 33% to 75% less than a new transmission line while unlocking double the capacity, according to separate studies by the 
Department of Energy
, and researchers at 
GridLab
 and UC Berkeley. But adoption by incumbent utilities has been slow, in part because their profit model overseen by state regulators rewards spending over efficiency, and ATTs can lack clear cost-recovery pathways. 

Zuckerman said there are a lot of barriers to getting advanced conductors on the grid, including the utility profit model and installers’ unfamiliarity with the technology. But Google and AEP — the parent of Ohio Power — have a track record of innovation, she added, citing 
the clean transition tariff launched in 2024
. The tariff is aimed at allowing Google to pay a higher rate for power to fund emerging generation technologies, such as advanced geothermal or 
long-duration energy storage
. 

Google’s investment in advanced reconductors is an extension of that work, Zuckerman said. “You can think of this as expanding the clean transition tariff from generation to also incorporating the transmission system.”

Policy tailwinds and FERC pressure

Ohio in 2025 also enacted a law directing state regulators to map out how data centers and other large load customers can partner with utilities to deploy ATTs. That law is part of a broader push by state and federal policymakers to accelerate adoption. 

President Donald Trump 
had considered issuing an executive order
, but held off as DOE instead 
issued $1.9 billion in grants under a program called SPARK
 for advanced transmission projects. FERC, in its recent 
show cause orders
 to grid operators and utilities directing them to speed up grid connections for data centers, required an evaluation of ATTs when studying transmission service. They also have to explain why the technology isn’t used.

“All of those things taken together are indicative of a path toward greater adoption in North America,” said Theodore Paradise, chief policy and grid strategy officer at CTC Global, an advanced conductor company that 
has a partnership with Google
. Most of the companies’ business is outside the U.S., in countries like Indonesia and Bangladesh.

Other tech companies including Amazon, Microsoft, and Anthropic could adopt a similar model to Google, Paradise added. The work in Ohio will prove-out a new strategy to invest in ATTs that can be replicated across the tech industry — especially at a time when data centers and utilities are being asked to cover their own energy costs, he said.

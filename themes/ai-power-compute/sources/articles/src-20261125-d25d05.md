							By Monica Casey, WRAL Reporter
						

Rules around AI in the classroom
are changing soon, and that could impact your children.

Google says
 students using its software in the classroom cannot access
search features unless the school opts in to AI tools.

Other WRAL Top Stories

As different districts grapple
with how they’ll implement the change, UNC researchers are taking a closer look
at the impact of AI in the classroom.

McMichael Professor Jeff Greene
worked on a report with 30 experts about how generative AI can help and hurt
education.

Greene says the tool is not as helpful when students are producing their
own work.

"Students need to put in mental effort to learn and get good at
something, and if AI is taking away that productive struggle, that's not going
to help them learn,” Greene explained.

He says experts are particularly concerned about the impact of AI on
younger students.

"Generative AI sometimes doesn't tell you the right thing or send you
in the direction you need to go,” Greene said. “If you don't know a lot about
the topic, you won't be able to spot that."

Greene says AI can help with some portions of learning.

"We know that first someone needs to kind of absorb the information,
get a general understanding of it. Then, they need to practice with it. They
need to think about it and understand it, and then they need to apply it,”
Greene said.

Researchers believe generative AI can help in the middle step of learning.

"Generative AI can create quizzes for you,” Greene explained. “It can
ask you questions to make sure you understand."

Greene emphasized the need for teachers to be in control.

He says he’s supportive of Google giving educators the ability to decide
when AI should be available.

“Until they have helped educators have the ability to make good decisions
for their students to decide if they should be using generative AI, I don't
think they should be using generative AI in schools,” Greene said. “Until the
big companies can ensure us that educators have that kind of control, it's
probably best to dial back that sort of work."

ROANOKE, Va. (WDBJ) - For Jeff Todd, Roanoke’s outdoor spaces are part of what makes the region special. They’re also at the center of his concerns about a $2 million Google donation.
Todd, the owner of Roanoke Mountain Adventures, has resigned from Roanoke Outside’s advisory council. He says he didn’t feel comfortable helping distribute the money while questioning the company’s motivations for giving it.
Google’s donation went to Project Outside
, a Roanoke Outside grant program that supports outdoor projects and organizations across the region.
“They want to use our local goodwill to put a happy face on their development project, but we don’t even know what we’re giving up as an outdoor community,” Todd said.
Todd calls the donation “greenwashing,” saying it helps Google’s public image while questions about the potential impacts of data center development remain. He said his decision isn’t a blanket rejection of data centers, but he wants local leaders to weigh potential effects on water, natural resources and recreation.
“We don’t know what the real tradeoffs are yet,” he said.
Roanoke Outside Executive Director Pete Eshelman said the donation could help fund projects that might otherwise be years away.
“What this represents is a tremendous opportunity to fund projects that probably would be ten years down the road before they could happen,” Eshelman said.
In a written statement, Eshelman said the money has already been received, has no conditions and is independently managed by Roanoke Outside. He said its advisory council and board of directors will decide how the funds are spent.
Todd, a former lawyer who purchased Roanoke Mountain Adventures about six years ago, said he does not blame local organizations that choose to accept funding. He hopes the disagreement won’t divide the outdoor community.
“I’m not selling personally and others, again, have to make their own assessment,” he said.

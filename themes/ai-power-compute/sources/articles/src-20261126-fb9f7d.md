Google Research posted a 
54-page paper
 to GitHub on Nov. 25 claiming an AI system has closed a 34-year-old theoretical gap in integer factorization, the math problem that underpins RSA encryption. 
The paper does not break modern encryption, and no outside mathematician has verified it yet.

According to Google, its multi-agent system Cogentic, built on Gemini 4, produced the proof from a single prompt with no further human help. If it holds up, the result matches the speed scaling of the fastest factoring method used in practice without relying on the unproven assumptions cryptographers have leaned on since the early 1990s.

The two factoring preprints arrived inside a 
six-paper batch
 that also covers discrete logarithms, approximate Nash equilibria in two-player games, communication complexity and graph reconstruction. That release shifts away from Google’s 
Sept. 30 preprint
 introducing Cogentic, which said domain experts independently verified early results produced with Gemini 3.1 Pro and an early version of Gemini 4 Argon. Four Cogentic papers in game theory and learning theory followed between Oct. 30 and Nov. 23, before the Nov. 25 batch claimed single-prompt proofs paired with automated checking. Releasing batches of AI-generated proofs ahead of outside review has already led to quick retractions. After OpenAI released 722 AI-generated math manuscripts on Oct. 6, it 
withdrew three

R&D World reported

Google’s new papers await outside review. By midday Nov. 25, as the release passed 300 points on 
Hacker News
, the repository held only PDFs and READMEs. Each README says Cogentic completed a proof in Lean 4, software that checks mathematical steps line by line, using only Mathlib, the community-built Lean math library. The commit says Google will release all six Lean formalizations in a companion submission, allowing outside researchers to run the proof checker and compare the formal statements with the papers’ theorems. Later on Nov. 25, Google 
revised
 the batch’s Nash equilibrium paper to cite earlier work, writing that no algorithmic or proof content changed. That afternoon the repository still held only PDFs and READMEs.

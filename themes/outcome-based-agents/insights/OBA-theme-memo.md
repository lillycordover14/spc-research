# Outcome-Based Agents and Agentic Orchestration

## Investment theses and category views on AI agents that do enterprise work and get paid for results

# 1. Investment theses: tailwinds, key takeaways and macro

This theme covers AI agents that complete a piece of enterprise work from start to finish, such as resolving a support ticket, working a denied medical claim or placing a purchase order, and that are paid per result or per unit of work rather than per user. It also covers the orchestration software that lets a company run many agents under common rules. Five groups compete for this work, namely the model labs and cloud providers, the systems of record such as customer relationship management (CRM) and enterprise resource planning (ERP) platforms, enterprises building their own agents, systems integrators and outsourcers, and vertical applications built for one job in one industry.

1. **Spending on agents is moving from software budgets to labor and outsourcing budgets, because agents now finish whole jobs and seat pricing breaks once software replaces the people who held the seats**

- Model releases in the past year made agents reliable enough to run inside old enterprise software, and an insurance operations startup says its success rate on tests that drive old insurance screens rose from 30% to over 95% with a single new model (company claim) [src-20260527-cecfde]. Gartner expects agentic AI funding to grow 31.8% in 2027 against 3.7% for IT budgets overall (Gartner) [src-20260831-2bda07].
- The budget agents compete for is labor, which buyers measure in wages and outsourcing invoices. The US employs 2,666,000 customer service representatives at a median wage of $44,770 (BLS) [src-20260827-b22de5], about $119B a year by our estimate, and HFS Research expects $422B of business process outsourcing (BPO) spending to shift to software that does the work by 2035 (HFS Research) [src-20260831-73c927].
- Seat pricing stops working once software does the work, because a vendor whose agent replaces five people loses four of the seats it used to bill. The chief executive of a help desk platform says "seats are going to go away over time" (company interview) [src-20260805-5a9fa7], and the share of AI software vendors using outcome-based pricing rose from 18% to 23% in six months (investor survey) [src-20260715-e16b49].
- The leading customer service platforms already charge per result, so buyers can compare prices per resolution across several vendors today. Published prices run from $0.50 per resolved conversation to about $2.00 per automated resolution (vendor pricing pages) [src-20260924-1b6619] [src-20260413-63104e] [src-20260924-93c5ed].
- The labs and cloud providers are paying to get agents installed, which shortens the path to production for every vendor. Partner funds of $100M and $750M now pay integrators to deploy agents (company announcements) [src-20260312-73c25c] [src-20260422-72407e], and postings for forward-deployed engineers (FDEs), engineers who build each customer's deployment on site, passed 7,300 in the first nine months of 2026 against under 200 in 2023 (labor market data) [src-20261022-d5ad54].
2. **New companies have an edge where they sell a finished job in one industry and take responsibility for the result, because the labs sell general tools and the incumbents mostly add AI to the seats they already sell**

- A vertical company knows the rules, exceptions and systems of one kind of work, which a general model does not learn from public data. A software chief executive argues that closing the gap between the roughly 70% accuracy of a model off the shelf and the 97% a business needs is what makes work automatable (podcast) [src-20260915-68c6ef].
- Getting an agent into production is mostly integration and testing, so a company that brings its own engineers and owns the result gets through where software alone stalls. A contact-center AI vendor's head of forward-deployed engineering says one month of building an agent can mean two to four months of testing and evaluation (expert call) [src-20260618-f36dc6].
- Companies that take legal or regulatory responsibility for the work keep more of the price, because buyers otherwise keep a person on the last step. An AI-native law firm whose lawyers take liability for the contracts its AI reviews (investor research) [src-20260331-50e583] sells the finished review of a contract instead of software that helps a lawyer do it.
- The incumbents often bolt agents onto old products and the labs avoid customer-specific work, which leaves room for specialists. A card issuer found the AI products of the contact-center incumbents "more of a bolt-on" (expert call) [src-20241114-f0ab80], and a research agent founder says the labs avoid training on each customer's data because "they don't want to have a different model for every customer" (founder interview) [src-20260526-6ea6d3].
3. **Buyers price an agent against the outsourcer or headcount it replaces, so vendors win fastest by guaranteeing a measurable saving at a predictable price while buyers keep switching easy**

- The price an agent replaces is the outsourcer's invoice, and savings at the first buyers are wide enough to close deals quickly. A software company says its offshore support model cost "4X to 7X" what its AI agent costs (expert call) [src-20260515-de88a1].
- Agent companies increasingly sell into the operating department's services budget, which is easier to reach than the IT software budget. An orchestration founder sells from the "service budget line, because it is easier to procure" (founder interview) [src-20260701-3b0c17].
- Buyers want to know what they will spend, so pure outcome pricing slows a sale even when the buyer likes the idea. In a fintech vendor selection, the buyer wanted "a more transactional pricing without outcome commitment" (expert call) [src-20260708-a5fc76], and 27 of 50 buyers in an investor survey preferred credits tied to work over tokens (investor survey) [src-20260827-fb556f].
- Buyers keep their data, procedures and tools in their own systems so any vendor can be replaced, which caps the price a vendor can hold once rivals catch up. A fintech routed tickets across three customer service vendors through a router it built itself (expert call) [src-20260518-714b0a].
4. **We should back agent companies whose resolution rate and deployment cost already improve with each customer, because margins sit between outsourcing and software and move toward software only when both improve**

- AI products earn gross margins of 50% to 60% against 80% to 90% for traditional software (investor research) [src-20260210-496d37], and an investor survey projects AI product margins rising from 45% in 2025 to 59% in 2027 (investor survey) [src-20260924-1b6619]. An insurance operations founder puts outsourcers at "10, 15 percent gross margin" and targets 80% for his own company (founder interview) [src-20260203-a5df90].
- Under per-resolution pricing the vendor pays for model usage on every conversation but bills only the resolved ones, so the resolution rate drives margin directly. The finance chief of a customer service agent company says a resolution rate that rose from about 25% to close to 70% improved margin (company interview) [src-20260522-b1dbe3], and a rival runs about 90% of its workloads on cheaper fine-tuned open-source models (trade press) [src-20261013-d7909a].
- Deployment cost falls with each agent a customer adds, which is where margins improve over the life of an account. At a national retailer the first agent took six months and the second one month, and setup ran to "at least 35%-40% of your first year spend" (expert call) [src-20260710-37d40b].
- The vendor keeps a thin slice of the savings it creates, so it needs volume to build a business. The same retailer measured a 35% to 40% cost cut and gave its vendor about 10% of it (expert call) [src-20260710-37d40b], so on $10M of handled cost the vendor earns $350,000 to $400,000 a year by our estimate.
5. **The labs and systems of record will absorb any agent that sells a generic, countable task, so we should back companies that hold industry knowledge or legal liability the platforms lack**

- The labs now ship finished agents and give away the tools around them, which moves them into the job itself. A model lab now sells a complete customer service agent with written procedures, guardrails and automated scoring of its answers (company announcement) [src-20260722-a3993b], and another has released plugins for 12 legal practice areas (company announcement) [src-20260512-5c6e2d].
- The systems of record are selling agents into their installed base with the customer data already in hand. The leading CRM vendor's agent product passed $1.5B of annual recurring revenue (ARR) on a definition widened to include its workplace chat assistant (company filings) [src-20260826-0b53e8], and the leading IT service platform's AI products passed $1B of annual contract value (company filings) [src-20260722-d4586c].
- The main exit so far went to an incumbent at a price well below the private valuations of the leaders, which limits the return on late entries. A CRM incumbent paid about $3.6B for a leading customer service agent company (company announcement) [src-20260615-74b583], while the two leading private rivals raised at roughly 75 to 100 times and about 45 times ARR by our estimate (company reports) [src-20260504-c9d184] [src-20261025-07358d] [src-20260127-5d9191], with the lower multiple pairing a January 2026 valuation with revenue reported later in 2026. A company priced at 100 times ARR has to grow revenue about tenfold before a sale at 10 times ARR returns the entry price, by our estimate.
- Industry knowledge, a license and work that runs across many outside parties are the hardest things for a platform to copy, so new companies win in back-office and regulated work that enterprises already pay outsiders to do. Startups are concentrated there as well, with 87 of the 121 companies we track building vertical applications (our count) [insights/landscape-cuts.md].
# 2. Interesting categories

The categories follow the five groups in our primer, with vertical applications split into front office, back-office operations and regulated services, and each company is listed in one category only.

## A. Labs and Hyperscalers

### Agent Platforms, Frameworks and Infrastructure

- **Tailwinds: Agents are moving into production at most companies that build them, which turns agent building from an experiment into a recurring budget line.** In a survey of agent builders, 57% said they already had agents in production (developer survey) [src-20251202-cd7dc4], and one agent governance product registered nearly 40 million agents across tens of thousands of companies two months after launch (company filings) [src-20260729-8b3c16].
- **Each major lab and cloud now sells a full platform to build, run and govern agents, so enterprise agent spending flows through a handful of suppliers.** The platforms launched in the past year add tools to build, deploy and manage agents, a managed agent loop and agent payments (company announcements) [src-20260205-ea19f4] [src-20261022-a0f852].
- **Open-source agent frameworks have become a standard way for developers to build agents, and the clouds now plug them into their own deployment tools.** A cloud provider's command-line tool now deploys, tests and monitors agents written in a leading open-source framework (developer documentation) [src-20261021-16f218].
- **Every agent in production also needs testing, monitoring, permissions and billing, which adds spending around each agent a company runs.** In a widely reported incident, a coding agent deleted a production database and its backups in nine seconds using an access token with broader rights than the task needed (press reports) [src-20260428-8baab9].
- **Headwinds: The labs give away the harness, the software that runs a model through a task and calls its tools, or price it near cost, which caps what an independent vendor can charge.** Lab agent APIs add no fee on top of model usage or charge about $0.08 per session-hour for a managed version (vendor pricing pages) [src-20260910-014b24] [src-20260408-e5d37b].
- **The labs are also building testing and improvement tools into their own agents, which removes the reason to buy them separately.** A lab-built customer service agent now ships with test conversations, automated scoring of answers and a loop that improves the agent, all of which independent testing vendors sell as products (company announcements) [src-20260722-a3993b].
- **Buyers rarely pay for neutrality across models, which weakens the main argument for an independent layer.** A managing director at a global integrator calls models "such commodities" and says routing between them is "hardly ever" used by his clients (expert call) [src-20261017-72ec30].
- **Why we should not focus here: The labs and hyperscalers own the models and agent platforms that every other category runs on, so in this theme they act as the suppliers and likely acquirers our startups depend on.** Independent frameworks and infrastructure sell to engineering teams on usage while the labs bundle the same tools free, so these companies compete with their suppliers and, by our estimate, tend to sell to a lab or cloud at a modest price.

**Example companies:** OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, LangChain

## B. Systems of Record

### Incumbent Platforms as Channel, Acquirer and Absorber

- **Tailwinds: Outcome pricing moves value to the company that holds the record, because a result can only be counted and billed where the ticket, claim or order is opened and closed.** Equity research says outcome pricing especially favors systems of record, which hold the detailed customer and operating data a result is measured against (equity research) [src-20260428-14f0c8].
- **The record holders define and count the resolutions they bill, which lets them set the effective price.** Help desk vendors decide for themselves when a resolution counts, either after their own model reviews the conversation or when the customer leaves without asking for more help, and cases handed to a person are not billed (vendor pricing pages) [src-20250806-3921b6] [src-20260924-93c5ed].
- **The incumbents sell agents to customers they already have, which reaches buyers faster than any startup can.** At the leading CRM vendor half of agent bookings came from existing customers buying more prepaid usage (company filings) [src-20260826-580137], and an advisory partner says the leading IT service platform's AI faces "few viable alternatives" in core IT service management (expert call) [src-20260911-0742fb].
- **The record holders are buying or backing agent companies built on their platforms, which gives those startups a likely acquirer.** In the past year incumbents in HR software, ERP and insurance core systems each bought or invested in an agent startup that runs on their platform (company announcements) [src-20261013-7962db] [src-20261001-056aad] [src-20260129-3fadee].
- **Headwinds: Buyers distrust resolution counts they cannot check, which slows the move to per-result billing at every vendor.** A software buyer audits its customer service vendor's resolution count itself because it cannot see how a resolution is defined (expert call) [src-20260318-033caf], and a common default test treats 72 hours without a customer reply as a resolution (industry research) [src-20260924-1b6619].
- **Incumbent outcome pricing is uneven outside customer service, so the pricing shift at the platforms is slower than the headlines suggest.** The leading CRM vendor sells most agent work through prepaid credits at $0.10 per action (trade press) [src-20261013-7d8358], and the finance chief of an ERP vendor says revenue linked to outcomes will not move its 2027 numbers (company filings) [src-20261021-551046].
- **Seat-based incumbents lose seats as agents do the work, which pushes them to raise prices and leads buyers to question the platform.** An advisory partner reports shrinking seat counts and growth led by price increases at IT service platforms (expert call) [src-20260911-0742fb], and a retail chief information officer now asks whether he needs an IT service platform at all (expert call) [src-20260402-8d84cb].
- **Why we should not focus here: Systems of record are established incumbents that most agent spending will run through, so they matter to us as the channel, the likely acquirer and the main absorber of any startup whose work they can copy.** They hold the data, permissions and audit trail that outcome pricing needs, so we should test every startup in the theme against how hard its work is for the record holder to rebuild.

**Example companies:** Salesforce, Zendesk, HubSpot, ServiceNow, SAP, Oracle, Workday

## C. Enterprises Building Their Own

### Horizontal Orchestration and Work Context

- **Tailwinds: Enterprises run hundreds of systems and many agents, and someone has to coordinate them, set permissions and record what each agent did.** A founder in the category describes enterprises with "hundreds of applications" and thousands of people acting as glue between them (founder interview) [src-20261025-7ef133].
- **Agents perform far better when they know how work is actually done, which makes a record of real work patterns valuable to every agent a company runs.** A data governance chief executive cites agent accuracy rising from about 30% without business context to about 90% with it (trade press) [src-20261020-7922f1].
- **Companies selling into this layer report fast growth from enterprise customers, although the figures are their own.** Reported figures include bookings over $150M at an orchestration vendor and revenue growth over 300% at a work-context vendor whose existing customers spend about 50% more each year (company claims) [src-20260930-9ee545] [src-20260812-4cb1fc], and a global integrator plans to deploy 1,000 engineers on a work-context platform (trade press) [src-20251229-050f87].
- **Headwinds: Enterprises that buy a horizontal platform learn from it and build their next agents themselves, which caps how far each account grows.** An industrial conglomerate chose a platform over a 12 to 14 month internal build, now builds its other use cases without the vendor and expects its spending to grow only 5% to 10% (expert call) [src-20260224-8c72f4].
- **Work context goes stale quickly, so a map of how work gets done loses value unless someone keeps it current.** The same data governance chief executive says accuracy fell back to 60% two months later (trade press) [src-20261020-7922f1].
- **The clouds and systems of record give the coordination layer away to win the platform.** An IT service platform made its agent control tower free for a year (company announcement) [src-20260507-e6c2cd], and a cloud provider now sells a product to govern every agent a company runs (company announcement) [src-20260501-fc687c].
- **The labs are moving into work context, so the companies that map work face the suppliers of the models they feed.** A director at a work-context vendor names the labs as his biggest competition and says customers will not need his company once the models learn to map work themselves (expert call) [src-20260616-48f4c6].
- **Why we should not focus here: Horizontal orchestration sells a platform that enterprises increasingly build themselves or receive free from a platform they already own, so pricing power falls as the customer's own team learns.** Sales cycles for the biggest enterprise deals run about nine months by a founder's account (founder interview) [src-20261025-7ef133], and expansion stalls once the customer builds its own agents. Work context has the better economics of the two, because a record of how work really gets done improves every agent a company runs, but process-mining incumbents and the labs already compete for that layer, so we should not back standalone work-context companies. Most horizontal vendors now sell kits for building agents, which belong with the platforms in category A, so few companies remain in this category.

**Example companies:** Ema, Duvo, Workfabric AI

## D. Systems Integrators and BPOs

### AI Services and AI-Native Outsourcers

- **Tailwinds: Systems integrators are a main channel for enterprise agent deployments, and the labs are paying them to do the work.** Lab and cloud partner funds of $100M and $750M pay integrators for agent building, deployment and embedded engineers (company announcements) [src-20260312-73c25c] [src-20260422-72407e].
- **Outsourcing is the budget agents replace, and the incumbent outsourcers are already losing ground.** A listed customer experience outsourcer wrote down $1.05B of the value it had booked for past acquisitions after its share price fell, and another's core services revenue fell 1.0% in the second quarter of 2026 on a comparable basis (company filings) [src-20260929-4fcfe9] [src-20260730-4cc420].
- **Automation lets services firms trade revenue for margin, which gives them a reason to adopt agents quickly.** A practice head at a listed IT services firm says the agent platform costs under $10,000 per worker a year against revenue above $20,000 per outsourced worker, and "Our revenues go down, but our margins go up" (expert call) [src-20250627-297f7b].
- **Headwinds: AI-run outsourcing is already in a price war, because the same models are available to every provider.** A vice president at a customer experience outsourcer says outsourcing rates for health insurers fell "between 20% and 40%" in two years and that "Right now, there is no protection in the industry" (expert call) [src-20260930-124dc3].
- **The customer keeps the data, so switching providers is easy and contracts do not compound.** An executive at an insurance company that buys outsourced operations work says "All the data is living within our existing systems" (expert call) [src-20260811-a0aa1e].
- **Discounting before the automation works erases the margin a services firm starts with.** An AI-native outsourcer cut prices 10% to 40% while its costs stayed flat, and a former director there says "Gross margin did tank heavily" (expert call) [src-20260724-865e53].
- **Why we should not focus here: Services firms earn on people and hours, so automation shrinks their revenue even as it lifts their margin.** AI-native outsourcers that undercut the incumbents fight a price war with no switching costs because the data stays with the customer. Integrators matter to the theme as the channel that installs agents in enterprises, and the recurring value goes to the software they deploy, so we should back the vertical products that integrators resell and treat the services firms as partners and acquirers.

**Example companies:** Crescendo, Otera, Distyl AI, Accenture, Deloitte, Cognizant, TCS, Teleperformance, Conduent

## E. Vertical Applications

### E1. Front Office: Customer Service and Sales

- **Tailwinds: Customer service is the first job paid per result at scale, because a resolved ticket is easy to count and the labor it replaces is easy to price.** Software companies spend $20 to $30 per ticket resolved by people (founder interview) [src-20250821-666ad1], while customer service agent vendors publish prices of about $0.50 to $2.00 per resolution (vendor pricing pages) [src-20260924-1b6619] [src-20261029-0118e7].
- **Savings at early buyers are wide enough to make the business case easy to defend.** A national retailer pays under $2 per transaction handled by an agent against $7.50 to $9 for a person (expert call) [src-20260710-37d40b].
- **Voice is opening a second wave of spending in contact centers that still run mostly on people.** A contact-center software incumbent reports AI and self-service ARR of $362M growing 52% (company filings) [src-20260805-3deb2c].
- **Sales teams already pay agencies per meeting and per lead, which gives agents a known price to replace.** A customer service platform charges $9.99 for each qualified lead it hands to a sales team (vendor pricing pages) [src-20260924-1b6619], another vendor bills per qualified lead once it verifies the lead through the customer's CRM or other connected systems (trade press) [src-20261008-fb6a3d], and a head of sales says a re-engagement agent produced about $1M of revenue on spend of around $160,000 (expert call) [src-20251126-6d86c8].
- **Headwinds: The labs, the help desk platforms and the contact-center incumbents all sell a customer service agent now, which pushes prices down for everyone.** A model lab launched its own customer service agent (company announcement) [src-20260722-a3993b], and a CRM incumbent bought a leading agent vendor for about $3.6B (company announcement) [src-20260615-74b583].
- **Buyers spread work across vendors and consider building their own, so no vendor holds its account securely.** A fintech buyer says it would not bet on any single vendor and is considering building on local models of its own (expert call) [src-20260518-714b0a].
- **Results vary widely by customer, so buyers will soon compare agents with each other on resolution rate and price.** A leading agent resolves 30% to 40% of conversations at a software customer (expert call) [src-20260326-b3dd2d], against the 76% average its owner reports (company filings) [src-20260910-2cf2eb].
- **Sales agents churn heavily, because agents that send cold email burn through prospects and fail to show value inside short pilots.** An advisor to sales agent startups puts churn at 80% to 90%, the worst of any category he advises (expert call) [src-20250507-e13bdd].
- **CRM incumbents bundle their own sales agents to replace point tools, which squeezes standalone vendors in their accounts.** One CRM bundle priced at $550 claims to replace roughly $1,300 of point-solution spend per US sales rep (company announcement) [src-20260909-c1900d].
- **Why we should not focus here: Customer service proves that buyers will pay per result, but the labs, the help desk platforms and the contact-center incumbents all sell the same countable unit at falling prices.** Sales agents are paid on meetings and leads that are easy to count and easy to game, so buyers churn when quality slips, and the CRM owners bundle their own agents. Buyers in both functions keep their data so they can switch, and the customer service leaders already raised at roughly 45 to 100 times ARR by our estimate (company reports) [src-20260504-c9d184] [src-20261025-07358d] [src-20260127-5d9191], which leaves a new entrant little room to win share or earn a strong return. We should use customer service prices as the benchmark for other functions and make no new bets in the category.

**Example companies:** Sierra, Lorikeet, Gradient Labs, Vida, Decagon

### E2. Back-Office Operations: Finance, Accounting and Procurement

- **Tailwinds: Finance teams repeat the same work every month, and each invoice, collection or reconciliation has a clear count and cost.** A payables vendor reports processing over 115M invoices a year, and a collections vendor reports overdue balances cut by 40% (company claims) [src-20250721-54e512] [src-20251120-b7dfc3].
- **Procurement savings are measured against total spend, which ties an agent's value to the money it saves on purchases as well as the labor it replaces.** For a manufacturer spending $10B a year, a 50% more efficient team saves $50M, while a 1% to 2% cut in purchase prices is worth $100M to $200M (founder interview) [src-20260923-47e348], and a procurement agent vendor says it charges only when savings are realized (company claim) [src-20260723-5994c7].
- **Procurement and finance agents run on top of the ERP without replacing it, which speeds approval and lets the buyer check results in its own records.** A payments company's procurement lead says "We weren't trying to replace our system of record" (expert call) [src-20260716-88dbe4], and a global retailer's agent already handles 45% to 50% of its procurement volume (expert call) [src-20260209-7ec831].
- **Accounting and payables agents now sell the finished job and are paid for it.** An accounting agent vendor says it never had a seat or token price for its core product, and a payables vendor says its agents decide whether to pay each invoice and "take accountability for the outcome" (company interviews) [src-20260730-3a49fd] [src-20260729-f4bed6].
- **Headwinds: ERP vendors are building agents into the systems many finance teams already run, which limits how far a startup can reach inside those accounts.** A leading ERP vendor sells its agents through paid AI units, with a rules layer validating every action the model proposes (trade press) [src-20261003-10d57c].
- **Buyers keep a person on the final step, so the full price of the job moves to the agent slowly.** The chief executive of a procurement outsourcer says "I don't have any client that lets it go on its own" (expert call) [src-20260617-937cc1].
- **Consumption licenses with no share of savings cap revenue below the value created, and buyers can still switch without much pain.** A global retailer pays its procurement agent vendor a consumption-based license with no share of the savings and reports little stickiness, with a replacement possible within seven to eight months (expert call) [src-20260209-7ec831].
- **Most finance agent companies sell subscriptions or keep their pricing private, so the move to pricing per unit of work is less proven than in customer service.** Two published finance agent price lists are annual subscriptions set by the customer's revenue (vendor pricing pages) [src-20261029-a2fafb] [src-20261029-795928], and recent funding announcements in payables and collections do not say how the vendors charge (press releases) [src-20250721-54e512] [src-20251120-b7dfc3].
- **Why we should focus here: Finance and procurement work repeats every month, is easy to count and costs money when it goes wrong, so buyers pay for agents that cut the cost of each invoice, collection or order.** The agents sit on the buyer's ERP, so the result is counted in a record the buyer already trusts, deployment can take days and nobody has to replace a system to start. The work also runs across suppliers, banks and email outside any single record, which makes it harder for an ERP vendor to absorb than a help desk task. Revenue recurs with the monthly close and grows with the spend the agent handles, and we should back companies whose cost per invoice or order already falls with each new customer, because that is when margins move toward software levels.

**Example companies:** Basis, Magentic, fileAI, Freehand, Augment, Lio, Didero, HappyRobot

### E3. Regulated Services: Healthcare, Insurance, Legal and Life Sciences

- **Tailwinds: Healthcare billing, insurer approval before treatment and referrals are rule-bound work that providers pay for by the claim or by the dollar recovered.** A denial-recovery vendor says it helps clients at 3,000 clinical locations recover claims worth "nearly $1 billion per year" (company claim) [src-20260422-d6ae9d], fees to health plans for prior authorization can include a charge per automatically approved request (trade press) [src-20260213-d1e7fa], and a coding review vendor bills hospitals only for findings that are approved and billed (vendor pricing pages) [src-20261029-559e73].
- **Insurance runs on outsourced servicing, underwriting support and claims work, much of it on old systems that agents can now operate directly.** An insurer's chief operating officer says its servicing agent resolves more than 90% of service cases without a human touch (company claim) [src-20261025-c77b6e].
- **Companies that hold a license or act as the service provider can sell the whole job and keep its economics.** An AI-native commercial insurance brokerage reports over 160 carrier partners and over 5,000 customers (trade press) [src-20260225-3917a2], and AI-native claims administrators sell claims handling from first notice to payout as a service (trade press) [src-20250604-fd80b0] [src-20260401-133cee].
- **Legal agents are paid from the legal services budget that pays outside counsel, often per matter.** Personal injury demand packages, the file of evidence and damages a lawyer sends an insurer to settle a claim, are now priced per package (investor research) [src-20241205-4ac3cf], and an AI-native consumer law firm reports revenue above $35M (trade press) [src-20260205-e13567].
- **Drug companies are starting to buy research agents, beginning with target discovery, finding the gene or protein a new drug should act on, which gives vendors a paying customer for a clear unit of work.** A research agent vendor says one run equals about 6 months of a scientist's work (company claim) [src-20251105-cdfbed], and a listed drug developer is deploying a research agent across its research work (trade press) [src-20260519-f0a637].
- **Headwinds: The dominant electronic health record vendor controls the record at most big health systems and limits outside AI, which slows startups in those accounts.** A revenue cycle executive says the vendor does not "allow integrations of AI outside of what they're already working on" (expert call) [src-20260608-5e7d7e].
- **Providers still review claims before submission because errors carry liability, so the agent does not yet replace the whole job.** A dermatology group's chief information officer asks, "If any of the wrong claims go in, who takes a liability?" (expert call) [src-20251016-371c9b].
- **The labs are moving into healthcare administration with their own tools and sample workflows.** A model lab released healthcare connectors and a sample skill for reviewing requests for insurer approval before treatment (company announcement) [src-20260111-f1affc].
- **Insurance buyers are pitched constantly and expect cheaper offers built on open models, which caps prices for vendors that sell tools.** An insurance executive says he is pitched "at least two or three a day" and expects a rival offer "at a 10th of the price by using open-source models" (expert call) [src-20260811-a0aa1e].
- **Why we should focus here: Regulated services are paid by the claim, the policy, the matter or the run, so outcome pricing ties revenue to a unit the buyer already buys from outsourcers and law firms.** Companies that hold a license or take legal responsibility for the result keep more of the price than tool vendors and face the least pressure from cheaper open models. Healthcare billing agents are paid on cash the provider sees in its own books, and the record vendor's limits matter least in independent and specialty practices. As drafting tools get cheap, we should back the firms that sell finished legal work under their own license, because they keep the price of the outside counsel they replace. Research outcomes take years to prove, and a research agent priced at $200 per run ended its founding subscriptions seven months after launch (company announcements) [src-20251105-cdfbed] [src-20260519-9cb3f1], so in life sciences we should back companies that already show repeat paid runs at the same customer.

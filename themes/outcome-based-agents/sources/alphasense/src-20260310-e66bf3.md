TA KE AWAYS

 The company introduced an AI agent product (Fin) that required customers to initially purchase both the platform and the AI agent, with
 pricing based on resolved conversations (outcome-based pricing).
 Customers typically started with small-scale or pilot deployments of the AI agent, often targeting low-risk use cases or specific customer
 segments, before expanding usage based on success.
 The company did not have a formal, paid proof-of-concept (POC) model like some competitors; instead, it offered short trials or small
 contracts, which required significant customer effort to set up and evaluate.
 Customer success managers and sales engineers played key roles in driving adoption and expansion of the AI agent, focusing on metrics
 such as involvement rate, resolution rate, and deflection rate.
 Adoption of the AI agent often led to a significant increase in customer spend, with Fin sometimes doubling the original contract value
 over time, though results varied widely by customer.

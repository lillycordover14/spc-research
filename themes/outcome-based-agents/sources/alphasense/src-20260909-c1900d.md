Claudeforce Partnership and Strategic Alignment

• Q: How did the relationship between Salesforce and Anthropic come together, and how have you essentially commercialized what would have taken significant engineering resources to develop independently?

• A: The relationship came together naturally because customers were pulling both companies together to work more effectively. Salesforce is a huge Anthropic customer, and Anthropic is a huge Salesforce customer, creating natural
  synergies between the two technologies working together to drive better quality agents, better productivity for employees, and better product synergy. The number one AI platform and the number one CRM platform coming together
  validates core use cases served in common roles. Claudeforce was a product already being built, utilized, activated, and deployed in almost every customer before the announcement, making it a natural fit for the two product
  organizations to work together and deliver the product.

• Q: What have you learned in the last two weeks since making the announcement that was unexpected or that you hadn't predicted?

• A: The market and customer community breaks down into three tranches. The first tranche consists of heavy early adopters doing a lot of innovation and building connections and integrations themselves, validating the pattern for
  creating a product in this area and providing great feedback, validation, and conviction about the magic of these two products becoming a strong partnership. The second tranche includes users still trying to figure out how to make this
  work, scale it, and make it economically viable for their organization size, as not every small business has an endless token budget, requiring more knowledge and know-how to make it work right. The third community has skepticism
  around what AI means for them, with more AI hesitancy in different geographies outside San Francisco, requiring leaders to convince them this is not a destructive force but a force to fundamentally transform work.

Partnership Risk and Competitive Concerns

• Q: How do you think about the risk that Anthropic could eventually turn around and say they are happy to take it from here, given that tech partnerships come and go?

• A: The fundamental mission of both organizations is different from one another. Anthropic's mission is to prove that AI can have a ubiquitous moment in businesses and lives, while Salesforce's mission is fundamentally to make
  businesses better businesses. The mission of what they innovate on and what Salesforce innovates on are very different technologies with different paces of innovation and different responsibilities for what customers need to know,
  learn, and manage to be successful. There has been a long list of new innovations throughout history that were predicted to be the death of CRM systems, such as websites, which were thought to eliminate marketing outreach but
  instead transformed CRM to be a world of outside-in engagement as much as inside-out operational responsibility. There is a long list of companies and technologies trying to kill the CRM business, yet Salesforce is still here and doing
  well.

• Q: How would you respond to the bear case that Salesforce is essentially giving up the user interface, with the end customer now interacting with Claude as their first port of call, making Salesforce essentially a
  database?

• A: This argument is not agreed with, as minimizing Salesforce or CRM in general to a database is somewhat glib and an oversight into what has actually happened in CRM systems. So much of what people have done is express how
  their company operates in the form of metadata in Salesforce and their CRM, which is not very replicable and is what people are excited about accessing through other services like Claudeforce or Slack. While you may be giving up
  some time people spend in the interface, you are actually increasing the amount of work happening through the services that support the interfaces, with the bigger bet being that ultimately more work gets done through the CRM system.
  Internal data on Salesforce usage shows that usage has skyrocketed within Salesforce, with the overwhelming majority happening through Slack and Claude, meaning the amount and quality of work has dramatically skyrocketed and
  productivity has improved as seen through financial metrics, all because of moving to a headless manner that makes it more convenient for users to get work done.

Slack as Operating System for Work

• Q: What does it mean for Slack to be the operating system for work, and what does that look like for your R&D roadmap in investing in Slack not just as a communications tool but as an essential gateway to all
  underlying systems of record?

• A: The biggest asset at Slack is the aggregated attention of knowledge workers, with the average Slack user having the app open 10 hours a day and actively using it 2 hours a day. While this would be a great place to put ads if it were a
  consumer app, Slack will never put ads in the platform, making it an amazing place to distribute software as B2B software is increasingly being distributed through Slack as agents are. When you increase the amount of stuff coming at
  humans, they can become distracted, defeating the purpose of attention aggregation. The role Slack can play is similar to what original operating systems did by abstracting or hiding the complexity of hardware from the end user with a
  user interface paradigm, effectively doing the same for a litany of apps and agents now coming at people in addition to humans reaching out to them. From an operating system perspective, Slack can provide overall design guidelines
  and systems that apps and agents can adhere to, abstracting the complexity of thousands competing for people's attention and making it a single place where people can orchestrate their day and be a cockpit of. Ultimately, Slack
  becomes where the AI stack starts and stops.

• Q: What are the alternatives for customers that are hesitant and not using Slack as one of their primary user interfaces, and what does that look like?

• A: The alternative is alternatives from competing products that are functionally deficient. The key thing about Slack is seen in all major AI companies that use it, as Anthropic, OpenAI, and Vercel, all frontier labs and AI-first companies,
  operate their Slack in a very unique way by operating totally in public. Every single Anthropic employee has a notebook channel, which is basically a channel with themselves where they think out loud through the course of the day, and
  OpenAI, Vercel, and Slack do this as well. What this does is radically flatten an organization and immediately disseminate all information from that person to every other person in the company and importantly to all other agents. The
  model in Slack and the way the software encourages people to participate in a very social and public way is not something seen in the alternatives that are out there today.

• Q: What is Anthropic doing with Slack that allows them to power an army of agents behind the interface?

• A: Anthropic does as much as humanly possible in public, with every single product channel and feature channel being public for the entire company to access and all agents to access. Every single person has what is called a notebook
  or brain channel where they think out loud in these brain channels, and Vercel does a similar thing. Everybody in the company can see what each person is thinking, and every agent in the Slack can see what they are thinking because it
  is public. The CEO of Shopify wrote a piece on Twitter called Learning on the Shop Floor about using their River agent in Slack but only doing so in public, deploying their coding agent in Slack and only allowing it to be used in public
  channels. What this does is effectively immediately disseminate to the entire company every single thing being built, allowing everybody to search and see if somebody else is working on it yet, radically reducing duplication of effort and
  allowing people to learn from each other. The bear case to individual contributor AI tools is that the AI learns and the person using it learns, but their peer doesn't learn and the company doesn't learn from it, but moving that into public in a
  Slack channel means everybody else can learn from it and it immediately becomes a knowledge artifact for employees and other agents.

• Q: What else is in the IP of Slack that allows that use case to flourish?

• A: Some of it comes down to how Slack works, with one of the traditional competitive advantages being the quality of user experience and the customer love generated from that user experience. Slack has a really high NPS, customers
  love to use the stuff, they tell their friends about it, it is viral, and it takes off within a market. When people are asking their company for access to software, they ask if it can be put in Slack, allowing Slack to develop groundswell and
  grassroots support and pull from users.

Monetization Models and Pricing Strategy

• Q: How does the pricing model change for users like Shopify who are getting boatloads more value, especially for the AI examples given, when historically Slack has been a seat count model?

• A: Slack has a series of plans, and has been one of the more successful premium products with the overwhelming majority of invoice business or sales business initiating as self-serve. Users start as a free team, grow through the plan
  through nudges in the product to use more features, start paying for it, and eventually convert into an invoice enterprise plan that salespeople help with. From a monetization perspective, the total TAM for knowledge workers is around $1
  billion at this point, so there is still a ton of upside as it relates to user acquisition. Increasingly, there will be more agents in Slack than humans, creating a fascinating monetization opportunity that has not been totally cracked yet but
  creates incredible upside for Salesforce. Agents can potentially be thought of as users, and consumption-based mechanisms for monetization can be considered as agents, with the access patterns for information in Slack by agents
  being very different than from humans, so the company is in early stages of this.

• Q: Can you break down the 60% to 80% uplift for premium SKU, and if we are a Salesforce customer not having invested engineering resources into making Claudeforce happen, how soon can we onboard?

• A: When thinking about all the software required to make a salesperson productive, it often expands beyond the CRM system for managing sales pipeline, contacts, and accounts to include sales performance management systems for
  quota, commission, and compensation management, sales enablement software for content, and externally call recording software or coaching software. The average spend on a customer with all of those other technologies in the
  United States is about $1,300 for an average sales professional. Salesforce has been able to put a lot of this value into one price edition called Agentforce 1 Edition, which retails for $550, taking money out of the extremities where every
  sales organization has to recruit, hire, activate, and make salespeople productive and performant and putting it into one offering. It is early days for premium levels of monetization, really only started in the last couple of years, and as
  customers go through the life cycle of a contract, more and more are trading up to these editions as they come up for renewal. It is not something that will be a big bang immediately overnight where everyone is on a premium edition, but
  rather as companies have other contracts rolling off from other vendors and consolidating onto Salesforce. The same is true on the Service Cloud side, where you have to have a case management solution, a telephony solution, and
  sometimes a chat or channel solution, with the same play being engineered on that side for more consolidation of extraneous spend into a consolidated Salesforce platform.

• Q: Can you help put the different offerings into cohorts so we can understand what different customers should purchase?

• A: With Agentforce 1 Edition, customers get Claudeforce, Agentforce, Data Cloud, and the best of all the Sales Cloud, with the goal being to have one edition that they all come into as a premium tier of all those offerings. Customers can
  buy all those items discretely, but Salesforce wants to make it an economically viable and attractive offering for companies to get the best of Salesforce in this one edition because for organizations running Agentforce 1 Edition, their
  salespeople are more productive and using the software more because they have Slack. The edition also includes the Tableau offering so they have all the analytics needed to be productive in their role, and this is really not just a
  strategy of loading someone up with all the Salesforce they could have but rather engineering this from the start to make it the best edition for workers to have the best performance of their lives.

• Q: Explain the difference between Fin and Agentforce, as Fin in practice sounds a little bit like Agentforce in practice.

• A: As the world of agents and agentic platforms are starting to clarify, it is becoming very clear that there are many customers that want to have purpose-built, specialized agents perform discrete tasks on behalf of their business. Fin is a
  solution engineered for the customer service domain to help streamline and automate a lot of routine interactions that a company has with its customers, making it a purposeful agent that is hyper-specialized and focused on that area.

 Investment Summary
 We are initiating coverage of SailPoint, Inc. (SAIL) with an Outperform rating and $30 price
 target. SailPoint is a leading enterprise identity security provider built on Atlas, the
 company’s cloud-native platform that applies a common identity graph and policy engine
 across human, machine, and AI agent identities. SailPoint’s portfolio is organized around
 Human Fabric, which governs employees, contractors, and partners, and Agentic Fabric, a
 standalone offering for machine identities and AI agents. Its legacy IdentityIQ product is
 also available for customers requiring on-premises deployment. SailPoint combines a large
 enterprise installed base, established Chief Information and Security Officer (CISO)
 relationships, and a growing portfolio of identity governance and security capabilities at a
 time when the number and complexity of human and machine/AI identities are increasing.
 Our bullish view on SailPoint is framed by three debates. First, can SailPoint play a leading
 role in agentic AI identity security? We believe it can, capitalizing on its established
 discovery and governance functionality and emerging protection layer capabilities. Second,
 can ARR growth reaccelerate above management’s FY29 target? We believe it can. We
 forecast a 23.8% three-year ARR CAGR to approximately $2.14 billion, above
 management’s $2.1 billion target and 23.1% implied CAGR. We expect durable core
 execution, supplemented by Human Fabric and Agentic Fabric adoption. Our view reflects
 a base case with upside potential. Third, can the stock appreciate despite Thoma Bravo’s
 ~85% ownership? We believe this ownership overhang is primarily a liquidity and sentiment
 issue, rather than a fundamental impairment. While future sponsor sales could create
 temporary periods of volatility, we believe investors will appreciate SailPoint’s differentiation
 and longer-term potential. If investors don’t, strategic buyers likely would, making SailPoint
 a potential acquisition target.
 Debate 1 – Can SailPoint play a leading role in Agentic AI Identity Security? We believe
 SailPoint’s core identity governance and administration (IGA) capabilities map well to the
 challenges created by AI agents, addressing lifecycle management, access certification,
 policy modeling, owner assignment, and accountability. SailPoint’s Agentic Fabric extends
 these controls to agent discovery and governance, while the company’s recent Entro
 acquisition adds non-human identity, secrets, and credential-layer capabilities. We believe
 any AI security solution needs to address three core competencies: discovery, governance,
 and protection. SailPoint’s discovery and governance capabilities are well-developed, while
 its protection capabilities, including real-time authorization and SOC-integrated response,
 are in early development. We expect the protection layer to follow a partner model, with
 SailPoint providing identity context, policy, and authorization, while security partners provide
 runtime telemetry and enforcement. We believe this approach can work well as most
 competing point solutions address only discovery or narrow non-human identity use cases;
 whereas SailPoint brings a deep connector library, mature policy modeling, large regulated-
 industry reference customers, and established buyer relationships that are difficult to
 replicate. Its strong discovery and governance foundation supports our view that SailPoint
 will become a leading AI security provider as enterprise AI agent adoption proliferates.
 Debate 2 – Can ARR Growth Accelerate and Achieve FY29 targets? Based on our
 proprietary ARR scenario analysis, we believe consensus estimates embed little to no
 contribution from AI security. Our analysis suggests management’s $2.1 billion FY29 target
 is achievable with only modest Agentic Fabric adoption. Our core human-fabric based
 assumptions alone produce results near consensus, indicating current estimates largely
 reflect normal customer expansion and migration trends, rather than meaningful AI security
 adoption. Assuming just 10% installed-base adoption of new AI identity security products
 by FY29 contributes an estimated ~$90 million of incremental ARR and lifts our total ARR
 estimate to approximately $2.14 billion, ~$75 million above consensus and ~70 basis points
 above management’s 23.1% implied CAGR. We view 10% penetration as a conservative
 and low bar given SailPoint’s large regulated-enterprise customer base and more than $200
 million of current AI-driven pipeline. If adoption reaches 20%-30%, which we believe is
 achievable as enterprise AI production deployments scale, total ARR could approach $2.2-
 $2.3 billion (25.1%-26.9% CAGR). This upside does not require heroic outperformance of
 our core assumptions and suggests that adoption of SailPoint’s AI identity security products
 can potentially drive significant positive revisions over a multi-year horizon above
 management’s existing target.

 Company Overview
 SailPoint offers identity governance security solutions that manage identity access to
 specific enterprise resources and continuously verify that access remains appropriate.
 Founded by Mark McClain in 2005, SailPoint has evolved its product portfolio from on-
 premises identity governance to a unified, AI-powered cloud platform spanning human,
 machine, and AI agent identities. Thoma Bravo took SailPoint private in 2022 before relisting
 the company in February 2025 under the ticker SAIL, retaining control as majority
 stockholder.
 SailPoint's portfolio is built on Atlas, a multi-tenant software-as-a-service (SaaS) platform
 that unifies identity, data, and security context through a common identity graph, a shared
 policy engine, and hundreds of prebuilt connectors. Built on Atlas is the SailPoint Identity
 Security platform which includes two products: Human Fabric and Agentic Fabric.
 Human Fabric, the successor to SailPoint’s Identity Security Cloud (ISC), governs human
 identities such as employees, contractors, and partners. Key capabilities include lifecycle
 management, access certification, access modeling, and analytics capabilities. The solution
 is available through three suite tiers: Standard, Agentic Business, and Agentic Business
 Plus. Standard covers human identities only, while Agentic Business extends governance
 to human and non-human identities. Agentic Business Plus extends this further, adding zero
 standing privileges, just-in-time access, and real-time remediation. Customers can also add
 modules, including Non-Employee Risk Management, Access Risk Management, Cloud
 Infrastructure Entitlement Management, Password Management, and Accelerated
 Application Management, to extend the capabilities of any of the Human Fabric suites.
 Agentic Fabric, generally available since August 2026, provides similar controls to machine
 identities and AI agents, with expanded discovery capabilities built with SailPoint’s recent
 Entro Security acquisition. It is available as a standalone product, and is offered in three
 tiers, Standard, Enterprise, and Platinum. SailPoint’s standalone option gives legacy on-
 premise customers and new customers an agentic entry point that does not require a
 prerequisite human identity suite purchase.
 Pricing for both products follows an annual subscription model, licensed by human and non-
 human identity counts. Agentic Business and Agentic Business Plus suites offer coverage
 for five non-human identities per human identity, with additional capacity packs available
 should agent, API, or other NHI workflow volumes grow beyond the initial allocation.
 Navigators, a set of flexible purchasing constructs (Modernization Flex, Premier Flex, and
 Digital Identity Flex), let customers reallocate spend across identity types and capabilities
 without renegotiating a contract.
 SailPoint also maintains an on-premises product, IdentityIQ (IIQ), a customer-hosted
 version used primarily by organizations with data residency or self-hosting requirements.
 Most of the existing customer base has since transitioned to an earlier version of Human
 Fabric called Identity Security Cloud (ISC), for which the newly introduced Agentic Business
 and Agentic Business plus suites serve as a direct upgrade path. However, a significant
 portion remains on IIQ today, representing a high visibility source of future migrations.
 SailPoint employs a hybrid sales and go-to-market model that combines direct sales with a
 central enablement role for indirect channel partners. In FY26, more than 90% of new
 customer transactions involved a partner. The partner ecosystem spans global system
 integrators (such as Accenture, Capgemini, Deloitte, KPMG, PwC), value-added resellers
 (CDW, GuidePoint, Optiv), and a growing list of MSPs (Simeio, Kommando, MajorKey).
 As of 2QFY27, SailPoint had $1.23 billion of total ARR, growing 25% YoY, of which $847
 million was SaaS ARR, growing 36% YoY as the company’s mix shifts away from term and
 perpetual licenses. The company serves approximately 3,300 customers across roughly 65
 countries, including about 53% of the Fortune 500 and about 28% of the Forbes Global
 2000. SailPoint ended 2QFY27 with 1,330 customers generating more than $250,000 in
 ARR (up 22% YoY) and 235 customers generating more than $1 million in ARR (up 27%
 YoY), while its dollar-based net retention rate (NRR) stood at 113%. Geographically, the
 United States accounted for 65% of FY26 revenue, with international markets making up
 the remaining 35%. SailPoint had 3,229 employees as of January 2026 and is
 headquartered in Austin, TX with additional offices in Pune, India; Tel Aviv, Israel, and
 London, England. The company holds approximately 80 US patents, with approximately 20
 additional applications pending.

 Investment Thesis
 Our positive view on SailPoint is best framed with three debates that dominate investor
 conversations. One, can SailPoint play a leading role in agentic AI identity security? As the
 leading incumbent in identity governance with entrenched CISO relationships and a platform
 retooled for AI agents, we believe SailPoint can. Two, can ARR growth reaccelerate toward
 management’s FY29 target? We believe so. We forecast a 23.8% three-year ARR CAGR,
 reaching approximately $2.14 billion in FY29, achieving management’s >$2.1 billion ARR
 goal, driven by on-premise to SaaS migration, suite up-tiers, and Agentic Fabric cross-sell.
 Three, can the stock appreciate despite Thoma Bravo’s ~85% ownership overhang? We
 believe it can. Investors have already shown a willingness to own SailPoint shares well
 above Thoma Bravo’s cost basis. We believe concern here is more psychological than
 fundamental. Below, we expand on each debate.

 Debate No. 1: Can SailPoint Play a Leading Role in Agentic AI Identity Security?
 SailPoint’s core IGA business is experiencing solid demand, but investors’ focus is squarely
 on the company’s ability to participate in the agentic AI identity security opportunity. In our
 opinion, SailPoint is well positioned to play a meaningful role in AI agent identity security
 and will become a leading provider to large enterprises. Our view considers that SailPoint’s
 solution is not yet complete and requires further development, though recent M&A and
 product innovation shows meaningful progress in developing a comprehensive solution.
 At its core, SailPoint is an identity governance company, not an authentication provider,
 directory, or vault. Its key advantage lies in the capabilities it applies to human identities.
 These strengths map directly to the problems agents create, including lifecycle
 management, access certification, policy and separation-of-duties modeling, and access
 intelligence. Agents require identity lifecycle management with rich context, fine-grained
 policy enforcement, secure delegation, and real-time oversight, and not just simple access
 to API keys. SailPoint’s core competency is in contextual identity modeling, policy-driven

 governance, and lifecycle control. Its Agentic Business suites and its standalone Agentic
 Fabric solutions extend these controls to agents, adding owner assignment and
 accountability, real-time governance across human and machine identities, and AI/ML-
 based anomaly detection.
 It is important to consider that governance is only one layer of agentic security. Agent
 security can also be addressed at runtime (workload identity and secrets brokering), in the
 authentication fabric, through cloud-native workload identity, or at the authorization layer.
 SailPoint’s governance plane is one element of several needed to address the challenges
 of AI Agents. What SailPoint adds to the governance layer is a deep connector library,
 strong policy modeling, large reference accounts in regulated industries (banks, insurers,
 federal/DoD), and a sales force with established head of identity/CISO relationships. What
 SailPoint historically lacks are runtime authentication and governance of secrets primitives,
 which are important as runtime brokers need to work in conjunction with governance to
 credibly handle recertification and ownership attestation, and to provide audit evidence and
 separation-of-duties modeling.
 SailPoint’s recent acquisition of Entro, a non-human identity and secrets vendor, gives
 SailPoint secrets and credential-layer primitives it previously lacked. This partially closes
 the gap we describe. Additionally, the company’s May 2026 launch of Agentic Fabric also
 extended its capabilities beyond human governance into native agent discovery and
 governance, and the partial protection of agent and machine identities. With Agentic Fabric,
 SailPoint addresses AI agent identity security by governing the systems of record in which
 agents exist, understanding who owns them, what they can do, and whether their
 permissions remain up to date. In short, SailPoint is making strong progress in expanding
 its agentic security capabilities and developing a comprehensive solution.
 Management frames the company’s new Agentic Fabric portfolio around three capabilities:
 discover, govern, and protect. The discover and govern capabilities are more developed
 and mature, and bolstered by the Entro acquisition. The protect capabilities are emerging
 with some kill-switch and enforcement capabilities available today. SailPoint is still
 developing real-time authorization, prompt security, and SOC-integrated response and
 remediation capabilities. SailPoint plans to introduce these controls into the security
 operations center (SOC) through the Shared Signals Framework (SSF). SSF provides
 SailPoint identity context and allows it to receive threat signals through integrations with
 other vendors, including its announced CrowdStrike integration. We expect protect
 capabilities to follow a SailPoint-plus-partner model, with SailPoint providing identity
 context, policy, and authorization, while security partners contribute runtime telemetry and
 enforcement. Continued execution on this roadmap remains key, though SailPoint’s
 integrated discovery and governance foundation is more complete than other point solutions
 which gives us confidence it can close the remaining protection gap.
 Overall, we’re confident SailPoint will take a leading role in the agentic AI security market.

 The Atlas Platform: A Unified Identity Infrastructure
 SailPoint's architecture, Atlas, serves as the common infrastructure layer underlying
 SailPoint Identity Security, and its Human Fabric and Agentic Fabric solutions. Atlas
 provides a unified data model, known as the identity graph, enabling a shared policy engine,
 workflow automation, security orchestration, a connectivity and extensibility layer, and a
 shared set of AI services engineered for real time telemetry and inline threat response. This
 foundation lets SailPoint develop new product capabilities without separate infrastructure
 builds, while accelerating innovation and improving economies of scale.
 A critical differentiator of Atlas is its identity graph, which aggregates a 360-degree view of
 every enterprise identity, human and non-human. The graph captures attributes,
 entitlements, roles, policies, historical access patterns, risk scores, and behavioral signals
 across all connected enterprise systems. SailPoint’s recent Entro acquisition, expanded
 Atlas capabilities adding the ability to map the credentials, secrets, and tokens attached to
 non-human identities. This multidimensional identity context enables SailPoint's AI models
 to make sophisticated recommendations, flag anomalous access patterns, and drive
 automated remediation, capabilities that other vendors cannot easily replicate.
 Atlas incorporates four major AI capabilities. (1) Recommendations, which provides AI
 driven access request, provisioning, and certification decisions; (2) Discovery, which
 automatically identifies and models roles and access patterns; (3) Insights, which surfaces
 risk signals and compliance gaps proactively; and (4) Harbor Pilot, a conversational AI

 configurations, and drive workflow automation with low code and no code tooling.

 Risks and Investment Concerns
 Competitive Intensity from Large Platform Vendors. Microsoft, Okta, and Palo Alto
 Networks (which owns Idira, formerly CyberArk) possess substantial scale advantages,
 product depth, distribution reach, and financial resources that could enable more aggressive
 bundling and pricing strategies across the broader identity security market. If any of these
 vendors materially improves their IGA capabilities, or effectively bundle their broad identity
 security solutions, SailPoint could face price pressure or displacement risk, especially in
 accounts where customers prioritize consolidation over best-of-breed depth. While we
 believe SailPoint's enterprise differentiation and product breadth mitigates this risk today,
 the competitive landscape is dynamic and will continue to evolve.
 LLM and AI Platform Cannibalization Risk. The rapid advancements of large language
 models from vendors such as Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google introduces the long-term risk
 that general purpose AI platforms could embed “good enough” identity governance
 functionality directly into their infrastructure layers. This could reduce the need for
 standalone IGA solutions for some customers. In addition, same agentic AI frameworks
 SailPoint currently secures could, over time, internalize identity context natively, potentially
 commoditizing the governance layer. We view this as a low-probability event but a
 structurally important tail risk to consider. SailPoint’s depth of entitlement-level governance
 across thousands of heterogenous applications, two decades of domain-specific policy
 context, and the regulatory requirements mandating purpose-built IGA infrastructure all
 serve as meaningful mitigating factors.
 Thoma Bravo Ownership Overhang. Thoma Bravo remains a significant shareholder of
 SailPoint following the company’s initial public offering in February 2025. As Thoma Bravo
 seeks to monetize its position over time, potential share sales could create supply pressure
 on SailPoint's stock. Thoma Bravo has not yet exited much of its position. The timing and
 pace of any Thoma Bravo sell down could create periodic headwinds for SAIL shares.
 SaaS Transition Execution Risk. While SailPoint’s SaaS transition is a long-term margin
 tailwind, the near-term revenue recognition dynamics create complexity and could introduce
 quarterly volatility. Term subscription deals (primarily IdentityIQ renewals) recognize
 revenue upfront in a lump sum, while SaaS deals recognize ratably over the contract period.
 As the mix shifts toward SaaS, reported revenue growth rates and free cash flow can be
 temporarily depressed even as ARR growth remains strong. Investors less familiar with the
 distinction between ARR growth and revenue growth may misinterpret this dynamic as a
 slowdown.
 Macroeconomic Sensitivity. While identity security has proven more resilient than
 discretionary IT spending, a prolonged macroeconomic downturn could delay enterprise
 security investment decisions, lengthen sales cycles, or cause customers to defer platform
 modernization projects. SailPoint's enterprise focus means deal sizes are meaningful
 (average ARR per customer of approximately $350,000+), and any material slippage of
 large enterprise deals could impact quarterly results. To date, management has not seen
 noteworthy macro impact on demand, but this risk warrants monitoring.
 Platform Modernization Execution Risk. A significant portion of SailPoint's growth
 opportunity involves migrating existing IdentityIQ customers to Identity Security Cloud.
 These migrations are complex, multi-year programs that require significant customer effort
 and SailPoint professional services support. If platform modernization programs slow,
 encounter implementation difficulties, or face customer budget constraints, the associated
 ARR uplift and cross-sell opportunity could be deferred. Execution risk is elevated in highly
 complex environments (e.g., large manufacturing, healthcare, financial services) where
 IdentityIQ integrations are deeply customized.
 AI Agent Governance Adoption Uncertainty. Agent Identity Security is a new and
 nascent product category. While the market opportunity is compelling, enterprise adoption
 of formal AI agent governance frameworks is still in its earliest stages. Regulatory
 frameworks governing AI agent security are still being developed, and enterprises may defer
 formal governance investment until regulatory requirements become clearer. Our base case
 assigns modest near-term ARR contribution from Agent Identity Security; if adoption is

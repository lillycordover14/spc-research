[agent notes, not verbatim] Primary Claude blog post dated January 30, 2026. This is the research-preview launch of plugins in Claude Cowork, which the February 24 enterprise agents program then extended with private marketplaces and admin controls (see separate record).

What plugins are: "we're adding support for plugins, which let you bundle any skills, connectors, slash commands, and sub-agents together" so Claude can act as a specialist for a role, team or company. Each plugin is set up once and Claude draws on that context when relevant. "Plugins work for any use case, but they're especially powerful for tailoring Claude to specific job functions."

Initial plugins (11), open-sourced: Productivity, Enterprise search, Plugin Create/Customize, Sales, Finance, Data, Legal, Marketing, Customer support, Product management, Biology research. "we're open-sourcing 11 plugins built and used by our own team." Install from Cowork, browse on the website or upload your own. "Every component of plugins (skills, connectors, slash commands, and sub-agents) is file-based."

Availability: "Plugin support in Cowork is available today as a research preview for all paid Claude users." "Plugins are currently saved locally to your machine."

No pricing beyond existing paid plans. No customers named. Context for the database: this is the packaging unit Anthropic later used for its finance (Feb 24, May 5) and legal (May 12) solution sets, and for cookbooks deployable as Managed Agents.

[agent notes] Why it matters for the thesis: the 11 starter plugins map almost one to one onto the departments where agent startups sell (sales, finance, legal, customer support, marketing, data, product). Because the format is open and file-based, the same packaging is later reused by third parties: on February 24 Slack by Salesforce, LSEG, S&P Global, Apollo, Common Room and Tribe AI shipped partner-built plugins, and by October 21 Anthropic cited over 6,000 connectors and plugins available to its Claude Duet agent. The initial release lacked enterprise distribution (plugins saved locally), which the February 24 private marketplace release addressed. Search-result excerpts of TechCrunch's February 24 piece (not fetched) also date the plugin research preview to January 30, consistent with this page.

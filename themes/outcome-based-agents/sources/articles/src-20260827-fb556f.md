summary: a16z growth partners Tugce Erten and Sarah Wang argue applications should not import model-layer token pricing, and should instead price at the highest layer of value they can reliably measure, attribute and defend. Model access is priced in tokens, useful work in recognizable units often packaged as credits, and clear attributable business results as outcomes. In a survey of 50 technical AI buyers, 27 preferred credits tied to recognizable work and 14 preferred tokens. Good credit systems separate what the work is worth from what it costs, which protects margin and lets the vendor keep part of falling model costs; Clay is cited as splitting Data Credits, Actions and token pass-through. Outcomes such as a resolved conversation, processed claim or recovered dollar should be priced once they are observable, attributable and valuable enough for a stable price, often inside hybrids.

- Magentic has raised $18 million in its Series A funding round, with Felicis as the lead and Sequoia and the Westly Group also participating.
- Magentic’s AI agents carry out more than one million purchase orders every year for a single manufacturer.
- Since its launch, Magentic has raised a total of $23.5 million in just 14 months, including its most recent funding round.
Robin Van Aeken and Odhran O’Donoghue first met when they were both winning hackathons at Oxford. Seven years later, Van Aeken, who had previously been a supply-chain consultant at McKinsey, and O’Donoghue, a machine-learning PhD with work experience at OpenAI, NASA, and the Crick Institute, got in touch again.
They regarded procurement as a significant problem in enterprise software and founded Magentic, which has since secured $18 million in Series A funding. This round of financing brings Magentic’s total funding to $23.5 million, with Felicis as the lead investor and Sequoia Capital and The Westly Group also participating.
From Oxford hackathons to tackling supplier compliance issues
Van Aeken, now chief executive, spent many years witnessing large companies suffer losses due to what McKinsey calls supplier compliance “leaks,” amounting to about 2% of total spending. That is, $40 million on a $2 billion budget, according to the company’s research. O’Donoghue, the chief technology officer, was in charge of the AI engineering.
The company launched Magentic in July 2025 with a $5.5 million seed round provided by Sequoia Capital, The Westly Group, First Momentum Ventures, and angel investors from SAP, Airbus, McKinsey, and Hugging Face. Eleven months later, they had raised more than three times the original amount.
Magentic’s product, Mages, links with manufacturers’ email accounts, Microsoft Teams, and their internal systems and functions more like a member of the team than most software does. It handles decisions on whether to buy or build, selects suppliers, negotiates contracts, places orders, and processes invoices.
The system can manage large-scale operations, including billions of data rows, tens of billions of dollars in spending, and legacy ERPs that still use Excel. One manufacturer uses the platform to handle more than a million purchase orders every year, while another discovered $4 million in savings that human teams had overlooked.
A crowded field in procurement automation
Pactum from Estonia raised $54 million in its Series C funding in mid-2025, bringing its total funding amount to more than $100 million, even though its main focus is on automating supplier negotiations. Freehand from New York raised $75 million and already has agents operating at Meta, Unilever, Johnson & Johnson, and Pfizer. Didero, which was founded by another former McKinsey procurement expert, raised $30 million less than a year ago.
What sets Magentic apart is that it aims to handle the whole process, from the decisions regarding whether to buy or build through to direct and indirect spending and invoice reconciliation for the largest industrial buyers.
Feyza Haskaraman, a partner at Felicis, a firm that has provided funding to more than 50 unicorns such as Shopify, Canva, and Notion, stated, “Supply chains are the least glamorous part of the economy, yet the most consequential, deciding what gets built and what does not. That’s also what makes them so hard to automate. Getting an agent to understand a manufacturer’s complex systems well enough to take action inside them is no small feat, which is why we haven’t seen anyone else build autonomous AI workers for the physical economy.”
How Magentic will use the $18 million
The additional funds will be used to extend its coverage to more procurement and supply-chain workflows and to support long-term AI research into complex optimization problems.
“The physical world is dealing with the biggest capex cycle in history, driven by AI demand, during a time of trade disruption and geopolitical challenges. The companies that build the best intelligence into every decision they make will be the ones that compound their competitive advantage,” Van Aeken stated.
Despite the attention it receives, the procurement software market remains fairly small. The global market will amount to $10.1 billion in 2025 and rise to $21.3 billion by 2033. Goldman Sachs estimates that there will be about $8 trillion in AI-related capital spending by 2031, with the majority allocated to physical infrastructure, which still needs to be procured, constructed, and financed.
Various startups rely on the idea that chief procurement officers will one day allow AI agents to place orders and negotiate supplier contracts without any human supervision. Magnetic’s early customers are already operating on this scale. However, it is not yet clear whether the wider Global 500 will adopt this method in the near future or wait until after a major error occurs.

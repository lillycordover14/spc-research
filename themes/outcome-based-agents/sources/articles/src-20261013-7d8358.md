Fin Beats Zendesk & Agentforce: $0.99/Ticket [2026]&nbsp;&nbsp;tech-insider.org

[page text]
Every Irish support team has the same spreadsheet somewhere: ticket volume climbing, headcount flat, and a board asking why the helpdesk budget keeps growing. The answer most are reaching for in 2026 is not a smarter general-purpose chatbot. It is a purpose-built AI resolution agent that sits inside the helpdesk itself, reads the knowledge base, and closes tickets without a human ever opening the thread. That is a different product category from ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and it comes with its own pricing logic, its own accuracy benchmarks, and its own GDPR headaches.
Four platforms dominate the conversation among Irish support leads this year: Intercom Fin, Zendesk’s AI agents, Salesforce Agentforce and Freshworks’ Freddy AI. Each charges differently, each claims a different resolution rate, and each handles EU data residency in its own way. This comparison breaks down the specs, the real per-resolution cost, the benchmark evidence behind the marketing claims, and which platform actually fits a given Irish business, whether that is a twelve-person SaaS startup in Dublin or a regulated financial services firm bound by the Central Bank’s outsourcing rules.

What Makes an AI Customer Service Chatbot Different From ChatGPT or Claude
General-purpose assistants like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini answer open-ended questions from training data plus whatever you paste into the chat window. AI customer service chatbots do something narrower and, for a business, more valuable: they are grounded in a specific company’s help centre articles, macros, order data and CRM records, and they are built to take actions, not just produce text. A resolution agent can look up an order, issue a refund, update a subscription tier or escalate to a named human agent, all inside the same conversation.
That functional difference shows up in how these tools are priced. A general chatbot subscription is usually a flat monthly seat fee. An AI support agent is usually billed per outcome, per action or per session, because the vendor is selling a measurable unit of work rather than access to a model. Intercom’s own agent pricing guide frames this directly: businesses pay Fin’s fee only when a conversation is actually resolved, not for every message exchanged.
For an Irish business evaluating these tools, the practical question is rarely “which model is smartest.” It is closer to: which platform resolves the highest share of real tickets at the lowest blended cost, while meeting Irish and EU data protection obligations. That is the lens this comparison uses throughout.
Meet the Four Contenders: Fin, Zendesk AI Agents, Agentforce and Freddy AI
Intercom Fin is the AI resolution layer built into Intercom’s messaging platform. It is sold separately from Intercom’s seat-based plans (Essential, Advanced and Expert), so a business pays for the helpdesk seats its human agents use, plus a per-outcome fee for every conversation Fin resolves on its own.
Zendesk’s AI agents are built into the Zendesk Suite and Support plans rather than sold as a fully separate product. Each plan ships with an included allowance of AI resolutions, and businesses that exceed it purchase additional allowance. Zendesk markets this as an outcome-based model similar to Intercom’s, starting from a lower headline per-resolution price.
Salesforce Agentforce takes a different approach entirely. Rather than resolutions, Salesforce charges for actions consumed through Flex Credits, a prepaid currency that also covers other Salesforce AI features. Agentforce is strongest where a business already runs its support desk inside Salesforce Service Cloud and wants the AI agent to read and write CRM records natively. Salesforce is also packaging Agentforce inside industry editions: on October 7, 2026 it said that for Financial Services Cloud, Enterprise Edition was renamed Core Edition, Unlimited Edition was renamed Advanced Edition, and Agentforce 1 Sales/Service became Sales Max / Service Max Edition, so older edition names may still appear in quotes and contracts.
Freshworks Freddy AI is the AI layer inside Freshworks’ Freshchat and Freshdesk products. It uses a session-based model: a business gets a batch of AI sessions included in its plan, and buys more as volume grows. Freshworks positions this as the easiest entry point for smaller teams that are not ready to commit to per-resolution pricing.
These four are not the only AI customer service agents on the market. Zendesk’s own comparison material lists 13 competing platforms in this category, including smaller specialist vendors alongside the household names. The four compared here were chosen because they cover four genuinely different pricing philosophies, per outcome, per allowance, per credit and per session, which makes them the clearest set to use when explaining how this category’s economics actually work rather than just listing features side by side.
Why This Isn’t Another ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini Story
It is worth drawing a hard line here, because the two categories get conflated constantly in procurement meetings. Our earlier comparison of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini for enterprise use covers seat pricing, context windows and model quality for a workforce using a general assistant to draft emails, summarise documents and write code. None of that maps cleanly onto what Intercom Fin, Zendesk’s AI agents, Salesforce Agentforce or Freshworks Freddy AI actually do, because those four tools are judged on a completely different metric: how many customer tickets they close without a human, not how well they write a paragraph.
The two categories do intersect on regulation, though. Our breakdown of EU AI Act exposure for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini applies almost word for word to a customer-facing AI resolution agent, since Article 50’s transparency obligations do not care whether the AI system behind the chat window is a general-purpose model or a narrow resolution agent built on top of one. A business that has already done the compliance homework for a general chatbot rollout is not starting from zero when it evaluates Fin, Zendesk’s AI agents, Agentforce or Freddy AI.
There is also a feature-level overlap worth flagging. Our look at agent mode across ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini found that general assistants are themselves adding the ability to take multi-step actions rather than just answer questions, which is precisely the capability that defines this entire customer-service category. The direction of travel is the same even though the starting products are different: assistants are becoming agents, and agents are becoming the thing businesses actually pay for by the resolved outcome.

Pricing Compared: Per-Resolution, Per-Action and Per-Seat
Pricing is where the four platforms diverge most sharply, and where an Irish finance team needs to do actual arithmetic rather than compare headline numbers. A per-resolution price only tells part of the story once seat costs, allowances and credit consumption are added back in.
| Platform | AI pricing unit | Published rate | Human seat pricing | Notes | 
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intercom Fin | Per resolved outcome | $0.99 | Essential $29, Advanced $85, Expert $132 per month (annual billing) | No charge when Fin fails to resolve the conversation | 
| Zendesk AI Agents | Per resolution | From $1.50 | Plans from $19/month, resolution allowance included | Additional resolution allowance purchased separately once included volume is used | 
| Salesforce Agentforce | Flex Credits per action | $500 per 100,000 credits ($0.10 per 20-credit action) | Requires existing Salesforce Service Cloud license | A separate 2026 industry comparison cites roughly $2 per conversation, though this figure is not confirmed as universal across all Agentforce editions | 
| Freshworks Freddy AI | Per AI session | First 500 sessions included, then variable by plan | Bundled into Freshdesk/Freshchat plans | Freshworks does not publish one single universal per-session rate for all business sizes | 
The gap between Intercom’s $0.99 and Zendesk’s $1.50 per-resolution floor looks small in isolation, but at the volume an Irish mid-market support desk handles, a $0.51 difference per resolved ticket compounds fast. A team resolving 10,000 tickets a month through the AI agent alone would see roughly $5,100 more in monthly AI fees on Zendesk’s published floor rate (the $1.50 committed resolution price, with pay-as-you-go resolutions at $2.00, as Sistava reported for May 2026) than on Intercom’s, before either side’s seat costs or allowances are factored in. Zendesk’s resolution allowance model can offset part of that gap for businesses that stay within their plan’s included volume, which is why the real comparison has to be run against a specific business’s actual ticket volume rather than the sticker price alone.
Salesforce Agentforce breaks the comparison in a different direction. Because Flex Credits are consumed per action rather than per resolved conversation, a single customer query that requires three CRM lookups and a record update burns more credits than a simple FAQ answer, even if both end in a successful outcome. That makes Agentforce’s effective cost per resolution highly dependent on how complex a business’s typical ticket is, not just on sticker price.

The real question for an Irish business is how many actions its average ticket actually consumes once Agentforce is measuring every discrete step rather than the finished conversation, and that number is specific to each company’s own workflow rather than something a vendor comparison page can answer in the abstract.
Resolution Rates and Accuracy: What the Benchmarks Actually Show
Vendor-reported resolution rates are the most-quoted number in this category and also the hardest to compare like for like. Intercom’s own customer-service comparison material cites a 76% average resolution rate across its customer base, and Intercom claims Fin averages that 76% across more than 12,000 customers. Intercom’s pages also carry customer-specific claims, such as a 50% resolution rate for one customer, which is a reminder that a vendor’s own figures describe different customers and are not directly comparable.
Zendesk takes a more cautious public stance on a single resolution-rate average. Its own ranking of 13 competing AI customer service agents markets AI agents as a category and lists plan-based resolution allowances rather than a single headline accuracy percentage for its own product, which makes a direct Zendesk-versus-Fin resolution-rate comparison difficult to do on published numbers alone.
Freshworks publishes an “up to 80%” query resolution figure, explicitly framed as a maximum achievable under ideal conditions, specifically when Freddy is searching a well-maintained, approved knowledge base. That framing matters: an 80% ceiling figure describes best-case performance, not an average a typical Irish support team should expect on day one of deployment.
Salesforce’s Agentforce material does not publish a directly comparable resolution-rate percentage at all in current vendor documentation, instead emphasising action volume and workflow completion. A third-party 2026 industry comparison cites an approximate $2-per-conversation cost for Agentforce, but that source does not establish an independent accuracy or resolution benchmark to set alongside it.
The honest conclusion across all four platforms: there is no single independently audited benchmark that scores Intercom Fin, Zendesk’s AI agents, Salesforce Agentforce and Freshworks Freddy AI against the same test set of tickets. Every number in this category currently comes from the vendor itself or from a third-party comparison that uses the vendor’s own reporting. Any Irish business evaluating these tools should ask each vendor for a pilot period against its own real ticket volume rather than trusting a single published percentage.
GDPR, EU AI Act and Data Residency for Irish Deployments
Every Irish business deploying an AI customer service agent is a data controller under GDPR for the personal data that flows through that agent, which usually includes customer names, order history, billing details and sometimes payment information. The Data Protection Commission remains the lead EU regulator for several of the larger US-headquartered vendors in this space, which gives Irish businesses more direct regulatory leverage than companies in most other EU member states.
Of the four platforms, Intercom publishes the clearest statement on this point: EU-hosted workspaces can run Fin with processing described as occurring within Europe, which simplifies the data processing agreement conversation for an Irish legal team. Zendesk offers enterprise-tier data residency options, though these typically sit behind higher-priced plans rather than the base Suite tier. Salesforce’s data residency for Agentforce inherits whatever region a business’s existing Salesforce org is already provisioned in, so a company already running an EU-region Salesforce instance carries that setup forward. Freshworks does not publish a specific EU or Irish data-residency region in current material available to this comparison, which means any business considering Freddy AI for regulated data should request written confirmation of processing location before signing.
There is also a newer regulatory layer to factor in. The EU AI Act’s transparency obligations for AI systems that interact directly with consumers became enforceable across the bloc, including Ireland, in August 2026. In practice, that means any of these four platforms deployed on an Irish-facing support channel needs to clearly disclose to the customer that they are talking to an AI agent, not a person, unless that is already obvious from context. None of the four vendors compared here are new to this requirement, but it is worth confirming during procurement that the disclosure language is configured correctly for the Irish market rather than left on a US-default setting.

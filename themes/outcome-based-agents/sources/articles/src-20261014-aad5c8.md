Customer Service AI Agents in 2026: Enterprise Examples and How to Measure Cost per Resolution&nbsp;&nbsp;InfotechLead

[page text]
Customer service AI agents are helping businesses process refunds, troubleshoot products and handle growing support volumes. However, faster responses and higher automation rates do not automatically establish lower service costs.
Disclosures from L’Oreal, Air India, Telstra, Fisher & Paykel and other businesses show measurable operational improvements. For CIOs and finance leaders, the next step is connecting those results to cost per successfully resolved customer issue.
The examples below combine 2026 case studies with earlier results available for comparison. Most disclose productivity or automation metrics rather than a fully loaded cost-per-resolution figure.
How to Calculate AI Customer Service Cost per Resolution
Blended cost per resolution = Total customer service operating costs ÷ Verified resolved customer issues
Costs should include human support, AI usage, software, infrastructure, integration, knowledge maintenance, monitoring and an allocated share of implementation expenditure.
Count each customer issue consistently across channels. An AI conversation followed by a human call about the same unresolved problem should not become two successful resolutions.
Track three measures separately:
AI-only cost per resolution: Costs attributable to issues completed without human intervention.
AI-assisted cost per resolution: AI and employee costs for issues resolved jointly.
Blended cost per resolution: Total service costs across the complete support operation.
These measures require consistent cost allocation and a defined period for checking reopened cases and repeat contacts.
1. L’Oreal: Case Handling Falls From Seven Minutes to 2.5 Minutes
A September 2026 Salesforce case study reports that L’Oreal reduced average case-handling time by 64 percent, from approximately seven minutes to 2.5 minutes.
Promotional-code generation fell from 10 minutes to one minute, while more than 300 advisers use Agentforce daily. Adoption reached 100 percent among advisers with access.
The L’Oreal AI service deployment assists employees with summaries, email drafts, intent validation and promotional codes. Advisers review customer communications.
The financial test is whether released employee capacity reduces spending or supports additional service output after technology costs. A 64 percent handling-time reduction does not establish a 64 percent cost reduction.
2. Air India: Internal Refund Processing Drops to Four Hours
Air India’s September 2026 Agentforce expansion reported a reduction in internal refund-processing time from approximately 14 days to four hours. Passenger name-correction turnaround fell from three days to 30 minutes.
Its customer service automation announcement also describes automated email handling for eligible cases when system confidence exceeds 95 percent, with human oversight where required.
Bank refund-processing time remains unchanged. Furthermore, the confidence threshold is an operating rule, not evidence of 95 percent answer accuracy.
Air India’s relevant financial measures include cost per completed refund, exception-handling effort and repeat inquiries after processing.
3. Fisher & Paykel: Approximately 80 Percent Self-Service Resolution
Fisher & Paykel’s September 2026 case study reports approximately 80 percent self-service resolution, compared with 30 percent for its earlier chatbot—an improvement of about 50 percentage points.
The deployment operates across six markets, and 45 percent of technician and service appointments are completed online without a contact-centre call.
The Fisher & Paykel self-service deployment guides customers through appliance troubleshooting and passes context to employees when escalation is necessary.
The cost assessment should distinguish remote troubleshooting from issues requiring technician visits. Avoiding a call is useful, but it does not necessarily eliminate the cost of repairing an appliance.
4. Telstra: Refund Automation Supports More Than 1,000 Representatives
Telstra’s September 2026 announcement describes AI agents supporting more than 1,000 contact-centre representatives.
An interim-service workflow validates eligibility, calculates compensation, monitors service restoration, applies refunds and updates customer records. Billing, payments and business-email triage are additional roadmap applications.
The announcement does not disclose a measured cost-per-resolution reduction. Telstra’s assessment on refund automation should compare processing effort, refund accuracy, correction costs and compliance performance before and after automation. Projected future benefits should remain separate from live deployment results.
5. HCLTech’s Healthcare Customer: 88 Percent Automation for Simple Requests
An August 2026 HCLTech case study describes contact-centre modernisation for an unnamed healthcare and medical technology business.
Reported outcomes include 88 percent automation for simple service requests, approximately 600 hours of operational savings between Q2 and Q4, and 35 percent lower manual operational effort.
These results cover different operational activities and do not establish the automation rate for every customer interaction. The customer is unnamed, and the reporting periods should not be assumed to represent 2026 outcomes simply because the case study was published that year.
6. Klarna: AI Handles 80 Percent of Customer Service Chats
Klarna’s AI assistant handled 80 percent of customer service chats during 2025 and had conducted 31 million conversations since launch.
This demonstrates deployment scale, but chat coverage is not equivalent to verified resolution across all support channels. Cost comparisons must include human escalations and distinguish customer-service expenditure from broader operations costs.
7. Intercom: Reports $7.5 Million–$9 Million in Annual Savings
In March 2026, Intercom reported that Fin resolved more than 81 percent of its own customer support volume.
The company said it absorbed a 300 percent-plus increase in demand since 2022 without proportional headcount growth. Its internal AI support case study estimates $7.5 million–$9 million in annual savings relative to hiring at least 100 additional support employees.
This is a company-reported avoided-hiring estimate, rather than a disclosed reduction of the same amount in historical payroll expenditure.
8. Lightspeed: Up to 65 Percent AI Resolution
Fin’s customer disclosures report 99 percent involvement and a 65 percent resolution rate for Lightspeed Commerce.
The distinction matters: an AI agent can participate in almost every conversation while resolving a smaller share. Financial analysis therefore needs the volume and cost of unresolved interactions, including human handoffs. These vendor-reported metrics do not provide a complete cost-per-resolution calculation.
Wells Fargo: Information retrieval falls from 10 minutes to 30 seconds
Wells Fargo’s AI agent integrated with Microsoft Teams supports 35,000 bankers across 4,000 branches. The agent helps employees find information about banking procedures and internal policies when assisting customers.
Microsoft reported that 75% of searches moved through the agent, while information retrieval time fell from 10 minutes to 30 seconds. Faster access to internal knowledge helped bankers respond to customer queries more quickly.
Bayer: Researchers save 3–6 hours per week
Bayer’s Copilot-based AI agent enables crop science researchers to retrieve information using natural-language questions. It helps scientists navigate research knowledge that previously required time-consuming searches.
Microsoft reported savings of 3–6 hours per researcher per week. The measured benefit is employee time recovered for research activities, rather than a disclosed financial saving or increase in product revenue.
Cineplex: Refund handling drops to around 30 seconds
Canadian entertainment company Cineplex’s Microsoft Copilot Studio with Power Automate is aimed at streamlining ticket refunds. Employees enter a booking ID and date, and the agent identifies the refund type and triggers the appropriate processes across backend systems.
Handling time fell from 5–15 minutes per request to less than one minute, typically 30 seconds. The agent processed more than 5,000 refund requests in five months, reducing employee handling time and back-office work. Cineplex’s wider automation programme saved over 30,000 hours annually, but that total covers multiple solutions.
Nsure.com: Manual processing time falls 60%, associated costs decline 50%
Digital insurance agency Nsure.com’s AI-powered agent using Microsoft Copilot Studio and Power Automate to support insurance workflows and customer interactions.
Microsoft reported a 60% reduction in manual processing time and a 50% reduction in associated costs. These figures relate to the affected workflows, rather than the company’s entire operating cost base, demonstrating measurable benefits from combining AI agents with process automation.
The Home Depot — Google Cloud: Customer phone support becomes four times faster
Home Depot’s AI voice agents built on Google Cloud’s Gemini Enterprise for Customer Experience. The agents help customers check product availability, track orders and initiate service requests, with access to human associates when needed.
In a 50-store pilot, the agents understood the reason for a customer’s call in 10 seconds and helped customers reach a solution four times faster than traditional phone menus. Store associates also reported higher job satisfaction and more time to assist in-store shoppers. These results relate to the pilot.
Ryanair — AWS: AI handles 120,000 daily chats
Ryanair’s customer-service AI assistant using Amazon Bedrock AgentCore and Amazon Nova 2 Lite to address questions about baggage, refunds, bookings and flight disruptions.
AWS reported that the assistant handled 120,000 customer chat interactions daily across seven languages, with an 80% containment rate, meaning those interactions did not require escalation to human agents. Response latency fell from 18 seconds to 2.9 seconds, an 84% improvement. The deployment increased automated support capacity as passenger volumes grew.
CrushBank — IBM: IT support resolution time declines about 25%
IT service technology company CrushBank uses IBM watsonx Orchestrate, watsonx.ai and watsonx.data to classify support tickets, assign priorities and route work to the appropriate engineers.
IBM reported a roughly 25% reduction in average time to resolution and a 20% increase in first-call resolution. Its agents retrieve relevant information and trigger support workflows, helping teams resolve problems faster and reduce repeat support interactions.
What a Credible Financial Improvement Looks Like
Consider a hypothetical business spending ₹12 lakh monthly to resolve 10,000 issues. Its cost per resolution is ₹120.
If AI reduces total monthly costs to ₹10 lakh, including technology and human support, while verified resolutions remain at 10,000, the figure falls to ₹100, a 16.7 percent improvement.
If only 8,000 issues are genuinely resolved, however, cost per resolution becomes ₹125.
Enterprise buyers should therefore evaluate verified resolutions, repeat contacts, customer satisfaction and total expenditure together. The strongest business case demonstrates lower cost for completed customer outcomes while maintaining service quality.

Duvo Raises $32M Series A Led by Balderton Capital, Appoints Michael Green and Spencer Insolia&nbsp;&nbsp;citybiz

[page text]
Duvo
, an enterprise artificial intelligence automation startup, has 
raised $32 million in a Series A financing
 round led by 
Balderton Capital
, bringing its total funding to $48 million.

Existing investors 
Index Ventures, Northzone and Credo Ventures
 also participated in the round.

The company plans to use the financing to expand its enterprise AI platform, initially targeting retailers and consumer packaged goods companies in the United States and other markets.

As part of its expansion, Duvo has appointed Michael Green as Chief Commercial Officer and Spencer Insolia as Managing Director, U.S.

Green joins from Databricks and previously served as a senior vice president at Salesforce. Insolia previously served as president of Kmart and managing director at Accenture.

The company has also appointed 
Christoph Schwaiger
, former CEO of ALDI SÜD Holding, as a senior advisor.

Founded in 2025 by a team associated with European online grocery company Rohlik Group, Duvo develops an enterprise operations platform designed to automate business processes across existing software systems.

The company describes its technology as an Enterprise Execution Operating System, combining AI agents, process discovery, workflow automation and human approval mechanisms within a single platform.

Duvo’s software connects enterprise resource planning systems, supplier portals, spreadsheets, email and legacy applications, allowing businesses to automate repetitive operational tasks without replacing their existing technology infrastructure.

The platform begins by identifying how employees currently perform tasks through AI-assisted interviews, screen recordings and existing business documentation.

Its Duvo Clarity technology uses that information to map operational workflows and identify opportunities for automation.

AI agents can then execute approved tasks through application programming interfaces or existing software interfaces, while employees retain responsibility for decisions requiring human judgment.

The company said this approach allows customers to deploy an initial automated process within weeks rather than undertaking extensive systems integration projects.

Duvo operates across business functions including finance, supply chain management, commercial operations, customer service, marketing and human resources.

According to the company, its technology has helped customers identify or recover more than $100 million in business value through operational improvements.

At Notino, a European online beauty retailer, Duvo said its platform reduced the time required for quarterly reconciliation of approximately $45 million in annual supplier bonuses from 70 days to 15 days.

The company also reported that specialist labor required for each reconciliation cycle declined from approximately 120 hours to fewer than 10 hours.

Duvo’s customer base includes Morrisons, Holland & Barrett, Boden, Notino and Allegro.

“Our business processes have evolved over decades, and Duvo gives us a way to understand them in detail, and quickly,” said 
Gordon MacPherson
, Group Productivity Director at Morrisons.

MacPherson said the platform helps the supermarket chain identify repetitive tasks that can be simplified, redesigned or automated based on operational evidence.

Tomáš Čupr
, co-founder and CEO of Duvo and founder and Group CEO of Rohlik Group, said the company’s approach focuses on increasing employee productivity rather than simply reducing headcount.

Čupr cited Rohlik’s supply chain operations as an example of how AI can expand the volume of work employees can manage.

He said planners previously reviewed approximately eight products at risk of shortages per day in each market, while automated systems can now monitor relevant signals continuously.

The company believes that extending automation across operational workflows can help enterprises capture business value that traditional processes leave unrealized.

The funding comes as businesses increase spending on automation software while facing challenges deploying AI agents at scale.

According to Gartner projections cited by Duvo, spending on business orchestration and automation technologies is expected to exceed $21 billion by 2029.

Gartner has also projected that more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 because of rising costs, uncertain business value or inadequate risk controls.

Duvo aims to address those challenges by integrating process discovery, automation, monitoring and governance within one operating environment.

The platform is designed to work with different AI models, allowing customers to use approved cloud-based systems, dedicated computing infrastructure or on-premises deployments.

Duvo said its platform holds SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and ISO 42001 certifications. U.S. customers can also purchase the software through Microsoft’s Azure Marketplace.

Rob Moffat, partner at Balderton Capital, said reliable enterprise-wide AI automation remains difficult because many organizations lack a clear understanding of how their operational processes function.

“Duvo’s unique ability is in how it uses AI to capture how work actually happens in the enterprise and what the causes of loss are,” Moffat said.

He added that the company’s combination of process discovery and automation was a central factor in Balderton’s investment.

Duvo is also working with Deloitte Central Europe to support enterprise transformation projects.

Jan Hejtmánek, partner and AI Institute Leader at Deloitte Central Europe, said the collaboration combines Deloitte’s consulting capabilities with Duvo’s process mapping and automation technology.

The partnership is intended to help enterprises move AI initiatives beyond pilot programs and into regular business operations.

Duvo’s initial U.S. expansion will focus on retail and consumer goods companies, particularly operational processes such as freight audits, invoice reconciliation and accounts payable reviews.

Insolia said his experience in retail operations and consulting highlighted the gap between identifying opportunities for improvement and implementing operational changes.

The company plans to apply its technology to additional industries as it expands its commercial operations.

Duvo currently employs nearly 60 people and intends to use the Series A proceeds to strengthen its product development, customer support and sales capabilities.

With $48 million raised to date, the company is positioning its platform as a centralized system for enterprise AI automation, combining existing business applications, AI agents and employee oversight to manage operational workflows at scale.

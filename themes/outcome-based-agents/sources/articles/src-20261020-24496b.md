Supply chains platforms are becoming increasingly effective in unlocking visibility, but BackOps is working to shift insights into valuable resolution
In the past few years, it has been critical for supply chains to detect disruption in advance. With ongoing volatility, businesses have been looking to develop resilience in a variety of ways – many of them turning to AI and other predictive supply chain platforms.
Despite this, teams are still spending significant time gathering evidence and coordinating solutions. 
In response, 
BackOps is scaling
 a resolution layer designed to extend workflow beyond predictions. 
Solving bottlenecks 
Global 
supply chains are a complex system
, with the movement of goods around the world and systems that rely on hundreds of people. Conditions change and legislation varies, resulting in the need to be agile and flexible. Teams need to have a coordination of plans and an ability to standardise and improve operations at any given point.
Issues include rising costs, customer demands, a growing skills gap and external pressures such as climate change and geopolitics makes it difficult to operate without disruption. 
The best that teams can do is to ensure visibility across the entire organisation. For this, many of them are turning towards AI tools.
AI can synthesise information, relate to the team and make decisions on the company’s behalf. However, the AI tool – when developed as a general-purpose tool – does not have full visibility into the supply chain process or the means to improve an organisation’s outcomes. Teams still need to have oversight, capturing and connecting the process, define requirements and rules and ensure the tool is continuously working. 
This results in an ongoing maintenance issue, slowed time to achieve meaningful value and a waste of resources. 
To prevent this, BackOps has developed Relay, an AI-native operations platform which intimately learns internal processes.
“When Sean and I started building BackOps, we weren’t trying to add another dashboard to supply chain software. We wanted to solve a deeper problem,” Henry Ou, Co-Founder and CTO of BackOps says on LinkedIn.
Henry Ou, Co-Founder and CTO of BackOps
“Most supply chain tools are built to show you what’s wrong — delayed shipments, missing updates, unfiled claims. But the real work still falls to human operators who have to chase emails, call warehouses, file paperwork, and keep systems updated across dozens of tools. That’s not scalable, and it wasn’t keeping up with the volume of demand. So we built BackOps to do the work.”
At a glance
With BackOps, one customer has processed more than 500,000 claims, growing its monthly volume 150x
For another customer, BackOps reduced the average billing cycle from 28 hours to 14 minutes
BackOps has processed more than US$136m in claims in the last 10 months
Almost US$12bn in shipment value has been processed by BackOps in this period
With BackOps, teams can build 4-6x faster
Supply chain capabilities 
In September 2026, BackOps announced a US$42m Series B, led by Insight Partners, with continued support from Theory Ventures, Construct Capital, Gradient Ventures and 10VC. 
The platform identified what it has called the ‘Resolution Gap’ – the space between identifying what needs to happen and achieving that outcome. Historically, this has been closed manually, but BackOps is working to change this with AI.
The recent funding allows the company to 
expand its product and engineering teams
, better supporting the enterprises relying on BackOps. Through this, it is building a system where every resolution in the system increases the understanding of the business, developing a powerful intelligence layer. 
The announcement of its Series B funding came alongside a name change. The brand moved from BackOps AI, to BackOps, representing a shift in what the company is focusing on.
“
AI is still at the center
 of everything we do, but we feel BackOps better reflects who we are, how our customers already know us and where we are headed as a company,” explains Sean McCarthy, Co-Founder and CEO at BackOps on LinkedIn.
Sean McCarthy, Co-Founder and CEO at BackOps
“We’ve been AI-native from day one, but what excites us most is the intelligence we’re building as BackOps gets deployed across more workflows, companies and eventually entire ecosystems. Every workflow creates more context and data, and as our distribution grows, so does the intelligence behind the platform.”
The Relay solution
BackOps, therefore, is distanced from control towers and copilots, as it has wider scope for controls and forecasting. Each deployment runs on two connected engines - one that learns how the operations work and another which 
executes the work autonomously
. 
Relay builds a complete model of how operations work, analysing ticket history, recording workflows and unveils processes that have previously been overlooked. 
It turns this surfaced information into a prioritised automation plan and is trained on the company’s SOPs, exception patterns and approval rules. As a result, it handles work from start to finish, adapting to and absorbing new information from all types of workflow, such as SMS, email, Slack and Teams. 
While Relay makes decisions and oversees the workflow autonomously, there is still a human-in-the-loop system. Leaders can decide where it runs on its own and where a human still has the final say, meaning that full oversight remains. 
BackOps is in place to close the resolution gap, with growing capabilities to continuously improve. 
Every workflow creates more context and data, and as our distribution grows, so does the intelligence behind the platform.
Sean McCarthy, Co-Founder and CEO of BackOps

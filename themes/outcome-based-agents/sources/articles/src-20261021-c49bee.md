AI Startups Are Betting On What The Big Platforms Cannot Credibly Sell. By Güney Yıldız, Contributor. Oct 21, 2026.

In the space of two weeks, OpenAI, Google and Workday each made a pitch to corporate buyers bidding to become the control point where company AI agents are chosen, governed and paid for. None of the three is eager to answer who pays when an agent errs, a question where vendors still park the liability with a human in the loop. Yet their expansion leaves every independent AI startup facing an equally blunt question: what do you sell that the platform cannot absorb on its next roadmap?

The deal numbers back his reading. Venture-backed AI companies bought 195 other AI startups through September 29, more than in all of 2025, according to Crunchbase. OpenAI led with 10 acquisitions this year, while Anthropic made five.

Domain depth and workflow QA. May Habib, CEO of Writer, argued that horizontal platforms underestimate how brittle enterprise workflows are. "The harness decides how the model works," she said. "And if you don't have a harness that is really domain specific, you are going to reteach AI constantly." ... "The value is in the workflow and the QA of the workflow," Habib said. Google's own Gemini Enterprise launch concedes part of that ground: its legal workflow version remains in preview and relies on startups Harvey and Onit as partners.

Even the strongest independent startups cannot avoid the platforms' gravity. ... OpenAI names Harvey, Sierra and Decagon among its Frontier partners and lets enterprise customers draw down their OpenAI commitment on 32 partner applications through its new marketplace.

Meanwhile, the giants are hiring the human expertise that once protected startups. AWS committed $1 billion to embedding thousands of forward-deployed engineers with customers, and OpenAI dispatches its own forward-deployed engineers into Frontier accounts.

That leaves buyers and founders with a clear test: does a startup sell a feature or a position? Basic connectors, cost dashboards and single-session memory are features. They already sit on Google's and Microsoft's roadmaps, and selling one means fighting a price war against the company that owns the cloud bill. Portable cross-model memory, multi-cloud neutrality, domain-specific workflow QA and operator-built decision logic are positions. Google cannot credibly promise neutrality about Google, and Anthropic cannot be the neutral custodian of memory a customer wants to take to OpenAI.

Positions erode too. Governance across HR and finance looked like natural ground for an independent startup until Workday made its own permissions and audit trail mandatory for any agent touching its records. Neutrality can also be bought: Opaque pitched confidential, sovereign AI across clouds right up until its team joined Anthropic last week, leaving enterprise buyers to ask what happens to an independent trust layer when one lab absorbs it.

The evidence from Amsterdam points to a squeeze rather than an extinction.

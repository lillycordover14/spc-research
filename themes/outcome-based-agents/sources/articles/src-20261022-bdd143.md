Saviynt partners with Saint Fox to expand identity security business in India

The partnership will target banking, healthcare, manufacturing and technology enterprises, combining Saviynt’s identity security platform with Saint Fox’s local cybersecurity delivery capabilities.

Saviynt has partnered with cybersecurity solutions provider Saint Fox to expand its go-to-market strategy and delivery capabilities in India, as the identity security company looks to strengthen its presence in the country through a partner-led approach.

The partnership will bring together Saviynt’s cloud-native identity security platform and Saint Fox’s cybersecurity expertise to address enterprise requirements across identity governance, privileged access and Zero Trust security.

The companies will focus on banking, healthcare, manufacturing and technology, with the partnership intended to expand Saviynt’s regional reach and delivery capacity.

The combined offering will cover Identity Governance and Administration (IGA), Privileged Access Management (PAM), Just-in-Time Access (JITA) and Identity Security Posture Management (ISPM).

For Saviynt, the agreement forms part of its broader effort to build a partner ecosystem in India. The company says it is prioritising partner enablement, loyalty and profitability as it develops its go-to-market strategy.

“Saint Fox is not just a new partner, we see them as a force multiplier,” said Nitin Varma, Managing Director, India and SAARC at Saviynt.

“As we shape our India GTM strategy, building the right ecosystem becomes imperative. We are investing in a scalable, trusted partner ecosystem built on the foundational pillars of enablement, loyalty, and profitability,” Varma said.

He added that the companies would work together to help enterprises rethink how they manage privilege, access and trust around identities rather than relying on static security perimeters.

Identity security moves beyond conventional access management

The partnership comes as enterprises manage identities and access across increasingly complex IT environments.

Hybrid infrastructure, changing workforce requirements and compliance obligations make it important for organisations to govern who can access systems, what permissions they hold and how those permissions change over time.

Saviynt and Saint Fox will combine four identity security capabilities within their joint offering.

IGA helps organisations manage digital identities, access rights and compliance throughout the user lifecycle. PAM focuses on protecting privileged accounts and controlling access to sensitive systems.

JITA enables temporary elevated access when required, while ISPM helps organisations identify and address risks associated with identities and their permissions.

The companies say the combined capabilities will support industry-specific compliance requirements, improve identity operations and help enterprises adopt Zero Trust principles.

Zero Trust security operates on the principle that access should be verified according to identity, context and permissions rather than automatically trusted because a user or system is inside a corporate network.

However, the announcement does not identify specific customer deployments, technical integrations or implementation timelines for the combined offering.

Saint Fox to support regional reach and delivery

Saint Fox will play a role in expanding Saviynt’s reach and delivery capacity in India. The companies have not disclosed the precise geographic coverage planned under the agreement or whether Saint Fox will recruit additional resellers, systems integrators or managed service providers to support expansion.

Sunil Tak, co-founder and CEO of Saint Fox, said enterprises need to look beyond conventional access management tools as identity-related threats and compliance requirements evolve.

“Further, a strategy that relies only on MFA or SSO, i.e., ‘Access Management’, is no longer enough, as the burden of compliance and reporting continues to increase,” Tak said.

He described Saviynt as a cloud-native, identity-centric platform that brings IGA, PAM, JITA and ISPM requirements together.

The partnership will target organisations across banking, healthcare, manufacturing and technology. These sectors are among those identified by the companies for their joint go-to-market efforts, although no named customers or sector-specific projects have been announced.

Partner economics remain a key question

Saviynt has identified enablement, loyalty and profitability as the foundational pillars of its partner ecosystem in India. The announcement, however, does not detail how those priorities will translate into commercial benefits for Saint Fox or other potential partners.

The companies have not disclosed revenue targets, investment commitments, partner margins, incentives, deal-registration arrangements or training and certification plans. They have also not specified whether the agreement is exclusive.

The extent of the opportunity for other channel partners will depend on how the company develops its wider ecosystem and translates its stated partner priorities into concrete programmes.

For now, the agreement establishes a joint go-to-market direction focused on identity security, compliance and Zero Trust adoption among Indian enterprises.

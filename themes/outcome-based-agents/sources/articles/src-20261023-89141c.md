Visa’s AI Agents Can Now Pay Business Invoices&nbsp;&nbsp;AIM Media House

[page text]
Visa announced three capabilities across commercial card growth, virtual card security, and agentic commerce on October 21, 2026, designed to help financial institutions, fintechs, and businesses automate payment processes while maintaining "the trust, visibility and control those transactions require."
The most direct move into AI agents is a live pilot with BILL and Billtrust that enables trusted AI agents to initiate and complete approved business invoice payments through supplier portals. The pilot is processing end-to-end transactions with participating BILL customers and Billtrust suppliers.
"Businesses are moving quickly to automate the work around payments, from identifying supplier opportunities to approving invoices and executing transactions," said Darren Parslow, global head of Visa Commercial Solutions. 
He said the capabilities are designed to help businesses do that "whether they are growing commercial card acceptance, helping protect virtual-card payments from fraud, or testing how trusted AI agents can act on their behalf."
The pilot draws on Visa Intelligent Commerce and Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol to establish the agent's identity, confirm that it is authorized to act, and ensure each transaction follows business-defined controls. 
Instead of relying on shared supplier-portal login credentials, it uses tokenized payment credentials and connects buyer-side invoice approval with supplier-side payment acceptance., according to the company
Visa said the setup gives businesses visibility into who is acting, what the agent is permitted to do, and which transactions have been approved.
SESA 2.0, the next generation of Visa's Supplier Enablement Spend Analytics, combines AI-driven supplier scoring, Visa commercial payments data, buyer benchmarks, and business-value modeling. 
According to the company, it helps financial institutions identify suppliers that may be more likely to accept commercial card payments and estimate potential commercial card growth. 
Since its launch, SESA has helped financial institutions uncover more than $600 billion in potential cardable spend, Visa said.
Visa Commercial Fraud Solutions covers three stages of a commercial payment. Supplier Validation helps verify payments reach the intended supplier. Virtual Authentication verifies commercial virtual card users through 3-D Secure authentication. Payment Controls let businesses set transaction-level limits on spend, merchant category, location, and timing.
Payment Controls are available now to eligible issuing financial institutions in supported countries and territories. Supplier Validation and Virtual Authentication are in pilot and will be generally available in 2027.
Visa cited Juniper Research projections that global B2B virtual card payment volume will reach $14.6 trillion by 2029, representing 83% of the global virtual card market. 
As adoption grows, Visa said, organizations face increasing pressure to verify suppliers, authenticate users, and ensure payments are made as intended.

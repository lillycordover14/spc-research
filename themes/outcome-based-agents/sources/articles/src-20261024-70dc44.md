Seaholm Caps Tennis Finals with D2 Repeat, Crosby Concludes Perfect Run at No. 1&nbsp;&nbsp;Michigan High School Athletic Association

[page text]
MIDLAND – By the time the sun dented the dense fog at the Midland Tennis Center on Saturday, Birmingham Seaholm had accumulated enough points to repeat as MHSAA Lower Division 2 tennis champion.

Seaholm finished the two-day tournament with 35, followed by Midland Dow with 25 and Grand Rapids Forest Hills Northern at 24.

The Maples were in a three-way battle for the top spot heading into the flight semifinals, leading with 24 points, followed by Forest Hills Northern with 21 and Dow with 20. 

And all three teams were familiar with hoisting the trophy. FHN has won three of the last seven Division 2 team titles, its most recent in 2024, and Dow’s championship in 2023 was the most recent of its eight total since 2009. 

“We try to prepare ourselves for anything that we’ll face here,’’ said Seaholm’s Carter Griffith, the team’s No. 1 singles player. “When everyone is gunning for us, we rise to that occasion. We focus on ourselves and not the outside noise.”

Said senior Kalil Wahab, who paired with freshman Hunter Helfman at No. 1 doubles: “Every team we went out and played, especially in the regular season, really went after us. You could tell their level rose when they were playing us. No matter what team we played, rather they were in our division or not.’’

Wahab and Helfman clinched their flight title with a 7-6 (7-1), 6-4 victory over Byron Center’s Brayden and Brody Slot. Seaholm’s victory lap started at No. 4 doubles with Deven Badhwar and Ryan McArdle defeating Byron Center’s Colten King and Jonah Wiechertjes, 6-0, 6-3.

“The start of the season was tough for us to get the spot,’’ said Badhwar. “We’ve been playing together since we were 8. I’m glad we could do it as seniors. We got what we wanted.’’

Said McArdle: “There’s no one else I’d rather win with. We had chemistry from the start.’’

Seaholm’s No. 2 doubles pair of Arhen Gupta and Kyle Car defeated Dow’s top-seeded Ethan Clark and Jashwanth Thamminana 4-6, 6-2, 7-5 to claim that flight. The Maples also captured No. 4 singles with freshman Luke Janz downing Jayden Qi of Forest Hills Northern, 6-4, 7-5. Carter Griffith’s brother, Charlie Griffith, captured No. 3 singles with a 6-2, 5-7, 6-2 victory over Dow’s Raymond Chai.

Dow claimed the No. 3 doubles title, with Vettel Xu and Zander Salisbury besting Davis Gryzenia and
 
Weston Jerabek of Grosse Pointe South, 7-5, 6-2.

South Lyon East senior Mason Crosby clinched No. 1 singles against Carter Griffith with a 6-1, 6-2 victory. He had finished No. 1 singles runner-up last fall, and his four victories this weekend advanced his record to 30-0 to finish the season.

“I thought I had control of the match; it got a little tight at the end,’’ said Crosby. “I was doing whatever it took. If I had to come to the net, I would. If I had to sit back and grind it, I did that.’’

Forest Hills Northern freshman Andrew Yang won No. 2 singles, 6-2, 6-2 over top-seeded Chase Martin of Dow.

HOLLAND – It was a bittersweet moment for Holland Christian seniors Michael Gorno and Graham Tanis last weekend as they walked off the court at Midland Tennis Center for the final time.

A straight-set loss at No. 1 doubles against Detroit Country Day in the semifinals of the Lower Peninsula Division 3 Finals marked the end of their high school careers together. 

And while the finality of it all was unsettling, the pair took solace in the bond that was formed and the success garnered from playing together and racking up more than 100 victories. 

“It’s been a tremendous gift to play with Graham over the last four years,” Gorno said. “We’ve had some amazing success, and although it wasn’t the ending we were hoping for, I wouldn’t trade these last four years with Graham for the world.” 

Although a Finals title would’ve been the ideal close to their banner careers, Tanis also appreciated the time spent with his close friend. 

“At the moment we were both really bummed,” Tanis said. “We gave it our all and we left it all out there, but I think we both know we could’ve played a touch better. But stepping back, I’m so thankful for the four years, especially playing with Michael. I’m sad we are done, but our friendship is still a big part of our lives.”

Gorno and Tanis, who went an astounding 106-6 over their four years together, never imagined that this would be the path they would follow. 

As freshmen, neither even considered doubles. They wanted to play singles. 

“Both of us tried out for singles originally as freshmen, and both of us were very successful, but our coach ended up putting us down to (play) doubles together,” Gorno said. “It seemed really weird at the start because neither of us were looking to play doubles or expecting to play doubles, but it couldn’t have worked out better for both of us.” 

Said Tanis: “I did want to play singles, and that was my goal since middle school, but I played with Michael that first year and I really enjoyed doubles, especially playing with him. We had a great time. I know coach wanted us to make the sacrifice for the betterment of the team.” 

Holland Christian tennis coach Steve Gorno – Michael’s father – saw early on that the pairing had the potential for success. 

“From the first time I paired Michael and Graham together, their games and their on-court personalities complemented each other,” Steve Gorno said. “Michael brought consistency, intensity and an analytical approach that kept them focused, while Graham brought athleticism, brilliant shot-making and a fun-loving personality that kept them loose. 

“That chemistry made them one of the most dominant doubles combinations in recent memory. There have been better players in doubles over the years, but there have been very few doubles players (better) than those two.”

The learning process didn’t take long.

Michael Gorno and Tanis developed quick chemistry on the court and eventually emerged as a dominating force that few teams could overcome.

As freshmen, playing at No. 3 doubles, they finished 29-2 and advanced to the Division 4 semifinals at their flight.

The following year, they went 31-2 and advanced even farther, winning a Finals championship at No. 3 while also helping the Maroons to a Division 4 team title.

“We found a lot of raw success and raw chemistry together, which was really fun for us to see,” Michael Gorno said. “Especially since (we’ve been) good friends outside of tennis and close friends all through high school. To come down and try something new and find that immediate success was definitely really fun.

“After making it to the semifinals as freshmen, we saw that we still had a lot of work to do to make that transfer from good to great, and we both put in an absurd amount of work in the offseason. Coming into that sophomore season, we both came in with the goal of a state championship in mind and we executed it very well, I would say.” 

Tanis said the thrill of winning a Finals championship with his team and his good friend was the highlight of his career.

“It was really fun, and what a blessing to be in that position,” he said. “To have that opportunity and then pull through and win was pretty cool.

“We both just really love playing tennis and have great chemistry. We played really well when we were just having fun out there. He has a great forehand, I have a good serve, and we both are good at the net. Pair that together with the great coaching we had, and it brought out the best in us.” 

Holland Christian moved to Division 3 two years ago, but the success didn’t stop as the standouts also shifted to No. 1 doubles. 

Michael Gorno and Tanis were unbeaten entering the Finals as juniors, but were upset in the semifinals. 

This past season, Gorno was slated to play No. 1 singles but suffered a tear in his hip flexor during his second match.

“I was out a couple weeks, and we decided it was safer to run back doubles with Graham again,” Gorno said. “I love playing with him, and there’s no one else I would want to play with on the court. Obviously, our aim was for a No. 1 doubles title.

“We were the one seed our junior year and came up short, and this year undefeated, too. Unfortunately, we fell just short of that, but after the match I think we were more sad that our run of two almost-undefeated seasons was over.”

The duo will go down as the winningest doubles tandem in MHSAA history.

“Just an amazing accomplishment, and having 106 wins together and only six losses is an amazing record for both of us to think about,” Michael Gorno said. 

The Maroons finished third as a team for the second straight year at this season’s Division 3 Finals behind champion Detroit Country Day and runner-up Bloomfield Hills Cranbrook Kingswood. 

Holland Christian seniors Jonathan Haig and Carter Leep won their flight at No. 3 doubles despite entering the Finals as the fourth seed. 

“While Graham and I were upset by how we did, it was so fun to watch those guys win that match,” Michael Gorno said. “Coming back from a set down and watching them lock back in and win that match … they both deserved it so much. Jon put in so much work over this season, and Carter's serves were some of the best I’ve seen. It was truly an amazing accomplishment for both of them.”

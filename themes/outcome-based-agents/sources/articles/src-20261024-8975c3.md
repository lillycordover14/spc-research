The 1971 album David Crosby, Carole King, and Elvis Costello called the greatest
 
 
 
 
Tim Coffman
 
 
 
October 24th, 2026 · 4:00pm BST
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Follow us on Google Discover
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
There’s no real way for 
any great songwriter
 to find the perfect formula for any of their tunes. 
The greatest writers of all time are usually the ones who change things up, and there is no shortage of bands that are considered the greatest of all time that don’t feel the need to make something a bit outside the box whenever they work on any number of their tunes. But there are also going to be a few instances where even the legends find something new when listening to their contemporaries.
Even though 
David Crosby had been around the block
 a couple of times in terms of guitar playing by the time he left The Byrds, he never wanted to stop learning. Some of the greatest musicians that he ever heard were the ones who still tried their best to make something new, and he was going to do the same thing when interpreting his favourite jazz licks whenever he played.
But there was something else going on in the Laurel Canyon rock and roll scene. So many people had been showing the world what great songs could sound like on only an acoustic guitar, and when Joni Mitchell stepped out of the shadows and showed everyone the kind of musical dynamo that she was outside of the folk circles, Crosby was determined to turn her into the biggest rock and roll star that anyone had ever seen.
He could see that she was about to turn into one of the foundational artists of his time, and he could only sit and watch as 
Blue 
eventually took over the world, 
saying
, “Bob Dylan’s as good a poet as Joni, but nowhere near as good a musician. Paul Simon and James Taylor made some stunners – but for me, Blue is the best singer-songwriter album. Picking a song from it is like choosing between your children. Can you imagine a better song than ‘A Case of You’? She was so brilliant as a songwriter, it crushed me. But she gives us all something to strive for.”
This was songwriting at its most excellent, but there was still so much more to dissect on the other side of the musical spectrum. Mitchell had stopped writing music for the charts, and while she did end up writing the best songs that she could for herself, Carole King was knocked out over the fact that someone could have made something that sounded so perfect when they sang.
Tapestry 
was 
slowly coming into view
, but no one seemed to be on Mitchell’s level in her mind, saying, “When the album was released in 1971, I was blown away by Joni’s open guitar tunings, unpredictable chord changes, and amazing vocal chops that allowed her to move effortlessly from warm, rich low notes to bell-like high notes and back again. I loved the simplicity of her rhythmic accompaniment on piano, guitar, or dulcimer.”

In 1962, Ringo Starr played one song. Paul McCartney called it the real beginning of The Beatles
In 1985, Phil Collins released his biggest-selling album. He later called it his least favourite
The 1968 album Cat Stevens hailed as a thing of “perfect beauty”
But as the punk movement was coming into view, no one wanted to hear music that sounded like this. They needed something more aggressive, but Elvis Costello wasn’t about to shun an album that sounded this beautiful, 
saying
, “The stories are very personal or seem very personal. I don’t think it’s very important that we know that those things happened. What’s important is that we think they’re true, and 
Blue 
is a great example of that.”
Then again, that’s the mark of what any great songwriter hopes to do. No one is able to claim ownership of their songs once they’ve given them out to the world, and the only thing that any of them can hope to do is make sure that their audience finds a piece of themselves in their music and use that to help make the world a better place.

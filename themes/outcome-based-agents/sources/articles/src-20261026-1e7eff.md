PREVIEW: Venice meets Naples as Crescendo ignites ‘Galant Fire,’ Oct. 31 and Nov. 1

From operatic drama to instrumental virtuosity, the program explores the contrasting musical traditions of 18th-century Italy and the emergence of the galant style.

Written by
 
David Noel Edwards

October 26, 2026
 

Venice’s Church of San Giorgio Maggiore. Courtesy of Crescendo.

Great Barrington —
 Led by founding artistic director Christine Gevert, the Crescendo Chorus and Period String Ensemble will present “Galant Fire — Masterpieces of Venice and Naples,” featuring sacred vocal and instrumental works by Antonio Vivaldi, Alessandro Scarlatti, Baldassare Galuppi, Leonardo Leo, Antonio Lotti, Antonio Caldara, and Francesco Durante. Performances are Saturday, October 31, at 4 p.m. at Saint James Place in Great Barrington, and Sunday, November 1, at 4 p.m. at Trinity Church in Lakeville, Conn.

The concerts bring together the Crescendo Chorus and Period String Ensemble, led by concertmaster 
Jeremy Zhang-Rhizor
, with soprano 
Caroline Christopher
, alto Laura Evans, tenor 
Kevin Ray
, and bass-baritone 
Douglas R. Williams
.

The program explores the interplay between sacred and operatic music as Italian composers moved from the intricate polyphony of the High Baroque toward the more melodically direct 
galant style
. Although Venice and Naples were both major centers of musical innovation, their composers developed distinctive approaches to vocal expression, instrumental writing, and the relationship between sacred music and opera.

The Neapolitan selections highlight the growing influence of opera on sacred and instrumental music. Leonardo Leo’s compositions combine contrapuntal sophistication with operatic drama, while Francesco Durante’s string concerto reflects the transition from traditional polyphony toward the emerging galant style. The program also features a newly adapted sacred version of a bass aria from Alessandro Scarlatti’s serenata “La gloria di primavera,” arranged for bass-baritone Douglas R. Williams.

The Venetian works emphasize instrumental virtuosity, elaborate counterpoint, and lyrical expression. Antonio Vivaldi’s sacred compositions include the solo motet “In furore iustissimae irae” and his rediscovered “
Laetatus sum
.” Baldassare Galuppi’s “Dixit Dominus” combines contrapuntal writing with the graceful melodic qualities associated with the galant style. The program also includes sacred music by Antonio Lotti and instrumental works by Antonio Caldara.

Together, the selections reveal how Italian composers balanced established sacred traditions with innovations in melody, harmony, and instrumental expression. They also demonstrate how the dramatic language of opera increasingly influenced religious music, even as composers continued to draw on centuries-old contrapuntal practices.

Christine Gevert, a Chilean-German conductor, organist, and harpsichordist, holds degrees in organ and early music performance from Hamburg’s Hochschule für Musik und Theater and in music theory from Chile’s Conservatorio Nacional. She studied conducting in Berlin and harpsichord in London. Gevert has taught at institutions in Germany and Chile and performed with numerous European and South American ensembles and orchestras. As founder and artistic director of Crescendo, she has led its chorus and period instrument orchestra in several East Coast and U.S. premieres of Baroque works. Crescendo received the 2014 Chorus America/ASCAP/Alice Parker Award. Gevert is also music director at Trinity Church in Lime Rock, Conn.

Based in Lakeville, Conn., Crescendo is a regional choral and instrumental organization that presents programs ranging from Renaissance and Baroque music to contemporary and Latin American works, often performed on period instruments. Its concerts take place primarily in northwestern Connecticut and the Berkshires.

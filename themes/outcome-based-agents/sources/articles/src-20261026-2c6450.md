LAWRENCE CO., Ala. (
WAFF
) - Almost a year in to the position, Lawrence Co. EMA Director Steven Carlton has upgraded the department with new additions.
With a mild storm season this year, emergency management officials say they’ve had some time to focus more on training and upgrading departments.
The Lawrence County EMA Director, Steven Carlton, has hit the ground running on gathering supplies for the community. 
Carlton has a background as a first responder. He started as a firefighter and later became a deputy.
He says that when he took office, he decided to ask first responders what they needed. He says it’s important to help departments so that they can in turn help the community. 
And he has a great working relationship with the fire departments and law enforcement agencies. They all work together to help the community.
“I just want to do my job, help the community,” Carlton said. “Try to provide them the resources that otherwise they wouldn’t have the means to afford and utilize.”
So far, they’ve added a Starlink satellite internet for remote areas such as Bankhead National Forest. As well as a ranger for the exact same reason.
The agency also put up severe weather cameras in Speake, Mount Hope, and Hatton. And speaking of storms, the department is going around and looking at how to improve the storm shelters in the county.
And they’ve bought a boat for river emergencies, so they don’t have to wait on neighboring departments for help. 
“There was a time you’d be waiting 45 minutes to an hour for somebody to go get the boat wherever it was, bring it to the nearest boat ramp, put it in the water,” Carlton explained. “And you know, in a water rescue, you’ve got someone that has fallen out of a boat, that’s precious moments that can turn between life and death.”
Now it only takes 10 to 15 minutes to get a crew on the river.
Carlton says he is thankful it’s been a mild storm season so that he’s been able to focus on what he needs to to be proactive instead of reactive.
And as we head into winter, he says there’s not much to prepare for other than start communicating with state officials about plans for roadways.

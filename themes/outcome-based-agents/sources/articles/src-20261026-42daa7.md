This is not the tortoise and the hare. The Cyclones cannot continue to crawl in the first half, go down a couple scores and pray the other team falls asleep.

That has been the story so far in conference play, and if it continues, there will not be much change to the win column. The Cyclones went down 24-3 at the half to Utah, 14-3 at the half to BYU and now 25-3 at the half to Arizona. The one time they led 21-14 at the half against West Virginia, they went on to win.

The first drives of games are usually scripted by teams coming in. It seems like those scripted plays are always working for teams playing against Iowa State. Meanwhile, offensively, the Cyclones are dishing out three-and-outs like Halloween candy. Something has to change in the way Iowa State approaches the first few drives of the game.

Iowa State is in an interesting place at this point in the season. The team is 3-4, but they have lost to four teams that came in as heavy favorites. That will not change this week against one of the nation’s best offenses in Oklahoma State.

The offense has not been cutting it, there is no way around that. There are a million articles out there about how they need to pick up their slack. The coaching staff tried to bring 
Connor Moberly
 in for a second half spark, and the Cyclones went from a three-point first half to being blanked in the second. Both quarterbacks were not close to the standard, but they are just the face of an offense overall that is not doing its job. Plenty of blame can be handed to plenty of guys.

What I’m here to talk about is that the defense has not gotten it done either since conference play began. Going into the year, most people had some concerns about the offense consistently moving the ball. It has been worse than a lot of people expected, but the expectations were not that high. Where the expectations were high was the defense. People, including me, thought this team could go out there and win some Big 12 football games with scores in the low 20s. Sure, the defense has had some solid second halves, but the offense is not good enough to make up for giving up 15 points over the first two drives like what happened against Arizona.

The only group on the defensive side of the ball that has played up to my standard for them is the linebackers. That core is made up of some excellent tacklers. The defensive line has its moments, but does not get enough consistent pressure to shut down offenses. The secondary worried me since before the season started. The Cyclones were burned on the deep passes a couple of times against the low-level non-conference opponents that created some worry for what was to come when hit with a stretch of elite quarterback play. That elite quarterback play arrived, and the Cyclones did not patch up that leak. On the second play of the game against Arizona, 
Noah Fifita 
found a wide open man down the sidelines for a touchdown. Plays like that get the home crowd bumping, which leads to three-and-outs on offense and a Wildcat crew playing their hearts out.

To say there is a single game the Cyclones could have won that they didn’t because of the defense’s play is probably a reach. To say the defense needs to play better is a fact. I personally find it hard to put the majority of the blame on a side of the ball that I expected to perform poorly over a side of the ball I expected to perform well that is not living up to it.

Now, onto another complaint. This year’s schedule had to be scientifically created in a lab to make fans upset. When you go on a stretch where four of your five conference games are against top-five Big 12 opponents, a team with a new coach and low expectations are going to come out looking rough. Fans need to be patient, and if Iowa State is outmatched over the final four very winnable games after Oklahoma State, let the arguments begin.

Humanist Secures Workplace Accommodations from Personal AI Use on the Basis of His Beliefs&nbsp;&nbsp;American Humanist Association

[page text]
WASHINGTON – Citing his beliefs as a Humanist, a research administrator employed by the University of Kentucky, David Durman, requested and successfully obtained workplace accommodations excusing him from personally using AI in his job responsibilities. 

With the support of the American Humanist Association, Durman sent a letter to the University’s Office of Equal Opportunity (OEO) in late August requesting reasonable accommodations on the basis of his “sincerely held beliefs as a Humanist.” After meeting with the OEO in mid September, Durman’s request was approved on September 25, when the OEO determined it posed no undue burden on his department. Durman continues to perform all of his job duties without using AI.

“As a Humanist, promoting the wellbeing and flourishing of humanity is paramount to how I live my life, and I believe that our capacity to think and reason for ourselves is worth protecting,” said 
David Durman
. “When it comes to AI technology — particularly generative AI and LLMs, it’s clear to me that the existence and use of these technologies run contrary to human wellbeing. AI is interfering with people’s ability to earn a living, the environmental costs spell disaster for humanity and our planet, and its supplanting and theft of human creativity threaten elements of our culture like art and literature that make society flourish. I sought out workplace accommodations because using AI violates my deeply held Humanist beliefs which demand I take personal responsibility for the morality of my actions. I cannot, in good conscience, partake in the use and proliferation of this technology.”

“Mr. Durman’s Humanist beliefs are sincerely held, and his employer was right to grant him an accommodation on those beliefs,” said 
Alexandria Huiskens, Staff Attorney at the American Humanist Association
. “Comprehensive non-theistic belief systems like Humanism are entitled to the same religious protection under Title VII and the United States Constitution as ‘traditional’ religions, and it is a reflection of our country’s deep, longstanding commitment to religious freedom for all that non-theists like Mr. Durman are entitled to these same protections and freedom of conscience.”  

The AHA helped draft and is a signatory to the 
Luxembourg Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Human Values
, which holds that AI must never displace human judgment, reason or responsibility, and raises concerns about AI’s effects on workers, creators and the environment. Humanists hold a range of views on how to act on those concerns, and AHA defends each person’s right to follow their conscience.

					The American Humanist Association				

The American Humanist Association (AHA) works to protect the rights of Humanists, atheists, and other nontheistic Americans. The AHA advances the ethical and life-affirming worldview of Humanism, which—without beliefs in gods or other supernatural forces—encourages individuals to live informed and meaningful lives that aspire to the greater good of humanity.

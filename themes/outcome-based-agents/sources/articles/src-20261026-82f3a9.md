With a life rich in faith, family, and love, Kenneth Dahnke never took a blessing for granted. He constantly thought of others before himself, and his generosity has left an unforgettable impact on many in his family, his church, and his community. There was nothing that brought Ken greater joy than being surrounded by his loved ones. His steadfast strength came from his unwavering trust in the Lord he loved, and though he could be a quiet, gentle giant, he spoke up when it mattered and always made sure his friends and family knew just how deeply they were cherished. Already so missed, Ken will be forever loved and remembered. 
The mid 1950s was an influential chapter in history. The Korean Conflict had come to an end overseas at last, ushering in an era of hope and healing. Here at home in the United States, the historical civil rights movement was in full swing. Rock and roll took the world by storm, and iconic TV shows of the era such as I Love Lucy entertained families all across the nation. It was during this exciting time that Robert and Geraldine (Tolsma) Dahnke of Grand Rapids, Michigan, were happy to announce the arrival of their son. Born just a couple of days before Christmas, Kenneth Allen made his entrance into the world on December 23 of 1954. 
Ken grew up as a typical boy of his generation in most ways. He was raised in the family home on Herrick Avenue NE alongside his younger sister, Kathy. Ken’s parents were both employed at Radio Bible Class. His father, Robert, was a printer while his mother, Geraldine, worked as a secretary. The roots of Kenneth’s lifelong faith were planted early while attending Homestead Bible Chapel with his family. The Dahnkes looked forward to spending a week every summer at Gull Lake Bible Conference. Throughout his formative years, Ken ran a paper route. He attended local schools, including Kent Hills Elementary and Creston High School, and graduated in 1973. 
As a young man he knew he liked working with his hands better than being a student. Ken followed in his parents’ footsteps and stepped into a career working at Radio Bible Class, a ministry that is now known as Our Daily Bread. Over the span of his 40 year career, Ken worked primarily as a printer, but he also designed and built many of the presses. Since he had built them himself, he was the go-to trouble-shooter and repairmen for his presses. Ken was very skilled with his hands, especially with electrical and plumbing work, and could fix just about anything. 
In 1980, Ken’s life was forever changed when he was introduced to a co-worker, Kathleen Barger. A relationship blossomed, and the two enjoyed attending hockey games together. It didn’t take Ken long to realize that Kathy was the love of his life. With a desire to spend the rest of their lives together, the happy couple were united in marriage on August 28 of 1982 at Kathy’s home church, South Boston Bible Church in Saranac. The newlyweds celebrated with their family and friends with a reception hosted at Saranac High School. Ken and Kathy then enjoyed a honeymoon to Traverse City before settling down in Ada. 
Of all the things Kenneth loved in life, his family was undoubtedly his greatest treasure. In 1987, he and Kathy were overjoyed to welcome their daughter, Michelle, into their hearts and home. Ken thrived in his role as a devoted father, and Michelle was the greatest gift he could ever receive. He was very involved in his daughter’s life, supporting her and cheering her on in everything she pursued. Holidays were always spent with extended family. The Dahnkes followed in Ken’s family tradition and took memorable vacations to Gull Lake every year, where they loved boating, fishing, and tubing. They also made trips around the beautiful state of Michigan, visiting locations such as Traverse City, Mackinaw, and the Upper Peninsula. In the 1990s, the family enjoyed a seasonal camping spot at Grand Rogue Campground, where Ken served through various building projects around the grounds. 
As all who knew him can certainly attest, Ken’s faith was his firm foundation. It touched all areas of his life, from his career where he worked to lead others to the Kingdom and the way he went above and beyond to serve others. Kenneth and Kathy were charter members of Ada Bible Church. He poured many hours into building the facilities and helping out with whatever handiwork needed to be done. Each and every day, Ken strove to live out his faith in word and deed. 
Later in life, Kenneth was thrilled to become a doting grandfather to Emma and Riley. He adored his granddaughters and loved being involved in their lives. Ken took the girls on special day trips, and their summers were filled with swimming and countless ice cream trips. He often dropped by to visit Michelle’s family at their campsite. Ken affectionately called Emma and Riley “his buddies,” and they were truly the light of his life. 
Ken loved lake freighters and in his free time he would study their history and track them through the lakes. He enjoyed simply watching the freighters, and Port Huron was his favorite place to be. Known for his lifelong passion for classic cars, Ken had some knowledge on auto mechanics and repair. He enjoyed dabbling in 3D printing and genealogy as well. 
When reflecting on the overflowing life of Kenneth Dahnke, it is easy to see both the strength of his faith and the fierce, unconditional love he held for his friends and family. He strove to serve those around him with compassion and selflessness, and as a dedicated husband, father, and grandfather, Ken led his family with wisdom and integrity. Although he will be dearly missed, Ken leaves behind a priceless collection of memories that his loved ones will hold close for many years to come. 
Kenneth Allen Dahnke, age 71, entered his heavenly home October 26, 2026. Ken was born December 23, 1954 in Grand Rapids, to the late Robert and Geraldine (Tolsma) Dahnke. He was also preceded in death by his brother-in-law, Earl “Jack” Barger. On August 28, 1982 Ken married Kathleen Barger. He is survived by his wife of 44 years, Kathleen; his daughter, Michelle (Brandon) Hopkins; granddaughters, Emma and Riley; sister, Kathy Dahnke; sister-in-law, Sue Barger; and several nieces and cousins. In accordance with his wishes, cremation has taken place. A memorial service will be held at Ada Bible Church Cascade Campus, 8899 Cascade Drive SE. Friends may visit Ken’s family beginning at 10 AM. Memorials are suggested to Our Daily Bread Ministries.

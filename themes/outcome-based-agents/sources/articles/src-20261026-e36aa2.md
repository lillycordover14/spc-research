CLAY N.Y. — 
As construction moves forward at Micron’s massive semiconductor manufacturing site in the town of Clay, residents are heading to the polls with questions about the future of their community and the local leaders who will help shape it.
Years of debate over development, environmental impacts and increased traffic have put the town’s changing landscape in the spotlight. Now, with several local races on the ballot, those concerns are part of the conversation among early voters.
For Liverpool resident Linda Dasitt, the changes are personal.
“I do get sad watching like them building the houses or building like Micron, and I drove down Caughdenoy and I grew up in that area and to see all the trees gone and it just was terrible,” Dasitt said.
Micron’s planned chipmaking complex promises thousands of jobs and significant economic growth for Central New York. But the scale of the project has also raised concerns among residents about its environmental impact and how future development will be managed.
Those issues have been discussed at public meetings for years, and some voters see the upcoming local elections as an opportunity to 
make their voices heard
.
“Every election is important. I mean that is your voice in your community,” Clay resident Anne Simon said. “You know if you’re unsatisfied with things instead of grousing to your friends and family, you know do something about it.”
Clay has more than 42,000 registered voters. Although registered Democrats outnumber Republicans, Republicans have long controlled town government.
This election, voters will decide several contested races, including town supervisor, town clerk and all three Onondaga County Legislature seats representing Clay.
Early voting has already drawn thousands of residents. More than 2,400 people have cast ballots in person, while nearly 1,400 others have returned mail ballots.
Simon said she and her family decided to vote early in anticipation of strong turnout.
“We figure this year it was going to be a lot of people hopefully out voting, so we wanted to make sure we came and we were actually surprised how crowded it was today,” Simon said.
While Micron’s development is expected to bring economic opportunities to the region, some residents are weighing those potential benefits against concerns about the environment and the community’s changing character.
“It matters for future generations, not just me,” Dasitt said.
She acknowledged the potential economic benefits of the project while expressing concerns about what she has seen so far.
“I know it’s probably good and bringing jobs in, but I don’t like the way it’s looking right now,” Dasitt said.
As early voting continues, Clay residents have an opportunity to choose the local officials who will help guide the town through its next chapter, including the continued development surrounding Micron’s semiconductor project.
The 2026 Midterm Election is on Nov. 3.

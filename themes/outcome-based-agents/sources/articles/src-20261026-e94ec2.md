For Palestinians, Israeli election will only determine the pace of dispossession&nbsp;&nbsp;Middle East Eye

[page text]
Israel’s general election on 27 October is widely being framed as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Polls 
suggest
 that neither Netanyahu’s bloc, nor the opposition bloc of four Jewish-led parties, will win a majority in the Knesset, 
Israel
’s parliament. 

Arab parties, representing 20 percent of the citizenry, remain outside the arithmetic of government. 
Their exclusion
 reveals much about the political terms on which this election is being fought.  

Across history, the settler-colonial democracies that most thoroughly dispossessed indigenous peoples - the 
United States
, Canada and Australia - have held elections that advanced this process. Because 
elimination
 and land seizures underpinned their existence, they never put these issues on the ballot; voters chose only who would do it most effectively and rapidly. 

Similarly, Israelis are not choosing a direction in the coming election. They never have. They are choosing the pace of 
ethnic cleansing
, 
genocide
 and dispossession of the 
Palestinian
 people.

Indeed, Israel’s election campaign follows a familiar settler-colonial pattern. Voters are being asked to judge Netanyahu on wars, security, the 
ultra-Orthodox draft
, the 
judicial overhaul
, and other domestic issues. Yet on the questions that decide Palestinian lives, Netanyahu’s bloc and the opposition offer different routes to the same destination. They may differ in intensity, pace and timing, but not in direction.

This shared direction is forcefully reflected in the abandonment of the rhetoric of peace, negotiations and two states, which once served as a facade to conceal Israeli settler-colonialism. Dispossession is now pursued in the daylight, across the left-right divide. 

Consider Yair Golan, leader of the leftist Democrats, who dismisses the two-state solution as a “
galactic leap
”. 

His alternative involves more of the same Israeli settler-colonial policies: “
security coordination
” with the Palestinian Authority, support for its “moderate” and “effective” administrative authority, and “
civil separation
” between Palestinians and Israelis. Separation without equality is a euphemism for 
apartheid
. 

Stealing land

In settler-colonial democracies across history, infrastructure has fuelled land expropriation and won votes. 

Israel has developed a similar network of legal and material infrastructure to dispossess Palestinians. The 
Absentees’ Property Law
 of 1950 has been used to transfer property rights from Palestinians forced out in 1948 to Jewish settlers and infrastructure projects. 

For decades, successive Israeli governments have classified extensive tracts of the occupied West Bank as “
state land
”, paving the way for colony construction and expansion.

What is at stake today is no longer simply the scope of discrimination. It is the political neutralisation of an entire constituency

Jewish-only roads, settler outposts, the apartheid wall, military zones, and projects to 
Judaise
 land in the West Bank, the Galilee and the Negev (Naqab) have been 
designed
 to displace Palestinians and expropriate their land. This has continued under virtually every Israeli government, with or without Netanyahu.

Just as settler-colonial states in the past used direct and indirect means to eliminate and dispossess indigenous peoples, Israel has pursued 
genocidal campaigns
 to 
drive
 Palestinians, who cannot vote, out of Gaza and the West Bank. This continues a pattern set in 1948, when Zionist forces 
massacred
 and 
ethnically cleansed
 Palestinians, destroying their villages and towns. 

Israel has delegated some of its dirty work to West Bank settlers. Since 2023, it has intensified a campaign to 
arm and shield
 settler militias that have been 
seizing
 Palestinian land and carrying out 
pogroms
 in Jenin, Nablus, Ramallah, Jerusalem, Jericho, Hebron and elsewhere. 

For Palestinians, none of this is a campaign pledge. It is an ongoing reality, uncontested and pursued by successive Israeli governments.  

Demographic engineering

By excluding native peoples from the electorate, settler-colonial democracies were able to guarantee that their dispossession incurred no electoral costs. On the contrary, the policymakers who facilitated it have gained public support. 

Unlike indigenous peoples in other settler-colonial states, Palestinians are not a remnant minority, but make up roughly half of the population between the river and the sea. Only a small proportion have the right to vote.  

After the ethnic cleansing of Palestine in 1948, approximately 
150,000 Palestinians
 remained within what became Israel. They were subsequently granted a form of subordinate Israeli 
citizenship
, which offered formal inclusion within a liberal settler-colonial state, while simultaneously denying them the right to self-determination in their homeland. Through demographic engineering, their numbers have been kept to around one-fifth of the total Israeli population.

While their citizenship entitles them to vote, Israel has neutralised the political power of their vote through a series of laws and policy measures. Since 7 October 2023, this process has intensified, with Palestinian citizens and politicians 
arrested
, prosecuted, 
disqualified
 or expelled for expressing an opinion or liking a social media post. 

Meanwhile, the state has allowed 
violent crime
 to spiral in Palestinian communities inside Israel, with murder rates reaching 
record highs
. Home 
demolitions and forced displacement
 have become routine in the Naqab and elsewhere. 

In 2021, 
Ra’am
 became the first independent Palestinian Arab party to join the Israeli governing coalition. It won a crime-fighting budget, recognition of three villages in the Naqab, and some municipal concessions. Land and self-determination, which the 
2018 nation-state law
 reserves exclusively for Jews, were off the table.

What is at stake today is no longer simply the scope of 
discrimination
. It is the political neutralisation of an entire constituency, and the wilful destabilisation of its communities.

For the indigenous peoples of North America and Australia, it made little difference who won elections. In Israel, a “liberal”, “centrist” or “moderate” government might slow the pace of dispossession and restore the facade of peace, giving western governments an excuse to look away - but it would not stop settler-colonial expansion or return a single inch of land to Palestinians.

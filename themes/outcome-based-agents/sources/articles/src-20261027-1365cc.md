An Asian fusion restaurant and cocktail lounge inspired by the flavors of China, Japan and Thailand has opened in Harper Court, with its owners hoping to draw more foot traffic to a complex that has struggled to retain tenants.
Mi Mi Dao, at 1516 E. Harper Ct., celebrated its opening Friday, Oct. 23, with a ribbon-cutting ceremony. The restaurant, whose name means “secret island” in Mandarin, offers a menu of shareable dishes and specialty cocktails, including some tiki-inspired creations.
Owner Vince Tian said he and his partners, Pattie and Tom Kidwell, who also own Chant and Noodles Etc. in the neighborhood, first considered opening a hot pot place in the space vacated by the former Jade Court. They settled instead on what he described as “modern Asian cuisine” paired with a full cocktail program.

“Let’s do something new over here,” Tian said.
Tian said the flavors and drink ideas were inspired by his travels in China, Japan and Thailand, as well as restaurants on the East and West coasts. He often travels to try new foods, then experiments with recreating the dishes when he returns home.
“My friends call me a food connoisseur,” he said.
The menu is designed for sharing, with dishes including Mi Mi Orange Chicken, a crispy Japanese-style chicken cutlet served with house-made Hennessy orange sauce; Cauliflower on Fire, served with yuzu dipping sauce and “firecracker sauce”; and Sesame Chili Udon, tossed in a sesame-peanut chili sauce. 
Other offerings include scallion pancakes with roasted vegetable dip, mala Sichuan wontons, cola wings and honey walnut shrimp. Small plates generally cost about $15 to $18, while entrees range from $26 to $38.
Behind the bar, bartender Chris Allen makes cocktails including Tingles Tingles, a drink made with tequila, St-Germain, lime juice, house-made ginger syrup and Sichuan peppercorn oil. It is garnished with an edible flower that diners can chew, producing a mild numbing sensation.
“It’s a full-on experience,” Tian said.
Other cocktails play on dishes from the kitchen, including a Thai beef salad-inspired martini made with peanut-infused coconut rum and garnished with beef jerky, a five-spice old fashioned and a spicy papaya margarita.

Lunch and happy hour offer lower price points than the dinner menu. Happy hour specials on drinks and food run from 3 to 5 p.m. every day, and a bento lunch special starts at $19.95. The bento includes rice and two protein dishes chosen from orange chicken, mapo tofu, tomato silky egg, cola wings and honey walnut shrimp. 
Tian said monthly and seasonal specials will follow, including some classic street foods.
Harper Court, just off 53rd Street, has struggled to retain some tenants in recent years. At least five businesses have closed in the court since 2020, including Jade Court, the previous restaurant in the Mi Mi Dao space, which
 
cited low foot traffic
 
as a reason for closing.
Mi Mi Dao's opening also follows the September 2025 debut of
 
nearby Hyde Park Labs
, which could bring additional customers to the complex. Hyde Park Labs is a 13-story commercial laboratory building spearheaded by the University of Chicago, with space for science startups, corporate partners and university researchers.
Tian said he hopes the restaurant will draw more visitors to Harper Court and is exploring partnerships with neighboring businesses.
“We want to make it as community as possible,” he said.
Tian said U. of C. students have already begun visiting after seeing the restaurant's social media posts. He said the restaurant has also partnered with the university to offer student discounts.
Asked what success would look like a year from now, Tian said he wants the restaurant to be a place where friends can share small plates and drinks, with “a little bit more conversation than just watching sports on TV.”
Tian said the choice to open another restaurant, despite
 
economic headwinds
 
in the industry, came down to neighborhood fealty. 
“We love Hyde Park,” he said. “We’ve been here for a long time.”

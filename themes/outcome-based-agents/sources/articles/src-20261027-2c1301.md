            Saviynt recognised in Gartner PAM report for third year
          

            Tue, 27th Oct 2026
          

                JOSEPH GABRIEL LAGONSIN
              

News Editor

Saviynt has been recognised as a Challenger in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Privileged Access Management, marking its third consecutive year in Gartner's PAM research.

The recognition comes as companies face broader privileged access requirements across cloud workloads, automation, non-human identities and AI agents. That shift has expanded PAM beyond traditional administrator accounts and increased the focus on controlling elevated access for shorter periods.

Privileged access management has become a growing concern for security teams as organisations adopt more automated systems and machine-led processes. The challenge is no longer limited to identifying which staff need administrator rights. It now also includes governing access for software processes, service accounts and emerging AI systems.

Saviynt said this environment is driving demand for tighter control over why access is granted, how long it remains active and who is responsible for it. It also linked the change to a push to reduce persistent privileged access across enterprise systems.

Anirudh Sen, Senior Vice President of Products at Saviynt, said the rise of AI and machine identities was changing how companies think about privileged access.

"Privilege is no longer limited to a defined group of administrators. As AI agents and machine identities become mainstream in enterprises, organizations need to understand the context of why access is needed, how long it should be granted, and who is accountable for it," said Anirudh Sen, Senior Vice President of Products at Saviynt.

Broader scope

PAM tools now need to operate alongside wider identity governance processes rather than as a standalone security layer, the company argued. In its view, organisations want systems that combine identity governance, privilege lifecycle management, lifecycle automation and just-in-time access in a single platform.

That reflects a wider trend in enterprise security, where access controls are being reshaped by cloud adoption and the growing use of software-based identities. Security teams are under pressure to ensure sensitive access is approved, monitored and removed quickly, particularly when systems can act with elevated privileges without direct human intervention.

Saviynt said its approach is based on Zero Trust principles and a continuous, risk-based model for privilege management. It also highlighted its work on Zero Standing Privilege, a model designed to minimise always-on elevated access and shift organisations towards granting privileges only when justified by context and risk.

The company uses what it calls a Privilege Spectrum framework to describe different levels of access across human and non-human identities. The goal is to match the level and duration of privileged access to the task while maintaining oversight.

AI pressure

The spread of AI systems in business operations has added urgency to the debate. As AI agents begin to take on operational tasks, security teams must decide how those systems receive access to applications, data and infrastructure, and how that access is reviewed.

For vendors in the sector, that creates an opening to present PAM as part of a broader identity security strategy. Rather than focusing only on vaulting passwords or monitoring administrator sessions, providers are increasingly framing privileged access as an issue spanning the full identity lifecycle.

Saviynt said its repeated appearance in Gartner's PAM research reflects its direction in the market.

"We believe our repeated recognition in the Gartner PAM research reflects our commitment to securing the full Privilege Spectrum through a unified identity security platform that brings identity governance and privileged access together," said Sen.

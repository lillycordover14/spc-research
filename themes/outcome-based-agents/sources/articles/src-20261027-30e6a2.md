Ranking the Penguins’ top 16 forwards, from Sidney Crosby to Andrei Kuzmenko

The Pittsburgh Penguins have considerable depth at forward. Some say they have too many forwards, while others say, because of injuries, they can never have too many good options. Both can be true.
All of the Penguins’ forwards are helping the cause right now. Five of their best 17 forwards were unavailable for Saturday’s 3-2 overtime win against the Nashville Predators, so that depth has value. Still, you don’t want so many forwards available that young players may get blocked from making an impact.

Through a dozen games, some young players have thrived, and some veterans have disappointed. The Penguins have seen such mixed results that determining their forward units when/if this team gets healthy must feel impossible.
Based on statistics, history and the eye test, here is my ranking of the best 16 available Penguins forwards.
(Filip Hållander was traded to Edmonton and Justin Brazeau hasn’t played a game this season, so we’ll keep them out of this list.)
16. Andrei Kuzmenko
Zero goals, 1 assist, 1 point in 8 games
Thoughts:
 It’s a bit unfair to criticize him too much after his father’s tragic death. But based solely on his performance, he looks slow and isn’t helping the Penguins win. If he keeps playing at this level, I can’t imagine him being in the lineup for much longer.
15. Rutger McGroarty
Zero goals, 0 assists, 0 points in 2 games
Thoughts
: McGroarty’s sample size is obviously too small to draw many conclusions. I want to see more of him in the NHL, and I’d encourage the coaches to leave him in the lineup for a while, even at the expense of veterans. It’s time to find out what he can do.
McGroarty is good defensively and a strong penalty killer. That much is clear. The question is whether he can score consistently. In two NHL games this season, he’s looked like the same guy we’ve seen before. He won’t hurt you, but you’d sure like to see him light the lamp more often. He has four goals in 34 NHL games. Though scoring is often the last thing to come for a prospect, it’s time for him to figure it out.
14. Avery Hayes
Zero goals, 0 assists, 0 points in 1 game
Thoughts
: Hayes’ sample size is even smaller, but I love his game. He fired five shots on goal in his season debut on Saturday. You can’t miss the energy he brings to the lineup.
13. Elmer Söderblom
Zero goals, 1 assist, 1 point in 6 games
Thoughts:
 He’s been a little disappointing, given what he did late last season. From the first day of training camp, when Söderblom showed up late for a workout, something has seemed off. He’s not been bad. His size and skill are so rare that it’s understandable if the Penguins push him into the lineup. Söderblom played his finest game of the season against Nashville. That was more like it.

He hasn’t been as productive as expected, and his turnover that led to a goal in Colorado a couple of weeks ago was a killer.

12. Blake Lizotte
Zero goals, 4 assists, 4 points in 12 games
Thoughts
: He’s been fine but hasn’t played at last season’s level. His speed and pest behavior haven’t been as noticeable this season. I’m sure he’ll be fine, but I haven’t loved his game.
Lizotte is winning only 45 percent of his draws. He’s been at 51 percent and 50 percent in the last two seasons. This might not seem like a big deal, and it’s been just 12 games, but he’s lost several big draws this season.
11. Connor Dewar
Two goals, 3 assists, 5 points in 12 games
Thoughts:
 I’ve liked his game marginally more than Lizotte’s, though Dewar’s energy, penalty killing and overall effectiveness aren’t where they were last season. That said, he’s a good, reliable player, and I have no real problems with how he’s played.
10. Ville Koivunen
Two goals, 3 assists, 5 points in 10 games
Thoughts:
 I have to be honest: He’s growing on me. I didn’t love Koivunen’s training camp, and if not for a rash of injuries, he wouldn’t have made the team. He was then set to be a healthy scratch in Denver two weeks ago, when an Egor Chinakhov injury propelled him back into the lineup. Since that point, Koivunen has been a different, better player.
Koivunen has three points in his past five games and has been a positive influence, especially in distributing the puck. Does he need to start scoring more goals? Yes, he does. He only has four in 57 career games, which obviously isn’t nearly enough. But there are signs he’s putting things together, and I think it would be difficult to remove him from the lineup at the moment. He’s playing with tenacity and making things happen every game.
9. Tommy Novak
Three goals, 5 assists, 8 points in 12 games
Thoughts:
 I sincerely don’t know where to rank him. Only two Penguins have more points than his eight, and he never screws anything up. So, I might not be giving him enough credit. The coaching staff is smitten with him. You can’t deny the good things he’s done, and I’m hardly trying to.

However, the Penguins often look a step slow and a hair disinterested in being physical. Novak represents both. Still, he plays an intelligent game and knows how to collect points. He’s a confusing player to analyze.
8. Hendrix Lapierre
Two goals, 4 assists, 6 points in 8 games
Thoughts:
 Lapierre is really winning me over. He’s a smart player who has been very productive. It seems that whatever line’s on suddenly improves. He didn’t overly impress in training camp but has been very good under the bright lights. I’d consider leaving him in the lineup even as the Penguins get healthy.
7. Rickard Rakell
Two goals, 3 assists, 5 points in 12 games
Thoughts:
 I know, I know. He’s struggled and looked a step slow. He’s blown several golden opportunities. Most of you want to trade him now. But he’s earned the benefit of the doubt as a strong player for a long time. He’s also streaky. Rakell scored only four goals in his first 19 games of last season and bounced back just fine. Let’s not forget that.
6. Ben Kindel
Two goals, 2 assists, 4 points in 9 games
Thoughts
: Kindel has obviously endured a miserable week. Before the season even began, he was forced to deal with an injury. In between, the teenage center has been one of their best forwards. He’s going to be a good player for a very long time.
5. Nick Robertson 
Six goals, 0 assists, 6 points in 12 games
Thoughts
: How can you not be impressed? I love his tenacity as much as his goal-scoring touch. Terrific stuff.
4. Egor Chinakhov
Two goals, 1 assist, 3 points in 8 games
Thoughts:
 People have been a little hard on Chinakhov. He’s fine, and his numbers would look a bit different if that beauty he scored against Montreal had stood. He needs to start producing more when he gets healthy, but I suspect he will.
3. Bryan Rust
Three goals, 2 assists, 5 points in 7 games
Thoughts:
 He’s still terrific, though his defense has gotten a little spotty, and he’s had trouble staying healthy. Rust is out for at least six weeks, probably more, and the Penguins will miss him.

2. Evgeni Malkin
Six goals, 3 assists, 9 points in 11 games
Thoughts
: I thought about making him No. 1. He’s been excellent. His production is where it needs to be, and the bad penalties and turnovers haven’t been a problem. The question, as it has been for years, is whether he can do it for a complete season. At 40 years old, the odds are against him, but you never know.
1. Sidney Crosby
Three goals, 4 assists, 7 points in 12 games
Thoughts:
 He hasn’t been at his best this season. His production isn’t where it needs to be. He looks off and is struggling to find his game.
But if anyone deserves the benefit of the doubt, it’s Crosby. It’s not surprising that he started the season slowly. It will be surprising if he finishes it slowly. His game took a small jump on the recent road trip. It will again soon enough.

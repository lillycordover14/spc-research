[page text]
Turnout for
 Israel’s 2026 elections
 is at its highest pace in more than 50 years, officials said — with Wonder Woman actor Gal Gadot among those who cast their ballot on Tuesday calling for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s ouster.

Nearly 45% of voters have cast their ballots as of 2 p.m. local time, the highest turnout recorded at that hour since 1973, according to the Central Elections Committee. 

That equates to nearly 3.3 million out of Israel’s 7.3 million voters, with citizens continuing to flock to the polls in a heated race between
 Netanyahu’s right-wing alliance
 and the opposition coalition led by 
ex military chief Gadi Eisenkot.

Gadot posted an Instagram story of herself voting Tuesday morning as she called on her
 fellow Israelis to make their voice count.

“That’s it, I’m exercising my right to vote, and I ask all of you to go out and exercise your most basic, most important, the most democratic right, and to vote for who you believe in,” Gadot said at her polling site.

“I’m voting for change, and I hope that today, Tuesday… that it will only be good for us all,” she added, blowing a kiss at the camera.

Israel is the only nation in a region still dominated by hereditary monarchs that has regular free and fair democratic elections. This is the Jewish state’s sixth election in seven years.

Gadot cast her ballot after receiving an honorary doctorate from Israel’s Bar-Ilan University on Monday night, where she also called on the crowd to head to the polls. 

6

An voter casts a ballot at a polling station in Nazareth, Israel, while holding a child. 
Anadolu via Getty Images

Though Gadot did not name who she voted for, 
opposition leader Eisenkot 
is the main figure in Israel’s so-called “change bloc,” which is running to oust Netanyahu, who has served as Israel’s longest running prime minister.

Prior to the election, nearly all of Israel’s major polls put Eisenkot’s Together coalition ahead of Netanyahu’s Likud and allied parties, but it remains to be seen if either side can secure the 61 seats necessary to claim the majority in the Knesset. 

May Kurtz, 25, is among those who voted for Eisenkot after being disillusioned with Netanyahu, accusing the prime minister of waging war on Iran and continuing the conflict for political gain. 

6

Israelis wait in line to vote at a polling station in Tel Aviv’s neighborhood of Florentin during Tuesday’s election.

“When Bibi chose to go to war ‘by coincidence’ on the same day it was politically beneficial, come on,” she told The Post. “we’re not stupid. I don’t think that was a coincidence.

“I wouldn’t be surprised if tomorrow Bibi will be in the news saying we need to stop the election because there’s the biggest war in the world,” she added. 

Ella Alkabets, 39, was another Eisenkot supporter who fears that Netanyahu has become too reliant on the US and President Trump to properly make decisions for Israel. 

6

Israeli Prime Minister and leader of the Likud party Benjamin Netanyahu smiles after filling out his ballot at a polling station in Jerusalem.

“I’m hearing now that Bibi’s people are reaching out to Trump to say he supports Bibi again. Trump is the big guy and Bibi is the little girl who’s pulling his shirt,” Alkabets fumed. “It’s ridiculous.” 

Many Israelis, however, still back Netanyahu and believe he is the best choice to
 complete the war in Iran.

“I think Bibi is the better alternative for the Iran situation.” Itzik Cohen, 27, said. “Bibi won’t attack Iran without the backing of the U.S., but he also won’t pass up an opportunity that might help bring an end to that regime.” 

6

Former Israeli military chief Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, casts his ballot in Herzliya.

Benik Gregorian, 41, said he was sure that Netanyahu would win the election and “finish Iran.”

Most voters also said they believe that US-Israeli won’t change even if Netanyahu loses, with Moshe Oberstein, 19, casting her ballot for an opposition party to support change. 

“I think we can have a good relationship with Trump without Bibi,” Oberstein said. “I think a different prime minister could have an even better relationship with him. 

“And Trump is going to be switched in two years anyway, so the issue is even less important.”

Along with a record pace turnout, the Israel Defense Forces said military turnout was also at a higher pace than the 2022 election, with at least 48% of registered voters coming out as of 1 p.m. local time.

Despite having been regarded as four of the greatest songwriters of their generation in their own regard, and coming together every once in a while to make something spellbinding together, 
Crosby, Stills, Nash and Young
 endured something of a rocky existence.
From being a trio sans Neil Young, to suddenly bringing the Canadian songwriter into the fold, to just as quickly falling apart, to reuniting as a trio, merely trying to follow the ups and downs of the project is just as hard as it evidently was to be in the band. CSNY always appeared to be falling apart at the seams, and weren’t exactly cut out for a long and fruitful career together, and this was a crying shame considering how they had all of the potential to work well as a project.
That being said, in whatever configuration of the group, they always managed to see beyond their differences on record, with their music always reflecting the different styles and voices that they possessed. It could have been 
a collaboration between any combination of the four members
, and they’d still produce something excellent, even if they were at each other’s throats all the time.
That being said, even when they were at the top of their game, it’s not like the general morale or mood of the camp was particularly inviting or warm, and despite the trio version reuniting in 1976 for the third time and sometimes choosing to bring Young back into the fray, their eventual separation in 2015 was well and truly the end, owing to their personal differences.
A huge part of the fractious dynamic was down to the waspish personality of David Crosby, and even though his contributions were among the greatest the band ever made, the tolerance of his former bandmates appeared to have soured significantly upon this most recent separation. On several occasions since the band finally decided to call it quits, 
Crosby was the one who was most vocal about his dislike for his former colleagues
, and didn’t exactly hold back when asked about his relationship with any of them.
Even right up until his final months on this planet, Crosby was taking potshots at the other members of the folk rock supergroup, and during an interview with 
Songfacts 
in late 2022, he claimed that he was still actively avoiding communication with two of the members.
“I don’t have a relationship with Neil or Graham [Nash] at all,” he stated when asked about his current feelings about his former associates, adding: “I haven’t spoken to either one of them in two years.”

However, he did make an exception for the other remaining member, claiming that there wasn’t anything about their relationship that had changed over the years, and that there was still a profound underlying respect. “I still really love Stephen Stills,” he noted. 
“I just can’t help it. There’s something about his music, something about the way he plays that just thrills me. It always has. But I don’t get along well with Neil or Graham, either one. So that’s that.”
Of course,
 with Crosby passing away just two months
 after the interview was conducted, there’s no time for him to patch up his differences with Nash or Young, and all prospects of a reunion, no matter how slim, are well and truly over forever. Even though other members of the band felt as though it could have been possible for them to simply get back together for the sake of the music, there was absolutely no way that some of them would be able to stomach being in the same room together, and getting Crosby to agree would have been near impossible.

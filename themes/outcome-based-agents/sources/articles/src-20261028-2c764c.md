Apple’s 
iOS 27
 brings several cool new features to mobile Safari that make the latest OS worth grabbing for your iPhone. And a few of them use your device’s AI smarts to assist you. Since upgrading my iPhone, I’ve been checking out these latest enhancements and have found three that I think are worth highlighting.

One new AI-based feature lets you create your own extensions just by describing what you want. Another automatically uses AI to organize all your open tabs based just on their content. A third taps into AI to notify you of changes to a website so you don’t have to manually check the site yourself.

More from ZDNET

To take advantage of Safari’s new AI features, you’ll need an iPhone that not only can be upgraded to iOS 27 but also supports Apple Intelligence. That means an iPhone 15 Pro, 15 Pro Max, any iPhone 16, any iPhone 17, the iPhone 18 Pro, 18 Pro Max, or the iPhone Duo.

Though I’m covering how the features work on an iPhone, you’ll also find them in Safari on an iPad with iPadOS 27 and a Mac with MacOS 27. For an iPad, you’d need an iPad Pro or Air with an M1 chip or higher or an iPad mini with an A17 Pro chip or higher. Mac users would need a model with an M1 chip or higher.

Now, here’s how my three favorite Safari AI features work and how I use them.

1. Create your own extensions

Apple offers a variety of third-party extensions for mobile Safari. To check them out on your iPhone, go to Settings, select Apps, and then tap Safari. Swipe down the screen and select Extensions. Here, you can see which extensions are already enabled or at least available. Below is a list of recommended extensions. Swipe to the bottom of the screen and select More Extensions. That takes you to the Safari Extensions store, where you’re able to browse or search for others.

Screenshot by Lance Whitney/ZDNET

But rather than hunting for the right extension, you can create your own. To get started, launch Safari on your phone and then browse to a web page that you want to use as an example for the extension. Tap the three-lined menu icon to the left of the address bar and select Describe Extension.

At the next screen, choose the category that best describes your desired extension. Otherwise, select the option to describe something else. If you choose one of the preset categories, a list of possible descriptions appears. Choose the one you want or offer your own description. Tap Create to submit the description and then wait for the extension to be generated.

Screenshot by Lance Whitney/ZDNET

When the extension is ready, tap the Try button at the top to run it. Tap the checkmark to proceed. To turn it off, tap the three-lined menu icon, swipe down the list of items, and tap the name of the extension. To launch the extension on any other page in the future, return to the menu and tap its name.

Screenshot by Lance Whitney/ZDNET

To modify the extension, open the menu and select Manage Extensions. Select it and then describe the change you wish to make. From here, you’re also able to allow the extension to work in Private Browsing mode, set its permissions, or delete it entirely.

For this one, I created an extension that sets a minimum font size of 20 points on a web page. This way, I can more easily see and read pages with text that otherwise might be too small.

Screenshot by Lance Whitney/ZDNET

2. Let AI organize all your open tabs

As you open more and more websites, you collect more and more open tabs. And the more tabs, the more difficult they can be to navigate and manage. One option is to organize them into groups. But doing that manually is time-consuming. Instead, why not let the built-in AI organize them for you?

To try this in Safari, tap the Tabs button and tap the three-lined menu icon in the upper left. On the menu, you may see the groups that Safari has already automatically created. To continue, select Organize Tabs and then tap the option to automatically create topics. You’ll then see the tabs organized by group.

Screenshot by Lance Whitney/ZDNET

To open a specific group, tap its thumbnail. Tap the ellipsis icon, and you can copy or move the tabs to a different group or close them entirely. Otherwise, tap a specific thumbnail in a group to view the page.

I often open web pages to articles I’ve written so that they’re quickly accessible. But the more tabs that are open, the more challenging it is to keep track of them. Asking AI to organize them into distinct groups helps me more easily view and work with them all.

3. Get notified of website changes

Ever keep revisiting a website to see if it’s been updated with specific changes? Instead of doing this yourself continually, let Safari notify you when it’s been updated. To do this, navigate to the website, tap the menu icon to the left of the address bar, and select Notify Me.

Describe the change for which you’d like to be notified. For example, maybe you’re looking at a product page on a retail site and want to know if the price drops. By default, you’re notified each day at a specific time. To change the frequency and/or the time, tap Edit and then make your modifications. Finally, select the checkmark to allow Safari to send you notifications.

Day 153 & 154: Mahoosuc Notch and getting sick

Follow Gerjan "Hungry" Hertgers

Get new posts by email. Unsubscribe anytime.

They call the Mahoosuc Notch the hardest and / or funnest mile on trail, depends on who you ask. It’s a very technical “flat” rock scramble where you have to squeeze in between rocks.

It’s slow going, but not too hard for me. I’m actually having fun! For about a minute. My dad isn’t as confident with it and I want to make sure he doesn’t fall, so it’s a lot of waiting and looking back. If you’re hiking alone and are confident in situations like this, it’s a very fun little playground! A very fun playground where I almost lost my phone while trying to take a picture for you guys. I had to stash my phone in my pack, so not a lot of pictures today, I’m sorry. 

This mile took us about 3 hours, excluding lunch. After doing the Notch, you’re not out of the woods yet. The Mahoosuc arm is another hard and steep mountain. We’ve had a lot of those lately. But we did it! 

Getting sick

Not me, but on top of Mahoosuc Arm, Dreamcatcher let us know she was feeling ill. Being cold and wet the entire night definitely took a toll on her health. Going further into town doesn’t seem to be too smart and spending another night with our failing winter gear in the outside is even dumber. Dreamcatcher got a hold of Young Blood, a trail angel who dropped us off at the trail before. He can pick us up and drop us off at a motel if we go down a blue blaze. So, down the blue blaze we went. 

And.. we very much enjoyed it. Since we entered New Hampshire the white blazes have been going over the gnarliest mountains and terrain, almost never able to just hike. This blue blaze was very gradual and nice. We actually were able to have a conversation for a change. My dad also was able to enjoy the nature again, instead of constantly looking at his feet trying not to trip. I guess we’re pretty burnt out from the constant climbing, huh. Anyway, we got to a motel in Bethel and got some pizza along the way. Thanks Young Blood! Our buddy Timmy was here too!

Day 154: sometimes a zero just makes more sense

Town days are always a bit tricky. We need to get a resupply, go to an outfitter, eat some food and preferably go back to trail, all without own way of transportation. Some towns are walkable, but usually they are not. Before you know it, it’s already late in the afternoon. Sometimes it makes more sense to just zero. So, that’s what we did. 

This motel is far from everything, so we wanted to go to a place closer to town. We tried to hitch, but no luck here. We had to wait on the shuttle, but they could only shuttle us later in the morning. Guess we have to play some cornhole! Something my dad is always very sad about. He’s not competitive at all. 

We had some brunch at the diner with Timmy. He finally got his lobster roll. After this we went to the Inn. We’re all ready for nap time. 

We can’t sleep all day though, because we have to go to the outfitter and get proper winter gear. We bought everything, from fleece thermal base layers, to new gloves, drybags, sleeping bag liners and for me even a new sleeping pad. This was, uhh.. expensive. But one night shivering in the cold and wet makes you forget about finances really quick. I’m happy we have have what we need for the Maine cold. 

Dinner and a resupply later, we’re back at the Inn. Time to go to bed.

ElevenLabs Debuts Live Speech Translation API&nbsp;&nbsp;Slator

[page text]
ElevenLabs
 has 
announced
 a dedicated 
AI live speech translation
 offering for enterprise developers, targeting contact centers, telecom operators, and live events.

Announced on October 21, 2026, Eleven Realtime Translation is available in alpha, an early release, through ElevenAPI. Developers can use the API to add translated speech to their own products.

Each participant speaks in their own language and hears translated speech in return. For contact centers, ElevenLabs presents the service as a way for an agent to help customers across languages without an interpreter joining the call. Telecom operators can integrate it into phone calls, while event organizers can deliver translated audio to attendees’ devices.

The service uses 
Eleven v4 Turbo
 by default to generate translated speech, with spoken output in more than 90 languages. It also detects the source language automatically and provides translated text and transcripts of the original speech alongside the audio.

According to the 
product FAQ
, translated speech does not automatically reproduce the original speaker’s voice exactly. Customers can choose from more than 17,500 voices in ElevenLabs’ library or use their own voice clone created from recordings. The selected voice stays consistent throughout the session.

Developers can also adjust when translation begins. Starting earlier reduces the delay, while waiting for more of a sentence provides additional context for translation.

Building Translation into Customer Calls

The launch 
builds on earlier work
 with Deutsche Telekom. In March 2026, the companies 
demonstrated
 the Magenta AI Call Assistant, which included live translation during phone calls. At the time, the companies 
described
 a planned rollout to Deutsche Telekom customers in Germany. The October announcement makes a dedicated translation service available to other enterprise developers. ElevenLabs says Deutsche Telekom is already using it to develop new calling features for the assistant.

A 
prototype shared on LinkedIn
 by Alexander Teusz, a forward-deployed engineer at NiCE Cognigy, shows a contact center application. He connected ElevenLabs’ translation API to NiCE Cognigy’s system for handling voice calls. In the demonstration, a German-speaking caller first interacts with a CognigyAI agent before being transferred to an English-speaking human agent. Translation then runs in both directions, allowing the human agent and caller to continue in their respective languages. The agent also sees translated text alongside the original transcript. Teusz described the integration as a prototype built in days, rather than a real customer deployment.

ElevenLabs joins providers including 
Google
, 
OpenAI
,

,

, and

 in offering speech translation technology that developers can build into their own products.

Separately, ElevenLabs 
introduced
 Eleven Realtime, a family of voice agent models a day earlier. Its Mini and Max versions support more than 90 languages and are available through ElevenAgents and an API.

Pace Gallery
 has finally filed its 2024 accounts with Companies House, the British government’s business register. The mega gallery was ten months late in its filings. This past summer it 
announced
 deep cuts to its artist roster and staff, dropping a total of 100 artists and staffers in its retrenchment.

Pace, a mega gallery, has seven international locations. Its eight-story flagship in Chelsea cost $100 million to renovate in 2019. The group’s turnover totaled $67.69 million in 2024, a 35.5 percent drop from 2023, which cashed in $104.95 million. A pre-tax loss of $2,429 was recorded for 2024 compared to a $6.8 million profit for 2023. Pace’s UK registered company, The Pace Gallery Limited, is an umbrella company for all of its operations, excluding their Tokyo and US locations.

 

 

 

 

 

 

Net assets totaled $2.48 million compared to $4.51 million for 2023, while $9.39 million was payable to its US parent company, The Pace Gallery LLC, which in 2023 was $17.31 million. The Pace Gallery Limited’s debt of $19.2 million will be turned into shares by the end of 2026. It is unable to repay $19.2 million to its US parent company.

“The proposed debt-for-equity conversion is part of a broader global financial management strategy, which reflects a capital structure decision within the Pace group and should not be interpreted as a loss or a liquidity issue for the UK business,” a Pace spokesperson shared with the 
Art Newspaper
. “As part of the ongoing management of our global operations, we periodically review how our subsidiaries are financed to ensure that their balance sheets appropriately reflect the long-term nature of the capital invested in those businesses.”

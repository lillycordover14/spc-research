LAFAYETTE, La. (Acadiana News First) - A controversial campaign video featuring U.S. Rep. Clay Higgins and depicting an execution is sparking discussion about the use of graphic imagery and emotional appeals in political advertising.
Pearson Cross, a political scientist, said the ad uses ominous music, warnings about threats to the United States, and disturbing visuals to capture voters’ attention.
“You want to put out an ad that’s going to grab people by the collar and shake them, and certainly this latest Higgins ad does that,” Cross explained.
He added the graphic imagery is intended to leave a lasting impression on viewers rather than fade into the background.
“These are all just horrific images designed to grab our attention, and also designed to stick with us so that we’ll keep some image of this in our minds and we won’t forget it. It won’t fade into the background,” Cross described.
In the video, Higgins is portrayed as a candidate who will defend the country against perceived threats, including communism, Cross noted.
With Election Day approaching, Cross said the provocative message could help Higgins stand out among other campaigns and influence voters as they prepare to cast their ballots in the race for Louisiana’s 3rd Congressional District.
“We’re a few days away from actual casting of the votes. Maybe the last thing voters will think of before they cast their vote for the 3rd District Congressional seat is, ‘Clay Higgins is going to take care of me. He’s going to protect me against these threats,’” Cross stated.
Although political advertisements, such as the video, are protected speech, Cross encouraged voters to look beyond their emotional reactions and research candidates before making a decision.
“Elections are not a great place to go with your gut. Try to go with your head. Try to do a little research,” he emphasized.
Higgins’ office was contacted for comment but did not respond.

Commission looks to Winneshiek County for EMA coordinator 
	

Despite calls to hire part-timer, members votes 8-2 to seek a sharing agreement

                    By Fenske Bob                

on 

                    October 27, 2026                

By Bob Fenske
 

The Chickasaw County Emergency Management Commission voted Monday evening to try to cast its lot with Winneshiek County for its emergency management services.
 

The 8-2 vote — with only Bassett Mayor Jim Ashley and Ionia Mayor Randy Taylor voting no — means that the EMC will try to negotiate a sharing agreement with Winneshiek County, and members said they are hoping to finalize a deal by Dec. 1.
 

Following the vote, the dissenting mayors, along with Fredericksburg resident Doreen Cook urged the commission to reconsider and asked them to consider hiring a part-time Emergency Management Agency coordinator.
 

Members of the Chickasaw County Emergency Management Commission, which oversees the county’s EMA coordinator, meet Monday night.

“Pay them like $40,000 a year for the part-time position — have them in the agreement not work for EMS,” Cook said, “because I think it’s extremely important for the person that you
 
have in charge be familiar with businesses in the county. … To me, having someone here is really, really important. It’s critically important that they know our county, and I don’t think you can get that with someone who’s a county away and working for three counties.”
 

A number of commission members, though, said they felt like they had little choice but to reach out to Winneshiek County to see if its commission will share its EMA coordinator, Sean Snyder.
 

Winneshiek and Howard counties last month reached a 28E agreement to share Snyder, and Howard County is paying $57,000 to Winneshiek and also investing $10,000 to hire a “boots-on-the-ground” person.
 

But that agreement, commission members say, won’t work for Chickasaw County because of a decision made by the Board of Supervisors earlier this year and a law enacted by the Iowa Legislature and signed into law by Gov. Kim Reynolds in June.
 

Supervisors cut the county’s funding for EMA from $130,000 to $65,172, with the idea being that the EMA could spend down its ending fund balance. County leaders also hoped that their decision would lead to the county’s cities to help pay for emergency management in the county.
 

Supervisors have long expressed frustration that for the past decade or so the cities haven’t shared in the costs for the EMC, which oversees the EMA. Cities contend that the county funds EMA through its General Supplemental fund, which is supported by both urban and rural property taxes. County leaders, though, argue that rural property owners provide 72 percent of the funds for GS.
 

After the county certified its 2026-27 budget and tax levy, though, Iowa law changed and now allows EMA to collect only 3 percent more in revenue from all taxable entities each year.
 

That means the most funds EMA can receive in 2027-28 from taxing bodies is $67,127.16. So, in essence, if cities contributed funds in 2027-28, they would only be reducing the county’s funding of EMA.
 

Fredericksburg Mayor Jimmy Mitchell said he didn’t necessarily think Chickasaw County needs a “boots-on-the-ground” person.
 

“We have incident commanders right now,” he said. “If it’s a fire, it’s the fire chiefs. If it’s an accident, it’s law enforcement. … I think this is the way we have to go.”
 

The Dec. 1 date was set because earlier Monday, during the Board of Supervisors regular meeting, current EMA Coordinator Jeff Bernatz was offered a full-time EMT position with Chickasaw County EMS.
 

He said Monday evening that he intends to accept the position, but that he was holding off submitting a resignation letter to the county because “I want to be able to help with the transition and make sure it goes smoothly.” But he also conceded following the meeting that “we can’t wait forever, either.”
 

Earlier in the meeting, the commission voted 8-1 to allow Board of Supervisors Chairman Jake Hackman, who is the board’s representative on the EMC, to see if his board is willing to have Chickasaw County EMS Director Joel Knutson “take over” the county’s first responders from the EMA coordinator.
 

Left undecided Monday night, though, was the future of the Rescue Squad, which EMA currently oversees.
 

During a meeting earlier this month, both Snyder and Winneshiek County Sheriff Dan Marx said that the Winneshiek County EMC has no desire to oversee the squad.
 

For now, though, the commission is hoping it can reach an agreement with Winneshiek County.
 

“I think that’s what we need to focus on right now,” said North Washington Mayor Jason Tenge. “Hope we can get a deal with them, and if not, we know we’re going to be scrambling.”

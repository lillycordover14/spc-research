‘Wouldn’t Even Think Twice About It’ — 3-Time Super Bowl Champion Fully on Board With Trading $27.29M Cowboys Star to Bring Maxx Crosby to Dallas

Mark Schlereth backs trading George Pickens to the Raiders for Maxx Crosby as the Cowboys weigh contract concerns and defensive needs.

October 28, 2026 | 2:44 AM EDT
 

Trading George Pickens would be a tough call for the Dallas Cowboys, especially when the receiver has shown he can turn an ordinary play into a highlight. But keeping him comes with its own questions, from his contract demands to concerns about his effort on certain plays.

With the Cowboys sitting at 3-4, former NFL guard and three-time Super Bowl champion Mark Schlereth believes there is a deal worth making, even if it means sending Pickens to the Las Vegas Raiders to bring Maxx Crosby to Jerry World.

Mark Schlereth Endorses Idea of Cowboys Trading for Maxx Crosby

During an appearance on “105.3 The Fan,” Schlereth was asked whether Dallas should offer Pickens to the Raiders as part of a potential trade for defensive end Maxx Crosby. His answer left little room for doubt.

“Oh, yeah. Would I do that? I’d do that in the RV. Yeah, I wouldn’t even think twice about it,” Schlereth said.

His willingness to move Pickens comes down to concerns about the receiver’s week-to-week effort, despite his ability to produce highlight-reel plays. Schlereth acknowledged the spectacular touchdown catch Pickens made but argued that his impact is not always consistent throughout a game.

“That dude will make some spectacular catches,” Schlereth said. “But, you know, there’ll be plenty of times during the course of the game when you’ll put on the film, and he’s just standing around, killing grass.”

Pickens is playing on a one-year, $27.29 million franchise tag after failing to reach a multi-year extension with Dallas before the July 15 deadline.

The Cowboys have reportedly told his representatives that they will not negotiate a long-term deal during the season, leaving the receiver’s future uncertain. A second franchise tag would cost approximately $32.8 million in 2027, while Pickens is expected to seek $35 million or above annually on the open market.

Speaking on “105.3 The Fan,”
 Pickens spoke about Zay Flowers’ recent $140 million deal with $35 million APY. He said in August, “Personally, I let the world speak for me, but me personally, I think I’m definitely in that range or above.”

These financial considerations make a trade worth discussing, particularly with Dallas sitting at 3-4.

Pickens delivered 93 receptions for 1,429 yards and nine touchdowns in 2025, but his playing time dropped from 69 snaps in Week 6 to 41 in Week 7 after Brian Schottenheimer criticized his blocking effort. Currently, he sits 20th in 
PFN’s NFL WR Impact Metric
 with a 79.8 grade.

His pending free agency and recent concerns have generated speculation that the Cowboys could consider moving him before the Nov. 10 deadline.

Crosby, however, would be a difficult player to acquire.

The Raiders have reportedly reassured the defensive end that he is not being traded, and his value remains substantial. Las Vegas previously agreed to send him to Baltimore for two first-round picks, but the deal fell through after he failed his physical.

This history suggests Dallas would need to offer significant compensation beyond Pickens to have a realistic chance.

‘Stolen valor’: Clay Travis caught lying about Cheesecake Factory&nbsp;&nbsp;Yahoo

[page text]
Clay Travis Cheesecake Factory
OutKick founder Clay Travis has spent the past few days defending the honor of the Cheesecake Factory after former President Barack Obama used the restaurant chain as the punchline of a joke about President Donald Trump. But it turns out that Travis' own Cheesecake Factory story might not hold up.
Travis claimed that the chain was a special-occasion treat for his family when he was growing up. Now, The Bulwark's Tim Miller and Pablo Torre have done some digging and found that the first Cheesecake Factory in his hometown of Nashville didn't open until he was in his mid-20s, accusing him of Cheesecake Factory "stolen valor."
Obama's Cheesecake Factory Joke
While campaigning for Ohio Democrats in Cleveland on Saturday, Obama poked fun at Trump's gold-heavy renovations to the White House.

"What's with the gold? … He's got the White House looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory," Obama said,

Rather than taking the joke as a shot at Trump's taste, Travis took it as an insult to the restaurant and the people who eat there.
"Obama mocking Cheesecake Factory tells you everything about how out of touch political elites are. Growing up, eating there was a special occasion for my family, just as it is for millions of Americans today. Obama is telling on himself here," 
Travis wrote
 on Monday.
Former Obama speechwriter and 
Pod Save America
 co-host Jon Favreau wasn't buying it, asking Travis if he thought his audience was "dumb enough to fall for this s*** without watching the actual clip of Obama."

"Your boss Obama's joke is Cheesecake Factory has a cheap & garish design. Most normal Americans consider Cheesecake to be a high end, expensive place to eat reserved for special occasions. You guys are tone deaf. And, yes, I'm clearly too dumb to comprehend your brilliance too," 
Travis replied
.
Travis has made the same argument on air, too.
"I've grown up going to Cheesecake Factories if I have the money to be able to afford it. Uh, there were lots of times in my life where the Cheesecake Factory was the most expensive place that we could go to for a fancy dinner," Travis said in a clip played by Miller and Torre, 
via The Bulwark
.
'Stolen Valor'
While Travis' argument was pretty clear, there is a significant hole in his story: there wasn't a Cheesecake Factory in Nashville when he was growing up.

"A couple problems with that. Clay was born in 1979. Grew up in Nashville. The first Cheesecake Factory opened in his hometown in 2005 after he had graduated from the George Washington University," Miller said.

Miller didn't hold back.
"You never even went to a [freaking] Cheesecake Factory, Clay. Stolen valor," Miller said.

"We're granting the fact that a dictator was the subject of the sentence that Obama had as the joke, and we're just gonna take you on your own terms. We are now swift-boating Clay Travis for Cheesecake Factory stolen valor," Torre said.

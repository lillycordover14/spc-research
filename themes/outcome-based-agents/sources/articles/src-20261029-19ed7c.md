Water flows on a stretch of the North Fork Feather River will be higher than normal this weekend, prompting PG
&
E to issue a statement urging the public to take extra precautions.
The higher flows will occur in the Cresta Reach, a 4.9-mile portion of the North Fork Feather River in the Plumas National Forest, between PG
&
E’s Cresta Dam and the Cresta Powerhouse near Pulga in Butte County.
At higher flows, the river can have Class III, IV and V rapids that only skilled paddlers should attempt. Tubing can be dangerous in these conditions.
Around 9 a.m. on saturday, Oct. 31, 2026, flows in the Cresta Reach will be increased to a minimum of 800 cubic feet per second (cfs) until Sunday, Nov. 1 at 2 p.m., when flows will be gradually reduced to the season normal of around 150 cfs.
The recreational flows on the Cresta Reach are conducted in cooperation with the American Whitewater organization and the Rock Creek–Cresta Ecological Resource Committee.
PG
&
E advises people to avoid cold water, which can trigger an involuntary gasp and lead to inhaling water, cardiac arrest, temporary paralysis, hypothermia or drowning. Swift currents can overwhelm even strong swimmers, and hidden underwater obstacles can add to the danger. The utility recommends guided trips for inexperienced paddlers and prohibits recreation in its canals and flumes, where slippery sides, fast-moving water, underwater hazards and transitions into tunnels and pipes pose serious risks. 
More safety tips are available on the
 PG
&
E website.

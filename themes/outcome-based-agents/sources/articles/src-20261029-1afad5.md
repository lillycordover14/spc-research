HUDSON – Drive through Hudson and you’ll notice lawn signs supporting a multi-million dollar expansion and renovation of the Hudson Public Library.
Library Trustees – and supporters of the project – redoubled efforts to spread accurate information about the project before it goes to Town Meeting for approval.
Library Trustee chair Christine Tridente, in a written statement to the Select Board, urged passage of the warrant article. Tridente said “we have noticed an uptick in rumors and misinformation” about the project. “To be clear, the $13.2 million grant award [from the state library commissioners] will only be paid out” if the project and plan submitted to the state is approved at Town Meeting, Tridente’s statement said.
The grant money cannot be used for a different library renovation plan, and rejection at Town Meeting could set Hudson back years in securing state assistance again.
Library Trustee Lori Morton stressed in a statement that Town Meeting must approve the spending by a two-thirds vote. “Anything less than a two-thirds vote and the new construction project will pass us by,” she said. “The state will offer the $13 million to another municipality and we will be left with our current building -- and all of its problems.”
Select Board member Diane Bemis said senior-citizens are likely to be concerned about the tax impact. She said they need information “to understand the value of the library expansion with meeting rooms and all of the advantages” to both seniors and younger people, and recommended the information be spread “far and wide.”
The tax impact would be around $260 a year for the average home, until the debt is paid off. It is not a permanent tax increase. The payback period would be affected by the bond market, but was estimated at 10 to 15 years.
Town Meeting must pass the warrant article by a two-thirds margin. Technically, the article authorizes the town to raise $37 million through bond issuance, but that total would be offset by the state grant of $13.2 million, bringing the town’s obligation to an estimated $23.8 million.
The first hurdle – a debt-exclusion authorization in the special town election on September 2 – passed overwhelmingly with a 939-vote margin.
Library Building Committee members have been at work for several years, and until recently did not expect to have access to grant money until at least 2027. But the list of towns looking for help from the state suddenly grew shorter over the summer, and the library realized it had to act this year or the entire project would be in jeopardy.
More than 100 lawn signs in support of the project were distributed.

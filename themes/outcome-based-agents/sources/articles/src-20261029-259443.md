The ServiceNow Claim

Chief executive Jake Stauch told Forbes that some large customers are moving years of data from ServiceNow’s systems to Serval’s. Of EV maker Lucid Motors, he said: “That’s a 10,000 person 
organization
 that actually did a full rip and replace, migrated off the ServiceNow 
platform 
in just 45 days”.

ServiceNow said it maintains a 98% renewal rate and that inbound from first-time customers is up 60%, according to Forbes.

The Round Path

Reuters reported on 11 December 2025 that Serval had raised a $75 million Series B led by Sequoia at a $1 billion valuation, bringing its total capital raised to $127 million. Reuters said Serval was valued at $232 million in August 2025, according to PitchBook data, and had just under 30 employees.

Forbes now says Serval grew from 20 to almost 200 employees in a year. The Series C is the third round Forbes counts in the last 12 months; the article as read does not size the other one besides December’s.

What ServiceNow Spends in a Quarter

ServiceNow’s third-quarter 2026 results release reports 
subscription
 revenues of $3,988 million, up 21% from a year earlier, research and development 
expenses

marketing
 
expenses

By our arithmetic, Serval’s $150 million round equals about 16% of what ServiceNow spent on research and development in that one quarter.

The Sequoia Angle

Forbes notes that Sequoia also invested in ServiceNow in 2009. Anas Biad, the Sequoia partner who led Serval’s round, told Forbes: “When you sell into IT, they’re the ones that the 
business
 asks to do all of these other things”. Forbes says Sequoia does not currently own ServiceNow shares.

Sequoia partner Pat Grady told Forbes: “Bill McDermott is a transcendentally talented CEO”. He added: “I 
think
 having Serval coming after him puts a little fire under him”.

Forbes also reports that Serval is launching a professional services arm, ProServal, that will place 100 “automation engineers” inside enterprises, roughly half hired straight out of college.

As we read the two reports, Serval pitches two routes into ServiceNow’s base: a full replacement of the system that holds years of IT records, as Stauch told Forbes Lucid Motors did, and acting as an AI layer on top of existing systems, an approach Reuters reported in December 2025.

As we read the two reports side by side, Serval’s headcount went from just under 30 in December 2025, per Reuters, to almost 200 now, per Forbes, while ServiceNow’s response in Forbes points to a 98% renewal rate.

Serval Raises $150 Million Series C Led by Sequoia at $3 Billion Valuation&nbsp;&nbsp;citybiz

[page text]
San Francisco-based 
Serval
 has raised 
$150 million
 in Series C financing led by 
Sequoia Capital
, valuing the artificial intelligence-powered enterprise service management company at $3 billion as it expands its automation platform and professional services operations.

The financing brings Serval’s total capital raised to 
$277 million
. Existing investors 
Elad Gil, Evantic, First Round, General Catalyst and Redpoint
 participated alongside new investors Conviction and Headline.

The Series C marks Serval’s third funding round in 12 months, following a $75 million Series B in December 2025 and a Series A announced in October 2025.

Serval plans to use the capital to accelerate development of Catalyst, its AI automation agent, and launch ProServal, a professional services business that will deploy automation engineers within large enterprises to redesign workflows across departments.

The company’s platform enables employees in information technology, human resources, finance, security and legal departments to develop and manage automated workflows without traditional software engineering expertise. Catalyst uses AI-assisted code generation to help employees automate repetitive tasks and streamline internal service operations.

“Serval kills tedious work so people can focus on what matters. Everyone experiences some sort of friction in their day-to-day life that blocks their productivity and drains their joy,” said 
Alex McLeod
, co-founder and chief technology officer of Serval. “Serval gives all employees, beyond just engineers, a powerful code generation tool that allows them to automate their least fulfilling work.”

Serval reported that its annual recurring revenue has doubled each quarter since its Series B financing, although the company did not disclose its current revenue base. It has added two Fortune 20 customers in recent months for enterprise-wide automation deployments.

Customers include 
Fox Corporation, WPP, Live Nation, Ramp, Notion and Lucid Motors
. According to the company, most customers begin operating on its platform within 30 days.

Serval competes with established enterprise service management providers, including ServiceNow. The company cited an automotive manufacturer that migrated its IT service management, configuration management database, hardware asset management and software asset management operations from ServiceNow to Serval in 45 days.

“Serval is winning over major enterprises, becoming their system of record, and taking on their most critical workflows,” said Anas Biad, partner at Sequoia. “Jake, Alex, and the team have earned the trust of some of the world’s most complex organizations, and they’re building the platform those enterprises are running on.”

A significant portion of the new investment will support ProServal, which plans to hire more than 100 automation engineers over the next 12 months. These employees will work directly with enterprise customers to identify processes suitable for automation and implement AI-enabled workflows.

Approximately half of the new automation engineers are expected to be recent graduates who will complete a four-week training program before working with customers.

“The best people for this job are the ones who’ve grown up building with AI,” said Jake Stauch, co-founder and CEO of Serval. “We’re putting them inside the world’s most complex organizations, beside the teams who know how the legacy processes really work, to redesign those processes with AI at the center.”

The company is also undertaking a broader workforce expansion. After increasing its employee count tenfold over the past year, Serval plans to add more than 500 employees across its operations, including the ProServal engineering positions.

The expansion includes opening offices in New York City, London and Austin, alongside additional locations.

Serval did not disclose the ownership stake acquired by investors in the Series C or its anticipated timeline for deploying the new capital.

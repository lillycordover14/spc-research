Serval Raises $150M Series C at $3B Valuation as It Takes on ServiceNow With AI Automation&nbsp;&nbsp;Wowtale

[page text]
Serval
, the AI-native enterprise service management platform, has 
raised
 a $150 million Series C led by Sequoia at a $3 billion valuation, bringing total funding to $277 million. Existing investors Elad Gil, Evantic, First Round, General Catalyst and Redpoint participated, and Conviction and Headline joined as new investors. It is Serval’s third round in 12 months, following a $47 million Series A in October 2025 and a 
$75 million Series B
 in December 2025, which Reuters reported at a $1 billion valuation.

Enterprise service management is the system employees use to ask IT, HR, finance and legal for help, from new laptops to software access. Serval replaces tracking those requests with AI agents that resolve them. Its agent, Catalyst, turns business requirements into code-based workflows, so non-engineers in IT, HR, Finance, Security and Legal can build automations in plain language while admins manage guardrails, permission checks and approvals. The company likens it to giving every department the power of AI coding tools.

The company says annual recurring revenue has doubled every quarter since the Series B, and that it recently onboarded two Fortune 20 customers. Customers include Fox Corporation, WPP, Live Nation and Ramp, and most go live within 30 days. One auto manufacturer moved from ServiceNow, its main competitor, in 45 days, covering ITSM, CMDB, hardware and software asset management plus workflow automation.

New funds go to Catalyst and to ProServal, a professional services arm that will hire more than 100 Automation Engineers over 12 months and embed them in large enterprises. About half will be recent graduates trained in a four-week program. “Nobody has ten years of experience doing this, because two years ago the tools didn’t exist,” CEO 
Jake Stauch
 said. Serval grew headcount 10x in the past year and plans to add more than 500 people, with new offices in New York, London and Austin.

Competitive Landscape

ServiceNow is the long-time incumbent and agreed to buy enterprise AI agent company Moveworks for $2.85B. 
Glean
, the enterprise AI platform, raised $150M at a $7.2B valuation.

Where Serval Differs

Serval says departments can build their own automations without engineers, that its AI resolves requests rather than only tracking them, that it replaces the system of record to consolidate spend, and that its Automation Engineers shorten deployment. These are company claims; revenue was not disclosed.

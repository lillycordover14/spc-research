Wayne Johnson retiring as EMA director

Published 7:00 am Thursday, October 29, 2026

By 
Billy Hobbs

 

                                        Baldwin County EMA Director Wayne Johnson poses with Baldwin County District 1 Commissioner Emily C. Davis during his retirement luncheon on Friday. (Billy W. Hobbs/Staff)

Wayne Johnson says he has enjoyed his time serving as director of the Baldwin County Emergency Management/Homeland Security Agency, but it’s time to sail off into retirement.

Johnson began his public service career with Baldwin County Fire Rescue and has served as EMA director for nearly 11 years.

“I’m going to miss it, but it’s time for me to leave and turn it over to someone who will do a fine job,” Johnson said.

Mandy Ptak, longtime director of the Baldwin County Sheriff’s Office E-911 Center, has agreed to continue serving in a dual role as E-911 director and EMA director.

Ptak had nothing but kind words for Johnson and the legacy he leaves behind.

“Wayne Johnson is a good man, and there’s going to be some very big shoes to fill,” Ptak told The Union-Recorder during a surprise retirement luncheon at the E-911 Center on Friday, Oct. 23. “I certainly hope I can fill those shoes.”

For the past several weeks, Ptak said she has been training under Johnson.

“I’ve learned that people really rely on him because he’s so willing to help,” Ptak said. “He drops anything that he’s doing to help our neighboring counties. He goes the distance to deliver them whatever they might need during an emergency situation.”

For years, Ptak has helped thousands of people in the aftermath of tornadoes and hurricanes.

She didn’t do it in the field. She did it through E-911 and helped coordinate communications during such natural disasters.

“I’ve seen a whole lot of things, including wildfires,” Ptak said. “We’ve seen a wide array of things.”

When it comes to her dual roles, Ptak will get assistance from Haley Meeks, who was recently promoted to assistant E-911 director. She said she’s hoping the county will hire a new deputy EMA director to help her sometime in the near future.

Asked just how much Johnson will be missed as EMA director, Ptak said, “It will definitely take an Army to replace him. I’ll do my best, but I’m going to need a lot of help. He is very knowledgeable, does his homework, and is very involved in every aspect as EMA director. He’s going to be greatly, greatly missed and he’s very much appreciated.”

Ptak helped organize the surprise luncheon in honor of the man she will soon replace.

Baldwin County Fire Rescue Chief Phillip Adams said he first met Johnson when he assisted former Baldwin County Fire Rescue Chief Troy Reynolds with EMA.

“I got to know Wayne that way, and I learned a lot from him about our reporting systems and our weather software that we use,” Adams said. “He then went on to become EMA director under our former fire chief, Steve Somers and he’s been EMA director ever since.”

Adams said Johnson is a class act.

“He’s always available,” Adams said. “He keeps us informed about weather issues, etc. He’s just a good guy. And now Mandy will pick up the reins, and I don’t foresee us missing a beat.”

Baldwin County District 1 Commissioner Emily C. Davis said she thinks the world of Johnson.

“He’s always been smart and knows what he’s doing,” Davis said. “He’s very considerate of others. And he’s been very considerate of us as county commissioners. He always keeps us informed. He’s just been great.”

Davis said Baldwin County could not have been more blessed to have a person like Johnson serving as EMA director.

“Wayne is good people; he really is,” Davis said.
 

Davis presented Johnson with a framed certificate of recognition at last week’s luncheon.

It read, in part:
 

“To highlight your dedicated and exemplary service as Baldwin County’s Emergency Management Agency director; for your unwavering commitment to protecting lives, safeguarding property and strengthening the resilience of our community.

“Throughout your career, you have demonstrated exceptional leadership, professionalism and dedication in preparing for, responding to, recovering from and mitigating emergencies and disasters not only in Baldwin, but other counties in Georgia and across the Southeastern United States.”

Davis said she wanted to publicly wish Johnson continued success, happiness and fulfillment in his years ahead.

Johnson County Fire Chief and EMA Director Shawn Wombles considers Johnson one of his best friends.

The two men grew closer two years ago when Helene devastated parts of Johnson County and Wrightsville.

Wombles said he had enjoyed getting to know and working with Johnson for the last 10 to 15 years.

“When he became EMA director, we attended a lot of Area 3 meetings together and we just hit it off and became good friends,” Wombles said. “He’s a great guy. He is very professional and knows his job. Wayne is a caring person.”

Wombles said Johnson was one of the first EMA directors to assist him after Hurricane Helene struck Johnson County two years ago.

“My county was hit extremely hard, and for 72 hours, me and my deputy director had our hands completely full,” Wombles said.
 

Wombles said he called Johnson and sought his help.

“He came busting in the door about an hour after I made that phone call to him,” Wombles said. “That’s just the kind of person Wayne Johnson is … a great guy and friend.”

Johnson admitted that he would miss his job as EMA director, but said he looks forward to the future.

He will assume a similar role at Georgia College & State University, working part-time as the assistant EMA director under GCSU EMA Director Jeremy Nobles.

Johnson’s official retirement date with the county will be Oct. 30.

“I’m planning on taking a little time off, and we’ve planned a deer hunting trip to Bates County, Missouri next month,” Johnson said, noting it will be with his son, Hunter, and a couple of other good friends.

Johnson said having served as EMA director had allowed him the opportunity to network with a lot of people and to become lifelong friends with many of them.

“I’ve enjoyed this job; I really have,” Johnson said. “It’s been great helping people.”

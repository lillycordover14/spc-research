Shaping clay and creative minds

A passion for ceramics and a global sabbatical are helping Jarred Pfeiffer inspire the next generation of artists

Jarred Pfeiffer, associate professor of art at UWL, combines a passion for teaching and a lifelong curiosity about ceramics to help students discover their creative potential.

Posted 
9 a.m.
 
Thursday
, 
Oct.
 
29
, 
2026

Since 2019
, 
Jarred Pfeiffer﻿
 has inspired UW
-La Crosse art
 students through his enthusiasm for ceramics
 and his 
ded
i
c
a
tion to helping young artists grow.
 
 
Pfeiffer’s
 passion for 
his
 
craft is matched by the 
high expectations
 he sets in the classroom, where students consistently rise to the challenge and discover their own creative potential. That impact earned Pfeiffer a UWL Eagle Teaching Excellence Award last spring
 — a testament to the connection and creativity he brings to the classroom.
 
Now
, Pfeiffer is channeling that passion into a sabbatical that has taken him to artist residencies across the United States and Europe. We caught up with him to discuss his research, his journey into ceramics and the deep connection he feels to UWL.
 
Q: What do you most enjoy about teaching?
 
A:
 
There are countless aspects of teaching that I find deeply rewarding. 
Working with clay, in particular, presents a steep learning curve, especially for beginners.
 What I value most about teaching ceramics is 
witnessing
 the transformation that occurs when students commit time and effort to the process of progress. 
It’s
 especially meaningful to see those who initially struggle — such as with centering on the wheel in the introductory course — 
continue on
 to intermediate and advanced levels, where they gain confidence, develop their own voice and make remarkable improvements.
 
Q: 
You’ve
 stepped away from the classroom this year to go on 
sabbatical
. How are you spending this time?
 
A:
 
During my year-long sabbatical, I am 
participating
 in several national and international artist residencies. In September, I spent five weeks making work and researching French ceramics at A.I.R. 
Vallauris
 in 
Vallauris
, France, the town where Picasso created much of his ceramic work.

Pfeiffer is taking a year-long sabbatical to research and practice global traditions in ceramics — experiences he looks forward to sharing with this students.

Currently, I am serving as the Visiting Artist in Residence at San Diego State University. While at SDSU, I am continuing sculptural projects that I began in France, demonstrating to their classes and leading a firing of the university's soda kiln with advanced ceramics students.
In February, my family and I will head to Anderson Ranch in Snowmass Village, Colorado, for a five-week residency. There, I will 
exp
e
riment with the studio's 3D clay printer, combining wheel-thrown and hand-built forms with 3D-printed elements. The work will 
ultimately be
 fired in one of Anderson Ranch's wood kilns.
 
Following that residency, I will spend six weeks at the renowned 
Guldagergaard
 International Ceramic Research Center in Denmark. While there, I will create both sculptural and functional work with a focus on wood firing, 
utilizing
 several of the center's six wood-fired kilns.
 
Q: 
What do you appreciate about the opportunity to step back from teaching and focus on research this year? How has the 
College of Arts, Social Sciences & Humanities
 
supported
 your efforts?
 
A:
 
I love teaching, but after 18 years in the classroom, I am incredibly grateful for the opportunity to spend a year focused on my own creative work and research. This sabbatical has 
provi
ded
 a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity to 
participate
 in residencies that would have been difficult, if not impossible, to pursue while following an academic calendar.
What excites me most is the opportunity to bring everything I learn back to UWL and share those 
exp
e
riences with my students.
 
CASSH
 has been extremely supportive throughout this process. I 
benefited
 from examples of successful sabbatical applications, 
strong support
 from my department and department chair, and grant funding that has helped make many of these opportunities possible.
 
Q: 
Y
our father was a high school ceramics teacher. How did that help spark your own interest and career path in art?
 
A:
 
My father graduated from UWL with a degree in 
art education
. 
He discovered clay here, and I love that I now have the opportunity to spark that same joy in others at UWL.
 
Growing up, art
 was always a part of our lives. When friends came over to play, we often spent time in my dad's studio working with clay. Ceramics was something I loved, but I never 
con
sidered it a 
possible 
car
e
er
 path. It was simply a part of who I was.
 
I initially atten
ded
 UW
-
Madison as an engineering student, but I struggled with my math and science courses for the first time. At the same time, I continued taking ceramics classes just for fun. After changing my major three times and feeling increasingly uncertain about my direction, a friend asked me what I could see myself waking up and doing every day. My immediate response was, "I could see myself waking up and making pots every day."
 She replied, "
Congratulations
. You're an art major."
 
I changed my major to art and never looked back.

Though ceramics has been a constant throughout his life, Pfeiffer says he is still inspired by the creative potential of clay.

Q: 
What is it about ceramics that you particularly enjoy?
 
A
:
 
I could 
write
 a book in response to this question.
 
I love clay as a material, the countless processes used to transform it, and the community that surrounds it. More than anything, though, I am inspired by the incredible potential of clay. It exists virtually everywhere on Earth and is a material humans have worked with for tens of thousands of years.
 
Clay can become a porcelain teapot, an earthenware tile, a sculptural figure or 
almost anything
 else imaginable. Its possibilities are 
nearly limitless
. If I had to choose one thing that excites me most about ceramics, it would be that sense of endless potential.
 
Q: What are some of your interests outside of art and teaching?
 
A:
 
I love spending time with my wife, 
Erika,
 and our two-year-old son, Leo. 
I've
 always wanted to be a father, and it has been a joy watching Leo experience life's milestones and discover the world around him.
 
I also love 
to travel
. My parents instilled in me a curiosity about other places, cultures and perspectives, and that sense of exploration continues to shape my life today.
 
I'm
 a 
ded
i
c
a
ted Wisconsin sports fan and enjoy following the Packers, Badgers, 
Brewers
 and 
Bucks.
 I also love fantasy football and have served as commissioner of our home league for the past 16 years.
﻿

For Pfeiffer, one of the most rewarding aspects of teaching is watching student artists grow from one year to the next.

When possible, I enjoy being outdoors, whether 
that's
 hiking, playing disc golf or spending time on the water. But if 
I'm being
 honest, my favorite place to be is in the studio making work.
 
Q: 
What do you enjoy about the teaching and learning environment at UWL?
 
A:
 
UWL is a wonderful place to teach and create. I am grateful to be part of a community filled with talented colleagues who are passionate about both teaching and their own creative work. I look forward to seeing them in the 
a
rt 
b
uilding, at campus 
events
 and throughout the community.
 
The university administration has also been incredibly supportive of the Art Department and ceramics area, helping us build facilities and resources that are competitive with programs anywhere in the country. During my time at UWL, I have secured more than $100,000 in new and replacement equipment with support from the Dean's Office and Provost's Office.
 
Most of all, I enjoy working with our students. I hold 
high 
exp
e
c
t
a
t
i

 in the ceramics program, and our students 
con
sistently rise to the challenge. Watching

 progress from their first 
exp
e

I especially enjoy seeing students 
con
tinue their creative practice after graduation. Many of our students become art educators, and it is exciting to know they will go on to inspire the next generation of artists.

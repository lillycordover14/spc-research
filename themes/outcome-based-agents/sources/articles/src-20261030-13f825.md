Roxbury’s Olivia Benedetto Receives Adaptive Tennis Wheelchair Grant 
	

                    By The Wave                

on 

                    October 30, 2026                

By John Schilling
 

Share this:
Olivia Benedetto, an 18-year-old from Roxbury, attends Clemson University, where she competes in Division I wheelchair tennis. Wave File Photo

For Olivia Benedetto, the road back to an active lifestyle has come with plenty of challenges — and now, she’s getting a boost to keep doing what she loves.

Just recently, the 18-year-old Roxbury resident was awarded a grant through the Kelly Brush Foundation’s Active Fund program to help fund a specialized tennis wheelchair. Benedetto discovered wheelchair tennis after a skiing accident left her paralyzed from the waist down at age 13, and the sport has since helped her rediscover her competitive side.

The Kelly Brush Foundation, a Burlington, Vermont-based nonprofit, provides grants for adaptive sports equipment to people with spinal cord injuries, helping them stay active and connected to their communities. The organization has awarded more than 2000 grants across all 50 states since its founding in 2006.

Adaptive sports equipment can be expensive and is rarely covered by insurance, according to the foundation, leaving many people with mobility challenges without access to recreational opportunities. Through its Active Fund, the organization provides financial assistance and resources to help people with spinal cord injuries participate in sports and other physical activities.

“Everyone who reviewed Olivia’s grant application was incredibly impressed with her passion and excitement to be active and connect with their community through an active lifestyle,” said Kelly Brush, the foundation’s founder. “Recovering from a spinal cord injury is hard and requires a lot of prescriptive work under medical supervision. Adaptive sports help survivors break free from the monotony of medical care and physical therapy offices while finding joy – and healthy living – in activity by engaging with the world, moving their bodies, and spending time with family and friends. I’m so excited to see how this new equipment helps Olivia live a fulfilled, active life.”

For Benedetto, the grant marks another step in a journey that has taken her from recovering from a devastating injury to competing at the collegiate level.

As The Wave reported last year, Benedetto discovered wheelchair tennis after a local Rockaway family donated a tennis wheelchair and introduced her to the sport. Before her injury, she had been a gymnast and a member of the Scholars’ Academy cheer team.

Olivia Benedetto previously told The Wave that playing tennis has kept her “competitive” spirit alive. Wave File Photo

Although she initially struggled to adjust to a sport so different from gymnastics, she continued playing and eventually found a renewed sense of purpose through tennis.

“I love the sport; it keeps me active and brings back the competitive part of me I felt was taken away,” Benedetto previously told The Wave. “I like a challenge a lot, and I love the community and the people. I get to meet and play against people who are just like me in a wheelchair.”

Her dedication helped her earn a spot on Clemson University’s Division I wheelchair tennis team in South Carolina. She is currently attending Clemson, where she is pursuing a B.S. in Psychology and is expected to graduate in 2029.

Benedetto has previously spoken about the importance of finding new opportunities after a life-changing injury, encouraging others facing similar challenges to remain open to new experiences.

“The world doesn’t end when you get injured, and you can still do so much,” Benedetto previously told The Wave. “You do get through the hard times, and it’s not all darkness. There are still so many opportunities.”

And now with her new tennis wheelchair, Benedetto is ready to keep rolling toward new opportunities.

Samaya Says 3B-Active Model Tops Fable 5.1 at 11x Lower Cost&nbsp;&nbsp;FourWeekMBA

[page text]
Samaya, a 
startup
 building AI research agents for finance, said on 28 October 2026 that its new 
model
, Samaya Sterling, scores 58.9% on FrontierFinance at $0.61 per question, against 55.9% at $6.89 for Claude Fable 5.1, according to its 
blog post
 and its 
leaderboard page
. FrontierFinance is Samaya’s own benchmark.

Sterling is a post-trained version of NVIDIA’s open-source Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, a 30B mixture-of-experts 
model
 with 3B parameters active at a time. By our arithmetic, $6.89 divided by $0.61 is about 11.3 times, close to the “11× decrease in 
cost
” Samaya states.

What Samaya Reported

Samaya says Sterling “satisfies 58.9% of rubric items on the 220 public questions of FrontierFinance”, which it describes as “3.0 pp above Claude Fable 5.1”. It says training lifted the base 
model
 from 42.5% to 58.9%, and that the 
model
’s median number of tool calls per question rose from 3 to 17.

On speed, Samaya gives 282 seconds per question for Sterling against 268 seconds for Fable 5.1, which it calls “a similar latency”. By our arithmetic, Sterling is 14 seconds, or about 5%, slower per question. In a high-effort mode, Samaya says Sterling reaches 60.6%.

How It Was Built

Samaya says it trained the 
model
 with LoRA on Tinker, Thinking Machines Lab’s training API, in four stages: two supervised learning stages that learn from “eight diverse teachers”, then offline and online reinforcement learning. It reports scores of 51.4% after synthetic SFT, 54.8% after best-teacher SFT, 57.5% after offline GRPO and 58.9% after online GRPO.

The company says the 
model
 and its research harness were trained together: “we train and evaluate in the harness we serve”. It adds that early results with a larger 
model
 on the same recipe show more gains, to be shared in a future post.

What the $0.61 Covers

The two 
cost
 figures are not measured the same way. Samaya’s leaderboard says Sterling’s reported 
cost
 “includes the research 
model
, tools and helper models, with cached agent tokens”, while other systems’ 
cost
 calculations count only the agentic 
model
 
cost
 and exclude related costs such as the search API and data-index 
pricing

Samaya says that with its trained 
model
 “the research costs $0.04 per question”, against $0.56 to $0.95 for frontier closed models doing the same research in the same harness. By our arithmetic, the $0.04 is about 7% of Sterling’s $0.61; the rest, Samaya says, goes to tool calls and the helper models.

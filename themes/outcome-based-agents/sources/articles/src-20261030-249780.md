LISTEN: Lo Barnes’ “Heaven Was His Name” Builds a Slow Crescendo to Sublime Heights

			
				By 
Glide

Catharsis is carved into Lo Barnes’ terrain. The British self-producing artist treads a restless path between intimate folk, Western melodrama, and 60s-inflected rock ‘n’ roll. A captivating live performer, Barnes commands a pin-drop audience both solo and with her band. She has shared stages with artists including Jesse Jo Stark, Romeo of The Magic Numbers and The Libertines, while building a reputation for intensely immersive live shows of her own.

HEAVEN
 is a collection of three EPs, journeying from expansive disillusionment to raw feminine defiance, with the undiluted power of Barnes’ voice carving through self-produced arrangements. “Heaven Was His Name” is the second single, with 
Part I: The Veil
 due for release on 10th December 2026.

“Heaven Was His Name” opens with guitar and voice and slowly builds to a dramatic crescendo. Dramatic, but not histrionic. Lo Barnes’ voice can arrest a cop while he’s reading the Miranda warning. It is expansive in its restraint, the kind of voice that makes you stop what you’re doing to be sure you’re hearing it properly. Bradley Burgess plays a stoic lead guitar, and Joshua John Parker and Elias Gargallo Aguilella play bass and drums, respectively. In addition to singing, Barnes plays the rhythm guitar that carries the opening verses, the piano that solidifies the build on the outro, and arranges the strings that lead that outro to sublime heights.

HONOLULU (HawaiiNewsNow) - Honolulu Police Chief David Lazar defended staffing shifts that would add 100 more officers on patrol.
Lazar made the comments after a “talk story” session Thursday night at the Mission Memorial Auditorium. 
Residents said they liked the idea of having more patrol officers, but the chief said the decision was made to control the department’s overtime budget.
“We’re spending money that doesn’t belong to HPD, we’re spending so much of it,” Lazar told the gathering. “That was really another reason why we needed to put more officers back on patrol.”
The chief admitted that he had to adjust after an outcry that some of the officers would be taken away from popular youth programs.
“They said, hey, do not get rid of our Police Activities League program. We like our boxing program in Waianae and all these things. Don’t get rid of our Junior Police Officer program. And so the community’s voice matters to me,” Lazar told Hawaii News Now.
Some community members at the event wanted to know what the chief plans to do about ongoing homeless encampments, especially in the Iwilei area.
“Part of the homelessness or housing issue is the fact that IHS (Institute for Human Services) lives there, so people live there. The problems are associated because they are there,” said St. Louis Heights resident Dylan Davis.
“We have a place for services, (but) we can’t let encampments build up on sidewalks outside,” Lazar replied, adding that the department is building its homeless outreach.
Traffic issues also took center stage, especially excessive speeders.
“Every week I get somebody driving over 90, almost every week over a hundred,” said traffic defense attorney Patrick McPherson. “What can we do to make our roads safer so the people in this room don’t get killed?”
Lazar said right now, the department has only ten solo bike officers who have patrolled for speeders on Oahu’s freeways. McPherson wants to see more.
“The first thing you do when you see a cop? You either slow down, or at least check your speedometer to make sure you’re not doing something that you’re not supposed to do,” he said.
McPherson said there have been as many as 47 solo bike officers to enforce traffic laws. Lazar said he’s working to build that number back up.
“We’re working on sending four down to traffic to make that number to 14, but we have to get 40 to really, realistically have enough. And I even think we’ll go higher than that at some point,” Lazar said.

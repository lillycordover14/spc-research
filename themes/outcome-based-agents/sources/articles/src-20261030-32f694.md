For the first time, Encinitas mobile home parks now are covered by rent control, at least on a temporary basis.

The City Council unanimously approved a temporary, “urgency” measure Wednesday establishing limits on how much rent can increase each year at mobile home parks. Council members also agreed to start taking steps to make this measure permanent.

“This is something we should have addressed a long time ago,” Mayor Bruce Ehlers said, mentioning that mobile home parks provide much-needed, low-income housing in Encinitas.

Councilmember Jim O’Hara said he wasn’t generally a supporter of rent control, but this case was different because of the unusual building ownership situation.

Unlike apartment dwellers, mobile home park residents typically own their structures, but rent the land their mobile homes sit on. It’s very difficult, if not impossible, for residents of one mobile home park to relocate their structures into another park, so they feel forced to pay whatever land rental rate the park owners choose to charge, residents say.

The Riviera Mobile Home Park in Encinitas on Friday, October 30, 2026. (Hayne Palmour IV / For The San Diego Union-Tribune)

Encinitas has 11 mobile home parks containing about 729 spaces. Rent control came up as a city issue earlier this fall after some people who live at Riviera Mobile Home Park on Vulcan Avenue received notices from the park’s new owners saying that their land rental rates would increase by up to 17% starting in November. They mobilized and began appearing at city meetings to plead with council members to enact a rent control ordinance.

“I can’t tell you the tears of joy they had” when they heard this item was on Wednesday’s agenda, said Heidi Holzworth, a Riviera resident who organized the SOS Encinitas advocacy group.

The new city urgency ordinance, which required a super majority vote by the council, goes into effect immediately.
 

Council members also gave initial approval Wednesday to another temporary ordinance, which will supersede the urgency ordinance. It requires a second council vote in November and a 30-day waiting period before it can go into effect. It’s scheduled to expire on July 30, 2027. By that date, council members said, they hope to have enacted a permanent replacement.

Mike Vondle, a supporter of rent control, power washes the mobile home he’s owned since 2017 so that he can paint it, which Vondle says is one of several things he has to fix before he’s allowed to sell his mobile home at the Riviera Mobile Home Park in Encinitas on Friday, October 30, 2026. (Hayne Palmour IV / For The San Diego Union-Tribune)

Both of the two temporary measures allow rent to increase, but only once a year and only up to a set amount of either 4% or 75% of the area’s annual Consumer Price Index figure, depending on which number is lower. The current annual CPI rate is 4.7% for the San Diego region, the federal Bureau of Labor Statistics reports.

If park owners believe that they will not receive “a fair return” at this new rental rate limit, they have the option of petitioning the city for a higher increase, and the council will hold a hearing on the matter, a staff report states.

Details of what might be included in a permanent ordinance will be worked out starting early next year, council members and city staff said Wednesday. Council members cautioned that the permanent ordinance may have different regulations than the temporary ones.
 

“I kind of see this as a market correction,” Councilmember Luke Shaffer said as explained why he was willing to support the temporary measures. He added that he doesn’t want the city to create “a whiplash effect where we over-swing” toward too much control of rental rates.

Mentioning that he’s a business owner, Councilmember Marco San Antonio said he wants to “do right” by both park residents and park landowners.

“We have a lot to think about, a lot to talk about, a lot to learn” before enacting a permanent ordinance, he said.

Julie Pauli, a representative for the Western Manufactured Housing Communities Association, and Ken Johnston, whose family owns Green Valley Mobile Estates in Encinitas, both said that they were very concerned about the council’s decision and would be monitoring the city’s plans in the coming months. Johnston, who mentioned that he already keeps his rental rates low and ties them to the CPI rate, told the council that he thought the new ordinances were an unnecessary, additional regulatory burden.

“This issue is complicated and you shouldn’t rush into such a harsh ordinance,” he said as he mentioned that some parks contain both mobile homes and RVs, and the age of the structures varies greatly from one park to another.

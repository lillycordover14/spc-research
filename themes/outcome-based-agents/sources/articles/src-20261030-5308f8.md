Happen (NASDAQ: HAPN) Still Trades Below Historical Book Value Despite Sixfold Profit Growth Over Two Years&nbsp;&nbsp;Foreign Policy Journal

[page text]
Even after a 12% single-session rally, Happen (NASDAQ: HAPN), the lender formerly known as LendingClub, trades at roughly 1.2 times tangible book value, still under its five-year average of 1.3 times.

That discount persists despite net income growing approximately sixfold over two years, a trajectory that few consumer lenders can match in the current rate environment.

Thursday’s surge was triggered by third-quarter earnings per share of $0.50, clearing Wall Street’s $0.45 consensus by a meaningful margin and prompting the company to lift its full-year EPS guidance range to $1.87-$1.92, up from $1.80-$1.90.

Revenue, however, told a different story, coming in at $263.2 million against analyst expectations of $272.7 million, a shortfall the company attributes to a fair-value accounting transition implemented in January.

That shift moved credit costs on newly originated loans from the provision line into revenue, which mechanically suppresses the top line while improving reported credit metrics elsewhere in the income statement.

A $15.9 million provision benefit on loans the company still carries at cost contributed roughly one-fifth of Happen’s record $76.8 million in pre-tax profit, which investors should weigh when assessing the quality of the earnings beat.

That benefit is set to diminish as the legacy loan book, already down to $2.7 billion from $4.3 billion at the close of 2025, continues to shrink with each passing quarter.

Where the numbers are unambiguously positive is credit performance, with net charge-offs declining to 3.1% in the third quarter from 3.4% during the same period a year ago.

CEO Scott Sanborn pointed to that trend directly, stating that “we continue to outperform on credit,” a claim the charge-off data supports without qualification.

Happen also crossed $10 billion in loans distributed through its structured certificate program, indicating that institutional buyers remain willing to absorb its loan production at scale.

The competitive read-across matters here: SoFi Technologies (NASDAQ: SOFI) targets the same prime borrower segment and also marks loans at fair value, yet its return on tangible common equity stands near 10%, well below Happen’s 15.8%.

Upstart Holdings (NASDAQ: UPST) competes for many of the same loan buyers and reported 50% growth in second-quarter originations, raising the question of whether appetite for consumer credit assets is broad enough to sustain both platforms.

Happen’s guidance incorporates a December Federal Reserve rate increase, meaning any mark-to-market pressure on loan portfolios could weigh on both Happen and SoFi heading into year-end.

The arithmetic of the updated guidance is also worth examining: with $1.44 earned across the first nine months, the implied fourth-quarter range of $0.43-$0.48 represents a step down from the $0.50 just reported.

That sequential deceleration, combined with a shrinking provision tailwind, makes the next quarterly report the true measure of whether Happen’s earnings trajectory is structurally durable or partly accounting-assisted.

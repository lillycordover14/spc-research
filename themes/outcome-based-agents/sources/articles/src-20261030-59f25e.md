Clarissa Gilbert, a homeowner in Harper Valley, points to a crawl space, where ground water continued to seep in on Wednesday after devastating floods on Oct. 24. (Debra Mayeux/Tri-City Record)

A San Juan County Sheriff’s lieutenant put the lives and property of others first, as his home, his parents’ home and his grandmother’s house were lost in Harper Valley when floodwaters from the San Juan River raged through the neighborhood on Oct. 23.

Lt. Andrew Gilbert was working Oct. 22 when he received a notification that Bloomfield was beginning to flood, but said no one “was understanding the significance of what was coming our way.”

As the scale was growing on Oct. 23, he was trying to coordinate evacuations and responses in Wild Horse Valley as it filled with water and people rushed to free livestock and get out.

By the time he arrived in his neighborhood of Harper Valley, “floodwaters had taken everything,” he said, adding continued to focus on helping with evacuations. “I started helping people gather their property,” Gilbert said.

Gilbert even commandeered a boat to float an elderly man and his two dogs to safety.
 

“I was trying to help rescue people, and then you start realizing – your house – but life came first,” Gilbert said.

San Juan County Sheriff's Lt. Andrew Gilbert stands outside of his flooded home Wednesday in Harper Valley as San Juan County Sheriff-elect Ken Christesen talks to him about installing a new furnace. (Debra Mayeux/Tri-City Record)

While he was saving lives, his wife Clarissa Gilbert stayed in Harper Valley and filled sand bags.

“It was just eerie. We didn’t expect for it to get our house,” Clarissa said. “We went down and helped our neighbors, but when the water started rising, we thought we should get a bag and move the car.”

The water rose, but Clarissa stayed filling sandbags and giving them to her neighbors. Some of those sandbags were propped on the garage frame outside of her house, which sustained significant damage.

Families and volunteers filled sandbags in Harper Valley before floodwaters hit on Oct. 23, but the bags now sit next to the garage of a home that had 3-feet of standing water inside. (Debra Mayeux/Tri-City Record)

 

“It was sad. It was devastating,” she said.

The water filled the Gilbert’s crawl space, where their furnace and cooler were destroyed.

The floodwaters continued to rise filling the house with three feet of water on the ground floor, leaving the family with soggy floor boards and drywall and insulation that had to be removed.

“Right now we are trying to dry out as best as we can,” she said.

She shared that her father-in-law, who lives next door was in the hospital when the flood hit. “They didn’t get to see their home,” before the damage, Clarissa said.

Andrew noted that his 91-year-old grandmother, who lives around the corner had to be evacuated. “Our family lost three homes in this,” he said.
 

Despite the loss, there has been a tremendous outpouring of support.

San Juan County Sheriff-elect Ken Christesen “was one of the first people to show up to help,” Clarissa said.

“This whole family was devastated,” Christesen said. “They were doing their jobs while their houses were getting hammered.”

He and his friend Hector Rangel spent an entire day using a 2-inch pump to get the water out of the Gilbert’s home. They stripped the flooring, shoveled mud and helped move debris.

Rangel’s sons, Derrick and Chris Rangel, who own Hiwest Auto and Hi Mesa Towing, donated several trailers on which 35 to 40 loads of debris were hauled away to Rangel’s property, where it will be disposed of later.

Taco Bell donated food, Mesa Sand and Gravel showed up to help and A-1 Auto Salvage was out there helping too, Rangel said.
 

A pile of trash sits in front of a house in Harper Valley Wednesday, as families work to clean out the debris from flooded homes. (Debra Mayeux/Tri-City Record)

Because people need to be careful of the bacteria and hazardous waste left behind by the floodwaters, Christesen approached Home Depot to ask for heavy duty rubberized work gloves. A box of 400 was donated, and he bought the second box of 400 for half-price.

Lowes also has donated cleanup kits, which were being disbursed at a small stand in Harper Valley, where neighbors and other volunteers handed out food, beverages and personal protective gear.

David Powers, 13, volunteered by walking up and down the street with a wagon offering food and beverages to those working on their homes.

“It’s a privilege to help,” Powers said. “People lost stuff. I want to give back what I can.”

More than 2,000 homes are going to have to be rebuilt, and if the homes can be saved many will need new coolers, furnaces, duct work, drywall and flooring, Christesen said.

“People don’t know how bad it is,” he said, adding “nobody has flood insurance. They’re either paying out of pocket or putting it on a card.”

But the donations and the outpouring of support keeps coming. “It’s overwhelming to see how much good is in our community,” Rangel said.

Harper Valley is a “tight-knit neighborhood. You love them all,” Andrew said.

“All of these people are trying to help, but I’m used to being the one helping others,” Andrew said. “It’s hard to be on the other side, but I’m grateful for the help.”

Andrew would love to go out and help others devastated by the flooding. “Crazy how in one day everything changes,” he said.

                            Adaptive TEM delivers a context-aware threat briefing tailored to the airport, runway and approach                        

Threat and Error Management (TEM) has long been a cornerstone of flight safety. It asks crews to anticipate what could go wrong on a given flight, recognize errors early and manage them before they escalate. In practice, however, the information needed for a good TEM briefing is scattered across weather reports, NOTAMs, airport briefing pages, terrain charts, runway limitations and procedural documents. Bringing it together is largely manual work that depends on crew experience and the time available and some threats may not be identified until workload and time pressure are already high.

Airport Briefing, the digital briefing platform developed by Synapse Aviation, has now launched 
Adaptive TEM
, a data-driven evolution of traditional TEM designed to close that gap. The system continuously evaluates operational, environmental and procedural conditions and produces a context-aware threat briefing for each flight, helping crews identify threats, prioritize mitigations and adapt as conditions change.

As the company puts it: modern aviation does not lack data – it lacks operational clarity.

From fragmented data to a prioritized threat picture

Adaptive TEM draws on more than 300 million operational data points across airports worldwide, including FMS-grade aeronautical data from Lido’s SkyData infrastructure, and is kept current with AIRAC cycle updates. It analyzes weather, runway configuration, NOTAMs, terrain, airspace, approach characteristics and operational limitations, then organizes the resulting threats into categories such as Environment, Airport, ATC & Airspace, Operational, Aircraft and Human Factors.

Each threat is scored and ranked as a high, medium or low risk factor, together with suggested mitigations. For an arrival into Hong Kong International Airport, for example, the system flags the terrain-constrained ILS approach to Runway 25R as a high risk factor and prompts the crew to review terrain during the approach briefing and maintain strict ILS tracking. Lower-priority items, such as noise abatement procedures, curfew limitations and traffic flow restrictions, are listed separately, so crews can focus their attention where it matters most.

Operational threats for Hong Kong (VHHH), ranked by risk level in Adaptive TEM.

Deterministic logic, not generative AI

A key design choice behind Adaptive TEM is how its conclusions are produced. According to Airport Briefing, threat outputs are generated by structured operational logic, deterministic evaluation formulas and contextual operational rules – not by probabilistic or generative AI reasoning. Every output can be traced back to the individual inputs and the evaluation logic behind it.

AI is used only in back-office tasks such as content handling, NOTAM interpretation assistance and data management. Adaptive TEM is positioned as a decision-support tool: it extends the traditional TEM framework and does not replace pilot judgment, standard operating procedures or company decision-making.

Built for the whole operation

Adaptive TEM is designed to serve several groups within an airline or operator. For flight operations, it standardizes threat identification and briefing quality across fleets and routes. For safety management, it provides a measurable, structured evaluation of operational risk and verifiable records of crew briefing engagement. Training and standards departments can use it to expose crews to structured threat awareness that reflects real operational complexity.

For flight crews, the benefit is a prioritized threat picture for the airport, runway and approach under consideration – with less time spent interpreting fragmented information and better support for reassessment when conditions change, including during diversions and delays.

Easier access to support and resources

Alongside the launch, Airport Briefing has introduced an AI chat assistant that helps users and prospective customers find information about the platform more easily. For more in-depth guidance, the 
Airport Briefing Resource Center
 brings training videos, user manuals and frequently asked questions together in one place.

Availability

Adaptive TEM is available to airlines and aviation operators, which can 
request a demo
 through the Airport Briefing website. Applications from individual pilots are not accepted at this stage.

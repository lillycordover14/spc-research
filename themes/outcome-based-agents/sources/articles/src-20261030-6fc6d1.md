Mississippi State vs Texas prediction
This is the third straight season Mississippi State will play Texas since the 
Longhorns
 joined the SEC, so there is some familiarity between the teams. Quarterback Arch Manning will be making his third career start against MSU. Texas won the two previous games, most recently overcoming a 17-point deficit in the fourth quarter to beat MSU at Davis Wade Stadium last season.
The Longhorns have been an up-and-down team in 2026 with great wins against Ohio State, Tennessee and Ole Miss but also two unranked losses to Oklahoma and Florida. They can be slightly unpredictable week-to-week, but shouldn't have much trouble against Mississippi State's backup quarterback. The Longhorns are considered a heavy favorite.
Look for Mississippi State to try to muck up the game, similar to what it did in 2024 at Texas when freshman quarterback Michael Van Buren Jr. made his first career start. Mississippi State's path to victory likely comes from slowing the game down, which is the opposite of what coach Jeff Lebby's offense likes to do, and taking advantage of a couple turnovers.
Mississippi State vs Texas scouting report
Why Mississippi State has an advantage:
 The element of uncertainty could be an advantage for Mississippi State, since there are two possibilities at starting quarterback. Swann and Puckett are expected to be significant dropoffs from Taylor, though.

Fluff Bothwell has been excellent at running back and will be leaned on heavily. He's averaging 102.6 rushing yards per game excluding the Alabama game where he played one series before exiting. Mississippi State will need to scheme ways to get the ball to Anthony Evans III, who is second in the SEC with 92.4 receiving yards per game.
Texas will be without standout left tackle Trevor Goosby, who reportedly suffered a season-ending injury in the last game against Ole Miss. His replacement, Andre Cojoe, allowed a strip sack that was recovered in the end zone for an Ole Miss touchdown. Perhaps that's an area MSU can exploit.
Why Texas has an advantage:
 Avoiding turnovers could be all Texas needs because it will be challenging for Mississippi State's offense to keep up.
Manning has been scrutinized for not quite living up to his ranking as the No. 1 player in the 2023 recruiting class, but against Ole Miss had 212 passing yards, one passing touchdown, two interceptions and two rushing touchdowns. Running back Raleek Brown had 157 rushing yards and is 11th in the SEC in total rushing yards. Cam Coleman, Ryan Wingo and Emmett Mosley V are Texas' standout wide receivers.
Colin Simmons leads the SEC with 10.5 sacks but was listed as questionable on the initial SEC availability report.

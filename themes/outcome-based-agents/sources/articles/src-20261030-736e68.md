Enthusiastic, but early - users react to SAP’s Business AI strategy

To co-incide with TechEd in Berlin, the UK & Ireland SAP User Group (UKISUG) has released a new study suggesting strong enthusiasm for SAP's Business AI strategy, despite concerns over the complexity of licensing it.

The study of 460 UKISUG members finds that nearly nine in 10 SAP users (87%) see the company’s Business AI offering enhancing their organizations’ use of SAP. The same percentage worries that SAP’s licensing has become more complex since the introduction of SAP BTP and its latest AI offerings.

But the good news for SAP is that it’s still early days and attitudes may change. To date, only three percent of respondents are currently using SAP Business AI, although two thirds (67%) plan to do so in the future.

Overall familiarity with SAP's AI portfolio also remains mixed, with fewer than half (43%) of respondents saying they are familiar with the products and services.  But 56% say that SAP will play an important role when it comes to deploying Generative AI across their organization, while 53% reckon the vendor will play an important role when it comes to deploying Agentic AI across their organization.

Conor Riordan, Chair of UKISUG, states:

Over the past year, we have seen a significant increase in interest in SAP's AI capabilities. The focus is now on moving from experimentation to scaled adoption, where value can be clearly demonstrated and commercial models are easy to understand. Customers need greater transparency around AI pricing, licensing, credits and consumption so they can make informed investment decisions. As organisations increasingly evaluate the business case for AI, simplicity and predictability in licensing will be critical to accelerating adoption and maximising value from SAP's innovations."

The winners of the AI era will not simply be the organisations that adopt the technology first, but those that understand how to apply it effectively. SAP sits at the heart of many organisations' critical business processes and data, which means its AI strategy will have a significant impact on how customers adopt and scale AI in the years ahead. As the landscape continues to evolve, it is essential that customers have access to clear, independent guidance to help them understand both the opportunities and the practical implications for their organisations. Our research shows a clear demand for guidance, education and practical insights.

Delivering the future of work - the agentic way at ADP

I wake up every single morning thinking about our clients, thinking about the world of work, how it's evolving.

So says Maria Black, CEO of HCM specialist ADP. She’s not alone here, she goes on:

Work is one of the most important parts of the world, candidly. It's the most important part of people's lives outside of their families. It's their craft, it's their purpose, how work is organized, how it's compensated, how it evolves at this juncture alongside technology. These are questions that ADPers wake up every single day thinking about and every single day answering for our questions.

ADP has some structural advantages in tackling this issue, she pitches beginning with its industry-leading data:

We know that AI is only as good as the data it's trained on. With 1.1 million clients supporting over 42 million workers across roles, industries and geographies, we have one of the largest sources of real-time workforce data in the world, providing unparalleled insights into the workforce and its emerging trends. This unique perspective means we have a new responsibility in the AI era, not only to design HR and Payroll solutions for people, but to help design the best possible future of work with people at the center.

We are also using our data to fuel best-in-class innovation. Our massive global workforce data platform and decades of expertise have been optimized into an intelligence foundation that powers our AI capabilities at scale with security, privacy and compliance built in from the ground up.

The second structural advantage is the firm’s brand trust and responsible approach to AI, she argues:

ADP is actively building across the full life cycle of AI in HCM from AI that assists people with tasks to AI that facilitates an entire function end-to-end with human oversight to AI that orchestrates work across domains and systems to support enterprise transformation. ADP is advancing and embedding each AI use case in our products and in how our teams operate. A perfect example of this is ADP Assist, which is becoming more deeply embedded into workflows and driving measurable impact with 100,000 more clients coming on since Q4. Since the fourth quarter alone, we have added 100,000 more clients on ADP Assist.

ADP now has close to 50 agents in general availability, says Black:

I think all of these agents are making real impact. And that's really our guidepost...It's still early days, but I'm really pleased sitting here. It's almost hard to believe it's been four years since this entire conversation started, but I think we're at the point now where we're seeing measurable progress, again, 50 agents generally available with more to come and many of these being significant needle movers that really create values-based outcomes for our clients.

The final structural advantage she cites is 77 years of deep domain expertise, she concludes:

What we are sharing today is a journey that's gaining momentum. Adoption is growing, and client value is becoming more tangible. That value is the foundation of the financial story we're building. And as we continue to scale our AI tools across the business, we expect that story to come into sharper focus. There is a lot of conversation right now about what AI means for work. Some of it is speculative. But what we know from serving more than 1 million businesses and tens of millions of people they employ is that managing a workforce is far more complex than it appears from the outside.

A transformative update from WPP 

Transformation at scale isn't easy and the benefits do not materialize overnight, according to Cindy Rose Quackenbush, Group CEO at media conglomerate WPP:

It takes time and progress may not always be linear.

But WPP is plowing on with its own transformation for the AI era, she says. Last month, it opened Devon's Point, a state-of-the-art AI-powered global flagship production facility in East London to offer clients large-scale virtual production sets, dedicated creator facilities, such as live streaming studios, rapid shoot and edit facilities, collaboration spaces and multiple AI-enabled post-production technology suites.

The firm is also building out its partner ecosystem, with 50 additional data partners on board with WPP Open Intelligence. Quackenbush says:

This takes the total number of data partners to around 400, further strengthening the open differentiated ecosystem that is central to our long-term data strategy and a source of competitive advantage for our clients.

We also expanded our strategic partnership with Kyndryl to accelerate enterprise AI transformation and bring new innovations to our shared clients. As part of this partnership, WPP was named Kyndryl's Global Media and Marketing Partner, delivering a broad portfolio of integrated services powered by WPP Open, our agentic marketing platform.

Based on her own experience of spending time with clients every day, she adds:

What I can tell you is that they're responding very positively to the changes that we're making. Why? Because we're showing up as one unified company, and we're able to present the best of WPP, not hundreds of different agencies. So we can offer our clients the right talent to the right client at the right time without historical agency constraints. And now that the vast majority of our pitches are integrated, the old WPP just wasn't set up to keep pace with the evolving needs of clients in that way, and now we are.

The UK NHS prescribes Netcompany digital services

Netcompany was recently named as a digital delivery partner for NHS England's adult screening programs in a five year contract valued at £250 million. Part of the NHS Digital Prevention Services Programme (DPSP)The contract covers seven national adult screening programs, including breast, cervical and bowel cancers, covering around 15 million invitations a year. The objective is earlier diagnosis and faster treatment

The services will be UK sovereign and NHS-owned. Netcompany will run the digital services behind the existing screening pathways while building the next generation of solutions. CEO André Rogaczewski explains:

These programs reach millions of people across England every year, making this a generally mission-critical partnership. Beyond operating and maintaining existing screening services, we are also driving next-generation innovation in this contract. We'll be delivering a new national breast screening service and an enhanced cervical screening service that includes digital invitations and at-home HPV self-testing kits.

But these aren't just technological upgrades. They represent a shift towards a more personalized data-driven approach that specifically targets communities that screening has historically been underserved with. The ultimate goal is clear and compelling, find diseases earlier, reduce the time between invitation and result and increase uptake through more accessible and targeted screening pathways.

This contract reinforces Netcompany's track record in delivering mission-critical health care services for the NHS and demonstrates our capability to lead complex, high-impact digital programs at a very high scale.

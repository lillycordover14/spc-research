For years, Adaptive Sports Program New Mexico based its operations at the Santa Fe ski basin in a building known as the “old shed.”
The organization, which offers year-round recreational activities — including skiing and snowboarding — to people with disabilities, had long since outgrown the shed’s 300 square feet, Executive Director Camille Romero said.
Even worse, the tiny building had steps, rendering it inaccessible to athletes who use wheelchairs and some other mobility aids. That’s not a good look for an adaptive sports program that’s “all about accessibility and inclusivity,” Romero said.
Ahead of this year’s ski season and after more than a decade of planning and fundraising, Adaptive Sports Program New Mexico opened a new facility at the Santa Fe ski basin nearly three times the size of the old shed and accessible to everyone.
“What it means for the program is basically a more welcoming and accessible experience for our athletes, which is really the heart of the work that we do,” Romero said.

Adaptive Sports Program New Mexico’s new facility at Ski Santa Fe stores sit skis, ski bikes, outriggers, tethers, snowboard rider bars, sliders and other adaptive gear that offers therapeutic recreational activities to children and adults with disabilities, such as skiing, snowboarding, watersports, river rafting, archery and rock climbing.

Nathan Burton/The New Mexican

Adaptive Sports Program New Mexico got its start in 1985 as an organization with the equipment, volunteers and know-how to adjust skiing and snowboarding to meet the needs of disabled athletes.
Vince Marchi volunteered to join the organization’s board nearly two decades ago and has been board president for most of that time. An avid skier, cyclist and scuba diver, he said joining the program was “a natural thing.”
During Marchi’s tenure, the organization has expanded its scope from skiing and snowboarding to a larger variety of adaptive sports, including rock climbing, kayaking, paddleboarding, river rafting, archery and fly fishing.

“Just because the snow melts doesn’t mean that the need isn’t there,” Marchi said.
Today, Adaptive Sports Program operates year-round with the help of more than 250 volunteers, serving athletes ages 5 and up. Its winter activities occur at Ski Santa Fe, Pajarito Mountain Ski Area in Los Alamos and Sandia Peak Ski Area outside of Albuquerque, though Santa Fe represents the organization’s largest program.
“It's really been powerful for me to see it grow over the years,” Marchi said. “We give as many lessons in a week now that we used to give all season back in the day when we were just a ski operation.”

Camille Romero, executive director of Adaptive Sports Program New Mexico, stands in the bay door of the program’s the new facility at the Ski Santa Fe resort Oct. 23.

Nathan Burton/The New Mexican

The idea for a new facility at the Santa Fe ski basin has been among the organization’s goals for more than a decade. After getting its start as board meeting talk in late 2014, the project turned into blueprints and the launch of a fundraising campaign in 2015, and eventually into permitting applications and construction plans, Marchi recounted.
Part of the organization’s fundraising efforts included securing a nearly $100,000 grant from the New Mexico Outdoor Recreation Division, which Romero called the “linchpin" to making the new facility a reality. Records from the agency show the Adaptive Sports Program was awarded a $99,999 Outdoor Recreation Trails+ grant in 2024 to help construct an “inclusive center” for skiers and snowboarders.
In addition to providing a welcoming space for everyone — including wheelchair users — the new facility is expected to be a point of pride for athletes, staff and volunteers, said Alex Grunstein, a wheelchair user and former participant who now coordinates Adaptive Sports Program New Mexico’s development and marketing efforts.
“It shows ASP's commitment to making sure that our adaptive athletes feel just like all of our counterparts, and that we get the same resources and types of facilities that everyone else gets to experience,” he said.

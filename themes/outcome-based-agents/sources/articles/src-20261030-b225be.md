SILICON VALLEY — U.S. technology companies are still posting revenue growth on the back of the artificial intelligence boom, but deteriorating cash flow is unsettling investors. The companies say they cannot halt large-scale spending if they want to secure an early lead in the AI market, yet earnings uncertainty is rising as the core businesses that have served as their cash cows begin to wobble.
Amazon said in a filing on the 29th that third-quarter revenue rose 12% from a year earlier to $201.94 billion, or about 270 trillion won. That fell short of the $202.31 billion consensus compiled by the London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG). Net profit slipped to $20.7 billion from $21.2 billion a year earlier.
Amazon Web Services, the company's cloud unit, posted $46 billion, up 39% from a year earlier and above the $45.61 billion expected by analysts. It was the sharpest increase since the fourth quarter of 2021 and the sixth straight quarter of growth, driven by demand tied to the AI development boom.
The strong cloud performance was overshadowed, however, by the $220 billion a year Amazon is pouring into AI. The company spent $77.3 billion in the third quarter on data center sites and equipment, pushing free cash flow to negative $42.4 billion. That marked a second consecutive quarterly deficit after negative $7.6 billion in the second quarter, with the shortfall widening sixfold in three months. Dave Wagner, portfolio manager at Aptus Capital Advisors, said free cash flow was the worst part of the report, noting that operating activities generated plenty of cash but capital spending consumed it entirely.
The bigger problem is that e-commerce, Amazon's main business and cash cow, is also faltering. Online store revenue rose just 2% to $68.5 billion, the slowest growth since early 2023, a sign that online market conditions are no longer as favorable as they were in the period right after the COVID-19 pandemic. Revenue growth at physical stores was also limited to 2%. Amazon attributed the slowdown to moving Prime Day, its biggest annual discount event, from the third quarter to the second.
The fourth-quarter outlook is weak as well. Amazon projected revenue of $240 billion to $247 billion. Set against an average analyst estimate of $244.51 billion, the guidance was a disappointment. Despite the fourth quarter being the peak season with Thanksgiving and Christmas, year-end spending is expected to fall short of expectations, with analysts pointing to consumers tightening their wallets as oil prices and inflation surge amid the prolonged war in Iran and the effects of interest rate increases.
Google, Amazon's biggest rival in cloud computing, is grappling with both worsening cash flow and trouble in its own cash cow business. The company reported negative free cash flow of $5.9 billion in the second quarter, its first such shortfall since going public, followed by a $9.07 billion deficit in the third quarter. Google's search revenue rose only 12%, missing market expectations. As investor disappointment mounted, parent company Alphabet lost $500 billion in market value in a single day.
U.S. memory chipmaker SanDisk is in a similar position. Rising demand for solid-state drives used in AI servers drove revenue up 378% from a year earlier in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, covering July to September 2026, and operating profit jumped 49-fold. Free cash flow, however, came in at $6.309 billion, below market expectations.
Intel, which had been reaping sizable profits from server central processing units as AI agents spread, has also run into difficulty. Its data center and foundry businesses are growing rapidly, but its core PC business is in crisis. Revenue at the data center and AI division rose 78% and the foundry division 58%, while the client computing group, which makes PC chips, saw revenue fall 5%.
The weakness in Intel's foundational PC business stems from a memory shortage. As the three largest memory makers — Samsung Electronics (005930.KS), SK hynix (000660.KS) and Micron — shifted production lines toward high-bandwidth memory to meet AI demand, PC memory became harder to secure. PC manufacturers were hit, and demand for PC processors plunged. Intel expects the PC market slump to last at least through the first half of 2027. Chief Financial Officer David Zinsner said funding for the ramp-up of the company's new Intel 3 and 18A manufacturing processes and preparations for 14A mass production in 2028 would make it difficult for free cash flow to turn positive next year.

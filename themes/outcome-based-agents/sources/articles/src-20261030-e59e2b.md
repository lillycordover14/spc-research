Democrat Consuelo Redhorse has raised nearly six times as much money in campaign contributions as Republican challenger Miguel Martinez in the race for Colorado House District 13, giving her significant financial advantage heading into Election Day. 

The 
two candidates are vying
 in the Nov. 3 election to succeed House Speaker Julie McCluskie, a Dillon Democrat nearing the end of her term limits. House District 13 encompasses Chaffee, Grand, Jackson, Lake, Park and Summit counties. 

Redhorse has collected $43,730 in contributions, compared with $7,505 for Martinez, according to campaign finance disclosures filed with the Colorado Secretary of State’s Office. The totals include fundraising during the Democratic primary, which 
Redhorse won narrowly in June
.

Martinez has also loaned $9,000 of his own money to his campaign, accounting for more than half of its combined contributions and loan proceeds. Redhorse’s campaign, meanwhile, has reported just $373 in loans. 

According to the latest campaign finance reports filed before Oct. 19, Redhorse had approximately $35,794 on hand, compared with $8,756 for Martinez — giving the Democratic school board president roughly four times as much money available for the 
final weeks of campaigning
.

Redhorse has spent approximately $11,412 over the course of her campaign, while Martinez has reported about $7,749 in expenditures, according to state election finance records.

Redhorse said fundraising has been “night and day” since her primary victory, describing a shift from a largely grassroots campaign to one receiving consistent financial support. She attributed some of that change to the legislative connections she’s made since securing the nomination, as Democrats seek to expand their majority in the House. 

“There’s a really big push to aim for a supermajority,” Redhorse said, explaining that Democratic leadership political action groups, or PACs, have pledged monetary support toward that goal.

Redhorse, who serves as president of the Summit School District Board of Education and works for a property management company in Frisco, has received dozens of contributions from individual donors, political action committees and organizations representing a range of political and professional interests. 

Among her largest reported contributors are the 
Apartment Association of Metro Denver’s small donor committee
, which gave $3,100; 
Colorado Wins’ small donor committee
 and the 
Colorado Trial Lawyers Association’s small donor committee
, which each contributed $1,000; and 
One Colorado’s PAC
 and small donor committee, which contributed a combined $1,150. 

Other contributors include political committees representing teachers, healthcare professionals, environmental interests and reproductive rights advocacy organizations, along with leadership PACs associated with Democratic state lawmakers. Redhorse has also received $500 from the Park County Democratic Central Committee and contributions from individual donors across the House district and local elected officials. 

Redhorse said she appreciates the additional financial support but makes clear to contributors that “their donation is not going to equate to them buying influence.”

Her campaign expenditures have included yard signs, flyers, social media advertising and more than $2,200 in radio advertising with Krystal 93.

Martinez, a former Lake County assessor, has relied more heavily on personal financing, although his campaign has also received support from a handful of individual donors and county Republicans organizations. 

In the race for Colorado House District 13, Miguel Martinez of Salida and Consuelo Redhorse of Silverthorne discussed issues of affordability, public safety, state budget issues and more. Martinez said he believes automated license plate readers pose serious privacy concerns, while Redhorse said communities should suspend use of the technology before better understanding its impacts.

Martinez has also contributed $2,980 directly to his campaign, in addition to $9,000 he’s reported in loans. His expenditures have included advertising and personal materials, with $810 in spending reported during the most recent filing period.

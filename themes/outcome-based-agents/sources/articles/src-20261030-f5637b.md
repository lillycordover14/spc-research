                On October 30, 1978, the Uganda Army launched a full-scale invasion of Tanzania, crossing the southern border into the Kagera Salient, a 710-square-mile tract of Tanzanian territory north of the Kagera River.
            

                
The offensive marked a decisive escalation in the conflict between Ugandan President Idi Amin and Tanzanian President Julius Nyerere, triggering a war that would ultimately bring an end to Amin’s eight-year rule.
            

                
The invasion followed years of hostility between the two countries. Tensions escalated after Amin seized power in Uganda in January 1971, overthrowing President Milton Obote. Nyerere refused to recognise Amin’s government, granted Obote asylum in Tanzania and allowed Ugandan dissidents to operate from Tanzanian territory.
            

                
An unsuccessful attempt by Obote loyalists to invade Uganda from Tanzania in 1972 further deepened the animosity between the two leaders.
            

                
By 1978, Amin’s government was facing mounting political and economic difficulties. An assassination attempt on Vice President Mustafa Adrisi triggered unrest within the Uganda Army, with mutinous soldiers fleeing towards the Tanzanian border. The crisis heightened fears of an internal coup and contributed to the growing instability of Amin’s regime.
            

                
Military accounts also point to a border confrontation near Mutukula in early October 1978 involving Lieutenant Colonel Juma Butabika and the Suicide Battalion. The incident escalated into clashes between Ugandan and Tanzanian forces, setting the stage for the full-scale offensive.
            

                
Ugandan troops had conducted preliminary assaults on October 9, while another attack on October 25 was repulsed. Five days later, between 2,000 and 3,000 Ugandan soldiers, supported by armour, artillery and air power, crossed into the Kagera Salient. The lightly armed Tanzanian garrison was overwhelmed, forcing its troops to retreat south of the Kagera River.
            

                
The invading forces occupied the territory, destroying infrastructure, looting livestock and machinery, and killing hundreds of Tanzanian civilians. The destruction of the Kyaka Bridge over the Kagera River further disrupted movement and communication in the area.
            

                
On November 1, 1978, Amin declared the annexation of the Kagera Salient, claiming that the Kagera River had become the new boundary between Uganda and Tanzania. The declaration transformed the invasion into an overt territorial claim and provided Nyerere with a clear justification for a military response.
            

                
Nyerere declared war on November 2, vowing to defend Tanzania’s territorial integrity. The Tanzania People’s Defence Force (TPDF) mobilised tens of thousands of troops, supported by Ugandan anti-Amin groups, including the Front for National Salvation (FRONASA) and Kikosi Maalum.
            

                
By January 1979, Tanzanian forces had cleared Ugandan troops from the Kagera Salient and launched a counter-offensive into Uganda. The campaign continued through the capture of Entebbe and Kampala in April 1979, forcing Amin to flee into exile and ending his presidency.

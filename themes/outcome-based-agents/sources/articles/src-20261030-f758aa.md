Natural gas flows on El Paso Natural Gas (EPNG) have tightened after the pipeline declared force majeures at its Caprock, Roswell and Lincoln compressor stations on Tuesday, two days after planned maintenance zeroed capacity at Caprock N between Oct. 20 and Sunday. Capacity fell to 180 MMcf/d, from 616.8 MMcf/d, before EPNG restored it to about 552 MMcf/d on Thursday. San Juan Triangle (SJ TRI) throughput averaged about 1,298 MMcf/d during the planned maintenance, compared with 983 MMcf/d in September. Flows stood at 1,063 MMcf/d on Wednesday before falling to 871 MMcf/d Thursday, according to 
NGI’s Entropic Analytics
 flow data.

During the planned outage, 
El Paso San Juan
 spot prices surged 70.5 cents as Caprock N went offline and retreated 39.0 cents when flows returned, with the same pattern playing out on Wednesday and Thursday. El Paso San Juan, 
El Paso Bondad
 and 
Transwestern San Juan
 rose about 40 cents Wednesday to $2.305/MMBtu, $2.270 and $2.320, respectively, then retreated by an average 32.2 cents on Thursday as Caprock capacity returned. 
Waha
 also fell 23.0 cents to $1.270, keeping San Juan’s premium elevated at 71.5 cents.
Meanwhile, a third-party line strike downstream of Waha on Wednesday cut a Line 1600 segment that had averaged about 555 MMcf/d throughout October to almost zero by Thursday. According to an EPNG notice, the repair would require pipeline replacement. Even so, westbound throughput flows at Cornudas averaged just under 2,000 MMcf/d on Thursday, near its October average, as scheduled flows shifted to other Permian segments.
November offers little relief, as EPNG had already planned work on the damaged section. EPNG’s updated schedule carries the force majeure through Nov. 5, then moves straight into planned remediation from Nov. 6–11 with L1600EST, WA CORN and the Blake Draw and Imperial points cut to zero. The remediation was shortened from an original end date of Nov. 19. WA2CORN stays capped near 420 MMcf/d all month, and the North Mainline remains limited to 62% of capacity.
NGI’s Forward Look
 treated the Caprock squeeze as temporary and priced little westbound strain from Line 1600. El Paso San Juan’s November premium to Waha for Thursday trading narrowed to 39.4 cents from 52.6 cents a week earlier, about half the cash premium. 
SoCal Border - Ehrenberg
, which sits at the end of the South Mainline path that Line 1600 feeds, saw its November premium to Waha slip 3.1 cents on the day to $1.322, while 
Waha
 November basis to Henry Hub held steady at minus $1.313.

Edison Scientific Inks AI Deal with Pfizer
 

		The pact spans Pfizer’s biopharma R&D pipeline, uniting Edison’s AI capabilities with Pfizer’s data repositories.	

October 30, 2026
 

By: 
Patrick Lavery

Content Marketing Editor
 

Save

Editor’s Take: Kosmos, Edison’s AI scientist, learns continuously, providing Pfizer with certain advantages.

Edison Scientific
 will deploy its AI platform and Kosmos, the company’s AI scientist, in a new collaboration with 
Pfizer
.

The deal, spanning Pfizer’s biopharmaceutical R&D pipeline, unites Edison’s AI capabilities with Pfizer’s robust expertise and data repositories. These include the areas of oncology, internal medicine, and immunology, to name a few.

How Edison Tech Will Work

Under this arrangement, Kosmos will access Pfizer’s data to support its scientists, inform critical R&D decisions, and accelerate drug discovery. Kosmos possesses the ability to learn continuously, which will provide Pfizer with advantages in compounding and proprietary intelligence.

From there, the AI scientist will build integrated world models by combining access to two different channels of data sources. These would be data already publicly available, and proprietary from Pfizer’s internal drug development endeavors.

Edison says it has “forward-deployed” scientists and engineers who will work with Pfizer research teams. This serves to build Kosmos into specific workflows, helping shape how research gets organized, and decisions get made.

Impact of Pfizer Knowledge

“Pfizer has spent decades generating data about what works and what doesn’t work,” Edison CEO Sam Rodriques, PhD, said. “That experience serves as the foundation for Kosmos, ensuring lessons from each program are maximized to inform other programs. Every experiment and clinical readout strengthen[s] the entire system. At Pfizer’s scale, the opportunity to turn accumulated scientific experience into better decisions is extraordinary.”

Earlier in 2026, Edison Scientific 
reached a similar deal
 with Incyte to deploy Kosmos. The goal of this partnership is, in part, to generate predictive models of therapeutic performance.

Edison spun out of FutureHouse, a nonprofit research lab, in 2025, backed by $70 million in funding.

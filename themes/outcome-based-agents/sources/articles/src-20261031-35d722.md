In the Driveway and at the Pop-Up

Our testers used the PowerSmart for a weekend of camping, to power a friend's small food truck pop-up, and in a simulated emergency backup test in the driveway. Across those they alternated a house refrigerator and a chest freezer, charged phones, ran camp lights and ran an electric kettle.

It handled the compressor start of the fridge surprisingly well for a budget unit. The kettle was the problem: with the fridge running, switching it on made the generator bog down hard, the overload light flicker, and it struggled to recover.

The listing claims 59 dBA. On a UNI-T UT353 at 23 feet at a quarter load it read exactly 60. Past 50% load it gets noticeably clattery and climbs into the high 60s, and you have to speak up to be heard next to it.

The 1.06-gallon tank lasted exactly 5 hours keeping the household fridge and freezer cycling. The listing claims 7.5 hours, which you only reach with Eco mode on and nothing to do but charge laptops. On a scale it weighed 40.5 lb with oil, the lightest of our newest three.

Where It Asks Something of You

A kettle on top of a fridge was too much. Keep it to the fridge, a freezer and small chargers, and don't switch on a heating element.

It floods easily. The cold start is the hard part, and it's easy to flood the engine if you are careless with the choke.

The oil fill is awkwardly angled and messy, so use a funnel and don't overfill.

It has no 30 amp outlet, so you are restricted to standard 20 amp household plugs. That rules out an RV hookup.

Spec Sheet vs Our Test

1,900 W running, 2,500 W surge.
 The listing rates it at 1,900 W continuous and 2,500 W at the peak. It handled the fridge compressor start, with the chest freezer alternated, and it overloaded when a kettle was added, which puts a heating element on top of a compressor. The rating is a fridge-and-chargers rating in practice, and the kettle shows where it ends.

59 dBA claimed.
 The UT353 read 60 at 23 feet at a quarter load, one above the claim. Past half load it clattered and climbed into the high 60s, so the figure on the listing is a quiet-load figure and a loaded generator at a campsite is loud enough that you speak up.

7.5 hours claimed.
 The listing quotes 7.5 hours for the 1.06-gallon tank. With a fridge and a freezer cycling it lasted 5 hours, so the claim only holds with Eco mode on and a laptop-charger load. For a storm, plan on refuelling about every 5 hours.

Outlets.
 The listing shows a 120V 20A duplex, a 12V port, two USB ports and Eco mode. Our testers' point is the outlet that's missing: there's no 30 amp outlet, so it's a household-plug unit and can't feed a camper.

Warranty and support.
 The listing offers a 2-year limited warranty with free lifetime technical support. We haven't used either, and for a budget unit that's the one thing in the listing we would want to test before leaning on it in an emergency.

Should You Buy the PowerSmart 2500?

Buy the PowerSmart 2500 as a cheap insurance policy for a homeowner who wants to keep a fridge from spoiling during a storm. At 40.5 lb and 1,900 running watts it's a light, budget way into a generator that starts a fridge compressor.

Avoid it for daily site work or RV boondocking. For a quieter unit that starts on the first pull, the 
Honda EU2200i
 is the one, and for a camper or house on gas or propane the 
Westinghouse iGen2800DFc
 does both jobs better.

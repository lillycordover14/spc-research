Clay Fuller and Shawn Harris renew rivalry in District 14&nbsp;&nbsp;Chattanooga Times Free Press

[page text]
Staff file photos / Democrat Shawn Harris is challenging Republican incumbent Clay Fuller for Georgia's 14th Congressional District. The two met in an April runoff for the seat, which Fuller won with 57% of the vote.

Nearly seven months after U.S. Rep. Clay Fuller, R-Lookout Mountain, first beat Democrat Shawn Harris for Georgia’s District 14 seat in Congress, the two are squaring off again with competing visions about the future of the district and the role President Donald Trump will play in shaping it.

The pair first went up against each other in an April 7 runoff in the special election, which 
Fuller won with 57% of the vote to Harris’ 43%
. Two years earlier, Trump won the district by 37 points.

Harris said he believes his campaign has further closed that gap through recent get-out-the-vote efforts. He pointed to the president’s recent appearance in Dalton, Georgia, arguing Trump’s presence did not impact polling as it has during previous election cycles.

“Clay’s in a pickle. His base is not turning out,” Harris said by phone. “And the president’s been here two times to our district. He’s been here to Georgia three times, but two times right to the 14th. And not one of those times did we actually see a bump come out of that.”

 

Fuller, meanwhile, said he’s confident in his campaign come Election Day. He’s spent recent months traveling across the district, he said, talking to residents and business owners.

“We’re traveling to every community, every business that we can sit down with and talk to them about problems or issues that they’re having, so I think the campaign is going great,” Fuller said. “We hear a lot of positivity. People are fired up.”

Both candidates said that in speaking to residents, one topic came up time and again: the economy. They both agree Americans are struggling financially, but they differ in what they see as a solution. Fuller, long a champion of the president, said he would continue to support Trump’s economic vision.

(READ MORE: Clay Fuller wins election to replace Marjorie Taylor Greene)

 

The economic inflation rippling across the country, Fuller argued, is a byproduct of the administration of President Joe Biden, and he said he believes that Trump’s economic policies are beginning to reverse that trend. He pointed to Republican tax cuts included in Trump’s tax and spending bill, including policies that eliminated taxes on tips.

“So I expect we just continue to support the economic policies that solve this inflationary crisis, and we’ll continue to make sure we’re putting money back into the pockets of hardworking Americans,” Fuller said by phone.

Harris pointed to Trump’s policies as the root of the country’s current economic woes, saying the Trump administration’s tariffs have only hurt American business interests and American consumers.

“A tariff is a tax on the American people,” Harris said. “So we’ve got to get rid of these tariffs to make sure that we can bring down the cost of living.”

 

Outside of lowering consumer costs, Fuller said one of his biggest priorities moving forward is to bring a robust manufacturing sector back to the North Georgia mountains. He pointed to 
a $1.2 billion economic investment in Rome
 by international tire manufacturer Pirelli as evidence of the region’s economic momentum, which Fuller said he hopes to continue into what would be — if elected — his first full term in office.

“I want long-term economic viability for every community across Georgia 14,” Fuller said. “And that would be something where we can ensure that we’re bringing direct federal investment back to these communities that we represent.”

The pair also differed sharply on the ongoing war in Iran, which Harris called a “war of choice” that has driven up costs at the gas pump. 

Harris said ending the war would be one of his top priorities if elected, and he would also work with colleagues to remove Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth from his position.

 

Fuller said the ongoing conflict with Iran makes the United States safer, effectively removing the offensive capabilities of those who he said wish harm to U.S. residents. He said he anticipates Trump will bring an end to the war quickly but also responsibly.

As the former district attorney for the Lookout Mountain Judicial Circuit, Fuller said he’s always prioritized law and order and public safety. He said that the Trump administration’s immigration policies have helped reduce fentanyl overdoses and violent crime, and he said he would continue supporting these policies. 

“Fighting crime has been my career,” Fuller said. “The Trump administration has done so many incredible things by securing the border.”

Harris has previously called for increased funding for law enforcement and advocated for firearm safety measures, including gun training and responsible storage.

Another issue both candidates addressed was the presence of PFAS, or “forever chemicals,” and the threats those chemicals pose to the district’s water and air quality and the health of its citizens.

For Fuller, the issue is personal. The congressman was diagnosed with thyroid cancer at age 31 and has pushed for federal funding to help address PFAS contamination. 

(READ MORE: Clay Fuller sworn in as member of Congress)

“I’m going to make sure we’re getting federal resources back to make sure that Northwest Georgians have clean water, clean land, and that their family’s safe,” Fuller said.

With Election Day approaching, Harris appealed to voters to look beyond party affiliation when deciding who should represent the district in Congress for the next two years.

“When you vote, vote for your interests, and those interests are what’s best for you, your family, your kids, and your grandkids,” Harris said.

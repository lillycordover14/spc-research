Raiders Mailbag: Should Las Vegas sell Maxx Crosby, Kolton Miller, other players ahead of NFL trade deadline?
Returning Raider Nation’s questions for the week
by
 
 
Matt Holder
Oct 31, 2026, 2:11 PM UTC
The 
Las Vegas Raiders
 have a chance to end their four-game losing streak 
against the New York Jets in Week 8
. But we’re also less than two weeks away from the NFL Trade Deadline (Tuesday, Nov. 10 at 1:00 p.m. PST), which is the primary focus of this week’s mailbag. Let’s get to it!
Q: Would be interesting to hear your take on trading Maxx Crosby, Jackson Powers-Johnson, Kolton Miller, Isaiah Palo-Mao and Kirk Cousins? Seems like the right thing to do given the team’s realistic timeline.
A: I’ll start with the elephant in the room. I was a strong proponent of trading Crosby in the offseason and still think it would be in the Raiders’ best long-term interests to get multiple high-end draft picks for the 29-year-old. I think two first-rounders is probably out of the question now, but it doesn’t hurt that Jeffery Simmons — who is the same age and was in the same draft class as Crosby — just got dealt for two firsts and a fourth, with a fifth going back to Chicago. Also, Simmons is only under contract for one more year than Crosby is.
So, I think John Spytek could get at least a comparable return for the edge rusher, and I don’t know how Spytek could say ‘no’ to an offer like that after the team’s playoff hopes came crumbling down this month. It doesn’t seem like Crosby is going anywhere, though, with 
Adam Schefter saying on Monday
 that “he means so much to that franchise” while pouring cold water on the trade talk/rumors. But if I’m calling the shots, I’m pulling the trigger if I can get at least a first- and a second-round pick for Crosby.
I touched on this last week
; it feels like Powers-Johnson’s days in Las Vegas are numbered after getting benched. I don’t know if that happens ahead of the trade deadline, but barring a material change, I think JPJ will be shopped in the offseason. I liken it to Tyree Wilson from this past offseason, where a fresh start might be best for both sides, and don’t expect much more than a fifth-rounder in return.
I’m all the way out on trading Miller ahead of the deadline. I know he has regressed, but if the plan is to have Fernando Mendoza play at any point this year, I’m not trusting Charles Grant to protect Mendoza’s blindside. Grant has barely seen the field, and at least the coaching staff knows what it can get from Miller, who has been a good pass protector in the past and is still solid right now.
Could Grant be the long-term solution at left tackle? Sure, but turning the reins over to him now would be a massive risk, especially with a rookie quarterback/the future of the franchise. In the offseason, I could be convinced that it’s worth it to trade Miller because then the coaching staff has several months to get Grant ready and can bring in a veteran insurance policy in free agency. But midseason, trading Miller would be a bad idea, in my opinion.
If there’s an offer, Pola-Mao would be easy to part with since he’s just a backup and special teamer right now. I just don’t know if there’s much of a market for that, barring an injury over the next two weekends. If IPM gets dealt, don’t expect much more than a sixth- for seventh-round pick swap or just a seventh-rounder. Also, he’s an impending free agent, so I could see Pola-Mao getting waived after the deadline to make room for Dalton Johnson on the game-day active roster.
I feel like I’m in the minority on this, but I would rather see the Raiders hold onto Cousins even if Mendoza is playing. Trust me, as a draft freak, I love the idea of getting more picks. However, I think it reduces part of the rationale for having Cousins be Mendoza’s mentor if the former gets shipped away when the latter takes over.
I know that has to happen at some point, but I think it’s valuable for the rookie to have a peer as a sounding board when things go wrong during his first handful of starts. Especially since Cousins has seen it all in the NFL, and those two have been forming a big brother-little brother type of relationship over the last six months.

A: Well, as someone who watches and breaks down the Raiders’ defense every week, the past month was already pretty unwatchable, LOL! But to your point, if Crosby does get traded, that creates a need for an edge and adds to the already rather long list of needs. That’s why I wouldn’t make that trade for just one first-round pick, and the return would have to include multiple Top 100 selections.
The plan for a Crosby-less defense in Vegas would hinge on Keyron Crawford bouncing back from injury and

It’s a matter of preference, and there’s a good amount of risk involved in either scenario. But, again, I’d take my chances if the right offer comes across the desk.
Q: Is it time to have a realistic discussion on Spytek?
(Photo by Ethan Miller/Getty Images)
 
Getty Images
A: I got a lot of questions about Spytek this week, with several people asking if (or outright saying) he should get fired. I get it, the Raiders need to get a lot more return on their offseason investments. There’s no denying that, and Spytek’s Day 2 picks have produced little to nothing. Darien Porter is the best one so far, and Porter hasn’t been able to hold down a starting spot.
But part of the reason why the organization can’t get out of the downward spiral that it’s been in over the last two decades is that there has been no stability when it comes to the head coach and general manager. Granted, the Raiders didn’t have a true GM until Reggie McKenzie was hired in 2012, but it’s not like Mark Davis has been patient with his decision-makers.
To be fair, McKenzie got seven years. But even he had a few dud offseasons before putting together the 2016 roster, which has been the franchise’s best team since making the Super Bowl over 20 years ago. To put it in perspective, the Raiders were 10-37 during McKenzie’s first three years. Then, he had a great draft in 2014 (Khalil Mack, Derek Carr and Gabe Jackson), signed some quality free agents (Rodney Hudson, Kelechi Osemele and Michael Crabtree), and hit on his 2015 first-round pick (Amari Cooper) to put together a quality roster.
The problem was that 2017 was a major disappointment, so Davis fired Jack Del Rio and hired Jon Gruden, which essentially stripped any power away from McKenzie and led to his firing a year later. In stepped Mike Mayock for three seasons until Gruden’s email scandal led to another leadership turnover, where Davis brought in the Josh McDaniels-Dave Ziegler duo for a season-and-a-half. That didn’t work out, so it was Tom Telesco’s turn for a year, only for Tom Brady to come in and hire Spytek.
At some point, the Raiders have to stick with a plan and ride it out, even if the road gets bumpy.
General managers — especially ones taking over bad teams/rosters, which is the majority of them — get hired because of the long-term plan they present to the owner, which typically is a three- to four-year vision. However, Davis hasn’t given a GM three seasons since Mayock, who was hired pre-COVID, and McKenzie is the only one of the five GMs Davis has hired to get four or more years!
Do I think it’s fair to say Spytek should face criticism and feel a lot of pressure right now? Absolutely. But this franchise needs stability, and firing a third general manager in five years would be a mistake. The other thing to consider is who is going to want the job if three people who previously held the position were given, at most, two years to turn it around. In my opinion, Davis, Brady or whoever is calling the shots have to give Spytek another offseason.

A: At least one of those positions is going to have to get addressed in free agency, and I’d lean toward the two trench spots. Q: Is Quay Walker actually just bad?
A: For some context, I’ve 
noted in winners and losers that opposing offenses have been targeting Walker
 in coverage and having a lot of success over the last few weeks, and I broke down what that looked like against the Rams on the 
Tape Don’t Lie 
review of the game
.
Walker is playing well as a run defender, currently owning a career-high 71.4 run defense grade from 
Pro Football Focus
 that ranks 25th among linebackers this season. And he’s typically fine in man coverage, where he can lean on his natural gifts and doesn’t have to think as much.
However, the former first-round pick’s awareness/instincts in zone coverage are the primary reason why he hasn’t lived up to his draft potential, and that’s reared its head over the last few weeks. Also, he has a bad habit of not plastering to his man on scramble drills.
When the Raiders signed Walker in free agency, I was hopeful that he’d start to put it together in year five and that reuniting with Nakobe Dean would take enough off the former Packer’s plate since Dean wears the green dot and takes control of getting the defense set in Las Vegas. But that hasn’t made a difference, as Walker’s issues in Green Bay traveled with him to Vegas.
Now, he’s still only 26 years old, and we still have half a season left to play. So, there’s time for him to bounce back and improve down the line. However, it certainly isn’t comforting to see that a $13.5 million per year linebacker’s biggest weakness still exists and hasn’t gotten any better.

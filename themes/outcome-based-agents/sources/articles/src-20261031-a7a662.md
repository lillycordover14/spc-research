Russia increases missile production sixfold: The technological prowess of the Kh-101 and Kalibr missiles.
Ukraine warns that Russia is increasing its production of Kh-101 and Kalibr missiles by 5-6 times. From 120 missiles per year, it has now risen to 60 missiles per month of each type – these deadly long-range weapons are changing the landscape of the Russia-Ukraine conflict.

According to assessments from 
Ukrainian
 military experts, Russia's production capacity for its two 
main cruise missile lines, the Kh-101 and Kalibr,
 has skyrocketed by 5-6 times compared to the period before 
the Russia-Ukraine conflict.
 Moscow's maintenance and expansion of the assembly line for this strategic weapons system not only reflects the adaptability of its defense industry but also poses complex technical challenges regarding guidance technology, stealth design, and transnational component supply chains. 
 The Kh-101 cruise missile mounted on a strategic bomber. Photo: Russian media/newsukraine.rbc.ua
 
Increase in production and actual capacity of Russia's defense industry.
 Based on publicly available analyses from 
RBC-Ukraine
 , Ivan Kyrychevskyi, a military expert from Ukraine's 413th Unmanned Systems Regiment, 
believes that
 Russia's capabilities in manufacturing subsonic cruise missiles have made significant breakthroughs recently.
 In 2021, the Russian defense industry's production scale was estimated to reach a maximum of only about 120 
Kalibr missiles
 and approximately 120 
Kh-101 missiles
 per year.
 However, current tracking data suggests the factory can produce approximately 60 
Kh-101 missiles
 and an equivalent number of 
Kalibr missiles
 per month. 
 Kh-101 cruise missile. Photo: UNIAN
 Thus, the estimated total production capacity for each of these weapons types could exceed 700 units per year if the current production rate is maintained.
 Analysts note that this increase reflects the operational capacity of industrial production lines, which is entirely different from the concept of increasing overall strategic stockpiles or the actual frequency of missile launches on the battlefield.
 Increasing manufacturing capacity requires coordinated efforts between the mechanical engineering industry, composite materials technology, and ensuring a reliable supply of integrated electronic circuits.
 At the same time, Ukrainian President's special envoy Vladyslav Vlasiuk also raised concerns about the continued presence of Western-made microelectronic components in the control systems of these weapons, prompting Ukraine to request even tighter sanctions on the Russian supply chain.

The Kh-101 missile features stealth design and advanced operating mechanisms.
 The 
Kh-101
 strategic air-to-ground cruise missile was developed by the Raduga Design Bureau to replace the older Kh-55 series.
 
The Kh-101 missile
 has a length of approximately 7.45 meters, a body diameter of 0.51 meters, a maximum range of up to 4,500-5,000 km, and a launch weight of approximately 2.4 tons.
 A distinctive feature of 
the Kh-101 missile
 's aerodynamic design is its slanted body combined with a foldable propeller structure that unfolds after launch, significantly reducing its radar cross-section (RCS) to an estimated 0.01 m². 
 Russian Kalibr cruise missile. (Source: Wikimedia/united24media.com)
 The exterior 
of the Kh-101 missile
 is covered with a special radar-absorbing paint, making it one of the most stealthy weapons in the Russian 
military
 arsenal.
 In terms of propulsion, 
the Kh-101 missile
 uses a compact NPO Saturn TRDD-50A turbofan engine that retracts inside the body and descends during flight, allowing it to maintain subsonic speeds of approximately Mach 0.68 to Mach 0.78 (equivalent to 700-900 km/h) at extremely low altitudes of only 30 to 70 meters above the terrain.
 
The Kh-101 missile
 is equipped with a complex combined guidance system including GLONASS/GPS satellites, inertial navigation system (INS), and TERCOM/DSMAC terrain comparison system (SVP-24), allowing for a circular error probability (CEP) of only about 5-10 meters over distances of thousands of kilometers.
 
The Kh-101 missile
 carries a 400-450kg high-explosive warhead or a dual warhead consisting of directed fragmentation and a thermobaric projectile. 
 Kalibr cruise missile. Photo: Russian Ministry of Defense/TASS
 
The Kalibr missile family features a multi-platform and combat architecture.
 In contrast to 
the Kh-101 missile,
 which is equipped on strategic bombers such as the Tu-95MS or Tu-160, the 3M-54 Kalibr cruise missile system (NATO designation SS-N-30A) is designed for multi-platform deployment, from Kilo-class submarines and Yasen-class nuclear submarines to corvettes and frigates of the Russian Navy.
 
The Kalibr missile
 has a length ranging from 6.2 meters to 8.9 meters depending on the variant, a diameter of 0.533 meters, and an effective range of 1,500km to 2,500km against land targets.

The Kalibr missile
 's technological strength lies in its satellite guidance system combined with the ARGS-14 active radar seeker in the terminal phase, allowing the missile to automatically lock onto its target and flexibly adjust its flight path to avoid airspace with high air defense density. 
 Russia's Kalibr missile, with a range of 2500km, is a 'death scythe' that renders Western defenses ineffective. Photo: Topwar
 Similar to 
the Kh-101 missile, the Kalibr missile
 travels at an altitude close to sea level or flat terrain (approximately 10-20 meters at sea and 50-150 meters on land), making it very difficult for ground-based surveillance radars to detect it from a distance.
 
The Kalibr missile
 carries a warhead weighing approximately 400-500 kg and uses a solid-fuel propulsion system to support the launch phase before switching to a turbojet engine to sustain flight.
 Overall, maintaining large-scale mass production 
of Kh-101
 and 
Kalibr missiles
 demonstrates Russia's self-sufficiency in much of the mechanical, engine, and fuel processes.
 Despite ongoing controversies surrounding semiconductor control, the technical specifications and deployment realities demonstrate that 
the Kh-101
 and 
Kalibr missiles
 remain two of the most important long-range strike technology pillars in Moscow's modern military strategy.
 
(According to newsukraine.rbc.ua)

David Crosby
 was never the kind of person who liked to sugarcoat pieces of the rock and roll experience. 
He was one of the greatest survivors in rock and roll history, and up until the day he died, he was going to be nothing if not candid about every single detail that he learned along the way to being one of the best songwriters of his generation. He knew when something was going well and when some of his contemporaries needed to pack it in, but he also understood when some people had a lot of nerve calling themselves proper musicians whenever they started picking up their guitars.
Then again, Crosby’s definition of what a true musician sounded like was always going to be a bit more detailed than what his friends were focused on. He wanted to make some of the greatest pop songs ever written, and even though a lot of people think that means making the catchiest songs possible, Crosby knew that it was a bit more nuanced than that whenever he played his tunes.
His heroes were the biggest names in jazz when he first started learning how to play rock and roll, and although he gave his best to make every single song sound like a winner, it was more interesting for him to see where his artistry could go. He felt that his music needed to be in the same conversation 
as people like Joni Mitchell
, which is probably why he was content to leave the traditional pop formula behind halfway through his career.
The albums he made became more dictated by genres like jazz and even got a bit more ethereal over time, so to see other bands that did nothing but make noise was the worst thing in the world. He wanted to hear earnest songwriters that had something to say, and when heavy metal started to take over the world in the 1980s, he felt that there was no need for bands like Iron Maiden.
Granted, you can see why a band like Maiden would have been utterly alien to what Crosby was used to. This was a band that was meant to be one of the most streamlined metal acts of all time, and while they pumped out one classic after another whenever they performed, Crosby felt that there was no reason anyone should have listened to them for more than three seconds, let alone an entire album.
They certainly had their fans, but Crosby certainly didn’t count himself as one of them when asked about the metal giants, 
telling
 his friend Vin Downes, “It’s so weird to me that you like that noise.” But when you look at the kind of music that Maiden makes, it’s not really all that weird when you really break down what every song is about.

People like to claim that metal is nothing but a bunch of meatheads singing about Satan, and while the latter is certainly true on a few of Maiden’s tunes, they were also looking to make more episodic tunes as well. Say what you will about how the screaming isn’t for you or how the band don’t have the largest harmonic vocabulary, but there are tunes like ‘Rime of the Ancient Mariner’ that not even Crosby could have been able to make even if he tried to go against the grain.
It’s that kind of epic nature that steered the band to over 130million record sales, and even if some people thought that it sounded a bit strange at the time, there was no reason for Maiden to take answers from a folk-rock icon. 
They were in a completely different ballpark from Crosby
, and they were going to keep galloping their way through every other song that they made regardless of what people thought.

Everyday Clay: Planting trees and penning poetry

By 
Clay Bowen / Special to The Dispatch 
 • 4 hours ago  

  • 5 mins to read

		Clay Bowen	

I
 often get writer’s block, which in turn saddles me with a fear of redundancy. I search for topics to write about and desperately force words into paragraphs, hoping to avoid overlap and achieve tangible results. Upon the completion of my writing by way of desperation, I then read what’s in front of me and subsequently toss it into the garbage bin.

Writing is a process, and it’s a process that must be trusted. A disingenuous idea comes off as unnatural and contrived. But when the process is allowed to run its course, the stories write themselves.

For me, my topics of discussion must strike like a bolt of lightning, causing my fingers to type without intention. What’s more is that those ideas strike me in the most obscure of places, resulting in yarns I never intended to spin.

Take, for example, my work this past week. Planting trees is a near-daily occurrence for me as a landscaper, but planting them for two family members within a 4-day period is a rare happening, albeit a joyful one worth sharing.

Seldom do I plant a tree for a friend or family member without mentioning my stepfather, Alan. His memory is rooted in his work, and his work was found in planting trees and shrubs for others. Alan was a landscaper for every bit of 40 years, and he loved almost every moment of it. I, on the other hand, feel that I was chosen by this profession rather than choosing it for myself, and I maintain what I consider to be a love/hate relationship with horticulture. 

Regardless, the flame of my family’s proverbial torch was flickering after Alan passed away and needed to be stoked and carried. Somehow, that torch found its way to me.

The idea of writing a column about planting trees and writing poetry came to me as I lay in bed on Monday night waiting to fall asleep. I began thinking about the conversations that I had with my brother-in-law J.D. and my mother while planting
  
trees at their respective homes last week. Those two shared moments were poetic in their own right, and I found inspiration within them.

When I showed up at J.D.’s house with his trees, he came outside to greet my crew and me. Within minutes, we began telling stories about landscaping and how two of my employees that were present that day were once employed by Alan. We laughed at life coming full circle and then began to ponder the perfect location for his new trees. 

Throughout our visit, we talked of live oaks, swamp oaks, cherry trees, ginkgos, redbuds and many more. J.D. showed me a fallen half of a massive oak tree lying in the field next to his driveway. He mentioned that it was 100 years old and spoke of cutting a nice chunk out of the center in order to make a table. His eyes were filled with wonderment just talking about it; I smiled. 

J.D. and my sister Rachel have a little piece of land that is very special. It’s the type of place where the wind whispers and beauty shouts. Each day out there holds a promise from nature for it to appear just a little bit different than the day before. I was honored to plant a couple of trees there. 

Earlier that week, I planted a Chinese pistache tree for my mom. We recently overhauled her backyard and went with a more minimalist look. I often found myself laughing as we deconstructed the amalgamation of plant material my stepfather accumulated over the years, specifically because I can still hear him preaching to me that less is more when it comes to landscaping. 

He loved that backyard. Being trusted to take on such a project at my parents’ home was a sacred duty. I could feel Alan’s shadow cast in every corner. 

My mother spent some time planting annuals while we placed her tree in its hole. Perhaps she was there to keep a watchful and skilled eye on our progress, or to simply share the moment; probably both. 

One of my favorite things about working in the yard alongside someone else is the little talks that may have never existed without that moment.

Up until recently, it hadn’t dawned on me how poetic my days can be. After all, nature is a powerful muse, and I work in it every day. I don’t always love what I do, but I do what I love, which is finding poetic beauty in everything I see. 

So with that, I’ll leave you with a poem that I wrote, about a purpose that’s been chosen for me.

“And then one day, the charge you will take:

Planting trees for memories to make.

The work is small, the gesture is grand.

Grown with purpose, planted by hand;

With love.”

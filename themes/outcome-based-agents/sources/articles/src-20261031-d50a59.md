Latest on Maxx Crosby Trade Rumors&nbsp;&nbsp;Raiders Beat

[page text]
Trade rumors around Maxx Crosby were quiet during the first month of the season, but the Raiders have a big decision coming on the their star pass rusher before the November 10 trade deadline.

Unlike 10 months ago when Crosby requested a trade, the Raiders and Crosby are in a better place, but that doesn’t mean the best situation for everyone involved is to stay the course. 

The NFL is believed to have a couple of strong suitors for Crosby, and one of those teams is without question the Dallas Cowboys.

Since the Cowboys offered a first and second-round pick for Crosby in March, Crosby’s knee has only improved and despite the fact that he has only two sacks on the season, Crosby has been pressuring quarterbacks at an elite level.

According to 
Pro Football Focus
 analytics, Crosby’s 37 quarterback pressures are fourth most in the league through the first seven weeks of the season. 

Crosby trade would come with challenges

Despite his contract being constructed to accommodate a trade, there will still be some hurdles to getting a deal done.

The obvious, of course, being that the Raiders are believed to be firm on their asking price of two first-round picks for Crosby.

On Saturday, league insider 
Tom Pelissero
 talked about some of the other dynamics that would be involved in a potential Crosby trade…

“After the failed trade to Baltimore back in March, Crosby felt it was a sign he was supposed to stay in Las Vegas, and that hasn’t changed. Crosby wants to be a Raider, he has no desire to leave, and he is definitely not forcing his way out.

But GM John Spytek is sure to get calls over the next 10 days about adding one of the NFL’s top edge players, who was worth two first-round draft picks until the Ravens balked over the medical report on Crosby’s surgically repaired knee. Crosby has played 88 percent of the snaps this season and hasn’t been on the injury report once.

If a 29-year-old Jeffery Simmons can get traded for two first-round picks and more, why can’t a 29-year-old Maxx Crosby? And if the Raiders were willing to trade him for that package in March, why wouldn’t they do it again now if the value is there to accelerate their rebuild? That doesn’t mean Crosby would accept a trade to just any team, and his close relationship with owner Mark Davis could help him scuttle any deal.

Another potential stumbling block to a Crosby trade: He’s due about $18.333 million for the rest of this season, which would create a cap and cash crunch for some suitors. Crosby’s $29 million salary for 2027 is also fully guaranteed.”

What could the Raiders get for Crosby?

Before this week, a first-round pick and maybe a little more seemed like the best-case scenario for the Raiders in terms of compensation for Crosby, but after Jeffery Simmons was traded to the Bears for two first-round picks and then some, it’s fair to wonder if 
Crosby’s trade value
 might have received a boost in the last few days. 

The Cowboys, Vikings and 49ers are all teams with a legitimate chance to get to the Super Bowl this year, and adding a player like Crosby would be a tremendous boost to all three teams.

On the other side of the coin, it would be devastating for a team like the Cowboys to see Crosby go to the 49ers or Vikings.

In March, 
ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler
 said the Cowboys were low-key devastated that they weren’t able to get a deal done for Crosby, and if that report was accurate, the Cowboys should be considered candidates to circle back to Crosby before the November 10 trade deadline.

Sidney Crosby comes to life, but Penguins show same problems in another loss
Sidney Crosby had a vintage performance in a losing effort, dishing out some big hits and notching three points.

Sidney Crosby emerged from his funk in style. The Penguins very much remain stuck in theirs, however, and whatever answers they’re looking for might not be available on their current roster.
Crosby recorded his first three-point game of the season and was dominant Saturday night, but the Penguins’ penchant for allowing goals in droves continued with conviction as the Montreal Canadiens held for a 
5-4 victory
 at Bell Center.

The Penguins have already allowed 49 goals this season. Their 3.5 goals against per game is the worst number in the Eastern Conference, and it becomes substantially more concerning when looking at the big picture. Rather shockingly, the Penguins have recorded two shutouts this season, one against the struggling Philadelphia Flyers on opening night and the other against an emotionally exhausted Florida coming off Brady Tkachuk’s return to Ottawa 24 hours earlier. In the Penguins’ other 12 games, they’re allowing 4.1 goals per game.
That inability to prevent the opposition from scoring was very much on display Saturday.
Sergei Murashov, the much-hyped rookie goaltender who has struggled in three of his four starts this season, allowed goals on the first two shots he faced. He stopped only 22 of 27 shots in the game.
Top Plays from Penguins @ Canadiens
The Penguins’ blue line frequently looked ill-equipped to handled the Canadiens’ quick forwards, allowing a plethora of golden opportunities throughout the evening.
A couple of the Penguins’ big names kept the game within striking distance. Crosby scored a goal in the final minutes with Murashov pulled to cut Montreal’s lead to 5-4. The Penguins’ captain physically overpowered the Canadiens for much of the evening.
Erik Karlsson was spectacular. He buried a Crosby feed in the first period and made a number of high-level plays throughout, finishing with three points. Karlsson leads the Penguins with 16 points in 14 games.
Rickard Rakell and Tommy Novak also scored for the Penguins.
While the Penguins did carry the play when Crosby’s line was on the ice, they were otherwise overwhelmed by the Canadiens. Their inability to resemble a professional defensive unit once again proved to be an insurmountable problem.
“We don’t play well enough for 48 minutes,” Karlsson said. “That’s pretty much the bottom line. We’re still in the game for the most part, score-wise, which is a good thing. But we don’t play well enough to sustain it for long enough to come away with a ‘W.'”
Karlsson continued with his thoughts on the Penguins’ defensive work.
Advertisement
“Defensively, we’re fine once we’re in the zone,” he said. “Our rushes against is hurting a lot. It takes a lot of energy to be back checking long ways all the time. Our in-zone defensive game is fine.”
Head coach Dan Muse keeps getting asked about his team’s poor defensive work. He only has so many answers to give.
“I know I sound like a broken record, but we didn’t play consistently,” he said. “Hockey is a game of mistakes. Mistakes are going to happen. But in terms of them being able to get two quick ones, or even sometimes when they get shifts with multiple caches back to back, we need to be better about controlling momentum. A lot of it is very preventable.”

Muse then offered a warning.

Ten postgame observations
• What Karlsson is saying about the Penguins’ defensemen having to deal with so many rush opportunities is understandable and undeniably a problem. But it’s hard to agree with his belief that the blue line is fine once its members are in the zone. Karlsson is fine. Some other the defensemen are, too. But not all of them.
The defensemen aren’t the only accountable ones. How many good defensive forwards do the Penguins employ at the moment? Blake Lizotte? Sure. Connor Dewar? Yes. Ben Kindel? Yes. Crosby, in general, yes, though he’s not been good defensively this season.
It’s a real problem. There are a number of players on this team who are not good when they don’t have the puck.
• Murashov needs to be better, without question, but he also isn’t being put in the best of situations. He hadn’t played in two weeks and was then asked to play in Montreal. That’s tough. Four of his five starts have come on the road, also not easy.
Then, there’s the way the team has played in front of him.
Murashov didn’t play well. He’s got a lot of talent but hasn’t been good enough so far this season.

But again, the degree of difficulty that the Penguins’ goaltenders are dealing with is absurd. If Artūrs Šilovs wasn’t playing some seriously elite goal right now, those Penguins’ goals against numbers would be considerably worse.

• Declan Carlile struggled significantly again. I’m not sure how much longer you can leave him in the lineup. He was a minus-3 in this game, while no one else on the Penguins’ blue line was worse than minus-1. He appears to need a reset.
Karlsson might be right about some of the Penguins’ defenseman being fine in the defensive zone, but Carlile is not. It doesn’t matter which defensive partner he’s playing with. Right now, he is consistently the biggest problem on the Penguins’ blue line.
• Andrei Kuzmenko has one point — an assist — in 10 games. It’s getting hard to justify leaving him in the lineup at this point. You can’t tell me that Avery Hayes or Rutger McGroarty wouldn’t be more effective, and those two will obviously play a bigger role in the Penguins’ future. The whole third line was bad in this game, in fact, as Hendrix Lapierre and Ville Koivunen also struggled.
But those two have been pretty good lately. Kuzmenko has not.
• It wasn’t a strong outing for the second line, either. Evgeni Malkin, Egor Chinakhov and Kindel were quiet after playing very well against Utah two nights earlier.
Chinakhov is a split-second late in releasing his terrific shot and that needs to stop. It’s his greatest weapon and he’s got to turn a little selfish in terms of using it.
• This was a vintage Crosby performance. Moments into the game, he rocked Kaiden Guhle with a hit that knocked the defenseman down. Crosby threw a couple of other big hits in this game and was a force throughout. He looked far more engaged and challenged defenders one-on-one behind the net on a regular basis, something we haven’t seen this season. This was a different Crosby. He wanted the puck and his confidence level appeared to return.

Crosby was the first player on the ice at Friday’s practice. He’s trying to work his way out of this slump, as he’s done many times in the past. This was a huge step in the right direction.
• The same goes for Karlsson. He made some brilliant plays, and while he’s hardly been perfect, he’s trending toward last season’s form.
• The Penguins simply can’t handle teams that employs speedy forwards. Muse said a lot of their problems are preventable. I’m not sure they are.
• Rakell is starting to heat up. This isn’t a surprise. He’s often streaky and often a slow starter. Rumors of his demise have been greatly exaggerated.
• Incorporating youth into the lineup isn’t always the correct answer. The game isn’t that simple, and you need some veterans around for a number of reasons.
But … McGroarty and Hayes should be in this lineup right now. They’re better than certain other players and their energy could be beneficial. It makes more sense to find out about them than to keep ineffective older players in the lineup.

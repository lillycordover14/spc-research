Foreign Students at Korea's Junior Colleges Jump Sixfold in Eight Years&nbsp;&nbsp;Seoul Economic Daily

Source: Korea Educational Development Institute
The number of foreign students at South Korea's junior colleges has risen sixfold in eight years, as the country's total foreign student population surpassed 300,000. Dropout rates have fallen overall, but remain high at four-year and industrial universities in the Seoul metropolitan area and at junior colleges in the Gangwon region.
Foreign enrollment at four-year and industrial universities rose 70.4% to 153,477 in 2025 from 90,095 in 2017, according to "Reading Our Education Through Data," published on the 1st by the Ministry of Education and the Korean Educational Development Institute (KEDI). Over the same period, foreign enrollment at junior colleges surged 504.6% to 37,260 from 6,163. The two-year growth rate at junior colleges, at 117.5%, was more than triple the 35.1% recorded at four-year and industrial universities.
The mix of countries of origin has also shifted. Chinese students at four-year and industrial universities fell to 44,804, or 29.2% of the total, in 2025 from 53,943, or 59.9%, in 2017. Vietnamese students, by contrast, rose to 44,082, or 28.7%, from 9,856, or 10.9%. The combined share of students from Uzbekistan, Mongolia, Nepal and Myanmar climbed to 20.8% from 6.4%. At junior colleges, Vietnamese enrollment grew to 22,342 from 2,766, lifting their share to 60.0% from 44.9%.
Academic retention is also improving. The dropout rate, which covers withdrawals and failures to register, fell 1.25 percentage points to 5.60% in 2025 from 6.85% in 2023 at four-year and industrial universities. At junior colleges, the rate declined 2.79 percentage points to 7.23% from 10.02%.
Regional gaps were stark, however. The dropout rate at four-year and industrial universities in the Seoul metropolitan area rose to 6.2% in 2025 from 5.9% in 2023, exceeding the national average. At junior colleges in the Gangwon region, the rate stood at 19.5%, meaning roughly one in five foreign students abandoned their studies.
KEDI said that while reliance on China has eased, the top three countries of origin still account for more than 60% of foreign students, making further diversification necessary. The institute added that support for academic retention should be strengthened in regions with high dropout rates, including four-year and industrial universities in the Seoul metropolitan area and junior colleges in the Gangwon region.

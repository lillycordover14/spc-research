MONTREAL —
 The Pittsburgh Penguins currently have the second-worst winning percentage in the Eastern Conference, surpassed only by the bottomless struggles of the Philadelphia Flyers. After another profoundly disjointed loss Saturday, the Penguins have shown no signs of pulling out of their own self-destructive tailspin. 

Coach 
Dan Muse
 said the team has to figure it out quickly. 
Sidney Crosby
 said until they figure it out, they’re not going to win games. 

“Yeah, I mean, we got to clean them up,” Crosby said. “If we don’t, then we’re not going to give ourselves much of a chance to win games. So, whether it’s off the rush or in our zone, yeah, we’ve got to do better.”

The Penguins’ struggles rest on a couple of issues. As part of this week’s PHN Q&A, more than a few readers wondered about Crosby. Should he go to the wing, or has Father Time finally caught him? The other pressing issue staring down the Penguins is something entirely unfixable at the coaching or locker room level.

The Penguins roster construction is entirely in line with summer criticisms. Puck possession skills, but entirely soft. Ability in the offensive zone, but can’t find the defensive zone with GPS. 

Yes, general manager 
Kyle Dubas
 put together a puzzle, but the roster turnover was not to the team’s benefit, and it seems the struggles will persist, at least until there are changes. That blunt analysis isn’t something that should be written just 14 games into the season, but when the results immediately line up with the expectations, it’s a short leap to confirmation.

And that’s where we are. 

Penguins Q&A

Do you think it’s time to move Sid to the wing? Is this really just a slow start, or has Father Time finally caught up with him? Moving Geno to the wing worked wonders for his game. Maybe it’s time for Sid as well? — Paula Sleeman

No to both. Crosby has had a few slow starts in recent years, but the difference for this one is a lack of cheap points and assists. 
Rickard Rakell
’s snake-bitten start combined with 
Bryan Rust
’s forced visits to the trainer’s room have left Crosby on an island. 

When he played with Robertson, there was no connectivity and zero chemistry. It’s no coincidence that Crosby’s uptick came a few games into the season when Rust returned. 

I don’t think time has caught up with him. I see him flying in practice. But it is fair to say he cannot defend like he once did; 
Logan Cooley
 showed that with bright spotlights Thursday. Crosby needs some help on his line, both in defensive assignments and in the offensive zone. But no, he has not regressed so greatly as to think the end is nigh, or that it’s time to move him.

However, his point-per-game streak probably ends this season, even after a great game in Montreal.

In your opinion, what are the Penguins missing to get over the hump to becoming a contender? — Logan Krienke

About a half dozen A-level players. 

It’s important to understand that the Penguins are not going to become contenders with the crew they have now, and that includes the handful of angry prospects in Wilkes-Barre/Scranton. Even Dubas admitted in his preseason presser that the gap between where they are and where they want to be is great.

So, do not expect them to become contenders soon. It’s going to take years. The Penguins have a lot of changes and likely pain ahead, which would allow them to acquire the premium draft picks that become front-line players or tent-pole defensemen before they can even consider mentioning themselves in Stanley Cup conversations.

If you’re a fan now, the name of the game is learning to enjoy the process, rooting for some good trades, some rapid development, and as little pain in the process as possible.

Is Dubas’ clock ticking, New Ownership and all? — JP88Blitz

Isn’t everyone’s clock ticking?

Within the spirit and context of your question, no, Dubas isn’t under pressure or on the hot seat. Indeed, he’s into Year 4, but the first two were about attempting an ill-fated salvage operation and a quick retool. Some of that was at the direction of ownership, so it’s not entirely fair to compare Dubas’s progress to a GM who has been rebuilding for four years.

Now, the new owners seem to have handed him full control of hockey operations. As Dubas gets through this season and into Year 5, that’s when the questions will become valid. Where are you headed, and how are you going to do it? 

This writer probably shares the same impatience as many readers. Some truths are too loud and too obvious to ignore. And the truth is that the Penguins are not close to contending; they are not close to rock bottom, and they’re not on the upswing. 

Truly, very soon, the path needs to emerge, and it can’t be serving all sides. The world rebooted and the trade market updated, making Dubas unable to trade his caliber of prospects for the big-time impact players. Nor does he have the bait for the disgruntled young players like 
Simon Edvinsson
 or even 
Dylan Larkin
, either.

There’s only one way to get that bait. In the words of Clubber Lang, pain. 

Do we miss Accari? — Aurore

In the beginning, yes, but Nick Robertson has been very good on that line. 

Why isn’t Robertson getting another chance on the first line with Rust out? –Lisa

No. Sorry. 

Robertson has been very good in his fourth-line role, and he’s putting the puck in the net with a deceptive wrist shot and good speed. BUT…to be a top-line player, a unique hockey IQ and connectivity with the team’s best players is absolutely necessary. Robertson showed no chemistry with Crosby and Rakell in camp. None in preseason. And even less than none in the first few games of the season.

That doesn’t make him a bad player, but what he doesn’t do well is use his teammates. He doesn’t work the game and create space for others. That’s most of the job of playing with Crosby. That’s not a knock on Robertson or a criticism of anyone. His game just doesn’t fit there. 

With all due respect to Kuzmenko’s horrific personal situation, at what point does the team have to start having the hard conversation about continuing to suit him up? — Kyle in Cryptoland

Kuzmenko’s game was a poor fit from the beginning. He’s a slow east-west on a team that is mostly north-south. The eyes and the numbers are largely in agreement that Kuzmenko hasn’t been effective, and please forgive me for stopping there in light of the personal tragedy.

The team would probably be better with Avery Hayes or Rutger McGroarty beside Hendrix Lapierre and Ville Koivunen. Some puck retrieval, defensive stops, better forechecking, and some physicality would do well on that line. 

With the team hopefully getting healthy at some point before the offseason. How likely is Dubas to follow through with not being afraid to lose a veteran to waivers? — Awompa1

I suppose we’re going to find out. Justin Brazeau is the next veteran forward who will return to the team in a bit less than two weeks. Brazeau’s game in the first half of the season in 2025-26 was solid. He was a big man with soft hands who played in the dirty areas and could move the puck to keep Evgeni Malkin and Anthony Mantha fed. 

In the second half, he was not good enough. So, the Penguins and plenty of other teams will get a good look at Brazeau’s game in Wilkes-Barre/Scranton and when he returns. Expect the Penguins to shoehorn Brazeau into the lineup, perhaps in place of Kuzmenko, as a showcase. 

Within the next month, if the team keeps up the sub-.500 pace, very few people will be safe. 

The difference in the team play and spark infused into the lineup when McGroarty, Hayes, and Solovyov played was noticeable. How long before it can’t be ignored? Broz, Zonnon, Brunicke (if not injured), and Pickering are all knocking as well.– Brian Cersosimo

If you’re buying tickets for the holidays, my bet is on January. The mass migration will begin as soon as they can evacuate a veteran or three, but by the mid-season, Wilkes-Barre’s economy is going to take a big hit with a lot of guys leaving town. 

One name Jason Spezza mentioned on the GM’s Show on Wednesday was Atley Calvert. He was plucky in preseason, too. 

After 14 games, we’ve seen nothing to indicate this team has “it” or has another gear that will allow them to go on a tear to make up lost ground. 

Remember Dubas’s reply to our query about his willingness to take a step backward if things don’t go well this season. In that answer, Dubas redefined success this season to include finding out about his group of prospects. 

Brunicke is currently injured, so his timeline is different. Pickering didn’t get off to a great start, but quite frankly, the group was told the AHL playoffs mattered. And any subpar performances should be considered in the context of struggles to overcome the disappointment of getting passed over harder than Fredo. 

There’s no point in missing the playoffs with mediocre veterans. There’s no interest in watching them, either. There will be plenty of interest and every point in embracing the future by letting some younger players begin to take their lumps at the top level. By January is my bet. 

Bryan Rust and Rickard Rakell have been on the trade block now for 2 years. Is this finally the time that one of them gets moved? How likely is it for both? How has Brunicke looked in WBS? Does that impact a possible Erik Karlsson trade?  — Yinzer Mike

OK, first, let’s set the proper context. I think many people think a team MUST trade a player, and therefore the price is a sliding scale until someone bites. 

That’s not how it works. That’s how a trade goes down in Fantasy sports and Xbox when you don’t have to live the consequences, and they don’t affect millions of dollars. In real life, the Penguins do not NEED to trade Rust and Rakell, so parting with them for unhelpful returns is, in fact, unhelpful. 

The Nikita Kucherov situation will put a hold on a lot of teams, who will wait to see, but it could also wet a few appetites for a reliable, skilled top-six winger. 

He’s won exactly ONE of those 10 NHL games. Maybe the media should have pushed pause before defining him as a “can’t miss” prospect who would push Silovs to the bench by midseason.

(Murashov) has won exactly ONE of his 10 NHL games. Maybe the media should have pushed pause before defining him as a “can’t miss” prospect who would push Silovs to the bench by midseason — Mike Anitori

Yes, the media is lying to you. Murashov didn’t look spectacular climbing through the organization’s ranks. Goalie coaches don’t rave about him. And he is not supremely talented. We made it all up, and all of the proof you’ll ever need was Saturday. 

Ah, the media. We’re one big group with secret meetings where we discuss how to deceive you, what we want you to think about the most important topic on the planet, the Pittsburgh Penguins, and on Sundays we do a potluck dinner with the local chapter of the Bilderbergers. 

The Sergei Murashov furry Saturday was silly. Sarcasm aside, it seems the crazies have settled on Murashov as a scapegoat to proclaim their frustrations and limited hockey knowledge. Included in my replies were suggestions that the team should bring up Taylor Gauthier, sign a veteran journeyman, start the suddenly exalted Arturs Silovs every game, and figuratively toss Murashov on the nearest truck driving back to Wilkes-Barre. 

Settle down, people. It’s 10 games, and in some of them, Murashov has played quite well. He would look much better with competent defensive support, too. Hey, there are no guarantees that Murashov becomes an NHL starter, just as there are no guarantees of anything in life. But you can see the gifts when he made the sterling saves in the third period. 

He is only 22, which is extremely young for a goalie, but he’s also very talented. That is undeniable. Remember, it took Marc-Andre Fleury a few years of ups and downs to find himself, and even then he struggled again several years later. Matt Murray was unbeatable for the first two years of his career, then bounced around the league and is currently out of hockey at 32 years old. 

Goaltending isn’t easy. It’s even harder when a soft goal is compounded with facing high-danger chances seconds later and for the rest of the period. 

The overwhelming odds are that Murashov will be just fine, and while I don’t think a railroad spike could drive home the point to some people, this is what a rebuild looks like. Young players. Young players will struggle. It is an unavoidable part of the process.

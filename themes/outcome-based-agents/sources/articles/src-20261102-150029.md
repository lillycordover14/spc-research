EMA Reviews Allergic Reactions With Immunoglobulin Batches&nbsp;&nbsp;Medscape

[page text]
Increased reporting of hypersensitivity reactions with specific batches of human normal immunoglobulin medicines has triggered a review by the European Medicines Agency’s (EMA’s) Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee (PRAC).
The review was opened at the committee’s October meeting and announced on October 30. Human normal immunoglobulins are used to help prevent infections in patients with antibody deficiency, including some with immune disorders or blood cancers. They are also used to treat certain conditions in which the immune system attacks the body’s own tissues.
Batch Recalls 
Hypersensitivity reactions are a known adverse effect of these medicines, and product information already warns that serious reactions can occur even in patients who have previously tolerated treatment. However, reports suggest that these reactions may be occurring more frequently than expected with specific batches of some products.
Reported cases have ranged from rash and itching to serious, potentially life-threatening reactions, including 
anaphylaxis
 and 
angioedema
. Clusters of these cases have led to recalls of specific batches of Flebogamma DIF, Yimmugo, and Gamunex in certain EU member states in recent months. The affected batches have already been withdrawn from the market as a precaution.
Cause Not Yet Established 
Despite investigations to date, the cause of these clusters has not yet been established, and it remains unclear whether other human normal immunoglobulin medicines could also be affected. The PRAC review will therefore cover all human normal immunoglobulin medicines, whether given intravenously, subcutaneously, or intramuscularly.
The committee will request relevant data from the companies marketing these medicines and assess it alongside the available evidence to investigate possible causes and determine whether further risk-reduction measures are needed.
Advice for Clinicians and Patients 
Healthcare professionals are reminded to follow existing warnings and precautions in the product information, including recommendations to monitor patients during treatment. Patients should seek prompt medical attention if they develop signs of a hypersensitivity reaction, such as rash, dizziness, palpitations, wheezing, or difficulty breathing, even if they have tolerated previous treatment without such reactions.
EMA said it will communicate further on the outcome of the review.

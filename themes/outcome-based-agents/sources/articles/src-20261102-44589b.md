Abstract
Adaptive immune cell populations (T cells and B cells) retain a molecular record of immune exposures, including infection and vaccination, in their specific antigen receptor repertoires. These receptor repertoires arise through V(D)J recombination, which assembles distinct combinations of germline-encoded V, D and J gene segments and introduces extensive junctional diversification to generate highly diverse antigen receptors that are subsequently shaped by developmental selection and antigen exposure. Recent advances in high-throughput sequencing enable adaptive immune receptor repertoires to be profiled at scale, across large cohorts and with increasing depth within individual samples. As a result, receptor repertoire profiling is emerging as a powerful approach for monitoring disease course and treatment responses in cancer, quantifying immune responses to infection and vaccination, and dissecting the immunological basis of inflammatory disease. In this Review, we discuss how human T cell and B cell receptor repertoires are formed, how inherited genetic variation shapes their architecture and how experimental and computational advances are enabling repertoire data to be translated into biological insight, clinical utility and repertoire-guided therapeutic opportunities.

Glossary
β-selection

A developmental checkpoint in thymocytes at which productive rearrangement of the T cell receptor (TCR) β-chain enables pre-TCR signalling, proliferation and progression towards αβ T cell development.

Adaptive immune receptor repertoire sequencing

High-throughput sequencing of rearranged B cell receptor or T cell receptor genes to characterize the diversity, abundance and clonal structure of adaptive immune receptor repertoires.

Affinity maturation

The process by which B cell clones acquire somatic mutations and are selected for improved antigen binding, resulting in antibodies with increased affinity.

Antigen-presenting cells

Cells that process antigens and display antigen-derived peptides on MHC molecules to T cells. Professional antigen-presenting cells include dendritic cells, macrophages and B cells.

Broadly neutralizing antibodies

Antibodies that neutralize multiple strains or variants of a pathogen, often by targeting conserved antigenic regions.

CDR3

The highly variable antigen-contacting region generated at the V(D)J junction of B cell receptors and T cell receptors, often being a key determinant of receptor specificity.

Class-switch recombination

An activation-induced cytidine deaminase-dependent recombination process that changes the immunoglobulin heavy-chain constant region, thereby altering antibody isotype without changing antigen specificity.

Clonotypes

Groups of T cells or B cells defined by shared rearranged antigen receptor sequence features, typically including the CDR3 sequence and V and J gene usage.

Complementarity-determining region

(CDR). One of three antigen-contacting loops within an immune receptor variable domain; CDR1 and CDR2 are primarily encoded by germline V genes, whereas CDR3 is generated at the V(D)J junction.

Conformational epitopes

Antigenic surfaces formed by amino acids that may be distant in the primary sequence but are brought together by protein folding and recognized by antibodies or B cell receptors.

Constant region

The portion of an immunoglobulin or T cell receptor chain that lies outside the antigen-binding variable region and, in antibodies, determines isotype and effector function.

Epitopes

Specific molecular features of antigens recognized by immune receptors, including native antigen surfaces bound by antibodies or B cell receptors and peptide–MHC complexes recognized by T cell receptors.

Framework residues

Amino acids within receptor variable domains that support the structure and positioning of complementarity-determining regions and can also contribute to antigen recognition.

Germinal centre

A transient structure within secondary lymphoid organs where activated B cells proliferate, undergo somatic hypermutation and class-switch recombination and are selected for improved antigen binding.

Heavy-chain allelic exclusion

The process by which a developing B cell expresses a productive immunoglobulin heavy-chain rearrangement from only one allele, ensuring expression of a single heavy-chain specificity.

Immunodominance

The tendency of an immune response to focus on a limited subset of possible epitopes rather than responding equally to all available targets.

Isotype

The antibody class, such as IgM, IgD, IgG, IgA or IgE, which is defined by the heavy-chain constant region and associated with distinct immune effector functions.

Linear epitopes

Continuous amino acid sequences within antigens that are recognized by antibodies or B cell receptors.

Measurable (minimal) residual disease

The small burden of malignant cells that remains after treatment and is detectable only by sensitive molecular, sequencing or flow cytometric assays.

MHC restriction

The requirement that conventional T cell receptors recognize antigenic peptides only when they are presented by specific MHC molecules, known as HLA molecules in humans.

Phage immunoprecipitation sequencing

A high-throughput method that uses libraries of phage-displayed peptides to profile antibody binding specificities in serum or plasma.

Receptor editing

A tolerance mechanism in immature B cells in which renewed light-chain recombination can replace an autoreactive B cell receptor with one of altered specificity.

Somatic hypermutation

An activation-induced cytidine deaminase-dependent process that introduces point mutations into immunoglobulin variable regions during B cell responses, enabling selection of clones with altered or improved antigen affinity.

V(D)J recombination

A somatic DNA rearrangement process that joins selected germline-encoded variable (V), diversity (D; where applicable) and joining (J) gene segments to generate diverse antigen receptor sequences in developing B cells and T cells.

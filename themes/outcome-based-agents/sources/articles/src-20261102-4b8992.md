Hundreds of costumed children and their families filled the decorated 5700 and 5800 blocks of South Harper Avenue on Saturday for an annual trick-or-treating tradition that dates back nearly 30 years.
Houses festooned with gossamer and strings of lights, a candy tornado, spiders, a Jurassic Park dinosaur sitting in the back of a Jeep, witches and an entire family dressed as characters from the anime series “Naruto” greeted hundreds of trick-or-treaters who began streaming down the street between 3 and 4 p.m. 

Dressed as a Pan Am flight attendant and a dinosaur, Chelsey and Adam Parrott-Sheffer bring “Jurassic Park” to life on Harper Avenue, Oct. 31, 2026.

Marc C. Monaghan

Karen Rechtschaffen lived on the street from 1971 to 2005 and returns every year from her apartment at Vista Homes for the festivities. This year, she was joined by her 25-year-old grandson, who was visiting from Florida.
Rechtschaffen said the event started in the late 1990s in response to concerns about "all those stories about razor blades and apples."
"So when our kids were little, we started saying, ‘You know, what the hell? Send them up and down the block,’" Rechtschaffen said. “Everybody knew everybody, and this whole thing grew from that."

By 2010, the event had become so popular that the city hired school buses to bring children and their families from nearby neighborhoods, said Karl Pinc, who has lived on the street for 21 years. The buses no longer run, but thousands manage to find their way on their own.

Yan Yan Xu enjoys Halloween on Harper Avenue dressed as

The amount of candy given out along the street each year is prodigious. Pinc remembers handing out more than 1,300 pieces of candy one year when "it was raining and hailing." The most he’s ever handed out in a single year was about 4,000 pieces.
Josh and Lynn Wright bought a house on Harper just a couple of years ago. They and their young daughter are already steadfast in their love of the Halloween tradition.
"Oh, it's a dream. It's a dream," said Josh Wright, who was decked out in a 1990s disco outfit. "I've been looking forward to this all year."
"We're so excited to do it," Josh Wright said.

    Former Muslim Chaplain sues Northwestern on basis of ‘discriminatory actions’

 

Daily file photo by Cayla Labgold-Carroll
 
 Tahera Ahmad, who worked under the University’s Department of Religious and Spiritual Life, was fired on Jan. 10, 2025. 
 

Lucas Kubovchik
 and 
Emily Dissanayake
 

November 2, 2026

This is a developing story and will be updated as more information becomes available. Last updated Nov. 2 at 5:00 a.m.

Former Northwestern Muslim Chaplain Tahera Ahmad filed a lawsuit against the University on Oct. 26, more than a year after she was fired by NU on Jan. 10, 2025.

Ahmad, who worked as one of the University’s chaplains in its Department of Religious and Spiritual Life for more than 15 years, alleges her supervisors imposed “a series of discriminatory actions” against her during her last two years in the position.

Ahmad is suing for retaliation against “protected opposition” to discrimination toward herself and Arab and Muslim staff, discrimination on the basis of race, color, religion and national origin as violations of Title VI, Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Pregnancy Discrimination Act of 1978, Section 1981 of the Civil Rights Act of 1866 and the Americans with Disabilities Act.  

The lawsuit alleges that one of Ahmad’s former supervisors compared Muslim students to “dogs” during a 2023 meeting. The meeting was about the supervisor’s decision to reduce funding for Ramadan and prayer space accommodations on campus “without prior notice.”  

The lawsuit also alleges that during the Israel-Hamas war, the environment on campus “became increasingly hostile” toward members of the Arab and Muslim community. When Ahmad advocated against this climate, NU “(let) her know that her opposition to discrimination should cease,” the lawsuit stated. 

According to the lawsuit, flyers attacking Ahmad and Muslim and Arab students had been distributed around campus, and in April 2024, Ahmad’s car was broken into with a hateful note left behind. The lawsuit alleges another student, who was Arab and/or Muslim, experienced a similar incident. 

The lawsuit claims the University’s response to the incidents was not reasonable, alleging a University official did not “meaningfully take any action” in response to the reports and denied Ahmad a request for a meeting regarding her safety concerns.

In May 2024, the lawsuit alleges, when Ahmad notified the University official that the Coalition Against Antisemitism at Northwestern had doxed her, she was told the University could not control what CAAN publishes and took no action.  

Days later, the lawsuit alleges the same University official “reprimanded” Ahmad for signing a petition that supported “a ceasefire in Gaza and opposed a racially hostile climate for Muslims and Arabs on campus.” The lawsuit further alleges that the official told Ahmad the University was being pressured by “certain groups” to fire Ahmad and advised her not to post about the war or campus environment on personal social media accounts.

The same month, the official wrote to Ahmad that the department was “restructuring,” demoting Ahmad from “Associate Chaplain and Director of Interfaith Engagement” to “Associate Chaplain for Muslim Life,” according to the lawsuit. 

The lawsuit also cited an instance in which Ahmad contacted one of her former supervisors around April 2024 with a request to reschedule a staff retreat marked for June 17, 2024, due to her participation in Eid-ul-Fitr that day. After her supervisor denied the request and Ahmad explained the reasoning for her absence following the retreat, her supervisor told her the event was more important than Ahmad’s “summer activity,” the lawsuit alleges.  

In October 2024, students asked Ahmad to speak at a 
prayer vigil
 for lives lost in Palestine, Lebanon and Syria. The lawsuit alleges her supervisor gave Ahmad permission to speak but warned her not to go “off script.” Two days after the vigil, the lawsuit alleges her supervisor was “visibly angry” and “threatened to fire her.”  

In December 2024, Ahmad requested to work remotely while she received treatment for her disability, advanced fourth-stage patellar chondromalacia, which caused her “limited mobility and pain,” according to the lawsuit. The University denied her request, despite Ahmad’s submitted paperwork. 

After she reported this to NU’s Americans with Disabilities Act Compliance Office, the office informed Ahmad on Jan. 8, 2025, that the information she had provided the University was complete. NU terminated Ahmed’s employment two days later.  

The University did not immediately respond to The Daily’s request for comment.

EMA Analyst Impact Briefing: Flexera One AI Cost Management

Flexera

November 2, 2026

As AI spending spreads across applications, agents, models, data platforms, and cloud infrastructure, many organizations are discovering that existing cost management practices don’t provide a complete picture. In a recent analyst impact briefing, 
Enterprise Management Associates (EMA)
 examined how Flexera is expanding its offering to address the growing challenge of AI economics, attribution, accountability, and optimization.

Read the full analyst impact briefing 
here
.

How has Flexera expanded its AI Cost Management offering?

According to EMA, Flexera is expanding with an AI Cost Management strategy designed to help organizations understand and control the increasingly fragmented economics of enterprise AI. Rather than treating AI as a single cost category, Flexera organizes visibility and management across six domains: AI applications, AI agents, AI models, AI data clouds, AI compute, and AI platforms. The goal is to connect the AI tools business teams consume with the infrastructure and services that technology teams build, operate, and fund.

How does Flexera track AI spending across the enterprise?

Flexera One brings together technology discovery, data enrichment, analytics, reporting, and automation to create a unified view of AI consumption. By combining information across hardware, software, SaaS, cloud, and AI resources, organizations can better understand where AI is being used, how costs accrue, and which teams or business functions are responsible for that spend.

Does Flexera track AI token usage and consumption?

Yes, and Flexera’s strategy extends beyond token tracking. The platform includes capabilities for token consumption visibility, usage trends, forecasting, spend attribution, and alerts. EMA notes that while tokens are an important unit of AI cost, focusing on tokens alone can leave significant portions of enterprise AI spending invisible.

Why isn’t token tracking alone enough to manage AI costs?

EMA argues that enterprise AI economics extend well beyond model consumption. Organizations may pay for AI through application subscriptions, agent-based pricing, credits, workflows, compute resources, data platform consumption, and bundled AI platform charges. Understanding token usage may explain part of an AI bill, but it does not provide a complete picture of where spending is occurring or why costs are increasing.

What types of AI costs can Flexera help organizations manage?

Flexera One manages AI spending across multiple consumption models. These include seat-based applications, AI agents, foundation models priced by usage, cloud compute resources, AI data platforms, and broader AI platforms that bundle infrastructure, services, and models together. This reflects EMA’s view that AI introduces a more complex cost structure than traditional software licensing.

How does Flexera attribute AI costs to users, teams, and business units?

One of the central themes in the briefing is attribution. EMA reports that Flexera can attribute spending across users, service accounts, business units, tools, and cost centres. The purpose is to answer three fundamental questions: who is consuming AI, what are they consuming, and how much does it cost? By connecting usage to organizational ownership, enterprises can establish accountability and make more informed decisions about AI investments.

How does Flexera help organizations understand who is consuming AI?

Beyond raw consumption metrics, Flexera aims to connect usage with business context. EMA notes that organizations need more than a report showing growing AI usage. They need to know which application or agent created that consumption, which team owns it, which model supported it, and what business purpose it serves. Flexera’s approach is designed to provide that broader context.

Can Flexera provide visibility into AI applications, agents, models, and infrastructure?

Yes. EMA specifically highlights Flexera’s six-part AI taxonomy: AI applications, AI agents, AI models, AI data clouds, AI compute, and AI platforms. The goal is to provide visibility across the different ways organizations buy, build, and consume AI, rather than limiting management to a single layer of the technology stack.

How does Flexera support AI financial accountability?

EMA identifies financial accountability as one of the most important outcomes of attribution and visibility. By connecting spending to owners, teams, and cost centres, organizations can better allocate costs, monitor consumption, identify underutilized resources, forecast spending patterns, and detect potential overruns before they escalate.

How does Flexera connect AI spending to business context?

The analyst briefing repeatedly emphasizes business context as a differentiator. Knowing that AI consumption increased provides limited value on its own. Organizations also need to understand which team generated the consumption, which application or agent was responsible, what business purpose it supports, and whether a more efficient configuration could achieve the same outcome. Flexera’s discovery, enrichment, and analytics capabilities are intended to help answer those questions.

How does Flexera help organizations optimize AI costs?

Flexera organizes its AI Cost Management strategy around three actions: track, implement, and optimize. According to EMA, optimization includes applying existing capabilities such as model evaluation, cloud commitment management, and workload optimization to the infrastructure and services that support AI. The objective is to turn cost visibility into operational action.

How does Flexera combine ITAM, FinOps and SaaS Management for AI?

Much of AI’s cost structure already exists within established technology disciplines. AI applications are often acquired through SaaS subscriptions, models are consumed through usage-based pricing, and AI workloads rely on cloud and data infrastructure. Flexera brings together ITAM, SaaS management, cloud cost management, FinOps, and technology discovery capabilities to help manage those interconnected costs.

Can Flexera forecast AI spending and identify cost anomalies?

Yes. EMA notes that the platform includes forecasting, usage analytics, and alerts that help organizations monitor changes in spending and identify potential issues. These capabilities are intended to help organizations move beyond historical reporting and take a more proactive approach to managing AI consumption.

Does Flexera integrate with AI gateways and AI routers?

Flexera recognizes that AI gateways and routers are emerging as important control points for managing AI consumption. Its strategy includes integration with both open-source and commercial gateways and routers, helping organizations connect visibility with operational controls.

Why does EMA believe visibility must precede AI control?

This is the core conclusion of the briefing. EMA argues that organizations cannot effectively govern AI if they cannot see its economic footprint. Before businesses can optimize spending, establish accountability, evaluate ROI, or implement governance controls, they need visibility into who is consuming AI, what they are consuming, and how costs accumulate across the organization.

Why is AI considered a new technology economics challenge?

EMA’s central thesis is that AI introduces fundamentally different consumption and pricing models than traditional software. Organizations may pay by seat, credit, workflow, token, compute capacity, or data consumption, often across multiple platforms simultaneously. This creates a management challenge that requires visibility across technology domains that have historically been managed separately.

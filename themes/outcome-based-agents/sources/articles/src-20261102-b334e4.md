In 1964, David Crosby met Bob Dylan. Up until his death, he still struggled to talk to him
 
 
 
 
Dale Maplethorpe
 
 
 
November 2nd, 2026 · 9:30pm GMT
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Follow us on Google Discover
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
There are very few musicians out there who aren’t influenced by 
Bob Dylan
. 
His lyrics weren’t just good; they were revolutionary. There are a lot of writers out there who are able to tap into something and make it so that you feel emotional about a topic; however, Dylan did more than that. He peeled back the layers to your very soul, to your perception of reality and society, in turn showing you things you didn’t previously know were there. Bruce Springsteen called him the “father of my country” for as much. 
“I was very influenced by Dylan,” said The Boss, “
I always say he’s the father of my country
. He initially provided me with a picture of a country that I recognised. One that feels real, feels like the truth.” 
Dylan’s unique lyricism wasn’t just present in his songs either, but they could also be heard in the way he spoke to people. It was this that made it difficult for a lot of people to talk to Dylan, as he was impossible to get a coherent sentence out of. David Crosby, who was rising to the top of the music industry around the same time as Dylan, recalls meeting the musician in 1964 and being surprised at just how difficult it was to chat. 
“He’s friendly, but he’s not out front. He doesn’t let you in,” said the musician during an interview with 
Stereogum
. “You’ll say, ‘Bob, where do you live?’ And he’ll say, ‘Well, you’re looking at a man that has no home.’ He’d be telling you about life instead of telling you he lived in Malibu. He’s not an easy guy. To this day, he’s not an easy guy.”
Of course, Crosby didn’t need to understand the way that Bob Dylan spoke; he just needed to understand his music, and he certainly did that. In fact, you could say that Crosby understood Dylan more than Dylan understood himself, as it was his ornate ability to hear his music in a different way to others that led to Dylan picking up an electric guitar, 
and that some people even credit with the invention of folk rock
. 
You see, there was no getting around the fact that Bob Dylan was one of the biggest musicians on the planet in 1964, and because the songs were relatively simple to play, there were a lot of artists who covered him. However, Crosby and his band The Byrds took things to another level, as rather than simply cover a Bob Dylan song, they decided to change up the time signature and make it something that people could dance to.

His band weren’t too sure about the original song when they first heard it, but they were eventually won over when presented with the new idea. Granted, it was Roger McGuinn who originally came up with this new style of folk, but Crosby’s ability to pick up the ball and run with it helped get things over the line. Who needs to be able to hold a conversation with Bob Dylan when you can do so much more with his music?  
“Bob Dylan had written it in a very countrified groove, a straight 2/4 time signature, and Roger takes the song home and works with it, puts it in 4/4 time, so you could dance to it,” said the band’s bassist, Chris Hillman, “Bob heard us do it and said, ‘Man, you could dance to this!’ It really knocked him over and he loved it.”

Why You Have 27 Tabs Open (and How to Finally Close Them)&nbsp;&nbsp;ADWEEK

[page text]
By 
Matthew Kayser
 

 

 

 

For many internet users, it is not uncommon to have dozens of tabs open across work, shopping, research, and entertainment websites. While some might believe that these tabs can be permanent fixtures on one’s browser without causing problems, the truth is that multiple tabs can impact a variety of things, including personal productivity, work performance, and even hardware slowdown. Platforms such as the 
Ace Browser
 aim to change this.
With new sorting tools now available, and grouping options possible in modern browsers, those browsing online now have the option to take control of their browsing habits, all starting in the browser.
Why people end up with too many browser tabs open
When it comes to work-related tasks, or even personal projects, the idea of losing a piece of information often spurs people to keep tabs open longer than they should. The belief that they may lose them forever, which often revolves around the concept of “unfinished business,” is rooted in psychology, particularly the Zeigarnik Effect.
The Zeigarnik Effect, a phenomenon in which people remember uncompleted or unfinished tasks more than completed ones, affects productivity in many ways. While 
research on unfinished or unfulfilled goals
 has been shown to influence motivation and goal setting, the concept of keeping tabs open to prevent the loss of information may go beyond motivation and goals, resulting in decision fatigue.
How too many tabs hurt productivity and browser performance
Too many open browser tabs can hurt your productivity in a variety of ways.
Tabs can create mental clutter. An overabundance of open tabs may create a distracting environment. This can easily lead to slower decision-making, making it harder to retrieve information, especially if the tabs you have open have not been organized into groups. As a result, a user may be unable to find information, especially in tabs that have multiple versions of the same page open.
Physical hardware limitations can also be influenced by the number of tabs open. Since a web page stores information at all times, leaving multiple web pages open across tabs can reduce your browser performance and may even slow down a computer due to the increased amount of RAM it uses. This can result in not only browser crashes, but can also drain power on laptops more quickly.
How to reduce tab overload without losing important information
Reducing tab overload can be as simple as sorting tabs more effectively or by creating reading lists.
Some ideas may include:
 
Bookmarking intentionally.
 Bookmarks don’t store information actively, and instead, allow you to sort the web pages you might later need in specialized folders or tabs on your browser.
 
Utilizing reading lists.
 A reading list, when paired with a bookmarking folder, may help you read more intentionally, especially if you pair them with other sorting ideas.
 
Creating project folders.
 Using a folder in your browser, or even hyperlinks on your desktop, can help you organize links by project and stay organized.
 
In addition to these ideas, you should also schedule weekly tab cleanups in order to ensure your browser is running at its optimal speed. Closing duplicate tabs immediately may also help you stay organized, as can adopting a “one task, one window” workflow.
How Ace browser makes tab management easier
For programs like Ace Browser, tab management comes in the form of the functionality it offers. For many, this can come in the form of a cleaner, less distracting workspace, while others may value the way the platform organizes tabs and workspaces.
Certain platforms concentrate on five key things, which include:
 
A cleaner workspace
 to help users maintain focus during long sessions.
 
Better organization
 to help manage time and reduce visual clutter.
 
Easier navigation
 to aid in switching between projects.
 
Features that reduce tab clutter,
 such as AI-powered sorting.
 
Focus integrations
 that help users find what they’re looking for without hunting for a specific page.
 
These tools are designed to reduce excessive open windows, automate tab organization, and limit visual clutter.
Daily habits to keep tab chaos from returning
While many users are well aware of the many tabs they have open at any point in time, there are a few things that can be done to keep tabs from overwhelming one’s life.
The first is to utilize an end-of-day cleanup to close unnecessary tabs. This can also be combined with archiving needed tabs in folders for easier access.
The second is to review bookmarks weekly. By removing those you don’t need, you can better sort through the visual clutter that your folders may be filled with, and help you streamline the information you need. Keyboard shortcuts and browser extensions can also help aid in reducing the number of open tabs you have.
Last but not least, consider separating your work browsing from your personal internet searches through a browser’s profile features, which many have.
Tab overload is common, and small habits can create lasting improvements to your organization, which may reduce stress and improve your focus. With browsers such as Ace Browser, you can work to refine your workflow through a variety of features, all by utilizing built-in tools.

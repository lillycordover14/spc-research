Playing with heavy legs and a shorthanded roster, the Celtics fell to the Houston Rockets, 109-93, on Monday.

Boston rested veteran wing Paul George on the second night of a back-to-back and couldn’t replicate his production at either end of the floor. Mainstays Jayson Tatum, Derrick White, Payton Pritchard and Sam Hauser shot a combined 9-for-41 from the field (22.0%) and 2-for-22 from 3-point range (9.1%) as Boston’s five-game win streak came to an end against a tough and talented Houston squad.

The Celtics got solid scoring efforts from big men Neemias Queta (16 points, 6-for-7, six rebounds) and Luka Garza (14 points, 4-for-7 from three) but were overwhelmed by two-time All-Star center Alperen Sengun, who posted a monster stat line (26 points, 12-for-15, seven rebounds, 10 assists, two steals, two blocks).

Led by Sengun, the Rockets outscored the Celtics 50-34 in the paint. They also finished with an 18-2 edge in fast-break points.

Joe Mazzulla’s squad kept it close for three quarters before caving in the fourth. The loss — Boston’s first since opening night — dropped the Celtics to 5-2. They’ll return home to face the 1-6 Milwaukee Bucks on Wednesday (7:30 p.m.) before playing 10 of their next 13 away from TD Garden.

Houston improved to 7-1 in its fourth season under former Celtics head coach Ime Udoka.

“We came in here last year on a tough back-to-back … and won,” Mazzulla told reporters in his postgame news conference, referring to his team’s 
win in Houston under similar circumstances
 back in February. “I thought we came in with the right mindset. I thought when we had it to seven (points) at halftime, we were in good shape. We had it to five, and then they went on that run at the end of the third quarter and at the beginning of the fourth.

“Especially in games like that, you have to be able to manage those runs. I thought those two were the ones that kind of got away from us.”

Mazzulla made a couple of notable tweaks to his starting lineup. Baylor Scheierman made his first start of the season in George’s absence, and Queta got the nod over Mitchell Robinson at center. Robinson started the Celtics’ first five games before sitting out Sunday’s win in Orlando for rest purposes.

Hugo Gonzalez, who replaced Jordan Walsh in the starting five against the Magic, remained there against the Rockets.

Boston’s top unit of those three, Tatum and White had not played together before Monday.

Scheierman was a handful for Houston in the opening minutes. He picked Kevin Durant’s pocket to start a fast break, denied a lob to Sengun at the rim and drew a charge on Amen Thompson. At the other end, the third-year wing hit a corner 3-pointer and a turnaround one-footed fadeaway.
 

Gonzalez also was active early, scoring six first-quarter points on a putback, a steal-and-score and a well-timed baseline cut. Tatum, who came in averaging 7.7 assists per game, found Gonzalez for that backdoor layup, then connected with Hauser on a similar make later in the first quarter.

The Rockets built a 10-point lead against the Celtics’ second unit thanks to two Tari Eason threes and a bizarre own goal by Tatum. Third-string center Garza denied a long outlet pass to Sengun, and in the ensuing fight for the rebound, the ball deflected off Tatum’s hands and into Boston’s basket.

But the Celtics pulled even by the end of the first, 29-29, on two Garza 3-pointers and a pair of strong Tatum drives.

Boston opened the second quarter with a new three-guard lineup featuring White, Pritchard and seldom-used veteran Mike Conley, who saw the first non-garbage-time minutes of his Celtics tenure. That grouping was short-lived, and the Celtics struggled to generate offense during Tatum’s first breather of the night. Threes by Conley and Garza were the only points Boston scored in the more than six minutes that Tatum sat.

Arguably the Celtics’ biggest first-half issue, though, was Robinson. The 7-footer had a few rough possessions against Sengun during his first four-minute shift, suffered a thumb sprain that sent him to the locker room, then committed fouls on back-to-back plays after returning.

Robinson logged just seven minutes before halftime, during which Boston was outscored by 14 points. Queta was a plus-9 in the first half, and Garza was a plus-7 as the Celtics trailed 57-50 at the break.

Mazzulla changed tactics to start the third quarter, sending out Robinson and Queta together in a double-big lineup. That grouping was more successful, staging a 12-3 run early in the second half to cut Houston’s lead to one.

Queta started that rally with a 3-pointer from the corner — his second of the season and third of his NBA career — followed by consecutive putbacks. Robinson finished it with a lob dunk from Tatum.

Overall, though, it was another underwhelming outing from Robinson, who has not been the same floor-tilting frontcourt presence in his first season with the Celtics that he was for the New York Knicks. Boston has been outscored with him on the court in five of his six appearances, and his total plus/minus is a team-worst minus-42.

Asked by reporters in Houston about his muted impact on the offensive glass, Robinson replied: “I don’t know, bro, to be honest. I’ve got to figure it out soon, though. I don’t know.”

And that one-point deficit was as close as the Celtics got. The Rockets regained control, holding Boston to seven free throws and zero made field goals over the final six-plus minutes of the third quarter.

Old friend Marcus Smart stepped into the spotlight during that stretch. Now a backup guard for Houston, the former longtime Celtic delivered his trademark mix of feisty defense and over-the-top theatrics, drawing charges against Tatum on consecutive plays and trying in vain to draw two more on Pritchard and Garza.

Those were two of Tatum’s season-high five turnovers in the loss. He finished with 19 points, nine assists and six rebounds, partially offsetting his erratic shooting by going 8-for-8 from the foul line.

Smart, who went 1-for-11 and 0-for-7 in his first two games against the Celtics after his 2023 trade, was a similarly inefficient 2-for-10 from the field in this one. But he drilled two late 3-pointers to help his new club pull away in the fourth quarter.

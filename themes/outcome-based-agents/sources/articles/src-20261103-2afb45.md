Ripping Packs at Lunch, Then the Bathroom
On Oct. 29, Cam's son, who's also a card collector, came over for lunch. Between bites, Cam showed him how Packs works. They ripped Football, Hoops, and Pokémon packs and pulled a few cards, hitting a few, but nothing crazy.
Then his son went back upstairs, and Cam went where physical packs should never be ripped — the bathroom. That left only one option — more PrizePicks Packs.
"I did what any dude would do after lunch: I go to the bathroom and sit on the toilet for a little bit,” Cam said. “Whenever I do that, of course you've got to have your phone, and of course you've got to have PrizePicks. That's when I started opening up the packs."
He stuck with Hoops, moving between $10, $25, and the occasional $50 pack. But he said $25 “felt really good for some reason,” so he stuck with it for a while, pulling GOAT, Legendary, and MVP cards — the tiers best for pulling higher-value cards. But he was just getting started.
The $2.8 Million Rip
Still sitting on the toilet, he eventually noticed one rip looked different after he swiped to open the pack.
"It just seemed like it was different from all the other rips,” Cam said. “You're seeing the white, the silver, the gold, the red, and then you see this purplish. I'm immediately like, 'No way, dude. It is there. It does exist.’”
The reveal almost slid past it, as PrizePicks Packs collectors may be familiar with in the animation while opening their packs.
"It did that thing where you thought it was going to go by it, and then it clicks back, and boom: Grail,” Cam said. “Did I expect to see an NBA Debut Patch? Absolutely not. It blew my mind."
Cam had been hunting for an NBA Debut Patch card since the first ones came out. Now he had the Harper 1/1 sitting in his Locker on PrizePicks Packs.

Sell It or Ship It? Inside Cam's $2.88 Million Decision
Every pull on PrizePicks Packs gives you a choice: sell it back instantly at 100% of fair market value, or keep it in your Locker and ship it home. Cam didn't rush it. He put the card in his Locker first. He still needed to let the emotions of what just happened truly wash over him.
"I took some screenshots of it and everything. Just really thinking, 'Dude, is this real? Did I seriously just pull this card?'” Cam said. “Just put it in the locker for a second, look at it, and really be in the moment. 'Wow, I really did just hit this?'"
Keeping it was tempting. He's a Spurs fan, and he'd thought about having the card shipped so he could hold it. He even considered offering it to Dylan Harper himself; remember, the Mavericks offered 31 years of season tickets and a signed game-worn Flagg rookie jersey as part of a bigger package to secure Flagg’s Debut Patch card to the collector who pulled it.
"It's his best card ever, and I think the athletes should have an opportunity to have their best card ever,” Cam said. “I'd reach out to him personally and be like, 'Look, dude, look what I got. It's yours if you want it.'"
Instead, Cam and his wife took some time to process before making the next move. They grabbed food and went to his office, away from the kids and the noise, to make a plan.
He ultimately decided to sell the card back at 100% of fair market value, and $2,880,000 hit his PrizePicks balance.

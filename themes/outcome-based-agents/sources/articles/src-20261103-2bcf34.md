The Republican gubernatorial team of Vice Speaker Tony Ada and running mate E.J. Calvo may have started out just behind their Democrat opponents as the votes rolled in Tuesday night, but that disadvantage wasn't meant to last, as the two ultimately managed to eke out the victory this general election. 
Supporters at the Ada-Calvo campaign headquarters in Hagåtña appeared cautious, but still optimistic, as early results for the general election showed the Democrat gubernatorial team of Sen. Therese Terlaje and running mate Sen. Sabina Perez leading the tally, but only by a razor-thin margin of 80 votes.
The atmosphere quickly turned ecstatic as later results reversed that trend and showed Ada-Calvo leading the vote count.
The Terlaje-Perez campaign eventually conceded to the Ada-Calvo team around 1:40 a.m. Wednesday. 
"(Terlaje) just congratulated E.J. and I on our successful bid for governor and lieutenant governor. I thank her for that," Ada told The Guam Daily Post after he and Calvo celebrated with their supporters. 
"(Terlaje and Perez) ran a very good campaign. Both campaigns ... were great. You've never seen any type of gubernatorial campaign like this in the past where we're very respectful of each other. It's always the supporters that have different views, but overall, the general respect we had for each other was first and foremost and top-notch," Ada added. 
Final unofficial results showed the Ada-Calvo team leading the governor's race by 1,020 votes.
After eight years of Democrat leadership under Gov. Lou Leon Guerrero, Guam is now poised to return to a Republican administration.
Leon Guerrero had succeeded Republican former Gov. Eddie Calvo.
"We're just going to continue to move forward. We have the transition with (Gov. Lou Leon Guerrero's) administration. ... We're looking forward to going with this transition now, going through the agencies, and move forward from there, all the way up to inauguration day," Ada told The Post. 
The Post also asked Ada what he planned to do first after assuming office. 
"Like I said, we're going to see where we're at in government. Once the transition reports come in, all those things that have been an issue, we'll try to address them immediately once we get into office," Ada said. 
Ada and Calvo thanked supporters, volunteers, their wives and family, and everyone who participated in the election in a release Wednesday morning. 
"Regardless of how you voted, we are committed to serving all the people of Guam with respect, integrity, and dedication," the release stated.
"The campaign may be over, but our work together is just beginning. We look forward to bringing our community together, listening to our people, and working toward a stronger future for our island," the release added. 
The Post had caught up with the Ada-Calvo team Tuesday morning, as Ada was casting his ballot at C.L. Taitano Elementary School, the Sinajana voting precinct.
He was joined by his wife, Annette Ada, and daughter, Alejandra Ada, as well as former Guam Police Department Chief Joseph Cruz, who served as the police chief under Calvo.
Cruz has been joining Ada on the campaign trail, and when asked if he would be the next Guam police chief, Ada said that hadn't been discussed. 
"What we've been discussing is how we're going to move forward in this campaign, getting over the primary election, and now, getting over the general election," Ada said Tuesday morning. 
"Once we become victorious in the general election, then all those things will come into play. That's how we'll be able to determine our cabinet and everyone else who will be participating in our administration," Ada stated Tuesday morning.

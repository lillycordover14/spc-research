Serval Raises $150 Million to Expand Enterprise AI Automation

Serval has raised $150 million in a Series C funding round led by Sequoia, valuing the enterprise service management and automation company at $3 billion. Announced on October 29, 2026, the round brings the company's total funding to $277 million and marks its third funding round in 12 months.

Existing investors Elad Gil, Evantic, First Round, General Catalyst and Redpoint participated, alongside new investors Conviction and Headline.

The latest financing comes as enterprises explore how AI agents can automate operational work beyond software engineering. Serval is positioning its platform around a broader ambition: enabling employees across departments to build and operate automated workflows without requiring extensive coding experience.

Bringing AI Automation Beyond Engineering

Serval's platform is designed to help organisations automate repetitive processes across IT, security, human resources, finance, legal and other business functions.

Its Catalyst automation agent translates employees' requirements into code-based workflows that can run within enterprise controls. Rather than relying exclusively on custom scripts, traditional workflow builders or lengthy implementation projects, employees can describe what they need and use AI to help build the automation.

The platform includes administrative safeguards such as permissions, approval processes and controls over how workflows operate. These capabilities are important as organisations move AI agents closer to business-critical systems, where an automated action can affect access rights, sensitive information or operational processes.

The company's broader strategy is to combine AI-driven automation with enterprise service management, allowing organisations to manage requests, assets, changes and approvals alongside the workflows designed to address them.

Challenging Established Enterprise Software

Serval is pursuing an ambitious position in the enterprise software market, where established platforms manage IT service operations and internal workflows.

The company argues that AI-generated code can make it easier to build and maintain automations that traditionally required specialist engineering resources. Its strategy is to compete directly with legacy service-management platforms rather than simply adding another AI layer to an existing technology stack.

Serval says one automotive manufacturer replaced ServiceNow with its platform in 45 days, across IT service management, configuration management, hardware and software asset management, and additional departmental workflows.

The company also reported that its annual recurring revenue doubled every quarter following its Series B. It said two Fortune 20 companies became customers in the six months before its latest announcement, while Fox Corporation, WPP, Live Nation and Ramp are among the organisations using its platform.

These figures and customer examples were disclosed by Serval and have not been independently audited in the funding announcement.

Funding to Accelerate Hiring and Enterprise Adoption

Serval plans to use the new capital to expand its product capabilities and deepen its work with enterprise customers. The company expects to hire more than 500 people over the next 12 months, including more than 100 Automation Engineers for its new professional services arm, ProServal.

These specialists will work alongside customer teams to identify opportunities for automation, redesign operational processes and reduce repetitive work. The company also plans to train some recent graduates for these roles, combining technical capabilities with the practical knowledge of employees who understand how enterprise operations function.

The approach reflects a growing focus on implementation as a critical part of enterprise AI adoption. Beyond developing AI agents, companies must identify suitable workflows, integrate systems, establish governance and ensure automated processes operate within organisational policies.

For Serval, embedding specialists within customer organisations could help accelerate that process while creating opportunities to expand platform adoption.

A Larger Bet on AI-Native Enterprise Systems

The $3 billion valuation represents a significant increase from Serval's reported $1 billion valuation following its December 2025 Series B round. The latest financing signals continued investor interest in companies attempting to use AI to reshape established enterprise software categories.

The opportunity, however, extends beyond automating individual tasks. Enterprises must determine whether AI-native platforms can reliably handle complex workflows, integrate with existing systems and meet requirements for security, oversight and accountability.

Serval's strategy rests on making automation accessible to a wider range of employees while retaining the administrative controls required by large organisations.

With its latest funding, the company is preparing to scale that model across more enterprise customers and operational functions. Its progress will help illustrate whether AI-powered workflow creation can become a viable alternative to established service-management software.

About Serval

Serval
 is an AI-native enterprise service management and automation company headquartered in San Francisco. Its platform combines service management capabilities with AI agents that help organisations build and run workflows across IT and other business functions. Its Catalyst product enables employees to create governed automations, while its wider platform supports requests, assets, access management and operational processes.

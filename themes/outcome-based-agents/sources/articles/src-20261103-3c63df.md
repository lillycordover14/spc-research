Cresta Fills An Important Gap In Salesforce’s Customer Service AI Story
 

Share

 

Salesforce has agreed to acquire Cresta
, adding voice agents, real-time agent assistance, and conversation intelligence to its customer service portfolio. Salesforce already owns much of the customer context and many of the systems where customer service work gets done. What it lacked was a credible conversation intelligence offering to understand what happens within those interactions and take action. Cresta, 
one of the market’s most technically innovative providers
, fills that gap.

But Salesforce should resist treating Cresta only as a customer service AI asset. Cresta’s conversation-derived feedback and predictive customer satisfaction score could also strengthen Salesforce’s much weaker 
customer feedback management
 story, particularly for CX teams under pressure to consolidate their tech stacks and get more from platforms they already own.

Salesforce Is Buying The Missing Pieces Of Its Agentic Architecture

The Cresta deal is the latest in a series of acquisitions Salesforce is using to fill gaps in its 
agentic architecture
. Cresta is a smart addition, bringing sophisticated technology and expertise in an area where Salesforce had a meaningful gap. But Salesforce is layering these acquisitions onto an already complex and fragmented product estate, with overlapping capabilities and a 
complicated buying experience
. A crisper architectural vision doesn’t fix that.

Make Salesforce Prove That Ownership Creates Value

As customer relationship management and contact center platforms 
increasingly converge
, customer service leaders must decide which platform should own which capabilities. The promise of that convergence should be less complexity for vendors’ customers, not simply more functionality under one vendor’s roof.

Existing Cresta customers should watch whether Salesforce preserves what made Cresta valuable: its product velocity, access to its teams, and the ability to work across the contact center technology they already use. Salesforce has committed to maintaining support for leading contact center and telephony platforms after the acquisition closes. Buyers should hold it to that promise.

Prospective buyers that already have Salesforce but still haven’t invested in strong conversation intelligence capabilities have plenty of reasons to be excited. But Salesforce should clearly distinguish what they can buy and deploy today from capabilities that depend on future product integration.

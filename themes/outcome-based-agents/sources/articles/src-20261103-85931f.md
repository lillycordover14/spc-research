Lisa Harper Brings Theology to the Masses
						
						
					

						By Ann Byle
						
							 |
						
					

						Nov 03, 2026

Lisa Harper has spent her career teaching around the world across every possible platform: television, conferences, radio, a podcast, books, Bible studies. But she never lets herself or anyone else forget that “theology is not about academia; it’s about intimacy with Jesus.”
Her new book 
Theology for the Rest of Us: A User-Friendly Guide to Super Cool, Really Transformational Stuff About God and Why It Matters 
(Revell, out now) is a case in point.
“In my opinion, in first-world Christian circles we think that theology is for old men in seminaries. We tend to assume it’s an academic pursuit,” Harper told 
PW
. “But theology is more life-giving. I’m with people who don’t give two hoots about a multi-syllabic theological word, but about the concept behind it.”
Harper recalls a story from when her adopted daughter first came from Haiti as a young child. “All of a sudden she’s in Nashville with this old windbag,” she said, laughing. “I was trying to explain something, and her face was set in an ‘I don’t get this’ look. I sensed the Holy Spirit say, ‘Use less words.’ I said, ‘Yes Sir.’”
That “less words” suggestion began informing her life. In her book, she explains 50 terms—from 
apophatic theology
 (defining God by what he is not) to 
theopneustos
 (breathed by God)—using everyday language and stories from real life. At the end of each chapter, Harper writes about why the word is worth attention. For example, her chapter on “Why 
Teleological Arc 
Matters” concludes that it counts “because it provides the framework for understanding the ultimate meaning of life.”
Harper remembers, in the last academic class of her doctorate, her professor talking about cataphatic theology and apophatic theology. (She describes the former as learning about God by what we can know and the latter as learning about God by what we don’t know.) “I thought, wouldn’t it be cool to take these big words and find the biblical truism behind them?” she recounts. “We don’t need to know the term ‘theodicy,’ for example, but we do need to know the concept that God is good when life is hard.”
Rachel McRae, executive editor at Revell, tells 
PW
, “Lisa has spent decades helping the Bible come alive for readers around the world. But in the last few years, she's doubled down on helping us understand that theology isn't just for pastors and professors—it's for all of us. Each term reflects the intentional and transformative love God has for each of us.”
Harper is also the author of 
A Jesus-Shaped Life: How Diving Deeper into Theology Can Transform Us and Our World with the Radical Kindness of God
 (Revell, 2025) as well as devotionals and other titles, each blending her love of theology and her storytelling prowess honed over years as a Bible teacher. She holds a doctorate from Houston Theological Seminary, and speaks at 50 or so conferences or churches per year.
Harper says her work “is less about making theology accessible than about God making himself accessible. Less about ethics than about a relationship with God.”
"I’m stunned I get to talk about God as a job. It’s all of our jobs, but I get to make a living at it,” she adds. “The longer I’ve walked with Jesus, the more it’s the kindness of God that slays me. It’s not the big words, but God’s kindness.”

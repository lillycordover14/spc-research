Salesforce Buys Cresta, and AI Stack Questions Grow
Salesforce’s Cresta agreement gives Agentforce a major voice AI asset, but analysts warn the harder test is coherence. CX buyers now need to assess integration, platform neutrality, and whether voice automation improves real customer resolution economics.
Rob Wilkinson
Save
Follow
share
Salesforce’s agreement to acquire Cresta gives the Salesforce Cresta strategy a stronger voice AI story, but the deal also raises a harder enterprise question: can Salesforce turn another AI acquisition into a coherent customer service platform?

Salesforce announced today that it had signed a definitive agreement to acquire Cresta, which it describes as a voice-first operating system for customer experience. The company said the deal will bring Cresta’s voice agents, real-time conversation intelligence, coaching, and quality capabilities closer to Service Cloud and Agentforce.

The transaction remains subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approvals. Salesforce expects it to close in the first quarter of fiscal 2028. 
Dave Michels, Lead Analyst at TalkingPointz
, framed the acquisition as an integration test rather than a simple feature upgrade:

“Salesforce plans to integrate Cresta with Service Cloud while maintaining support for existing contact center and telephony platforms. The acquisition expands Salesforce’s CX Frankenstack.”

Michels added that the deal leaves two questions open: whether Salesforce can combine its growing AI acquisitions into a coherent offering, and where Alan Ranger, who moved from Cognigy to Cresta after Cognigy was acquired by NiCE, goes next.

The first question is the one enterprise buyers will need to track most closely. Cresta may strengthen Salesforce’s voice AI capabilities, but the value of the acquisition depends on how well Salesforce connects voice agents, CRM data, Service Cloud workflows, Agentforce orchestration, coaching, and quality management into one usable operating model.

Salesforce Cresta Deal Creates a Coherence Test

Salesforce framed the acquisition around the difficulty of automating phone-based service at enterprise scale. Cresta counts Marriott, Comcast, and Alaska Airlines among the voice agent customers named in Salesforce’s announcement. 
Kishan Chetan, EVP and GM of Agentforce and Service Cloud at Salesforce
, positioned voice as the service channel where AI has the least room to fail:

“Voice is where service is hardest to get right, and where it matters most to customers. Cresta has built voice agents that handle complex, high-volume calls for some of the world’s largest brands. As more service is resolved in the conversation itself, together we’ll bring the customer, the conversation and the outcome into one place.”

Salesforce already had a contact center story before the Cresta agreement. CX Today recently reported on 
TTEC Digital landing the first Agentforce Contact Center go-live
, a deployment that showed Salesforce moving Agentforce from announcement to production use.

Cresta adds a different layer. Salesforce says the company’s platform can resolve complex calls end to end, guide human representatives in real time, analyze interactions, coach representatives, test voice agents, and measure both human and AI performance against the same quality standard.

Those capabilities could make Agentforce more relevant in high-volume service environments. Yet Michels’ “Frankenstack” warning points to a familiar enterprise risk: more acquired capability does not automatically create a simpler operating model for service teams.

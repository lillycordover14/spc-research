Cyril Adonis Shaw
August 3, 1977 — November 3, 2026
Cyril Adonis Shaw, fondly known as Donnie Black, was born in Chicago, Illinois, on August 3, 1977. On November 3, 2026, at the age of 49, Donnie left this world, leaving behind a legacy of love, laughter, and unforgettable memories. He was a devoted father, a loyal partner, and a cherished friend, whose presence brought warmth to all who knew him.

Donnie's life was a tapestry woven with deep connections and joyous moments. He was the beloved son of France’s Shaw, never having met his father, and he grew up surrounded by siblings who shared in his journey through life. Though he did not complete high school, Donnie was a self-educated and intelligent man who always aspired to earn his GED, demonstrating a lifelong commitment to bettering himself.

His career took him through the doors of H&A and Scott Peterson warehouse as a young adult, where he worked diligently to provide for his family. He was a man of strength and sacrifice, always ensuring his loved ones were cared for. Donnie's partnership with Felicia Pack, which spanned over 27 years, was a testament to enduring love and companionship. Together they raised a beautiful family, including his beloved children: Diamond Donesha Nicole, Darren Sky, Dontia, Amir, and Trevon Jr. He took immense pride in his role as a father and was blessed with two grandchildren, who brought him endless joy.

Donnie's life was filled with simple pleasures that brought him happiness. He had a passion for music, found thrill in playing Mortal Kombat, and cherished evenings spent watching old movies. His heart remained tied to the streets where he grew up, where friendships and family ties flourished. Known for his radiant smile and infectious laughter, Donnie had a personality that could light up any room. Even in moments of adversity, he exuded positivity and offered unwavering support to those around him. He was a man of understanding, never quick to judge, and always ready to lend a listening ear to his children, assuring them with his comforting words, "Daughter, I understand."

Though he attended church only a few times, Donnie's faith in God was unshakeable. He spoke often of returning to church, a testament to his belief and spiritual yearning. His humorous spirit and active nature, even as a big man, were endearing qualities that earned him the nickname "Big Donnie."

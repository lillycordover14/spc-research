CHATTANOOGA — 
For most of us getting out on the water is as simple as walking down a dock, stepping into a boat, and heading off for a day of fishing or paddling.
For others, that seemingly simple process can be an almost insurmountable challenge. However, a new dock and kayak launch on Chickamauga Lake can change that. TVA recently held a special ribbon-cutting at a brand new, adaptive dock that gives individuals with physical challenges the opportunity to access the lake.
Officials and several politicians were on hand for the ceremonial ribbon-cutting for the new ADA-compliant, adaptive dock at Kings Point on Chickamauga Lake. (Photo by Richard Simms)
In partnership with the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency, TVA’s Environmental Resource Stewardship team recently completed a new "BoardSafe" adaptive kayak launch and courtesy pier at the
 Kings Point Launch Ramp
 near Chickamauga Dam.
Michael McCall, TVA Vice President for Environment and Stewardship, told the crowd gathered for the ribbon-cutting, "We recognize the importance of recreational access to the reservoirs that we manage, and being a partner with you all to put infrastructure like this in place is just as important as what we do managing the river system and generating power for the Valley every day."
The new dock is built especially to allow the physically-challenge to access a kayak, even without assistance from others.
Paul Pearman, the TVA Senior Manager over Environmental Resource Stewardship, demonstrating how to use the new dock. (Photo by Richard Simms)
Paul Pearman, the TVA Senior Manager over Environmental Resource Stewardship, explained and demonstrated some of the features that make the dock adaptive, including handrails, steps, and a sliding seat that someone can lower themselves onto from a wheelchair, then transfer from the seat to the kayak.
"There are straps they can hold onto. It's very stable, very secure," said Pearman. "And then, once you're in your kayak, you can use your arms and the rails to pull yourself out into the water. Again, it's very, very easy and very accessible to people of all abilities."
Pearman said the cost of the project was $125,000, paid for by TVA ratepayers. In partnership with TVA, TWRA engineers worked closely with a company called BoardSafe (HOTLINK: https://boardsafedocks.com/) to design and install the dock.
"Everything we do has to be done under ADA guidelines," said Jay McClellan, TWRA's Chief Engineer. "The very nature of this particular unit is so that anybody with ADA disabilities or with disabilities can use it."
Jay McClellan, Chief Engineer for the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency, explains how the new dock at Kings Point was built using ADA guidelines. He said, "This particular unit is so that anybody with ADA disabilities or with any disabilities can use it." (Photo by Richard Simms)
McClellan said they have worked with BoardSafe on many similar projects across the state, however this is the first such adaptive dock in the Chattanooga area. Booker T. Washington State Park does offer all-terrain wheelchairs to provide individuals with mobility impairments the opportunity to access and enjoy outdoor recreation areas that might otherwise be inaccessible.
"We've been working to get more opportunities to get more people on the water," said McClellan. "Not just those who are healthy but anybody who has a desire to get on the water. And when we discovered BoardSafe and what they were doing with this particular type of unit, that just created a partnership made in heaven because they came to us with experience from other parts of the country."
Pearman added, "Projects like this bring people here. People want to come here, live here, work here, and play here. So, we're excited to celebrate all that today."
The dock, designed and built by a company called BoardSafe, includes detailed instructions on how it can be used by the physically-challenged to safely board their kayak. (Photo by Richard Simms)
Additional local support for the project came from Hamilton County, Chattanooga Parks and Outdoors and Outdoor Chattanooga.

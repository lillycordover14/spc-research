"I'm trying to trust myself more": how 'Rivals' star Bella Maclean is stepping into her power&nbsp;&nbsp;Harper's BAZAAR

[page text]
Pip
Bella Maclean
, the breakout star of the blockbuster television series 
Rivals
, is sitting beside me on a bench on Hampstead Heath, looking effortlessly cool in Chopova Lowena tracksuit bottoms, a fleece and trainers. I’ve just asked her how it feels to have shot to global fame when a passing dog trots up to urinate by her foot.
Maclean bursts into a fit of laughter. If this were 
Rivals, 
it would surely be an omen of another romantic disaster involving Rupert Campbell-Black, the charismatic, self-sabotaging womaniser, and her character Taggie O’Hara, his on-off love interest in the Disney+ adaptation of 
Jilly Cooper
’s novel. For the 28-year-old, though, the moment is an amusing reality check in a life that is becoming more glamorous by the day.
Just two years ago, she arrived at 
Harper’s Bazaar
’s Women of the Year event as a fresh face, there to support Cooper as she collected our 
Writer of the Year award.
 Now, she is returning as a cover star to receive the Breakthrough award in association with the Platinum Card by American Express. The recognition still leaves her "speechless", she admits. "I have to practise patting myself on the back – it’s so rare that we do it."

Her thoughts quickly turn to Cooper, who died less than a year after that night, aged 88, following a fall at her home in Gloucestershire. The loss was devastatingly sudden, but her presence in 
Rivals
 – whose latest run of episodes is about to reach our screens – still feels strong. "It’s never going to not be – Jilly is the heart of everything we do, and I feel her naughtiness a lot in the second half of the new season," says Maclean. "This series is so juicy, it gets so intense. That’s all Jillyness. She pushed things."
Since 
Rivals
 landed in October 2024, the battle between Tony Baddingham (David Tennant), the ruthless boss of Corinium Television, and his nemesis, the charismatic politician and equestrian Campbell-Black (Alex Hassell), has dominated the action. To recap: Campbell-Black and the television presenter Declan O’Hara, played with debonair aplomb by Aidan Turner, form a rival production company; the competition turns deadly when Baddingham’s wife Monica discovers his affair with O’Hara’s wife. The last episode to air on TV left viewers with a cliffhanger in the form of a fatal car crash amid the Great Storm of 1987; the new episodes pick up in the aftermath of this catastrophe with Monica’s funeral.

For roughly 15 minutes, the show behaves itself: sombre faces, black clothes and exaggerated displays of grief. Then, before the flowers have wilted, affairs are reignited, a liaison unfolds in a church courtyard and a new love triangle forms around Taggie, the sheltered innocent who is about to become the show’s most surprising sexual force. No longer willing to wait for the much older Campbell-Black to decide what he wants from her, she has taken up with a new paramour: Basil ‘Bas’ Baddingham (Luca Pasqualino), the charismatic, fun-loving younger half-brother of Tony Baddingham and the owner of the local pub, Bar Sinister. "She doesn’t want to wait around for a man who’s unsure about her," says Maclean. "There’s another who she thinks is presenting himself in a way that is charming, who sees her, cares, and is more her age. She’s showing Rupert there’s more to life than him."

Against a backdrop of hedonistic parties and Bonfire Night chaos, the drama reaches its climax at the Hampshire Hunt Ball, one of Rutshire’s various grand occasions, at which Taggie makes her entrance in a red, spaghetti-strap dress – quite the transformation from her usual waistcoats and cowboy boots. For Maclean, it felt a little as though she was making her own big reveal. "I remember walking through the doors and my heart going 'bang, bang, bang'," she says. "None of my mates had ever seen that part of Taggie, and they hadn’t seen me like that before either. It was exciting. At work, I’m normally in a tracksuit."

Rivals 
has been a phenomenon since its launch, a balm of British humour, delightful machinations and jolly polo sticks. With the fervour for all things Rutshire came sudden attention, and Maclean appeared to become famous overnight. In fact, though, she had been working towards an acting career since childhood. Born in New York to British parents, the youngest of three sisters, she moved to East Sussex aged 10; while at boarding school, she would spend her weekends in London attending drama classes, and she eventually graduated from the Guildhall School of Music & Drama in 2020. A year later, she made her professional debut in 
Spring Awakening
 at the Almeida Theatre, followed by roles in 
Silent Witness
 and 
Sex Education
 in 2023, and 
London Tide
 at the National Theatre in 2024.
From the outset, Maclean was clear about the boundaries between her professional and personal life. When she was cast in 
Rivals,
 she negotiated the inclusion of a no-nudity clause in her contract – a remarkable act of self-protection by a young actress entering a series fuelled by sex, desire and the constant possibility of exposure. The clause still protects her now. "It was important because of how pure Taggie’s character was," she says.

"I didn’t want to sexually exploit her, and I didn’t want to do that to myself either. That was really important to me, and I think it will stay important to me. There are some things I just want to keep to myself.
"I don’t judge anyone who does it," she continues. "Sometimes it’s so beautiful. If I had Victoria Smurfit’s [who plays Maud O’Hara] body, I’d probably be walking around naked! But in that sense, we’re all lucky because there are amazing intimacy co-ordinators on our job. I know that’s not the case for other people. We’re all good friends who can just say 'No' to the directors and they’ll listen."
"I have to practise patting myself on the back, it's so rare that we do it "
Indeed, the cast and crew have become her extended family. A few days before we meet, she was in southern Spain celebrating the wedding of her co-star Emily Atack, who plays the glamorous television presenter Sarah Stratton ("She looked breathtaking – I could cry thinking about it!"), along with numerous other cast members: Hassell, Tennant, the executive producer Felicity Blunt and her husband Stanley Tucci. Hassell, 46, has become one of her real-life emotional anchors. "He is such a contemplative man. We’ve become so close over this last series – it’s a bizarre feeling, being friends with a man who’s 18 years older than me and having such an earnest friendship." Equally, Pasqualino has proved a true companion: "He makes me laugh so much. Hopefully there’s good chemistry between us because we became really good friends. You want to believe that there is a possibility Taggie might move on. Wait until the last episode!"

Blunt, meanwhile, has been a valued mentor in Maclean’s professional journey so far, particularly as she has made the transition into the world of film, with forthcoming roles in Netflix’s 
A Matter of Time
, the old-school romantic comedy 
Hello & Paris
, and the indie drama 
In Starland.
 When she admitted she was feeling nervous about what lies ahead, Blunt texted her sister 
Emily
, the Hollywood veteran with two decades of prestige drama, blockbuster cinema and comedy behind her, who called Maclean immediately. They spoke for an hour.
"Emily talked me through her experiences and gave me amazing advice. She said, 'I’ve been doing this for 20 years, and it’s OK to feel nervous – you’re there for a reason, and you must remember that. The industry isn’t perfect – there are going to be moments when things are really unfair, and you need to know when to stand up for yourself.' She’s given me so much time and support." As for their first in-person encounter, it was typically irreverent. "I was doing an interview at the
 Rivals 
premiere in New York, and she walked past and pinched me on the bum!" says Maclean, laughing. "The Blunts are truly fabulous! They’re sophisticated, chic, intelligent, naughty and hilarious."

With or without the Blunts at her side, something tells me that Maclean won’t struggle to hold her own in the entertainment world. Her latest projects see her star alongside industry veterans: Javier Bardem and Kate Hudson in 
Hello & Paris,
 and Ben Stiller in 
A Matter of Time 
("It’s a real pinch-me moment, acting opposite someone I’ve spent my life admiring"). She’s also well on the way to proving she can play more than just an ingénue, transforming into an American drug addict for 
In Starland
, in what she calls "one of the most seminal experiences on any job I’ve ever done". The work has taken her from Bristol, where 
Rivals
 films, to Paris and Mississippi. She has barely been home, she explains, but now, having bought a new place of her own, she is looking forward to spending more time there this year.

By now, rain is beginning to spit across the Heath. Maclean looks out over the grass, reflective but not sentimental, as if the future has finally become something she can walk towards rather than keep trying to predict. "I used to be obsessed with fortune-tellers," she says. "But I stopped doing that over the past year. I put so much pressure on myself… This year, I’ve tried to take some of that pressure off and trust myself more – to know that I’m going in the right direction."
With the Women of the Year award, three films ahead of her and a third season of 
Rivals
 on the horizon, for once, the omens are impossible to misread.

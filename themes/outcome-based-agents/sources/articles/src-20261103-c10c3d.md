What Is Glean?

Glean provides enterprise search, knowledge discovery, and AI assistants that connect to a company’s applications and permissions.

The company sells recurring enterprise subscriptions, increasingly centered on AI assistants and agents grounded in internal business data.

Its competitive set includes Microsoft, Google, Coveo, Elastic, ServiceNow, Slack, and other enterprise-search or AI-assistant vendors. For investors, the important question is not simply whether the market is large, but whether Glean can turn product adoption into repeatable revenue, defensible economics, and a financing path that does not depend on increasingly expensive capital.

Glean Products, Projects, and Operating Milestones

Permission-aware enterprise search

Glean connects company applications and respects source permissions so employees can search across documents, messages, tickets, people, and knowledge without creating a second access-control system.

The enterprise knowledge graph

Relationships among people, content, teams, and activity help rank results and ground answers in the way a particular organization actually works.

AI assistants and agents

The company is extending search into assistants and agents that summarize knowledge and complete tasks across connected applications.

Connectors as distribution

A broad connector library increases usefulness, but every integration must remain secure and compatible as third-party applications change.

Notable Deployments and Recent Execution

Glean’s product has evolved from enterprise search into a permission-aware work layer for assistants and agents. The company says it has indexed 
more than 27 billion enterprise documents across over 100 connectors
, processes an annualized 20 trillion tokens, and has surpassed $200 million in annual recurring revenue. Those are company-reported metrics, but they help define the technical moat: relevance depends on connecting many systems, respecting each source’s permissions, and modeling relationships among people, content, and work activity.

The next product step is 
independent agents
 that can operate across applications while remaining grounded in company context and access rules. That expands the addressable market from finding information to completing multi-step work, but it also increases the consequence of an authorization mistake.

Key Risks

Competition from Microsoft, Google, and enterprise platforms

Glean competes with well-funded vendors that can bundle products, cut price, or use existing distribution. Differentiation must show up in retention, win rates, performance, and expanding customer spend.

Data-access and security failures

A failure here could directly damage customer trust and create remediation, legal, or regulatory costs. Investors should ask about independent testing, incident response, insurance, auditability, and how quickly problems are detected and contained.

High AI infrastructure costs

Growth may not create attractive economics if service, delivery, compute, or acquisition costs rise just as quickly. Cohort retention and contribution margin matter more than headline customer growth.

Fast-changing customer expectations

This risk can affect growth, financing needs, valuation, or exit timing. Investors should seek company-specific evidence, downside scenarios, and contractual protections instead of relying on the sector narrative.

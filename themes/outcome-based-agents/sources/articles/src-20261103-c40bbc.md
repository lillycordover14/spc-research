Enterprise architecture: Dell and Microsoft integrate hybrid AI

																																	by								
Mark Albertson

How can software and cloud giant Microsoft Corp. translate its platform vision into fully deployed enterprise architecture in the age of AI?

This central question has defined the next chapter in a decades-long partnership between Microsoft and Dell Technologies Inc. Dell provided some answers at this year’s 
Dell Technologies World
 by 
expanding
 its private cloud offering to deploy the latest Microsoft software implementations.

Since that event, Microsoft has worked to enable a 
disaggregated architecture
, simplifying operations across Dell PowerEdge compute, Dell PowerStore storage and Azure Local. Flexibility is the key attraction for enterprise customers evaluating Dell and Microsoft’s joint solutions, according to 
Paul Nashawaty
, principal analyst at theCUBE Research.

“Microsoft and Dell are moving beyond infrastructure partnerships to deliver a more integrated go-to-market strategy for enterprise AI, hybrid cloud and application modernization,” he said. “Since Dell Technologies World in May, the focus has been on connecting Microsoft Azure Local, SQL Server and hybrid-cloud capabilities with Dell’s automation platform and enterprise infrastructure to help customers deploy, govern and operate AI workloads wherever their data resides.”

This feature is part of SiliconANGLE Media’s exploration of the architectural shifts powering continuous, production-grade AI. Be sure to check out theCUBE’s “
Dell AI Data Platform Event: From Ambition to AI at Scale,”
 and past coverage of this year’s 
Dell Technologies World
. (* Disclosure below.)

Integrating enterprise architecture from hardware to workload

The market opportunity for both Dell and Microsoft centers on the need to operationalize AI applications. Meeting this goal requires integration across the full AI deployment lifecycle.

Dell 
Automation Platform
 and Dell 
Private Cloud
 provide an operational backbone for deploying Azure Local on-premises and managing the hardware lifecycle, including firmware and updates. Azure Arc provides consistent management across environments. SQL Server can be run in virtual machines or Kubernetes containers without learning separate skill sets for cloud and on-premises environments.

“The key architecture for AI capabilities is integrating with things like embedding models for vector searching or chat completion models for AI agents, but we do it all inside … the SQL Server Engine,” 
Bob Ward
, principal architect at Microsoft, 
told theCUBE
. “We [formed a] partnership with Dell so that we can connect to anything on premises. I can connect to Azure Local, Dell [and] Nvidia Factory. We just got a framework built in. It doesn’t matter where you’re running these AI models; we can easily integrate with it.”

A central element of Dell and Microsoft’s partnership involves integrating database, automation and hybrid cloud capabilities into a stack that can feed AI the data it needs. Data fragmentation is often cited as a 
top blocker
 to scaling AI beyond pilots, and integrating the two companies’ services addresses a pressure point that surveys have identified across enterprise information technology.

Data fragmentation 
remains a barrier
 to scaling AI beyond pilots. Dell and Microsoft’s partnership brings database, automation and hybrid cloud capabilities into a common stack.

“From an AppDev perspective, that integration matters because the challenge is no longer simply building AI applications, but operationalizing them securely, consistently and at scale,” said Nashawaty. “TheCUBE AppDev Research highlights the execution gap: Approximately 88% of AI pilots fail to reach production, while 64% of enterprise AI teams identify integration complexity as a leading barrier to adoption.”

Delivering AI for real-world outcomes

Dell’s partnership with Microsoft also extends to unstructured data storage. The two companies are bringing familiar storage capabilities into hybrid and multicloud environments.

Dell 
PowerScale for Microsoft Azure
 brings Dell’s OneFS file system software into Azure as a managed storage service. Customers can provision file storage directly from the Azure portal while retaining familiar PowerScale management capabilities.

The solution supports a 
wide range
 of production workloads across industries. These industries increasingly depend on high-throughput, low-latency file access to drive the next generation of agentic AI and advanced analytics. Media and entertainment firms can use it to handle massive video files, real-time editing and tight production timelines. Semiconductor and electronics design houses can accelerate simulation and modeling using large datasets.

“This isn’t really about running the software just in the cloud,” said 
Rachna Lalwani
, senior consultant, director and product manager at Dell, in an 
interview
 with theCUBE. “It’s about creating a truly integrated experience that empowers businesses to do more with their data. What they really gain is the operational consistency across their entire storage landscape.”

Integrating agents with PCs

A recent 
IDC analysis
 found that 95% of enterprises already run at least one AI agent workflow in production, and it predicted 2.5 billion agents by 2030. With the rise of agentic AI, Dell and Microsoft are collaborating on solutions designed to help enterprises deploy autonomous agents while controlling costs.

Managing the 
cost of tokens
, the fundamental units of data processed by large language models, is one of AI’s financial challenges. To help address it, Dell introduced 
Deskside Agentic AI
 in May in collaboration with Microsoft and Nvidia Corp., providing a local environment for building and testing AI agents.

“What’s resonating with customers right now is that the cost of tokens is 
through the roof,
” explained 
Jon Siegal
, senior vice president of Dell portfolio marketing at Dell. “We know even though the cost of a token has come down pretty dramatically, the amount of tokens that are being consumed right now is just astronomical. The cloud costs are just pretty high right now. How do customers balance that out and run the right workload in the right place? We’re trying to give customers a place to start. The PC is a great place to start.”

In early October, Dell 
announced
 an expansion of its Windows PCs and workstations for agentic AI. In partnership with Microsoft, Dell broadened its AI development portfolio to provide creators, developers and enterprises with more choice in how they build, test and scale agentic AI on Windows.

This marked a key step in the evolution of Windows, where the platform has evolved into a broader resource for agentic AI. Tools such as Windows AI Foundry give developers a structured path to build, package and scale agents inside a governed environment.

“With Windows, we’re going beyond just it being an operating system — we’re thinking about it as really 
a canvas for AI
,” said 
Mary Ann Anderson
, worldwide marketing director of Dell partnerships at Microsoft. “Pretty soon there’s going to be a point where individual employees are managing their own agents, maybe a small army of agents. You have to do that in a way that is governed and is very secure.”

Dell and Microsoft see a significant market opportunity in closing the gap between AI development and production deployment.

“Microsoft and Dell have an opportunity to address that gap by simplifying deployment, automating infrastructure operations and giving partners repeatable solutions they can take to market,” said theCUBE Research’s Nashawaty. “The real measure of success will be whether their combined ecosystem helps enterprises move from AI experimentation to production-ready applications with stronger governance, predictable costs and measurable business outcomes.”

Martha Abraham, Nicole Crosby, Antonio Martinez and incumbent Kent Lee are leading San Diego City Council runoffs by comfortable margins with Election Day vote counting finished.

A win by Abraham over incumbent Henry Foster in a Democrat-versus-Democrat battle in southeastern District 4 would be only the third time since 1992 an incumbent San Diego councilmember has lost re-election.

Abraham leads Foster by 1,585 votes. The Registrar of Voters still must count about 540,000 late-arriving ballots, but those ballots are unlikely to change the results of the four council races.

A win by Crosby, a Democrat, over independent Richard Bailey in coastal District 2 would likely keep the council all-Democrat and add a third former deputy city attorney to the council.

Crosby leads Bailey by 3,044 votes.

A win by Martinez, who is battling fellow Democrat Gerardo Ramirez, would mean a shift away from Assemblymember David Alvarez and his former staffers in South Bay District 8 for the first time since 2010.

Martinez leads Ramirez by 2,639 votes.

Lee, a Democrat seeking a second four-year council term, is battling Republican Mark Powell to represent Mira Mesa, University City and other north central neighborhoods.
 

Lee leads Powell by 5,200 votes.

Abraham said Tuesday night that the early results speak loudly about voter frustration with City Hall.

“These results so far say that District 4 is ready for change,” she said. “It’s time for a new direction and I’m honored they’ve entrusted me to represent them.”

Abraham, a 39-year-old neonatal nurse, said she identifies with the angst.
 

“My run started with my frustration with City Hall,” she said. “I understand the pain because I come from the same place.”

She said it took enormous effort for a political novice with no connections to be on the verge of accomplishing the rare feat of beating an incumbent.

“It’s not an easy thing to do,” she said. “We’ve been working and meeting with voters for a long time — this has been a long journey.”

Abraham said she hasn’t met most of the city’s other council members and conceded she will have a lot to learn. But she said that won’t slow her down.

“We expect to get to work as soon as we get there,” she said. “We don’t have a lot of time to waste.”

Crosby said by phone, just after the initial results were posted, that she was pleased to be ahead in a tough race.

“The numbers look pretty good,” she said. “We put in a lot of work in a race where a lot of money was spent against us.”

Crosby, who vowed to focus on details like fixing sidewalks and streetlights, said she thinks voters sent a positive message by choosing her over Bailey.

“San Diegans are looking for a positive tomorrow,” she said.

The battle between Abraham and Foster has divided politicians at City Hall and the city’s labor unions.

Mayor Todd Gloria tried to rally opposition to Foster, who has been perhaps his most vocal critic on the City Council, while the most progressive members of the council tried to bolster Foster’s campaign.

The Municipal Employees Association, which represents more than 40% of city workers, has sent mailers that support Foster and oppose Abraham. But the city’s firefighters union has sent mailers in support of Abraham.

Abraham would be the first nurse ever to serve on the San Diego City Council.

A win for her would also be notable in another way: Only two incumbents have lost San Diego council races since 1992 — Lorie Zapf in District 2 and Myrtle Cole in District 4, both in 2018.

Abraham lives in Emerald Hills. Foster, 54, lives in Valencia Park. District 4 also includes Encanto, Skyline, Paradise Hills, Oak Park, Chollas View, Webster and many nearby neighborhoods.

The battle between Bailey and Crosby has focused on slashing city jobs, taking money from developers, carpetbagging and whether there’s a need for contrarian voices at San Diego City Hall.

The race has attracted an unusually large amount of campaign cash.

Independent expenditure committees supporting either Bailey or Crosby spent more than $750,000 this fall.

Bailey, 40, lives in Point Loma. Crosby, 46, lives in Clairemont. District 2 also includes Mission Beach, Ocean Beach and Old Town.

The District 8 battle between Martinez and Ramirez has featured nasty mailers, big campaign donations and efforts to be seen as the outsider candidate.

The battle between Martinez, a San Ysidro schools trustee, and Ramirez, chief of staff to outgoing District 8 Councilmember Vivian Moreno, has also divided workers at City Hall and organized labor at large.

Martinez, 41, lives in San Ysidro. Ramirez, 32, lives in Otay Mesa. The district also includes Logan Heights, Sherman Heights, Nestor and Palm City.

Housing has been a key issue in the District 6 race, with Powell criticizing housing developer incentives that Lee has mostly supported.

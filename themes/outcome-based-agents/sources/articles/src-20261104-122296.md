LOWNDES COUNTY, Ala. (
WSFA
) - California-based AI infrastructure company Lambda announced Wednesday it will anchor Project Red Clay, a 2,200-acre artificial intelligence data center planned for northeastern Lowndes County.
Lambda is committing $33.1 billion to the campus.
County officials say the deal will bring more than 1,000 construction jobs, 225 full-time jobs and more than $1.6 billion directly to Lowndes County Public Schools through 2049.
Lambda says it will protect ratepayers and reduce water usage.
Citizen group Protect Lowndes County, the Alabama Rivers Alliance and others filed a lawsuit against the Lowndes County Commission on Tuesday afternoon.
The lawsuit claims commissioners violated Alabama’s Open Meetings Act and Open Records Act to “fast track” a multibillion-dollar tax abatement package for developer Cloverleaf Infrastructure.
The plaintiffs are asking a circuit judge to declare the commission’s votes void.
Opponents also raise concerns about resource strain. They note the facility will demand up to 1,500 megawatts of power from Alabama Power and hundreds of thousands of gallons of water daily.
Another lawsuit filed against the Lowndes County Commission is still pending.
Construction on the data center is expected to take about five years and could begin as early as January. The site is off Highway 80, a few miles west of the Montgomery airport.

Israelis voted in a general election on 27 October 2026. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud came first with 27 seats, but his bloc won 53 seats, short of the 61 needed for a majority in the 120-seat Knesset, as 
reported here
.

As coalition talks began, posts claimed that Netanyahu had suffered a “very dangerous attack” in Tel Aviv, was severely injured, and that an aircraft was being sent to fly him to the United States. We checked both videos used as proof, and Netanyahu’s public activity since the vote.

Social Media Posts:

The claim reached Sri Lanka through Tamil-language Facebook reels. The first reel used a clip of a man pointing a gun at a speaker on stage; the second used footage of a fight in a parliament chamber.

Claim under review:
 Netanyahu was seriously injured in an attack in Tel Aviv on or after 28 October 2026 and is being flown to the United States, and the two videos show that attack.

Fact Check:

No credible report says Netanyahu was attacked or injured

An attack that put a sitting Israeli prime minister in hospital would lead news bulletins worldwide. A news search for such an attack on 28 October found nothing, as 
reported here
. We found none either.

Instead, Netanyahu has been publicly active since the vote. AP photographs show him addressing supporters at Likud headquarters in Jerusalem on 28 October 
here
. On 29 October he posted that the election had been “stolen”, according to 
this report
.

On 2 November he said, “We aim to form a broad government with our partners.” Results were due to be certified on 4 November, after which President Isaac Herzog will consult the parties on who should try to form a government, as 
reported here
.

The first video shows a 2013 attack on Bulgarian politician Ahmed Dogan

We ran keyframes from the first video through a reverse image search. The same footage appeared in a BBC news report published on 20 January 2013.

Source: 
BBC

The attack took place on 19 January 2013 at the National Palace of Culture in Sofia, during the annual conference of Bulgaria’s Movement for Rights and Freedoms (MRF), according to 
the Associated Press
. A 25-year-old man, Oktai Enimehmedov, climbed onto the stage and pointed a gas pistol at party leader Ahmed Dogan. He was also carrying two knives. No shot was fired; delegates wrestled him to the ground and police arrested him.

The Cyrillic letters “ДПС” are visible on the lectern. This is the Bulgarian abbreviation of Dogan’s party, the MRF, as pointed out 
here
. Footage of the same moment was also published 
here
 in 2013.

Bulgarian courts later cleared Enimehmedov of attempted murder and sentenced him to three and a half years in prison for threatening to kill Dogan, as 
reported here
.

The Bulgarian clip has been passed off as Netanyahu before

This is not the first time. In November 2024 and again in April 2026, the same clip was shared as Netanyahu being “beaten up” in the Israeli parliament. Fact Crescendo checked it both times (

The second video shows a brawl in Armenia’s parliament on 29 October 2026

The second video showed lawmakers fighting inside a chamber. We turned several frames into still images and ran a reverse image search. The footage matched the brawl in Armenia’s National Assembly on 29 October 2026.

The fight broke out during a debate on appointing a member of the Audit Chamber. Ruling Civil Contract MP Taron Chakhoyan raised a remark made months earlier by Sargis Karapetyan, a cousin of opposition leader Narek Karapetyan, and said Sargis had never apologized for it. Narek Karapetyan objected, and the exchange turned physical, as 
reported here
.

Deputy Speaker Vahagn Aleksanyan was seen throwing a chair towards opposition MPs. One opposition MP was reported to have a bleeding nose. It was the third clash in the chamber in October, according to 
this report
.

The row was over a “ministry” remark made before Armenia’s election

The remark dates back to early 2026. On a podcast he co-hosts, Sargis Karapetyan, son of businessman Samvel Karapetyan, floated a “ministry of sex” and said, “A strong Armenia will have no sexually dissatisfied women.” He later said the phrase had been taken out of context but did not apologise, as 
reported here
.

In the 7 June 2026 election, the Strong Armenia alliance came second with 29 seats, while Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan’s Civil Contract kept its majority with 64 seats, according to 
final results
. After the brawl, Pashinyan condemned the violence but backed his MPs, and Armenia’s Human Rights Defender called for an investigation (
source
).

Why the claim felt believable

Two real events made the posts look plausible. Netanyahu’s political future was genuinely uncertain after the vote, and the Armenian brawl was real and only a day old. Neither video, however, shows Netanyahu or Tel Aviv.

Simple checks expose such posts: look for writing or logos in the frame (here, Cyrillic letters on the lectern), run keyframes through a reverse image search, and see whether major news outlets report the event.

Conclusion:

Both videos shared as a serious attack on Netanyahu in Tel Aviv are unrelated to him. The first is footage of the January 2013 attack on Bulgarian politician Ahmed Dogan in Sofia, recycled once again as a Netanyahu clip. The second shows a brawl in Armenia’s parliament on 29 October 2026.

No credible report says Netanyahu was attacked, injured, or flown to the United States. Since the election he has addressed supporters, posted on social media, and spoken about forming a government. The claim is therefore False.

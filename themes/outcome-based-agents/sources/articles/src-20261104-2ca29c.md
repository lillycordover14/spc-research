Shealy, GOP notch local wins, Andrews tops Graham&nbsp;&nbsp;The Laurens County Advertiser

[page text]
Rick Shealy
 
A “Blue Wave” that swept Democrats into office across the country did not come ashore in Laurens County Tuesday night in the midterm elections, but Democratic challenger Annie Andrews flipped a U.S. Senate seat occupied by Republicans since 1964.
 
Republican Rick Shealy, who defeated incumbent Luke Rankin in the GOP Primary earlier this year, defeated Democrat Michanna Tate for the Dist. 14 seat with 62% of the vote, 8,959-5,321.
 
Shealy will join a local Republican state delegation that remains unchanged after the election. Rep. Mark Willis (61.4%) and Dist. 42 Rep. Doug Gilliam (56%) both won re-election over Democratic challengers.
 
“I am humbled and greatly appreciative of the support District 14 voters have shown me,” Shealy said. “I will work hard for the district and for the state of South Carolina. It’s a clear showing of the district’s feelings toward conservative government, and we want to do something about the gasoline tax and property taxes. We want to help out working families, who need some help with inflation.”
 
National networks declared Andrews as the winner just after 10 p.m. in her race against Republican incumbent Darline Graham, who had been picked by President Donald Trump to fill the Senate seat held by her late brother Lindsey Graham after he died in July. With 80% of precincts reporting, Andrews led by nearly 80,000 votes.
 
Eighth Circuit Solicitor David Stumbo was elected South Carolna Attorney General Tuesday.
 
At the state level, 8th Circuit Solicitor David Stumbo was the presumptive winner to become state attorney general with 52.4% of the vote over Democrat Richard Hricik.
 
Republican Alan Wilson and running mate Mike Reichenbach appeared poised to keep the Governor and Lt. Governor’s office in GOP hands with a 4-point lead over Democrats Jermaine Johnson and Sam Skardon.
 
Incumbent Republicans Craig Gagnon (82%) in Dist. 11 and John McCravy (85%) in Dist. 13, who both represent small areas of Laurens County, also won re-election handily.
 
Republican candidates appeared on their way to keeping the state in GOP hands with victories across the board in state offices. 
 
In contested, nonpartisan Laurens County School District 55 races, incumbent Cathy Little, who is chair of the Board of Trustees, defeated Melissa Brown 933-709 for Seat 2, and Nick Babb leads Lee McDaniel by 21 votes (694-673) for Seat 4. There were also 22 write-in votes, which kept Babb from getting more than 50% of the vote. Election officials are awaiting the tally of provisional ballots. No results are official until the election is certified Friday.
 
In District 56 contested races, Elizabeth Plaxico Lollis defeated Jake Patterson with 59% of the vote, 365-244, and David Nabors collected 63% (429-241) of the vote to defeat Jason R. Mode for Seat 7.

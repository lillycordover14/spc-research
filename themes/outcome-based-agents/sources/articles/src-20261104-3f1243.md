MOORHEAD — Clay County residents elected an incumbent and a newcomer in two contested races for seats on the Clay County Board of Commissioners.
Voters elected incumbent Clay County Commissioner Jenny Mongeau and Scott Steffes during the election on Tuesday, Nov. 3.

ADVERTISEMENT

Scott Steffes answers questions during the League of Women Voters’ Clay County Commission candidate forum on Monday, Sept. 21, 2026, at the Moorhead Public Library.
Alyssa Goelzer / The Forum

Mongeau, who represents Clay County District 3, faced Sabin Vice Mayor Tom Berglind for the seat. Mongeau got 62% of the vote, according to early and unofficial totals from the Minnesota Secretary of State. Berglind trailed with 38% of the vote.

Mongeau

    
       was first elected to the Clay County Commission in 2014 and last elected in 2022. During her time on the board, Mongeau has worked on issues like public safety, infrastructure and mental health services.
"I'm honored to continue to have the opportunity to do the work and be part of the team that has really advanced so many local issues," Mongeau told The Forum.

Berglind

    
       has served on the Sabin City Council since 2013, and is a small business owner and volunteer firefighter.
Clay County District 3 includes much of southern Clay County, including a portion of south Moorhead, Barnesville, Sabin and Comstock.
Steffes was elected to represent Clay County District 4. He 
    
      
competed for the seat

    
       against Julian Dahlquist. Steffes received 55% of the vote, while Dahlquist had 44%, according to early and unofficial results.
Steffes has 
    
      
served on the Moorhead School Board since 2013

    
       and is a retired Clay County sheriff’s deputy. On the campaign trail, Steffes called out mental health and chemical dependency as issues the county is facing.

ADVERTISEMENT

"I look forward to serving the citizens of Clay County and thank them for all their support, and I'm going to try and do my best to be a good commissioner," Steffes said.
Dahlquist runs a political consulting business focused on local races, and has been an organizer for the Minnesota Democratic-Farmer-Labor Party. 
Steffes and Dahlquist beat one other candidate during a primary in August to proceed to the Nov. 3 election.
Clay County District 4 includes parts of north Moorhead and Oakport Township. The District 4 seat is being vacated by Commissioner Kevin Campbell, who 
    
      
plans to retire

    
       after 24 years on the board.
The Clay County Commission is a nonpartisan board. It oversees policies for county programs and sets the budget for the Clay County government. Commissioners serve four-year terms. The five-member board is composed of one member from each of the county’s five districts.
Clay County voters narrowly elected Robert Anderson in a 
    
      
contested District 4 race for the Clay Soil and Water Conservation District Board.

    
       The board directs the Clay Soil and Water Conservation District, a local unit of government that oversees conservation assistance programs implemented by landowners across Clay County.
Anderson led over incumbent supervisor Jerald Butenhoff with 49.97% of the vote. Butenhoff got 49.4% of the vote, according to unofficial results.

ADVERTISEMENT

Clay Soil and Water Conservation District 4 includes six townships in the southwest corner of Clay County: Moorhead, Kurtz, Holy Cross, Alliance and Barnesville.

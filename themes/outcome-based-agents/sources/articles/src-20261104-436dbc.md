(Credit: Matteo Della Torre/NurPhoto via Getty Images)

ChatGPT is getting an interface overhaul, merging its Chat and Work tabs into a single experience.

The change, announced in an 
email
 to ChatGPT Pro subscribers, will begin testing with a small group of users soon. OpenAI says it will use any "feedback to improve the experience before bringing it to more people."

For readers in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Egypt, this is a test-stage change for now. OpenAI has not published a regional timeline, so don't expect the merged view to appear on your account right away. Until it does, the current Chat and Work tabs are what you have.

ChatGPT currently offers separate tabs for its Chat and Work tools, with the former designed for everyday tasks and conversations, and the latter for more complex projects that require broader knowledge. OpenAI says combining them will make your experience smoother, streamlining the process of switching between modes, and giving you access to more tools from a single view. OpenAI notes how you would be able to explore an idea with ChatGPT, but then build on it, such as making a presentation or building a website, without manually switching over to the Work tab. OpenAI also confirmed plans to end support for the ChatGPT Classic app on macOS on Jan. 30, 2027. A message shown to users, as spotted by 
9to5Mac
, urges them to switch to its newer ChatGPT app, with a notification that says, “Your full chat history comes with you, so you can pick up where you left off.”

What this means for Middle East readers

If you use the Classic app on a Mac in the UAE, Saudi Arabia or Egypt, you will need to move to the newer ChatGPT app before support ends on Jan. 30, 2027. OpenAI's in-app message says your full chat history transfers with you, so you won't lose past conversations in the switch.

OpenAI has not released pricing for the merged experience in the region, and this report doesn't include local prices. Check the subscription details shown in the app in your country before you upgrade or change plans.

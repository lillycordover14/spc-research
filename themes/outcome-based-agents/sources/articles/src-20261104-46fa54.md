MOORHEAD — For the first time in 20 years, Clay County will have a new top prosecutor after voters overwhelmingly elected Amber Gustafson to serve as Clay County attorney Tuesday, Nov 3. 
With all precincts reporting, Gustafson had captured 62.5% of the vote compared to Brian Melton's 37%.  

ADVERTISEMENT

Gustafson had nearly lost her voice after talking to supporters at her campaign watch party at the Hen House when she was reached by phone late Tuesday, but said it was a "true honor for the people of Clay County to recognize that it's time to see some changes in that office and that I'm the person to do it." 
The matchup with Gustafson was Melton's first challenged election in 20 years, since he was first elected to the post in 2006. 
Gustafson is a former Minnesota Army National Guard member and was appointed as Seventh District judge in 2018. She resigned from that position after her husband, 
    
      
West Fargo Police Lt. Adam Gustafson,

    
       died suddenly in 2021.
"Public service is something that I'm passionate about and I had to take a little break from it due to my family's circumstances but I'm ready to come back and I'm so excited for what I can do to make Clay County better, to make sure that justice is served efficiently and effectively," she said.  
The Clay County attorney serves as the state's lead prosecutor for the county. 
Melton focused his campaign on his 20-year tenure in the role, overseeing budgets, leading the office, and working with the county board, while also trying more than 50 jury trials and managing civil matters.
Gustafson's message prioritized public safety, building a culture that employed high-quality attorneys who stay with the office, and strengthening collaboration with partners. She also committed to restoring the county attorney office's full participation in 
    
      
treatment courts,

    
       from which Melton withdrew earlier this year.

Gustafson said Clay County under-performed from a trial win-rate perspective compared with neighboring counties and saw higher dismissal rates for criminal cases under Melton's leadership. 
She also criticized Melton's decision to discontinue his office's participation in drug court, DWI court and veterans treatment court. 
“A strong leader ... wouldn’t give up a seat at the table to be of influence in a program they’re saying isn’t working,” she said. “The best way to influence this direction is by participating. I don’t know how his decision helps get this or anything back on track.”
Melton cited a lack of accountability and oversight and repeated violations by drug court participants for his decision.

Clay County Attorney Brian Melton, running for reelection, participates in a candidate forum on Wednesday, Sept. 23, 2026, at the Moorhead Public Library.
Alyssa Goelzer / Forum file photo

Ultimately, Gustafson said, the election results showed that the voters of Clay County wanted change at the top of the county attorney's office. 
The Clay County Commission approved Melton for a 7% raise, plus a 4% cost-of-living adjustment last December, bringing his total raise to 11%. 
In 2025, Melton made $182,910, according to the County Commission. The 11% raise brought his salary to just over $203,000 in 2026.

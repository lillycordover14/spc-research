Three thieves posing as Amazon workers zip-tied a 70-year-old man in an armed stick-up at his mother's Bronx home, nabbing $24 and a cell phone, cops said. (NYPD)

Three thieves disguised as Amazon workers forced their way into an 89-year-old woman’s Bronx home at gunpoint, tying up her 70-year-old son and nabbing $24, a phone and credit cards, 
cops
 said.

The bandits, sporting blue Amazon vests and carrying boxes with the online retailer’s curved arrow logo, rang the bell to Vincenta Febres’ Bainbridge Ave. apartment near E. 199th St. in 
Bedford Park
 around 11:15 p.m. Tuesday, police said.

The bandits, wearing blue Amazon vests and carrying boxes with the online retailer's logo, rang the bell to the elderly woman's Bainbridge Ave. apartment near E. 199th St. in Bedford Park around 11:15 p.m. Tuesday, police said. (NYPD)

When the Febres’ son opened the door, one of the trio put a black revolver at his head and demanded cash, his brother said.

“They rang the doorbell and said, ‘Amazon!'” said William Burgos, 71, who referred to his brother as Junior. “One man rushed inside and pointed a gun to his face. They zip-tied him and were saying, ‘Where’s the money? We’re going to kill you if you don’t tell us where the money is.'”

The victim pleaded with the robbers, saying, “I don’t live here. We don’t have any money,” according to his brother.

“They kept saying, ‘Give us the money, or I will kill you,'” Burgos said.

After binding 
the man’s hands and feet
 with zip ties, the thieves nabbed his wallet, containing credit cards and cash, and his phone, according to police.

“They went into the bedroom looking for the money.  They put a gun to his face and zip-tied him,” said Febres. “They were saying, ‘Where’s the money? Where’s the money?'”
 

Burgos said his brother was “a quiet guy” who “doesn’t have problems with anybody.”

“We don’t know why they chose us,” Burgos said. “It’s strange. I can only think they had the wrong address.”

The NYPD released images of the suspects, a woman and two men, and is asking for the public’s help tracking them down.

Anyone with information is asked to call 
Crime Stoppers
 at (800) 577-TIPS. All calls will be kept confidential.

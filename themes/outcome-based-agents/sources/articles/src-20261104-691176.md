SALT LAKE CITY (KUTV)  — 
Republican incumbent Blake Moore held a narrow lead over Democratic challenger Peter Crosby in Utah's 2nd Congressional District race, with thousands of ballots still awaiting counting.
Moore received 49.5% of the vote compared to Crosby's 43.6%, according to preliminary results from The Associated Press.
With an estimated 64% of votes counted, Moore had received 89,516 votes compared to Crosby's 78,781, a difference of 10,735 votes.
Three additional candidates, Carlton E. Bowen, Robert M. Moesinger and Daniel Cottam, received a combined 6.9% of the vote.
The Associated Press had not yet called the race.
Moore was seeking another term in Congress after first winning election to the U.S. House of Representatives in 2020. He previously represented Utah's 1st Congressional District, succeeding longtime Republican Congressman Rob Bishop.
The 2026 election placed Moore in a newly configured district following a court-ordered redrawing of Utah's congressional boundaries.
Third District Judge Dianna Gibson approved new congressional boundaries in November 2025 after ruling against the state's previous congressional map, which had divided Salt Lake County among all four districts.
The new map created a Democratic-leaning 1st Congressional District centered on Salt Lake County and significantly reshaped the state's other three districts.
The changes shifted Moore's reelection campaign into the 2nd Congressional District, where he faced Crosby and three additional candidates.
The race remained undecided as election officials continued counting ballots across the district.

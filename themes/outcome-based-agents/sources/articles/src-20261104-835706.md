Vanessa Hall-Harper,, the long-time incumbent for Tulsa City Council District 1, earned her sixth term Tuesday after defeating challenger Corinice Wilson.

With all precincts reporting, Hall-Harper led with 51.2 percent support (4,689 votes) to Wilson’s 48.8 percent (4,473 votes). All 
results
 are unofficial until certified by the Oklahoma State Election Board.

Hall-Harper now joins four other Tulsa City Council incumbents who retained their seats in the Aug. 25 primary election. Representing much of North Tulsa, the District 1 seat headed to a runoff partially owing to the large field of four that sought to unseat Hall-Harper, who led the five-candidate race with 45.8 percent support in August (1,936 votes).

“For 10 years, you have entrusted me with something that I have never taken lightly. You’ve trusted me to be your voice at City Hall,” Hall-Harper said in a comment reported by the 
Tulsa Flyer
. “I never went to City Hall to be friends with the power structure. I went to City Hall to represent the people that sent me there.”

Wilson substantially grew her support from the 33.7 percent (1,548 votes) she garnered in August, though the gains were not enough to unseat Hall-Harper.

In office since 2016, Hall-Harper has been a driving force behind the effort to establish an independent oversight body above the Tulsa Police Department and other public safety agencies. In an 
October interview with NonDoc
, she said she intends to continue pushing for the formation of such a body. A recent version of the proposal fell short of approval in a 4-4-1 vote following Ward 2 Councilman Anthony Archie’s abstention.

Elsewhere in Tulsa County, voters soundly supported passage of the city’s 
Proposition 1 hotel tax adjustment
. With 56.8 percent of voters in favor of the proposition (61,524 votes) and 43.1 percent in opposition (46,762 votes), residents easily approved raising the City of Tulsa’s lodging tax from 5 percent to 9.9 percent. The tax applies to guests staying in short-term rentals and hotels citywide. According to the city website, the increase is expected to generate $20.6 million annually.

The new rate will take effect Jan. 1.

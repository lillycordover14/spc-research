Ada County voters were split right down the middle this election when it came to selecting their county commissioners.
On Tuesday voters narrowly decided to keep incumbent Ada County Commissioner Rod Beck while cutting Commissioner Ryan Davidson.
DAVIDSON UNSEATED

In District 1, Davidson lost his seat to Democratic challenger Kelceymarie Warner. With 50.09% of the vote, Warner surpassed Davidson, a Republican, by just 0.18%, or 400 votes. She will serve a four-year term.
Warner attributed part of her win to endorsements from former commissioners Kendra Kenyon, a Democrat, and Rick Visser, a Republican.
“We are seeing voters who just want accessible, authentic people who want to make a positive impact in their lives,” Warner said. “There’s a lot of discourse, particularly online, about partisan issues and partisan divide, but when I speak to the average person in Ada County, they’re somewhere in the middle.”
Watching the neck-in-neck race Tuesday night had her heart racing, Warner said. Looking forward, she plans to hit the ground running and prepare for her role.
“I really am grateful for the voters of Ada County,” Warner said. “People told me they weren’t going to vote because their vote didn’t matter, but every vote counts.”
Davidson told the Idaho Press that coming so close to a win was disappointing. He believes a variety of factors resulted in the narrow race, including a national trend.
“This is kind of part of the national mood that we see across the whole country, with a lot of Republicans losing office just because of national sentiment against Republicans,” he said. “I guess ‘the blue wave’ or whatever you want to call it. Obviously that’s going to have an impact in Ada County as well.”
Davidson reflected on his past six years as commissioner. He is happy to have overseen the completion of Ada County’s Park at Expo Idaho, a 50-acre facility that was opened 
earlier this fall
.
“That was a great honor,” he said. “It felt really good to be able to get in office, come up with a project and something of that scale, and then be able to actually finish it.”
In his remaining time as commissioner, he hopes to continue to push rat abatement efforts.
At this time he does not plan on requesting a recount of election results and commended Warner on her success.
“She ran a good campaign, I think she’s a competent and good person, and I think she’ll do a good job for the taxpayers of Ada County,” Davidson said.

In District 2, Beck retained his seat against Democrat Michael Fitzgerald. Beck received 50.14% of the vote, beating Fitzgerald by 0.28% or 594 votes. Seat 2 has a two-year term.
Beck, a Republican, has served on the commission for five years following time in the Idaho Senate as majority leader. On Wednesday Beck said he was humbled by the constituents that were endorsing the work he has completed as a commissioner.

Rod Beck

“By me getting reelected and with our colleague (Commissioner Tom Dayley), that means we’re going to keep doing the same things that we’re doing, which is all good for the county,” Beck said.
As for the competitive race, Beck pointed to paid door-knockers by the Democratic Party as a reason Fitzgerald garnered support.
“It wasn’t any key issues because frankly the Democrat that was running against me didn’t really raise any issues, he didn’t campaign much but he was able to benefit from all the grassroots efforts,” he said.
Moving forward, Beck aims to maintain a balanced budget, something that has especially been a struggle due to HB 389. The 2021 legislation caps the annual growth of a municipality’s budget.
In addition, Beck said over 90% of Ada County’s budget goes toward spending areas that are mandated by law.
“I’m not complaining about it,” Beck said. “I’m just saying that’s what it is, and we do the best we can with the tools that we have.”
While he lost the race, Fitzgerald saw his overall campaign as a success.
“Having this being my first time running for office, (and) it being one of the closest races in the state, it’s really an honor and surprise,” he said.
Fitzgerald comes from a background in HVAC and volunteers with mutual aid groups. He believes the close race is reflective of voters’ interest in housing affordability and having a candidate they can relate to.
“They want Idaho to kind of, with the inevitable growth, still maintain what we all had when we were growing up here,” he said. “And I think that that’s something that a lot of people are willing to fight for.”
Fitzgerald intends to keep the momentum going and does not anticipate this being his last run for office.
“Anyone that is reading this quote: get involved in your community, get involved in mutual aid groups, whether it’s a group or some designated organization,” he said in a message to voters. “Get involved and make sure that we work every day to make our community a better place.”
As of Wednesday Fitzgerald said he does not intend on initiating a recount.

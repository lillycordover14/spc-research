LINCOLN – Special Strong, an adaptive fitness provider for people with disabilities, has expanded into Rhode Island and southeastern Massachusetts, partnering with several gyms in the Blackstone Valley.
Co-owners Michael Yamin and Christy Paiva held an open house Saturday, Oct. 24, at The Overlook at Meehan in North Providence to introduce the program, which offers individualized training for children, teens, adults, and seniors.
Local partner gyms include Coastal Strength Compound in Lincoln, CrossFit 401 in Smithfield, and American Muscle Corps in Pawtucket. Special Strong uses existing gyms, with its own trainers developing workouts around each member’s needs.
“We develop programs tailor-made to the specific individual based on their cognitive, sensory, and physical needs,” Yamin said. “So we make it fun; it’s inclusive, and it’s empowering.”
The program serves people with autism, Down syndrome, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, mobility limitations, and other physical, cognitive, and mental health challenges, according to the company.
Special Strong calls everyone who joins an “athlete,” regardless of age or experience. Yamin said its trainers are certified in adaptive training, and many have spent years working with people with disabilities.
Yamin said families have told them they searched for a long time for someone who could work individually with a son or daughter, while other members struggled to find training for themselves. Paiva said personal trainers may lack the additional training needed to adapt workouts for people with disabilities.
“We had a few people say we’ve been waiting for you,” she said.
For some members, entering a gym can feel intimidating, Yamin said. Trainers work with them during less busy hours, helping them become comfortable exercising alongside other gymgoers.
Workouts focus on core strength and flexibility, brain and sensory development, strength and muscle development, and endurance. The broader goal, Yamin said, is to help members become more independent and carry their progress into everyday activities.
Yamin said athletes across Special Strong have experienced benefits beyond the gym.
“It’s not just about lifting weights. It’s helping mom and dad more at home,” he said. “Some of them are getting jobs.”
Yamin said families have shared positive feedback as members become more active and enjoy their training. Some have referred others to the program.
“The athlete feels good about themselves. The family’s feeling good about how much more the athlete’s doing, how much happier the athlete is,” he said.
Both owners said their interest in adaptive fitness came from knowing friends and loved ones with disabilities. Paiva said they had seen the obstacles those individuals faced while growing up.
Yamin said he had been searching for a business that would allow him to help people with disabilities when he discovered Special Strong.
“The more I looked into Special Strong, the more I realized, wow, what they’re doing is awesome, and it’s something that we’ve been looking to do for a long time,” he said.
Yamin has more than 30 years of experience owning and running businesses, and Paiva is a practicing board-certified physician, according to the company.
They are the first to bring Special Strong to Rhode Island and Massachusetts. Daniel Stein founded the company in 2016 and began franchising in 2020.
Along with individual training, Yamin said they are building a community through events where members, families, and trainers can get to know one another.

GREAT LAKES (NNS) – Seaman Christopher Harper graduated as the top Sailor from Recruit Training Command (RTC) Great Lakes, earning the Military Excellence Award (MEA) Nov. 5, 2026.

Harper, 42, of Marathon, Florida, arrived at boot camp 25 years after he first dreamed of the military being a possibility, and left as the top recruit in his graduating training group.

“I was honestly shocked to be named the top recruit of my class,” Harper said. “At 42, I was just hoping to graduate without breaking a hip. But in all seriousness, it was an incredible honor. I pushed myself to my absolute limits and never let mediocrity be acceptable. I let my failures become opportunities for growth.”

The Military Excellence Award is presented to the recruit who best demonstrates enthusiasm, devotion to duty, military bearing and teamwork throughout training. As part of the recognition, recipients receive a flag letter of commendation.

The dream that brought Harper to RTC was older than most of the recruits standing beside him. At 17, he had wanted to join the Navy, but chose the corporate world instead, a path that led to nearly a decade as a travel-company CEO and the founding of a Florida nonprofit. Two years into retirement, his teenage son brought the old dream back.

“Twenty-five years ago, when I was 17, I had a dream to serve my country and join the Navy,” Harper said. “After talking it over with recruiters and my parents, I chose the corporate path instead. Then, two years after I retired, my son, Gabriel, came to me and said he wanted to join the Navy and go to the Naval Academy to become a commissioned officer. He showed me a video of a father who had served saluting his son at his commissioning ceremony, and it reawakened something in me I’d forgotten decades ago. My dream now is to serve alongside my son, to salute him as a commissioned officer and have two generations of my family serving this nation at the same time.”

Winning the award changed how far he intends to take that dream.

“Before winning, I only planned to serve alongside my son for a few years and then get out,” Harper said. “This reawakened a burning desire to pursue commissioning as an officer myself, alongside him, and to serve a full 20 years or more with honor.”

Through the hardest stretches of training, the steadiest voice came from home.

“My top motivation throughout boot camp was my wife,” Harper said. “Her letters were encouraging and uplifting. She kept reminding me of my ‘why,’ telling me how proud she and my kids were of me. They pushed me to excel at every level, no matter how difficult it got.”

He found a second source of motivation in his first RDC, Chief Wood, and in one line he delivered after the division’s first big failure.

“We were all so disappointed in ourselves, and my Chief told us, ‘All storms run out of rain. You just have to weather the storm,’” Harper said. “He embodied something I used to teach in business: be firm, fair and consistent. He never folded on principle or discipline, but he was always fair enough to hear anyone out before making a judgment. If our RDCs are a reflection of our Navy careers, he’s an exceptional first impression of what mine can become.”

For Harper, the toughest part of boot camp was the distance in years between himself and everyone else, though it turned out to be the making of him.

“The generational gap between me and the rest of my class naturally put me in leadership roles the entire time, and it never really let me decompress,” Harper said. “But looking back, that may have been the reason for my success. Because so many were looking up to me, I made sure I set an example, always challenging and motivating them instead of competing against them.”

That standard was set long before he arrived.

“Before joining, I trained four to five days a week and memorized every piece of information my recruiter gave me,” Harper said. “The minimum was never acceptable to me. I focused on the high scores and built my training around that, and it gave me an edge.”

His RDCs were Chief Petty Officer Ronald Wood, Petty Officer 1st Class Adriana Ramirez-Esparza and Petty Officer 1st Class Jonathan Dawson.

Following graduation, Harper will report to Cryptologic Technician “A” school at Corry Station in Pensacola, Florida, for further training.

Training at RTC is approximately nine weeks long, and all enlistees in the U.S. Navy begin their careers at the command. More than 40,000 recruits train annually at the Navy’s only boot camp.

The 
Boston Bruins
 have developed a habit of finding ways to win at TD Garden, but their next challenge will require them to carry that momentum into a different environment. Boston improved to 11-4-1 with Monday's 3-2 shootout victory over the Vegas Golden Knights, extending its home winning streak to six games.
David Pastrnak delivered the deciding shootout goal, while Jeremy Swayman stopped all three Vegas attempts.
Those performances helped Boston collect another two points, but the Bruins also allowed a two-goal lead to disappear during regulation. The next opponent presents another difficult test: Sidney Crosby and the Pittsburgh Penguins.
Boston's Home Success Has Been Impressive
The Bruins have received contributions from several different parts of their lineup during the opening weeks of the season. Pastrnak remains the team's most dangerous offensive player, while Morgan Geekie has provided important scoring support.

Younger players have also contributed, giving Boston more offensive options than opponents can comfortably ignore.
But the Bruins have not always protected leads effectively. Against Vegas, Boston established a 2-0 advantage before the Golden Knights recovered and forced overtime.
The Bruins eventually secured the victory, but the game demonstrated that controlling a contest for long stretches does not guarantee a regulation win.
Crosby Still Commands Attention
Pittsburgh remains a dangerous opponent whenever Crosby is on the ice. The veteran center continues to approach major career milestones and remains capable of influencing games through his passing, puck protection and offensive awareness.
Boston must avoid allowing Pittsburgh extended offensive-zone possessions, particularly when Crosby's line is involved. The Bruins will also need disciplined defensive coverage and dependable goaltending to maintain their strong start.
That responsibility extends throughout the lineup.

A Chance To Build A More Complete Identity
Boston has already demonstrated resilience by winning close games and recovering from difficult situations. The next step is producing more consistent performances that do not require dramatic late-game finishes.
The Bruins have the offensive talent to challenge strong opponents, but they must also manage leads more effectively. Facing Pittsburgh provides another opportunity to demonstrate that improvement.
A strong performance would reinforce the idea that Boston's early success is built on more than home-ice momentum and timely individual heroics. Hagens and the Bruins continue their season on Thursday against the Penguins.

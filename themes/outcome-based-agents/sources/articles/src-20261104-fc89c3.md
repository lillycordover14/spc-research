‘There’s no blue wave’: Clay Travis was spectacularly wrong about midterm elections&nbsp;&nbsp;Yahoo

[page text]
Clay Travis
Conservative sports analyst and commentator Clay Travis spent Election Day insisting that the blue wave everyone was talking about simply wasn't coming. He was confident, he was loud, and he claimed that he had the numbers to prove it. Unfortunately for him, he was very wrong.
By the time the first results started rolling in Tuesday night, it was clear that his predictions were not going to age well. When the dust had settled, Travis' predictions turned out to be laughably incorrect.
'There's no blue wave'
Travis laid out his midterm predictions on Sunday as he made it clear that he did not think it would be a significant Blue victory at the polls on Tuesday.

"I nailed all my election predictions in 2024, not so much in 2022. Here are my 2026 calls with a reminder that all of you should get out and vote. Byron Donalds wins Florida Gov comfortably," 
Travis wrote
. "Senate: Ken Paxton wins Texas, Mike Rogers wins Michigan, Jon Husted wins Ohio, Ashley Hinson wins Iowa, Susan Collins wins Maine, the real Dan Sullivan wins Alaska, Kansas, South Carolina & Nebraska aren't close, Republicans win senate races easily in those states."
He also claimed that "polls are badly biased against Republicans for the 16th out of the past 17 election cycles."
He doubled down as voters headed to the polls on Tuesday.
"It's 1 pm eastern and no blue wave has shown up at all on Election Day so far. Keep voting big, Republicans! Flood the polls! Make the MSNBC and CNN employees cry on air tonight!" 
Travis wrote
.

A few hours later, the OutKick founder 
posted a video
 making his case.
"The data so far, and again, I am speaking at 3 PM Eastern, a little bit after now, there's no blue wave. A blue wave has not materialized in the country so far," Travis said.
"If you look at the numbers coming out of Arizona, if you look at the numbers coming out of Nevada, there's going to be a lot of close races. But there has not been a tremendous blue wave, certainly no blue tsunami," he continued. "Maybe independent voters are going to break in a certain way. But to me, I gave you my predictions yesterday. I think Republicans are gonna do pretty well."
He even explained why he expected close races to all break toward Republicans.

"What happens in elections is close races tend to end up all going one way or the other because a few points impacts things across the entire country, whether it's Maine, whether it's Alaska, whether it's Ohio, whether it's Texas, wherever these battleground states are," Travis said. "Now, maybe there's going to be a blue wave that materializes on the East Coast in the next 4 hours."
The blue wave showed up
Despite Travis' predictions, the blue wave did indeed arrive as Democrats won control of the House, and NBC News later projected that they had won the Senate as well, giving them full control of Congress.
Travis' Senate picks fell apart one by one. James Talarico beat Paxton in Texas, Sherrod Brown unseated Husted in Ohio, and Josh Turek beat Hinson in Iowa, according to NBC News projections.
The states that supposedly "aren't close" didn't go much better. Democrats flipped seats in both South Carolina and Kansas, where Adam Hamilton upset Sen. Roger Marshall. In Nebraska, independent Dan Osborn led Sen. Pete Ricketts in early returns.

As of late Tuesday night, the race in Maine had not been called, with Collins and Democrat Troy Jackson separated by a fraction of a percentage point. The Senate race in Michigan had not yet been called either, but Abdul El-Sayed had a lead over Rogers.
To be fair, Travis did get one thing right. Byron Donalds was projected to win the Florida governor's race, just as he predicted.
But Travis did say that close races "tend to end up all going one way or the other." On that point, at least, he was right. It just wasn't the way he expected.

Crescendo Holds First Close of Fourth Blind Fund, Raising $350 Million Overseas

Private equity firm Crescendo Equity Partners is accelerating work on its fourth blind fund, targeting $1 billion (~1.3 trillion won). It has completed a first close after securing more than $350 million (~470 billion won) from overseas institutional investors (LPs) alone.

According to investment banking sources on the 5th, Crescendo completed the first close of Crescendo Private Equity Fund IV. The fund targets $1 billion in total, with plans to raise $500 million (~670 billion won) each overseas and in South Korea. The first round secured more than 70% of the overseas target.

Overseas LPs led the first close. Including overseas institutions currently considering participation, the overseas target is expected to be oversubscribed. Major South Korean institutions are also conducting due diligence on commitments, which is expected to give fundraising momentum.

The new fund will be deployed to acquire control of South Korean small and mid-sized technology companies with enterprise values of 50 billion to 500 billion won (approximately $37.4 million to approximately $373.6 million). Semiconductors, electronics and electrical equipment, business-to-business (B2B) software and cybersecurity are cited as core investment areas. The plan is to focus on fields the South Korean government is fostering as next-generation growth industries as AI spreads and technological upheaval accelerates. Crescendo will also pursue a "buy-and-build" strategy of acquiring companies that hold Korea's leading technology but face stalled growth, then expanding them through follow-on M&A.

Crescendo's differentiator is its link to Silicon Valley. Executives from Thiel Capital, founded by PayPal co-founder Peter Thiel, sit directly on Crescendo's investment committee. Thiel's global network is used for portfolio companies' overseas expansion and cross-border M&A.

Crescendo has grown its scale steadily, starting with its first fund (58.7 billion won, approximately $43.9 million) in 2015, followed by its second (413.1 billion won, approximately $308.7 million) in 2017 and its third (1.1513 trillion won, approximately $860.3 million) in 2021. Since its founding, it has invested a total of 1.5162 trillion won (approximately $1.1 billion) in 29 companies, with no loss of principal on any deal. It manages three blind funds and 10 project funds, with cumulative assets under management (AUM) of about 1.8 trillion won (approximately $1.3 billion).

Exit results are also accumulating. The second blind fund recovered more than its entire principal by selling PDF software company iText to a global technology-focused private equity firm. The first blind fund is posting a gross internal rate of return (IRR) of more than 30% and a distributions-to-paid-in capital (DPI) ratio in the high 1x range. The net IRR of the first through third blind funds is assessed as far exceeding the top quartile (top 25%) of global private equity firms compiled by global investment advisory firm Cambridge Associates.

Lee Ki-doo, CEO of Crescendo, said: "Even amid investment conditions at home and abroad that are more uncertain and difficult than ever, we were able to successfully complete the first close of Fund IV thanks to the steadfast trust of global institutional investors." He added: "By combining deep technology insight, our partnership with Chairman Peter Thiel and our global network, we will serve as a partner helping competitive South Korean technology companies leap onto the world stage."

The fund's formation is notable because it comes at a time when overseas LPs' commitments have contracted amid a global regime of high interest rates. It is also a case of a South Korean private equity firm securing overseas institutional capital preemptively, and is expected to affect the competitive landscape of the buyout market for small and mid-sized technology companies going forward.

CLAY COUNTY, Neb. (KOLN) - A Nebraska couple created a glamping site near the remnants of a ghost town along the Oregon Trail.
Owen and Kim Nelson built 
Spring Ranch Campground
 around an old gravel pit in Clay County, turning
 
overgrown terrain into an outdoor getaway under the prairie sky.
“What’s amazing how many people come from the city and say, oh, we’ve never seen stars like this before,” Kim Nelson said.
The property required substantial work before opening to visitors.
“It’s an old gravel pit,” Owen Nelson said. “So it was trees and cedar trees and a hole in the ground and just kind of waste ground.”
The couple, originally from South Dakota, moved to the Hastings area in 1991 so Owen could work with an operation raising swine. In 1998, he launched a smoked ham business that the couple still operates during the colder months. The Nelsons also ran Kenny’s Roadhouse for 14 years before selling the establishment to a new owner.
“(We were) both farm kids grew up 6 miles apart from each other,” Owen Nelson said. “We’ve done a lot of stuff together, which is a testament to the strength of our marriage.”
The campground project took shape after Kim purchased a cabin at Husker Harvest Days.
“I thought it would be great for the grandsons,” Kim Nelson said. “They love to deer hunt and turkey hunt.”
The couple officially launched the glamping business in 2022, opening the grounds to the public as their grandchildren grew older.
“This has always been part of our little acreage here for our grandkids and our kids to play, and now that they’re growing up, we thought we’d just open this space up for the public to come and enjoy,” Kim Nelson said.
The grounds now feature two wagons purchased from Kansas and two yurts manufactured in Sidney, Nebraska. The couple decided to add the wagons after a stay during an out-of-state trip.
“We actually were in a city in Oklahoma that we actually stayed at a campground that had these wagons,” Kim Nelson said. “We experienced a night stay in one and we loved it so well.”
Owen Nelson credits his wife for the interior design of the accommodations.
“I’ve got to make sure I give the correct plug for the decorator, which is my wife,” Owen Nelson said.
The yurts remain open year-round, while the regular season for the remaining structures runs from April through October.
The property is named after the nearby historic community. Visitors can drive to the cemetery that bears the names of the Spring Ranch residents. The town was first spelled as Spring Ranche with an “e” when it was founded in the 1860s. 
Otherwise, the campground offers access to walking trails, fire pits and a pond. 
“I mean I think it’s, it’s a best-kept secret, those types of places,” Kim Nelson said. “And people just love that atmosphere, you know, to grill a burger on the on the fire and go down to the beach and have the kids swim or fish and it’s just relaxing.”
While the campground operates for the public through the summer and fall, the Nelsons reserve the grounds each Fourth of July for their own family.
“It brings the family together, I think,” Kim Nelson said. “You know, it’s just a little R&R together never hurts. A little fun and play.”
“Seeing grandma and grandpa smile,” Owen Nelson said.
Spring Ranch Campground is located at 30551 Road D in Fairfield. Those interested in learning more can contact the business at 402-726-2282.

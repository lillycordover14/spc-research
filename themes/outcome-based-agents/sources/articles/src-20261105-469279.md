Kellie Harper remains undeterred despite narrow escape in season opener&nbsp;&nbsp;247Sports

[page text]
Missouri women's basketball erased a 19-point deficit to win its season opener. But head coach Kellie Harper isn't letting one comeback change her plans.
After defeating Howard 91-88 in overtime Monday, the Tigers (1-0) now face their first road test of the season Friday against Little Rock. Missouri will then return home to face Tennessee Tech on Monday. For Harper, the next two games are opportunities to find out how quickly her team can grow.
"We're excited that we're 1-0. We'll start with that," Harper said Wednesday. "We're looking forward to continuing the journey with this group, and obviously, we've got a tough road game and a home game pretty quickly after that. We just look forward to seeing the growth that hopefully this team will have early in the season."
Missouri showed its resilience against Howard, overcoming a 19-point second-half deficit behind a career-high 29 points from McKenzie Mathurin. The Tigers outscored Howard 30-13 in the fourth quarter before securing the victory in overtime.
But despite the dramatic finish, Harper said the comeback won't change how she approaches future games.
"No, I don't think so," Harper said. "I think what we saw is what we need to see in terms of our fight and our toughness. But in terms of the execution of what we want to do offensively and defensively, we're not changing things from our Howard game."
Instead, the focus remains on developing chemistry and improving execution on both ends of the floor. With several newcomers playing significant roles, Harper believes experience will be important as Missouri continues learning how to operate together.
"It's just understanding the system, trusting the system, and trusting one another to do our jobs," Harper said. "And a lot of that just comes with experience together."
That experience will extend beyond the basketball court Friday. Missouri's trip to Little Rock will be its first opportunity to navigate the challenges of playing away from Mizzou Arena this season. Harper said she is interested in seeing how her players adjust to the unfamiliar environment and the routines that come with traveling.
"You have to learn how to get on the bus, how to pack your bag, you've got to learn how to walk into a shootaround on a different court," Harper said. "You've got to learn how to adapt."
The Trojans enter Friday's matchup 1-0 after defeating Central Baptist College 108-12 in their season opener.
Missouri will have little time to regroup afterward. The Tigers return to Columbia for an 11 a.m. matchup against Tennessee Tech on Monday, giving Harper just one day of preparation between games after a scheduled Saturday off.
The quick turnaround will test a team still developing its identity, but Harper isn't looking for a finished product in November. The Tigers have already shown they can fight their way back into a game. Now comes the challenge of showing they can execute consistently, regardless of the opponent or venue.
Missouri and Little Rock tip off Friday at 7 p.m. CT at the Jack Stephens Center in Little Rock, Arkansas.

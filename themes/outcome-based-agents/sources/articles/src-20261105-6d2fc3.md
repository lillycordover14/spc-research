Nooks, a San Francisco sales automation startup, has lined up $500 million from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund to fund a sales and marketing buildout and bring new products to market, the company said on November 4.

The arrangement is not a conventional equity round. CEO Dan Lee said General Catalyst is not taking a stake; instead the firm will collect a portion of the returns its capital generates. He declined to disclose the terms.

"In order to completely transform any industry, you need a lot of capital," Lee said, adding that the structure was "the best way to finance both aggressive growth and also taking new products to market."

Alongside the commitment, Nooks formally introduced its Revenue Agent Platform, which it says covers tasks from the first customer contact through renewal. The company claims more than 2,000 customers, among them Cursor, Notion, Replit, Glean and HubSpot, and reported a net revenue retention rate of 135%. It said annual recurring revenue is on pace to nearly triple by year-end but did not give the current figure.

Pranav Singhvi, a partner and co-head of the Customer Value Fund at General Catalyst, said the platform "has shown a highly predictable revenue engine and strong unit economics, which is what we look for in companies with CVF."

The deal reflects General Catalyst's wider move toward flexible funding structures, including non-dilutive financing, as opposed to straightforward equity purchases.

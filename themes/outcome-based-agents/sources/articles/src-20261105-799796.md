EMA shares update on Greenville, Mississippi, chemical plant explosion&nbsp;&nbsp;Yahoo

[page text]
Washington Co., Mississippi, Emergency Operations Center
The Washington County, Mississippi, Emergency Operations Center (EMA) shared an update Thursday, Nov. 5, on the Wednesday, Nov. 4, chemical plant fire in Greenville, Mississippi, which prompted a brief shelter-in-place order for some nearby businesses and residents. 
Greenville Mayor Errick D. Simmons shared the 
EMA's update
 Thursday on social media. 
The explosion occurred at the Loveland Products plant on Platte Road just after 7 a.m. Wednesday. Loveland Products produces plant- and crop-related chemicals for "seed treatment, plant nutrition, fertilizer, adjuvant and crop protection," according to
 LovelandProducts.com.

According to EMA's update, the Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality "conducted air monitoring throughout the night" and "no hazards have been detected." This is especially notable given the nature of the chemicals produced at the plant.
The EMA's update also confirmed that "glyphosate was not involved in yesterday's fire." Glyphosate, according to the 
National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences
, is "a broad-spectrum herbicide used to control grasses and broadleaf plants." Because chemical fires can raise concerns about airborne exposure to herbicides and other hazardous materials, emergency officials often identify whether widely used chemicals such as glyphosate were involved in certain disasters. 
The update also addressed the ongoing smoke from the still-smoldering fire and said, "Residents may continue to see black smoke coming from the site over the next several days."
EMA's statement concluded by reminding residents to be mindful of the potential side effects of chemical exposure.

"While current air monitoring has detected no hazards, we must remain cautious, avoid unnecessary smoke exposure, and continue following official safety guidance," Simmons' statement said.

PAOLI — The Orange County Council approved funding for a replacement emergency management truck and an Eagle Scout courthouse bench project Oct. 29, while agreeing to invest $583,000 in food and beverage tax revenue in a certificate of deposit.
The council also held off on formally establishing a MUST committee, with members seeking more information about potential legislative changes before making decisions concerning local income taxes.
Additional appropriations approved during the meeting totaled $725,200. Of that amount, $723,200 came from the Food and Beverage Tax Fund, including the certificate of deposit purchase, $100,000 for Orange County Economic Development Partnership payments, $35,000 for an Emergency Management Agency truck and $5,200 for courthouse benches.

The remaining $2,000 was a General Fund appropriation associated with an EMA Community Foundation grant.
Council members approved purchasing the $583,000 certificate of deposit through Springs Valley Bank & Trust on a 5-0 vote, with council President Zachary Brown abstaining. The minutes do not state the certificate’s term, interest rate or reason for Brown’s abstention.
The other appropriations passed 6-0. Members also separately approved the Food and Beverage Fund application for the replacement EMA truck, which had previously received commissioners’ approval.
The courthouse bench funding will support Aiden Bradley’s Eagle Scout project to replace deteriorating concrete benches on the square with metal benches matching those around the square.
Bradley appeared with Keith Condra, JR Carmickle, Lucas Carmickle and Luke Bruner to discuss the project. Plans call for plaques commemorating Lions Club members.
Bradley requested approval of the project and a beneficiary representative to work with him. Commissioners had previously approved using $5,200 from the Food and Beverage Fund, and the council approved the advertised appropriation unanimously.
The council also heard a $20,000 Health First Indiana funding request from Robert Longest of Grace Haven.
Longest said the grant would help purchase a commercial reach-in cooler, repair a 12-passenger van and provide partial salary support for case management and health navigation staff.

Commissioners approved the request Oct. 14. According to the council minutes, the additional appropriation will be advertised for consideration at the November meeting. Council members gave verbal consent to pay the claim before that meeting.
Local income tax discussions remained unresolved after John Noblitt urged the council to take action on a MUST committee.
Brown said Noblitt was the only municipal representative who had contacted him about the committee. The council expressed interest in learning more about legislative changes that could emerge during the 2027 session before making further decisions.
Brown plans to communicate with other parties about local income taxes and the committee, but no formal committee will be designated at this time, according to the minutes.
Jessie Cornwell of the Orange County Health Department reported that the department plans to implement a new credit card payment system through Deluxe.
Cornwell said the county attorney and commissioners had approved the contract and that the system would have no cost.
Sheriff David Henderson reported that the jail housed 38 people at the time of the meeting. The population had ranged from 31 to 61 during the reporting period.
The department served 4,701 meals during the previous month at an average cost of $2.04 per meal.
Henderson also reported several crashes involving deer and encouraged motorists to report deer collisions. He urged drivers to watch for deer and school buses.
In other business, the council approved its 2027 meeting dates and the purchase of a new clock for the council room.

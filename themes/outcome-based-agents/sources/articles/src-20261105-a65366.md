Arvind Jain, Co-Founder and CEO of Glean, has tripled revenue in 15 months by making enterprise AI search the context layer for the AI agents used at work
Glean
 took six years to reach US$100m in annual recurring revenue. It needed just 15 months to triple it. 
The Palo Alto company, founded by a former 
Google
 Search engineer, passed US$300m ARR in May 2026 as enterprises discovered that AI agents are only as good as the company context they can see. 
Its Fortune 500 customer count nearly doubled in a year, 85% of customers now use it across five or more departments, and its engagement ratio runs at more than twice the SaaS benchmark. 
Glean has gone from a search tool to the system of context for 
enterprise AI
.
They need more than access to models. They need AI grounded in their company's own context, connected across their existing systems, and built with security and governance at the core.
Arvind Jain, Co-Founder and CEO of Glean
Arvind Jain
 founded Glean in 2019 after more than a decade working on Google Search and co-founding 
Rubrik
. 
The problem he set out to solve was familiar to any large company: knowledge scattered across hundreds of applications, with no single place to find it. 
Glean built a permission-aware index across more than 250 connectors, so employees only see what they are allowed to see. 
That index, built long before ChatGPT arrived, has turned out to be the hardest part of making generative AI useful at work.
The product has grown in layers. Glean Assistant answers questions across company data, Glean Agents automate multi-step work and customers can choose from more than 40 models. 
In 2026 Glean added NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra and post-trained its 
agentic search model
, Glean Waldo, on Nemotron 3 Nano, cutting latency by half and token use by a quarter. 
In tests it was preferred about 2.5 times as often as off-the-shelf MCP tools, which consumed 30% more tokens on average.
“They need more than access to models,” says Arvind Jain, Co-Founder and CEO, Glean. 
“They need AI grounded in their 
company's own context
, connected across their existing systems, and built with security and governance at the core.”
Revenue has followed. Glean passed US$100m ARR in February 2025, US$200m in December 2025 and US$300m in May 2026. 
Its last round, a US$150m Series F led by Wellington Management in June 2025, valued it at US$7.2bn. Customers include Booking.com, where it was the first AI platform rolled out company-wide, and DBS, which has more than 40,000 users. Ericsson staff have built nearly 3,000 agents on the platform.
Growth is now international and partner led. In May 2026 Glean formalised a legal entity in Australia, where its ANZ customer base grew by more than 60% in a year with names such as Canva, Xero and REA Group. 
In August it launched a Global Partner Network, naming Bain & Company its AI Transformation Partner of the Year, with partners holding more than 2,000 Glean accreditations. 
AWS and Snowflake support its regional push.

Amar Maletira
, Chief Operating Officer at Glean
Top technology partners

NVIDIA
: Glean added NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra in June 2026, and its agentic search model Glean Waldo is post-trained on Nemotron 3 Nano, cutting latency by 50% and token use by 25% for enterprise customers.

Bain & Company
: Named Glean's AI Transformation Partner of the Year when the Global Partner Network launched in August 2026, a programme whose members have earned more than 2,000 Glean accreditations helping enterprises deploy its platform.

Snowflake

Dell Technologies
: Glean worked with Dell Technologies to bring its AI agents and enterprise search to on-premises environments, one of several partnerships announced alongside the Series F in June 2025.

Glean's bet is that the winners in enterprise AI will not be the companies with the best model but those with the best grasp of a company's own knowledge and permissions. 

Tripling revenue in 15 months suggests buyers agree.

The next phase is about reach: more agents per customer, more partners selling and deploying the platform, and new markets such as Australia. 

With no new round since June 2025, the next valuation will show how far the market rewards that growth.

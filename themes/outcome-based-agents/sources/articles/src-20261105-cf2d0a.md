Crescendo Raises $350 Million Overseas in First Close of 4th Fund
Firm Targets $500 Million Each at Home and Abroad
Fund to Buy Mid-Sized Tech Firms in Chips and Software
Lee Young-ho
·
Published
2026-11-05 09:39 KST
Crescendo Equity Partners logo. Crescendo Equity Partners
Crescendo Equity Partners, a South Korean private equity firm, has completed the first close of its fourth blind-pool fund, raising more than $350 million from overseas institutional investors alone. That covers more than 70% of its overseas target.
Crescendo has closed the first round for Crescendo Private Equity Fund IV, according to investment banking sources on the 5th. The fund targets $1 billion in total, split evenly between domestic and overseas investors at $500 million each. The firm expects to exceed its overseas target once overseas limited partners currently under review are included. Several domestic institutions are conducting due diligence on potential commitments.
The new fund will be used to acquire controlling stakes in small and mid-sized Korean technology companies valued at 50 billion won to 500 billion won ($34 million to $340 million). Its main investment targets are semiconductors and electronics, business-to-business software, and cybersecurity. The strategy is to buy companies that hold leading technology in Korea but face limits on further growth, then expand them into global markets. The firm also plans to use a buy-and-build approach, in which an acquired company grows by purchasing other firms.
Crescendo is backed by Peter Thiel, the PayPal co-founder. Executives from Thiel Capital sit directly on the fund's investment committee. Thiel's overseas network is used to support portfolio companies' expansion abroad and cross-border mergers and acquisitions. Since its founding, Crescendo has invested 1.5162 trillion won ($1.03 billion) in 29 companies without a single loss of principal. Its cumulative assets under management total about 1.8 trillion won ($1.22 billion).
The firm's exit record is also building. Its second blind-pool fund returned more than the entire fund's principal through the sale of iText, a PDF software company, according to the firm. Its first blind-pool fund has posted a total internal rate of return above 30% and distributions to paid-in capital of close to twice the amount invested.
"The firm closed the first round of the fourth fund successfully thanks to the solid trust of global institutional investors," said Lee Ki-doo, chief executive of Crescendo. "We will serve as a partner so that Korean technology companies can make a major leap on the global stage."

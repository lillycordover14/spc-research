CLAY, N.Y. — 
After nearly 70 years of Republican leadership, Clay is preparing for a change at the top.
Democrat Dave Capria’s election as town supervisor marks a political milestone for the Onondaga County community. 
His victory also brings new attention to what community members can expect from the town’s leadership moving forward.
Onondaga County Democratic Committee Chairman Josh Ludden said the result reflects an interest among voters in a different approach.
“Clay has been Republican led for a long time and I think that people are ready to try something different,” Ludden said.
Community members also had an opportunity to share their thoughts during a town hall meeting Wednesday night. One attendee spoke about Capria’s previous involvement in the community, saying, “Dave Capria has helped me a lot, and I really appreciate that.”
For Capria, the focus now shifts from campaigning to governing.
He said he wants to hold Micron accountable for commitments made to the community, including following through on promises tied to the company’s development in the area.
Capria also pointed to pursuing more grant funding as an opportunity for the town. He said previous town boards had not pursued grants as aggressively as he believes they could have.
“Why leave money sitting on the table,” Capria said.
As he prepares to lead the town, Capria acknowledged there will be a learning curve but said he intends to make the position his full-time focus.
“I’m gonna learn and do my best,” Capria said. “This is going to be my full-time job.”
While the election represents a significant shift in Clay’s political history, the next test for the incoming supervisor will be translating his priorities into results for the community.

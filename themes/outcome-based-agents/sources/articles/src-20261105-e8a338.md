Ukrainian from Croatia Challenged Violations in Reserv+: Why the Court Did Not Help Even Without a Fine

                                                                                            21:19, 5 November 2026
                                                                                        
                                                                                                                                    

3 min

A man legally residing in Croatia updated his military registration data remotely, but with a delay.

A military liable person legally residing in Croatia found a mark about a violation of military registration rules in "Reserv+" and information about the Territorial Recruitment Center’s (TRC) appeal to the police. The reason was that the man did not update his personal data by July 16, 2024, but only did so in February 2026.

At the same time, no fine was imposed on him, no protocol was drawn up, and no administrative offense ruling was issued. The man went to court insisting that without these documents the TRC had no right to enter violation information into the register. Moreover, he believed that the statute of limitations for holding him accountable had expired.

Whether the TRC can leave a mark about a violation in "Reserv+" and appeal to the police if the person was not officially held administratively liable was considered by the Fifth Administrative Court of Appeal in ruling No. 420/12091/26.

Essence of the Case

The military liable person was registered at the Hadzhibey District Territorial Recruitment and Social Support Center.

As of February 4, 2026, the Unified State Register of Conscripts, Military Liable Persons, and Reservists contained a mark "Violation of Military Registration Rules" regarding him.

Additionally, the register showed information about the TRC’s appeal to the National Police dated June 12, 2025. The reason was failure to update personal data by July 16, 2024.

The courts established that the man indeed failed to fulfill the obligation to update military registration data within the prescribed period. He updated the relevant information only on February 4, 2026, via the "Reserv+" app.

Meanwhile, the plaintiff legally resided in Croatia. When updating data, he independently indicated his actual address abroad.

Considering the mark in the register illegal, the man filed a lawsuit in April 2026 with the Odesa District Administrative Court.

He requested to recognize the TRC’s actions of entering information about the violation of military registration rules as unlawful and to oblige the removal of this information from the register.

The plaintiff’s main argument was that he was not held administratively liable. The TRC did not draw up a protocol on an administrative offense against him nor issued a ruling on imposing an administrative penalty.

The man’s representative referred to paragraph 20-1 of part one of article 7 of the Law of Ukraine "On the Unified State Register of Conscripts, Military Liable Persons, and Reservists."

This provision stipulates entering into the register information about a person’s administrative liability for offenses under articles 210 and 210-1 of the Code of Ukraine on Administrative Offenses (CUAO), indicating the date, number, and brief content of the protocol and/or ruling.

According to the plaintiff, if such documents do not exist, then there is no legal fact provided by law allowing the entry of such information into the register.

Separately, the man emphasized the expiration of the statute of limitations for administrative liability.

According to his representative’s calculations, the deadline for updating personal data ended on July 16, 2024, so the date of the offense should be considered July 17, 2024. Accordingly, the plaintiff argued that the limitation period for holding him liable expired on July 17, 2025.

At the same time, he pointed out that the mark in "Reserv+" has not only informational significance.

In particular, the TRC sent an appeal to the National Police regarding his delivery. The plaintiff believed that the possibility of applying such measures affects constitutional rights to freedom of movement and personal inviolability.

The man also insisted that his stay abroad is legal, and his personal absence from the TRC under such circumstances does not indicate evasion of military registration duties. After all, he performed available remote actions, including updating information through "Reserv+" and reporting his actual residence address.

However, on September 8, 2026, the Odesa District Administrative Court dismissed the lawsuit.

The first instance court reasoned that the plaintiff updated the data with a significant delay, and the TRC entered the information based on current legislation and the information available at that time.

Disagreeing with this decision, the military liable person filed an appeal.

Positions and Conclusions of the Court

During the appeal, the Hadzhibey District TRC insisted that the information entered into the register reflects the fact of the man’s untimely fulfillment of the legal obligation.

The TRC representative noted that the military liable person did not update personal data within the period prescribed by law but did so only on February 4, 2026.

At the same time, the defendant distinguished between entering a mark about violation of military registration rules and the direct imposition of an administrative penalty.

According to the TRC, the corresponding mark has an informational character. It does not mean that the person has already been found guilty of an administrative offense or fined but indicates non-fulfillment of a legal obligation.

At the same time, such information, according to the defendant, is grounds for exercising the TRC’s powers provided by law, including appealing to the National Police regarding the delivery of the person to resolve the issue of administrative liability.

The TRC also confirmed that the man did not personally appear at the territorial center, the administrative offense case procedure was not initiated against him, and no penalty ruling was issued.

After reviewing the case materials, the Fifth Administrative Court of Appeal agreed with the conclusions of the first instance court.

The key point in this case was the distinction between violation of military registration rules and administrative liability for such violation.

The panel of judges referred to the provisions of the Law of Ukraine "On Military Duty and Military Service," which stipulates citizens' obligation to comply with military registration rules.

The court also considered the Law of Ukraine "On Mobilization Preparation and Mobilization," the Regulation on Territorial Recruitment and Social Support Centers approved by the Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 154 dated February 23, 2022, and legislation on maintaining the Unified State Register of Conscripts, Military Liable Persons, and Reservists.

Is a Fine Ruling Required for a Mark in "Reserv+"?

The appellate court agreed with the first instance court that the mere presence of the note "Violation of Military Registration Rules" in the "Reserv+" app does not indicate that the person committed an administrative offense punishable under article 210-1 of the CUAO.

According to the court, such a note in the circumstances of this case indicates that due to violation of legal requirements, the plaintiff is subject to delivery to the relevant TRC for resolving the issue of administrative liability.

At the same time, the court separately established that 
no information about the man’s administrative liability was entered into the register
.

Thus, the court distinguished information about violation of military registration duties from information about the results of administrative offense proceedings.

Therefore, the absence of a ruling imposing a fine was not grounds for recognizing the disputed mark as unlawful.

Does the Expiration of the Liability Period Matter?

The plaintiff’s arguments about expiration of the administrative liability period under article 38 of the CUAO and the need to apply article 247 of the CUAO were a separate issue.

The appellate court rejected these arguments.

The panel of judges reasoned that the dispute concerned the legality of entering information into the register, not the legality of a ruling imposing an administrative penalty.

Since no such ruling was issued against the plaintiff and no information about his liability was entered into the register, the court found references to the relevant CUAO provisions irrelevant in this dispute.

Thus, 
the appellate court did not determine whether the statute of limitations for holding the man administratively liable had expired
. It examined the legality of the TRC’s actions in entering information about violation of military registration rules.

Did the TRC Have the Right to Appeal to the Police?

The court also examined the TRC’s legislative powers regarding appeals to the National Police.

In particular, the panel referred to part three of article 38 of the Law of Ukraine "On Military Duty and Military Service," which provides for delivery by the National Police upon TRC requests of persons who committed administrative offenses under articles 210 and 210-1 of the CUAO in the manner prescribed by law.

Additionally, the court cited part one of article 27 of the Law of Ukraine "On Mobilization Preparation and Mobilization," regulating the TRC leader’s appeal to the police regarding administrative detention and delivery of a citizen under conditions established by law.

The panel reasoned that the Hadzhibey TRC established the fact of the plaintiff’s failure to fulfill military registration duties.

According to the appellate court, under such circumstances, the TRC had legal grounds to send an appeal to the National Police to deliver the man to resolve the issue of administrative offense and grounds for imposing a penalty.

At the same time, the court supported the first instance court’s conclusion that the presence of the corresponding note in "Reserv+" alone does not mean the person was administratively liable.

Does Living in Croatia Exempt from Military Registration Duties?

The court separately evaluated the plaintiff’s arguments that he legally resides outside Ukraine.

The man emphasized that he did not personally appear at the TRC because he was in Croatia. At the same time, he updated personal data remotely and indicated his actual residence address, so the territorial center knew his location.

The plaintiff believed these circumstances do not allow interpreting his absence as evasion of duties.

However, the appellate court disagreed with these arguments.

The panel supported the TRC representative’s position that residing abroad does not exempt a Ukrainian citizen from the constitutional duty to defend the homeland and comply with military registration rules.

Therefore, the court did not recognize the plaintiff’s residence in Croatia as grounds for satisfying his demands to remove information from the register.

Final Decision of the Appellate Court

Summarizing the case circumstances, the panel concluded that the first instance court correctly established the facts and made a decision in compliance with substantive and procedural law.

The Fifth Administrative Court of Appeal dismissed the military liable person’s appeal and left the Odesa District Administrative Court’s decision of September 8, 2026, unchanged.

Thus, in this case, the court recognized the legality of entering and maintaining the mark about violation of military registration rules, although no protocol was drawn up and no ruling on administrative penalty was issued against the man.

At the same time, the ruling reflects a fundamental distinction: a mark about violation of military registration rules is not equivalent to establishing the person’s guilt in committing an administrative offense and imposing a fine.

The appellate court ruling took legal effect from the date of its adoption. Its cassation appeal is possible only on grounds provided by article 328 of the Code of Administrative Procedure of Ukraine.

Nooks Launches Node, an AI Sales Assistant, and Secures $500M in Growth Capital&nbsp;&nbsp;Wowtale

[page text]
AI has changed one job after another on the same curve: first the tools help a little, then the job itself changes.

It happened in coding, then in legal and support. Nooks says sales is next. Selling is about people and persuasion, with no single right answer, so AI has taken longer to get there, but agents are improving fast.

AI sales platform 
Nooks
 has 
announced
 Node, which it calls the first AI personal assistant for sales, alongside a Revenue Agent Platform and $500 million in growth capital from 
General Catalyst’s
 Customer Value Fund.

The Revenue Agent Platform is meant to let reps and AI agents work the whole revenue lifecycle together. Nooks says a true revenue agent has three properties. It is goal-oriented, owning an outcome such as moving a deal to its next stage instead of finishing one task and waiting for a prompt. It has durable context, remembering every call, email, stakeholder and objection over months or years. And it is self-learning, improving with each interaction. Most AI tools in sales have one or two of these, the company says, and the third is the hardest.

Nooks argues that any AI tool can generate an email, but it knows which email wins the deal. It makes calls and sends emails on its own infrastructure, so its agents see the reply, the meeting and the closed deal. Nooks Research trains the agents on real sales interactions, and before an agent reaches a real buyer it rehearses against a simulated one. Deal, Outbound, Expansion and Coaching Agents are running today, with Inbound Agents due in the first quarter of 2027.

Node flips the usual relationship. For years, people have prompted AI; with Node, the AI prompts you. Nooks calls it the “EA for the AE,” an executive assistant for the account executive. It works every deal in the background and comes to the rep first. Its first job is deal management. It remembers what was promised on last week’s call, drafts the follow-up and, once the rep approves, sends it. It notices when someone on the buying committee goes quiet and flags it before the deal stalls, and when a deal slows it drafts the move most likely to restart it based on similar deals. Every member of the deal team gets a Node, and the Nodes share what is working. Reps approve every send, and Nooks says 96% of emails drafted by its agents go out without edits. Node is currently open to a waitlist.

Dan Lee is CEO of Nooks and Nikhil Cheerla is cofounder and CTO. The $500 million is not an equity investment. General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund pays up front for sales and marketing at companies with proven, predictable customer economics and is repaid from the customer value that spending creates, so Nooks pays only when it is paid. Nooks says more than 2,000 companies run on it, including Glean, Notion, Deel, Factory and Replit, and that customers typically build 3x more pipeline. It reports growing about 3x year over year, net revenue retention of 135%, and more than half of customers using more than one product. Those economics, Nooks says, are what made the commitment possible, and because it runs its own go-to-market on Nooks, it knows what every dollar turns into. General Catalyst partner Pranav Singhvi said AI changed how software gets built and Nooks is changing how it gets sold.

The money will go toward new products, enterprise and international expansion, and hiring, with more than 100 open roles. Lee argues AI will make companies want more salespeople, not fewer, saying Node made Nooks’ own sales team productive enough that it is accelerating hiring. Agents take the follow-ups and admin, he says, while reps spend their time on strategy, creativity and earning a buyer’s trust.

Competition: AI sales

AI sales is crowded across data, content, execution and agents, and deal intelligence. Nooks bundles execution, deal management and coaching in one platform.

Its most direct rivals are outbound AI sales development reps. Artisan’s agent Ava finds leads and handles email and follow-ups, and 
11x.ai
 runs outbound across email, LinkedIn and voice. Many companies that tried fully autonomous AI SDRs have reportedly moved back to hybrids with human review, which makes Nooks’ rep-approves-every-send model look aimed squarely at that gap.

In inbound and coaching, 
1mind
 uses a face-and-voice AI agent to greet website visitors and 
announced
 $40 million in funding. Letter AI puts an AI coach on live deals and raised a $40 million Series B.

Gong
 pioneered deal intelligence, analyzing sales calls and emails to predict wins and revenue, and has passed $300 million in ARR. In sales execution, Clari and Salesloft have merged to combine forecasting and execution. Against them, Nooks pitches agents that go beyond analysis to take the follow-up action themselves.

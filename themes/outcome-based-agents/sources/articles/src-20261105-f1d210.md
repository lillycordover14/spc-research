Banished ex-FBI agent John “Zip” Connolly has lost his appeal to clear his name.

A Florida judge denied his latest attempt at redemption Thursday, forcing his Boston-based lawyer, Peter Mullane, to keep trying in a higher court.

“It was a shocker,” Mullane told the Herald of the swift denial. “The judge didn’t ask a single question. It’s very discouraging.”

Mullane said the hearing was on Zoom and the judge, listed on court documents as Judge Lody Jean, said she would file her written ruling at a later date.

Her decision comes less than a month after Connolly filed 
a new affidavit
 in his case stating the FBI was told by slain mobster James “Whitey” Bulger that Connolly, his old handler with the feds, was not corrupt — but his boss was.

Mullane accused the Florida judge of not reading that affidavit, saying: “It’s the only explanation.”

In that affidavit, retired Los Angeles FBI agent Scott Frank Garriola states that “Bulger told me John Connolly had been framed for crimes he did not commit and that it was Connolly’s former supervisor, John Morris, who was corrupt.”

Garriola was on the team that arrested the Most Wanted mobster after a 16-year manhunt that June day in 2011 in Santa Monica, Calif. Bulger had been hiding out with companion Catherine Greig in a condo. FBI agents pulled $822,198 in cash out of the wall inside the unit.
 

Garriola wrote he was the leader of the FBI/LA Police Fugitive Task Force where he “personally did arrest Bulger in a parking garage below the apartment complex in which he was living.”

That’s where South Boston’s notorious gangster told the G-man he was writing his sordid life story, with the agent then reading the manuscript and taking notes. Bulger “made similar statements about Connolly being framed” in those writings, the agent adds in the affidavit.

Connolly is 83 and on home confinement in Lynnfield. He was let out of prison in 2021 after being diagnosed with cancer.

Connolly was found guilty of the murder of World Jai-Alai President John Callahan, who was shot in the back of the head and left in the trunk of a car in Miami International Airport in July of 1982. Bulger hitman John Martorano, who has long been a free man, did the killing.
 

Connolly was convicted of second-degree murder for wearing his FBI-issued sidearm when he met with Bulger in Boston to warn him of what businessman John Callahan knew. Bulger was murdered in a West Virginia prison in 2018.

Callahan’s widow, Mary Callahan, told the Herald Thursday over the phone from a nursing home in Chelmsford that dragging out this case is “not fair.”

“They’re not taking him back? They haven’t done it,” she said of Florida prison officials. “It doesn’t seem to make any sense to me. I’m in a nursing home in hospice, and I’m 86. I guess Florida gets what Florida wants.”

Morris 
tearfully apologized
 in 2013 for protecting Whitey.

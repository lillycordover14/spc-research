Maika Harper’s Toughest North of North Scene Was One She Couldn’t Cry Through&nbsp;&nbsp;Vulture

[page text]
“It’s wild to see Neevee so vulnerable but trying to be strong in that moment.”
                  
Photo: CBC/APTN/Netflix

Spoilers follow for the second season of 
North of North,
 all eight episodes of which premiered on Netflix on November 5. 

In the Arctic-set comedy 
North of North,
 businesswoman Neevee Kudlak is, as Maika Harper puts it, a “wild card.” An unapologetic, opinionated, and brusquely sexual nonconformist in the small, tightly knit Indigenous community of Ice Cove, Neevee’s the kind of woman who says of a Christian elder insisting on spiritually cleansing her town store, “I’m purging the church spirits with my heathen titties.”

In 
the series’ second season
, though, Neevee wants to change. Harper still plays her with a sarcastic, mischievous edge, but there’s contrition and sincerity there too. After revealing to her daughter Siaja (Anna Lambe) at the end of the first season that she had another daughter who was taken by the girl’s father because of her drinking at the time, Neevee’s trying to be more present for Siaja and Siaja’s young daughter, Bun (Keira Belle Cooper). And in season finale “Experrrrt,” Neevee finally admits her feelings for Siaja’s father, Alistair (Jay Ryan), at Ice Cove’s glamorous Winter Ball. After a long on-again, off-again relationship in front of Siaja and the rest of the town, Neevee willingly goes into Alistair’s arms.

If 
North of North 
were to be renewed, Neevee’s relationship with Alistair — along with Siaja tracking down Neevee’s elder daughter, Olivia (Kesha Allurut), and advocating for a renewed relationship with their mother — would be further tests for Neevee’s new commitment to considering others, Harper says. “It’s the first time that she’s really thought of anybody else. She’s trying to be nice for the first time, like, ever,” Harper says.

What was your immediate reaction to reading what Neevee would go through this season? 
It was so much fun because she is putting herself out there. She’s looking at Siaja and taking a page out of her book: She’s seeing her daughter do what she wants, and Neevee wants to do what she wants too. Season one was me and Bun, and working with a child actor — I have children myself — is something special. But season two is so different. It’s all adults for me now. When Bun goes on her little hide-and-seek in the Arctic, that is terrifying. Then me being so nonchalant about it, I think, is even funnier. Neevee’s got real lived experience.

You’re from Iqaluit, Canada, where 
North of North
 is filmed. How has that affected your performance?
It’s been amazing to have all of the support from the people who really love this show. I had my grade-six teacher sitting beside me in a scene, my nephew, my best friend — so many people who are close to me. And seeing the reception from within the community — all I really cared about was them because it’s so close to home.

Was there one scene that stuck out to you this season as showing how Neevee has changed? 
It’s episode “Ice, Ice, Baby,” when I’m trying to apply for a grant and I keep asking my daughter for help and she doesn’t. Neevee doesn’t ask for help ever, let alone asking her 
daughter 
for help — that’s wild. So when she’s almost begging Siaja and not getting any response back, that was a growing moment. That really solidified the difference between the two because they’re always a unit, but this was the first time that it was a really separate experience for Neevee.

Neevee and Siaja’s dynamic is unexpected this season because Neevee is, as you said, trying to take on some of Siaja’s go-getter behavior, while Siaja is trying to stand alone a bit more. How do you two work together to maintain that mother-daughter relationship? 
We’re friends in real life, but when we’re on set, we don’t prep together very often. There’s certain scenes that you prep for and that you really work hard to make sure are perfect. But me and Anna, we jive on a different level. We know [if we prepared], it would be too much. You save that for the moment. Coming to an emotional scene is always really difficult, but she is somebody I love so much, and I love sharing those intimate mother-daughter scenes with her.

There’s a conversation in the penultimate episode, “Ghosted,” in which Siaja is regretful and depressed after finding Olivia and potentially ruining any kind of relationship they could have, and Neevee comforts Siaja by telling her that those big emotions are what make her her. Tell me about filming that outside and in the snow. 
It’s so funny. I remember doing read-throughs, and one producer jokingly was like, “Maika always cries during read-throughs.” I can’t help it! When I was trying to memorize the words and get the cadence of the scene, it was really difficult, so I would cry constantly. I’m a very emotional person. I love 
Titanic.
 I love girly movies like 
A Walk to Remember.
 I love to cry. For this one scene, I thought, 
For 
sure 
I’m going to cry
. Usually Anna and I look at each other before we do a scene, no matter what it is — happy, sad, whatever — so we have a moment to lock in. But I looked right into Anna’s eyes, and I realized, 
No, I’m not crying.
 As a mom, you want to be strong.

That was the hardest scene I’ve ever done with Anna. I hope there’s so many more because she pushes me to a limit. She really tests me, and it’s also the writing, the directors, everything. You think you’re going in one way and you come out completely a different way than you thought, and it’s because of the truth of that character. It’s wild to see Neevee so vulnerable but trying to be strong in that moment. Also, visually, the stunning beauty of the Arctic — when Neevee says, “I went to therapy. It was bullshit. This is my therapy,” that alone sums up this character so perfectly. She does try really hard with people, with Alistair, with her community. I like that she’s too blasé to care, but she comes from a place of caring.

In sixth episode “Missing Pieces,” Neevee tries to tell Alistair about her feelings, but she ends up hiding in his igloo sauna when he returns home with Kati, the author he’s newly dating, then runs out of the sauna half-dressed because she was so hot from hiding inside the steam. What do you remember from filming that? 
Oh my goodness. It was actually cold; [the sauna] was a prop. Popping out of there and being so vulnerable — and quite literally naked, almost — it was wild. I sent a friend a video of me whinging a little bit. I was like, “Oh my God, it’s all guys out there!” The set is usually all guys, although the director [and other crew that day] were women. I was given this suit that looked almost like skin, so I wore that, but they were like, “No, we can tell that’s fake. Would you be willing to …?” And I was like, “Of course.” I love this show so much, and I like to do physical comedy, being silly and flailing a little bit more than you normally would. And that line, “Don’t look!” was actually me. That was not in the script. It was a closed set, so it was only necessary people and they’re all friends of mine. I’m not being mean or anything. But it really was like, 
Don’t look at me!

In the season finale, Neevee does finally come clean about her feelings for Alistair to Kati and Kati tells her not to screw it up. Neevee and Alistair then dance together. What did you see as Neevee’s thought process throughout all that? 
Before season two, she doesn’t give too much thought to relationships — only with her daughter she lost, and the daughter she has. She focuses a lot on just the present, and Alistair comes along and makes her realize that there’s a lot more to “just the present.” There’s so many more people you affect, and she’s learning that along the way. She’s very naïve in it, though, because she’s never thought of other people. She’s just thought of her unit, and even really within that unit, she’s really only thought of herself. It’s so much fun to explore her moments with other people like Alistair, and when she sees him with Kati — that yearning, that longing but also trying to be better for him, in a weird way. She’s sacrificing herself, which she never does. If there’s a season three, I’m going to freak out because I don’t even know how much further you can push all these characters.

I did want to ask you about a potential season three.
I would love a season three. Keep talking.

What would you want to explore in this relationship with Alistair? And what would you want to explore between Neevee and Olivia? The season ends with Olivia reaching out to Siaja, so there’s a bit of an open door.
Alistair, she really longs for because she can be her own authentic, real self. She puts up a big front, but with him, she gets to be silly and dumb and dance around with her grandkid and her kid. She doesn’t do that with everybody. It’s so special. And with Siaja, it’s interesting because she has only known her as her daughter, quite fiercely, and she didn’t get that chance with Olivia. I’d love to delve into that.

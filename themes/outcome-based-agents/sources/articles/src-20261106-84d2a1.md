Positive Points
 
 
 
Emera Inc (
NYSE:EMA
) remains on track to deliver compound adjusted EPS growth above its 5% to 7% target range in 2026 and is committed to longer-term EPS guidance through 2030.
 
 
 
The company introduced a five-year $21.5 billion capital plan, a 5% increase over the previous plan, driving expected rate base growth of 7.5% to 8.5% through 2031.
 
 
 
Emera Inc (
NYSE:EMA
) has a strong track record of delivering capital projects, with approximately $4 billion invested in 2026, the largest annual capital plan in its history.
 
 
 
The proposed merger with Canadian Utilities is expected to strengthen the financial position, support a stronger credit profile, and lower the cost of capital.
 
 
 
Tampa Electric's rates are approximately 25% below the national average, reflecting disciplined investment choices and a focus on affordability for customers.

Negative Points
 
 
 
Third-quarter adjusted EPS of $0.85 decreased 3% year-over-year, and year-to-date adjusted EPS of $2.90 is modestly below last year's strong performance.
 
 
 
The increased capital plan raises equity funding needs, with DRIP and ATM programs expected to average approximately $600 million annually, up from $400 million in the prior plan.
 
 
 
Regulatory approvals for the Canadian Utilities merger are pending, with a decision from the Alberta Utilities Commission requested by the end of April 2027, creating uncertainty.
 
 
 
Nova Scotia Power faces challenges in earning a fair return, and the proposed performance-based regulation framework may not be implemented, leaving regulatory risk.
 
 
 
The securitization of approximately $1 billion of costs in Nova Scotia is subject to approval by the Nova Scotia Energy Board, and a delay or denial could impact customer savings and financial results.
 
 
 
 
Q & A Highlights
 
 
Q
: How does Emera view the strategic rationale of the proposed Canadian Utilities merger, particularly the comparison between Alberta and Florida? 
A
: CEO Scott Balfour explained that Alberta offers unique advantages, driven by Canada's energy-focused agenda and significant industrial growth tied to oil and gas, alongside strong population growth. He emphasized that the merger combines two strong growth platforms anchored in Florida and Alberta, providing significant exposure to two of North America's most attractive utility growth markets.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: What is driving the increased equity funding component (DRIP and ATM) in the updated capital plan, and what are the expectations for Nova Scotia's performance-based regulation (PBR) framework? 
A
: CFO Jared Green stated the increased equity funding is primarily because the new plan does not include asset sales, unlike previous plans. Regarding PBR, CEO Scott Balfour noted the province is exploring it to connect utility performance to financial health, and Emera looks forward to working with the regulator to see if it creates a better framework for managing customer costs while earning a fair return.
 
 
Q
: What are the key drivers for achieving the high end of the new 7.5% to 8.5% rate base growth range, and how will this convert into EPS growth? 
A
: CFO Jared Green attributed reaching the high end to strong project execution and regulatory success, minimizing lags between investment and recovery. CEO Scott Balfour added that the increased rate base guidance reflects a balance between addressing significant investment opportunities and managing affordability for customers.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: Can you provide details on the 350-megawatt data center opportunity in Florida, including required capital and potential for expansion? 
A
: CFO Jared Green noted the initial 350-megawatt load requires minimal incremental capital, in the tens of millions for infrastructure tie-ins. Archibald Collins, CEO of Tampa Electric, added that the contract is expected by year-end, and the Auburndale Technology Center site could potentially support up to 700 megawatts, with discussions ongoing for up to 1,000 megawatts.
 
 
Q
: How should investors think about the impact of the revised capital plan and increased equity funding on the 5% to 7% EPS CAGR guidance? 
A
: CFO Jared Green stated it is too early to speak to the specific earnings forecast from the new plan, noting that the company will update its forecast and provide an appropriate starting point during the next quarterly call.
 
 
Q
: Does the updated rate base growth forecast assume the successful completion of the Nova Scotia securitization process? 
A
: CEO Scott Balfour confirmed that the rate base growth forecast assumes the securitization is completed, which would exclude the thermal assets from the rate base. He expressed confidence in the process for calendar 2026.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: With a potentially stronger credit profile post-merger, would Emera consider accelerating its dividend growth outlook? 
A
: CEO Scott Balfour stated that the company remains comfortable with its current dividend growth rate, which was set to bring the payout ratio down. He noted it is too soon to consider a change, as evidenced by the recent dividend increase within the existing range.
 
 
Q
: How do recent political developments in Canada and the US impact the industrial opportunity set in Western Canada? 
A
: CEO Scott Balfour said that while it is too soon to identify specific projects, recent political outcomes are encouraging for the portfolio and growth opportunities. He noted the premise that these developments could accelerate industrial activity in Alberta and bring forward opportunities is correct.
 
 
Q
: How does Emera view the non-regulated assets within Canadian Utilities, and are there any changes to the strategy for these assets? 
A
: CEO Scott Balfour indicated there has been no change in thinking, and the company is comfortable with the underlying nature of these assets and the growth strategy in Alberta. He added that post-closing, Emera will look to optimize the combined unregulated portfolios, including Emera Energy, to support growth.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: How is Tampa Electric addressing the stigma around data centers and ensuring a win-win for all customers? 
A
: CEO Scott Balfour emphasized that existing customers come first and new large loads must pay their own way, with a tariff design that can help reduce rate pressure for others. Archibald Collins, CEO of Tampa Electric, added that the narrative is shifting post-election, and the company's large load tariff was intentionally designed to support economic development while protecting existing customers.

MOORHEAD — Clay County's next county attorney wants prosecutors back at the treatment court table.
Newly elected Amber Gustafson says restoring the office's participation will be an early priority when she takes office in January. Gustafson defeated longtime incumbent Brian Melton in Tuesday's election.

ADVERTISEMENT

Melton decided earlier this year to discontinue the office's participation in drug court, DWI court and veterans treatment court.
Melton cited a lack of accountability and oversight and repeated violations by drug court participants for his decision.
Gustafson says questions about treatment courts were a frequent topic during conversations with voters.
She supports using the programs to help reduce repeat offenses.
To restart participation, she plans to meet with the people overseeing those treatment courts and the attorneys who would represent her office.
She wants everyone involved to understand how they'll work together.
"What I intend to do is to reopen the line of communication between the treatment court teams, the judges, but everybody on those teams, and my own (office's) lawyers ... that we all have an understanding of the process,"  Gustafson said.

ADVERTISEMENT

Gustafson also plans to review internal policies before making other changes, saying she wants to preserve what the office already does well.
The Clay County attorney serves as the state's lead prosecutor for the county.
Gustafson is a former Minnesota Army National Guard member and was appointed as Seventh District judge in 2018.

PITTSBURGH — Penguins star center 
Sidney Crosby
 first met his pal, 
Patrice Bergeron
, over 20 years ago, the two matched as Team Canada roommates for the better part of two weeks during the 2004-05 World Junior Championship.
“That’s how it worked, older guy with the younger guy,” Crosby recalled before Thursday night’s 
6-2 loss to the Bruins
, during an interview at the Penguins training facility some 20 miles north of downtown. “So I was 17 and Bergy was 19. I remember just being excited to learn from him.”
Bergeron, the teenage senior statesman, slightly more than 24 months older than his roomie.
“Well. . . ,” said a smiling Crosby, who’ll turn 40 next August. “He’d already played in the NHL, right?”
Crosby, the sureshot sensation from Cole Harbour, Nova Scotia, at the time was still months away from being drafted No. 1 by the Penguins in the 2005 NHL entry draft.

 Bergeron, after playing the full 2003-04 season as a rookie with the Bruins, was on loan to Team Canada, given time away from AHL Providence while the NHL was in the thick of what became a season-long lockout.
The World Juniors was the start of a run of four international gold medals  Bergeron and Crosby shared in their time as Team Canada linemates. They followed a year later with a fourth-place finish in the 2006 World Championship, then  a pair of Olympic golds (2010 and 2014) and gold yet again at the 2017 World Cup.
The pairing clicked from the start, noted Bergeron, who’ll be inducted in the Hockey of Fame Monday night in Toronto.
“I think we were reading off each other well–creating plays and openings,” said Bergeron, who played pivot on the line at the World Juniors, then moved to the wing in their later pairings over the years. ”That meant trying to utilize his speed and his smarts.”
One key to their success, Bergeron further explained, was recognizing where Crosby didn’t need much help, in the offensive end, be it behind the goal line or in the corners.
“In those situations, you learn pretty quickly, ” said Bergeron, “that you go get open, or find plays to give-and-go with him. If you go in with him, that kind of takes away the time and space he needs to get out of the corner when he has a couple of guys on him.”
Crosby entered Thursday night’s matchup against the Bruins with a career 658 goals, 1,771 points and his name thrice on the Cup with the Penguins. Like Bergeron, he’s a first-ballot shoo-in for the Hockey Hall of Fame, though that will have to wait until he clears a three-year post-retirement eligibility requirement.
“We would just play hockey,” said Crosby, musing over the chemistry he shared with Bergeron. “Like a lot of centers will tell you, it’s nice when you have a winger who’s comfortable at center. I mean, he’s a pretty easy guy to play with anyway. I laugh because, you know, we played together so long and feel we had great chemistry. But I am sure anyone who’s ever played with Bergy would tell you they had great chemistry with him.”
Crosby will be in California Monday on a Penguins road trip when his close pal, and symbiotic linemate, enters the august HHOF.
“As a guy that played with him, even though not on the same [NHL] team,” Crosby mused, “I just appreciate so much the experiences I had with him, especially from the time I was a young player, being able to pick his brain, and then playing together as long as we did. That’s pretty rare. I’m just really happy for him.”
Brunet back
Rookie defenseman  
Frederic Brunet
, scratched the previous three games (St. Louis, Chicago, Vegas), was back in the lineup and logged 16:21 of ice time against the Penguins.  
Brunet atoned for a couple of boo-boos in a 2-1 loss to the Hurricanes on Oct. 27 that earned him some extended time on the pine.
“Just to simplify his game again,” said coach 
Marco Sturm
, asked pregame what he hoped Brunet would take from the ordered respite. “I think that’s what he did really well at the start. He got away from it a little bit.”
Brunet’s strong points, per Sturm, are his legs and his hockey IQ.
“He’s got to use that for his advantage,” noted Sturm.
In his 10 games prior to the three-game stay in the cone of silence, the 23-year-old Brunet produced 1-3—4. Brunet picked up his fourth assist of the season on Mark Kastelic’s goad-ahead goal, 2-1, at 15:04 of the first period.
Poitras healing up
Sturm said fourth-line center 
Matthew Poitras
 underwent a surgical procedure to repair his broken nose, which occurred when he was drilled by a Braeden Bowman elbow in Monday night’s matchup vs. the Golden Knights.
“He could play if we needed him, “ said Sturm. “But he’s not 100 percent. Still recovering, but doing fine.”
Prior to getting his nose moved from north-south to east-west by Bowman, Poitras chipped in with 4-3—7 in his 11-plus games this season.
DiPietro in net
Michael DiPietro
, last seen in a 5-4 overtime win against the Blackhawks Saturday, when he made 37 stops, was back in net. He made 16 saves and posted his fourth straight win to improve to 4-1-0.  
Sturm has been impressed with the elder rookie’s compete level.
“I think that is his biggest strength,” said the coach. “He competes very hard and is also very calm. We like what we have seen so far.”
DiPietro was on the Boston bench, in favor of an extra attacker, when 
Morgan Geekie 
drilled  the tying (4-4) goal vs. the Hawks with 10.2 seconds remaining in regulation on Saturday.  The crowd went delirious, and a jubilant DiPietro, still in full gear on the bench, joined the delirium, jumping up and down with the joy of a Bay State bantam at his spot near the end of the bench.

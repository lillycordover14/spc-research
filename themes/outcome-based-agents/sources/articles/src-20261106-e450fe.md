Ronnie Clay Williams, age 82&nbsp;&nbsp;A Natural State Funeral Service

[page text]
November 6, 2026

With heavy hearts and cherished memories, we announce the passing of Ronnie Clay Williams, who departed this life on Saturday, October 31, 2026, at the age of 82.

Born on June 16, 1944, in Conway, Arkansas, to Dibrell Williams and Lucille Williams Brewster, Ronnie was raised in Conway and graduated from Conway High School, Class of 1962. Though life would take him in many directions, Arkansas—and particularly the waters and woods surrounding Lake Conway—always remained home.

Ronnie was a man who was never easily defined. He was an enigma: a man capable of extraordinary kindness and generosity, yet one whose flaws and struggles were just as much a part of his story. He was neither ordinary nor simple, and his life was not without its contradictions. He was, in every sense, a complicated man who lived life on his own terms. Those who knew him best understood that there were many sides to Ronnie, and each was part of the man he was.

A businessman early in his career, Ronnie eventually found his trade in carpentry, where his ability to work with his hands and his resourcefulness, served him well. He valued hard work and took pride in his craftsmanship. He was the kind of man who could make something useful, fix what was broken, and find his way through life’s challenges with determination and ingenuity.

But Ronnie’s true home was outdoors. A lifelong fisherman, hunter, and outdoorsman, he found his greatest contentment in the woods, on the water, and in the quiet company of nature. He was also a storyteller, with a lifetime of experiences, adventures, and tales to share. Fishing and hunting were more than hobbies; they were woven into the fabric of his identity.

In the end, it seems fitting that Ronnie’s final days were spent in a humble trailer on the banks of Lake Conway, Arkansas. It was a place that reflected the life he loved—close to the water, surrounded by nature, and far removed from the complications of the world.

Ronnie had one final request that perhaps captured his personality and sense of humor better than anything else. He asked that his ashes be scattered in Lake Conway, explaining:

“I spent my life eating fish out of Lake Conway. Now it’s their turn to eat me.”

It was a distinctly Ronnie way of looking at life. And so, in a final return to the waters that had given him so much pleasure throughout his life, he will become part of the place he loved.

Ronnie is survived by his three children: Karen Solomonides and her husband, Michael, of Farmington, Connecticut; Dr. Ronald Clayton Williams Jr. of Honolulu, Hawaii; and Whitney Cowardin and her husband, Randy, of Casper, Wyoming. He was a proud grandfather to five grandchildren: Kiele, Michael Jr., Melissa, Haley, and Wyatt, each of whom will carry their own memories and stories of him. Ronnie was also blessed with three great-grandchildren, Kamaha’o, Nanea, and Kuehu of Hawaii.

He is also survived by his siblings: Jerry Williams, Betty Jones, Linda White and Dianne Shannon of Conway. He was preceded in death by his siblings Suzanne Cokeley of Conway, and George (Butch) Brewster of Conway.

His legacy is not simply measured by what he accomplished, but by the lives he touched, the stories he left behind, the lessons learned, and the memories that will endure. His children, grandchildren, great grandchildren, siblings, Hi-Way Landing Buddies and extended family remain an enduring part of his story.

As the waters of Lake Conway continue to move, so too will the memories of Ronnie Clay Williams live on—in the stories told, the fish caught, the woods wandered, and the lives forever connected to his.

Ronnie’s celebration of life will be held in the Spring of 2027.

Forever loved, always remembered, and never forgotten.

May he rest in eternal peace.

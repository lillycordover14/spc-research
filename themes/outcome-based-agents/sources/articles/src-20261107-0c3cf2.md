Playoff scripts come in all shapes and sizes, and for the Mayfield football team, Nov. 6 might as well been the perfect script.

The Wildcats’ offense, defense and special teams contributed throughout and that spelled a signature 32-21 win over visiting Wadsworth at Wildcat Stadium in a Division II, Region 5 quarterfinal.

For the third time in school history, Mayfield (the No. 4 seed in Region 5) improved to 11-0 and advanced to a D-II regional semifinal at top seed Medina Highland on Nov. 13.

It was game few outside of the Mayfield program believed it could win, and that’s just fine with Coach Ross Bandiera.

“I think banding together in the offseason knowing we had one goal in mind, and that was to win,” said Bandiera. “We wanted to win. That was our goal, win football games. I don’t think anyone cares who’s getting their name called. Just want to win.”

Mayfield's Ben Koret runs for yards during the Wildcats' 32-21 Division II playoff win over Wadsworth on Nov. 6. (Frank Gambatese/For The News-Herald)

The winning formula for the Wildcats on this night was score early, control the line of scrimmage and try to slow down the high-scoring Grizzlies offense, which was led by All-Ohio quarterback Brysen Hall.

Check, check and check.

Quarterback Dario D’Agostino threw a 13-yard touchdown to wide receiver Ryan Eppich for a 7-0 first-quarter lead. It stayed that way until Kingston Murchison intercepted a Hall pass.
 

On the ensuing drive, D’Agostino again found Eppich alone on a deep pass to the Wadsworth 2. On the next play, C.J. Melaragno ran 2 yards for a TD. After a missed extra point, it was 13-0.

“Great first half by the kids,” said Bandiera. “Score, hold them for a whole half, that’s not easy to do against Wadsworth. That’s an explosive offense.”

It stayed 13-0 until early in the third quarter when Hall connected on a 72-yard TD throw and catch to Jay’len King that brought Wadsworth (8-3) to within 13-7.

Mayfield responded on the next drive by pounding the ball at the the Grizzlies’ defense, a drive that was capped by running back Ben Koret’s 10-yard TD run.
 

With Wadsworth trailing by two scores, its offense went into up-tempo mode but the Wildcats’ defense had another answer as linebacker Nick Barna stepped in front of Hall pass and returned it inside the 50.

Then D’Agostino connected with Eppich again, this time for a 42-yard touchdown and it was 26-7.

All the while, the Wildcats defense was shutting Hall and Wadsowrth’s offense.

“There was a lot of chatter going into the game, and we came out fired up,” said Mayfield defensive tackle Jon Harris, who had two sacks of Hall and harassed the QB all night.

Still, the Grizzlies didn’t quit as Hall threw a TD pass with 7 minutes to play. Down, 29-14, Wadsworth executed a onside kick to perfection and recovered. A few players later, Hall and King connected for another TD and it was suddenly 29-21 with 6 minutes remaining.

Mayfield then put together another effective drive that was mostly running plays. D’Agostino picked up 10 yards on a key third-and-2 play that was a designed QB run.

However, that drive stalled at the 16, and with 3 minutes to play, Bandiera called on kicker Grady Spetz for the biggest boot of his career. The kick was true from 33 yards, and Mayfield led, 32-21, a play that essentially clinched the victory.

“We believe in Grady Spetz,” said Bandiera. “He does that in practice all the time.”

THE SCORE

Mayfield 32, Wadsworth 21

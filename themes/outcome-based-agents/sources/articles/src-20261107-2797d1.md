WAUSAU, Wis. (WSAW) - Zoey Ewer, 4, can’t walk on her own. A customized car is helping her learn to move independently at Lincoln Early Learning Academy in Wausau.
The adaptive car is part of the Wausau School District and is used by other students too. It increases independent mobility for students. It also helps with cognitive and social engagement.
Zoey is learning to navigate school hallways on her own with the car. It allows her to travel quickly and have some fun.
A chance to move without help
Zoey’s mother, Laura Ewer-Swarick, said the car gives her daughter a little more independence. 
“It’s a chance for her to actually be able to move without us controlling the whole thing,” Ewer-Swarick said.
Ewer-Swarick said Zoey faces a lot of obstacles. She said Zoey has trisomy 9 mosaic.
“We did not find out until after she was born and she was in the NICU at Aspirus,” Ewer-Swarick said. “So, she had a few things going on. And she has a chronic lung disease.”
Zoey’s older brother, Mason, said he has seen a difference in how she plays.
“It’s really great,” Mason said. “I usually like playing with Zoey on the ground, and it’s really nice to see her out actually having fun driving in a little vehicle.”
How the car was customized
The physical therapist for the Wausau School District worked with Fox Valley Technical College to customize the car. The work took an entire day.
“We designed a high back seat support for our students who have difficulty maintaining their posture,” Lodahl said. “We have harness supports on there for them so that they can stay upright. We also have lateral supports to keep them in the seat really nice.”
How the car works
To move, students press a red button. Lodahl uses a remote.
The goal is to increase independent movement, communication and social engagement.
“For Zoey, we’re working on stop and go,” Lodahl said. “So she has to figure out how she can move her body forward. We want her to work on going where she wants to go instead of just pointing.”
Lodahl said students are loving the car.
“It’s early power mobility,” Lodahl said. “And so if we can get a kid figuring out how they can use a powered mobility device early, that could open up doors for their mobility later on. So that might be like a power wheelchair or a different form or a device that they could use. But starting them young with that sort of mobility is really empowering.”
Plans for the car
Lodahl hopes the car can be part of the district’s track meet in the spring. That way, students who can’t walk or run can still be included.
The physical therapist travels with the car to different schools. She has received good feedback from both teachers and families.
Ewer-Swarick said she hopes the car has snow tires.
“I hope it has snow tires because, you know, she’s obviously gonna wanna cruise around,” Ewer-Swarick said.

Artisan Circle’s revival on full display in west Fort Worth&nbsp;&nbsp;Fort Worth Report

[page text]
Artisan Circle in west Fort Worth is in the midst of a revitalization, driven by recent restaurant, retail and entertainment openings, with more coming soon.

The five-block, mixed-use urban village, previously known as Crockett Row, was purchased by the investment arm of the Younger Partners commercial real estate firm in August of 2022. West 7th Street and Morton Street form the property’s northern and southern boundaries, with Foch Street and University Drive on the eastern and western edges, respectively.

Younger Partners chose the Artisan Circle name because of the property’s location in the Cultural District and to anchor their vision for what is to come.

Longtime area fan favorites like Social House and Movie Tavern on Crockett Street have been recently joined by a Tandy Leather flagship store, Collectiv Academy beauty school, a Sandbox VR entertainment venue, and restaurants that include Top of the Morning Brunch House, The Henry McCarty Irish Pub, La Cabrona and Terra Mediterranean, which returned after a more than five-year absence to its new, larger location. 

Tenants coming soon include the Fort Worth location of a popular Dallas-based pizzeria called Partenope Ristorante and the Silver Leaf Cigar Lounge, which will be relocating to Artisan Circle from its current downtown Fort Worth space in early 2027.

Co-managing partner Moody Younger said his firm chose to purchase the property because they viewed it as some of the best-located real estate in the city, which they see as having tremendous upside as Fort Worth continues to evolve.

“We want it to be an area that Fort Worth is proud of, where you’re comfortable bringing your family to a dinner, or to an event, or to the movies,” Younger said. 

Because of the area’s Cultural District ties, Younger said as the area fills out, he envisions future retail offerings to lean toward boutique-type products and art.

Area improvements

Younger Partners purchased the property at a time when many of the storefronts were vacant due to economic hardship from the COVID pandemic, parking frustrations, and a growing bar culture in the neighboring West 7th entertainment district that created an undesirable environment for family-oriented restaurants and retailers.

After a number of violent incidents in and around the neighboring West 7th bars such as the 
shooting death
 of a Texas Christian University student in September 2023, Younger Partners implemented improved security and installed additional cameras in all of their parking garages.

At the same time, the city increased police presence in the West 7th district and implemented a 
Golden Lasso
 accreditation program targeted at businesses that hold a Texas Alcoholic Beverage Commission license. An accredited business commits to train employees on TABC laws, fake identification detection, criminal and civil liabilities, occupancy management, and other safety-related criteria. 

Younger credits the Golden Lasso program, increased security, and the exit of some of the more problematic establishments with creating a more favorable environment for businesses to locate in his neighboring Artisan Circle. “It feels like it’s turned a corner, really towards the end of last year, early this year,” he said.

Fort Worth City Council member Elizabeth Beck who represents District 9 told the Fort Worth Report that she is proud of the Golden Lasso program. “One of the positives was it really brought bar and business owners together with the city,” she said. “It’s a way for us to engage bar owners on best practices for operation, so that they are safer, and our city is safer.”

Beck also pointed to 
statistics
 unveiled in 2025 that showed a 36% reduction in crime from the previous year in the broader West 7th area that includes Artisan Circle. “When you look at an area holistically and you make changes, you can reduce crime,” she said. She credited the reduction to a combination of smart policing, security cameras, enhanced lighting, traffic control bollards, an ordinance banning open containers, and a change in zoning regulations to limit the size of bars.

Other improvements the Younger Partners have made to entice business owners and customers include four-hour garage parking validation and more efficient street parking. They are also working with the city to reinstitute two-way traffic on some streets that had been converted to one-way in an attempt to manage late-night bar traffic.

“For a retailer, you need two-way streets, not one-way streets,” Younger said. 

Beck is supportive of the initiative to restore two-way streets. “As long as we have the proper safety studies, I’m supportive of any measure that is a positive improvement,” she said.

Celebrants take a break during a Nov. 1 Día de los Muertos street festival sponsored by Artisan Circle and La Cabrona restaurant. (Doug Wilhelm for the Fort Worth Report)

Business owner perspectives

The 
site plan
 for Artisan Circle shows nearly two-thirds of the district’s storefronts now occupied. Of the 18 remaining vacancies, Younger said that nine have deals in process, including a soon-to-be announced restaurant-entertainment tenant for the large space at the southwest corner of Crockett and Currie streets previously occupied by the Concrete Cowboy sports bar. 

The Fort Worth Report sat down with owners and representatives of some of the newer businesses to find out what motivated them to open in Artisan Circle.

Terra Mediterranean and La Cabrona

Leena Shanna is chief operating officer of La Cabrona and Terra Mediterranean, located across from each other on Crockett Street. The two restaurants are a true family affair.

Terra Mediterranean, opened in 2010 at the corner of Crockett and Norwood streets by Shanna’s father and uncle, operated there until late 2019, when rising rents, the bar culture, and loss of business due to parking issues forced it to close.

Fast-forward a few years, and an appeal from Younger Partners led Shanna to consider reopening in Artisan Circle. “They said that Terra was the restaurant people most wanted to see return to Fort Worth,” Shanna said. “They wanted to be the ones to bring it back.”

Terra reopened in its new location in March 2025. Like the original, it features Middle Eastern recipes straight from Shanna’s paternal grandmother’s kitchen.

La Cabrona opened in September 2024 and features authentic, elevated cuisine with roots in Shanna’s mother’s home cooking in Monterrey, Mexico. Leveraging her father’s restaurant expertise, Shanna and her mother, Rosario Ortega, developed the vision that would become La Cabrona.

Ortega is La Cabrona’s co-owner and chef and is also the restaurant’s namesake, in a way. Shanna said her father has always referred to her mother as ‘La Cabrona,’ which loosely translates to a tough, independent woman. “She was a super-mom and has a really fun personality. That’s what it means to us, and why we used the name.” 

Collectiv Academy

The Collectiv Academy sits at the northwest corner of Crockett and Norwood streets. Opened in July 2025, it is the sixth and newest location for the beauty school brand.

“There was a gap in the market for training beauty professionals for careers in high-end salons and studios,“ said business director Fielding Graham. “We thought that we should put a modern spin on beauty education.“

The school offers 34-week cosmetology and 25-week esthetics programs.

As for the chosen location in Fort Worth, Graham said, “We like our schools to be central and accessible, and Artisan Circle definitely checked that box.” He also credited convenient parking and alignment with the Younger Partners’ vision for the area.

Graham said that another plus is the proximity to TCU, which provides a customer base that can benefit from affordable haircuts, which in turn help the budding stylists with their training. “They get a good result,” he said, “Because the instructors are checking every step.”

Top of the Morning Brunch House

Top of the Morning Brunch House opened in February 2026 at 2948 Crockett St. in the space formerly occupied by Mash’D. Owner Nik Roditis is a first-generation Greek American, raised in Chicago, with parents and relatives who were all in the restaurant business. He’s been a Fort Worth resident for the last 12 years.

Roditis said that through experience gained as a partner in the Yolk restaurant chain, he decided to open a “casually sophisticated” brunch restaurant in Fort Worth, influenced by some of his favorite eateries in Greece and Italy. He narrowed in on Artisan Circle after a holiday visit to the area where he saw lighted, tree-lined and walkable streets with a neighborhood feel. “It kind of reminded me of Highland Park,” he said, “It’s gorgeous.”

Roditis is good friends with owners of two other new Crockett Street venues: The Henry McCarty Irish Pub and the soon-to-open Partenope Ristorante. He said they made a joint decision to call Artisan Circle home for their new establishments. 

Speaking of the area’s resurgence, Roditis said, “I think of the three of us as catalysts.” 

The Henry McCarty Irish Pub

Alan Kearney is the owner of The Henry McCarty Irish Pub, which opened January 2026 at 2869 Crockett St. Originally from Dublin, Ireland, Kearney owns several Irish pubs across the metroplex through his Merrion Group. He describes his newest venture as “good old Irish hospitality at its best.”

The pub’s name is a nod to the history of the Wild West and the Irish immigrant experience. Henry McCarty was the birth name of famous outlaw Billy the Kid, born in the United States to Irish parents who fled Ireland’s great famine in the mid-1800s. 

Kearney was already familiar with the Artisan Circle area as a result of his ownership of the Trinity College Irish Pub which he opened in 2018 and shuttered in early 2025. 

He’s happy to see the return of families to Artisan Circle, which he hopes will grow into a Fort Worth destination, and he says to watch for more concepts to come in the next 18 months.

 “We’re not just building restaurants here,” Kearney said, “We’re building a neighborhood.” 

Partenope Ristorante

Partenope Ristorante is slated to open by early December 2026 at 2949 Crockett St. This will be owner Dino Santonicola’s third restaurant in the metroplex; the other locations are in Dallas and Richardson. 

The Fort Worth location he chose was previously home to Fireside Pies and Si Tapas, but Santonicola said that anyone familiar with the old space will not recognize the new layout. A large wraparound bar has been replaced with a much smaller one, seating has been increased, and the floors and ceiling have been redone, all to reflect a comfortable dining aesthetic common to his restaurants.

Santonicola credits the Younger Partners with being willing to assist. “We talked with Moody, and he helped us with the build-up because he knew that the place needed work.”

Partenope will offer authentic Neapolitan cuisine, including pizzas and fresh homemade pastas. “I’m from Naples, and I’m very happy to present my culture,” he said.

Santonicola said he was influenced to open a restaurant here by customers at his other restaurants who kept asking when he was coming to Fort Worth. 

As for Artisan Circle, he said, “We saw the potential, we really liked the vibe, and we liked the space.”

Wausau affordable housing fund on pace to spend $1.79 million in a year budgeted for zero&nbsp;&nbsp;Wausau Pilot & Review

[page text]
By Shereen Siewert | Wausau Pilot 7 Review
Wausau’s affordable housing fund is on track to spend $1.79 million this year, though the city’s adopted 2026 budget authorized no spending from the fund.
The Wausau City Council takes up one piece of that spending Tuesday: a $650,000 budget amendment for a tax increment grant to Westside Commons. Gorman & Company built the 56-unit affordable apartment complex at 415 S. First Ave. As a change to the adopted budget, the amendment needs a two-thirds vote of the full council, or eight of 11 alders.
“The development has fulfilled requirements of the Development Agreement, and the $650,000 TIG payment is now due in accordance with the agreement,” Economic Development Manager Patrick Gatterman wrote in an Oct. 27 memo. The Finance Committee approved the change 5-0.
City budget documents do not explain the rest of the $1.79 million. The Housing Stock Improvement Fund, which holds the money, shows no spending in either the adopted or modified 2026 budget. The estimate published with the proposed 2027 budget shows $1,793,409 leaving the fund by year’s end.
The city has also committed $1.9 million to Commonwealth Development Corp. for a 50-unit affordable development at 700 Grand Ave. Development Director Randy Fifrick told Wausau Pilot & Review in September that the project received an occupancy permit the week of Sept. 21. He said the first payment comes due 30 days after occupancy. Commonwealth had drawn none of the money as of Sept. 25, Fifrick said. No budget amendment for a 700 Grand Ave. payment appears in Tuesday’s council packet.
The fund holds money from a one-year extension of Tax Increment District 6, which the council approved in December 2023 to raise money for affordable housing. The extension 
sent $3.87 million into the fund
 in 2025. That money would otherwise have been split among the city, the Wausau School District, Marathon County and Northcentral Technical College. The fund began 2026 with $4.27 million.
The Finance Department’s Oct. 5 corrections memo lowered the fund’s projected year-end balance from $3 million to $2.53 million, Wausau Pilot & Review 
reported
.
Asked whether the city has written criteria or an application process for awarding money from the fund, Fifrick wrote, “We follow the authorizing Resolution and funds for 700 Grand and Westside Commons were approved through Development Agreements.”
The city and the developer signed a development agreement March 26, 2024. Under that agreement, the city sold the Westside Commons site to the developer for $1. The city promised the $650,000 grant once the project reached completion, stabilization and permanent financing. Stabilization means 90% of the tax credit units occupied for three consecutive months.
The grant goes to Lutheran Social Services of Wisconsin and Upper Michigan, a member of the project’s managing member. Lutheran Social Services then lends the money to the ownership entity, Riverview Lofts Wausau LLC. Riverview Lofts was the project’s working name when the agreement was signed. Fifrick said the building received occupancy in 2025.
The 2023 extension resolution named four sites. Fifrick also gave updates on the other two:
Thomas Street:
 An initial environmental assessment of city lots found some that need cleanup. The rest have gone through the city’s property disposition process and are for sale.
Sherman Street:
 A six- to eight-unit project once considered for Sherman Street is being built by the city’s Community Development Authority on Eighth Avenue without extension money.
Wausau Pilot & Review requested records detailing payments from the fund in September. The city released a partial set Sept. 25 and said more would follow.
The 2027 budget plans $190,000 in spending from the fund.

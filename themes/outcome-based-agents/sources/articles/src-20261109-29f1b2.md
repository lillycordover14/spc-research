USA Today
‘s Bob Nightengale wrote
 Monday that Trea Turner and Brandon Marsh are “available” in trade talks, while the Phillies do not plan to move either Bryce Harper or Kyle Schwarber. 

In some senses as the MLB GM meetings begin in Las Vegas Monday, Nightengale’s report is fairly obvious. 

Of course the Phillies are willing to move Turner, who is coming off of a season where he hit just .242 and played defense poorly enough that 
he’ll have to prove to the team moving forward
 that he can continue to play his desired spot of shortstop. It would be hard to believe there’s much of a market for Turner, who will play next season at 34 years old and is still owed more than $189 million over the next seven seasons. Perhaps the Phillies could eat a substantial portion of that and take back a bad contract, but at that point, have you really improved? Even if all those hurdles could be cleared, Turner still has a full no-trade clause, so he could block any deal. 

Brandon Marsh is a more realistic trade candidate as he enters his final year of arbitration eligibility. Set to turn 29 next month, Marsh was an All-Star starter in 2026, batting .301 with an .829 OPS in the first half. However, after the Midsummer Classic he was one of the worst players in baseball, hitting just .184 with a .502 OPS. At this point, Marsh is probably best cast as a strong-side platoon player. And perhaps after four and a half seasons with the Phillies, it’s time for a change. If the Phillies could acquire a reliever or third baseman, for example, on an expiring deal in return for Marsh, that might be a fair trade. 

As for Harper and Schwarber, is it really surprising that the Phillies want to hold onto a pair of players who could one day where their cap into the Hall of Fame? 

Harper just played all 162 games for the first time in his career, and even though he’s now 34 years old, he’s owed less than $130 million over the final five seasons of his deal, which is pretty reasonable. He also has a full no-trade clause and made pretty clear when he initially signed with the Phillies that he wanted to lay down roots. 

Schwarber, meanwhile, just returned to the Phillies on a five-year/$150 million contract last offseason. While his second half was disappointing, he still finished in a tie with Pete Crow-Armstrong for the MLB lead with 45 home runs. Schwarber’s deal didn’t have a no-trade clause written into it, but he’s now gained 10-and-5 rights, meaning he can block any trade as well. 

Of this quartet, Marsh seems like the only realistic trade candidate. But who knows, crazier things have happend.

An arrangement with no published text

The "diesel deal" is neither a treaty nor a published contract. It results from a phone call between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin, which the US president described on his social network with specific volumes. The Russian readout of the call mentions Ukraine, Iran and bilateral relations, without repeating those volumes. The Kremlin's foreign policy adviser, Yuri Ushakov, only mentioned major economic initiatives, including possible deliveries of Russian fuel.

Two unilateral decisions give the arrangement substance. OFAC, the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control, issued a general license. The Russian government, for its part, decided to partially lift its ban on diesel exports, which had been introduced to protect its domestic market. A recent precedent for a US oil agreement, the one with Venezuela, is covered in our analysis: 
the US-Venezuela agreement rests on NABEP
.

What the license allows and excludes

The license authorizes transactions normally prohibited under the two US sanctions regulations targeting Russia: the Harmful Foreign Activities Sanctions Regulations and the Ukraine-/Russia-Related Sanctions Regulations. Imports into the United States are included. However, it expressly excludes any debit of accounts held at US banks by the Russian central bank, the national wealth fund and the finance ministry.

The text suspends neither the other US regimes that would prohibit the same transaction, nor the sanctions of the European Union and the United Kingdom. These continue to weigh on shipping, insurance and financing. Kirill Dmitriev, the Russian president's special envoy for economic cooperation and head of the Russian Direct Investment Fund, remains the main negotiator with Washington.

A diesel market under strain

The global diesel market has been in acute shortage for several months. The war waged by the United States and Israel against Iran and the disruption of the Strait of Hormuz have reduced refined product exports from the Gulf and global refining capacity. Ukrainian drone strikes have also knocked out part of Russia's refineries. The average pump price of diesel in the United States hit record levels in late summer and remained high in early November, weighing on road transport, agriculture and the cost of many consumer goods. That price ranked among voters' dominant concerns in the midterm elections, after which Republicans lost both chambers of Congress.

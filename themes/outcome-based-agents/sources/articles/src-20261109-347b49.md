JPMorgan's latest research report upgraded German software giant SAP to "Overweight" from "Neutral" and sharply raised its price target on the European-listed shares to €275 (approximately $310) from €175 (approximately $200), implying roughly 30% upside based on the latest price. The news drove SAP shares up as much as 2.1% in early trading on the German stock market.

The report's core argument is that enterprise artificial intelligence is entering a new phase. Lead analyst Toby Ogg notes that as AI projects move from proof-of-concept to full-scale production deployment of agents, the ability to scale and deliver returns on investment no longer depends on the model alone, but on three key elements: "context, control and cost." He argues this plays directly to the strengths of systems-of-record vendors like SAP.

Ogg elaborates that building an AI prototype is relatively simple, but once in a production environment, data quality, process context and system integration become the deciding factors. SAP delivers these capabilities through Business Data Cloud, the Business Technology Platform, the Signavio process-mining tool and its Knowledge Graph, and its reach is gradually extending to non-SAP data.

On the control front, the report cites the July hacking of Hugging Face, an attack carried out end-to-end by autonomous agents, underscoring the risks posed by agentic AI. By contrast, SAP's Joule assistant operates within customers' existing permissions and audit trails, and the company has pledged not to use customer data to train external large language models.

Cost is the third key factor. An IDC survey shows that 67% of organizations that have deployed AI agents in production have exceeded their budgets, while Gartner estimates that even as token prices continue to fall, agent-related costs will still rise roughly fivefold by 2028. Ogg believes SAP will be able to price by completed tasks in the future, helping customers route work to the most cost-effective models and trading business context for performance and cost advantages.

SAP's own AI product line was also cited as a plus. The report lists internal investment in the Joule agent, overall progress in industry models, and a string of bolt-on acquisitions, including WalkMe, LeanIX, SmartRecruiters, Reltio and Dremio. JPMorgan's checks with customers and partners show that these products are beginning to show early market traction.

Financial signals are also emerging. Ogg notes that SAP's Current Cloud Backlog has beaten expectations for three consecutive quarters, growing 26% in the third quarter, with organic new additions significantly higher than a year earlier and breaking through the prior second-derivative trend. He is also more confident on near-term margins, citing efficiency gains from internal AI and room for further workforce restructuring.

Based on these judgments, JPMorgan raised its 2026-2028 non-IFRS EBIT and earnings-per-share forecasts by low-to-mid single-digit percentages, mainly reflecting greater margin expansion, and slightly lifted its 2027 revenue growth forecast. Its 2027 EPS estimate rises to €8.38 (approximately $9.4) from €8.20 (approximately $9.2).

It is worth noting that SAP shares have rebounded markedly from their July lows, and valuations no longer reflect market fears of AI-driven disruption. Ogg does not shy away from this point, but stresses that the company's earnings growth model alone is enough to support attractive returns; based on the updated forecasts, non-IFRS EPS compound annual growth from 2026 to 2028 is about 17%, and the €275 price target equates to roughly 24 times forward earnings.

For investors, the significance of this report lies in offering a new valuation framework for European software stocks: AI is no longer just a theme, but a question of whether it can be converted into orders and margins. If enterprise AI spending does shift from scattered pilots to full-scale production as JPMorgan expects, established platform vendors with data governance, access control and process integration capabilities will be the main beneficiaries when capital is reallocated.

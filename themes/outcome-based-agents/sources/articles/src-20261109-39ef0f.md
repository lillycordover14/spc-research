Emera (EMA): Record Capital Commitment and Transformational Merger Reframe the Florida-Led Rate Base Story&nbsp;&nbsp;AlphaStreet

[page text]
Emera Inc. (NYSE/TSX: EMA) reported Q3 2026 results on November 6, 2026, posting adjusted earnings per share of CAD $0.85 — a 3% year-over-year decline — while unveiling two consequential strategic announcements: a record five-year capital plan and a transformational all-share merger. Year-to-date adjusted EPS reached CAD $2.90, with operating cash flow rising 7% year-to-date, excluding working capital adjustments. Despite quarterly earnings softness, management reaffirmed compound adjusted EPS growth above its 5-7% long-term target and maintained guidance through 2030.

Q3 Operating Story: Capital Deployment and Business Drivers

The headline EPS decline masked a nuanced operating picture. Q3 2026 adjusted earnings totaled CAD $260 million, compared to CAD $263 million in the prior-year quarter, with the year-over-year shortfall primarily driven by long-term incentive plan (LTIP) hedge timing mismatches that reduced Q3 EPS by approximately CAD $0.06 per share — a non-cash accounting item. Tampa Electric Company delivered results largely in line with the prior year, benefiting from revised depreciation rates expected to reduce annual depreciation expense by approximately USD $20 million on a go-forward basis. Peoples Gas Company performed strongly, driven by new rates effective January 1, 2026, and higher off-system sales. Nova Scotia Power benefited from new base rates and increased residential volumes, partly offset by higher operating costs.

Capital deployment is the central operating narrative. Emera deployed approximately CAD $2.8 billion in capital year-to-date through Q3 2026, on track for a full-year record of CAD $4.0 billion. The company simultaneously unveiled a CAD $21.5 billion five-year capital plan for 2027-2031 — a 5% increase from the prior plan — expected to drive consolidated rate base growth of 7.5% to 8.5% annually. Approximately 82% of that capital, roughly CAD $17.5 billion, is concentrated in Florida operations.

A meaningful near-term growth catalyst sits within Tampa Electric’s large-load strategy. The utility filed a large-load tariff application with the Florida Public Service Commission on September 30, 2026, targeting up to 1,000 megawatts of data center demand — with a decision expected in early 2027. The initial 350-megawatt contract at the Auburndale Technology Center would require only tens of millions of dollars in infrastructure tie-ins, making it a highly capital-efficient addition that could accelerate rate base growth beyond current guidance if approved. Tampa Electric’s customer rates remain approximately 25% below the national average, providing regulatory headroom to support investment recovery while maintaining customer affordability.

Revenue Mix, Balance Sheet, and What the Merger Implies for Investors

Year-to-date adjusted earnings of CAD $887 million represent a CAD $9 million improvement over the comparable 2025 period. The funding structure for the CAD $21.5 billion capital plan is diversified: operating cash flow is expected to fund 45-50% of requirements, debt 30-35%, equity through dividend reinvestment and at-the-market programs approximately CAD $600 million annually (a 50% increase from the CAD $400 million targeted under the prior plan), and hybrid securities 5-7%. The increase in annual equity issuance reflects the absence of asset sales in the new plan — a meaningful change from prior planning periods that partially offset capital needs through portfolio transactions.

The most strategically significant development was announced on October 6, 2026: an all-share merger with Canadian Utilities Limited valued at approximately CAD $14.3 billion. The combination would create a top-20 North American utility with approximately $72 billion in combined enterprise value, $45 billion in rate base, and six million customers, with Emera shareholders expected to own approximately 60% of the combined entity. A $32 billion capital program through 2030 is planned, with approximately 80% of combined earnings expected from Florida and Alberta — two high-growth utility jurisdictions. Management projects first-year EPS accretion following close, with improved credit metrics under both Moody’s and Fitch frameworks.

Emera approved its 20th consecutive annual dividend increase, reinforcing management’s confidence in the durability of the regulated earnings base. The company targets a cash flow-to-debt ratio of 12% by year-end 2026, maintaining investment-grade discipline through the capital intensity cycle. Following the TECO Energy acquisition, Emera’s earnings compound annual growth rate accelerated from 4.4% pre-transaction to 10.3% post-transaction — a track record management points to as evidence of its ability to extract value from large-scale combinations.

What Investors Should Watch Next

Three catalysts define the near-term outlook. First, the Florida PSC decision on Tampa Electric’s large-load tariff application, expected in early 2027: approval unlocks up to 1,000 megawatts of data center demand with minimal incremental capital and would represent a material upside catalyst to rate base guidance. Second, Canadian Utilities merger regulatory approvals — specifically the Alberta Utilities Commission decision expected by end of April 2027 — are the most consequential regulatory events on the horizon; overall closing is targeted for Q3 or Q4 2027. Third, Nova Scotia Power regulatory resolution: performance-based regulation implementation is unresolved, and a CAD $1 billion securitization application — with expected customer savings of approximately CAD $265 million over 30 years — awaits board approval; adverse outcomes on either front would pressure consolidated guidance.

The 50% increase in annual equity funding requirements is the primary financial headwind. This elevated issuance creates per-share dilution that must be more than offset by rate base growth and cost recovery to sustain the above-5-7% EPS growth commitment through the period. LTIP hedge timing swings will continue to inject quarterly volatility into reported EPS; operating cash flow trends and adjusted segment earnings are the more reliable indicators of business trajectory.

Linux 7.5 To Convey To User-Space Multi-Grain Timestamp Usage On Files

Written by 
Michael Larabel
 in 
Linux Storage
 on 9 November 2026 at 06:08 AM EST. 
3 Comments

Merged for Linux 6.13 back in 2024 was 
multigrain timestamp support
 for being able to switch between coarse and fine-grained change/modification time reporting on a per-file basis. This is useful if caring about accuracy more than the once-per-jiffy timestamps on files and was originally devised as a way of addressing NFS caching issues. A limitation of multigrain timestamps though has been the lack of  user-space to know whether a file's timestamps are multi-grain or not.
 
With Linux 7.5, via a new statx attribute there will finally be a way for user-space to know if a file's timestamps are multi-grain. Jeff Layton worked out this new 
STATX_ATTR_MULTIGRAIN_TIMESTAMP
 attribute for indicating if a file's timestamps are multigrain.
 
The patch
 is part of the VFS.git tree and thus expected to be submitted for the next merge window for Linux 7.5. A small but useful addition for those making use of multigrain timestamps or developing software and wanting to be aware of its usage or not.

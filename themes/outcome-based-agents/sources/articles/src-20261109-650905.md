Getting to Know Charlize Crosby: A Miss Black and Gold Contestant Spotlight&nbsp;&nbsp;WCUQuad.com

[page text]
Image Credit: Zeta Psi Alphas

When Charlize Crosby first introduced herself before the interview, she seemed a bit nervous, but appreciative of me taking the time to meet with her before practice and preparing the questions. However, as time went on and we started to get to know each other, I began to see her come out of her shell and become the inspirational and creative woman that I know the school will come to admire, as I have, after Miss Black and Gold.

Crosby is a third-year Public Health major from the city of Philadelphia. We spent some time talking about her background and discussed everything from where she went to high school, Lankenau High School, to the programs she helped found while interning at the Philadelphia School District. The details of events she hosted while still a teenager, creating opportunities that gave inner-city kids access to resources to explore different areas of STEM, showcased the commitment and appreciation she has for her community, which led to her wanting to participate in Miss Black and Gold in the first place.

When I asked her what inspired her to sign up for the pageant, she immediately mentioned the legacy of both the Zeta Psi Chapter of Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Inc. and the previous participants of Miss Black and Gold. More specifically, she spoke about the history of women of character who were active on campus, putting time into creating a community where people who look like her could feel safe.

Crosby mentioned several issues that she felt needed to be addressed in her community, such as gun violence and access to suitable education for children in the inner city. She says she makes it a point to dedicate her time, resources and effort to combating these issues head-on.

The time commitment for the pageant has not been easy. Between classes and other commitments, there were definitely challenges that presented themselves, and I found myself interested in who and what helped her find a way to balance everything.

Crosby made sure to emphasize, when I asked her what her support system looked like, that “God is who I have been leaning on first and foremost.”

Her relationship with God has been a strong source of strength for her since she first started attending West Chester University, and it certainly did not stop once practice for the pageant began.

She also wanted to show appreciation for her friends and family, who have always been an integral part of her life. Especially her mother, who she said has been one of her biggest cheerleaders and pillars of motivation throughout the years.

When it came to managing time, she placed a large focus on knowing her limits and making sure that things remained fun for her overall. Staying focused and on top of deadlines while also making sure things do not become overwhelming, allows her to maintain her motivation and joy throughout the entire process.

I was also told that previous Miss Black and Gold participants have been an enormous support system for her as well, and she made a point to shout out 2022 participant Kalia Summers.

As the interview with Crosby started to come to a close, I asked her what she looked forward to doing after the pageant,
 
“I just look forward to getting some more sleep honestly,” before mentioning catching up on her favorite show right now, and listening to some Beyoncé, who is her favorite musical artist.

Overall, in my short time with Crosby, I got to know a young woman with immense talent and a great amount of insight regarding our community and the world we live in. I can’t wait to see her and all the other contestants showcase this to the West Chester community on Nov. 20 at 7 p.m. at the Emilie K. Asplundh Concert Hall.

Small cars, big possibilities: Harper students help children gain independence

Small cars, big possibilities: Harper students help children gain independence

Harper College News Bureau

November 9, 2026

November 9, 2026

Harper College students recently put their skills to work to help young children with
                              physical disabilities explore their world more independently during the college’s
                              first GoBabyGo event. Part of a national initiative that adapts ride-on toy cars into
                              customized mobility devices, the project brought together 30 students from Harper’s
                              
Physical Therapist Assistant
 and 
Engineering
 programs.

Working alongside faculty, clinicians and community volunteers, students participated
                              in different stages of the project, from building and modifying the cars to completing
                              postural assessments and making individualized adaptations for the children on the
                              day of the event.

The team built five cars, and four children received their own adapted cars with features
                              such as supportive seating and accessible controls. Families received guidance on
                              using the cars, and children had an opportunity to practice driving in a supervised
                              setting.

The initiative received support through a Resource for Excellence (RFE) Grant from
                              the 
Harper College Educational Foundation
. The grant program funds innovative pilot projects that advance Harper’s strategic
                              plan.

Megan Dillon, associate professor and academic coordinator of clinical education in
                              Harper’s Physical Therapist Assistant Program, led the initiative in collaboration
                              with Associate Professor Chris Athanassopoulos from Engineering and Professor Rita
                              Gura from the Physical Therapist Assistant Program. The Illinois Spina Bifida Association
                              and regional GoBabyGo collaborators partnered with Harper on the project.

For students, the experience connected classroom lessons in accessibility, problem-solving
                              and patient care with the needs of children and their families.

“It was a cool experience working with multiple people to achieve the same goal! A
                              lot of teamwork was had, and seeing the kids smile was awesome,” one student wrote
                              in a post-event reflection.

For the children, the customized cars offered new opportunities for independent movement,
                              play and connection with others.

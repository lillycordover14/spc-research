The New York Jets lost their fourth consecutive game on Sunday, 35-12 to the Kansas City Chiefs.

Jets head coach Aaron Glenn has looked out of his depth in his two seasons with the franchise
 and has become an easy punching bag across NFL media for his 
head-scratching game-management decisions
 and his insistence on focusing on what he believes is going well after losing brutally 
during media availability.

ESPN’s Mike Greenberg, arguably the most prominent Jets fan in sports media, declared in October that Glenn has been an 
“absolute disgrace.”
 After Sunday’s thrashing 
by the Chiefs, 
The Ringer
 founder Bill Simmons took it
 a step further and asserted that the former Jets defensive back has been one of the “worst NFL coaches of the century.”

“I can’t believe how awful he is,” Simmons said on Sunday’s episode of his eponymous podcast. “Bob from Pearl River wrote in, ‘Aaron Glenn now has a worse winning percentage than Hue Jackson. Hue Jackson was 11-44-1, and Aaron Glenn is 5-21,’ and wants to know, ‘Is that two of the four in the Mount Rushmore since we’ve been doing it?’

“It certainly is,” Simmons added. “I went all time. Burt Bell was 10-46 for a .179 winning percentage. Hugh was second-worst all time, 11-44 with a .205. But, you know, this is only 2 seasons. Aaron Glenn’s not getting a 3rd one. Yeah. If you go 2-season guys, Brian Callahan, 4-19. Marty Morningwig and Chris Palmer, 5-27 each. And somebody named Rod Dowhower in the ’80s, 5-24. So Glenn is one of the five worst coaches who have won at least five games.

“He is one of the worst NFL coaches of this century.”

The Sports Guy went on to list Steve Spagnuolo (11-40 career record as a head coach), Gus Bradley (11-41), and David Shula (19-52) as the other coaches in Glenn’s tier, before joking that Glenn may be the best asset the Jets have for his ability to guarantee the franchise will have one of the first picks in the 2027 NFL draft.

Frequent guest of the show Sal Iacono joked that Glenn might ask the Jets’ brass for another season at the helm to see if he could break the record for the lowest winning percentage.

“Do you think if he asked nicely enough, Woody Johnson, the management,  or whoever’s in charge, ‘hey, I don’t deserve it, but can you give me another year? I really want to break this record.  Bill broke it out… We’re not going to win next year anyway. So let’s just come on. Let’s have some fun,” Iacono proposed.

“Hue Jackson, 11-and-44. I think I can beat it,” Simmons said, laughing. “I think I have 10-and-45.”

As much as Glenn may have tried to
 
push back on the New York media’s negativity
 when he first took the role, a 5-21 record has made him an easy target nationally. Even if he has finally
 figured out the basics of clock management
, unless New York has a miraculous turnaround down the stretch, it’s hard to imagine he’ll get a chance to prove he can be even worse than Hue Jackson.

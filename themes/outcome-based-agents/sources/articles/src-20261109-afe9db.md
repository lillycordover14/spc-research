MOORHEAD — In the five years since 
    
      
Moorhead barred the sale of flavored tobacco and nicotine products,

    
       vaping among high schoolers in Clay County has dropped significantly, according to state data.
The ban went into effect Jan. 1, 2022. Since Moorhead approved its ban in October 2021, use of e-cigarettes, also known as vapes, among high schoolers in Clay County dropped from 24% to 8% over the last five years, said Jason McCoy, a tobacco prevention coordinator with Clay County Public Health, referring to 2025 Minnesota State Survey results. The survey asked middle and high school students about their behaviors.

McCoy said the ban, plus other ordinances and education, has helped with the decrease.
“What we led with in Moorhead was the idea that Moorhead needs to protect Moorhead’s kids,” he said. 

Jason McCoy, a tobacco prevention advocate and the positive community norms coordinator for Clay County Public Health speaks during a public hearing at the Clay County Commission meeting on Tuesday, Dec. 17, 2024, where banning flavored tobacco was discussed. 
Forum file photo

E-cigarettes and pouches, both of which contain nicotine without tobacco, are more popular among youth than traditional cigarettes and chewing tobacco, according to national studies.
Other counties and cities are following Moorhead’s lead. The Polk County Commission in northwest Minnesota voted Tuesday, Nov. 3, to update its decades-old tobacco ordinance, which included a flavored tobacco ban. The ban will take effect on Jan. 1 but will not include tobacco, menthol, mint and wintergreen flavors.

Clay County’s ban,

    
       with the exception of menthol cigarettes and flavored chewing tobacco, went into effect Jan. 1, 2025.
Ten counties and more than two dozen cities ban or restrict flavored tobacco and nicotine products in Minnesota, covering more than 30% of the state's population.

Fargo Cass Public Health

    
       proposed a similar ban in Fargo, but the City Commission delayed a vote on a proposal in March. Amid a changing national landscape, which includes federal authorization of more nicotine pouch and vape flavors, Public Health paused its push for a ban and is working on other prevention efforts, including education, said Fargo Cass Public Health Promotion Director Abby Lange.

ADVERTISEMENT

Industry stakeholders have pushed back on such bans, suggesting smokers will buy their products elsewhere.
A ban in Fargo and West Fargo would force smoke shops to close their stores and move to another city, said Zach Johnson, owner of Sports Vape in Fargo. That could result in lost tax revenue for those communities, he said.
“We definitely see a lot more customers from Minnesota,” he said. “They don’t have any place to buy it, so they come across the river or they go online.”

Zach Johnson, owner of Sports Vape, looks around his shop on Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, in Fargo. 
Lexi Hills / The Forum

The Minnesota Wholesale Marketers Association reached out to The Forum ahead of Polk County’s vote, saying it easily purchased vapes online with few guardrails. The group that represents wholesalers in the state purchased 42 vapes from 14 stores, said Association Executive Director Thomas Briant.
Despite a federal law that requires age verification when customers buy tobacco and nicotine products online, eight of the 14 websites did not require identification, Briant said. Only five asked for the consumer's age when entering the website, and two required a signature upon delivery, he said.
“What that means is this: Anyone of any age can go on these sites and purchase electronic cigarettes because these sites are not checking age," he said. "That means it's a source for underage youth to obtain these products unlawfully through these online sites.”
Smoke shops have a system that prevents sale of tobacco and nicotine products to minors, Johnson said. Those protections, such as not allowing anyone under the age of 21 in stores, don’t exist online, he said.

ADVERTISEMENT

Online stores can’t see the face of the customer behind the screen, and they don’t play by the rules, Johnson said.
“Buying nicotine online is the Wild West,” he said. “Age verification is almost entirely nonexistent.”
McCoy pushed back against claims that students would go online if a ban was implemented. In citing the Minnesota State Survey, he said the percentage of students buying vapes online is less than 10%.
“If you think of 10% of 8%, that’s incredibly low,” McCoy said. “Most kids aren’t going online.”

Flavored vape products are displayed Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, at Sports Vape in Fargo. 
Lexi Hills / The Forum

'Already on a downward trajectory'

The National Youth Tobacco Survey from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration shows high schoolers' use of any tobacco and nicotine products dropped from 11% in 2022 to 7.2% in 2025. Youth who said they smoked mostly used e-cigarettes, with 9% in 2022 dropping to 5.2% last year.
Making something harder to obtain reduces uses, said Pat McKone, senior director of public policy and advocacy for the American Lung Association in Minnesota and North Dakota. Increasing the cost of cigarettes helped smokers quit, including those who previously struggled, she said.
“When you make it harder to get or more expensive to get, that helps support that desire to be off of these addictive products,” McKone said.

ADVERTISEMENT

Saying students will go online to buy vapes is “not a good argument” against bans, McKone said. Studies suggest children get products at vape shops or from family and friends at a much higher rate than websites, she said.
Johnson and Briant noted the drop in vaping among teens nationwide without a ban on flavored products. In North Dakota, which doesn’t have a ban, vape use among high schoolers declined from 22% in 2015 to less than 15% in 2025, according to the state’s Youth Risk Behavior Survey. In Minnesota, vaping dropped from 25% in 2019 to 8% in 2025, according to the state Department of Health.
“We're already on a downward trajectory,” Johnson said.

Zach Johnson, owner of Sports Vape, grabs a vape product to sell on Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, in Fargo. 
Lexi Hills / The Forum

More North Dakota teens used smokeless tobacco products, Lange noted. The state survey showed a jump from 3% in 2023 to 7% in 2025.
That is likely because of the popularity of nicotine pouches, which replace chewing tobacco, Lange said. State surveys haven’t asked about nicotine pouch use, but people on the ground have raised concerns about the products, McKone said.
Teenagers get their products through family members or friends, Briant said. Now they have an online source with limited or no restrictions, he said.
“That’s what we have to stop,” he said.

ADVERTISEMENT

Braint suggested states and local governments should pass their own electronic verification laws, as well as educate people to not give tobacco products to minors.
“People need to be aware of this and not act as a social source for underage youth,” he said.

Modernizing law

McCoy, the Clay County health official, acknowledged few teens are using vapes, which is part of the education component. Students who believe everyone is using vapes are more likely to smoke to fit in, he said.
“When they start realizing that 92% of Clay County students aren't vaping, then they realize, ‘Hey, nine out of 10 of my classmates are available for friendships without peer pressure,’ ” he said. “That becomes a really cool thing.”
The Clay County and Moorhead bans are part of a larger effort to promote best health outcomes, McCoy said. That included banning vapes indoors, much like smoking tobacco products, he said.
The next logical step is looking at what draws youth to tobacco products, which is flavors, McCoy said.

Vape products sit on shelves available for purchase on Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, at Sports Vape in Fargo. 
Lexi Hills / The Forum

“Flavors draw a person in long enough for the nicotine to create the addiction,” he said. “Then the tobacco company has them.”

ADVERTISEMENT

Polk County’s ordinance hadn’t been updated since 1998 and predated the creation of vapes and nicotine pouches. Approving the updated ordinance will bring Polk County into compliance with Minnesota and federal law, including setting the age limit for purchasing tobacco and nicotine products at 21 years old, Polk County Public Health Director Sarah Reese told The Forum.
Modernizing the county ordinance will ensure newer products, particularly those that are more addictive, are held to the same health standards as traditional tobacco products, she said.
“Certainly our health policy shouldn't live in the past while the industry continues to operate in the future,” she said.
Johnson cited studies that suggest vapes help cigarette users quit, noting vapes and nicotine pouches are healthier alternatives to tobacco products. Health officials shouldn’t lump vapes with traditional tobacco cigarettes, he said.
He called for an increase in compliance checks, increasing penalties and working with schools to educate children.
“We can work together with the city rather than fighting the city to solve this problem,” he said.

Local smoke shop owners want to be good stewards in preventing underage smoking, Johnson said.
“Our next goal is helping people who are smoking cigarettes get off the cigarettes and to a product that has been proven to be a healthier alternative, not the healthiest by any means,” he said. “If everyone was quitting smoking and vaping, it'd be a good day.”
Companies like Philip Morris International, the tobacco company behind Marlboro cigarettes, Veev e-cigarettes and Zyn nicotine pouches, are promoting vapes and pouches as harm reduction, a strategy used to prevent drug abuse, McKone said.
Harm reduction involves treatment and other strategies to get people off drugs and save lives, while Philip Morris’ goal is to switch to another product or dual-use for profit, she said.
“I don't know another harm reduction model that's about profit,” she said.
McCoy said he is aware that people go to Fargo to buy flavored products, but numbers suggest fewer children are smoking to the east of the Red River than in North Dakota, he noted.
“I think it is imperative that Fargo considers ending the sale of flavored tobacco because of the kids,” he said.

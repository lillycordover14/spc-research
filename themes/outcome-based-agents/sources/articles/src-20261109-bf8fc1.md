EvenUp Names Former Vanta CRO Stevie Case President of Field Operations as Contracted Revenue Tops $150 Million&nbsp;&nbsp;citybiz

[page text]
Stevie Case

EvenUp
 has appointed former Vanta Chief Revenue Officer 
Stevie Case
 as its first president of field operations as the legal technology company surpasses $150 million in contracted recurring revenue, doubling its revenue over the past year.

Case will report to EvenUp co-founder and CEO 
Rami Karabibar
 and oversee sales, customer success, marketing, revenue operations and business operations. Her responsibilities will include expanding enterprise relationships, developing partner channels and scaling the company’s commercial infrastructure.

The appointment comes as EvenUp, which develops AI software for personal injury law firms, reports growing adoption of its technology across litigation and pre-litigation workflows.

According to the company, thousands of personal injury firms use its platform, including 30% of the 100 largest firms in the United States. EvenUp supports approximately 20,000 cases weekly and has worked on claims involving more than $20 billion in alleged damages.

The company estimates that its technology currently reaches about 5% of U.S. personal injury cases annually.

Case joins from compliance software provider Vanta, where she became the company’s first chief revenue officer in 2022.

During her tenure, Vanta’s annual recurring revenue increased tenfold to more than $300 million, while its go-to-market organization expanded from approximately 20 employees to more than 800. She also led the company’s expansion into enterprise and international markets.

Earlier in her career, Case held leadership positions at Twilio and Visa. She is also a founding partner of 20SALES, a seed-stage venture capital fund focused on technology companies.

“Our next challenge is building the organization to deliver on that promise for more firms,” Karabibar said. “Stevie has done that at an enormous scale, and she’s the operating partner we need for this next chapter.”

EvenUp’s growth has been supported by expanded adoption of its Pre-Litigation as a Service (PLAAS) offering, introduced in May 2026.

The service combines AI technology with case operations support, helping law firms manage medical records, treatment histories, demand preparation and negotiations.

More than a dozen firms have implemented PLAAS, and over one-third of its customers have entered multimillion-dollar agreements, according to EvenUp.

The company also disclosed a $16 million annual customer agreement, its largest to date, representing more than $150,000 in annual spending per attorney.

EvenUp said the contract reflects demand for specialized AI systems that support broader legal workflows rather than individual software tasks.

The company also highlighted adoption of its Companion AI platform in litigation. In one case, California personal injury firm Russell & Lazarus used the technology to analyze trial records, prepare jury selection questions and review testimony during proceedings that ultimately resulted in a $19 million jury verdict.

The company cited the case as an example of how its AI tools are expanding beyond document preparation into active litigation support.

“I’ve spent my career building revenue organizations from the ground up,” Case said. “Doubling revenue in a year while landing a $16 million customer for a brand-new product is rare.”

Case said her priorities will include building the commercial organization needed to support EvenUp’s growth while ensuring customers achieve measurable returns from its technology.

Backed by investors including Bessemer Venture Partners, B Capital, Lightspeed, Bain Capital Ventures and SignalFire, EvenUp develops AI-powered tools designed to automate legal workflows, analyze case information and support personal injury attorneys throughout the case lifecycle.

Forty Years of Fair Trade: Do Not Leave the Artisan Behind

2026-11-10 · By Dr K. Panchaksharam (Panchu), SIPA

Dr K. Panchaksharam of SIPA reflects on forty years of fair trade and calls for artisan livelihoods to become part of climate action. He argues that climate-fair trade should combine fair prices, predictable orders, accessible finance, resilient workplaces and long-term business partnerships to protect craft communities, strengthen incomes and support a more circular economy.

Dear World Leaders,

In 1986, SIPA – Federation of South India Producer Associations was promoted with a conviction: people who create beauty with their hands deserve dignity, fair returns and a future. Forty years later, our work has enabled us connect more than 25,000 frontline craftswomen and men to skills, solidarity and markets. From village artisans, groups & cooperatives to international fair-trade networks, we have seen what happens when marginalised producers are treated not as beneficiaries, but as partners.

We helped open markets for South India’s handloom and crafts, supported producer organisations, and contributed to the early architecture of international fair trade. And, over the years 2the climate crisis threatens to unravel these hard-won gains. Heat exhausts home-based workers; unreliable rains disrupt fibres, dyes and production; and water insecurity strikes at the very heart of livelihoods. Those who have contributed least to this crisis are being asked to bear its high costs.

At COP31, I ask you to recognise livelihoods pf Artisan Community as part of climate action—not as an afterthought to industrial policy. Invest directly in climate-resilient craft clusters: safe, cooler workplaces, reliable water, efficient dyeing and drying, clean energy, and practical adaptation support. Make grants and affordable finance accessible to small producer groups and women-led enterprises, rather than trapping them in paperwork and debt.

And I ask the business leaders to move beyond occasional charity. Commit to fair prices, predictable orders, responsible purchasing and long-term partnerships. Support design innovation and international market access while protecting the knowledge, ownership and bargaining power of the makers themselves.

Craft is not merely heritage to be displayed behind glass. It is living, more than employment, cultural intelligence, legacy and an opportunity for a more circular economy. A young person should be able to choose weaving or craft with pride, not because every other door has closed.

After forty years, our message is unchanged, but the urgency is greater: fair trade must also become climate-fair trade. Measure your success not only in tonnes of carbon, but in the dignity, income and security of the hands that make our world.

With determination and hope,

Dr K. Panchaksharam (Panchu)

SIPA – Federation of South India Producer Associations

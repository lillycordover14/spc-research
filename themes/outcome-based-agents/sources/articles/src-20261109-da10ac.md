“Beach bunny” will soon have a new meaning in Miami after an iconic — and sometimes controversial — brand announced its plans for a members-only club in Miami Beach.

Playboy is under contract to purchase the boutique Freehand Miami hotel from European hostel giant Generator, executives announced Nov. 5. 

The $12.5 million deal will close Dec. 15. During a quarterly earnings call on Nov. 5, CEO Ben Kohn said Think Hospitality and Infinity Collective will work with the brand to develop the club, according to 
TradingView
.

“The new club will be a foundational piece of our hospitality strategy,” Kohn said.

Farewell, Freehand

The Freehand hotel is still taking bookings through November 2027, according to its website. Its representatives did not respond to New Times’ request for comment via email.

The property also houses two bars, the 
Broken Shaker
 and 
Ray’s Hometown Bar
, the latter of which debuted during 
Miami Art Week
 2024 and has housed several celebrity and brand collaborations, including one with 
Tommy Bahama
 earlier this year.

It’s unclear how new ownership will affect both entities.

Playboy’s move from Los Angeles to Miami

Playboy started its 
move from Los Angeles last summer
, when the brand inked a luxury penthouse office lease at The Rivani.

The “pleasure and leisure” brand also announced plans for the club last summer, but originally indicated that the venue would be located in the same building, according to 
CBS News
.

Executives started posting about the new office in August, featuring a sheet-metal version of Playboy’s famous bunny logo.

New club, new brand

This isn’t the first time Playboy has taken a swing at 
hotel ownership
 in Miami Beach. The brand’s founder, Hugh Hefner, founded the short-lived Playboy Plaza Hotel in 1972.

The gamble failed a second time in 2019, with a 
New York City
 members-only club (and 
public bar
) that lasted less than a year. Its four-tier structure included access to private rooms, concierge services, and members-only events in the city and around the globe. Memberships ranged from $5,000 to $1,000, 
Eater
 reported shortly after the closure.

Since then, the brand has expanded from publishing racy nude photos of models and celebrities to selling a wide range of sexual wellness products, apparel, cosmetics, fragrances and other items.

The magazine also recently restarted printing physical editions. 
Cara Delevingne
 posed for its summer cover, and its most recent edition features 
Charli XCX
.

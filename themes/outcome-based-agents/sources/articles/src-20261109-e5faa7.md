Rillet Expands into UK, Challenging Legacy ERP with AI-Native Accounting&nbsp;&nbsp;Korea IT Times

[page text]
Following a $100 million funding round, the startup targets European finance teams as AI agents move deeper into core accounting systems

 

Rillet’s founding UK team as the AI accounting software company expands into the British market. / Photo courtesy of Rillet

U.S.-based accounting software company Rillet announced its expansion into the United Kingdom on November 9, opening a London office as it seeks to compete with established enterprise resource planning (ERP) providers. The move follows a $100 million Series C funding round in August that valued the company at $1 billion, bringing its total capital raised to more than $200 million.

Rillet is entering a market where SAP, Oracle and NetSuite have long supplied the systems businesses use to manage financial transactions, reporting and accounting controls. Its approach places AI agents directly within the accounting platform, allowing finance teams to automate selected workflows while retaining oversight of financial records and approvals.

The expansion reflects an emerging competitive question in enterprise software: whether AI will primarily enhance existing ERP systems or accelerate the adoption of platforms designed around AI from the outset.

European Demand Drives Expansion

Rillet said it now serves nearly 1,000 customers across 75 countries, compared with more than 600 customers reported when it announced its Series C financing in August. Its European customers include Black Forest Labs, Temporal and Primer. The company also reported that international net new annual recurring revenue (ARR) more than tripled month over month.

The London office will support sales, implementation and customer services, with further recruitment planned. Rillet said British companies had adopted its platform before it established a local presence, creating demand for regional support.

“Within a year of launching in the US, we already had finance teams in the UK running on Rillet,” said Nicolas Kopp, the company's founder and CEO.

The company has established relationships with accounting and professional services firms, including EY and RSM, which could support its expansion into European enterprise markets.

AI Agents Move into the General Ledger

Rillet's technology combines a real-time general ledger, continuous accounting close capabilities and AI agents within a single platform. Rather than relying exclusively on separate automation tools, its architecture is designed to let AI agents work with financial records under the same accounting policies and controls used by human finance teams.

The distinction matters because the general ledger is central to financial reporting, reconciliation and audit processes. Integrating AI into that environment could reduce manual work and shorten reporting cycles, but it also increases the importance of transaction accuracy, access permissions and auditability.

Rillet says its platform maintains human approval authority and an audit trail of AI-assisted activity. The company has not disclosed independently verified figures demonstrating how much its technology reduces accounting costs or closing times across its customer base.

Dave Arkell, chief financial officer at Black Forest Labs, said his company selected Rillet because it wanted AI embedded in the architecture of its financial systems rather than added as a separate feature.

A New Competitive Test for Enterprise Software

Rillet's expansion comes as enterprise software providers face growing pressure to incorporate AI into financial operations. Established ERP vendors have extensive customer relationships, mature compliance capabilities and broad integration ecosystems. AI-native challengers, meanwhile, are seeking to compete through automation, faster implementation and more integrated workflows.

Replacing a core accounting system involves considerably more than introducing new software. Companies must migrate financial records, validate internal controls, integrate existing applications and ensure that reporting remains accurate throughout the transition.

These requirements may be particularly important in Europe, where businesses operate across different tax regimes, accounting practices and regulatory jurisdictions.

Rillet's relationships with accounting firms could help address some of those implementation challenges. However, its ability to compete with larger ERP providers will depend on demonstrated reliability, enterprise-scale deployment and the cost of maintaining its systems over time.

Implications for Korea's Enterprise Software Market

The development is relevant to South Korea's enterprise software industry, where companies such as Samsung SDS and Douzone Bizon provide business applications and digital transformation services.

As AI agents become more closely integrated with accounting and enterprise data, competition could increasingly depend on the ability to connect financial records, business workflows and automated decision-making within secure, auditable systems.

For Korean software providers, the opportunity extends beyond adding AI features to existing applications. The longer-term challenge is to demonstrate measurable improvements in financial operations while maintaining the controls required by corporate customers.

Rillet's European expansion offers an early indication of how AI-native ERP vendors are seeking to compete internationally. Whether they can displace established systems will ultimately depend on verified operational performance, customer retention and the ability to meet enterprise compliance requirements across markets.

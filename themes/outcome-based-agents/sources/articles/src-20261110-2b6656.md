Beyond the Bylines: ‘Kyle Dubas is at the mercy of the (Sidney) Crosby situation’&nbsp;&nbsp;TribLIVE.com

[page text]
Penguins GM Kyle Dubas speaks to with media Sept. 28 at UPMC Lemieux Sports Complex. (Chaz Palla | TribLive)

During this week’s “
Beyond the Bylines
,” Joe Starkey and I opened up our Pittsburgh Penguins segment this way:

Me: “What about the Penguinos?”

Joe: “What about them?”

Me: “Yeah. Exactly.”

Then Joe proceeded to rant for the next five minutes about how many bills he had to pay.

Which, let’s be honest, is a conversation less painful to endure than one about the local hockey team these days.

The Pens are now mired in last place of the Metropolitan Division with Columbus. Both teams have only 14 points, and the Pens have played 18 games while Columbus has played 15.

They have lost five of six. The special teams have been costly late in games lately. Their goals-against average (3.56) is 26th in the NHL.

But when one of our viewers asked specifically about president of hockey operations Kyle Dubas’ role in all of this, that crystallized the discussion.

“Kyle Dubas is at the mercy of the (Sidney) Crosby situation,” Starkey said. “Sid has given back tens of millions of dollars to this franchise by taking these unbelievable contracts for $8.7 million. There’s an implication that, unbelievably, his agent (Pat Brisson) put into (the) public last year, which was, ‘Sid deserves to be in the playoffs.’ The implication, if not the demand, is that all that money that he’s giving up is to be used to field something of a contender around him.”

Based on the decline of the veterans around Crosby, his own slow start, and the stagnation of those few developing assets within the organization, that ain’t happenin’ in 2026-27.

“I don’t know what you do with this conundrum because (Crosby) is the show right now,” Starkey continued. “He’s the franchise. He saved the franchise. But there’s a conversation to be had here, like, ‘We can’t be doing this. We’ve got to get rid of guys. We got to collect assets. We need a clearer direction. And it clearly needs to be youth — and lots of it.”

The problem is, those guys to “get rid of” (Erik Karlsson, Bryan Rust, Rickard Rakell, etc.) would all be dealt on a significantly low ebb of their career arcs, and the return would be minimal around Crosby if he is retained.

So how would the roster look for 2027-28 then? If Crosby actually signs a contract to stay here in the first place.

That’s a different topic for next week’s show — as will be the Steelers-Bengals game, which Joe and I also previewed 
during this week’s video stream
.

Plus, we examined the NFL trade deadline, the state of the AFC playoff picture, NBA nostalgia, the start of college basketball season and more.

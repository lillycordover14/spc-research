AI Agents Move Into Slack And Teams As Vendors Introduce Per-Resolution Pricing&nbsp;&nbsp;CIO Africa

[page text]
For most employees, the first AI agent they encounter at work may not be a new application. It may be a response in the workplace chat platform they already use.

Zendesk began rolling out its employee-service AI agents on November 3, bringing automated support into its help centre, Slack and Microsoft Teams. The agents connect to more than 20 knowledge sources, including SharePoint, Google Drive, Confluence, Notion and Box.

They respect existing permissions on company content. An agent can answer an employee’s question, escalate it to a human or trigger a workflow when a request involves multiple systems.

advertisement

The pricing model is particularly notable. Enterprise and Enterprise Plus plans include 15 automated resolutions per employee-service agent each month. Every additional resolution costs $0.99.

Zendesk is therefore charging for completed work rather than seats or tokens. The model also gives enterprises a direct way to link AI spending to the number of requests resolved.

Two days later, Box announced Box Architect, a tool for building, managing and tuning AI agent workflows across enterprise content and business processes.

advertisement

The product is expected to enter beta for Enterprise Advanced customers this quarter. Its central proposition is governance.

Box Architect includes Model Context Protocol (MCP) guardrails that restrict the tools agents can use, access policies based on content classification, alerts when predefined thresholds are crossed and human review at critical stages of workflows.

Box CEO Aaron Levie said the real value of AI lies in “rethinking how entire business processes get done.”

advertisement

The enterprise AI agent takes shape

Read together, the announcements illustrate two sides of the enterprise AI agent market.

Zendesk is placing agents inside the platforms employees already use to request support. Its pricing model puts a measurable cost on each automated resolution.

Box is addressing the governance challenge, giving organisations controls over the information agents can access, the tools they can use and when human intervention is required.

For CIOs, the distinction raises two separate questions: which employee or business process an agent serves, and what it is authorised to do.

The commercial and governance questions are becoming increasingly connected. As organisations deploy agents across internal systems, they must understand both the cost of automated work and the risks associated with granting software access to corporate information.

Several questions remain unanswered.

Zendesk has not disclosed how many requests the average employee-service agent handles each month, making it difficult to estimate costs beyond the 15 included resolutions. The $0.99 overage rate could become significant in organisations with high support volumes.

Box Architect is still awaiting its beta release, and Box has not announced a general availability date.

Zendesk’s announcement describes how its agents respect existing permissions, but that capability has yet to be independently assessed in real-world enterprise deployments.

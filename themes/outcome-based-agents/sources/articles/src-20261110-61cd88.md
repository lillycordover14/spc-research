Positive Points
 
 
 
Q3 revenue grew 8% year-over-year to $124.4 million, at the top end of guidance, with adjusted EBITDA margin expanding 9 percentage points to 17%.
 
 
 
Quarterly paid employers grew 10% year-over-year and 5% sequentially to 74,000, the highest since Q3 2023, with strength across new, returning, and retained employers.
 
 
 
Product improvements drove record marketplace engagement: job seekers applying to at least one job grew 10% year-over-year, average applications per job seeker rose 24%, and qualified applications surged 49% quarter-over-quarter.
 
 
 
AI platform traffic nearly doubled year-over-year following direct integrations with ChatGPT and Claude, providing a high-converting new channel for job seekers.
 
 
 
Performance-based revenue from larger employers grew 13% year-over-year and 10% sequentially, with enterprise adoption of automated campaign solutions up 23% year-over-year.

Negative Points
 
 
 
Revenue per paid employer declined 2% year-over-year to $1,683 due to timing effects from strong growth in new and returning employers.
 
 
 
Q4 revenue guidance of $118-122 million implies a sequential decline from Q3, reflecting a typical seasonal hiring slowdown.
 
 
 
Full-year revenue growth outlook of 4-5% remains modest, constrained by a low-hire, low-fire labor market.
 
 
 
Sales and marketing expenses remain high at 48% of revenue, limiting margin expansion despite improved efficiency.
 
 
 
Growth was uneven across verticals, with healthcare, retail, and education showing flatter performance compared to technology, transportation, and construction.
 
 
 
 
Q & A Highlights
 
 
Q
: Can you walk through the marketing efficiency that drove strong paid employer growth, and did momentum build throughout Q3? 
A
: CEO Ian Siegel credited marketplace product improvements, which make new employers more likely to convert, spend longer and post more jobs, plus marketing refinements such as a new "ZipRecruiters" commercial with a jingle that boosted brand recall. CFO Carmen Chan added that all of Q3 performed well, momentum built throughout the quarter, and quarter-to-date performance remains pleasing.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: What are you seeing in conversion and mix from AI platform traffic, and how might commercial arrangements with LLMs evolve as agentic search scales? 
A
: CEO Ian Siegel said traffic from AI platforms has nearly doubled since direct integrations with ChatGPT and Claude launched, and it is a high-converting source of engaged job seekers. He said ZipRecruiter is working closely with platforms on agentic job-search experiences, comparing the shift to the desktop-to-mobile transition, and views it as an important growth opportunity.
 
 
Q
: How confident are you that ZipRecruiter is capturing its fair share of LLM traffic as search behavior migrates? 
A
: CEO Ian Siegel said he is highly confident, noting ZipRecruiter was there from day one, built custom integrations, continues improving them, and acts as a thought partner with the platforms on the next wave of agentic capabilities.
 
 
Q
: Qualified applications appear to have roughly doubled over the last couple of quarters. How is that benefiting the platform, and is it translating into more hires? 
A
: CEO Ian Siegel said job seekers applying to at least one job rose 10% year-over-year, applications per job seeker rose 24%, and qualified applications rose 49% quarter-over-quarter after 34% in Q2. He said employer response rate per application tripled year-over-year, validating the algorithm, driven by the rebuilt AI-powered search and matching engine. President David Travers added that performance-based revenue grew 13% year-over-year, the fifth straight quarter of growth, as sophisticated enterprise customers quickly measure the impact.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: What are you seeing with Google organic and paid traffic amid the rise of AI overviews, and is there more product work ahead with OpenAI and Anthropic? 
A
: CEO Ian Siegel said it is early days with AI integrations and more product work lies ahead. Despite Google's increasing use of AI overviews in 2026, ZipRecruiter grew traffic and job seekers applying to at least one job rose 10% year-over-year in Q3, helped by brand strength, multichannel marketing and AI platforms nearly doubling their contribution. CFO Carmen Chan noted sales and marketing spend fell 1% year-over-year while quarterly paid employers rose 10%.
 
 
Q
: Where are you in adoption of automated campaign solutions, and how much room is left to grow? 
A
: President David Travers said ZipRecruiter is well into the adoption curve, with the majority of enterprise customers now using the automated campaign performance tool, and he does not think the benefits from better campaign performance are fully realized yet. Combined with improved job seeker engagement and matching quality, he expressed ongoing confidence in growing performance-based revenue.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: Which verticals are showing strength, and which are lagging? 
A
: President David Travers cited clear strength in technology, transportation and logistics, and construction over the past quarter, while healthcare, retail and education were flatter. CEO Ian Siegel emphasized that growth is broad-based across companies of all sizes and industries, and that ZipRecruiter is performing well versus the overall labor market.
 
 
Q
: Did the SMB side of the business accelerate in Q3, and was that market-related or driven by your initiatives? 
A
: CEO Ian Siegel attributed SMB strength to multiple quarters of product improvements compounding, especially for time-constrained small business owners. He highlighted Be Seen First, where applicants who write a message are 2.5 times more likely to hear back, and a new follow-up messaging feature that drove over 4.5 times more message exchanges, helping employers focus on the most qualified and interested candidates.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: What drove the Q3 revenue growth and margin expansion, and what is the outlook? 
A
: CFO Carmen Chan said Q3 revenue of $124.4 million grew 8% year-over-year, at the top end of guidance, with adjusted EBITDA of $20.8 million at a 17% margin, up 9 percentage points year-over-year. She guided Q4 revenue of $118 million to $122 million, or 6% to 9% growth, implying full-year revenue of $468 million to $472 million, or 4% to 5% growth, and full-year adjusted EBITDA of $61 million to $65 million, a 13% to 14% margin versus 9% in 2025.
 
 
Q
: How should investors think about capital allocation and the financial model under the new CFO? 
A
: CFO Carmen Chan said the model starts with the conversation strategy, with roughly 90% gross margins and operating leverage across R&D and G&A, while sales and marketing is where deliberate ROI-based choices are made. She said the company plans to prioritize high-return investments, maintain a strong balance sheet and return capital opportunistically, after repurchasing $4.7 million of senior notes at a discount and $4.5 million of common stock in Q3, ending with $174.7 million in cash and securities.
 
 
 
 
 
Q
: What drove the 10% year-over-year growth in quarterly paid employers to 74,000, the highest since Q3 2023? 
A
: CFO Carmen Chan said strength was broad-based, with growth in new and returning employers plus stronger retention of existing employers as product improvements resonated across sizes and industries. She noted revenue per paid employer of $1,683 was down 2% year-over-year due to the timing effect of new employers contributing for only part of the quarter, but rose 1% sequentially on a higher mix of performance-based revenue, which was 25% of total revenue.

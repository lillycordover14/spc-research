Maxx Crosby makes unsettling admission about violent 49ers-Raiders fan brawls&nbsp;&nbsp;New York Post

[page text]
Multiple videos of 
violent brawls between San Francisco 49ers and Las Vegas Raiders fans
 have gone viral on social media in the wake of Sunday’s game, which the 49ers won 38-17.

Thus far, there have been 
11 arrests and 45 people were ejected
 from the game. But Santa Clara Police are still investigating other potential crimes.

During Tuesday’s episode of his 
“No Rush” podcast
, Raiders star edge rusher Maxx Crosby 
spoke about these violent brawls
.

“I think everyone has seen the videos going viral. We don’t have to comment too much on that, but the amount of brawls in the parking lot, the passion is real, to say the least,” Crosby said with a laugh.

“I was not surprised at all when I saw those videos going viral. I’m like, ‘Oh my god,'” Crosby continued. 

“But that’s how it was in Oakland. That’s a real war.”

Crosby’s co-host and close friend, Brogan Roback, jokingly speculated that’s why the NFL decided to move the Raiders out of Oakland, to which Crosby said, “Yeah. S––t goes left real fast, to say the least.”

3

This clip focuses on the brutal repeated shots that were landed on the unconscious men. (X/@AllenSales).

Crosby briefly laughing when talking about these brawls doesn’t 
suggest that he found the violence funny
. He instead seemed to be intrigued by how far these “passionate” fans will go to show their allegiance, even if it ends with someone being knocked unconscious.

The unfortunate reality is that brawls like these will likely continue as long as Las Vegas and San Francisco keep playing each other. 

The 
history of violence between 49ers and Raiders fans
 is nothing new. Eight years ago, 32 people were arrested, mostly for public intoxication and assault. Seven years prior to that, a man was found beaten unconscious in the bathroom, while two others suffered gun shot wounds in the parking lot.

The rivals for decades were situated across the bay from one another. They first squared off the year of the AFL-NFL merger in 1970, but have only played 15 times in the 56 years since, and never in the playoffs. But the hatred among fan bases clearly runs deep.

The Raiders eventually left Oakland following the 2019 season for Las Vegas.

Democratic veterans notch wins in local races amid midterms sweep&nbsp;&nbsp;New York Daily News

[page text]
Cait Conley and Rebecca Bennett. (Getty Images)		

By 
Dave Goldiner
 | 
dgoldiner@nydailynews.com
 | New York Daily News
PUBLISHED: 
November 10, 2026 at 2:12 PM EST
 
Democratic veterans 
notched big wins over Republican incumbents
 in closely watched local congressional races amid a massive nationwide blue wave in the midterms.

As the nation marks Veterans Day on Wednesday, Rep.-elect Cait Conley, a 16-year Army veteran, was 
celebrating her win over GOP Rep. Mike Lawler
 in the northern NYC suburbs while ex-Navy pilot Rep.-elect 
Rebecca Bennett beat Rep. Tom Kean Jr.
 by a landslide in the NJ-07 district.

Democrat Chris Gallant, a former military pilot, 
fell short in a tight race
 against Rep. Nick LaLota on Long Island’s East End.

All three candidates highlighted their records of military service and mostly moderate political stances.

That mirrors a 
recipe for Democratic electoral success stories
 in recent years, including New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill and Rep. Pat Ryan (D-New York), who has won his Hudson Valley district by outsized margins in recent years.

Rep.-Elect Rebecca Bennett.  (Aaron Schwartz/Getty Images)

Democratic power brokers and pundits believe that 
military veterans tend to overperform expectations
, especially in purple states and swing congressional districts, because swing voters may see them as less partisan and more aligned with local values even if it means sometimes crossing the aisle to work with Republicans.

An estimated 80 military veterans from both major parties, including more than a dozen senators, are expected to serve in the upcoming Congress, which runs until the 2028 elections.

In the midterms, almost all Democratic candidates 
performed dramatically better than benchmarks
 in virtually all regions and across ideological spectrum, from progressives and Democratic Socialists of America members on the far left to Blue Dog moderates on the right.
 

The 
across-the-board sweep
 makes it tough to gauge whether the local candidates’ record of military service gave them any meaningful boost, or if other factors were primarily responsible for shaping the results.

Rep.-elect Cait Conley. (Seth Wenig/AP)

In NY-17, Lawler mostly lived up to his reputation as a dogged campaigner and 
kept the fight with Conley close
. With almost all votes counted, Conley led by about 6%, or a fairly modest swing of about 12% from Lawler’s 2024 reelection win.

In the New Jersey race, Bennett racked up a 
16% blowout over Kean
, a massive shift of 21% from his 2024 victory. While Bennett boasted of her military service and status as a mother of two, the most dramatic issue in the race was Kean’s headline-grabbing months-long disappearance from public view that he only later attributed to treatment for depression.

Gallant was considered a long shot candidate by most analysts because LaLota has won his previous two races with ease. The challenger lost some momentum when stories 
revealed his past as a gay bondage model
. Still, Gallant came closer than expected, losing by just 3%, in a sign that his overall political profile was attractive to suburban swing voters.

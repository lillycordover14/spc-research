MOORHEAD, Minn. — Clay County election officials are preparing for a potential recount in a closely contested Minnesota Senate race, with plans to manually review approximately 27,000 ballots during Thanksgiving week.
During an update to the Clay County Commission, election official Lori Johnson outlined the procedures, security measures and staffing arrangements that would be involved in the recount, which would cover portions of Clay and Becker counties.
Although county officials are anticipating a recount request, Johnson explained that the process cannot officially begin until the Minnesota Secretary of State’s Office completes its statewide canvass on November 19.
“Officially, we know that there’s going to be a recount request, but can’t officially request it until after the 19th,” she told commissioners.
Recount expected during Thanksgiving week
Clay County officials are planning to conduct the recount at the county government center during Thanksgiving week, although the precise date remains uncertain.
Johnson initially scheduled the recount for Monday, November 23, but said communications from the Secretary of State’s Office indicated it could instead take place Tuesday, November 24.
Becker County officials were also planning their recount for Tuesday.
“I got an email from the Secretary of State’s office saying that it’s going to be Tuesday. So I have to call them and clarify that,” Johnson said.
She explained that Monday would be preferable because of staffing limitations later in the week.
The process is expected to take at least one full day, although officials acknowledged it could extend into a second day.
“I’d like to get it completed in a day. But it might take a day and a half,” Johnson said.
Approximately 27,000 ballots to be counted by hand
The recount would involve approximately 27,000 ballots from Clay County, covering nearly the entire county except several smaller northern townships outside the district.
Election officials plan to establish four counting tables inside a training room at the government center.
Each table would have three workers responsible for reviewing and counting ballots.
Johnson explained that election workers would open sealed ballot containers, separate ballots into piles according to candidate selections and organize them into stacks of 25 for counting.
The process would be conducted entirely by hand rather than through voting machines.
“These are hand counted,” Johnson told commissioners.
Representatives from each candidate’s campaign would also be permitted to observe the process.
Johnson said she expects representatives from both candidates to be present at each counting table, similar to the procedures used during the recount involving former Minnesota U.S. Senate candidates Al Franken and Norm Coleman.
The counting workforce will include county employees, city staff members and election judges.
Challenged ballots subject to additional review
Commissioners also asked how election officials would handle disputed ballots.
Johnson explained that when a candidate representative challenges a ballot, election officials document the challenge, including who raised the objection and the reason.
The ballot is then identified and tracked separately.
County election officials make an initial determination regarding the ballot, but copies of challenged ballots are forwarded to the Secretary of State’s Office for further review.
Because the Senate district crosses county lines, the state will oversee the final canvassing process.
“The Secretary of State actually does the canvas on this,” she explained.
The state is scheduled to canvass election results November 19 and conduct another canvass following the recount.
Officials emphasize ballot security
Several commissioners questioned Johnson about the security of ballots awaiting a potential recount.
She said all ballots remain inside sealed transfer cases in a secure room at the Clay County Government Center.
Access to the room requires a key card and is limited to a small number of authorized individuals.
“They’re all sitting right outside our office in a secured room that has key card access. And only a few people have that access,” Johnson said.
She also confirmed that the county has already submitted its election results and official documentation to the state.
Election statistics were transmitted electronically, while signed election abstracts were sent overnight.
State will reimburse recount expenses
Commissioners also raised questions about who would pay for the additional election work.
Johnson confirmed the county would be reimbursed by the state for recount expenses.
The recount comes after several demanding weeks for election workers, with staffing concerns becoming more significant because of the Thanksgiving holiday.
“It’s been a tough few weeks, I’ll tell you,” Johnson said.

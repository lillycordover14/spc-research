Cisco Teams Up With Anthropic to Red-Team Enterprise AI Agents Using Claude Mythos
Add preferred source
At Cisco Live APJC in Melbourne, Cisco announced it will integrate Anthropic's Claude Mythos model into its AI Defense platform, helping enterprises run red-team tests on their own AI applications and agents, with access opening to select customers in the near term. Testing is restricted to customers' own authorized targets, must be initiated by named, authenticated operators, and must retain audit logs. Cisco also disclosed that Evaluation Tuner will launch this month, Splunk Tokenomics cost governance will enter private preview in December, and the capabilities will be listed on AWS Marketplace in early 2027, while updating Cisco IQ to connect with Splunk and ServiceNow. Cisco announced at its Cisco Live APJC conference in Melbourne that it will integrate Anthropic's Claude Mythos model into its AI Defense platform, enabling enterprises to run red-team tests against their own AI applications and AI agents to proactively uncover security vulnerabilities.

The capability will be made available to select enterprise customers in the near term, though Cisco did not specify which version of Mythos it will use. Anthropic has just released Claude Mythos 5.2, positioned for approved cybersecurity and life sciences research use cases. It runs on the same underlying model as the simultaneously released, fully open Claude Fable 5.2, but carries fewer restrictions and is available only to vetted institutions.

On execution, Cisco stressed that testing is confined to targets the customer owns and has authorized for assessment; every test must be initiated by a named, identity-verified operator, with audit logs kept throughout. The design is clearly intended to prevent red-team tools from being misused to attack third-party systems.

Cisco has recently been advancing its security strategy around the Cisco Security Cloud architecture, and this partnership is seen as an extension of that strategy. At the same event, Cisco also disclosed several product timelines:

Cisco also updated Cisco IQ, connecting infrastructure exposure data with Splunk's threat detection and ServiceNow's remediation workflows, allowing enterprises to move seamlessly from risk discovery to response.

The partnership is particularly significant for Anthropic. The company is preparing to launch its IPO in the week of November 16, aiming to raise up to $100 billion (about NT$3.3 trillion); if realized, it would set a record for the largest IPO in history. Although valuation expectations have eased in recent weeks from a peak of $2 trillion (about NT$65.7 trillion), the market still expects the listing valuation to exceed $1 trillion (about NT$32.8 trillion).

The new models also offer gains on performance and price. Anthropic said Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 deliver up to 30% faster compute on complex tasks such as coding, with costs reduced by 20%. Pricing is set at $8 per million input tokens (about NT$260) and $40 per million output tokens (about NT$1,300), lower than the previous version.

Notably, the initial versions of Fable and Mythos were pulled just days after launching in June, when the U.S. Commerce Department ordered a ban on foreign use citing national security concerns; the restrictions were lifted only after several weeks. That episode has made the rollout pace of the new versions a particular focus of attention.

From an industry perspective, as enterprises adopt AI agents at a faster clip, verifying that these agents cannot be compromised by prompt injection or privilege misuse has become a new consideration in security procurement. By embedding model capabilities directly into its defense platform, Cisco is effectively filling in the "attack yourself before going live" step for enterprises; for Anthropic, it adds backing from a major distribution channel ahead of its listing. Still, red-team testing tools are inherently dual-use, and whether authorization scope and audit mechanisms can be enforced will be a core consideration for enterprise customers evaluating the offering.

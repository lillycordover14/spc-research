On the Mountain

The aluminum ratchets gave solid, distinct clicks and cleared packed slush cleanly, an 8 that matched the Ride C-4 and Flux PR and trailed only the Re:Flex. When Ryan leaned his full 215 lb into the fiberglass-reinforced chassis during bench torsion checks, it refused to twist or creak.

Strap lengths, toe ramp extension and highback lean all moved with heavy-duty lever pins. The Indy went onto 4x4 and 2x4 boards cleanly, and setup took 7 minutes. That earned 8.5, tied with the Re:Flex, Union Flite Pro and K2 Sonic.

It took the abuse. The chassis and heel cup showed no stress whitening, and the ladder teeth barely wore even after the testers yanked them tight in cold mitts. Every locking pin held for the whole test, and no screw or bolt needed checking, which the Re:Flex, Ride C-2 and Ride C-4 couldn't say. Value scored 8.0.

Where It Asks Something of You

The footbed is a flat slab of dense foam with no canting. After four hours on firm morning snow, Ryan and Shaun felt mild fatigue on the outside of their knees, and comfort came out at 7.5, a full point under the Re:Flex and Salomon Rhythm. If your knees are the weak point, the canted K2 Sonic fits better.

The plastic forward-lean lever is the other soft spot. The testers felt it was vulnerable if stepped on in the T-bar queue, so watch your feet there. It didn't break in our test, but it's the part we'd protect.

Spec Sheet vs Our Test

GF ProFusion chassis.
 Rugged held and lightweight didn't quite: the maker describes a lightweight and rugged fiberglass framework, and the bench check settles rugged. At 860 g the Indy is 30 g heavier than the Re:Flex and 120 g heavier than the Flite Pro. Buy it for the toughness, not the grams.

Tool-free forward lean.
 No tools for the lean, no traditional forward-lean block: that's the maker's promise, and the Indy keeps it. Strap lengths and the toe ramp use the same lever pins, so a fit change on the hill needs no screwdriver.

CustomCaddi ankle strap.
 Its most padded and cushy ankle strap option is how the maker sells the CustomCaddi, with polyurethane laminate and ladder covers. The strap did its job: thick, multi-layered, no instep pressure points. But a cushy strap doesn't fix a flat footbed, and the testers tied their knee fatigue to the footbed. Padding up top and a flat slab below leaves the knees to pay.

Should You Buy the K2 Indy?

Take the Indy if you want a binding to learn on and still enjoy in your second season. It scored 80, tied for second, and the maker rates the flex 5 out of 10 and lists it for beginner and intermediate riders. Ryan and Shaun's verdict was that it suits ambitious beginners and intermediates who want enough backbone to support more aggressive riding as their skills advance. None of our four testers is a first-timer, so the beginner fit is our judgment from the tool-free adjustments and the thick strap.

Pass if your knees tire by afternoon. The K2 Sonic scored 2 points lower but has a canted footbed, and the Burton Freestyle Re:Flex scored 4 points higher with better cushioning, though its highback folded under heelside carves. Size Large is the maker's US 9 to 11, and we rode it in US 10.5 boots. For a second season and beyond, it's the budget binding we'd keep.

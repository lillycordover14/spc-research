We have been seeing Russia’s casualty numbers climb steadily to the point where 40,000 casualties a month is fast becoming an appalling baseline. No doubt Ukraine’s tactical innovations and growing drone superiority are front and center in bringing about this turn of events.

That is at the gee whizz tech level. But on a more prosaic everyday ordinary level, Ukraine has quietly but effectively incorporated into its battlefield defensive barriers, a new lethal evolution in the old WWII era concertina razor wire barrier.  Meet the 
Yegoza
(or Egoza) obstacles
. 

Ukraine is using the Yegoza obstacles, which are specialized spiral structures made of galvanized wire designed to entangle infantry and light vehicles. The Yegoza consists of a high-carbon steel core wire with razor tape crimped (pressed) tightly around it, 
making it nearly impossible to cut with hand tools.
 The reinforced barbed tape is almost impossible to cut with scissors or with pliers, requiring specialized tools which the average Russian soldier in a “meat assault” might not necessarily carry.  It is also quite durable with its zinc coating enabling it to withstand up to 25 years of outdoor use.

Ukraine has reportedly installed roughly 16,000 km of Yegoza barrier lines as of 2025 and has likely significantly superseded this number as new defensive fortifications come on line. 16,000 km is roughly 10 times the current length of the entire battle trace so this means Ukraine has created a pretty dense layer of Yegoza on the battle field. Furthermore, Ukraine being Ukraine, 
unmanned ground vehicles are now being used to deploy the dangerous coils remotely, thereby reducing the risk to Ukrainian combat engineers.

And the 
Yegoza is lethal
. According to Ukrainian military engineers, 
even a minor injury
 from these barriers can result in 
severe, long-lasting bleeding and requires extensive healing time
. Another attribute which enhances the lethality of Yegoza is 
its low-visibility
 and it is often deployed alongside anti-tank ditches and mines to slow enemy advances and prevent enemy troops from using ditches for shelter. You don’t see it until you are right up to it or already tangled up in it . Either of which is probably too late:

How military engineers are strengthening Ukraine’s defenses in the South

… servicemen are installing Yegoza barrier obstacles – spiral or flat structures made of reinforced barbed tape – as well as low-visibility obstacles (LVOs). The latter are designed to impede the advance of infantry and light armored vehicles. Combined with an anti-tank ditch, these obstacles can slow the enemy’s advance and buy time for Ukrainian forces to arrive. They are one element of the overall defense system, which also includes fortifications.

“Yegoza obstacles are specialized spiral structures made of galvanized wire. They must be handled very carefully, and personnel must wear gloves when working with them. Even a minor injury can take a very long time to heal. Here, we use Yegoza in combination with low-visibility obstacles (LVOs). For example, if a sabotage and reconnaissance group (SRG) approaches – and such groups usually operate at night – its members are bound to get caught in the trap. As you can see, these obstacles are almost invisible among the vegetation, even during the day. Once caught, they won’t be able to free themselves; the wire will have to be cut with wire cutters. Light vehicles will also be stopped, as the wire will become entangled in their wheels,”

Now imagine being an already hapless, harried, mal-nourished, under-equipped Russian 
Mobiki 
ordered to assault a Ukrainian defensive line fraught with Yegoza among other death/injury-dealing obstacles. Beyond the possibility that your commander may not have even seen the Yegoza given its low-visibility, the ever-present Ukrainian FPV drones might get you before you even get to the wire. And a First-Aid pack is probably not in your field kit. As is all too common now, your commander is unlikely to even think of getting help to stop you from bleeding out or evac’d off the field … thereby leaving you to your fate and the gods of the unfortunate “cargo 300″(wounded) or “cargo 200” (dead) of this hideous sanguinary war.

Ironically, the Yegoza/egoza, as a general concept, was initially developed by the Soviet Union. In the early 1980s, the 
15th Central Research and Testing Institute of the USSR Ministry of Defense
 conducted research on the topic, resulting in the development of steel-reinforced barbed barrier. They gave it the name 
egoza
(meaning a fidget or restless person in Russian). By 2008, Ukrainian innovators and manufacturers had mastered, improved the technology and patented the production of various forms of egoza, principally the version which came to be known as 
Kazachka
(Cossack Woman). 
Kazachka
, including other mobile military checkpoints, gabions and bastion made from welded mesh sections and connecting spirals are commercially manufactured in Ukraine and are used by the Armed Forces of Ukraine:

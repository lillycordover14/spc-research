            Zendesk unveils outcome AI platform with unlimited pricing
          

            Wed, 11th Nov 2026 (Yesterday)
          

                SOFIAH NICHOLE SALIVIO
              

News Editor

Zendesk has launched its Outcome AI Platform, including unlimited-seat pricing tied to service outcomes.

The platform brings together several products for customer service and internal support teams, including Open Service Directory, Zendesk Bridge, specialised AI agents and tools intended to improve workflows over time.

Zendesk said the move marks a departure from conventional seat-based pricing in service software. Under its Zendesk Unlimited plans, customers set an outcome budget and pay for eligible issues resolved and business work completed, rather than user licences.

The plans include access to Zendesk AI, with features varying by tier. Zendesk said it assesses each outcome for quality and completeness before counting it towards a customer's budget.

A global survey cited by Zendesk found that 52% of consumers are comfortable relying on personal AI assistants for everyday tasks, while 63% believe companies will need to redesign customer service to support that shift. The same survey found that 84% said human help should still always be available.

Tom Eggemeier, Chief Executive Officer of Zendesk, said the company is responding to a change in how people approach service interactions.

"People don't think about which channels, systems, or departments they need to navigate to interact with an organization. They simply want help to accomplish what they came to do," Eggemeier said.

"Outcome AI brings together the context and ability to act to help people reach the right outcome quickly, wherever their experience begins. It learns from each result and improves when it falls short. For organizations focused on loyalty and satisfaction, that's the standard," he said.

Pricing shift

Zendesk said the pricing model extends its earlier resolution-based approach. It argued that, as more service work is handled by AI, customers want spending to map more directly to completed work rather than software access.

According to Zendesk, customers using the platform can automate up to 84% of service requests, cut resolution times from hours to minutes and improve customer satisfaction scores. It did not provide independent figures in the announcement.

Industry analysts said pricing tied to outputs rather than seats reflects a broader discussion among software buyers about how to measure returns from AI spending.

"The evolution of Zendesk's outcome-based pricing model reflects a shift in how enterprise buyers want to pay for AI," said Tapan Patel, Research Director of Customer Data, Analytics and Decisioning at IDC.

"By tying AI investment directly to verified resolutions and business outcomes rather than traditional seat counts, Zendesk establishes a clear alignment between cost and realized value, providing a differentiator in the customer experience market," Patel said.

Verified channels

One part of the launch is Open Service Directory, which Zendesk described as an open specification and public directory for verified support channels. The tool is intended to help personal AI assistants identify official customer service routes for a brand, whether or not that business uses Zendesk.

Businesses can register their support channels and decide what information is shared, as well as when human review is required. Zendesk said the directory does not access private customer data or take service actions.

Early participants include Betterment, ElevenLabs, Flex, TeamSystem, TBA Auctions, Telekom Payment and Wireless Logic.

External tools

Zendesk Bridge is designed to let organisations connect external AI agents and interfaces to Zendesk's service platform. Administrators can control which tools are connected, what information they can access and which actions they can take, according to the company.

Zendesk said each action through Bridge is subject to identity checks, permissions, policy controls, human approvals, monitoring and audit records. It said this would allow businesses to use their preferred AI interfaces without replacing existing service workflows.

Improvement tools

Zendesk also outlined what it called Self-Improving Zendesk, which links analytics, knowledge, quality controls and workflow changes. The aim is to help teams identify recurring issues and reduce repeat contacts over time, according to the company.

Its specialised agents, including industry and custom versions, are intended to handle more complex customer and employee tasks using business rules and domain-specific logic. Related tools include Agentic Analytics, Admin Copilot and AI Agent Launchpad.

Zendesk said Open Service Directory is launching soon, Zendesk Bridge is in an Early Access Program, and the specialised agents and self-improvement functions are generally available.

Plurai: $13 Million Seed Funding Led By Team8 To Advance AI Agent Simulation And Reliability Platform

Plurai, a New York-based developer of simulation infrastructure for artificial intelligence agents, announced $13 million in seed funding and the general availability of its platform. The company aims to help enterprises identify and correct AI agent failures before deployment while improving reliability, accuracy, and operating costs through simulation and specialized small language models (SLMs).

The funding round was led by Team8, with participation from U&I, Velocity, and Mercer. More than 20 angel investors and industry executives also backed the company, including CyberArk founder Udi Mokady. Dr. Eyal Carmi, CTO of Microsoft 365 Copilot, has joined Plurai’s advisory board.

Plurai’s platform addresses a growing challenge for enterprises adopting AI agents: ensuring that autonomous systems perform reliably in production environments. Although building agents has become increasingly accessible, testing their behavior across complex, unpredictable scenarios remains difficult and expensive.

According to the company, its technology has helped design partners shorten agent development cycles from months to hours, reduce failures reaching real users by 82%, and lower the cost of operating reliable agents by 97%.

In joint work with NVIDIA and Nebius for a global enterprise customer, Plurai post-trained models that reportedly outperformed frontier models from OpenAI and Anthropic on accuracy while operating at approximately 40 times and 44 times lower costs, respectively.

Plurai’s technology creates a digital twin of an AI agent and its operating environment using an organization’s knowledge bases, tools, and policies. The platform then simulates thousands of realistic and adversarial user interactions within a sandbox to identify potential failures.

These simulations are designed to uncover hallucinations, policy violations, incorrect actions, failed tool calls, unsuccessful tasks, and sensitive data exposure before agents interact with actual customers.

The platform also uses simulation results to generate corrective actions and develop task-specific SLMs. These smaller models can replace more expensive frontier models for certain agent workloads while functioning as continuous evaluators and guardrails in production.

When new failures emerge, they are incorporated into subsequent simulations, creating a feedback loop through which agents can improve over time.

Previously available only to selected partners and customers, Plurai’s platform is now generally available to developers, including through a free tier. Developers can access its capabilities through tools such as Claude Code, Codex, and GitHub Copilot. Enterprises can also use the platform to simulate and improve agents developed on systems such as Salesforce Agentforce.

Plurai was founded by Dr. Ilan Kadar, CEO, and Dr. Elad Levi, CTO. Both executives previously worked at Nexar, where they helped develop scalable, reliable AI systems for autonomous driving applications. Kadar served as vice president of AI, while Levi worked as an AI architect.

Their experience in autonomous driving helped shape Plurai’s simulation-based approach to testing AI agents. The company’s technology also builds on original research, including BARRED, a method for generating synthetic training data for customized SLMs that was accepted at the International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML) 2026.

Additional research by the team has explored methods for serving hundreds of specialized SLMs simultaneously on a single GPU, potentially improving the economics of deploying these models at enterprise scale.

Plurai is already working with organizations in AI-native and highly regulated industries, including Fortune 500 companies and major financial institutions.

With its newly announced funding and broader platform availability, Plurai is positioning simulation and continuously trained, specialized models as foundational infrastructure for businesses deploying autonomous AI systems.

Today's mortgage rates put the 30-year fixed purchase rate at 6.98% on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, according to Zillow data reported by Yahoo Finance.
 That average is still 12 basis points below last week's reading, which is the weekly drop behind today's headline. The same report shows a 5-basis-point rise from Tuesday, the second straight daily increase, after a higher-than-expected October inflation report. Even with that daily move higher, purchase rates remain below last week's levels. Yahoo's Wednesday purchase table also lists the 15-year fixed, both common adjustable-rate loans, and VA options. For the current rates below, we use the report's purchase rate table.

If you are shopping for a home, treat a weekly decline as a reason to compare fresh quotes, not as a green light to stretch your budget. A useful rate is still one that works with the home price, the fees, and the amount you can comfortably pay each month. Keep your budget in view before you chase a larger loan.

The Weekly Drop Is Still the Bigger Story

The 30-year fixed purchase rate remains 12 basis points below last week's average, even after Wednesday's 5-basis-point move higher from Tuesday. The 15-year fixed purchase rate is listed at 6.41%, up 9 basis points from the prior day and unchanged from last week. The 5/1 ARM purchase rate is 6.74%, down 9 basis points in Wednesday's update and 6 basis points from last week. Those figures are national averages, not a forecast of the next move.

A basis point is a small unit used to describe changes in interest rates. One hundred basis points equal one percentage point. It helps keep the rate change separate from the change in your payment. A weekly decline can still lower borrowing costs on a new loan, but how much you feel it depends on the amount you borrow and the terms you choose.

Start With the Payment You Can Keep

A home search often begins with a price range. It helps to work backward from a monthly budget as well. Add the loan payment, property taxes, homeowners insurance, and any mortgage insurance or association dues. Then leave space for upkeep and savings.

The Wednesday averages help put those numbers in dollar terms. On a $300,000 loan at today's 6.98% 30-year rate, principal and interest run about $1,992 a month, with roughly $417,077 in interest over the full term. The same balance on a 15-year loan at 6.41% jumps the monthly payment to about $2,599, while cutting lifetime interest to about $167,730. Those illustrations are starting points only; your taxes, insurance, and lender fees still change the full bill.

The lender may approve a bigger loan than you want to carry. Decide what feels comfortable before you see a house that makes you want to stretch. The weekly drop can create a little room without requiring you to spend it all.

What a Fixed Rate Gives You

A fixed-rate mortgage keeps the interest rate unchanged through its term. That makes the principal-and-interest payment easier to plan around. The other parts of your housing bill can still rise, so fixed does not mean every cost stays the same.

The table lists the 30-year fixed at 6.98%. Its longer repayment period can make monthly payments more manageable than a shorter loan on the same balance. The tradeoff is more time spent paying interest. Ask your lender how extra principal payments work if you want the flexibility of a longer term while aiming to repay sooner.

Look Beyond the Lowest Fixed Rate

The 15-year fixed appears at 6.41%, and the 20-year fixed is 6.68%. Those choices can appeal to buyers who want to build equity faster and shorten their repayment timeline. Yet the lower rate on a shorter loan does not automatically mean a smaller monthly bill.

Get written payment estimates for each term using the same loan amount. See whether the shorter term still leaves an emergency cushion. If it forces you to pause retirement saving or rely on credit cards for repairs, a longer loan may be a better fit. The goal is a home you can keep, not just a loan you can pay off quickly on paper.

ARMs Need a Plan for Later

The current purchase table puts the 5/1 ARM at 6.74% and the 7/1 ARM at 6.60%. An ARM begins with a fixed rate period, then can adjust under the contract's rules. Its opening rate should never be treated as a promise for the whole loan.

Have the lender walk through the first adjustment, later adjustment dates, and the limits on rate changes. Ask to see a possible higher payment in writing. A plan to sell before the rate resets may sound simple, but moving dates and home values can change. Do not choose an ARM unless you understand what happens if you stay longer than expected.

VA Rates Belong in Eligible Borrowers' Comparisons

For eligible borrowers, the VA table shows 6.23% on the 30-year loan, 5.85% on the 15-year loan, and 6.21% on the 5/1 adjustable loan. These options deserve their own lender quotes rather than being folded into a general purchase estimate.

Ask about eligibility, any applicable funding fee, lender charges, and the cash needed at closing. Compare the full cost with other loans you qualify for. Be sure the quotes use matching loan amounts and lock periods. A rate can look attractive while the fee details tell a different story.

Locking Is About Managing Risk

You do not have to predict the bottom in mortgage rates to make a sound choice. If your closing is close and the payment fits, a rate lock may help protect your plans. Read the lock agreement, including its expiration date and any extension costs.

If you have more time, you may prefer to watch offers. Just decide in advance what you will do if rates move higher. Ask whether a float-down option exists, whether it costs extra, and what conditions apply. Waiting is a choice with risks, not a free chance at a better deal.

Read the paperwork:
 Check that the written terms match what you discussed.

Use Some Quiet Time to Get Your Papers Ready

If you are not ready to request a quote yet, use the time to organize what lenders may need. Gather recent income records, bank statements, and a clear picture of your debts. Ask the lender which documents apply to your situation, especially if you work for yourself or have income that changes from month to month.

Keep track of where your down payment money is coming from. If part of it will be a gift, ask what paperwork is required before moving funds. This preparation does not commit you to a purchase. It simply puts you in a better position to compare real offers when you find the right property.

The 12-basis-point weekly drop, with the 30-year fixed purchase rate now listed at 6.98%, gives buyers a clear reason to revisit financing with fresh numbers. The small rise from Tuesday does not erase that weekly improvement. National averages set the direction; your credit, down payment, and loan type still shape the offer you receive.

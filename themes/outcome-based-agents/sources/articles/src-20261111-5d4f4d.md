Immersive Audio: Building Flexible Immersive Home Audio Systems with UWB Spatial Sensing and Adaptive Rendering
 
 November 11 2026, 13:10
 
 
 
 
Immersive audio is no longer just about sound quality — it’s about intelligence, adaptability, and removing setup friction. By working closely with NXP Semiconductors, StreamUnlimited has created a platform that integrates precise ultra-wideband (UWB) ranging, offering audio OEMs a scalable solution for flexible remote speaker placement and user-friendly immersive home audio systems.
 
Immersive audio is no longer just about sound quality — it’s about intelligence, adaptability, and removing setup friction. By working closely with NXP Semiconductors, StreamUnlimited has created a platform that integrates precise ultra-wideband (UWB) ranging, offering audio OEMs a scalable solution for flexible remote speaker placement and user-friendly immersive home audio systems.

 


Consumer home audio systems are becoming increasingly complex and feature rich. Beyond access to local and streaming sources, users now expect advanced processing and multichannel architectures, capabilities previously limited to professional systems, to be integrated into everyday living-room products.


This trend is especially visible in soundbars, which are increasingly positioned as the primary audio system in the home. Support for immersive formats such as Dolby Atmos, DTS:X, 360 Reality Audio, and MPEG-H, together with higher channel counts and advanced post-processing, is becoming standard. Bundles of soundbars combined with satellite speakers for fully immersive experiences are currently adopted by only a niche segment of the market. While these technologies are evolving, adoption at scale is often limited by setup complexity and fixed speaker layout assumptions.


Flexible renderers such as Dolby Atmos FlexConnect address these challenges by adapting immersive rendering to arbitrary speaker layouts and dynamically adjusting to listener position.


Essentially, place your speakers anywhere, sit wherever you want, and let the systems tailor the best immersive audio experience for you.


StreamUnlimited worked alongside NXP Semiconductors to create a reference practice addressing the placement challenge. As a chip manufacturer, NXP can deliver radio frequency based sensors using ultra-wideband (UWB) technology. By combining UWB spatial sensing, adaptive immersive rendering, and modular embedded audio platforms, it enables manufacturers to develop new product categories and upsell modular concepts such as add-on satellite speakers, while maintaining high audio quality and system robustness.

Reference Implementation Overview

The reference system is centered around a soundbar platform based on the StreamUnlimited Stream1955 module, combined with the NXP Trimension SR250 UWB chip (Photo 1). Stream1955 is a high-performance, Linux-based embedded hardware module designed for networked audio streaming. It integrates powerful processing, versatile connectivity (Wi-Fi 6E, Ethernet, and Bluetooth), and advanced digital signal processing (DSP) to serve as the core of premium audio products. With support for multichannel I/O and 3D audio decoding, it enables immersive spatial rendering across both local and wireless outputs.

 

Photo 1: StreamUnlimited worked with NXP Semiconductors to create a reference implementation addressing the placement challenge.


Wireless speakers, each equipped with an NXP Trimension SR150 receiver, can be placed freely anywhere in the room, without requiring a fixed layout (Figure 1). The UWB module can act as a radar for detecting x,y user position, and a coordinator to detect x,y,z position of all speakers within the room. The angle of each speaker is also measured and accounted for in the calibration. This spatial data is then provided to the immersive renderer, which adapts the rendering parameters to the real speaker layout and listening position, enabling a consistent, optimized surround experience.

 

Figure 1: Dolby Atmos FlexConnect is a technology that allows users to place wireless speakers anywhere in their room, automatically optimizing the sound experience based on their location and seating arrangement, making it accessible and adaptable for various home environments. (Source: Dolby Atmos FlexConnect)

UWB Radar for Listener Detection

Due to their physical properties, UWB radio signals are extremely well-suited to detect environment changes and can then be used to create very accurate radars. UWB radar technology is increasingly adopted in automotive and infotainment systems. In home audio systems, it enables reliable listener position measurements that are critical for determining the sweet spot.


Listener localization is implemented using the Trimension SR250 UWB radar feature, which connects to the Stream1955 over SPI and can deliver either pre-processed position data or raw measurements for further processing. Alternatively, listener position can be derived from the listener’s UWB-capable smartphone using FiRa stack support.

Speaker Position Measurement

UWB positioning is well established in demanding environments such as industrial and automotive systems, where high spatial accuracy is required. In the reference design, a single Trimension SR250 acts as the coordinator while multiple Trimension SR150 devices are embedded into the speakers. Enabling precise spatial mapping of the entire audio system.


The soundbar collects distance, angle, and elevation data for each remote speaker, using defined Ranging sessions. In addition, “Ranging with In-band data transfer” allows each Trimension SR150 to determine its position relative to other sensors and forward this information directly over UWB back to the main device. This removes the need for an additional control or data channel, simplifying system design while improving robustness.


The resulting redundant data can then be processed into the main unit, further improving ranging accuracy and opening the door for non-line-of-sight (NLOS) use cases. If a speaker is not directly visible to the coordinator but is detected by other speakers, its position can still be resolved through cross-ranging.

Wireless Audio Distribution

Once the system is aware of the presence and position of speakers and users, respectively, to each other, the audio image will be computed and outputted on up to 12 channels from the main board.


Manufacturers can then decide which audio paths are already attached to the hardware, and which need to be sent out wirelessly to the targeted speaker. The StreamUnlimited Stream1955 module design is based on the NXP i.MX 8 processor and exposes Inter-IC Sound (I2S) outputs from the renderer.


Each channel could be connected to a digital-to-analog converter (DAC) for local output or to a wireless transmitter. Any reliable transceiver with a known fixed latency and sufficient audio performance could be used as long as it accepts I2S inputs. It is advised to use 24/32 bits at 48kHz configuration as it provides a good balance between reliability and audio quality preservation.


Bluetooth Low Energy (LE) Audio is supported by the Stream1955 module and can be used for selected channels. The industry is currently adopting LE Audio as the new standard for wireless audio transmission and support for more than two channels is on the roadmap of manufacturers and Bluetooth Core specifications.


However, Bluetooth distribution for high channel count is at an early stage because the higher channel bandwidth, as required for more than three wireless speakers, is not yet included in the Bluetooth Core specification. Additionally, Bluetooth interoperability with Wi-Fi and other 2.4GHz technologies is probably worse than for wireless transmission systems working in the 5GHz band. Current LE Audio implementations achieve a latency of around 30ms.


Wireless solutions based on WLAN or FSK transmission can achieve latencies of 20ms and meet Dolby key performance requirements. Such systems offer up to eight channels at 48kHz/24-bit, which can be transmitted wirelessly and is considered sufficient for a product that can produce left/right/center and some surround output locally.

One Speaker, Multiple Purposes

A modular immersive system becomes more compelling when satellite speakers are not single-purpose accessories. At one end, a speaker can be a minimal endpoint: an MCU controller for the speaker, Trimension SR150 for localization, the audio wireless chip if required, and potential audio peripherals (DSP, DACs, etc.).


At the other end, a speaker can be a standalone networked speaker that also participates as an immersive satellite when requested. Consider a typical family movie night. The parents bring their standalone speaker from the kitchen, while their daughter grabs her bedroom speaker. They carry both into the living room, placing one on a side table and the other in a corner, wherever there happens to be a power outlet and space. The host soundbar, through FlexConnect and UWB, instantly detects and maps the new devices. The system compensates for the asymmetrical, non-ideal placements. The result is an instantly expanded soundstage, utilizing the two additional discrete channels for a fully immersive acoustic experience. A representative building block is the StreamUnlimited Stream210 module, which provides a Linux platform with Wi-Fi 6E, Bluetooth (LE Audio), analog and I2S digital audio I/Os, sufficient for a self-contained streaming speaker design.


StreamUnlimited APIs could also be used to simplify configuration and interoperability across devices to enable versatile self-contained streaming speakers, as well as being part of a flexible immersive setup on-demand (Figure 2).

 

Figure 2: This system diagram details a configuration for a soundbar.

Conclusion

This joint StreamUnlimited–NXP reference implementation demonstrates how UWB spatial ranging and radar, adaptive immersive rendering, and modular embedded platforms can be combined into a practical and scalable home audio system design.


By removing fixed speaker layout assumptions and reducing setup complexity, the approach enables flexible product configurations and incremental expansion while preserving audio performance, providing a solid foundation for next-generation immersive soundbar and home audio products.

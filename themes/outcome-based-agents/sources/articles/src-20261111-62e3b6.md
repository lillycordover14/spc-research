Ashland Health Dept., EMA partner to strengthen emergency preparedness&nbsp;&nbsp;Ashland Source

[page text]
ASHLAND — Ashland County agencies are finding strength in working together.

Emergency preparations are essential when disaster strikes. That’s why the Ashland County Health Department and Emergency Management Agency are partnering to share staff resources and emergency preparedness.

The idea to trade services happened during a conversation where EMA staff was helping the health department program its MARCS radios. Staff members realized each department had resources that could help each other.

The health department can assist with processing checks and payments. In return, EMA staff can help the health department lead emergency preparedness exercises without the added expense for additional staffing. This helps health department staff focus on after-action reports after each exercise.

“I think this mutual relationship is what could really be helpful for all counties if they have a good relationship with their EMA,” said Health Commissioner Vickie Taylor.

 

 

The Ashland County Board of Health approved a memorandum of understanding with the county EMA on Tuesday night.

The health department participates in the Public Health Emergency Preparedness program, funded by an annual grant from the Ohio Department of Health. The program helps local agencies prepare for threats like bioterrorism, disease outbreaks or public health emergencies.

The grant requires the department to complete certain exercises, typically two each year. The exercises test staff and practice how to respond during an emergency.

One of the exercises tests the health department’s emergency communication system and how quickly employees respond. Other tests focus on how to handle a viral or bacterial public health threat.

Taylor said one statewide exercise involved a fictional anthrax exposure at a Jimmy Buffet concert.

The scenario involved about 1,500 residents attending the concert who could have been exposed. Staff walked through how to obtain medication, identify who needs treatment and how to distribute supplies to those affected.

“It’s more just a practice in case something happens that you don’t all sit there and go, ‘What do we do?'” Taylor said.

The Ashland County Emergency Management Agency works around the county with public safety officials, government offices and non-profit organizations for specific emergency-response training. 

 

 

Earlier this year, Ashland City Schools conducted a large-scale emergency drill, partnering with first responders to respond to a fictional active-shooter scenario.

The health department and EMA already include each other in training exercises, even when participation is not required. EMA has reviewed some of the health department’s emergency plans and the health department has also been invited to participate in EMA’s hazardous materials planning.

“They’re not bringing supplies, they’re bringing resources,” Board of Health member Doug Campbell said.

Even though these scenarios are fictional, it’s important to give staff a large-scale example before a real one occurs.

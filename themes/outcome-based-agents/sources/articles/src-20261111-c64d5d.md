Dialpad-Ada Deal Signals a Conversational AI Reset
Dialpad and Ada’s merger strengthens Dialpad’s agentic CX platform ambitions, but Craig Walker and Zeus Kerravala say it also points to a bigger shift: conversational AI is moving into the platforms that own customer context, communications, and outcomes.
Rob Wilkinson
Save
Follow
share
The Dialpad-Ada deal gives enterprise CX buyers another reason to question where customer service, sales engagement, communications, and AI-agent platforms now begin and end.

Dialpad and Ada announced they are joining forces to build what they call an “agentic operating system for customer work.” The combined company plans to bring voice, customer intelligence, and AI agents together so businesses can organize people, AI, and systems around each customer.

Enterprise communications analyst Dave Michels described the wider market moment as “The Big Blender,” pointing to Dialpad-Ada and Salesforce’s agreement to acquire Cresta as signs that enterprise communications, customer experience, and AI are converging.

For buyers, the issue is more practical than the label. If AI agents start moving work across service, sales, billing, and internal teams, the harder question becomes who owns the customer outcome.

Customers Do Not Experience Vendor Categories

Craig Walker, CEO and Co-Founder of Dialpad
, told CX Today that legacy software categories increasingly describe vendor packaging rather than customer reality.

“Those categories describe how vendors organize their products, not how customers experience a business. A customer doesn’t care whether their problem sits with the contact center, their account manager or billing. They want it handled, once.”

Walker argued that businesses have spent years buying separate systems for employees, service teams, sales reps, and now AI agents. Customers often end up connecting those pieces when their issue crosses a boundary inside the business.

AI can make that fragmentation more visible. Work may accelerate inside each system, while the handoff between systems remains slow, unclear, or repetitive for the customer.

Walker framed the Dialpad-Ada combination as a response to that gap. Dialpad brings communications inside and outside the business, its own voice network, and speech and language models. Ada brings AI agents designed to carry out more complex customer work.

The question, in Walker’s view, is shifting away from which software category an enterprise buys. Buyers need to decide what experience they want to deliver, and whether people and AI can work as one team to deliver it.

CX Today has recently reported that 
context is the difference between a handoff and a restart
, especially when customers move between automation and human support. Dialpad and Ada are now positioning that handoff problem as a platform opportunity.

Dialpad-Ada Deal Targets the Handoff Gap

The operational fit is clear to Zeus Kerravala, Founder and Principal Analyst at ZK Research, who told CX Today that the merger gives Dialpad a stronger agentic AI story while addressing a familiar customer service failure point.

“On paper, the technology fit makes sense: pairing Ada’s autonomous reasoning and digital self-service with Dialpad’s voice network, real-time speech AI, and UCaaS/CCaaS footprint directly addresses the messy handoff gap between AI agents and human reps.”

Dialpad’s announcement says it has analyzed more than 14 billion minutes of business conversations since introducing real-time AI in 2018. Ada, meanwhile, has deployed more than 500 AI agents across voice, messaging, email, and social channels in more than 130 languages.

The combined company says it will bring communications, customer intelligence, and AI execution together in one platform. AI agents will act through connected business systems, bring in people with the authority or judgment to help, and continue working alongside them after a conversation ends.

That creates a sharper enterprise CX proposition than chatbot containment alone. Dialpad and Ada are arguing for a model where the conversation becomes the starting point for business work, rather than an isolated interaction inside a contact center channel.

Walker used the example of a strong employee handling a difficult call. The person does not send the customer into another queue; they bring in the right colleague, make the introduction, and stay involved.

Dialpad and Ada want AI agents to support that same pattern. The customer should not have to repeat context or restart the journey because the business moved the issue from one system to another.

Conversational AI Moves Into Larger Platforms

Kerravala also sees a more sober message behind the merger. Dialpad gains capability and narrative strength, but Ada’s reported transaction value suggests that the standalone conversational AI market may be entering a reset.

Dialpad did not disclose financial terms. Public reporting has put the all-share transaction at around US$430 million, or roughly C$600 million, below Ada’s reported US$1.2 billion peak private valuation. Kerravala framed that as a warning sign for pure-play conversational AI vendors.

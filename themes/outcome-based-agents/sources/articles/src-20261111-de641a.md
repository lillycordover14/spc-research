Your 
October 27 report
, “Football star Kylian Mbappe faces huge China backlash over ‘unacceptable’ remark”, prompted me to revisit a long-standing concern about intercultural communication: rendering 伤害中国人民的感情 (shānghài Zhōngguó rénmín de gǎnqíng) as “hurts the feelings of the Chinese people” inadvertently diminishes the seriousness of the objection.
I served for more than two-and-a-half years as a Peace Corps volunteer in Guizhou, where I developed a deep appreciation for Chinese history, language and culture (and cultivated a deep love of Guiyang bean hotpot!). Readers may know the programme through Peter Hessler’s 
River Town
, which recounts his Peace Corps service in Fuling.
In everyday English, “hurt feelings” often suggests a personal slight. To anglophone ears, the phrase can carry associations of adolescent sensitivity and wounded pride. The formula has become a target of online ridicule, including during the 
2019 NBA dispute
, distracting audiences from the substance of the grievance.

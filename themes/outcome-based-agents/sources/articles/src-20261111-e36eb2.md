Lawrence County Emergency Management Agency (EMA) will be holding a Local Emergency Planning Committee (LEPC) meeting on Nov. 19 from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. at First Solar.
Scan the QR Code to register to attend. Seating is limited. Lunch will be provided. First Solar is located at 1st Solar Way in Lawrence County.
The Lawrence County EMA is working to reestablish the Lawrence County LEPC as part of an ongoing effort to strengthen hazardous materials planning, emergency preparedness, and coordination throughout the county.
The LEPC serves as an important partnership between emergency responders, local government, utilities, healthcare, industry and other community stakeholders. While hazardous materials preparedness remains the primary focus, EMA’s vision is to develop the LEPC into a broader resource for disaster preparedness, emergency planning and community resilience.
Lawrence County is home to many transportation routes, industrial operations and facilities that require coordinated emergency planning. By reestablishing the LEPC, EMA will provide an opportunity to bring these partners together, share information, identify potential hazards, and improve coordination before an emergency occurs.
EMA’s goal is to extend the benefits of these partnerships beyond hazardous materials incidents. During major disasters, severe weather and other significant emergency events, local industries may possess specialized capabilities, supplies, equipment, or other resources that could prove valuable to emergency response efforts.
By building these partnerships and relationships and identifying available resources before an incident occurs, the LEPC can improve communication between emergency responders and industries and potentially make additional resources available to support the community when they are most needed.
The upcoming meeting will bring together local stakeholders, assistance from the Lawrence County Industrial Development Board, and representation from the Alabama EMA. All of their involvement will help strengthen partnerships between local industry, county and state EMA, and other community stakeholders as they work to reestablish this necessary committee.
“Lawrence County EMA is encouraging participation from local industries, emergency services, healthcare providers, utilities, government agencies and other interested stakeholders,” said Lawrence County EMA Director Steven Carlton. “Our goal is to build a stronger, more connected emergency preparedness network that benefits Lawrence County not only during hazardous materials incidents, but also during severe weather and other emergencies that impact our community.”

ROCHESTER, Ill. — Refuge Ranch in Rochester is offering free adaptive riding lessons to children with disabilities. The program is just one part of the services provided by the non-profit organization. 
“What we do in Freedom Reins is we offer lessons for kids with disabilities,” said Jen McCormick, director of operations and adaptive riding director at Refuge Ranch. “And we teach them to ride a horse in a safe and fun way.”
Each student starts with three helpers. McCormick said one person leads the horse and two people walk alongside. As students become more comfortable, the number of helpers drops to two.
She said students tend to be nervous to ride the horses when they first start, but eventually grow to enjoy the experience. 
The program also focuses on building practical skills. McCormick said the skills taught at the ranch are meant to correlate with something in the real world.
For example, the students had to search for hidden stuffed animals around the arena, grab and hold them, and then throw them into a pool, all while staying on the horse.
“So if they’re looking or reaching for something, maybe at home, many of them are maybe low tone or high tone, we are practicing,” said McCormick. “So we’re asking them to stretch and to grip something, so that they can just focus more on the skills that they’re doing in occupational or physical therapy as well.” 
Another benefit of the program is increased communication skills and friendships with other participants and volunteers. McCormick and her team talk participants through the process, ensuring they feel comfortable and safe. They will even use sign language to provide instructions, if needed. 
“We’re focusing on their ability and not their disability,” McCormick said. “So whatever they can do, we’re going to help bring them up into that and just encourage them.”
Nineteen people currently participate in the program.
Refuge Ranch pays staff but runs solely on donations from the community, McCormick said. The ranch relies heavily on volunteers, and she said many of the people at the lesson were volunteers.
McCormick said she hopes to expand the adult riding program. She said it currently stops at age 21. She hopes to add a second evening of Freedom Reins for adults, but that would require more volunteers.

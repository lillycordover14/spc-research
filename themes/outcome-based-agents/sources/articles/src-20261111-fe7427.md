Apple Fitness+ Adds Adaptive Four-Week Strength Plans With Apple Watch Weight Tracking&nbsp;&nbsp;TodoMountainBike

[page text]
Apple is bringing a new level of personalization to strength training with Gym Strength, a feature that builds four-week workout programs and adjusts them based on how much weight users lift and how many repetitions they complete. Unlike traditional Fitness+ sessions built around following an instructor's video, the new experience lets users train at their own pace, follow a structured progression, and log their performance directly from an Apple Watch. The feature is currently available in beta as part of Apple's upcoming software updates.

Athlete lifting dumbbells at the gym while wearing an Apple Watch. Image: TodoMountainBike
Four-week strength programs that adapt to your progress
The biggest change introduced by 
Apple Fitness+ Gym Strength
 is the shift from standalone workout videos to progressive training programs tailored to individual needs. Users select their strength goals, experience level, preferred workout days, target muscle groups, and available equipment. Fitness+ then generates a four-week plan with recommended exercises, sets, repetitions, and weights.

Rather than repeating the same routine throughout the month, the system uses recorded performance to adjust subsequent workouts. The number of repetitions completed and the weight lifted during one week help determine the recommendations for the next. This 
adaptive strength training program
 is designed to make progressive overload easier to manage without manually planning every session, although its automated recommendations cannot fully account for individual recovery, fatigue, or exercise technique.

Users can choose between shorter sessions featuring five exercises, which Apple estimates will take approximately 20 to 30 minutes, and longer workouts with seven exercises, lasting around 45 to 60 minutes. The exercise library includes more than 350 movements across over 30 types of strength training equipment, including dumbbells, kettlebells, barbells, cable machines, and suspension trainers. A set of dumbbells is the minimum equipment needed to get started.

The system also accounts for the realities of training in a busy gym. If a machine is occupied, users can select alternative exercises targeting similar muscle groups, switch equipment, add sets, or skip movements. Short demonstration videos from Fitness+ trainers explain each exercise, with three viewing angles available to help users understand the movement. The flexibility also makes the feature suitable for home workouts with limited equipment.

Apple Watch adds weight and rep tracking directly from the wrist
One of the most practical additions is 
Apple Watch strength workout tracking
, which allows users to view their scheduled exercises and record completed repetitions and lifting weights without repeatedly reaching for their iPhone. The watch also monitors familiar workout metrics, including heart rate and estimated calories burned.

An Apple Watch is not required to use Gym Strength. The training programs run on compatible iPhones and iPads, while the smartwatch provides a more convenient way to interact with them during a session. Importantly, recording repetitions and weights requires user input rather than relying on automatic detection of every movement or load.

Apple is also introducing Training Note, an optional Apple Intelligence-powered feature that provides information about upcoming workouts, explains how a program is progressing, and highlights milestones. It requires an Apple Intelligence-compatible iPhone or iPad configured in English, adding another layer of guidance for users interested in understanding their training progression.

Why Gym Strength could be useful for cyclists
For mountain bikers, gravel riders, and road cyclists, the appeal of 
strength training with Apple Fitness+
 extends beyond general fitness. Resistance training can complement cycling by developing muscular strength and supporting a more comprehensive conditioning program. Having a structured gym routine that fits around riding days could make it easier to maintain strength work throughout the season.

Gym Strength lets cyclists select how many days they want to lift, how long each session should last, and which muscle groups they want to prioritize. That flexibility may be useful for riders balancing interval sessions, long endurance rides, and recovery days. However, there is no confirmed integration that automatically adjusts lifting recommendations using cycling power data, weekly mileage, or accumulated fatigue from rides. Cyclists following structured training plans will still need to coordinate their gym sessions with their on-bike workload.

Beta availability and Apple Fitness+ pricing in the United States
Apple Fitness+ Gym Strength is currently in beta
 through iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, and watchOS 27.3, with the first developer beta versions released on November 3, 2026. The feature requires an iPhone running iOS 27.3 or an iPad running iPadOS 27.3, while Apple Watch integration requires watchOS 27.3. A final public release date has not yet been confirmed, so the new training experience should not be considered generally available to all subscribers.

Gym Strength is included in the existing Apple Fitness+ subscription without an announced additional charge. In the United States, Fitness+ costs $9.99 per month or $79.99 per year and can also be accessed through eligible Apple One subscriptions. The service currently offers workouts across 12 activity categories, including Cycling, Strength, HIIT, Pilates, and Yoga. Gym Strength expands that offering with a more individualized approach to resistance training.

Zendesk Launches Outcome AI Platform and Zendesk Unlimited
 

 

Enterprise Software & Digital Workflows
 

 

Analyst(s): Keith Kirkpatrick

Publication Date: November 12, 2026

Zendesk has launched its Outcome AI Platform and Zendesk Unlimited, removing seat counts for new capabilities and having customers fund an Outcome Budget that pays only for verified resolutions and completed work. The move pushes outcome-based pricing from an AI add-on to the core commercial model, and puts pressure on competitors still anchored to per-user licensing.

What Is Covered in This Article:

Zendesk unveiled its Outcome AI Platform and Zendesk Unlimited at its AI Summit, dropping seat-based consumption for new capabilities in favor of unlimited seats and an Outcome Budget.

Customers pay for eligible issues resolved and business work completed, with Zendesk evaluating each outcome for quality and completeness.

New capabilities include Zendesk Bridge (EAP), Specialized Agents and Self-Improving Zendesk (GA), Agentic Analytics, Admin Copilot, AI Agent Launchpad, and the Open Service Directory (launching soon).

The move extends the resolution-based pricing Zendesk introduced in 2024 from an AI add-on to the platform’s core commercial model.

Futurum data shows outcome-based pricing has reached rough parity with per-user pricing in enterprise software, but verification and budget predictability remain the deciding factors for buyers.

The News:
 On November 10, 2026, at its AI Summit, Zendesk announced the Outcome AI Platform and Zendesk Unlimited, a pricing offering that moves the company “away from traditional seat-based software consumption models.” Zendesk Unlimited gives customers unlimited seats for the new capabilities. Instead of buying licenses, customers set an Outcome Budget and pay for “eligible issues resolved and business work completed.” Zendesk says it evaluates each outcome for quality and completeness so customers can see what counts against the budget.

According to the accompanying product blog, spend is tied to two outcome types. Conversational Outcomes apply when an issue is successfully addressed within a customer interaction. Operational AI Outcomes apply when an automated workflow is completed across business systems. Zendesk describes Zendesk Unlimited as the “next evolution” of the resolution-based pricing model it introduced in 2024.

The platform also includes:

Zendesk Bridge (EAP):
 a governed layer that lets businesses connect any AI interface to Zendesk’s AI, service context, workflows, and actions, with identity, permissions, policy, human approvals, observability, and audit applied to every action.

Specialized Agents (GA):
 Industry and Custom Agents that resolve complex customer and employee tasks using domain logic.

Self-Improving Zendesk (GA):
 connects insights and actions across AI agents, human workflows, knowledge, and quality, supported by Agentic Analytics, Admin Copilot, AI Agent Launchpad, and Quality Score.

Open Service Directory (launching soon):
 an open specification and public directory that helps personal AI assistants find a brand’s verified support channel, whether or not the brand uses Zendesk. Early participants include Betterment, ElevenLabs, Flex, TeamSystem, TBA Auctions, Telekom Payment S.A., and Wireless Logic.

Zendesk did not disclose per-outcome rates, Outcome Budget minimums, tier names, or availability dates for Zendesk Unlimited. See the

Zendesk Launches Outcome AI Platform and Zendesk Unlimited

Analyst Take:
 Zendesk’s Outcome AI Platform takes outcome-based pricing out of the AI add-on column and makes it the main commercial model. With Zendesk Unlimited, the seat stops being the unit of value. The Outcome Budget, spent on verified resolutions and completed workflows, takes its place. That is a bigger step than the 2024 announcement, and it is the clearest sign yet that outcome-based pricing in customer service is moving from experiment to default.

Zendesk has been heading here for two years. When I covered 
Zendesk’s original outcome-based pricing model for AI agents
 in 2024, I called the move bold, and I flagged the open questions: customer education, internal financial benchmarks, and unclear transition paths. At Relate 2026, I noted that 
Zendesk was betting on autonomous AI agents and outcome pricing to upend service models
, and that the bet could backfire if agents could not deliver verifiable, auditable outcomes at scale. Zendesk Unlimited is Zendesk doubling down on that bet.

The Market Is Ready for Outcome-Based Pricing, Mostly

Buyers are moving in Zendesk’s direction, but more slowly than the headlines suggest. In Futurum’s 1H 2026 Enterprise Software Decision Maker Survey (fielded January 2026, n=830), 18.7% of respondents who named pricing model as a selection criterion said they currently use outcome-based pricing for core software, up from 15% in 2H 2024. For future core software purchases, 21.7% preferred outcome-based pricing, reaching parity with per-user, per-month pricing (20.1%) for the first time. Pricing also matters more in vendor selection: 52.2% of respondents cited pricing model as one of the criteria they used to select their current software vendors.

For AI specifically, the preference data is more mixed. When generative AI is sold as an add-on, 31% of respondents preferred outcome-based pricing in Futurum’s 1H 2025 Enterprise Software & Digital Workflows Decision Maker Survey (fielded June 2025, n=865), behind consumption at 38%. By January 2026, outcome-based preference for GenAI add-ons had slipped to 26.8%, while consumption rose to 42.9% and per-user, per-month pricing held at 18.9%. As I argued earlier this year in 
Are Outcome-Based and Hybrid AI Pricing Models Rewriting the Vendor Playbook?
, vendors that offer only seat-based pricing risk being disqualified from AI-heavy deals.

The most recent data carries a warning for Zendesk. In Futurum’s 2H 2026 Enterprise Applications Decision Maker Survey (fielded through July 2026, n=833), buyers whose generative AI comes as a paid add-on (n=645) split 42.3% for per-user pricing, 36.6% for consumption, and 21.1% for outcome-based. As I argued in 
The Pricing Split: Why Core Software and AI Can’t Share One Commercial Model
, buyers want predictable, budgetable pricing for AI even as they move core software toward consumption and outcomes. Only 11.2% of those buyers say their preferred vendor offers outcome-based pricing today, so Zendesk is filling a real gap, but it is betting its new AI capabilities on the model that roughly one in five AI add-on buyers prefer. The next Decision Maker survey that will be published in January will provide additional insight into buyers’ preferences around outcome-based pricing.

“Unlimited Seats” Is Less Generous Than It Sounds

Unlimited seats is a strong headline, but the seat was already becoming the least valuable thing Zendesk sells. If AI agents automate a growing share of service requests (Zendesk claims up to 84%), customers will need fewer human agents, and seat revenue will shrink anyway. Offering unlimited seats lets Zendesk get ahead of that erosion and move its revenue onto the meter that grows as AI does more of the work.

That is a smart move for Zendesk, and it can be a good deal for customers. But buyers should see it for what it is: a shift in where the spend sits, not a discount. The budgeting question moves from “how many agents do we license?” to “how many outcomes will we consume, and at what rate?” Zendesk has not published per-outcome rates or explained what happens when an Outcome Budget runs out. Until it does, CFOs cannot easily model total cost.

Who Decides What Counts as an Outcome?

The hardest question is verification. Zendesk says it evaluates each outcome for quality and completeness. That is the right commitment, but it also means the vendor is grading its own homework. The definition of a Conversational Outcome, an issue “successfully addressed within a customer interaction,” leaves room for disagreement. Was an issue resolved if the customer came back two days later through another channel?

The tools Zendesk is shipping alongside the pricing, including Quality Score, Agentic Analytics, and the audit trail in Zendesk Bridge, are the pieces that make outcome-based pricing auditable. Enterprises should insist on contractual definitions of an eligible outcome, a dispute process, and access to the underlying data. Zendesk is not alone here. Salesforce made a similar bet when it 
launched Agentforce Help Agent with pay-per-resolution pricing
, and the same trust question applies to every vendor charging this way.

Bridge and the Open Service Directory Matter More Than They Look

The less-discussed pieces of the announcement may prove more durable than the pricing. Zendesk Bridge positions Zendesk as the governed system of action behind whatever AI interface a business or its customers use. The Open Service Directory goes further, helping personal AI assistants route to a brand’s verified support channel even if that brand does not use Zendesk.

Together, they signal that Zendesk expects a growing share of service requests to arrive from AI assistants rather than from people on a website. In that world, the vendor that owns verified routing, context, and governed action earns the outcome, regardless of which front end started the conversation. That is a stronger long-term moat than any pricing model, and it makes outcome-based pricing easier to defend because Zendesk sits where the work is completed and measured.

What This Means for Buyers and Competitors

For buyers, Zendesk Unlimited should lower the barrier to rolling out AI across more teams, because adding users no longer adds license cost. The tradeoff is less spend predictability until outcome volumes stabilize. Start with a constrained Outcome Budget, measure resolution quality against your own definitions, and expand only when the numbers hold.

For competitors, the pressure is direct. Vendors still pricing AI service capabilities per seat will face harder questions in competitive deals, especially when Zendesk can say a customer pays nothing for a user who never generates an outcome. Expect hybrid responses, such as consumption credits, flat-rate work units, or outcome tiers, rather than a wholesale abandonment of seats. As I noted in my analysis of 
Pega’s flat-rate AI pricing model
, vendors are converging on the idea of charging for completed work, but they disagree on how to measure it.

What to Watch:

Whether Zendesk publishes per-outcome rates, Outcome Budget minimums, and overage terms. Without them, enterprise procurement teams will struggle to compare Zendesk Unlimited with seat-based or consumption-based alternatives.

How Zendesk defines and defends an “eligible” outcome in practice, and whether Quality Score and Bridge audit data give customers enough visibility to dispute outcomes they believe were not resolved.

Competitive responses from Salesforce, ServiceNow, Microsoft, Intercom, and Decagon. Expect hybrid models that blend outcome components with consumption credits rather than a full move away from seats.

Whether large enterprises standardize on outcome contracts, which would force a wider pricing reset across CX and enterprise service by 2027.

Adoption of the Open Service Directory beyond its early participants. Its value depends on AI assistant providers and non-Zendesk brands using it.

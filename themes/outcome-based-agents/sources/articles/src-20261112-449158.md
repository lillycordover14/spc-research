Existing home sales fall to 3.75 million pace in October&nbsp;&nbsp;HousingWire

[page text]
The annual pace of existing home sales continues to decline compared to a year ago. In October, existing home sales slowed to an annual pace of 3.75 million, according to data published on Thursday by the 
National Association of Realtors 
(NAR). 

This represents a 5.5% decline from
 a month ago
 and an 8.8% drop from October 2025. 

“Home buying is difficult when 
mortgage rates
 have climbed to their highest levels since 2000, aside from a single month in 2023. As a result, home sales fell to their lowest level since August 2010,” Lawrence Yun,” NAR’s chief economist, said in a statement. 

HousingWire Data
 shows similar trends, noting that for the week ending November 6, 2026, an estimated 74,473 single family homes were sold, down 4.5% compared to a year ago. In addition, the median sales price declined 3.2% to $400,000, while active inventory rose 7.2% from a year ago to 842,242 single family homes.

Regionally, 
existing home sales
 fell in all-four regions compared to a year prior. The Northeast, which reported an annual sales rate of 470,000 existing homes, recorded the smallest decline at 4.1%, while the West (650,000 existing homes) reported the largest at 14.5%. On a month-over-month basis, the sales pace remained unchanged in the Northeast, but fell in the West (-9.7%), South (-6.5%) and the Midwest (-3.2%). The South reported an annual sales pace of 1.73 million homes in October, while the Midwest recorded a pace of 900,000 existing homes. 

At the end of the month, there were 1.59 million existing homes for sale, up 1.3% from a month prior and 4.6% from a year ago. This
 inventory
 represents 5.1 months’ supply at the current sales pace.

Despite the slower market conditions, the median sales price continued to rise, jumping 0.4% year-over-year in October to $416,700. This marks the 40th consecutive month of year-over-year price increases, according to NAR.  

More days on market, fewer first-time buyers

Reflecting the slower pace of home sales, the median time on market for properties rose to 35 days, up from 33 days a month ago and 34 days a year ago, according to NAR’s Realtors Confidence Index. 

NAR also reported that just 29% of sales were first-time buyers, down from 31% a month ago and 32% a year ago. Additionally, as mortgage rates remained at an elevated level in October, the share of all cash sales rose to 29%, compared to 27% last month. However, this is on par with a year ago. 

NAR’s 
housing affordability
 index also fell, dropping from 107.3 a year ago to 100.7 in October 2026, with the index posting annual declines in all four major regions. The Northeast reported the largest annual decline of 8.7% and the West recorded the smallest at 5.2%. 

“Housing affordability remains a major constraint, particularly when mortgage rates rise above the 7% psychological threshold for potential buyers,” Selma Hepp, the chief economist at 
Cotality 
and a 
2025 HousingWire Vanguard
 award winner, said in a statement. “High rates are keeping monthly payments elevated, while tariff-related construction costs are adding pressure to insurance premiums and property taxes are further straining homeowners. Rising everyday expenses, including utility bills, are also squeezing household budgets. Together, these pressures are limiting momentum in existing-home sales.

Despite the worsening affordability challenges, just 2% of sales were distressed sales, unchanged from last month and a year ago. 

A look ahead

Looking ahead, economists believe housing market activity will pick up if 
mortgage rates
 decline a meaningful amount. 

“For housing, the key question is not simply whether mortgage rates decline, but whether they fall enough to meaningfully improve purchasing power and bring more buyers and sellers back to the market,” Hepp said. “Until then, I expect activity to remain relatively subdued, making the spring homebuying season an increasingly important test of whether the market can finally turn a corner.”

But for the next few months, industry leaders anticipate a slower pace of home sales, thanks to the level mortgage rates reached in mid-October. 

“We could see further weakness, since buyers who stepped back when rates hit 7.4% in mid-October won’t show up as missing sales until November and December closings,” Sean Fergus, the vice president of economic research at 
NewHomeSource
 and 
Zonda
, said in a statement. “Rates have since eased, but buyers need to trust that relief will last before they come back in force.”

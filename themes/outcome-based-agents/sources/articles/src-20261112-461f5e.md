Nick Wright has become a sports media superstar on FS1’s 
First Things First 
and his own eponymous podcast, and as 
he recently explained to Andrew Marchand
, his lifelong dream had always been to parlay his sports broadcasting fame into a career in politics.

The past decade of American politics, Wright told Marchand, left him feeling “beaten down” and changed his mind about formally entering the political arena. However, in the aftermath of “
The Blue Tsunami
” in the midterm elections, Wright is slowly reclaiming his rightful place in political and social commentary.

Wright called out Stephen A. Smith
 for his grift-adjacent persona as a fair-weather political pundit before 
delivering a 32-minute analysis
 of the midterms on 
What’s Wright? With Nick Wright
 and 
burying Matthew Yglesias
 for baseless critiques of newly elected Sen. Abdul El-Sayed (D-Mich.).

On Wednesday, Tim Miller of The Bulwark 
welcomed Wright on his 
The Bulwark Daily 
podcast
 to “
lib out
.” 
Ben Stiller loved it
, but Clay Travis presumably did not.

“If what you’re saying is true, that you don’t really believe what you’re saying, it’s just for audience and enrichment, I actually think that’s worse.” https://t.co/EPnCJq8eih

— The Bulwark (@BulwarkOnline) 
November 12, 2026

“I’ve always been an unapologetic, you know, I’m a liberal Democrat,” Wright said. “And in my field, what is frustrating to me is—everyone’s allowed whatever politics they are. I really, really don’t like the folks who have the opposite politics of mine who pretend they’re centrists or independents. They’re a lot of folks in my space, the tell is, if they make a big mistake or a fact error or tell a story that’s not true accidentally, it’s always because it was fed from the right-wing ecosystem. Those guys who claim they’re down-the-middle neutral never tell a crazy, zany left-wing falsehood that they stumbled upon because all they’re stumbling upon is the right-wing crazy stuff.”

Smartly, Miller asked whether Wright had anyone specific in mind.

“One of my first TV things ever was with Clay,” Wright said. “And then we went to dinner after, and he told me, he was like, ‘You know I worked for Obama, and I just do this’ or whatever. Luckily, there was a third person at this dinner, so if he disputes it, I have a witness. I told him, I was like, ‘I want to be very clear. If what you’re saying is true—that you don’t really believe what you’re saying, it’s just for audience and enrichment, I actually think that’s worse, and it is impossible for me to ever respect you.’ And I left. That was the last time I ever talked to him. That was 11 years ago.”

Late Wednesday night, Travis responded to Wright.

“Eleven years ago I was still a Democrat so this entire story is completely made up,” Travis tweeted. “I guess this is what happens when you start voting Republican, people make up entire stories about you in their heads and share it with their far left wing friends.”

Hopefully, the anonymous third person who attended the dinner breaks the tie, but no word yet.

Travis officially departed OutKick
, and by August, 
he launched Sport & State
, for which 
he interviewed President Donald Trump
 in late September. He also hosts 
The Clay Travis Show
, which airs on FS1, and 
The Clay Travis & Buck Sexton
 
Show

This is not the first time Wright has called out Travis for fabricating his public persona.

In June 2017, Travis and Wright got into it over the Black Lives Matter movement. Wright 
tweeted
, “Here’s @ClayTravis when he’s not in character, performing for the masses. Careful who you call a liar, Clay. Folks keep receipts these days.”

The tweet included two screenshots of emails from Travis:

On 
The Bulwark Daily
, Wright didn’t stop at Travis.

“Listen, the biggest name in my space, Stephen A., has more than flirted with politics,” Wright said. “And I just would like him to actually be honest with himself and the audience about what his politics are, and so be it.”

“That he’s pro-Trump, you mean?” Miller interjected.

“Yeah, I mean, pretty obviously,” Wright said. “And so, pretending like, oh, I’m just calling balls and strikes, like, no you’re not. In my field, the job is not to be unbiased. The job, however, is to be honest with your audience about your biases, and then let them call it how they see it.”

Miller asked Wright to estimate “how many of those guys are faking it,” and while Wright didn’t keep naming names or put a specific number to it, he expertly demystified the grift.

“It is so much easier to get an audience and make money [by] appealing to the lowest common denominator of the MAGA-sphere than it is to do it in sports,” Wright said. “Like, I’m going to sound like an a**hole here, but actually achieving at a high level in daily sports talk, it’s a very competitive space. And you have to get lucky, but you also really gotta be on it.

“And so, I think it’s a lot of folks who are like I wasn’t good enough. I wanted to do commentary about X—pop culture, entertainment, sports—I’m not good enough at that. But I’m comfortable talking into a camera or a microphone, and I can con these people. I do think there’s an element of that.”

Last week, Awful Announcing’s very own Sean Keeley pondered whether 
the historic Democratic victories
 in the midterms forecast “
a sports media blue wave
.” If that indeed comes to fruition, Wright won’t be alone in poking holes in the red MAGA balloon that has hovered over media and society for 10-plus years.

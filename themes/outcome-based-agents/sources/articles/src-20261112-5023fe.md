How Good Eggs Cut CX Tech Costs by More Than 60% With Crescendo

By Jack M. Germain

November 12, 2026 6:00 AM PT

When 
Good Eggs
 shoppers complained about late deliveries or substitutions, the online grocer's support team had to piece together reports from several tools to spot recurring problems. Those tools were also getting more expensive, said Chris Jocson, the company's chief operating officer.

Good Eggs moved from Zendesk to 
Crescendo
 and cut its customer experience technology costs by more than 60%, largely by consolidating its support software on one platform, Jocson said.

Crescendo launched its Customer Experience Platform in August. It brings contact center operations, ticketing, staffing, quality assurance, customer feedback, and knowledge management into one system. Crescendo says its AI agents can issue refunds and refer more complicated cases to a support agent.

Crescendo co-founder and CEO Andy Lee said experienced support agents can review cases where the AI fell short and identify changes that would help other customers with the same problem.

Good Eggs COO Chris Jocson (left) and Crescendo co-founder and CEO Andy Lee.

"In the age of AI, the human role doesn't disappear. Instead, it becomes a specialization. People handle the complex, sensitive situations that require judgment, but they're not just an escalation tier," Lee told CRM Buyer.

When hiring and training support staff, Lee said, companies should put more weight on judgment and empathy than on familiarity with several software systems. An agent needs to recognize when a customer needs personal attention and know how to handle that conversation.

He also recommended a gradual transition for companies with custom integrations and multiyear vendor contracts. By connecting the new platform to existing systems first, support teams can see how well it handles customer requests while they still have access to their old tools and customer records.

"Then you can retire redundant tools as contracts expire, migrate the data you actually need, and eliminate integrations that no longer serve a purpose. It's a multi-pronged approach," he said.

Why Good Eggs Moved From Zendesk

Jocson wanted a single view of support requests so Good Eggs could spot recurring problems with products or deliveries.

"We didn't have that single pane of glass, and CX is the voice and heartbeat of the customer. Every refund, substitution complaint, produce issue, or late delivery often tells us there is something we need to fix in our operating system, so it became clear we needed to streamline how we do business to provide a better customer experience. Food is a very personal experience," he told CRM Buyer.

 

 
 
 

Good Eggs considered Zendesk's AI tools before choosing Crescendo's platform. The reported savings covered technology costs alone.

"We did gain administrative and team capacity, but we haven't quantified that as part of the 60% figure. The bigger benefit has been the leverage we're gaining as an overall enterprise," he explained.

With fewer routine requests to handle, the team has more time to work through difficult cases, assist its highest-value customers, and investigate what went wrong with an order. Jocson compared the team's role to that of a concierge.

"I came from the hospitality and travel tech space. Even beyond this, it is clear the expectation of consumers is ever-increasing, so providing this experience was way more important to me than simply trying to reduce headcount," he added.

How Good Eggs Tested Its AI Rollout

Good Eggs started with its knowledge base and basic support tasks before adding transactions such as refunds and credits.

"I was impressed by how quickly the AI ramped up on our knowledge base and adapted to our brand voice. The harder part was testing grocery-specific edge cases," Jocson recalled.

Good Eggs tested when the AI could resolve a request on its own, when it should ask the shopper for more information, and when it should bring in a support agent.

"We intentionally phased that rather than trying to automate everything on day one," he said.

For the first time, Good Eggs shoppers could contact support directly through the grocer's app. Jocson said customer retention improved and shoppers placed repeat orders more often after the move to Crescendo.

"Straightforward issues are getting resolved faster, while our team has more time for cases where empathy and judgment matter. That broader CX improvement is likely part of what we're seeing in the business," he said.

Rotunda Capital Partners: American Equipment Revenue Grows Sixfold Before Sale

Rotunda Capital Partners has completed the sale of American Equipment Solutions after building the overhead crane and material handling services company into a national operation with more than 40 locations.

Rotunda invested in American Equipment in May 2021. During the partnership, the company completed 16 add-on acquisitions and increased revenue sixfold while expanding its geographic reach and service capabilities.

Established in 1969 and based in Salt Lake City, American Equipment provides overhead crane, hoist, below-the-hook equipment, and maintenance, repair, and operations services. It serves thousands of customers across the U.S.

The company’s services include inspections required by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration, preventive maintenance, field repairs, replacement parts, engineering, fabrication, automated systems, equipment modernization, and employee training.

Rotunda invested in American Equipment’s leadership, corporate infrastructure, data systems, and acquisition capabilities. It also helped establish an internal acquisition team to identify and integrate additional businesses.

Another initiative was American Equipment University, an internal program created to train and develop technicians. The program supports the company’s ability to recruit skilled employees and maintain service quality as its location network grows.

Chief Executive Officer Tom Hoyer will continue leading American Equipment alongside the existing management team. Founder Adam Zimmerman also indicated that the company plans to continue pursuing demand for its products and services under its next ownership group.

The sale is consistent with Rotunda’s strategy of transforming family-, founder-, and management-owned industrial businesses into larger, data-driven platforms through operational investment and acquisitions.

Rotunda manages approximately $1.5 billion in assets, including a $735 million fund. It invests in value-added distribution, asset-light logistics, and industrial and business services companies with enterprise values generally ranging from $50 million to $400 million.

Lincoln International served as lead financial adviser, while Benesch Friedlander Coplan & Aronoff provided legal counsel.

Local baseball standout Adonis Smith has now stepped into a coaching role as manager of the Delmarva Shorebirds.
Smith, an Eastern Wayne High School graduate and Goldsboro native, was once a solid player for the Eastern Wayne Warriors during his high school playing days.
Now, he is the 19th person in Delmarva Shorebirds history to become its manager. The Shorebirds are a Carolina League team from Salisbury, Md., and a Single-A affiliate minor league team from the Baltimore Orioles organization.
Smith said he was excited to get that kind of opportunity.
“I was excited to continue to do what I do and just ecstatic to be able to help these young men grow, not only as baseball players but as great men as well,” Smith said.
But Smith said he had the same ambition as other young men who grow up playing baseball — becoming a professional baseball player.
Smith grew up in Goldsboro and lived on Balsam Place and on South Slocumb Street during his childhood. He would go on to play three sports — baseball, basketball, and football — but baseball was what Smith loved to play the most.
“Baseball was just fine. It was just gravitating,” Smith said. “I really don’t know why. It’s just something that I loved to do.”
He went to play shortstop for the Eastern Wayne Warriors baseball team for four years, before graduating and moving on to play with N.C. State University.
After playing with the Wolfpack from 1997 to 1999, Smith would transfer to N.C. Agriculture and Technical State and play for the Aggies from 1999 to 2002. He would later graduate with a civil engineering degree, Smith said.
Smith would go on to play two seasons in the Frontier League, an independent league that is an official partner of Major League Baseball.
He played for three different teams including the Johnstown Johnnies, the River City Rascals, and the Cook County Cheetahs.
His best season was in 2002 with Johnstown. In 21 games, he posted a .304 batting average, a .361 on-base percentage, had 17 hits, scored 10 runs, knocked in seven runs, and stole three bases, according to 
baseball-reference.com
.
Despite his playing career in the Frontier League ending in 2003, Smith still wanted to remain in baseball and became a coach.

“I just love the game and continually want to be around players that had the same drive that I did that could go to the majors,” Smith said. “I didn’t make it that far. But I still had a passion for it … teaching and continuing to give back the knowledge of what I learned along the way.”
Smith made his coaching debut in 2022 as the hitting coach for the Williamsport Crosscutters, a team from the MLB Draft League.
Next, he joined the Trenton Thunder in 2023 as their hitting coach before becoming the manager in 2024. In his first season managing, he led Trenton to a 37-36 win-loss record and finished tied for second place with the Frederick Keys.
In 2025, Smith was hired by the Baltimore Orioles as the fundamentals coach for the Aberdeen IronBirds, the former High-A Affiliate of the Orioles, before becoming manager of the Delmarva Shorebirds for the 2026 season.
Smith said he learned some big lessons during his coaching years that he uses as the Shorebirds manager.
“Most of the time, it’s just being yourself and allowing yourself to be humbled by the experience, and then the guys will gravitate towards you,” Smith said. “Then, along those lines, you’re allowed to kind of implement the things that you want.
“It’s building trust with those guys, just building relationships with them.”
He said it is all about meeting them “in the middle,” allowing players to also be themselves but also have them meet expectations.
“The rest will definitely fall in line,” he said.
Smith said he had envisioned being a professional baseball player since his youth. But today, he thinks being a manager and coach is something that comes natural to him.
He said all he wants to do is make a positive impact on his players, provide them with the most baseball knowledge he can give them, and inspire them to be good players and good people.
“I was always a super confident person,” Smith said. “So, it never was really a doubt in my mind that I would be doing something that was in professional baseball. I never doubted myself. Anything’s possible as long as you put the work in.
“Be yourself and continue to have fun. You have to have fun doing this.”

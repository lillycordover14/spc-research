Premium Interview
Paige Wei-Cox, Pactum’s Chief Product Officer, explains how AI agents are transforming procurement strategy, compliance and commercial execution at scale
Procurement technology has traditionally focused on helping people work more efficiently through automation, standardised processes and system integration.

Pactum
 is challenging that model by developing AI agents that can take responsibility for executing procurement tasks themselves.

For 
Paige Wei-Cox
, the company’s Chief Product Officer, this represents a fundamental shift in enterprise software: from supporting human decisions to carrying out defined work autonomously.

“I've built software to help people perform their job more efficiently,” she says.

“Agentic AI even challenges that very model […] because now AI can increasingly take responsibility for the execution work itself, not simply support it.”

Paige brings more than 25 years of enterprise technology experience to the role, including two decades at SAP, where she led SAP Business Network.

The platform brought together business networks supporting US$4.9tn in annual commerce, reinforcing her belief that connecting processes and information can create greater value than optimising individual applications.
Paige Wei-Cox, Chief Product Officer at Pactum brings more than 25 years of enterprise technology experience. Credit: Pactum
Connecting the procurement process

Pactum initially focused on enabling enterprises to negotiate with thousands of suppliers at scale.

Its ambition has since expanded towards a connected AI execution layer for indirect procurement, bringing together four core capabilities: Intake, Alignment, Negotiation and Sourcing.

The newly launched Intake Agent is designed to apply procurement policies and category strategies to purchasing requests, determine how goods or services should be bought, and prepare aligned, compliant requisitions.

Alignment supports this process by checking requests against relevant policies and contracts.
Pactum’s AI layer connects Intake, Alignment, Negotiation and Sourcing. Credit: Pactum
Pactum’s work with AstraZeneca demonstrates the potential of this capability: its Alignment Agent has reportedly reduced requisition and contract reviews from days to less than a minute, achieving 100% requisition coverage.

For Paige, the significance goes beyond speed. Embedding procurement requirements into purchasing decisions can reduce dependence on manual checks and help ensure established strategies influence how employees buy.

Negotiation builds on Pactum’s original proposition, enabling AI agents to engage suppliers at a scale that would be difficult for human teams to manage alone.

The goal is not just to secure the lowest price, but to improve overall commercial outcomes across pricing, payment terms, volumes and service levels.

“The objective isn't simply to negotiate the lowest price, but to achieve the best overall commercial outcome within the company strategy and its constraints,” Paige explains.

Sourcing extends the wider ambition to connect procurement activities, helping translate commercial intelligence into action. In combination, these capabilities are intended to reduce the gap between identifying what should happen and carrying out the necessary work.
Pactum’s Alignment Agent cuts requisition and contract reviews to under a minute. Credit: Pactum
Autonomy built on trust

Delegating procurement activities requires clear governance. Enterprises must establish which tasks agents can perform independently, where human approval remains necessary and when decisions should be escalated.

“The challenge isn't proving that AI can perform a task,” Paige says. “It's establishing the trust, the governance and the operational reliability required for enterprises to delegate meaningful work to AI at scale.”

She argues that human control does not need to mean reviewing every individual action.

Instead, policies, commercial objectives and escalation thresholds can define the boundaries within which agents operate, allowing procurement professionals to concentrate on strategic sourcing, supplier relationships and complex decisions.
AI agents can free teams for supplier relationships and complex decisions. Credit: Pactum
Working alongside existing systems

Pactum is designed to complement established enterprise platforms rather than replace them. Integrations with systems such as Coupa and SAP Ariba, alongside Microsoft Teams, connect AI capabilities with existing workflows and familiar working environments.

“Enterprise integration is about much more than connecting systems through APIs,” Paige explains.

Agents must understand the business context and respect the controls governing enterprise activity.
Pactum integrates with Coupa, SAP Ariba and Microsoft Teams to connect AI capabilities with existing workflows. Credit: Pactum
The company’s approach also extends to suppliers. Negotiations can take place over email, avoiding the friction of requiring suppliers to register for another portal or learn a new interface.

“Sometimes the most effective innovation is making existing ways of working significantly more intelligent,” Paige says.

For Pactum, the opportunity is to help enterprises manage more spend, improve compliance and deliver stronger commercial outcomes without scaling operational effort at the same rate. 

As its connected AI workforce develops – most recently through the launch of its Intake Agent – Paige believes the focus will increasingly shift from what AI can recommend to the work organisations can confidently delegate to it.

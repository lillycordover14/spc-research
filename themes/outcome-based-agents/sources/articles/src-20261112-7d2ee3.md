Jackalope Artisan Fair Returns to Central Park This Weekend as CicLAvia Closes Raymond Avenue Sunday&nbsp;&nbsp;Pasadena Now

[page text]
The Jackalope Indie Artisan Fair returns to Central Park in Old Pasadena this Saturday and Sunday, Nov. 14 and 15, from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., with free admission and what organizers describe as more than 200 local, hand-selected artisans.

The fall fair is back in the park where it held its first event in 2015, and on Sunday it will share the neighborhood with CicLAvia—Pasadena, which closes Raymond Avenue to cars from Pacific Alley to Dayton Street, near the park’s north end, from 7 a.m. to about 5 p.m.

“Pasadena will always hold a special place for us because it’s where Jackalope started,” said Gloria Baghdassarian, event manager for Jackalope Arts, who was born and raised in Altadena. “We’ve had so many loyal guests who have been coming to our fairs since the beginning, and we love seeing familiar faces year after year.” She said the team has watched people “go from attending single with a dog to returning with families of their own.”

According to the Old Pasadena Management District, which lists the fair as one of its signature events, shoppers will find original fashion and jewelry design, paper goods, home décor and housewares, art, photography and food.

The district’s listing names an interactive butterfly garden, DIY perfume workshops and live portraits among the weekend’s activities, along with live musicians on both days and a children’s interactive music set with Ricky England at 1 p.m. each day. Jackalope Arts’ event page lists free wood-slice ornament making by the company’s nonprofit, Jackalope Gives, free live screen printing by Print Renegades and holiday photos on Instax film by Oh Snap Studios. Baghdassarian said visitors can register in advance on the fair’s website for Dewy Monday’s signature scent-making experience.

Asked how Jackalope verifies that artisans’ work is handmade rather than AI-generated, and why that standard matters, Baghdassarian said “creativity shouldn’t be mass-produced.” “In our application, we ask artists questions about their handmade process, the materials they use, and what makes their work uniquely theirs,” she said. “Many categories, like ceramics, are also inherently rooted in a physical, hands-on process that simply can’t be replicated by AI.”

The mix of work has shifted over the years, she said. “When we first started, we had a lot of jewelry and candle makers, but during COVID and in the years since, we’ve seen ceramicists become increasingly popular.”

Jackalope Gives was established after the January 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires, which destroyed homes and studios belonging to several of the fair’s artisans, according to the organization’s website, and its first fundraising effort raised more than $11,000 for affected makers, according to Jackalope Gives. “After the fires, we realized just how many of our own artists had been directly affected, and we wanted to do whatever we could to help,” Baghdassarian said.

Altadena Maid Products, which makes hand-formed seed balls and bark from California native wildflowers in Altadena, is listed at booth 147. The organizer lists 32 food and drink vendors, including three food trucks on the Fair Oaks Avenue side of the park.

CicLAvia, a free open-streets event, runs Sunday from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. along a 2.7-mile route on Colorado Boulevard, from Pasadena Avenue to Allen Avenue, and on Raymond Avenue. According to CicLAvia, route streets are closed to cars from 7 a.m. to about 5 p.m., all driveways on the route will be blocked during that time, and no parking is allowed on the route from 4 a.m. to 5 p.m., with towing enforced. Cars will be able to cross the route at six intersections, according to the city.

Parking options named for the fair include the Fishbecks outdoor lot at 
150 S. Raymond Ave
., which is cash only, the garage on Raymond Avenue, the city’s Schoolhouse structure on East Green Street and metered street parking. Baghdassarian said parking details are posted at 
jackalopeartfair.com/old-
pasadena
. Metro A Line riders can use Del Mar Station, about a two-minute walk from the park. Pasadena Transit Routes 10, 20, 33, 40 and 51s will detour Sunday because of CicLAvia, according to the city.

Last year’s fall fair was moved back a week, to Nov. 22 and 23, because of weather concerns.

The fair is outdoors, open to all ages and handicap accessible, and leashed pets are welcome, according to the district’s listing. The next Central Park fair is scheduled for April 24 and 25, 2027. More information is at

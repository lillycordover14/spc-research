Amsterdam-based Nebius Group NV (NBIS) reports fiscal third-quarter 2026 results before the market opens on Thursday, and options traders are bracing for a violent session: the implied move in the stock is roughly 13.25% in either direction, according to TipRanks' options data. That figure measures the expected size of the swing, not its direction. The company will host a conference call at 8 a.m. ET.

The neocloud operator -- an AI-focused cloud provider that sells access to GPU computing capacity -- carries a history of outsized post-earnings reactions. Over its last four reports, shares moved -1.60%, +6.70%, +9.23% and -7.69% the following session, an average absolute move of 6.31%. The coming print is priced for roughly double that.

Momentum heading into the release is firmly positive. The stock jumped more than 15% on Wednesday and added to those gains in premarket trading Thursday, lifted by blowout quarterly results from rival CoreWeave Inc (CRWV): revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% from a year earlier, a revenue backlog of about $132.5 billion, and full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to a range of $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion. Wall Street read across the beat as a demand signal for AI compute capacity broadly.

Estimates Point to Another Quarter of Hypergrowth

Consensus on the top line has climbed steeply. Analysts expect Nebius to report revenue of about $907.2 million, against $146.1 million a year earlier, with a loss of roughly $0.72 per share versus a $0.41 loss in the prior-year period.

Bank of America's Tal Liani, a four-star analyst, models $906 million in quarterly revenue, up 520% year over year, and expects the company to exit the period with 675 megawatts of connected power and about 525 megawatts of active power, up from 170 megawatts of active capacity at the start of 2026. He reiterated a Buy rating and a $310 target, and sees pricing for shorter-duration AI infrastructure contracts rising as demand outruns supply.

Citi's Tyler Radke trimmed his target to $265 from $324 but stayed at Buy, arguing neocloud results should reflect a "continued insatiable demand backdrop." Piper Sandler's James Fish took a harsher view, cutting his target to $185 from $237 with a Hold rating, on the thesis that AI-as-a-service names will stay "in the penalty box" until free cash flow improves enough to validate returns on invested capital, or until management teams give investors more clarity on how expansion gets financed.

Consensus is a Moderate Buy, split 9 Buy, 3 Hold and 1 Sell, with targets ranging from $144 to $410 and an average of $296.73. That average sits well above Wednesday's close of $154.69 -- about 92% upside -- though the dispersion is unusually wide for a company of this size.

Financing and Data-Center Constraints Are the Real Test

The stock has traveled a long way in both directions. It bottomed at $73.52 on Feb. 5, roughly quadrupled to a record $299.86 on June 22, then gave much of that back; it now sits about 48% below that peak, even after this week's rally, and is still up roughly 85% year to date.

Freedom Capital Markets chief market strategist Jay Woods attributes the drawdown to investor skepticism that the company's heavy capital spending will generate adequate returns, compounded by rising financing costs and fears that new data-center construction could face restrictions.

"Investors will want to hear if those fears have been overblown when they report Thursday before the bell," Woods said. He described the chart as a "clear breakdown from a major support level," with shares "slightly oversold near term," and suggested accumulating in small tranches on further weakness: "In time we should get a relief rally back to these recent gaps and the major moving averages. The question is from what lower level."

The setup mirrors the second quarter, when revenue rose 454% and AI cloud revenue climbed 514%, alongside four contracts averaging at least $1 billion in total value each -- disclosures that sent the stock higher at the time. With CoreWeave having delivered a double beat and raised guidance, investors now appear to expect the same combination from Nebius: an earnings beat, a revenue beat and confident demand commentary. Missing on any of the three would leave the shares exposed, particularly with the options market already positioned for a double-digit move.

Rosenblatt initiated coverage with a Buy and a $304 target, joining the bullish camp ahead of the print. The company's ability to convert contracted capacity into billed revenue, and to fund the buildout without diluting shareholders, will likely determine whether Thursday's reaction lands closer to the upper end of the recent range or the lower one.

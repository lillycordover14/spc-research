Nvidia: Colette Kress takes charge on interim basis, but who will really succeed Jensen Huang?             

                    Jensen Huang's shock resignation, formalized Thursday, opens an unprecedented period for Nvidia. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is now serving as interim CEO, while the board searches for a permanent successor. Among the names being discussed, engineer Ian Buck appears to be a serious candidate, but several major semiconductor industry figures could also enter the race.

Thirty-three years with the same boss, and suddenly no one left to take charge of the empire. A real shock wave has swept through Silicon Valley! 
Nvidia
 formally announced Thursday the immediate resignation of Jensen Huang, its co-founder and CEO since 1993.
Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is taking over on an interim basis, while Harvey C. Jones becomes the group's first board chairman. A search for a permanent successor is now underway, both internally and externally.
The challenge is immense. Jensen Huang was not merely Nvidia's public face. He shaped its technological direction, maintained close relations with major customers and personally helped coordinate a vast industrial ecosystem. Over 33 years, he transformed Nvidia from a graphics processor maker into a global artificial intelligence powerhouse, ultimately making it the world's largest company by market capitalization.
His successor will therefore need to be a strategist, engineer and negotiator all in one. Several names are already circulating.
Colette Kress, continuity above all
At 59, Colette Kress knows Nvidia better than almost anyone. She joined the company in 2013 after stints at 
Microsoft
, 
Cisco
 and 
Texas Instruments
, and has overseen the group's finances throughout its revenue explosion and the rise of artificial intelligence.
A familiar presence at earnings conferences alongside Huang, she represents a reassuring figure for investors. Her appointment primarily ensures an immediate transition without disrupting the organization.
The question is whether she could permanently take the reins of the group. Her financial experience is a considerable asset, but Nvidia built its dominance on particularly bold technological decisions. The board could therefore ultimately favor a candidate with a stronger focus on engineering and products.
Ian Buck, the architect of CUDA, in pole position
Ian Buck is not an engineer like the others at Nvidia. He joined the company in 2004, and this Stanford PhD was the driving force behind CUDA, the software platform that enabled graphics processors to move beyond their original use and become indispensable tools for computing and artificial intelligence. Today, this ecosystem represents one of Nvidia's main competitive advantages, making migration to competing solutions particularly costly.
But Buck has not confined himself to research. Now vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing, he oversees AI hardware and software products for technology giants, working closely with Microsoft, 
Google
, 
Amazon
, 
Meta
 and OpenAI. He has also advised the White House on artificial intelligence and testified before the US Congress. His profile combines technological expertise, commercial relationships and an understanding of political issues.
His main unknown, and it is a significant one, concerns his experience running a company of this size. Being an exceptional technology architect does not guarantee the ability to steer a group worth several trillion dollars on the stock market.
On Polymarket, investors nevertheless appear to favor his application. Ian Buck was leading Thursday with a 36% implied probability, ahead of Colette Kress at 23% and Rene Haas at 6%. These figures remain speculative and do not prejudge the board's decision.
Rene Haas and Johny Srouji, the outside candidates
The name of Rene Haas, CEO of 
Arm
, comes up regularly. A former Nvidia executive, he knows the group's culture and has CEO experience that Buck lacks. He also understands the complex relationships among chip designers, suppliers and major technology companies.
Another profile is attracting attention: Johny Srouji, Apple's head of hardware engineering. The architect of Apple's Silicon strategy, he helped transform Apple into one of the industry's most successful processor designers. His industrial expertise and ability to lead large-scale technology programs make him a credible candidate on paper.
But both men already hold strategic positions. Attracting them to Nvidia would be far from easy, not to mention that neither has publicly expressed interest in the position.
Other names are circulating, including Nvidia veteran Jeff Fisher and Bryan Catanzaro, head of applied deep-learning research. Jensen Huang's children, Madison and Spencer, both of whom work for the group, have also been mentioned, although there is no evidence to make either a favored candidate.
Bloomberg has also mentioned Matt Garman, head of Amazon Web Services (AWS), Sanjay Mehrotra, CEO of 
Micron
, and Amin Vahdat, Google's head of AI infrastructure. These are three profiles with complementary skills spanning the management of large data centers, semiconductor manufacturing and accelerator design.
One position, two jobs?
That may be the board's real dilemma. Should it choose an executive capable of immediately reassuring markets or a visionary capable of extending Nvidia's technological lead over the next decade?
An intermediate solution could emerge. Armina Rosenberg, co-founder of Minotaur Capital, told Reuters that a leadership structure combining Colette Kress, tasked with stabilizing the company and its finances, with a technology executive such as Ian Buck was one possibility.
This structure would have the advantage of distributing responsibilities that were previously concentrated in Jensen Huang's hands. It nevertheless remains hypothetical.
The new leadership will also need to preserve relationships with hyperscalers, industrial partners such as 
TSMC
 and the investors financing the expansion of AI infrastructure.
Nvidia has the technologies, teams and resources needed to continue growing. What it now needs to find is a leadership team capable of maintaining its lead in a sector where each product generation can reshuffle the deck.
Replacing someone like Jensen Huang looks like a once-in-a-lifetime challenge for Nvidia's board. And the timetable offers little respite. The group will report its quarterly results on November 17, providing Colette Kress with a first opportunity to reassure investors and, most likely, answer questions about the succession.

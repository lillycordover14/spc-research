At 70 years old, Smugglers’ Notch Resort is blowing out more than just a lot of candles.
This year, it’s also poised to blow out a lot more snow, thanks to new resort operator Bear Den Partners’ hefty investment in its snowmaking infrastructure.
The snowmaking team was able to test out some of the equipment last month when the Notch temporarily shut down, and was stoked about the results, according to Matt McCawley, the resort’s content media specialist.

“We went up on the mountain and fired up like halfway down, and it was very successful,” McCawley said.
At 42, McCawley knows a thing or two about the resort. Although his current title is relatively new, his first job was at Smuggs, and throughout the years he’s held a slew of different titles. While this isn’t the first time new equipment has been put up on the hill, he would argue it’s one of the more significant investments in recent memory.
The project aims to increase snowmaking capacity across the resort’s three mountain faces through the installation of 100 new snow guns; 1,100 feet of snowmaking pipe replacement; new and replacement valves to improve system reliability; increased water distribution from existing snowmaking supply; and additional mountain operations equipment.
Through the whole deal, the mountain crew also scored nine additional snowmobiles. McCawley said all this will increase efficiency and make things a bit easier for the snowmaking team.
“All of that together makes it so our team can move from place to place more efficiently, and they’re going to get to a new trail sooner, and they’re going to finish that trail sooner,” McCawley said. “When you’re a rider and you’re itching to be somewhere, that word, ‘sooner’ — that’s a good word.”
The strides in snowmaking mark one of the first capital investments Bear Den Partners has made since announcing the resort purchase in February.
The commitment to capital investments appears on par with the owners’ modus operandi. When Bear Den Partners acquired Burke Mountain in 2025, it promised a $30 million investment in the resort, including an $11.5 million investment in lifts and snowmaking, according to Ski Vermont.
CEO Jon Schaefer said in a phone call this week the owners are taking a slow foray into the Smugglers’ Notch community. More bluntly, he said, “We got to spend more time just learning where the bathrooms are.”
Snowmaking, however, is a fundamental product when it comes to operations.

“We are ski resort operators first, and so we are relentlessly driven to fix what we can on the ski and snowmaking side,” Schaefer said. “And, we’ve been working in a lot of different areas of the resort, but that’s a very visible impact that we can do.”
He wasn’t quick to announce any other major capital improvements that Smuggs goers can expect on the horizon, particularly when it comes to hot button topics like the slow, old-school lifts or the base lodge.
Not only are those expensive conversations, Schaefer said, but the group still must get a lay of the land.
“And we have to wrap our heads around the resort and get involved with the community and just understand exactly how the place functions, probably for a year or two,” he said, adding that snow guns — a moveable piece of equipment — is much different than a piece of equipment that’s existed in a place for over 30 years.
“You don’t really fix a Swiss watch by jamming a pencil into it and making irrational changes,” he said. “And I would like to understand how the place functions before we go.”
This year, the team is gearing up in different ways, especially since announcing in September that Smuggs is now part of the Indy Pass, joining the more than 300 independent alpine and Nordic destinations, several of which are already on Bear Den Partners’ resort roster.
Schaefer wasn’t shy about the mixed reaction the news might have generated, which is why a large part of operations this year will be focused on capacity control.
“There’s ways we can put our foot on the brake or throttle from an overall volume standpoint that maintains the experience for people,” he said.
The move was a natural next step seeing as Indy Pass has been a strong partner to Berkshire East Mountain Resort and Catamount Mountain Resort, two places Schaefer not only comes from in the ski world but has also owned with his family since 1976 and 2018, respectively. They officially joined the Bear Den Partners group this year. It also brought the Indy Pass to Burke Mountain last year.
“In terms of business ethics, it’s pretty similar to us — you know, strong, independent mountains,” he said.
Looking ahead at the opening date, the crew is gunning for around Thanksgiving, although the date is flexible. In a perfect world, he said, if the resort could open Saturday, it would.
“As soon as possible,” he said. “I want to ski.”

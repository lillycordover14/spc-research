Clay Travis is an author, radio show host, lawyer, TV analyst, and the founder and lead writer of Outkick. He regularly appears on television with Fox Sports 1 and Fox News. Travis gave a speech Monday, titled “How NIL is Ruining College Basketball,” in the Center for Constructive Alternatives’ Economics of College Sports lecture series hosted by Hillsdale College. 

 

 What advice would you give to Hillsdale students who want to work professionally in either sports journalism or radio?

 Read all the time. I think in your generation — and I’ve got three young boys — the most foundational ability you can have is to make an argument and consider a variety of perspectives. Then, be able to analyze all that information and put it into place to essentially just make an argument for a case. So read a ton and be driven by story. What is the most compelling way to connect with the biggest possible audience? Figure out how to connect on that front.

 

 Why should people care about college athletics?

 First, because it’s fun. And without getting into the academic treatise on why it’s fun, I think college is uniquely reflective of America in general. It used to be anyway, but it’s before you become a full-fledged professional, and while you’re still deeply connected to a team that matters more based on the name on the front of the jersey than the name on the back of the jersey. This guy’s a Nebraska fan. I’m a Tennessee fan. The players are going to come and go all too frequently in this current world, but the pomp, the circumstance, the pageantry of college athletics to me is the best and most entertaining and most American of all of our sports.

 

 What is your favorite part of your job?

 I get to say exactly what I think, and most people in America feel like they can’t say exactly what they think. It’s an incredible privilege every day to get to say exactly what I think across sports, politics, pop culture, whatever it is.

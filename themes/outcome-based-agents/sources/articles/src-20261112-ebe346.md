Guterres: Early warning systems reduce disaster-related deaths sixfold&nbsp;&nbsp;سانا

[page text]
UN Secretary-General António Guterres. File Photo: Anadolu Agency

 

Antalya, Nov. 12 (SANA)
 United Nations Secretary-General 
António Guterres
 has stressed the importance of early warning systems in saving lives and limiting the damage caused by natural disasters and climate change, noting that countries with comprehensive systems record disaster-related death rates approximately six times lower than those without them.
Anadolu Agency quoted Guterres as saying on Wednesday, during a session titled “Early Warnings for All” at the 31st Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (
COP31
) in Antalya, that issuing warnings 24 hours before a disaster strikes can reduce damage by up to 30 percent.
Guterres said 
heatwaves, floods, storms
 and droughts claim lives, destroy livelihoods and undermine development gains. He noted that around one-third of countries worldwide still lack early warning systems.

UN initiative to protect everyone

Guterres said the United Nations launched the “Early Warnings for All” initiative to ensure that everyone worldwide is protected by early warning systems by the end of 2027.
Around 160 countries have received support under the initiative, with nearly $2 billion mobilized since 2022, he said.
He called for increased concessional and sustainable international financing and stronger cooperation to bridge funding and capacity gaps, warning that debt burdens and rising borrowing costs limit developing countries’ ability to establish and improve these systems.
Guterres further noted that countries that have contributed the least to 
the climate crisis
, particularly least developed countries and those affected by conflict, are among the most vulnerable to its consequences.
The COP31 conference
 opened in Türkiye on Monday and runs through Nov. 20. Its agenda focuses on climate finance, adaptation to climate change and the transition to clean energy.

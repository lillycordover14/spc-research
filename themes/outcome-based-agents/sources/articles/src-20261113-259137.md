                    The European Medicines Agency's expert committee CHMP is standing by its negative opinion on marketing authorization for Cessatech's and Proveca's KemSu nasal spray following a re-examination of the application.
"Although we are naturally very disappointed by the outcome of the re-examination, it is positive that CHMP has removed one of the two original grounds for refusal, meaning that there is now a more clearly defined regulatory issue to address. We remain confident in KemSu's overall benefit-risk profile and its potential to meet a significant medical need among children," says Jes Trygved, CEO of Cessatech.
"Together with our partner Proveca, the next step will be to seek scientific advice from EMA to better understand the remaining deficiencies and determine how they can best be addressed. We believe this will provide an important basis for defining a path toward a new application, with our long-term goal unchanged, to make KemSu available to children who need new treatment options for acute pain," Trygved continues.
KemSu, which combines ketamine and sufentanil, is being developed to treat acute pain in children between 1 and 17 years of age.
Proveca is Cessatech's commercialization partner in Europe, while the United States remains Cessatech's primary strategic market.

There's no better day to talk about the hit film 
Friday the 13th
's star 
Harry Lillis Crosby III

Bing Crosby
, who famously sang the popular song "White Christmas," and his wife Kathryn Grant, were the proud parents of Harry, who portrayed the role of Bill Brown in the movie.
The film centers around two camp counselors who were murdered at Camp Crystal Lake.

Harry Crosby is Bing Crosby's son

Harry, now 68, got the acting bug early, as he performed onstage alongside his dad during Christmas specials.

, he recalled: "I went down to watch him do the Hollywood Palace at age six, and they asked me if I knew any songs. And I said, 'well, I just learned a Christmas carol.'"
He continued: "So, of course, that fit well and ended up doing the show with them—complete black tie and shorts and shiny black shoes. And that was the beginning of the Christmas shows."

Harry's big break came thanks to
 Friday the 13th
 in 1980. He also appeared in the TV series 
Double Trouble
, which aired from 1984 to 1985. After making a name for himself in the entertainment industry, he switched lanes.
He studied at the London Academy of Music and Dramatic Art, where he earned a master's degree, as well as Fordham University, where he earned a business degree.

His first job role was at Lehman Brothers, followed by Cranemere and Snow Phipps. He became a partner of business development for the company Trilantic North America in 2019. He got married to Dr. Mihaela Skobe in 2003 and they welcomed two children into the world together. 
Bing passed away from a heart attack at the age of 74 in 1977, however, Harry and his family pay tribute to him every year in a special way when Christmas comes around.
Harry revealed to 
Express
: "My son, Nicholas, and daughter, Thea, sing and play 'White Christmas' on the piano for everyone."

He added: "We have friends around, and some years my mom and sister are here from California. It's an important tradition, a mainstay of Christmas Day."
Harry recalled: "It's funny, I used to cringe at the idea of playing my father's music but not now. People really love it – it's a sustainable thing."

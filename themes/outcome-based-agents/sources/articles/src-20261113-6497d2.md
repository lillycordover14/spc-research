SEGUIN — Burton’s dreams of earning a playoff win came crashing down in the second half on Thursday as its season came to an end.
The Panthers allowed 27 second-half points to take a 27-7 loss to Harper in the bi-district round of the Class 2A Division II playoffs at Matador Stadium.
Burton (6-4) scored its fewest points of the season and was held to fewer than 10 points for the first time since 2024.

“A lot of young guys grew up tonight,” Burton coach Jason Hodde said. “We had some freshmen, some new guys playing in the secondary and it is what it is. We will be back next year.”
Burton shut Harper out in the first half by consistently stopping the Longhorns’ rushing attack. The Longhorns adjusted their offense to primarily throw the football in the second half, throwing Burton’s defense off balance.
Harper completed four of its five passes in its first drive of the half. Longhorn senior quarterback Braydon Luke found open receivers near the sideline throughout the drive, including a 20-yard touchdown pass to wide receiver Jaylon Wilson.
Burton hung on to a lead, though, because of a blocked point-after attempt with 10:26 left in the third quarter.
Harper quickly got the ball back after a fumble from Burton freshman running back Hudson Frank. Luke took advantage on the fourth play of the ensuing drive, finding wide receiver Tate Garrett open in the left side of the end zone after extending the play with 9:03 left in the quarter.
“I thought we had some good coverage on some of them,” Hodde said. “They just jumped up and made some plays.”
The Longhorns nearly had another big play with a successfully recovered onside kick with no Panthers near the middle of the field, but an offside penalty negated the recovery.
Burton’s offense, meanwhile, could not find an answer. Playing without senior athlete Kayden Patterson, the Panthers struggled to find big plays while running the football with a variety of different players.
The Panthers had a turnover on downs in addition to their fumble and added a punt late in the period.
Hodde said trailing in the second half made the Panthers try to find answers through other avenues besides long, time-chewing possessions.
“It changes what we like to do as far as running the football and controlling the clock and then we had to get out and do things just a little different,” he said.
The offensive woes stayed with Burton in the fourth quarter. Harper punched the ball out of senior Rueben Rodriguez’s hands with 8:45 left in the period. Luke again took advantage by plowing through multiple Panthers on his way to the end zone on a 23-yard run with 7:13 to play.
Harper added one more touchdown with 3:02 remaining on a 33-yard fourth-down pass to Garrett Ressmann, who used his speed to gain separation from the Panthers on a vertical route down the right sideline.
Burton’s rushing attack gained only 52 yards in the second half. Freshman Jackson Burch led the second-half running effort with 27 yards on three carries. Senior Isaac Matthies tallied just eight second-half yards on six carries after gaining 116 yards in the first half, including Burton’s only score on a 64-yard run toward the left side in the second quarter.
Burch and Matthies shared quarterback duties throughout the night, with Hodde saying both players handled the playoff stage well away from their primary positions. Both players completed two passes, with Burch’s two completions coming after Matthies tweaked his wrist, according to Hodde.
“It is just part of the game and we were out of sync in the second half,” Hodde said.
For the game, sophomore Aaron Buchanan ran for 27 yards while Frank finished with 39 yards. Ross Roehling and Lawrence Allen also got one carry each.
Senior Logun Tosta made his presence felt on defense with two second-half pass breakups and a first-half interception.
Thursday’s playoff loss brought Panther football careers to a close for 12 seniors, including Hodde’s son, Houston. Burton’s senior class went 25-17 over its four years on the gridiron with at least six wins and playoff berths in every season.
Jason Hodde said Thursday’s loss was made more emotional by coaching his son for the final time after spending years coaching Houston and his classmates in different sports.
“Just coaching your son, it is hard to explain,” Jason Hodde said. “I am blessed to have done it and the seniors that he has [alongside him], I have coached those cats since they have been little dribblers, so I grew up watching them play T-ball and all different sports because they are my son’s class.
“It stings a little more probably and not to take anything away from the other senior classes, but I am just a little closer to this one just because of my son,” he concluded.

 /PRNewswire/ -- Novo Nordisk today announced the submissions of a New Drug Application (NDA) to the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and a Marketing Authorization Application (MAA) to the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for etavopivat, an investigational, once-daily, orally available, selective activator of pyruvate kinase red blood cell isozyme (PKR), for the treatment of adolescents and adults (aged 12 years and older) living with sickle cell disease (SCD). If approved, etavopivat could offer a new approach to treating SCD as a disease-modifying therapy.
The FDA has previously granted etavopivat Fast Track, Rare Pediatric Disease and Orphan Drug designations and is expected to take action on the NDA in 2027. Etavopivat was also granted Orphan Drug designation by the European Commission who is expected to take action on the MAA in 2027. A MAA is the formal application for seeking approval to market a medicine in the European Union (EU).
"For people living with sickle cell disease, severe vaso-occlusive crises and other symptoms can have a profound impact on everyday life, and treatment options remain limited," said Martin Holst Lange, executive vice president, chief scientific officer and head of Research & Development at Novo. "If approved, we are hopeful that etavopivat can provide a new disease-modifying treatment option for people living with sickle cell disease, and we will continue to work alongside the community to help advance health equity and overcome the longstanding disparities that impact those affected by this disease."
The NDA and MAA submissions are grounded in the results from the HIBISCUS study program, which was designed to establish the efficacy and safety profile of etavopivat, and evaluated the annualized rate of vaso-occlusive crises (VOCs) at week 52 and hemoglobin response (>1 g/dL) rate at week 24 compared to placebo, in adults and adolescents living with SCD. Participants were also allowed to receive standard of care treatment throughout the trial.
1 
In topline results announced in April 2026, etavopivat showed a 27% reduction in VOCs and a ~4-month delay to the first VOC compared to placebo. More detailed results from the HIBISCUS phase 3 trial will be presented at a future scientific conference and published in a peer-reviewed journal.
What is sickle cell disease?
Sickle cell disease (SCD) is driven by polymerization of mutated sickle hemoglobin (HbS) in red blood cells. The two main hallmarks of SCD are hemolytic anemia and vaso-occlusive crises (VOCs). Around 8 million people worldwide live with SCD, with the majority in low and middle-income countries.
4 
Approximately 100,000 people living with SCD are in the United States, as well as approximately 110,000 in Europe, 5.7 million in sub-Saharan Africa, 1.28 million in India, and 618,000 in the Middle East and North Africa.
3-5
 The disease is characterized by several associated acute and chronic symptoms and complications such as acute chest syndrome, stroke and kidney, liver and heart disease.
3 
Despite recent advances in treatment, many people with SCD experience significant levels of pain and reduced lifespan by approximately 20 years compared to the general population.
3
What is etavopivat?
Etavopivat is an investigational, once-daily, orally available, selective activator of pyruvate kinase red blood cell isozyme (PKR), a key enzyme in glycolysis. In SCD, PKR activation reduces 2,3-diphosphoglycerate (2,3-DPG) levels and increases adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production. Reduction of 2,3-DPG improves hemoglobin (Hb)-oxygen affinity, averting sickle hemoglobin polymerization and subsequent sickling. The increase in ATP production helps preserve RBC membrane integrity and deformability, improving RBC survival. These disease-modifying effects have the potential to reduce VOC events and improve Hb levels.
The US Food and Drug Administration has granted etavopivat Fast Track, Rare Pediatric Disease and Orphan Drug designations. Additionally, etavopivat was granted Orphan Drug designation by the European Commission based on a positive opinion from the Committee for Orphan Medicinal Products of the European Medicines Agency for the treatment of patients with SCD.
Etavopivat was acquired by Novo as part of the 2022 acquisition of Forma Therapeutics.
What is the HIBISCUS program?
The HIBISCUS clinical development program investigates etavopivat as a disease-modifying treatment for people with SCD. The pivotal program includes:
HIBISCUS (NCT04624659) - a 52-week efficacy and safety design phase 2/3 trial, comparing once-daily etavopivat versus placebo, enrolling approximately 440 people aged 12 years and over with SCD who had 2 to 15 VOCs within 12 months prior to enrollment. Participants were allowed to receive standard of care treatment throughout the trial. Following the completion of the 12-week phase 2 interim readout, the phase 3 dose was selected, and participants were randomized to the phase 3 main phase of the trial, which continued for 52 weeks.
1
HIBISCUS 2 (NCT06612268) - a 52-week efficacy and safety phase 3b trial, comparing once-daily etavopivat versus placebo, with expected enrollment of 510 people aged 12 years and older with SCD.

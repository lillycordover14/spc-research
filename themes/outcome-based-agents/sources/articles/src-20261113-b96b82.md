MANCHESTER, Ky. (WKYT) - The Clay County School Board narrowly passed a 13% raise for all staff, and some people are questioning the timing of the vote. The raise will take effect ahead of the holidays.
Board Chairman Mark Hoskins said the raise responds to pay increases that have not kept up with inflation.
Vote followed Election Day
Three members, including the chairman and two outgoing members, voted for the raise. The meeting was held after Election Day. Robert Marcum, a board member-elect who will start serving in January, defeated one of the members who voted for the raise.
Marcum said the board approved the money during a special-called meeting.
“There was a special-called meeting and during that meeting there was $4.5 million pushed out,” Marcum said.
Video from the meeting includes a speaker saying, “Let me talk for a minute. You don’t have the floor. I have the floor!”
Questions about sustainability
Marcum questioned whether the raise can be sustained.
“A 13% at one time from the outside looking in, is that sustainable?” Marcum said.
Hoskins said the raises are sustainable. He said Clay County has the largest contingency fund in the state of Kentucky and that the district is financially sound.
Marcum said he had hoped the board could address the issue after new members took their seats.
“That is what I was hoping that we could address this when we took the seat. And address those matters then. Everything seems to be rushed,” Marcum said.
Board member abstains
Board member Roy Allen is one of two members who abstained from voting on the raise. Allen said he believes teachers need higher pay. He questioned why no time was allotted to discuss the issue.
Hoskins said that because the board gave the raises all at once, it will not have to give another raise for at least five years.

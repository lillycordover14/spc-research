Results are trickling nationwide where Indigenous people are competing to join state legislatures in 24 different states, some of whom would be their state's first ever Indigenous legislator. 
As of Nov. 6 over 83 percent of Indigenous candidates in declared state legislature races have won. 
Among the winners, history is already being made. 
In Vermont, Alanna Ojibway, Sault St. Marie Band of Chippewa and descendant of the United Houma Nation, is believed to be the first-ever Indigenous person elected to the Vermont General Assembly.

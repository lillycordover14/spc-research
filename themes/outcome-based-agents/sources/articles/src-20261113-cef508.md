LangChain has raised $300 million at a $3 billion valuation. The company also says more than 7,000 organizations now use LangSmith, its platform for managing AI agents.

The details of the Series C

The Series C was announced November 12-13, 2026, and led by CapitalG, 
Alphabet
’s growth fund. The $3 billion figure is a post-money valuation, meaning it includes the fresh $300 million.

GIC and DFJ Growth joined as new investors. Existing backers IVP and Sapphire Ventures returned for another round.

The cap table also picked up Salesforce Ventures, Insight Venture Partners, QuantumLight, and MongoDB Ventures.

LangChain’s Series B in October 2025 set its valuation at $1.25 billion, so the new figure is a 2.4x increase in roughly a year.

With this round, LangChain’s total disclosed funding comes to approximately $460 million.

What LangSmith actually does

LangChain is a San Francisco startup focused on tools for building applications powered by large language models. LangSmith is its flagship platform, and it is where the new money is pointed.

LangSmith is described as framework-agnostic. Companies can pick whichever models they want while keeping governance and control over their data.

The customer list skews heavyweight. LangSmith users include 
NVIDIA
, Toyota, 
Salesforce
, and Workday, alongside startups such as Harvey, Rippling, and Clay.

The platform reportedly handles more than 1 billion events per day.

Workday offers the most concrete example. LangSmith reportedly cut the time it takes Workday to deploy a new agent from five months to one day.

Where the money goes

LangChain plans to use the capital to scale LangSmith and hire aggressively. The company currently has 435 full-time employees and aims to reach 850 by the end of 2027.

Hiring is earmarked for engineering, sales, and customer deployment.

New LangSmith capabilities are expected to be unveiled on November 20, 2026.

The shift from models to infrastructure

Enterprises increasingly want infrastructure and observability tools for AI agents, not just access to raw model power.

A company running an agent that touches payroll, customer records, or legal documents needs to know when that agent does something odd. It also needs records to explain those actions to auditors, regulators, and its own executives.

The 7,000-plus organizations using LangSmith suggest the gap is real.

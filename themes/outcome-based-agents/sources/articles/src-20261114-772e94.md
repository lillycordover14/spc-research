A port has marked more than 300 years of being involved in clay exports around the world.
The Port of Teignmouth in Devon handles more than 160,000 tonnes of clay each year, with shipments reaching markets as far afield as South America and Asia, according to Associated British Ports (ABP).
Formed some 30 million years ago in the Bovey Basin, Dorset, merchant William Crawford began exporting the material to London in 1726.
Now, 300 years on, Devon's ball clays are said to typically make up about 25% of the material used to make toilets and wash basins.

A consignment of 20 tonnes of Tobacco Pipe Clay sent from Teignmouth to London by a John Osland in 1700 marked one of the first documented exports of clay from the port.
By 1729, it was being transported by ship to Staffordshire and would later provide the raw material for pioneering potters.
John Pike/Ball Clay Heritage Society
The technology involved has developed during the 300 years - this image shows barges loading a steam vessel in Teignmouth harbour in the early 1920s
Ashley Curnow, divisional port manager for ABP, said the milestone was a testament to the "enduring importance of Teignmouth as a gateway for trade".
He added the port had supported south Devon's ball clay trade for more than 300 years by "connecting local industry to global markets and playing a vital role in the region's economy".
Ben Uphill, managing director UK for Sibelco - which mines, processes, and sells specialty industrial minerals - described it as "one of the region's great success stories" built on "the uniqueness of our natural resources, local expertise and entrepreneurship and long-standing partnerships".
Sibelco
ABP's Dan Pringle, Sibelco's Ben Uphill and ABP's Richard Frewin paid tribute to the  longevity of the industry
Campaigners, however, recently warned against Sibelco's plans to cut down a woodland in order to 
reopen a ball clay pit between Kingsteignton and Newton Abbot
.
The company plans to extract five million tonnes of ball clay over the next 50 years, which could mean removing six fishing lakes, surrounding trees and relocating Newton Abbot Rugby Club.

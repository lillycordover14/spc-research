RHINEBECK, N.Y. — Resident Erin Decker is pressing the Town Board to develop Americans with Disabilities Act-compliant access to the children’s play area at Thompson-Mazzarella Park.

During the board’s meeting on Monday, Nov. 9, Decker, a grandmother, delivered an emotional request for equity.

“There is not a wheelchair, (walkway) or accessible pathway from the front entrance to the play area,” Decker said.

“We have beautiful turf soccer fields, children’s playground, pickleball, tennis, bocce courts, basketball, baseball fields, beach volleyball, swimming pool, lovely walking trails and community gardens,” she added. “These are wonderful resources for all our of our residents, but … there’s a significant gap in these recreational opportunities. Children with disabilities are not being given the same opportunities to play as other children.”

The only accessible item is a swing that is the farthest point from the entrance, according to Decker.

“Once a child using a wheelchair or walker, if they can make it to the play area, they must then navigate a 10-inch containment wall surrounding the play area and then maneuver through 10 to 12 inches of loose wood chips,” Decker said. “Wheels sink into the loose material. Walkers become impossible to maneuver. Children with leg braces may struggle with balance and footing.”

Children with disabilities watch other children play freely while they struggle to move around the ground, she said.

“(Imagine watching) other children run freely from one piece of equipment to the other while you struggle just to move around the ground,” she said. “Imagine being an independent, spirited child wanting to join your friends but needing adults to pull, push or even carry you across the playground.”
 

Among her requests is that town officials develop a plan and budget for implementing accessibility features at the play area.

Supervisor Elizabeth Spinzia said, “I wholeheartedly support it. I know that there are groups that advocate for this. If there is someone who can do the assessment for us … get a quote and (we) will put it on the next agenda.”

Town officials have been creating sports and recreation facilities at the park on Traver Lane since 2015, after 13 years of planning the type of activities that could be built on the 71.95-acre property. However, none of the plans included the Starr Library, which shares an entrance but is separately owned and operated as part of a library district.

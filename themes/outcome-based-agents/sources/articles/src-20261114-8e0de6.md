Two Qutrits Construct Any Quantum Basis via Local Operations

The long-standing question of whether two qutrits could generate any quantum basis through local operations now has a definitive answer: they can. This finding establishes a clear boundary regarding the scalability of this property, as systems comprised of four or more qubits have recently been proven not to possess the same capability. This demonstration of universality for two qutrits extends beyond simply confirming a theoretical possibility.

Specifically, they successfully created these encodings in dimensions d×2 for d greater than or equal to 2, and in dimensions d×3 for d equal to 3, 4, and those greater than or equal to 6. These constructions highlight a pattern of universality in bipartite systems featuring one or two infinite-dimensional subsystems, suggesting a deeper connection between dimensionality and the ability to generate complete bases locally.

The work also establishes a necessary condition for universality in finite-dimensional multipartite systems, derived from a parameter count. Beyond the mathematical proof, the Düsseldorf team provided an operational interpretation of universal local encodability. They demonstrated that a system’s failure to be universally locally encodable is directly equivalent to a disadvantage in discrimination tasks. Specifically, non-ULE systems require general measurements, more complex than those that can be simulated by simple projections, to effectively detect random local unitary rotations.

“The most prominent example is the Bell-state measurement, which is an essential ingredient for

Further demonstrating the breadth of their findings, the researchers proved universal local encodability also holds for two harmonic oscillators, systems with infinite dimensions.

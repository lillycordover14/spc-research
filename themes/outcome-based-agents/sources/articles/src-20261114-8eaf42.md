Netflix is currently streaming a drama so compelling that viewers are watching the entire seven-episode series in a single sitting. The miniseries holds an impressive 96% rating on Rotten Tomatoes, with audiences raving that it is "amazing" and "masterful". It's available to stream right now, alongside 
another Netflix series that viewers simply cannot switch off
.
‌
‌
Released in 2020, the miniseries follows the journey of a woman who discovers chess in an orphanage basement during the 1950s and goes on to reach the very top of the chess world. It is adapted from the novel of the same name by Walter Tevis. The Queen's Gambit stars Anya Taylor-Joy as chess prodigy Beth Harmon, a role for which she claimed three major accolades, amongst them the Golden Globe Award for Best Actress. The series itself amassed an extraordinary haul of awards, reports 
the Mirror
, including 11 Primetime Emmys, two Golden Globes and two Critics' Choice Television Awards.
‌
Audiences have hailed it as "the best thing ever". One viewer wrote on Reddit: "I turned it on because I had nothing else to watch and I wound up binging the whole entire series in one night. I cheered for each chess match like it was the Super Bowl. I don't understand why I was so enthralled but the storytelling is so masterful and everyone is amazing in this show."
View 4 Images
The Queen's Gambit is the ninth most-watched Netflix series of all time
(Image: Chona Kasinger/Bloomberg via Getty)
One viewer commented: "I just got off a 12-hour graveyard shift, I usually get home around 8am and put Netflix on to fall asleep. But when I decided to start The Queen's Gambit I quickly got a second wind and before I knew it I was awake watching for 7-8 hours straight! Never have I been so quick to click on the play next episode! This is definitely a must-binge even if you don't know much or anything about chess. The story telling, character development, casting and acting was top notch!".
A further viewer remarked: "It's amazing that this is a Netflix original. This is like an old Hollywood production put on by pros in front of and behind the camera. So refreshing to see this level of art in this day and age."
‌

Professional critics are equally enthusiastic. Richard Roper of the Chicago Sun-Times wrote: "Raise your hand if you anticipated a coming-of-age, period-piece drama about a female chess prodigy in the 1950s and 1960s becoming perhaps the most addictive and binge-worthy series of 2020."
‌
View 4 Images
Pictured in this screengrab Anya Taylor-Joy, winner of Best Performance by an Actress in a Limited Series or Motion Picture Made for Television for “The Queen’s Gambit”, speaks during the 78th Annual Golden Globe Awards broadcast on February 28, 2021
(Image: NBC/NBCU Photo Bank via Getty)
Nancy Durant of the Evening Standard noted: "Even if you don't know your knight from your bishop it's thrilling, while the naturalistic script flows beautifully. Most satisfyingly, every time you think it's going to fall back on some awful 'woman in a man's world' trope, it just doesn't."
‌
Linda Marric, writing in The Jewish Chronicle, declared: "Creators Scott Frank and Allan Scott have managed the impossible here. They have succeeded in turning a story about one of the most docile and unglamorous sports into something truly magical and outstanding."

The Queen's Gambit Awards
The Queen's Gambit collected numerous accolades, amongst them the following prestigious honours:
11 Primetime Emmy Award wins (out of 18 nominations), including Outstanding Limited or Anthology Series, Outstanding Cinematography, Outstanding Music Composition
2 Golden Globe Awards, for Best Television Limited Series and Best Performance by an Actress
2 Critics' Choice Television Awards for Best Limited Series and Best Actress
1 Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor
1 Directors Guild of America Award for Outstanding Directorial Achievement

Pace pilots all-day bus service on McHenry County routes&nbsp;&nbsp;Shaw Local

[page text]
Pace
 is piloting expanded service in 
McHenry County
.
The changes include what’s being touted as all-day bus service on Routes 806 and 808 in McHenry County, the agency announced Friday.
Pace joined local leaders and regional transit partners at the McHenry County Administrative Building in 
Woodstock
 Friday for a ribbon-cutting ceremony to mark the launch, according to the release.
The expansion was developed with the Northern Illinois Transit Authority, which this fall replaced the Regional Transportation Authority, to improve public transportation access across northeastern Illinois, Pace, the suburban busing agency, said in a news release.

County Board member John Collins (left to right), McHenry City Planner Cody Sheriff, Pace Board member Erin Smith, McHenry County Board Chairman Mike Buehler, Pace Executive Director Melinda Metzger, NITA Board member Brian Sager, McHenry County Assistant Director of Transportation Scott Hennings and McHenry County Administrator Scott Hartman appear together Nov. 13, 2026, in Woodstock during a ribbon-cutting for expanded Pace service in McHenry County. 
(Photo provided by McHenry County)
Route 808, which connects Woodstock, 
Crystal Lake
 and 
Harvard
, now offers expanded weekday service, with buses operating every 20 to 60 minutes throughout the day.
Additional eastbound and westbound trips between the Woodstock and Crystal Lake Metra stations “provide greater flexibility for residents traveling to work, appointments, shopping, and other destinations,” the agency said.
The changes also bring a new service pattern on select midday trips, providing access to the McHenry County Government Center and Woodstock Secretary of State Facility.
Route 806, which runs from Crystal Lake to 
Johnsburg
 via 
McHenry
, is doubling its daily trips by adding 12 new weekday trips, providing service every 30 to 90 minutes throughout the day, according to the release. Midday service now runs hourly between the Crystal Lake 
Metra
 Station and the Walmart in Johnsburg, and southbound buses connect to Route 808 for through-service to Woodstock.
Pace said Crystal Lake transfers between Pace and Metra are being upgraded with enhanced real-time arrival signage in coordination with NITA. On routes 806, 807 and 808, passengers can board or exit at any safe intersection along the route, the agency said.
“This service expansion reflects Pace’s continued commitment to delivering flexible, reliable public transit for McHenry County residents,” Pace Executive Director Melinda Metzger said. “By filling midday service gaps and improving connections to Metra and regional destinations, we are making it easier for people to travel for work, medical appointments, shopping, and everyday errands without relying solely on a personal vehicle.”
Pace Board Member Erin Smith said: “At Pace, our goals are clear. We are working to enhance our fixed-route service wherever practical and add frequency to make regional transit more accessible and reliable for our riders. I’m proud to see these efforts happening in McHenry County.”
McHenry County Board Chairman Mike Buehler tied the expansion to the creation of NITA, which he said brought “the promise that McHenry County and the rest of the Chicago region would benefit from greater and more reliable mass-transit service. With the expansion of Pace Suburban Bus service in McHenry County, I’m pleased to see that promises made are starting to be promises kept, not even a year after the legislation creating NITA was signed into law.”
Said NITA Interim Executive Director Jamie Rhee: “Expanding transit service and strengthening connections between communities are key to building a stronger, more connected regional transit system. These improvements will bring more frequent, reliable service to McHenry County, making it easier for residents to access jobs, essential services and destinations throughout the region.”
Pace said its ReVision Draft plan, a proposal to increase suburban bus service by 50%, is available for review and public comment. The agency said full schedule details, route maps and trip planning assistance are available at 
PaceBus.com
.
The announcement comes as McHenry County grapples with the need for cost-cutting measures to close a multimillion-dollar budget gap. 
Reducing or changing the county’s dial-a-ride service
 has been looked at as one potential move, which could necessitate higher use of and need for Pace services in the area.

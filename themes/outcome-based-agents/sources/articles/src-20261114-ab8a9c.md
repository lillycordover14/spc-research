Procurement Magazine: Top 5 Stories of the Week
Procurement Magazine explores some of the week’s biggest stories, including NATO’s drone marketplace, Omnea’s NYC summit and Jabil’s five-firm integration
NATO & NCIA’s Counter-Drone Marketplace Speeds Procurement
Dr Dylan Browne, General Manager of the NCIA. Credit: NATO
NATO is accelerating defence procurement, giving member states faster access to counter-drone technologies through its Counter-Unmanned Aircraft Systems (C-UAS) marketplace.

Part of NATO’s US$40bn Drone Edge initiative, the marketplace will be managed by
 NATO Communications and Information Agency
 (NCIA). A further US$3.1m has been allocated to support additional testing by individual nations and NATO commands. 

The marketplace establishes a catalogue for Allies to
 directly procure counter-drone systems
, skipping complex and administration-heavy tender processes. Five suppliers have been selected for multi-award counter-drone framework contracts covering a mix of systems. 

“We are committed to making affordable, next-generation air defence capabilities available at the scale Allied countries need,” says
 Hadrien Canter
, CEO and co-founder of
 Alta Ares
, a selected supplier.

Omnea Summit Puts Procurement 100 Floors above Manhattan
Omnea's annual summit takes place in New York in November (Credit: Omnea)
Omnea
 will host its Annual Summit '26 on Thursday 19 November at One World Observatory, bringing procurement and finance leaders together on the 102nd floor of One World Trade Center.

Sponsored by KPMG, one of Omnea's partners, the evening is built around one idea: procurement is becoming the heart of the business, and the teams already running agents are pulling ahead.

The venue, One World Observatory, offers uninterrupted 360-degree views across New York City.

The entrance sits on the West Plaza at the corner of West Street and Vesey Street in Lower Manhattan, which makes it easy to reach.

Jabil Unites Five Firms in New Procurement Services Offering
Jabil launches integrated procurement & supply chain services portfolio (Credit: Jabil)
Jabil,
 a leading engineering, supply chain and manufacturing solutions provider, has launched its integrated Procurement & Supply Chain Services portfolio, bringing together Retronix, ProcureAbility, ID8 Global, Velocity Electronics and Rebound Electronics.

A trusted partner to the world’s leading brands, Jabil helps deliver engineering, supply chain and manufacturing solutions. With 60 years of experience across industries and more than 120 sites worldwide, it combines global reach with local expertise to provide solutions that are both scalable and tailored to each customer.

Jabil is committed to building sustainable processes that reduce environmental impact and support vibrant, diverse communities around the globe.

Volkswagen, NXP Strengthen Semiconductor Supply Resilience
Karsten Schnake, Member of the Board of Management for Procurement at Volkswagen Group (Credit: Volkswagen)
Volkswagen Group
 and NXP Semiconductors are joining forces through a strategic partnership that will see NXP's S32K5 family of microcontrollers (MCUs) become an important building block of the automotive manufacturer's future software-defined vehicle (SDV) architecture.

The partnership will support zonal control across all Volkswagen Group brands and vehicle segments, with the first vehicles featuring the new architecture currently planned for 2027.

The agreement forms part of Volkswagen Group's broader semiconductor strategy, which aims to strengthen supply resilience and accelerate the development of key technologies for future vehicle generations.

Through direct, long-term partnerships with leading semiconductor companies, Volkswagen Group intends to combine its automotive expertise with cutting-edge chip technology, creating greater supply chain resilience and opportunities to jointly shape future innovations.

Procurious: AI & Digital Fluency Priority Procurement Skills
Tania Seary, Founder of Procurious
Latest research from Procurious, an online business network for a new generation of procurement and supply chain professionals, reveals that procurement teams are facing critical
 training and capability gaps
. 

Gaps that could threaten a business’s ability to reduce costs, drive productivity and mitigate supply disruption. The report found that over 90% of procurement leaders say skills shortfalls are already impacting their ability to deliver on business objectives. 

“The procurement function has a generational opportunity to shape the business and economic decisions of the next decade, but businesses must invest in the people expected to lead it. Too often, capability development prioritises sales and revenue growth, leaving those managing cost, risk and compliance under-resourced and under-skilled,” says Tania Seary, Founder of Procurious. 

“Closing the gap requires more than static curricula and occasional training. Procurement professionals need ongoing learning that keeps pace with changing markets, supply chains and technology, informed by what peers are trying and which approaches are delivering results.”

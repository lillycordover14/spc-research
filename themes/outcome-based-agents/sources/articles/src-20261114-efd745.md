Before Saturday, we look at the top storylines going into UFC 334. Today: the rocket ship rise of Josh Hokit.
 
Unexpected.
 
If there’s one word to describe the run by Josh Hokit, it is unexpected. How else would you describe going from unsigned to potentially undisputed heavyweight champion of the world in less than two years this Saturday.
 
The former Division 1 wrestler and NFL hopeful went into MMA in 2023 as the last prospect to be recruited by the old Bellator regime. In fact, he made his MMA debut in the opening fight of Bellator 300.

Hokit’s contract took a confusing course following the acquisition of the company by the PFL, with him fulfilling one more fight before being released to pursue the UFC track. He recorded four fights in 2025: three in LFA that he won by first round finish and the fourth was his fight on 
Dana White’s Contender Series
 to earn his UFC contract.
 
That fight was 15 months ago.
 
What’s come since then has been, no exaggeration, one of the most meteoric and unexpected rises by a UFC contender in the history of the sport. Hokit recorded one fight in November of 2025, but it was following his January bout earlier this year that his loud persona really gained steam. Behind the brashness of “The Incredible Hok” (or “Down Vato” depending on the day) Hokit has become can’t miss television during fight week. Crass, unapologetic, and yes sometimes downright politically incorrect, Hokit’s verbal assaults when holding the microphone have garnered a huge following for his fights. Whether it be to cheer him on for his commitment to bringing entertainment or they wish to see him silenced in brutal fashion, fans have made it clear: they want to see Hokit.
 
It would all be for nought if it wasn’t for what he could do inside the cage though. He opened the year with a first round finish to set up a leap of epic proportions. In just his third UFC fight and just months into his UFC run, he went from unranked to fighting the perennially top ranked former title challenger Curtis Blaydes. In a true trial by fire moment, Hokit engaged in one of the best fights of the year to upset Blaydes and announced himself as a breakout contender in the heavyweight division.

Timing is everything, and Hokit was in the right place at the right time.
 
Seated cageside for his fight was the United States President Donald Trump who was impressed as anyone else following Hokit’s performance. Per UFC CEO Dana White, the President inquired about Derrick Lewis’ absence from the White House card and the opportunity materialized to add Hokit vs Lewis to the event before Hokit was sent to the hospital following the Blaydes fight.
 
Facing the UFC’s all-time knockout leader on the most seminal of all nights in the sport, Hokit turned in a virtuoso performance to shut down Lewis and secure the second round finish in front of a worldwide audience. Per his own admission, it raised his celebrity profile more than ever before.
 
Which brings us to this Saturday’s opportunity. The persistent injury to undisputed champion Tom Aspinall led to the UFC looking to make a fight to crown a new champion in his absence. Following the victory by Ciryl Gane on the White House card to win the interim title, “Bon Gamin” was always guaranteed to be one half of the equation. But following his all-time great run in 2026, Hokit has been granted the opportunity to make history with the fastest run to a UFC title in the modern era.

It would be quite the moment if Hokit won the title, to be sure. But even better for the promotion is that he may not even be close to done. He’s unleashed a barrage for months against UFC megastar Alex Pereira who has his own fight this December. Should Pereira also be victorious in his own fight it would could make collision against Hokit one of the biggest fights to be made in 2027.
 
Could Hokit-mania get any bigger? He’s proven this year that the answer is always yes.

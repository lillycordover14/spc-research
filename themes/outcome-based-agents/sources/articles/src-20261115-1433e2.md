Right now all eyes are on 
Operation Vivaldi 
and the battle to push the Russians away from the Ukrainian “Fortress Belt” onto the eastern banks of the 
Zherebets 
river and the larger 
Siverskyi Donets
 river. Rightly so. Putin has and continues to make the demand for the surrender/capture of all of Donetsk oblast his 
conditio sine qua non 
for ending the war. But in what now seems like a long time ago, the Russian war criminal, 
Igor “Strelkov” Girkin
, from his prison cell somewhere in Russia, argued that while Putin and the Russian Top Brass are fixated on Donetsk Oblast, the war will really be won or lost in Zaporizhia oblast.

And he continues to insist that it is from Zaporizhia that Ukraine(as it tried earlier in the 2023 Summer counteroffensive) could unravel and unwind the entire Russian misadventure in Ukraine. Away from the media spotlight which is largely fixed on Donetsk, the steady back and forth bloody grinding battle for Zaporizhia oblast continues. Girkin identifies Zaporizhia as the decisive theater of the war because he believes it is the only location where breaking through of Russian lines would open up the rest of occupied Ukraine. He argues that 
Pokrovsk
 and the rest of 
Donetsk
 oblast have become 
“resource-sucking bottomless pits” 
for Russia … draining manpower and equipment without achieving strategic goals. The still ongoing 745 Square Km “
Zaporizhzhia Run
“(also referred to as the 
Oleksandrivka counteroffensive
) in which Ukraine with seeming relative ease pushed the Russians out of Dnipropetrovsk oblast and the adjoining area of Donetsk and Zaporizhia oblasts … “
the Intersection
“(as former Ukrainian CinC Sirskyi called it) … is a case in point:

Ukraine’s 300 Km “Zaporizhzhia Run” On War’s 4th Anniversary Shocks Russia

In fighting reminiscent of the Ukrainian advances around Kharkiv and Kherson in late 2022, Ukraine’s forces have optimized what was originally some tactical opportunities and turned them into what is now widely called a counteroffensive. 

Having pushed the Russians back 300 kilometers in Zaporizhzhia and Dnipropetrovsk oblasts – an advance being called ‘The Zaporizhzhia Run,” 

Ukraine has not only disrupted a Russian gain that began last summer but has forced the Kremlin to redeploy forces from other sectors including Pokrovsk. Though much of the credit for the Ukrainian success has been attributed to the Russians’ loss of Starlink and Telegram access, the reality appears to be that the Russian ‘advances’ were really more like probing reconnaissance actions by small units, not territory gained and held by substantial units. When viewed against the backdrop of the Kremlin’s well publicized difficulties in recruiting new troops, these Ukrainian attacks appear to signal a further degradation of Russian military capabilities.

“When viewed against the backdrop of the Kremlin’s well publicized difficulties in recruiting new troops, these Ukrainian attacks appear to signal a further degradation of Russian military capabilities”.
 This is what Girkin sees happening to Russia on a large scale … a looming future Russian defeat in Zaporizhia and indeed in the war. He is a former Russian FSB Colonel, a war criminal now imprisoned in Russia but Girkin has proven to be an astute analyst and observer of this war and Russia’s serial mistakes and lost opportunities.

Girkin’s strategic assessment rests on three key points:

He contends that if Ukrainian forces can get around and behind the fortified Russian defensive lines in central to western Zaporizhia, the rest of Ukraine will become “wide open” to further advances. It’s a simply formulation: 
Zaporizhia falls, Kherson falls. Kherson falls, Crimea Falls … as the dominoes begin to fall … 
what I call “
Strelkov’s prophecy
“.

He sees Russia lacking the equipped and trained reserves necessary to stop a breakthrough in this sector, noting that transferring poorly trained units from other areas would only weaken other fronts. Girkin warns of Russian defensive lines being undermanned and exhausted. That Russian forces in the Zaporizhia sector have not been adequately replenished. He argues that the Kremlin’s leadership is making the situation worse. Russia’s winter 2026 offensive, which aimed at a 30 km breakthrough near 
Huliaipole
, has largely stalled. As of late October, Russian forces still have 
not breached the first Ukrainian defensive line
 in that sector, despite months of glide-bomb strikes.

Ukrainian defenses are actually being strengthened and beefed up. They have learned from their own debacle in the Summer of 2023. As a result Ukraine has quickly built 
five separate defensive lines
 in Zaporizhia, in roughly six months, in an area that previously had almost no prepared fortifications. These include anti-tank obstacles, concrete fortifications, minefields, specialized 
yegoza
 spiral razor wire barrier and drone-integrated artillery positions.

Girkin also criticizes Russian high command for focusing on what he considers to be secondary tactical victories in 
Donetsk
 and 
Kupyansk
, arguing that the main effort should have been directed at the Zaporizhia “intersection” to achieve a decisive result rather than continuing to grind down in the 
Pokrovsk
 area.

In sum Girkin argues that Zaporizhia holds this strategic linchpin status because a successful Ukrainian offensive there could collapse the Russian strategic position entirely, whereas success in other sectors would only yield limited tactical gains.

And recent events in Zaporizhia seem to confirm what Girkin has feared for the Russian effort. Try as the Russians might, they have not, in nearly three full years, been able to capture the strategic cross-roads town of 
Orikhiv 
or the nearby village of 
Mala Tokmachka
. Mala Tokmachka, quite apart from its strategic value, is also symbolic because it was the jump off point for Ukraine’s ill-fated Summer 2023 counteroffensive. That little village still stands Ukrainian despite numerous Russian attempts to capture it. But the prospects for Russia’s overall Zaporizhia effort point to it increasingly heading sideways … as the French OSINT analyst  
Clément Molin
 outlines:

The Ukrainian Armed Forces Have Achieved A Major Victory On The Zaporizhzhia Front

The Russian offensive has been virtually halted.

A new update to the map of airstrikes in the Hulyaypole and Orekhov directions indicates that Russia has abandoned its plans to capture Ternovate and has shifted its focus entirely to advancing westward. This is reported in a blog post 
reputable OSINT analyst 
Clément Molin
 writes in his blog.

It appears that Russia has abandoned its attempts to capture Ternovate. This is a major victory for Ukraine, as this settlement serves as an important defensive line, hindering the advance of Russian troops on the northern flank of the offensive. In addition, the settlement has several multi-story buildings that Ukrainian forces can use for cover.

Advancing north now appears extremely difficult due to the Ukrainian counteroffensive in the Oleksandrivka direction. As a result, Russian troops are forced to fight in low-lying areas without controlling the high ground.

https://charter97.org/en/news/2026/10/25/703153

Today[November 14, 2026] the Ukrainian Armed Forces General Staff battlefield report states that: “
On the Orikhiv sector
. 
The occupiers did not carry out any assaults
.” That is just stunning. No assaults on what is supposed to be one of Russians’ major offensive fronts? A location which also just happens to be a relatively short distance from what used to be a key Russian logistical base at 
Polohy
?  Polohy was all that until Ukraine’s 
mid-range drone campaign
. All of which, in my humble opinion speaks to what Girkin predicted about Russian lines becoming …
“undermanned and exhausted”.
 Lacking any real ability to punch through the Ukrainian defenses, the Russians are now left with making preposterous claims of capturing settlements that are far away from the actual Orikhiv sector front line. Assertions that have been quickly refuted by ADAPTIVE INDOMITABLE Ukraine, of course:

Voskresenka in Zaporizhzhia Region remains under Ukrainian control despite Russian claims, military says

Russian claims that they have captured Voskresenka in the Zaporizhzhia region are false. The settlement remains under the control of the Ukrainian Defense Forces.

The South Group of Forces told the Center that Voskresenka is located more than 20 kilometers from the line of contact. To confirm this, service members of the 142nd Separate Mechanized Brigade recorded a video directly in the settlement and raised the Ukrainian flag there.

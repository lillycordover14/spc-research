Sunday Overreactions: What would Penguins rebuild mean for Crosby?&nbsp;&nbsp;Sportsnet.ca

[page text]
Sidney Crosby redirects Evgeni Malkin's one-timer past Darcy Kuemper to extend Penguins' lead moving past Marcel Dionne into sole possession of sixth place in NHL's all-time points list.

It appears the 
Pittsburgh Penguins
 are finally ready to concede the inevitable: it’s time to rebuild. 

After a few years of trying to walk a tightrope of getting younger while staying competitive — which included a surprising trip to the playoffs last season — the writing is finally on the wall.

The Pens currently sit second from the bottom in the Eastern Conference and 10 points out of a playoff spot, with a core who are all pushing 40 years of age. It would be foolish for general manager Kyle Dubas not to take this opportunity to look at resetting his roster and seeing what he could get for some of his veteran players on the trade market. That said, the decision is complicated somewhat by the presence of 
Sidney Crosby
, who at this point has given no indication he’d be willing to leave the only franchise he’s ever known. You can bet that as long as Crosby is around, he’ll want the team to do everything it can to win.

Even at his age, it would be a mistake to doubt Crosby’s abilities and competitive spirit, but ultimately Dubas cashing in on some of his assets is probably the best course of action for the future health of the franchise.

A full Penguins rebuild could lead to Crosby finishing his career elsewhere

Dubas has delayed a rebuild in Pittsburgh for quite some time, though there are a few reasons why he may have little choice other than to finally move forward with it now. A glaring one is the status of Erik Karlsson, who is still nearly a point-per-game player but in the final year of his contract and turning 37 in May. It would be counterproductive for the Pens not to cash in on a decent return for him prior to the deadline, and it sounds like Karlsson would be amenable to a trade.

Evgeni Malkin is 40, and this very well could be the final season for a player that Crosby has been attached to for nearly his entire career. Same goes for Kris Letang, who will be 40 in the spring and has one more year on his deal after this one. It seems highly unlikely that Letang will play beyond his current contract. Other veterans like Bryan Rust, Rickard Rakell and Samuel Girard could bring back some decent assets as well.

Pittsburgh has already started to get younger, and that trend figures to continue as we approach the trade deadline and into the off-season. When that happens, it tends to take several years before things start turning around. Just ask the Chicago Blackhawks. Crosby will have to decide how much longer he wants to play and if he wants to finish out his career on a team that’s likely going to be spending a fair amount of time near the bottom of the standings. This may not be something he’s interested in.

Overreaction? Yes

We know what type of competitor Crosby is, so logic would tell us he wants to get back into the Stanley Cup mix, but there may be other things that are just as important to him. Playing for one franchise is likely something that Crosby values immensely, and everything we’ve ever heard from him is that he wants to retire a Penguin. Crosby is also approaching the top five on the all-time scoring list, and he could continue to climb up the ranks during the final few years of his career. In a perfect world, Crosby would probably want to reach those milestones in a Penguins jersey.

It's also important to note that Crosby could still have opportunities to scratch that competitive itch even if the Penguins are rebuilding. The return of best-on-best hockey means there’s a World Cup of Hockey next season and another Olympics a couple of years after that. Crosby isn’t showing many signs of slowing down and he could use the 2030 Olympics as a motivator to continue to play at a high level in order to try to crack the team.

Going out by mentoring a group of young Penguins and trying to win another gold medal would be a pretty good cherry on top of an incredible career.

Flyers can’t afford to lose Michkov

It seems we can barely get through a few days anymore without a big-name player requesting a trade. 
Matvei Michkov
 was the latest talent to make headlines when it was reported that he wanted a fresh start with another team. 
Philadelphia Flyers
 general manager Daniel Briere says he hasn’t received a trade request from Michkov’s camp and he has no plans to trade his young star. Still, the relationship between Michkov and the Flyers hasn’t been without bumps in the road. Head coach Rick Tocchet called out Michkov’s lack of conditioning in the past, and we recently saw a heated exchange between Michkov and Nick Seeler on the ice.

Michkov appears to be making great strides this season, so it would be a nightmare to have to give away all the progress he’s made.

Overreaction? No

The Flyers need to do everything they can to smooth things over with Michkov. If he were to want out, it would be another misstep from this management group as it relates to promising young players. They were forced to trade 
Cutter Gauthier
 a few years ago when he became disgruntled, and now he’s developed into an incredible goal scorer in Anaheim. Philly also put all its off-season eggs in one basket but failed to secure Leo Carlsson on an offer sheet, leaving it very thin at centre.

Losing Michkov this early in his career feels like something the Flyers would really regret. He’s still only 21 years old, already has a 63-point season under his belt and is on pace for 42 goals. Michkov is the exact type of player the Flyers don’t have enough of, and being forced to trade him would be nothing short of a disaster.

Oilers have solved their goaltending issues

The Edmonton Oilers are a very dangerous team when they’re getting some saves. Both Tristan Jarry and Devon Levi are providing them right now, and the past few outings from each of them have been sparkling. Levi has a .959 save percentage in November after Saturday’s shutout and owns victories in his past five starts. In fact, Levi now boasts an 9-1-0 record on the season, with his only blemish coming against the high-powered Utah Mammoth. Meanwhile, Jarry has only allowed five goals in his past four starts and has two shutouts over that stretch. The Oilers struggled to find any consistency out of Jarry a year ago, but he looks like a different goalie of late. Edmonton’s duo has been an overshadowed storyline of the Oilers' hot start to the season, and it appears their goaltending issues are behind them.

Overreaction? Yes

As we know, goalies can run very hot and cold, and Levi’s sample size this season is still very small. Despite his recent success, he’s still had a few rough outings that the Oilers were able to overcome, which included allowing seven goals against the lowly Vancouver Canucks. Levi has only appeared in 49 career NHL games, so it’s still premature to say he’s ready to handle half of the starts and produce consistently strong numbers. Plus, Jarry hasn’t faced the toughest competition thus far, as four of his five wins have come against the Flames, Islanders, Flyers and Sharks.

That said, adding Levi was still a great bet by the Oilers, who is providing incredible value on a low-risk contract. Edmonton really has nothing to lose where Levi is concerned and should be riding this wave as long as possible. Add in Jarry’s strong play and the eventual return of Frederik Andersen, and the Oilers crease is at least in better shape than it has been in quite some time.

Cutter Gauthier is now the NHL’s best goal scorer

Nobody has an answer for Cutter Gauthier right now. The 
Anaheim Ducks
 forward has been virtually unstoppable in his past eight games, scoring 15 times and notching three hat tricks. He’s clearly become an elite goal scorer and his $13.5-million salary is going to seem like a bargain if Gauthier continues to fill the net regularly. This has to be frustrating for the Flyers to watch, as they were forced to trade Gauthier to the Ducks and couldn’t secure Carlsson this summer on an offer sheet. That duo figures to give teams fits for the next decade, and we could be talking about Gauthier as the league’s next great goal-scorer.

Overreaction? No

Gauthier is still a goal off the league lead, but it really feels like there’s now no one better at finding the back of the net than him. Whether it’s Carlsson or even Mikael Granlund playing with him down the middle, Gauthier has a couple of great centres to set him up for goals on a regular basis. Let’s also keep in mind that heading into Saturday, the Ducks had the league’s best power play at 36.8 per cent, and Gauthier is going to be a big beneficiary of that when it comes to filling the net. With Alexander Ovechkin retiring and Auston Matthews not scoring at the pace he once did, there’s an opportunity for Gauthier to steal the title of the NHL’s best goal-scorer.

Alexander Ovechkin will fail to hit 20 goals this season

I’m not sure anyone thought they’d see the day when Ovechkin couldn’t score 20 goals in a season. Even at 41 years old, Ovechkin can still fire the puck and remains on the 
Washington Capitals
' top power-play unit, so you’d think he could sleepwalk to 20. As it stands, though, Ovechkin has found the back of the net just once and is only on pace for five goals this season. The future Hall of Famer’s lowest goal total in a season came in the 2020-21 campaign when he had 24, but that came in just 45 games. It would be foolish to doubt Ovechkin at this point given everything he’s done in his career, though it feels like a long shot that he’ll be able to catch fire and get to 20 this season.

Overreaction? No

Ovechkin would have to get on a crazy hot streak at this point to hit 20 goals. It’s not impossible, but Ovechkin’s role has been drastically reduced. He’s now playing in the Capitals' bottom six and receiving well under 14 minutes per game, the lowest ice time of his career by a mile. That’s down almost four minutes from last year alone. Ovechkin is also on pace for only 149 shots, which would be the fewest of his career by a wide margin. The only other time Ovechkin failed to hit 200 shots was that abbreviated 45-game season. Maybe Ovechkin has one final offensive explosion to close out his career, but it’s going to take some real magic for him to get to 20 goals at this pace.

Karen Lee (Crosby) Lichtenwalter

WATERVILLE – Karen Lee (Crosby) Lichtenwalter passed away peacefully on Oct. 30, 2026, in Waterville, Maine.

She was born and raised in Friday Harbor, Wash., the oldest of four children of Leonard and Margaret Crosby. She attended Friday Harbor schools, graduating in 1963 as Class Valedictorian and winner of the Betty Crocker Homemaker Award. She treasured her childhood and often spoke fondly of growing up “on the Island.”

After high school she briefly attended college, then moved to Seattle to enter the workforce. A cute guy with a funny name who lived in her apartment building asked her out, and she was smitten. Four months later they tied the knot, in a decision she recalled as the best one of her life. They were married for 56 years, until Bob passed away in 2024.

She raised her children while also working full-time as a secretary at Boeing. She was good at her job and was proud of it, but she was also devoted to her family, cooking dinner every night after a full day at work. She was a lifelong reader who always had a stack of library books close at hand, and an excellent speller who never hesitated to correct others. She enjoyed playing Scrabble and Trivial Pursuit, and was the undisputed family Jeopardy champion. In her retirement years she always had a puzzle in progress on a table at home.

Karen had many talents, including sewing, gardening, singing, and playing piano. She had a green thumb, and cared for some of her houseplants for more than 40 years and multiple moves. She joined the running boom of the 1980’s, and was a dedicated runner for most of her life, continuing with daily walks after running became too difficult.

She was naturally shy, but happy to socialize with Bob at her side. During their working years they lived in various places in Washington including Seattle, Kent, Fife, Port Townsend, and Enumclaw. They also spent time in Phoenix, Ariz., Greenville, N.C., Hot Springs, Ark., and Kansas City, Kan. Everywhere they lived, they took time to explore and learn about the area.

Upon retirement they moved to Waterville, Maine, to be near their grandchildren. Throughout their marriage Bob gave her the encouragement and confidence to try new things, including flying (she obtained her private pilot’s license in 1980), road biking, golf, and giving speeches at the local Toastmasters club.

She adored cats, spicy food, jellybeans, and being a grandmother. She never missed her grandchildren’s sporting events, concerts, and birthday parties, and was always available to babysit at a moment’s notice when they were small. She especially enjoyed her yearly tradition of making decorated cookies at Christmastime with family. She will be remembered for her unconditional love for her children and grandchildren, and for her long and loving marriage.

Karen was predeceased by her husband, Bob Lichtenwalter; her parents; her siblings Bob Crosby and Marilyn Crosby, and her brother-in-law and sister-in-law, Don and Betty Werner.

She is survived by her son, Bob (and Tawnya) Lichtenwalter, daughter, Brenda (and Christine) Nichols; sister, Wendy Emmons, sister-in-law Carol (and Lou) Kish, sister-in-law Amy Crosby; many nieces and nephews; and her granddaughters Emily and Kate Nichols.

At Karen’s request there will be no service. The family would like to thank the Maine General Hospice nurses, especially Julie, and the wonderful staff at Woodlands Senior Living in Waterville for their care and attention in her final days.

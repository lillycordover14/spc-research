[page text]
Comedy writer Harper Steele says she’s been “surprised” by a couple of things since beginning her transition in 2022.

“It takes me longer to get out of the house; that can sometimes be annoying,” she told Page Six with a laugh at the New York City 
premiere of “The Good Daughter”
 on Tuesday.

On a more serious note, the former “SNL” head writer, 65, noted that her life since transitioning is now infinitely better.

“Without being too Pollyanna(ish), I do wake up joyful every day, which I didn’t before, that surprises me. It still surprises me,” she shared.

Steele starred in the 2024 documentary “Will & Harper,” which documented a 17-day road trip Steele and pal Will Ferrell took across the country after Steele completed her transition. 

The film — which began after Steele came out as transgender in an email to the “Elf” star in 2021 — was critically acclaimed and won a Peabody Award.

“For me, it was such a labor of love,” the screenwriter explained, noting that she is constantly approached by parents with queer kids who have watched the documentary and say it helped them explore those things together.

“And that’s the most empowering thing,” she continued. “Like I write a lot of silly comedies, and I don’t get that kind of response…I’m very proud of it.”

In “The Good Daughter,” the Emmy winner plays a trans character who is “best friends with the father of the story” and acts as a maternal figure for his friend’s two daughters, played by Rose Byrne and Meghann Fahy.

“I have children, so it’s a role I felt I could take on,” she said before adding, “This is my first acting gig, so yeah, you have to tell me how I did!”

“The Good Daughter” is based on the novel of the same name, written by Karin Slaughter. The six-episode limited series follows the Quinn sisters (Byrne and Fahy) as they try to piece together a violent attack on their family more than 20 years later.

It’s available to stream now on Peacock.

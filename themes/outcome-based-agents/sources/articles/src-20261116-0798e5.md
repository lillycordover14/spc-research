Second-year guard Dylan Harper is finally starting and is already showing the San Antonio Spurs what they were missing last season. Harper is currently averaging 18.3 points, 5.5 rebounds, and 4.7 assists, while establishing himself as the Spurs' second-best player. 

Although many suspected that he would eventually get there, Harper has already surpassed De'Aaron Fox and Stephon Castle in the pecking order. He's done that by being a relentless scorer who attacks whenever he has the chance and is less concerned with setting up teammates.

Harper lets Castle and Fox handle the playmaking responsibilities, and that has worked like a charm, 
helping the starting five thrive.
 Had coach Mitch Johnson started Harper as a rookie, it's possible that he would be even farther along in his development.

Then again, having him come off the bench last season gave him a chance to get acclimated to the NBA without being thrown into the deep end first.

Either way, Harper is the perfect hybrid of Fox and Castle in the sense that he is a lefty like Fox and has plenty of craft as a finisher but has more size. He can easily get to the basket like Castle, though he doesn't share his tendency to make boneheaded mistakes. 

We saw that on full display against the Utah Jazz. Harper went off, scoring a career-high 31 points and grabbing nine rebounds while the Spurs picked up a crucial win on the road. In fact, he scored eight points in the final five minutes, helping the Silver and Black win their 12th straight game. 

Having Harper primarily be a bucket getter helps change the dynamic of the starting lineup by forcing defenses to actively guard every position. That's not to say that Julian Champagnie didn't, but there is a difference between guarding a spot-up shooter and defending a 6'6" guard who can get to the rim at will.

Dylan Harper is still growing into his new role

As Harper gets more comfortable in his role, he can see his ass scoring increase. He has already seen an uptick in 3-point shooting. If he can get that shot to fall consistently, then he could emerge as an All-Star candidate this season. 

From a team perspective, starting Harper has also been the right move. They have the best starting lineup in basketball, even while they have struggled at times to knock down threes. Once their shooting normalizes, they could be all the more dangerous.

Harper could be the catalyst with him constantly exploiting mismatches and putting pressure on defenses. Teams are already hyperfocused on Victor Wembanyama, while Castle and Fox also command defensive attention. 

Thus, Harper has benefited from that divided defensive attention, and his elite finishing at the basket has helped him and the Spurs take the next step.

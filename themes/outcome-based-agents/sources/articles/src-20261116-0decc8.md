ADA joins coalition urging Congress to maintain federal tobacco control funding 

 

by 
Olivia Anderson

November 16, 2026

 
 

 

The American Dental Association is signing onto a coalition letter urging congressional appropriators to maintain federal funding for tobacco prevention and control programs in the final fiscal year 2027 appropriations legislation. 

The letter, addressed to congressional appropriations leaders, requested at least $246.5 million for the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s tobacco control program and $712 million in user fees for the Food and Drug Administration’s oversight of tobacco products. 

The coalition also asked lawmakers to include language requiring agencies to maintain adequate staffing to carry out funded programs and to strengthen enforcement against unauthorized tobacco products, particularly those marketed to youth. 

“Tobacco use is the leading preventable cause of death in the United States, responsible for nearly 500,000 deaths each year,” the letter reads, noting that more than 23 million Americans smoke cigarettes and that more than 2 million youth reported using a tobacco product last year. 

The coalition points to the CDC’s tobacco control program as an important resource for reducing tobacco use through grants and technical assistance to state programs, public education campaigns, support for state quitlines and data collection on tobacco use trends. 

The letter expressed support for the $246.5 million included for the CDC tobacco program in the FY 2027 House Labor, Health and Human Services, Education, and Related Agencies appropriations bill. It also raises concerns about staffing at the CDC’s Office on Smoking and Health. 

“Despite legislative language in the enacted FY 2026 LHHS bill requiring the Department of Health and Human Services to provide adequate staffing levels to carry out funded programs and activities, CDC’s Office on Smoking and Health (OSH) remains shuttered,” the coalition said. 

The letter said the lack of dedicated staff has limited the CDC’s ability to support state tobacco control programs, run its Tips from Former Smokers public education campaign in all 50 states, and analyze and disseminate tobacco-related information. The coalition is asking Congress to include staffing requirements in the FY 2027 legislation. 

The letter also called for $712 million in FDA tobacco user fees, the authorized funding level included in the FY 2027 House Agriculture, Rural Development, Food and Drug Administration, and Related Agencies appropriations bill. 

In addition, the coalition urged lawmakers to include language strengthening enforcement against unauthorized tobacco products, including unauthorized e-cigarettes. The letter noted that many of these products come in flavors that appeal to young people. 

“Preventing youth from starting to use tobacco products and helping adults who use tobacco to quit saves lives and money,” according to the letter. “It will help advance shared goals of reducing chronic disease and protecting children’s health.” 

The requests are consistent with the ADA’s long-standing support for tobacco prevention and cessation efforts and federal public health programs.

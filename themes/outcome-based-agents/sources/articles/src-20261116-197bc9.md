WTI Price Forecast: Is the 200-EMA/50% Fibo. Decisive as Bears Appear Hesitant Above $87.00?

Fresh selling weighs on 
WTI
 on Monday, but further losses appear limited.
Uncertainty in the Middle East keeps crude oil prices supported.
For bears, a break beneath the confluence of the 200-day EMA and 50% Fibo. remains key.
Selling pressure has left West Texas Intermediate (WTI), the US Crude Oil benchmark, trading just above $87.00, around 0.75% lower for the day. However, its weaker start to the week has seen little follow-through. Prices continue to move within the range established over the past week or so, with traders holding off on fresh directional positions until there is greater clarity on developments in the Middle East.
TMGM Analysis: Financial Market News, Economic Calendar & Market Insights
Crude oil prices might retain support from a geopolitical risk premium as the US-Iran standoff and tensions surrounding the Strait of Hormuz persist. On Sunday, Iran carried out a drone attack on a US military base in Bahrain. Separately, a Houthi military spokesperson alleged that Saudi warplanes had conducted 75 airstrikes and missile attacks on civilian areas during the preceding 24 hours. The Navy of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) also said several oil tankers had caught fire after striking sea mines in the southern no-go zone of the Strait of Hormuz.
Near-term technical support comes from the 200-day Exponential Moving Average (EMA) at $84.84, close to the 50% Fibonacci retracement level, with crude oil prices holding slightly above it. However, the 38.2% Fibo. level at $89.92 continues to limit gains, leaving the outlook neutral-to-bearish following the recent retreat from above $100. Momentum indicators also point to fading upside strength: the Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) remains negative and below zero, while the Relative Strength Index (RSI) trades around 40.
A stronger case for further declines would therefore require a break beneath the 50% retracement at $85.58, with the 200-period EMA at $84.84 providing additional support just below. Such a move would bring the 61.8% Fibo. level at $81.24 into focus, followed by $75.06 and $67.19 should selling persist. Any recovery would first encounter resistance at the 38.2% Fibo. retracement of $89.92. Beyond that lies a stronger hurdle at the 23.6% level near $95.29, while the cycle high reference around $103.97 remains a more distant ceiling.

WTI Oil: Key Questions Answered

What does WTI Oil refer to?

Among the crude oil varieties traded internationally, WTI, short for West Texas Intermediate, ranks as one of the three major types alongside 
Brent
 and Dubai Crude. Its relatively low gravity and sulphur content account for the descriptions “light” and “sweet”, respectively. Regarded as a high-quality oil, it is easy to refine. Produced in the United States, WTI is distributed through the Cushing hub, known as “The Pipeline Crossroads of the World”. WTI serves as an oil-market benchmark, and its price is regularly cited in media reports.

What determines WTI Oil prices?

Supply and demand underpin WTI Oil prices, as they do prices of other assets. Stronger global growth can lift demand, whereas weaker growth can have the reverse effect. On the supply side, political instability, wars and sanctions can cause disruptions that affect prices. Decisions by OPEC, the group of major oil-producing countries, are another important influence. Since oil trades predominantly in US Dollars, movements in the currency also matter for WTI Crude Oil prices: a weaker US Dollar can make oil more affordable, while a stronger dollar can have the opposite effect.

Why do inventory figures affect WTI Oil prices?

WTI Oil prices respond to the weekly oil inventory releases from the American Petroleum Institute (API) and the Energy Information Agency (EIA), as stock movements indicate shifts in supply and demand. Falling inventories can signal stronger demand and lift oil prices; rising inventories can suggest greater supply and weigh on prices. The API releases its report on Tuesdays, followed by the EIA the next day. The two reports generally produce similar results, with the figures within 1% of one another 75% of the time. As the EIA is a government agency, its data is regarded as more reliable.

How do OPEC decisions affect WTI Oil prices?

Production quotas agreed by OPEC (Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries) often influence WTI Oil prices. The group comprises 12 oil-producing nations, which jointly set quotas for members at meetings held twice a year. A decision to cut quotas can restrict supply and lift oil prices, while an increase in production has the reverse effect. The broader OPEC+ grouping includes ten additional non-OPEC members, with Russia the most notable among them.

Amazon blocks ZIP code targeting for housing ads in US, Canada, EU and UK&nbsp;&nbsp;PPC Land

[page text]
Amazon Ads has set out a dedicated real estate policy in its Support Center that switches off ZIP code and postal code targeting, along with audiences built on age, gender, marital status and parental status, for ads promoting a specific home for sale or rent in the United States, Canada, the European Union and the United Kingdom. The page, titled "Real estate", carries an update date of November 15, 2026.
It also draws a map of where property advertising may and may not appear across Amazon's own estate. Seven placements are closed to the category, including the Amazon homepage, Brand Stores, lockers, in-store kiosks and on-package ads. Twelve are open, among them Prime Video and streaming TV, Twitch, Fire TV, Alexa, IMDb, Goodreads and the open internet. National sections add a licence number requirement in Ireland, energy ratings in Ireland and Poland, labels on floor plans in Japan, a default ban on developers' off-plan sales ads in Egypt, and limits on implied official endorsement in Singapore.
What the page does not show is what changed. There is no changelog, no effective date and no earlier version of the page that PPC Land could locate. Amazon's older creative acceptance policy, which is still online and undated, contains no real estate section at all. The November 15 date is also shared by at least five other pages in the same policy section, among them the Gambling and Games page, which showed a November 12 date when 
PPC Land covered its prediction market rules
 three days earlier.
In Short
Amazon has published rules for real estate ads that stop advertisers from aiming ads for a specific home at people by age, gender, family status or ZIP code in the US, Canada, the EU and the UK. Estate agents, property portals, developers and landlords that buy Amazon ads are affected, because the same page keeps property ads off the Amazon homepage, Brand Stores, lockers and packages while allowing them on Prime Video, Twitch, Fire TV and the open internet. Egypt, Ireland, Japan, Poland and Singapore add requirements of their own, and the page does not say which of these rules, if any, are new.
A policy built on fair housing
The page opens with a one-line summary: "Learn about real estate advertising policies to create compliant campaigns that meet targeting and content restrictions." Its scope is set out in a single sentence. "This section covers ads for housing and real estate search services," according to Amazon. The second half of that phrase matters, because it brings property portals and listing search sites into the policy alongside agents and landlords.
The foundation is legal rather than commercial. "Amazon allows real estate advertising that follows fair housing laws and our Housing, Employment, and Credit (HEC) advertising requirements. These policies ensure fair and inclusive access to housing opportunities," the page states.
That sentence points to a second rulebook. The 
HEC requirements
 are named but not reproduced, and the real estate page does not link to them. Among the 16 related topics it does link to are separate pages titled "Employment and Recruitment" and "Financial products and services", which share subject matter with the other two letters of the acronym. Whether those pages carry the employment and credit parts of the HEC requirements is not stated.
The page did not change alone. Other pages in the same Policies and guidelines section showing the November 15, 2026 date include Animal related products and services, Claims and substantiation, News and journalism, Pricing and Promotions, and Gambling and Games.
Five prohibitions
The core of the page is a list headed "Prohibited products, services, and content." It has five entries, reproduced here as published.

Prohibited item

Wording on the page

Predatory ads

"Ads that prey upon financial vulnerability."

Personal-characteristic targeting

"Targeting based on personal characteristics (such as age, gender, marital status, or parental status)."

Granular location targeting

"Highly granular geographic targeting such as ZIP or postal codes targeting."

Demographic signals for listings

"Demographic audience signals for Individual housing opportunities (please review Targeting Requirements section for additional details)."

Exclusionary creative

"Language or imagery that suggests preference, exclusion, or discouragement of any group."

Three of the five concern targeting, one concerns creative and one concerns the commercial character of the offer. None of the terms is defined. The page does not say what counts as preying upon financial vulnerability, what threshold makes geographic targeting "highly granular" beyond the ZIP code example, or which groups the creative rule protects. The last item is drawn in the widest possible terms: "any group."
Two targeting options removed in four jurisdictions
A separate section, headed "Targeting requirements," begins by placing legal responsibility with the buyer. "Advertisers are responsible for ensuring their campaigns comply with all applicable local laws and regulations," according to Amazon.
It then sets out what the page calls 
restricted targeting
. "The following targeting options are not available for individual housing ads that advertise a specific housing opportunity (for example, a home for sale or rental) in the United States, Canada, the European Union, and the United Kingdom," the page states. Two options follow:
"Audiences based on demographic signals (for example, age, gender, parental status, marital status, or other protected characteristics)"
"Zip code or postal code-level geographic targeting"

The phrase "not available" suggests the options are withdrawn from the buying tools for this ad type, rather than left in place and policed at review. The page does not describe the mechanism, and it does not say how Amazon identifies an ad as an individual housing ad.
Two scopes for one rule
The prohibitions list and the targeting section do not line up neatly. In the prohibitions list, targeting on personal characteristics and ZIP or postal code targeting appear with no geographic limit and no restriction to a type of ad. In the targeting section, the same two options are removed only for ads about a specific property, and only in four jurisdictions. Does a brokerage's brand campaign in Japan, which promotes no particular home, fall under the first reading or outside the second? The page does not reconcile the two.
Several details that other platforms spell out are also missing. There is no minimum radius for location targeting, so it is unclear whether a city-level or radius target is acceptable where a postcode is not. In Canada, where the first three characters of a postal code identify a forward sortation area, the page does not say whether that coarser unit counts as "postal code-level." And the restriction names demographic signals only. Amazon's distinctive audience assets are built on shopping and viewing behaviour, and the page is silent on whether lifestyle or in-market segments drawn from that behaviour may be used for a listing ad.

The pattern is clear enough. The closed list is Amazon's retail and physical footprint: the storefront homepage, brand storefronts, parcel lockers, kiosks, physical shops and the boxes that arrive at customers' doors, plus sponsorships. The open list is Amazon's media business. The abbreviations STV and PVA appear to refer to Streaming TV and Prime Video ads, which the page does not spell out. Fire TV, Amazon's 
connected TV
 platform, is open, as are Alexa and Fire tablets.
Two observations follow from what is absent. 
Sponsored Display
 appears both on Amazon and offsite, but Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands are not named in either list. Nor is Amazon's buying platform itself. The company retired the Amazon DSP name on September 29, 2026, 
when the platform became Amazon Ads Agent
 and the 
demand-side platform
 product became DVA, for display, video and audio. The supply list describes where ads land, not which tool places them.
The open list includes the inventory Amazon has placed at the centre of its pitch to advertisers. Alan Moss, Amazon Ads' vice president of global advertising sales, 
described Prime Video and live sports in May as a single business
, pointing to full-funnel capabilities. Live Sports and STV/PVA are both permitted for real estate.
Country-specific restrictions
The final section lists national rules. Five country sections, Egypt, Ireland, Japan, Poland and Singapore, are reproduced below. The page carries two further sections, headed South Africa and All other markets, whose contents are not covered in this article.

Country

Rule as published

Egypt

"Ads for real estate services and specific dwellings are permitted. Ads promoting the reservation or sale of real estate developments, subdivisions, land plots, or off-plan units by developers are not accepted in Egypt." Exceptions on request with three documents (see below).

Ireland

"Ads for real estate services must display the advertiser's PSRA license number. Residential property listing ads must display a valid Building Energy Rating (BER)."

Japan

"Ads for specific dwellings are permitted on the same placements as the rest of the world with additional content restrictions." Unit-specific imagery, layouts and floor plans must be labelled as samples (see below).

Poland

"Ads for sale/lease of property must include energy performance indicators where an EPC exists." Only officially registered establishments may use the terms "hotel", "motel", "pensjonat" or star ratings.

Singapore

"Ads for licensed property developments must not suggest or imply" presidential patronage, a government connection, or unsubstantiated attributes (see below).

Egypt: developers need paperwork
Egypt has the most restrictive rule on the page. Agents' services and individual homes may be advertised, but developer marketing of projects is refused by default, from off-plan units to land plots. "Advertisers may request an exception by providing," the page states, followed by three documents:
"Valid building permit"
"Ministerial decree approving the general urban plan"
"Ministerial decree approving the detailed urban for relevant development phase"
The third item appears to be missing the word "plan"; it is quoted as published. The page gives no timeline for reviewing an exception request.
Ireland and Poland: energy data in the ad
Ireland's section sets two display requirements. Real estate services ads must carry the advertiser's PSRA licence number, the registration issued by Ireland's Property Services Regulatory Authority. Residential listing ads must show a valid Building Energy Rating. Poland's rule is conditional: energy performance indicators are required "where an EPC exists", meaning an energy performance certificate. Poland's section also restricts accommodation vocabulary. Only officially registered establishments may describe themselves as a "hotel", "motel" or "pensjonat", a Polish guesthouse, or use star ratings, according to the page.
Japan: sample floor plans
Japan's section is the only one to describe the global supply list as a baseline, confirming that specific dwellings may run "on the same placements as the rest of the world." The added content rule is precise. "Ads for specific dwellings must not include unit-specific imagery, layouts, or floor plans unless clearly labeled as 'sample' or 'representative,'" the page states.
Singapore: no borrowed authority
Singapore's rule applies to licensed property developments and lists three things an ad must not suggest or imply:
"Patronage of the President or any member of their family."
"Connection with any government department, statutory body, or public building/place"
"Any attribute to the developer cannot genuinely substantiate."
How the page sequences a real estate ad check
Read in order, the page sets out six layers that a property ad passes through, according to Amazon:
Scope.
 The policy applies to "ads for housing and real estate search services," which must follow fair housing laws and Amazon's HEC advertising requirements.
Content and targeting prohibitions.
 Five items are barred everywhere the page applies: predatory ads, personal-characteristic targeting, granular geographic targeting, demographic signals for individual housing opportunities, and exclusionary language or imagery.
Supply.
 Seven placements are prohibited and 12 are permitted, with a pointer to the country section for further limits.
Restricted targeting for listings.
 For ads about a specific home for sale or rent in the United States, Canada, the European Union and the United Kingdom, demographic audiences and ZIP or postal code targeting are not available.
Country rules.
 National requirements apply in Egypt, Ireland, Japan, Poland and Singapore, with further sections for South Africa and all other markets.
Exceptions.
 In Egypt, developer ads can be accepted if the advertiser supplies a valid building permit and two ministerial decrees approving the general and detailed urban plans.
What changed, and what cannot be established
No earlier version of the real estate page has been found. The comparison that is possible is with Amazon's older policy documents, and it shows how much of this page is new to Amazon's published rules, if not necessarily to its practice.
Amazon's 
creative acceptance policy
, which remains online without a date, has no real estate or housing section. Its nearest provision is a general ban on ad content that advocates or discriminates against a protected group, listing grounds such as race, colour, national origin, religion, disability, sex, sexual orientation and age. It says nothing about targeting. Amazon's 
worldwide quick reference guide for display ads
 does not list housing or real estate among its restricted categories either.
Against that baseline, the November 15 page adds a named real estate category, a targeting restriction tied to four jurisdictions, a placement list and national rules for at least five countries. Whether those rules were already applied through account review or the buying tools before the page was published cannot be established from the page itself. The shared date across at least six policy pages points the same way. The Gambling and Games page carried a November 12 date three days earlier, and its main text is word for word the same under the November 15 date. The stamp therefore marks a revision of the section as a whole, and does not by itself show a change to the real estate text.
How the rules compare with Meta and Google
Restrictions on housing ad targeting are not new to the industry. Meta's 
special ad category
 has required housing advertisers in the US to declare their campaigns since December 4, 2019, following a settlement reached on March 19, 2019. Meta extended the requirement to Canada on December 3, 2020 and to parts of Europe on December 7, 2021. Under Meta's rules, age is fixed at 18 to 65 and over, gender defaults to all, and location targeting must cover at least a 15-mile radius around any chosen point, which rules out ZIP code targeting.
Google's approach is narrower in geography. Google 
clarified on June 3, 2026
 that its housing, employment and consumer finance rules in the United States and Canada bar targeting by gender, age, parental status or marital status, prohibit ZIP code targeting, and require a minimum radius of 1 kilometre, while allowing Canadian forward sortation area targeting.
Amazon's page reaches further geographically than Google's, naming the European Union and the United Kingdom alongside the US and Canada. It is less specific on mechanics: there is no minimum radius, no treatment of forward sortation areas and no list of fixed age bands.
In the EU, the restriction also sits on top of law. The European Data Protection Board 
clarified in September 2025
 that Article 26(3) of the Digital Services Act prohibits presenting ads based on profiling that uses special categories of personal data, such as health, religion or sexual orientation. Age, gender and marital status are not among the GDPR's special categories, so Amazon's rule for EU listing ads covers ground the Digital Services Act provision does not.
Why it matters for advertisers
Real estate is not a category Amazon sells, so its rules govern the media side of the business: streaming, audio, live sports and display across the open internet. That side has grown large. Amazon 
reported advertising services revenue of $19,637 million
 for the third quarter of 2026, up from $17,703 million a year earlier.
For property marketers, the targeting rule cuts against the way housing is usually sold. Homes are local, and a postcode is the most obvious way to reach people near a listing. In the four jurisdictions named, ads for a specific property on Amazon cannot use it, nor can they use age or family status to find likely buyers or renters. The placement list, meanwhile, shuts the category out of the surfaces closest to a purchase on Amazon itself, from the homepage to Brand Stores, while leaving open the streaming and video inventory where a brand campaign for an agency or portal would more naturally sit.
For agencies running campaigns across platforms, the three sets of rules differ in detail. Meta sets a 15-mile radius, Google a 1-kilometre radius, and Amazon names no radius at all. Meta applies its rules in the US, Canada and parts of Europe, Google in the US and Canada, and Amazon in the US, Canada, the EU and the UK.
What the page does not say
The page has no changelog, effective date or transition period, and it does not reproduce or link to the HEC requirements it cites. It does not define "individual housing opportunities," "financial vulnerability" or "highly granular," does not say how listing ads are identified or what happens when a rule is breached, and does not mention Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands or DVA. The South Africa and All other markets sections are not covered here.

Summary
Who:
 Amazon Ads, and the estate agents, property portals, developers, landlords and agencies that advertise real estate on Amazon's media inventory in the United States, Canada, the European Union, the United Kingdom and the markets with country rules.
What:
 A Support Center policy page titled "Real estate" that bars five types of content and targeting, removes demographic audiences and ZIP or postal code targeting for ads about a specific home in four jurisdictions, closes seven Amazon placements to the category while permitting 12, and sets national rules for Egypt, Ireland, Japan, Poland and Singapore.
When:
 The page shows an update date of November 15, 2026. It gives no effective date, and no earlier version has been found.
Where:
 The Policies and guidelines section of the Amazon Ads Support Center, applying to placements from Prime Video, Twitch and Fire TV to the open internet.
Why:
 According to Amazon, the rules exist so that real estate advertising follows fair housing laws and its HEC requirements, which the page says "ensure fair and inclusive access to housing opportunities."

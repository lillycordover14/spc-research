As he stood on a box speaking to a throng of gathered media, the phone of baseball’s highest profile agent went off.
“Just another owner begging to meet with [Tarik] Skubal,” Scott Boras said to laughs.
What are the chances it was 
Cubs
 chairman Tom Ricketts calling?
Though they shelled out $175 million for Boras client Alex Bregman last winter, the Cubs have a reputation — at least among frustrated fans — as a big-market team that doesn’t always spend like one.
Not helping to shatter that narrative, the Cubs launching themselves into the Skubal sweepstakes doesn’t seem likely this winter, even as Jed Hoyer’s front office seeks to remake the North Side pitching staff and get closer to competing with the world-champion Brewers in the NL Central.
Skubal, a two-time Cy Young winner, would be the ultimate top-of-the-rotation arm, a one-of-a-kind ace to counter the Brewers’ Jacob Misiorowski.
There’s one person, at least, who sees forking over gargantuan green for his services as a good investment.
“All these true No. 1’s, they’ve all led to world championships,” Boras said Wednesday during the GM meetings. “Over a 30- or 40-year period, we might have 10 of these aces that are capable of winning multiple Cy Youngs. And what that means is they have the ability to allow a franchise to clearly have an opportunity to win a coveted world championship.”
Boras shied away from addressing the Cubs too specifically. When asked about them and the White Sox making expensive signings this offseason, he agreed it’d be a good idea, no surprise coming from the guy whose job it is to land those kinds of deals for his players.
“It’s rare you have two baseball playoff teams [in Chicago] at the same time. So I would say, they have every reason to advance, they have every reason to say prosperity’s at their doorstep,” he said. “To not do so would be something that is going to allow them to step back … and I don’t think that’s in any way going to help their fan base or what they’ve worked so hard to build.
“I fully expect that anybody who truly wants to win … your fans are going to grade them by the pursuits of the best players on the market, or lack thereof.”
Skubal might be too much for the Cubs in an offseason where they need to bring in a lot more than just one pitcher. All five arms who finished the season in the rotation are free agents, and most of the in-house candidates to replace them are coming off significant injuries.
But might one or more of those guys who went from the Cubs’ rotation to free agency wind up making a return trip to the North Side?
The Cubs are interested in bringing back righty Clay Holmes, general manager Carter Hawkins said Tuesday, a reunion that makes an awful lot of sense considering why the team gave up their No. 2 prospect to add Holmes to a contending roster at the trade deadline.
“Clay’s got great stuff,” Hawkins said. “He puts the ball on the ground. … He’s a really good fit with our club, with the fact that we have such good infield defense. There’s some nice synergy there.
“He’s definitely somebody we’ll have interest in.”
Holmes’ status as a ground-ball specialist fits perfectly with a Cubs infield boasting a trio of Gold Glove winners.
Though he didn’t deliver what the Cubs hoped he would — after dazzling by allowing just one run during a four-start stretch shortly after being acquired, he had an 8.24 ERA in four September outings, issuing 15 walks in fewer than 20 innings, and didn’t pitch in the playoffs — he remains that perfect fit and someone with a long track record of success.
Acquired in a trade with the same team, the Mets, more than a month before Holmes, lefty David Peterson makes similar sense as someone who could return to the Cubs’ rotation.
Like Holmes, Peterson has a reputation as one of baseball’s best ground-ball generators, and the 2025 All Star did some of his best pitching at season’s end, allowing just three runs over his final four starts of the regular season. Unlike Holmes, Peterson did see playoff action, tossing a scoreless 1⅔ innings in Game 2 against the Padres.
Whether either pitcher fits what the Cubs are trying to do financially remains to be seen, and the looming lockout figures to throw an awful lot about the offseason into question.
But if the Cubs aren’t going to buy what Boras is selling and add Skubal to a Midwestern arms race with the Brewers, reuniting with Holmes and/or Peterson could be their strategy to close a massive gap in the division.

The chase for 2K: Seahawks’ JSN on pace for history

Jaxon Smith-Njigba is again threatening to break the NFL's single-season receiving yardage record. Here's a look at where he stands.

Week after week, Jaxon Smith-Njigba makes the extraordinary look routine.

‘Dynamite’ Witherspoon sparks Seahawks’ dominant defensive day

The superstar wide receiver’s latest jaw-dropping feat came on Sunday, when he exploded for a 144-yard third quarter on his way to eight catches for 171 yards and a touchdown in the Seattle Seahawks’ 21-13 win over the Las Vegas Raiders.

It continued one of the best receiving starts to a season in NFL history. Smith-Njigba now has 79 receptions for 1,207 yards and 10 touchdowns through 10 games, which puts him on pace to break the league’s single-season receiving yardage record.

Here’s a closer look at Smith-Njigba’s historic 2026 clip, which is outpacing even his sensational 2025 campaign. All historical data is according to 
Stathead
.

A historic 10-game start

• Smith-Njigba’s 1,207 receiving yards are the fifth-most through the first 10 games of a season in NFL history – including the second-most in the Super Bowl era, which began in 1966. The only players with more receiving yards through 10 games were Elroy Hirsh (1,268 in 1951), Raymond Berry (1,264 in 1960), Charley Hennigan (1,245 in 1961) and Tyreek Hill (1,222 in 2023).

• Smith-Njigba is the fourth player in NFL history and the first in the Super Bowl era with at least 1,200 receiving yards and 10 touchdown catches through the first 10 games of a season. He joins Don Hutson (1942), Hirsch (1951) and Berry (1960) as the only players to achieve that feat.

A record-setting pace

• Smith-Njigba is on pace for 2,052 receiving yards, which would break Calvin Johnson’s NFL single-season record of 1,964 in 2012. (The caveat is Johnson set that record in a 16-game season, prior to the NFL moving to 17 regular-season games in 2021.) In order to break Johnson’s record, Smith-Njigba needs 758 yards over Seattle’s final seven games – an average of 108.3 yards per game.

• Smith-Njigba is averaging an NFL-best 120.7 receiving yards per game. Over the course of an entire season, that would rank as the third-best mark in the Super Bowl era. Wes Chandler holds the NFL record with 129 receiving yards per game in 1982, while Johnson averaged 122.8 per game during his record-breaking 2012 campaign.

• Smith-Njigba has 61 more receiving yards than he did at this point last season, when he had 1,146 through 10 games. He finished last year with 1,793 receiving yards, the eighth-most in a single season in NFL history.

Triple crown watch

• Smith-Njigba leads the NFL in receptions, receiving yards and touchdown catches, which gives him a chance to achieve a receiving triple crown. Since the Super Bowl era began, just six players have finished a season as the NFL leader in all three major receiving categories: Ja’Marr Chase (2024), Cooper Kupp (2021), Steve Smith Sr. (2005), Sterling Sharpe (1992), Jerry Rice (1990) and Lance Alworth (1966).

• Smith-Njigba’s 1,207 receiving yards are a whopping 231 more than any other player in the NFL this season. The next-closest players are Dallas’ CeeDee Lamb (976), Green Bay’s Christian Watson (916), Cincinnati’s Chase (878) and Green Bay’s Matthew Golden (853). With 79 receptions, Smith-Njigba is slightly ahead of Arizona tight end Trey McBride (78) and Lamb (77). And with 10 TD catches, Smith-Njigba holds a slight edge over Lamb (eight).

Week-to-week dominance

• Smith-Njigba already has eight 100-yard receiving games this season. The NFL record for most 100-yard receiving games in a season is 11, held by Michael Irvin (1995), Johnson (2012) and Kupp (2021).

• Smith-Njigba also already has seven games this season with both 100-plus receiving yards and at least one touchdown catch. The NFL record for the most such games in a season is nine, held by Kupp (2021), Randy Moss (2007), Irvin (1995) and Hirsch (1951).

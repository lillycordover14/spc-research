SHILLONG, NOV 16
: The Khasi Jaintia Deficit School Teachers Association on Monday rejected the Chief Minister’s appeal to call off its agitation and announced it will continue its indefinite strike, saying the letter from the Chief Minister’s Office offered no assurance, no timeline and no solution.
Speaking after the association’s decision, KJDSTA president Boswell Pala said the teachers had met the Chief Minister on Thursday with a heavy heart and high hopes, but returned with only a vague promise of examination.

“With a heavy heart, we announce that we will continue with our indefinite strike,” Pala said. “On Thursday, we met the Chief Minister for half an hour, and in that meeting we raised issues, especially with regard to Scheme 2023 and Scheme 2026 not being legal.”
Advertisement
Pala said the Chief Minister assured the delegation that he would look into the matter, but the letter that followed in the evening undid that assurance.
“After we met, the Chief Minister assured us he would look into the matter, but in the evening of the same day, a letter was received from the Chief Minister’s Office requesting us to withdraw our indefinite strike on the ground that the Chief Minister has heard the issues and has forwarded the same to the Education Department, and the Education Department will examine the issues,” he said.

“We don’t know what it will examine, and then they will submit a report. There is nothing definite,” he said. “When will the report be ready? How will the report be? Despite the Chief Minister himself admitting that our issues are clear, what is there to examine?”
Pala said the letter gave the teachers no reason to trust the process. “It is a letter without assurance or confidence to call off the strike,” he said. “Therefore, today we feel our issues are serious, and we have decided to continue with our indefinite strike.”
He said the core demands remain completely unresolved. “The issues, including the Centralised Provident Fund, the NPS, the implementation of Scheme 2023, the withdrawal of Scheme 2026, and the Contributory Provident Fund contribution at 10 per cent of our salary, still remain unresolved,” he said.

From Pipe Cleaners to Clay, Baskets Break Craft Traditions in ‘Interwoven’

Conjure an image of a basket in your mind. Is it made of straw, or perhaps sweetgrass or willow? Does it have a handle? Was it made by hand, and what could it hold? At 
Mindy Solomon Gallery
, a forthcoming exhibition titled 
Interwoven: Basketry As Expanded Practice
 challenges our perception of both object and craft. 

Curated by 
Dee Clements
, the exhibition considers the way basketmaking techniques are far from limited to the splinted white ash trug we might carry out to the garden or the simple vessel that holds recipes in the kitchen. This vibrant survey “breaks craft tradition,” Clements says, positioning the practice as an art form. The show asks “what happens when basketry is understood not as a fixed category of craft, but as an open-ended artistic language?”

Xanthe Sommers, “Never an end to holding” (2026), glazed stoneware, 43.3 x 23.6 x 23.6 inches. Courtesy of October Gallery

The artists in 
Interwoven 
use a range of materials, from ceramic and steel to cotton and paper. 
Salvador Dominguez
’s vibrant pipe cleaner vessels are complemented by wall works like 
Eric Gushee
’s intricately interlaced wire sculptures and 
Miguel Arzabe
’s woven, deconstructed paintings.

Viewers will find further explorations by artists like 
Dienke Dekker
 and 
José Santiago Pérez
, who work with unexpected materials like paper and emergency blankets, respectively. And Colossal readers might also recognize work by 
Xanthe Somers
 and 
Sonia Barrett
, among the nearly two dozen participating artists.

Interwoven
 opens on November 29 and continues through January 9 in Miami. See more on the gallery’s 
Instagram
.

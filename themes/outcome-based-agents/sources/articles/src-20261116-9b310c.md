Ema Horvath on Eärien’s Death, Faith, and Journey in THE RINGS OF POWER Season 3&nbsp;&nbsp;Nerdist

[page text]
Quick Take

We spoke to 
The Rings of Power
‘s Ema Horvath about Eärien following her death on the series.
Horvath shared that in Eärien’s final moments, she was really talking to her brother Isildur and father Elendil.
In addition to Eärien’s brave ending, Horvath talked to us about Eärien’s faith, afterlife, and overall journey in 
The Rings of Power
 season three.
 

In 
The Rings of Power 
season three, episode two,
 the complicated character of Eärien, played by Ema Horvath, meets her death
, but not before delivering a spectacular episode that reveals how far she’s come since we first met her. Throughout her final episodes, Eärien balances personal shame with great bravery, as she works to give her father Elendil, brother Isildur, and Queen Míriel as much of a fighting chance as she’s can from deep within the ranks of Pharazôn. As we say goodbye to Eärien in 
The Rings of Power
, 
Nerdist 
sat down with Ema Horvath to discuss Eärien’s death, faith, family, and hopes for Númenor. You can read our full interview below.
Prime Video
Nerdist: In 
The Rings of Power
 season three, we meet a very different Eärien than we left behind in season two. What do you think happened to her in those five years that sort of brought her around so completely?
Ema Horvath:
 
I think she learned that Valandil died and how he died
. I think that was a big part of her coming around. I think that moment happened not too long after we left Eärien after season two of 
The Rings of Power
. And I think it’s been a long five years where she’s been having to keep up this charade, and she’s dealing with an incredible amount of shame this season. Self-medicating for sure. Not sleeping very well and very much looking for a way out.
Is there anything specific we didn’t see on screen that you considered while crafting your performance as Eärien in 
The Rings of Power 
season three?
Ema Horvath: 
 I think the focus for Eärien is on family. She’s so isolated now. Her entire family has left. Obviously, her relationship with her father has been pretty bad for a long time, and now it feels irreparable, or there were points in the last five years where it felt irreparable. So I think her political philosophy, and how it’s embodied in her isolation from her family, was primarily what I focused on.
Prime Video
Eärien’s death is so shocking and brave in 
The Rings of Power
 season three. How did you prepare to bring her to that place where she’s willing to sacrifice herself, and what was it like to perform that scene?
Ema Horvath: 
That was my favorite day on this show in the last six years. It’s obviously a very dark scene. Yeah, I did a lot of reading about shame-based behavior. For me, that option is on the table from the very beginning when you see her get her hair done. She doesn’t know if she’s ever going to use that option or how, but yeah, the performance is structured as if it’s already on the table. It’s not something that comes to her in that moment.
Some of Eärien’s final words before her death are “Maybe it [death] is a blessing,” referencing her earlier scene with Isildur. What conclusion does she come to in that moment?
Ema Horvath: 
I think it’s her realizing that she can exercise that option in that moment. It’s also a Faithful philosophy. I don’t think she… This is a great question. It’s a Faithful philosophy that has never made sense to her, but I don’t think that she misunderstands the value of death. I think that’s a moment where she’s realizing that the philosophies of her father and her family have some merit to them. 
I think she understands the Faithful better in that moment and right after that. I think in that moment too, she’s done appealing to all these men, and she’s having a conversation with her brother. She’s having the philosophical conversation with her brother that she’s never been able to have, and with her father. These are themes that she’s already, I think, warmed to in the last few years. I think it’s like, “I don’t want to speak to anyone in this room. The people I really love are not here, so let me have a private conversation with the people I wish were here before I do what I’m going to do.”
Prime Video
What do you ultimately think Eärien’s dream for Númenor is? And what do you think she hopes for her family after her death?
I think Eärien hopes that what she’s managed to do in the five years is going to matter. I don’t know if she fully believes that, but she hopes. I think she hopes Númenor won’t sink. And she hopes that their lives will be safe and protected.

Míriel and Eärien have a very complicated relationship. What do you think that looked like after Eärien repented, and what do you think they ultimately saw in one another?
I think they just kind of miss one another. I think when you see us go visit Míriel in 
The Rings of Power 
season three, Eärien’s plan is to stay in the shadows and be like, “My brother’s here to talk to her and I have some useful information, but I’m still ashamed to be around you, and I’m going to let my brother do most of the talking.” But yeah, there’s the scene in the dungeon that we had in season two; we don’t talk to each other at all. There are moments where you’re like, “Oh, this could have been such a fruitful relationship. This could have been a female figure in her life that would’ve been tremendously powerful and valuable, and we were just like ships passing in the night.”
Prime Video
And then we see Isildur and Eärien have come a ways back together in 
The Rings of Power 
season three. Where do you think that her relationship with Isildur concluded, and with Elendil? And do you think any of those were healed at all?
I think based on the conversation Maxim Baldry (Isildur) and I have in the courtyard, the chapel, I think her family has largely forgiven her, but she has not forgiven herself. And the only person she’s had physical contact with is Isildur. And so I think she’s taking his word for it that everything is forgiven, but there’s so much shame there that even if there was a reunion, I don’t think she would believe it because she hasn’t forgiven herself.
Prime Video
As we come to the end of our time, can you share a little bit about your favorite parts of being in 
The Rings of Power, 
and what do you think Eärien is doing in the afterlife?
Eärien is waiting for her siblings and her father, and I hope she’s drawing with her mother. I don’t think she’s picked up a pencil in five years.
And what am I going to miss? I’m going to miss calling Lloyd Owen about craft. I know he’ll still pick up the phone, but I hope I’ll miss him on speed dial.

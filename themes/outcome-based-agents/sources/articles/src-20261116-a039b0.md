ADA responds to continued reports of MetLife offsetting payments owed to dentists

Association encourages members to request detailed explanations from insurer

 

by 
Mary Beth Versaci

November 16, 2026

 
 

 

In light of ongoing recoupment issues, the American Dental Association encourages any dentist receiving a recoupment request, payment offset or overpayment recovery notice from MetLife to request a detailed explanation of why the recovery was generated.

This recommendation from the ADA follows continued reports of MetLife reducing, or “offsetting,” payments owed to ADA member dentists to recoup overpayments allegedly made to other, unaffiliated dentists — despite the insurance company stating in 2025 that it would modify aspects of its overpayment recovery practices after 
being contacted by the ADA
 with concerns.

Last year’s recoupments occurred when dentists separately provided care to the same patient or different family members covered under the same dental benefit policy. In an email, MetLife told the ADA that after reviewing market practices, it would change its policy “to only offset claim overpayments from the provider [it] overpaid.” The insurer said it was “working through the systems changes required for this change” and expected it would be done by the end of 2025.

However, after receiving additional reports from dentists who believed their payments had been reduced to recover alleged overpayments involving other dental offices, the ADA contacted MetLife on Oct. 19 of this year to clarify if the policy changes had been fully implemented and what the company’s current recovery practices are.

In response, MetLife stated its current policy is not to recover an overpayment made to one provider by offsetting payments owed to an unrelated provider. It characterized the examples provided by the ADA as isolated processing errors that were subsequently corrected and stated they do not reflect a policy shift.

The ADA advises dentists who receive a recoupment request, payment offset or overpayment recovery notice to ask MetLife for the reason for the alleged overpayment, the name of the provider associated with the overpayment, the recovery methodology used and information about any related claim adjustments. Some recovery efforts may involve 6 Degrees Health, so dentists can seek additional information from that company as well.

The ADA also encourages members to report their experiences to 
dentalbenefits@ada.org
.

“The ADA is continuing to monitor member experiences to determine whether the reports received align with the policy described by MetLife,” said Bertram Hughes, D.M.D., chair of the ADA Council on Dental Benefit Programs. “Member reports help the ADA identify patterns, evaluate whether problems persist and engage with payers when concerns affecting dentists are identified. We will continue to advocate for transparent and fair overpayment recovery practices and assess whether additional action is warranted.”

EMA Backs Two New Options for NSCLC&nbsp;&nbsp;Medscape

[page text]
European regulators have moved to expand treatment options for 
non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
. The European Medicines Agency’s Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) has recommended granting marketing authorization for Zybrivo (
sintilimab
, Fortvita Biologics), a PD-1 inhibitor for first-line nonsquamous disease, and Aumseqa (aumolertinib, SFL Pharmaceuticals), a third-generation EGFR TKI for 
EGFR
-mutated advanced NSCLC. Together, the two agents expand options for patients with 
EGFR
-mutated disease and for those without 
EGFR
 or 
ALK
 aberrations. 
Aumseqa 
Aumseqa is an oral, once-daily third-generation EGFR TKI (available as 55-mg film-coated tablets) that preferentially targets mutant 
EGFR
 over wild-type 
EGFR
. It is authorized as:
First-line monotherapy for advanced NSCLC with tumors harboring 
EGFR
 exon 19 deletions or the exon 21 L858R substitution
Monotherapy for advanced NSCLC with an 
EGFR
 T790M mutation
The indication for first-line treatment of advanced NSCLC with 
EGFR
 exon 19 deletions or the exon 21 L858R substitution rests on the phase 3 
AENEAS
 trial. In that study, 429 treatment-naive patients with locally advanced or metastatic 
EGFR
-mutated NSCLC were randomly assigned to receive aumolertinib or 
gefitinib
. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 19.3 months with aumolertinib vs 9.9 months with gefitinib. Median overall survival was about 39 months vs 31 months. Objective response rates were similar between arms (73.8% vs 72.1%), but median duration of response was longer with aumolertinib (18.1 vs 8.3 months). Rash (23.4% vs 41.4%) and 
diarrhea
 (16.4% vs 35.8%) were less frequent with aumolertinib. Rates of grade 3 or higher adverse events were comparable (36.4% vs 35.8%).
The indication for treatment of advanced NSCLC with an 
EGFR
 T790M mutation is based on the single-arm 
phase 2 APOLLO
 trial in 244 patients whose disease had progressed on a prior EGFR TKI. The objective response rate by independent central review was 68.9% (168 of 244 patients, all partial responses). Median duration of response was 15.1 months, and median PFS was 12.4 months. Among 23 patients with assessable central nervous system metastases, the intracranial response rate was 60.9%.
The most common adverse effects are elevated liver enzymes, 
hyponatremia
, elevated 
creatine
 phosphokinase, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, 
upper respiratory tract infection
, and rash. 
Zybrivo 
Zybrivo is supplied as a 100-mg concentrate for solution for infusion. 
Sintilimab
 blocks the binding of PD-1 to PD-L1 and PD-L2, thereby potentiating T-cell responses, including antitumor responses. The recommendation covers Zybrivo in combination with 
pemetrexed
 and platinum-based chemotherapy for first-line treatment of adults with nonsquamous NSCLC and no 
EGFR
 or 
ALK
 genomic aberrations. Eligible patients have either locally advanced disease that is not amenable to curative-intent surgery or chemoradiation, or metastatic disease. PD-L1 expression is not required.
The benefit was shown in the phase 3 
ORIENT-11
 trial, in which 397 previously untreated patients were randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio to sintilimab or placebo plus pemetrexed and platinum. Median PFS was 8.9 months with sintilimab vs 5.0 months with placebo. In the final survival analysis, median overall survival was 24.2 vs 16.8 months, and 47% of patients in the control arm crossed over to sintilimab monotherapy after progression.
The most common adverse effects are 
anemia
, 
neutropenia
, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, decreased appetite, nausea, fatigue, elevated aspartate and alanine aminotransferase, pyrexia, 
hypothyroidism
, and rash. 
Next Steps 
According to the CHMP, these treatments must be initiated and supervised by a physician experienced in the use of anticancer medicinal products. Both positive opinions now move to the European Commission, which typically finalizes marketing authorization decisions within about 67 days of a CHMP recommendation. Following approval, prescribing information, including guidance on patient selection, dosing, and safety monitoring, will be published in the medicines’ Summary of Product Characteristics in all official EU languages.

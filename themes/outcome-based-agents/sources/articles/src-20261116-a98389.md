Ema Bubalo didn’t know much about Colorado.

That’s understandable. Bubalo grew up in Podstrana, Croatia, a coastal town with a population of less than 11,000 tucked along the Adriatic Sea. It’s not exactly fertile recruiting turf for any CU sport.

That distance has stopped Bubalo from making a little Colorado tennis history.

In the first official year of a new championship calendar for NCAA women’s tennis, Bubalo has taken advantage by becoming the first CU women’s player to qualify for two different individual NCAA finals. The tournament begins Tuesday in Athens, Georgia, but Bubalo and partner Lera Alexin will get started when the doubles field begins play on Wednesday.

A fourth-year player, Bubalo also qualified for the doubles final during the 2024-25 season alongside Anya Nelson. She credits feeling an instant confidence in coach Anthony Pham with enticing her to cross the globe to compete in Boulder.

“I knew partnering with (Pham) as my coach, I’d get better,” Bubalo said. “I could have gone pro. But I also wanted to continue  with school, and I couldn’t do that at home because we don’t have athletics with the colleges. I think the right move for me was to go to the U.S. and prove myself as a player, and also get a degree. I think here I’ve built even more confidence. If I go pro later, I think I’m going to be more ready to do that after my few years here.”

After two years as a pilot program, this year’s individual championships marks the first year the tournament will be held in the fall, with the team championships to follow in the spring. The shift was made to curb the results in the team field would often spur players to withdraw from the individual field. Pham said he was a proponent of the change.

“If you were a good player on a good team, you could be there for three weeks,” Pham said of the all-spring championship format. “The oversight committee, which I sit on, they took feedback from all the coaches in Division I. Overwhelming support to move it to the fall. Whatever we need to do to keep our sport healthy and viable, we as coaches should be in support of it.”
 

Since 1985, the Buffs have had just three individual entrants at the NCAA  finals — Monika Milewski in singles in 2007, and then Bubalo and Nelson two years ago before the Bubalo-Alexin pairing qualified this year.

Bubalo and Alexin had never played together before prior to this fall, but Pham played his hunch believing they would make a formidable combo. Their success reflects the confidence Bubalo felt with Pham when she was being recruited.

Bubalo and Alexin have posted a 13-1 record this fall, including sweeping through three matches wo win the doubles championship last week at the San Diego Invitational. That extended the duo’s winning streak to eight consecutive matches.

Bubalo and Alexin will face Michigan’s third-seeded duo of Piper Charney and Reese Miller in Wednesday’s first round.
 

“I feel like we’re both very motivated, so I feel like that competitive energy that we both bring has helped a lot,” said Alexin, a third-year player from Lenexa, Kansas. “I feel like we’re both very aggressive-minded doubles players. I feel like our games work really well together and we’re able to set each other up.”

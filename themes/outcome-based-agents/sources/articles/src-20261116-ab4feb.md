As 2026 comes to a close, 
Cardano
 (
CRYPTO:ADA
) holders face a tough decision after the cryptocurrency has dropped 54% this year. With the Leios upgrade just weeks away, investors can buy in anticipation of potential gains, sell to lock in tax losses, or hold their assets and continue earning staking rewards.

ADA is trading at about 15.3 cents currently. That marks a 14.2% decline over the past week and 30% over the past month, leaving it 95% below its all-time high of $3.09. So should Cardano holders buy, sell, or hold ADA before the year ends?

Cardano Is Down 54% This Year, but Leios Is Just Around the Corner

Cardano is a proof-of-stake blockchain platform designed for smart contracts, and its market value has fallen to about $5.75 billion, making it the 18th largest cryptocurrency. For potential buyers, this steep drop in price means ADA is significantly cheaper than it was at its peak.

Leios, a major scaling upgrade, is expected to let the network handle many more transactions, and a Leios-compatible release is scheduled for November 26. That could provide a much-needed boost before the year wraps up. However, the road to recovery remains lengthy, as our look at 
whether Cardano can get back to $1
 by 2028 found ADA needs to climb more than 500%.

Bitcoin and Potential Fed Rate Hikes Could Push ADA Lower

Market trends seem to favor sellers for now. On November 14, ADA briefly hit 14.8 cents, just above its one-year low of 14.4 cents and about 6% below its current price. Sellers are watching the 20-day average, which stands at 18 cents, roughly 18% higher than ADA’s current price.

ADA also tends to follow 
Bitcoin
 (
CRYPTO:BTC
) lower and often falls more sharply. This year, ADA has lost 54% compared to Bitcoin’s 26% drop. Furthermore, Fed funds futures imply about an 89% chance of a quarter-point rate hike at the Federal Reserve’s December 9 meeting, which could cause funds to exit speculative assets like ADA.

In addition to market conditions, tax considerations may also prompt sellers to act. U.S. holders who sell at a loss before December 31 can offset taxable gains and claim up to $3,000 in excess losses against their income, as outlined in Section 1211(b) of the tax code.

This potential tax advantage is an important factor to consider, while our look at 
what 10,000 Cardano could be worth
 by 2030 shows the upside a seller could give up.

Staking Rewards Make Holding Attractive

On the other hand, holding ADA comes with its own benefits. ADA holders can delegate their coins to a stake pool, which helps validate transactions, and earn around 2.5% to 3% annually in new coins, all without locking up their assets. This means holders keep full access to their coins while weathering the current market slump.

Rebounds can happen quickly. For instance, ADA traded at 23 cents on October 21, 50% above its current price, just weeks before the November 14 low. Our look at 
what $1,000 in Cardano could be worth
 by 2028 provides further insights for potential buyers.

Should You Buy, Sell or Hold Cardano Before Year-End?

Right now, ADA seems more suited for long-term holders, particularly since they can earn staking rewards while waiting for the Leios upgrade to roll out. However, new investors should be aware that ADA might still test its 14.4-cent low before any significant recovery, and our 
Cardano 2030 price prediction
 takes the longer view.

For those with taxable gains from other investments, recognizing an ADA loss can be a strategic move at tax time, and this is worth discussing with a tax professional. The Fed’s December 9 meeting will serve as a key moment. If Bitcoin stabilizes afterward and ADA rises above its 18-cent 20-day average, it could find more solid footing. Conversely, if ADA dips below 14.4 cents, sellers may maintain control into the new year.

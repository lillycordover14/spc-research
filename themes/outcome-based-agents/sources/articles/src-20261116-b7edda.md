JERUSALEM, ISRAEL—According to a statement released by the 
Public Library of Science
, an evaluation of clay figurines unearthed at three sites in Israel suggests that Natufians made more complex objects than previously thought between 15,000 and 11,500 years ago. Laurent Davin of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem said that it had been previously thought that the first Natufian figurines were made mostly of stone or bone, and that clay only became more common as people began farming some 11,500 years ago. The objects represent birds, mammals, and humans. Orange and red coloring has been detected on some of the figures. The study also analyzed fingerprints on the clay figurines, revealing that children were involved with crafting them. “These discoveries change how we understand the beginnings of artistic expression in the first villages of humanity,” Davin said. “Thousands of years before agriculture and pottery emerged, people were already using clay to create figures, as part of the beliefs and knowledge of the natural world,” he explained.

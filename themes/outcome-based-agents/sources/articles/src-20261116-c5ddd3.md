ADA TOWNSHIP, Mich. (WOOD) — A DeVos-owned entity is looking to add a private heliport in Ada Township, sparking concerns about noise and neighborhood impact from some residents.
At its Thursday meeting, the Ada Township Planning Commission is expected to consider Sterling Fox LLC's special use request for a heliport at 4861 Michigan St. NE near Steketee Avenue NE. The LLC is owned by Daniel and Pamella DeVos, according to a manager's certificate from RDV Corporation included in 
township planning documents

Plans call for a touchdown and liftoff area with an adjoining final approach and takeoff area, as well as a 1,210-square-foot building with garage space and a waiting area, according to a Department of Planning memo. Sterling Fox has proposed using an Airbus H160 or a similar helicopter, with up to 100 takeoffs/landings per year and a general rule of up to three takeoffs/landings per day, its application says. Nighttime flights would be restricted, with no arrivals or departures between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m.
The Department of Planning memo lays out a number of standards for evaluating special use requests, including that "the special use shall be designed, constructed, operated and maintained in a manner harmonious with the character of adjacent property" and that it "shall not be hazardous to adjacent property, or involve uses, activities, materials or equipment which will be detrimental to the health, safety or welfare of persons or property through the excessive production of traffic, noise, smoke, fumes or glare."

In a compliance statement, Sterling Fox affirmed the accessory building's harmony with the surrounding neighborhood, adding that it plans to add more landscaping between the building and nearby properties. It also noted that the DeVos family already has a heliport on Fox Hollow Avenue SE and suggested imposing "substantially similar conditions" on the proposed Michigan Street heliport — though, it continued, use of the Michigan Street heliport would be "less extensive."

The Department of Planning memo pointed out "notable differences" between the Fox Hollow and Michigan Street sites, including the fact that there are more homes immediately surrounding the proposed Michigan Street helipad: four within 600 feet and four more within 800 feet.
Planning staff also raised questions about the noise that would be generated by takeoffs and landings. Ultimately, staff recommended that the Planning Commission table the special use request until a sound study can be completed.

Many community members have expressed concerns about the heliport. A joint opposition letter included in the Nov. 19 meeting's agenda packet garnered dozens of signatures from Ada Township residents and residents of other municipalities. The joint letter argued that the heliport would change the character of the "quiet rural residential neighborhood," suggested that the "repeated vibration" could cause physical damage to homes and worried about harm to wildlife and property values.

Individual letters sent by neighbors expressed similar concerns. One family, for instance, argued that "sudden, loud helicopter noise" would disrupt their baby's sleep.
Community members opposed to the heliport also cited zoning standards that call for these facilities to be "accessory to and ordinarily located on a lot occupied by the principal dwelling of the facility owners." According to Sterling Fox's application, the proposed heliport would be located on the adjacent lot. The planning commission has the ability to approve an adjacent location "if it determines that such a location best conforms with the special use approval standards," but opposed neighbors argued that the applicant has not demonstrated why this would be necessary.
The Ada Township Planning Commission meeting gets underway at 5:30 p.m. Thursday.

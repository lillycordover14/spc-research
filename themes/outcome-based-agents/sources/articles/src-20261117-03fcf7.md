‘Ma’ Harper, 97, shares her recipe for life
At an age when many would be content to retire, Ma Harper fulfilled her lifelong dream of opening her own restaurant — Ma Harper’s Creole Kitchen. Driven by a desire to offer second chances, Harper pays it forward with a message of hope and redemption.

By 
Hamilton Stewart
November 17, 2026 10:48 am
Arts & Culture
, 
Food & Drink
, 
Partner Organizations

Hamilton Stewart / NextGenJourno

Alice “Ma” Harper, 97, a restaurateur, reads a recipe book that contains some of her signature dishes – including gumbo. Her New Orleans-style cuisine has been an institution in San Antonio since 1991.

 
 

This story comes from the 
Next Gen Journalism project:

To hear Alice “Ma” Harper tell it, life began at age 12. That’s when she took on the responsibility of feeding her family. As long as she can remember, Harper has had a job. She didn’t get paid much, but she learned how to do it all: sewing, cleaning, shucking oysters.

“
I never been afraid to work
,” 
Harper said
.
 “I’m not ashamed to tell anybody the truth about my life.”

Harper was born in New Orleans in 1929, the second of 16 children. Her early struggles in life shaped her tireless work ethic, unshakable faith in God and a philosophy of fearlessness. 

Harper grew up during the Great Depression and World War II. She had various jobs in New Orleans before moving to San Antonio in the 1960s, where she eventually became an aircraft mechanic, working on fighter jets. In order for the family to eat, Harper often had to make the most out of leftover food scraps. 

“I was old enough to go to help bring food in, and that’s where we lived … [near] the naval base dump, where the government would bring three times a day food from the mess hall, and I learned survival. I learned love. I learned sharing,” she said.

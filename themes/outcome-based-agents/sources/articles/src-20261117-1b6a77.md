William Russel Pace of Centralia passed away peacefully Tuesday November 17, 2026 with family at his side. Celebration of Life services will be held Monday, November 30, 2026 at Arnold Funeral Home in Mexico, Missouri from 5 pm to 7 pm. He lived life simply and with kindness whether at the meat counter, leisurely at home with family or kicking around with friends.
Russel was born March 4, 1948, at Boone County Hospital in Columbia, Missouri to Alcie Ellen Davis Pace and Robert Truman Pace. His sister LaVerne, who shared his birthday, chose his name, honoring both of their grandfathers.
On August 21, 1971, Russel married Donna Mae Cox at her grandmother’s home in Centralia, which later became their home. Together they raised three children, Tamitha Rene’ (Overly) Ague (Thomas Ague) of New London, Brian Andrew (Tricia Howard) Pace of Centralia and Amber Dawn (Pace) Dixon (Alexander) of Hannibal. Carolyn Joy (Barnes) Mozingo (Bill Cobb) of Madison was dear to him and he considered her as another daughter. He had four cherished grandchildren, Alexei Rene’ Pace, Tianna Rushelle Pace of Columbia, Nathan Andrew (Kathryn Jones) Pace, and Bailey Lynn Pace all of Centralia and one great grandson, Noah Andrew Pace. He is also survived by three sisters, Neva LaVerne Black, Joetta June Davis and Martha Ellene Vanskike, a brother-in-law Chris (Connie) Cox, sister-in-law Tonnie Wainscott.
He was preceded in death by his wife, Donna of 49 years, his parents, three sisters, Rexene Boren, Saralyn Maine Pace and Nita Ruth Purcell, one brother, Robert Davis (Pete) Pace, one brother-in-law he considered a confidant and was ever grateful for his support, John Paul “J.P.” Davis, nieces and nephews Tiffany Wainscott, Stephanie Lin Cossairt, Richie Allen Vanskike, Cory and Casey Cox.
The seventh of eight children, he spent his early years on a farm across from the site of today’s Columbia Airport. In 1961, his family moved to Mexico when his father was transferred by Montgomery Ward. Russel enjoyed sports. He played tennis, baseball, billiards, horseshoes and cards. He was proud to have been a Mexico Bulldog where he competed in track, baseball and relished his “glory days” in football where he made lifelong connections. “Russel J,” the proud mama’s boy remained a devoted St. Louis Cardinals and Kansas City Chiefs fan, but in his “frugal” way he only saw them play when the tickets were free. Tennis was a sport he continued to “play” for many years with friends, family and his local buddies from D.C.’s.
After graduating from Mexico High School in 1966 Russel went to work at Jay’s IGA (located in the current Arnold Funeral Home building) in the meat department. There he honed his skills as a butcher during a time when the meat was butchered onsite at the store. He managed the meat department for a time at Eastgate in Columbia before taking a position at C&R Market in Centralia until he retired. Russel worked for 32 years before calling out sick with a heart attack. Through the years he moonlighted as a bartender at the Attic (part of the old Monterey Motel) in Mexico then at D.C.’s in Centralia.
Together, Russel and Donna modeled family devotion and made every effort to attend as many of their children’s and grandchildren’s choir performances, ball games, golf matches, and Madrigal events as they could. As a creature of habit, his friends and family grew to rely on certain annual traditions, like watching the Macy’s Thanksgiving parade or sharing his biscuits and gravy on Christmas morning while waiting for Santa and the firetruck. Russel often was the chief creative cook in the home.
Just a little bit ornery and quick with a “dad joke” or to share a story, Russel will be remembered with a smile by friends and family as a kind man of integrity, honor and commitment.
Memorial donations may be made to The Centralia Parks and Rec Department Tree Fund and sent in care of Arnold Funeral Home, 425 S. Jefferson St., Mexico, MO 65265.

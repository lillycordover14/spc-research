Goldman Sachs Group Inc. disclosed roughly $1.22 billion in Ether exchange-traded fund positions as of Sept. 30, a sixfold increase from the $211.6 million it reported three months earlier, according to its quarterly Form 13F filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

The filing, submitted Nov. 16, shows the bank expanding across nearly every major listed Ether product. Its stake in BlackRock's iShares Ethereum Trust (ETHA) rose 352% to 29,963,675 shares valued at $602.9 million, up from 6,630,550 shares worth $78.8 million at the end of June. That single position accounted for nearly half of the bank's reported Ether ETF exposure.

Goldman also lifted its holdings in Grayscale's Ethereum Staking Mini ETF by 180% to 5,683,732 shares, worth $144.9 million, and increased its position in BlackRock's iShares Staked Ethereum Trust by 62% to 8,180,000 shares, valued at $281 million. Four additional Ether funds, led by $169.6 million in Fidelity's Ethereum Fund, rounded out the total at a combined $195.4 million.

The disclosure lands in the middle of a strong stretch for the second-largest cryptocurrency. Ether gained almost 71% during the third quarter, and U.S. spot Ether ETFs pulled in more than $3 billion in net inflows over the period, their best quarter of 2026, according to industry flow data. Combined assets in those products stood at $18.1 billion at quarter-end.

One caveat matters when reading the numbers. The same SEC table lists put options on 13,027,900 ETHA shares valued at $262.1 million, and the filing does not explain their purpose. The reported long positions therefore should not be taken as a clean measure of the bank's directional bet on Ether. Form 13F also omits short positions and certain hedging instruments, which can make routine market-making activity look like a concentrated wager.

Camran Khosravi, a research analyst at asset manager Bitwise, made a similar point about how such filings should be interpreted. Market makers such as Jane Street can move between Bitcoin ETFs to meet client demand and hedge options positions without altering their overall Bitcoin exposure, he told CoinTelegraph, adding that "13F filings can make routine market-making look like a big bet on one fund."

Goldman's Bitcoin ETF book shifted in the opposite direction. The bank cut its iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) stake by about 792,000 shares to 16.44 million, even though the position's reported value climbed 36% to $778.3 million as the fund's price rallied 42% over the quarter. At the same time, its Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund holdings jumped more than 50-fold, from 105,840 shares to 5.48 million, lifting reported value to $397.9 million from $5.4 million.

Wells Fargo moved the other way on IBIT. The San Francisco-based lender reported 49,577,094 IBIT shares worth about $2.35 billion at Sept. 30, up 103% from 24,396,222 shares valued at $812.2 million three months earlier. Its ETHA position also grew 125% to 22 million shares. Galaxy Research estimated the bank's total reported spot Bitcoin ETF exposure at the equivalent of 30,553 BTC, which would make it the largest institutional holder of such products. Galaxy's head of firmwide research, Alex Thorn, noted in a Nov. 17 post that about 85% of the third-quarter IBIT shares were booked through Wells Fargo's brokerage businesses, describing them as "client positions not prop bank or trading business holdings."

The filings cover positions as of Sept. 30 and say nothing about activity since then. That gap is significant this time: U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs shed $1.28 billion in the week of Nov. 2-6 and another $1.065 billion the following week, according to Farside Investors, with IBIT alone losing $908.4 million in the second stretch. Bitcoin slid toward $62,000 on Nov. 15, well below its quarter-end level.

Market tone has cooled accordingly. Ether traded at $1,796.95 and Bitcoin at $64,324 as of mid-morning UTC on Nov. 17, both slightly lower over 24 hours and down roughly 14% over seven days. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index sat at 12, a reading classified as Extreme Fear.

For investors tracking institutional crypto exposure, the key takeaway is that the third-quarter figures are a dated snapshot rather than a live position. The next Form 13F, covering holdings as of Dec. 31, is due Feb. 16, 2027, and will be the first quarter-end look at whether banks held, trimmed or added to these stakes during the autumn selloff. Comparisons of ETHA share counts across periods also require adjusting for the October reverse split, which changed the number of shares outstanding without altering the trust's aggregate asset value.

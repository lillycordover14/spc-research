Builder Rate Incentives Give New-Home Buyers a 114-Basis-Point Edge

Nov 17, 2026

 

By 
National Mortgage Professional

New-home buyers secured significantly lower starting mortgage rates than resale buyers in the third quarter

KEY TAKEAWAYS

New-home buyers averaged starting mortgage rates of 5.01% in the third quarter, compared with 6.15% for existing-home buyers.

The gap narrowed to 49 basis points when comparing 30-year fixed rates, indicating savings beyond temporary buydowns.

Builders are using financing incentives to attract buyers while keeping listing prices relatively steady.

Homebuilders are gaining a financing advantage over existing-home sellers, offering mortgage rates more than a full percentage point below those secured by resale buyers, according to new research from 
Realtor.com
.
The average starting mortgage rate for new-home buyers was 5.01% in the third quarter, compared with 6.15% for existing-home buyers, a 114-basis-point difference. On a $400,000 purchase with 10% down, that translates to approximately $258 less in monthly interest during the first year, Realtor.com reported Tuesday.
The gap reflects builders' growing reliance on mortgage-rate incentives to attract buyers while avoiding deeper price cuts. Builders can negotiate financing arrangements with preferred lenders or fund temporary and permanent rate buydowns.
Rate Advantage Extends Beyond Temporary Buydowns
Realtor.com found that new-home buyers secured average 30-year fixed mortgage rates of 6.19% during the third quarter, compared with 6.68% for existing-home buyers.
That 49-basis-point difference represents approximately $117 in monthly interest savings on the same $400,000 purchase, with the lower rate lasting for the life of the loan.

"Builders have adjusted to a market where affordability is still a major hurdle, but that adjustment isn't always visible in the listing price," said Joel Berner, senior economist at Realtor.com. "Reduced mortgage rates can help make a new home more affordable for buyers while allowing builders to hold asking prices relatively steady."
Temporary buydowns, including 3-2-1 arrangements, provide lower payments during the first several years before the loan returns to its full note rate. Permanent buydowns reduce the interest rate throughout the mortgage term.
Comparing these offers requires looking beyond the advertised starting payment to the note rate, closing costs, and payment schedule. The national averages also reflect differences in borrower characteristics, loan terms, and geography, rather than rates available to identical borrowers.
Builders Hold Prices as Resale Market Softens
The financing advantage comes as builders have kept asking prices remarkably stable despite weaker demand.
The median new-home listing price reached $451,071 in the third quarter, virtually unchanged from the same periods in 2024 and 2025. Existing-home listing prices, meanwhile, fell 1.5% year over year to $402,883.
New homes also offered more space, averaging 2,056 square feet compared with 1,790 square feet for existing homes. On a price-per-square-foot basis, the two segments were nearly identical at $218 and $219, respectively.
Builders are still making concessions. About 20.9% of new-home listings had price reductions during the quarter, compared with 21.6% of existing-home listings.
Construction activity is slowing, too. New-home inventory increased just 0.1% year over year, while the completion rate for new single-family homes fell 11% in September.
New construction accounted for 16.7% of active listings, down from 17.2% a year earlier.
The findings point to a more competitive purchase market in which financing incentives can carry as much weight as the asking price. Comparing the full cost of builder-backed offers with conventional resale financing is becoming an increasingly important part of winning purchase business.

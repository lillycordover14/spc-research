[page text]
The 2027 Postal Service Health Benefits (PSHB) Plan Cost Explorer lists every PSHB plan available at a ZIP code and shows what a Postal Service employee or annuitant pays in 2027, how that compares with 2026, and how much of each premium the government pays.

It covers PSHB plans only. Federal employees and annuitants outside the Postal Service use Federal Employees Health Benefits (FEHB) plans, which are in the 
FEHB Plan Cost Explorer
.

PSHB Open Season runs on the same dates as FEHB, November 9 to December 14, 2026, which the U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) calls the 2026 Open Season for plan year 2027. Scroll down for instructions and notes. A larger screen gives a better view of the results table.

How to use the PSHB Plan Cost Explorer

Enter the home or work ZIP code. Overseas enrollees can select the overseas option to see nationwide plans only.

Choose active Postal Service employee (amounts per biweekly pay period) or annuitant (amounts per month).

Choose Self Only, Self Plus One, or Self and Family.

Optionally select the 2026 plan to see the annual difference between that plan and every other option.

Read the 2027 premium and the change from 2026, then check the annual deductible and the worst-case year.

Check where each plan sits against the government contribution line. Select any column heading to sort.

Why the worst-case year matters more than the premium

The worst-case year is the 2027 annual premium plus the plan’s in-network out-of-pocket maximum for the enrollment type selected. It is the most an enrollee would pay for covered in-network care in a year, not a prediction.

The nationwide Self Only options show why premium alone misleads. For an active employee, FEP Blue Focus costs $84.17 per pay period, or $2,188.42 a year, and has an $11,000 out-of-pocket maximum, so its worst-case year is $13,188.42. The NALC Health Benefit Plan High Option costs $116.06 per pay period, or $3,017.56 a year, which is $829.14 more, but its out-of-pocket maximum is $3,500, so its worst-case year is $6,517.56. The plan with the higher premium has the lower worst case by $6,670.86. Whether either plan is the better fit depends on how much care the household expects to use, which doctors it uses and what each plan covers, so the brochure comes before the premium.

Health savings account (HSA) and health reimbursement arrangement (HRA) contributions are shown in the tool but are not subtracted. The worst-case year does not include the Medicare Part B premium, which is paid to Medicare and not to the plan.

What the government contribution line shows

By OPM’s description, the government pays the smaller of two amounts: 72% of the program-wide weighted average premium, or 75% of the plan’s total premium. The PSHB Program has its own weighted averages and maximums, separate from FEHB. For 2027, OPM sets the PSHB maximum government contribution at the amounts below. A plan reaches the maximum when its total premium passes the maximum divided by 0.75, which the tool calls the line.

Below the line, the government pays 75% of the total premium, so each extra $1 of total premium costs the enrollee $0.25. Above the line, the government pays the fixed maximum, so each extra $1 costs the enrollee $1.00. Of the 73 PSHB plan options in OPM’s 2027 files, 42 are below the line for Self Only and 31 are above it. For Self Plus One the split is 40 below and 33 above, and for Self and Family it is 43 below and 30 above. A plan within 5% below the line is flagged because a modest premium increase could move it above the line in a later year. That flag describes the numbers and is not a forecast.

The effect shows in the 2027 rates. MHBP Standard Option for Self and Family went from a total premium of $856.01 to $1,121.38 per pay period, up 31.0%, which carried it from below the 2026 line to above the 2027 line. The enrollee’s payment rose from $214.00 to $372.83, up 74.2%.

Self Plus One can cost more than Self and Family

In OPM’s 2027 PSHB rate file, the enrollee share for Self Plus One is higher than the enrollee share for Self and Family in 24 of the 73 plan options. The total premium for Self Plus One is never higher than the total for Self and Family, and in 9 of those 24 options the two totals are identical. The reason is the government’s maximum contribution, which is higher for Self and Family ($748.55 per pay period) than for Self Plus One ($689.34), a $59.21 difference. In all 24 options the gap between the two total premiums is smaller than that difference, so the enrollee pays less for the larger family. Kaiser Permanente Mid-Atlantic States High Option is one example: the total premium is $1,012.65 per pay period for both, and the enrollee pays $323.31 for Self Plus One and $264.10 for Self and Family. Among nationwide plans, the NALC Health Benefit Plan High Option charges $290.00 for Self Plus One and $270.37 for Self and Family.

A household with one covered family member can compare both columns before choosing an enrollment type. The tool shows each enrollment type separately. This count comes from the rate file and has not been checked against OPM’s own Self Plus One versus Self and Family comparison file.

Medicare Part B for PSHB annuitants

PSHB annuitants who retire after January 1, 2025 must be enrolled in Medicare Part B to keep PSHB coverage in retirement unless they qualify for an exception. OPM’s 
PSHB annuitant Medicare page
 explains the requirement and the exceptions. The Part B premium is separate from the PSHB premium and from the out-of-pocket maximum, so it is not part of any figure in the tool. Some plans reimburse part of the Part B premium or waive cost-sharing for members with Medicare Parts A and B, and those offsets are also not included. A household with Medicare may pay less in plan costs than the tool shows, and it pays the Part B premium on top. Each plan’s brochure has a Medicare section.

What changed for 2027

OPM reports that the average PSHB enrollee share rises 8.2% in 2027, down from 11.3% in 2026, with an overall average premium increase of 6.7% and a government contribution increase of 6.0%. FEHB’s average enrollee increase is 10.9%. In OPM’s rate files, the Self Only enrollee share for an active employee changes by a median of 5.6% across the plan options with a 2026 comparison, with 15 of 72 options lower and 3 unchanged. That median is a simple count across plan options and is not weighted by enrollment, so it differs from OPM’s program-wide figure.

Does the Explorer include FEHB plans?

No. This tool covers PSHB plans only. FEHB plans, for federal employees and annuitants outside the Postal Service, are in the 
FEHB Plan Cost Explorer
.

When do changes made in Open Season take effect?

For PSHB, a new enrollment or an enrollment change made during Open Season takes effect January 1, 2027, according to OPM’s 
Federal Benefits Open Season Manual
.

Where do Postal employees and annuitants enroll?

Postal Service employees use the 
PSHB System
. Box 5001, Lawrence, KS 66046-0500. Annuitants with questions can call OPM’s Retirement Information Office at 1-888-767-6738 (TTY 711). OPM’s 
enrollment page
 lists the channels.

What happens if my plan is leaving the PSHB Program?

An enrollee in an ending option must make a positive election into another PSHB plan during Open Season, or OPM places the enrollee as described in the table above. The enrollee can compare the plans available at a ZIP code in the tool and choose a different plan before the last day of Open Season.

Where do the numbers come from?

Premiums come from OPM’s 2027 and 2026 PSHB rate files, the out-of-pocket maximums, deductibles and account contributions come from OPM’s 2027 PSHB plan benefits file, and plan availability comes from OPM’s 2027 PSHB service area file. The files are on OPM’s 
Public Use Files page
, and the premium tables are on OPM’s 
PSHB premiums page
. Amounts are before any tax effect. Premium conversion, which lets premiums come out before tax, applies to employees. Annuitants pay premiums after tax.

How to read the results

Premium is not total cost. Deductibles, copayments, coinsurance, out-of-pocket maximums, and prescription coverage differ by plan. Each plan's brochure is the official statement of benefits, and the 
OPM plan comparison tool
 compares benefits.

Worst-case year is the 2027 annual premium you pay plus the plan's in-network annual out-of-pocket maximum for the enrollment type selected, from OPM's 2027 plan benefits file. It is the most you would pay for covered in-network care in a year, not a prediction of what you will pay. Premiums, out-of-network care, services the plan does not cover, and amounts above plan limits are not part of the out-of-pocket maximum. For most plans OPM shows the prescription limit as part of the same maximum. Plans marked † list a separate prescription maximum, so check the brochure for how the two work together. Health savings account (HSA) and health reimbursement arrangement (HRA) contributions are shown but not subtracted. The annual deductible is the in-network amount from the same OPM file. Where OPM's file lists no deductible for a plan, the column says Not listed, which does not mean the deductible is zero.

Medicare is not factored in. PSHB annuitants who retire after January 1, 2025 must be enrolled in Medicare Part B to keep PSHB coverage in retirement unless an exception applies, as described on 
OPM's PSHB annuitant Medicare page
. The Part B premium is paid to Medicare, not to the plan, and it is not included in any figure here, including worst-case year. Some plans also reimburse part of the Part B premium or waive cost-sharing for members with Medicare Parts A and B. Those offsets are not included either, so a household with Medicare may pay less in plan costs than the figures shown. See the Medicare section of each plan's brochure.

Amounts are the enrollee's share of the premium for active Postal Service employees and for annuitants, from OPM's PSHB rate file, before any tax savings from premium conversion, which applies to most active employees. Annual figures use 26 biweekly pay periods for active employees and 12 months for annuitants.

Government contribution zone: the line is the total premium at which the government’s maximum contribution equals 75% of the premium. Plans at or below it get 75%; plans above it get the fixed maximum. The 2027 PSHB maximums, $318.23 for Self Only, $689.34 for Self Plus One and $748.55 for Self and Family per biweekly pay period, come from the PSHB program’s own figures on the 
OPM PSHB premiums page
, not the FEHB figures. A plan within 5% below the line is flagged because a modest premium increase could move it above the line in 2028. That flag is an observation about the numbers, not a forecast.

Plan changes for 2027, from OPM's PSHB significant events table: GEHA High Option ends and enrollees move to GEHA Standard Option; MHBP Consumer Option ends and enrollees move to MHBP Value; UnitedHealthcare Choice Plus Primary Postal East (High option) ends, and enrollees who do not choose another plan are placed in the lowest-cost nationwide option or an alternate OPM designates. Each of these appears in the list below the results. Enrollees may choose a different plan during Open Season.

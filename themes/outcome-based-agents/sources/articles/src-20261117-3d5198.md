Breaking: Cresta Court Hotel to stop housing asylum seekers&nbsp;&nbsp;Altrincham Today

[page text]
The Government is to end its use of Altrincham's Cresta Court Hotel as accommodation for asylum seekers, more than two years after its sudden closure to the public sparked widespread controversy.
Altrincham and Sale West MP Connor Rand confirmed today that the remaining asylum seekers will leave the Church Street hotel "over the next few months".
The decision follows the 
closure of the Britannia Ashley Hotel in Hale as asylum accommodation in August
 and paves the way for the potential return of one of Altrincham's best-known hotels to its former use.
The announcement marks the beginning of the end of one of the most contentious issues in Altrincham in recent years and raises the prospect of one of the town's best-known hotels returning to commercial use.
It was on October 29th 2024 that 
Altrincham Today
 confirmed that the Cresta Court had been closed to house asylum seekers
, having broken the news of its sudden closure the previous evening.
The 148-bedroom hotel, a fixture of Altrincham's hospitality scene since the 1970s, had shut with immediate effect, leaving customers with existing bookings for weddings, Christmas parties and other events facing last-minute cancellations.
Its owner, Vine Hotels, which had acquired the property in 2023, had reached an agreement with Serco, which oversees asylum accommodation on behalf of the Home Office.
The arrangement was initially expected to last 12 months, with the hotel believed to have the capacity to accommodate around 300 asylum seekers.
The decision caused considerable anger locally, particularly over the lack of advance notification and consultation, and the hotel has since become the focus of numerous protests and counter-protests.
Today's announcement comes just three months after the 
Britannia Ashley Hotel in Hale officially ceased operating as asylum seeker accommodation
.
That hotel closed to asylum seekers on August 11th, bringing to an end an arrangement that had been in place since February 2023.
The closure of the Cresta Court will therefore mean that neither of the two hotels in the Altrincham area used to accommodate asylum seekers will be operating in that capacity.
Rand, who was elected as Altrincham and Sale West's first Labour MP in a century in July 2024, has repeatedly called on the Government to end the use of the Cresta Court.
He said today that he had secured the support of Andy Burnham, both during his time as Mayor of Greater Manchester and subsequently as Prime Minister.
He said: “A backlog in unprocessed asylum claims, which built up under Conservative governments as small boat crossings soared, led to the Cresta Court becoming an asylum hotel. We are ending this because of the progress that Labour has made on fixing the broken asylum system it inherited.
“While I’m proud to represent such a diverse place, using hotels to house asylum seekers is a sign of a broken system. It’s bad for taxpayers, bad for community cohesion and bad for the people seeking asylum.
“I have repeatedly called for the Cresta Court Hotel to be returned to its normal use, and campaigned with the then Mayor of Greater Manchester, Andy Burnham, for this to happen as soon as possible.
“I have always had Andy’s backing on this issue and I am pleased that in his first four months as Prime Minister he has signed off on this decision.”
Last October, a major 
parliamentary report by the Home Affairs Committee
, of which Rand was a member, was highly critical of the Home Office's management of asylum accommodation.
It highlighted failures to engage with local communities, mounting pressure on public services and the damaging impact of prolonged hotel use on community cohesion.
The Government has made ending the use of asylum hotels one of its major policy commitments, following a rapid expansion in their use that saw the number reach around 400 in 2023.
Rand also used today's announcement to criticise local Conservative and Reform UK politicians over their response to the Cresta Court's use as an asylum hotel.
He added: “While I pushed for real action on the Cresta Court Hotel, I recognised how divisive these issues can be, so I sought to work closely with local churches and charities on community cohesion.
“In contrast, local Conservative and Reform politicians have taken every opportunity to whip up anger about this issue for their own political ends. I will always stand against their divisive politics.”
Attention will now turn to the future of the Cresta Court itself.
For around half a century, the hotel was an established part of Altrincham town centre, hosting countless weddings, business conferences, Christmas parties, charity fundraisers and community events.
Its owner, Vine Hotels, is owned by Greg Dyke, the former director-general of the BBC and former chairman of the Football Association.
While today's announcement opens the door to the hotel returning to its former use, there is as yet no confirmation from its owners as to whether they intend to reopen it to the public.
Councillor Nathan Evans, Leader of the Trafford Conservative Group, said the hotel must now return to its former use.
He said: "Altrincham is a thriving town with a proud hospitality industry. Cresta Court was a hotel serving visitors, businesses and the local economy, and we want to see it return to that purpose.
"But we will not consider the job finished until there is a confirmed closure date and a clear plan for the future of the building.
"We made a pledge to residents, and we have never stopped fighting to deliver it."
A precise date for the departure of the last remaining asylum seekers has also yet to be announced.

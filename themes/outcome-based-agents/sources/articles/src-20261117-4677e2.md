Green River’s Clay Tile Blend Bourbon combines 7 and 8 year old barrels to deliver a fruit-forward profile, impressive depth, and rich mouthfeel, showcasing how unique warehouse environments and thoughtful barrel selection can produce excellent bourbon.
Green River Clay Tile Blend Bourbon is the second release in the company’s new Distillery Select series, which they say features some of the distillery's standout barrels and will be released with different proofs, finishes, and/or highlights of specific rickhouses. For this release, it is a blend of two lots of 7 and 8 year old bourbons featuring two different mashbills, which were aged on the upper floors of a hollow clay tile rickhouse – a characteristic the company says is unique to their rickhouse(s).
Many distilleries play up their rickhouse’s unique characteristics and how they factor in how their whiskey ages. The environment can play an important role in how a whiskey ages inside a barrel, but these claims often come off more like marketing ploys than real science. With Green River promoting their clay tile rickhouse for their latest release, at first glance it seems trivial, but clay has been a natural building material used for thousands of years and is proven to promote temperature stability and heat retention by absorbing and slowly releasing heat, as well as moisture buffering that helps stabilize localized humidity.
That’s all well and good, but does it result in a bourbon that tastes significantly different? We’ve already tasted bourbon from Green River Distilling (and OZ Tyler before that) aged in this rickhouse, since it was built just after Prohibition ended. But true to the company’s claim of singling out special barrels from their warehouse for Distillery Select releases, it rings true. Clay Tile Blend Bourbon doesn’t taste like other
 7 and 8 year old bourbons
 from Green River so far. 
The aroma is surprisingly varied, with a strong raspberry cream scent up front, followed by cocoa powder, dried mango, toasted coconut, and cream soda. The palate and finish are very fruity, with some notes shared between them. Raspberry continues throughout the bourbon, but the palate adds darker berries alongside it. Plum follows along with a welcoming peach note. It’s here you’ll also notice its mouthcoating viscosity. The finish brings raspberry back against a unique Hummingbird cake note, with spiced southern cake flavors of bananas, pineapple, and pecans. The hummingbird cake taste lingers and blends with black cherry, seasoned oak, and leather. 
I’ve welcomed the older Green River bourbons the company has been releasing over the past year, though they are far from uniformly dialed in, with some heavily spiced, over-oaked, or lacking range of flavors. Although Clay Tile Blend Bourbon is a distillery-only release, it shows the greater heights barrels in this age range can reach. Its fruit-forward profile, paired with depth and range of flavor and capped off by an excellent mouthfeel, makes this one of Green River’s best high-aged releases to date. Although the previous release, 
Toasted Double Oak Bourbon
, was aged 8 years, 6 months, its toasted finish overshadowed some of the whiskey’s finer points. Here, it’s all on display, making for a thoroughly enjoyable pour.

Key bench players help Clippers notch back-to-back victories&nbsp;&nbsp;Annenberg Media

[page text]
The Clippers (4-8) will be seeking their third consecutive victory Tuesday night against the struggling (1-13) Utah Jazz.
L.A.’s new cast seems to be finding a groove, with Darius Garland settling into a more controlled, dominant role at point guard–he totaled 51 points in the home victories over Oklahoma City and Charlotte, shooting 10 of 18 from the floor en route to 27 points, 6 rebounds and 7 assists against the Hornets.
But the Clippers’ bench deserves credit, too. These three players have been making significant contributions:
Max Strus
Heat Clippers Basketball

(Jayne Kamin-Oncea/AP Photo/Jayne Kamin-Oncea) 
Strus delivered a season-high night against Charlotte, knocking down four triples, three of which came in the second half when the Clippers fought to erase a 13-point deficit. He finished with 14 points and 4 assists in 25 minutes.
Coming from Cleveland, Strus, a 6-foot-5 guard who originally signed with Boston after being undrafted in 2019, has embraced his glue-guy role after being sidelined with a foot injury for the first four weeks of the season. His presence on the court keeps defenders closer, not allowing for as much help defense on more explosive players such as Garland. He can also move the ball and keep possessions flowing.
Gradey Dick
Pelicans Clippers Basketball 
Los Angeles Clippers guard Gradey Dick is fouled by a New Orleans Pelicans player during the second half of an NBA basketball game Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026, in Inglewood. (AP Photo/Wally Skalij) 
(Wally Skalij/AP Photo/Wally Skalij) 
Thirteen points doesn’t read like a breakout game, but the fourth-year guard acquired from Toronto in the Kawhi Leonard trade was an underrated factor in the Clippers’ thrilling 113-112 victory over Oklahoma City, putting on an impressive display in 19 minutes. 
Dick isn’t a sharpshooter yet, but his mobility and agility allow him to get up and down the floor and finish by the rim. Owner of a versatile skill set, Dick reads defenses well and can punish his opponents when they collapse on Garland or are too distracted off the ball. 
Dick was 5 for 7 from the field against both the Thunder and Hornets, capitalizing in transition. His hidden value is that he’s efficient without needing plays called for him. With Garland running the offense and Brook Lopez anchoring the middle, Dick fills the gaps, giving the second unit pace and spacing without taking touches from the stars. 
Isaiah Jackson
Hornets Clippers Basketball 
Los Angeles Clippers forward Isaiah Jackson looks to pass the ball during the second half of an NBA basketball game against the Charlotte Hornets, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Inglewood, Calif. (AP Photo/Caroline Brehman) 
(Caroline Brehman/AP Photo/Caroline Brehman) 
Jackson, a 6-8, 205-pound post player and former first-round draft choice in 2021 by the Lakers out of Kentucky, who was immediately traded to Indiana, has been a bit more productive off the bench in the Clips’ last two outings. With Lopez as the starting center, anchoring the team in the paint, Jackson has faced less pressure to carry the interior workload. 
Jackson is the most explosive of the trio. With more freedom on offense, he can play to his strengths: sprinting the floor, finishing lobs and drop-offs, and protecting the rim as a vertical shot-blocker. In Sunday’s win over Charlotte, he scored 13 points on 4-of-5 shooting and blocked 3 shots. He was quieter against the Thunder, with five points, three rebounds and a block, but his energy and rim-running give the Clippers a change of pace that Lopez, a more methodical, floor-spacing big, can’t provide.
Together, the trio gives the Clippers shooting, bounce and rim protection off the bench, exactly the support Garland needs. And against a Jazz team that has lost six in a row on the road, that depth could be the difference in stretching the modest winning streak to three.

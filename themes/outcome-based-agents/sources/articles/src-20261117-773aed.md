Minneapolis Proper to be developed as adaptive-reuse of historic landmark&nbsp;&nbsp;hotelbusiness.com

[page text]
Proper Hospitality
, 
CDT Realty Corp
 and 
Kelly Wearstler Studio
 have revealed that 20 Washington Avenue South, the landmark building designed by Minoru Yamasaki and completed in 1965, will become Minneapolis Proper.

CDT Realty Corp, led by founder Chad Tepley, acquired the building in 2025 and will convert it into a 165-room hotel to be operated by Proper Hospitality, preserving Yamasaki’s architecture with creative direction by Kelly Wearstler Studio. Pending historic approvals, construction is expected to begin in fall 2027, with opening targeted for summer 2029.

“I grew up in Chicago, so bringing Proper to the Midwest is personal for me,” said Brad Korzen, cofounder/CEO, Proper Hospitality. “Minneapolis has a strong identity in music, food and the arts, and we want this hotel to reflect that. With Chad and Kelly, we have an opportunity to preserve a remarkable building and create a place that becomes part of everyday life in the city, somewhere people come to eat, gather, spend time and stay.”

Like every Proper hotel, Minneapolis Proper is being conceived as a gathering place for the city, with restaurants, bars, wellness spaces and cultural programming that give local residents reasons to return throughout the year. The hotel will span more than 200,000 sq. ft. across nine levels, with 165 guestrooms and more than 40,000 sq. ft. of amenities.

Plans include two restaurants, one at ground level and one on the rooftop, and three bars from underground to rooftop. More than 15,000 sq. ft. will be dedicated to wellness, including recovery, fitness, spa and space for movement classes. More than 10,000 sq. ft. of event space, including a rooftop ballroom with an outdoor terrace, will host music, conversations and community programming. A retail shop will carry Minnesota-made goods. The building is also connected to the Minneapolis Skyway by an underground tunnel, a critical link to nearby residents and workers.

The project will adapt the former corporate headquarters for hospitality use while preserving its defining architectural features. Its existing structure and layout are well suited to the conversion, allowing CDT Realty Corp to bring a new use to the building while retaining much of its original fabric. CDT Realty Corp is working with 
ESG Architecture & Design
 as architect, 
Greiner Construction
 as general contractor and 
Nimble
 as the project manager.

20 Washington was constructed in 1965 and designed by master architect Yamasaki as the headquarters for Northwestern National Life Insurance Company. Yamasaki, best known as the architect of the original World Trade Center towers in New York City, is considered one of the visionaries behind the New Formalism movement of the late 1950s and 1960s. Yamasaki described the aim of his architecture as creating “serenity, surprise and delight,” an intent on full display at 20 Washington through 85-ft. perimeter columns, more than 400 tons of Vermont Verde Antique serpentine stone, two reflecting pools, more than two acres of parkways and mature trees and a sculpture garden featuring three original Masayuki Nagare granite sculptures installed when the building first opened.

“This is one of the most iconic buildings in the entire Midwest,” said Tepley. “From the moment you see it, you won’t forget it, but it’s always been off limits to the public. Now we get to imagine a future where neighbors and visitors from around the world can experience this incredible building from top to bottom. This collaboration between Proper and Kelly Wearstler is so exciting because they approach each project hyper-focused on how the city and community takes the lead in crafting uniquely memorable experiences. It’s exactly what this building deserves, and what our city deserves. It’s an honor to be working on such a profound project for Minneapolis, and this is exactly the right team to steward this building’s next chapter.”

Kelly Wearstler, founder/CEO, Kelly Wearstler Studio, said, “Minoru Yamasaki is one of the great voices in American architecture, and the opportunity to awaken this sleeping beauty, an iconic Midwestern landmark, is both an honor and a responsibility. As with every Proper Hotel, our ambition is to celebrate the building’s architectural legacy while bringing forward the distinct creative spirit of the community it calls home.”

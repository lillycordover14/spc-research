Today's big story:
 
Could Nvidia Stock Fall 50%? Follow The Cash
 
 
Nutanix (NTNX) closed at $58.94 on Monday, Nov 16, which works out to 11.4 times its reported fiscal 2026 earnings of $5.17 a share. That looks like a bargain for a software company. It is not. About 79.6% of last year's reported profit came from a one-time $1.2 billion tax benefit, and once you take it out the stock trades at 56.7 times earnings.
 
 
 
 
 
So the plain answer is no: Nutanix is not as cheap as its 11.4x P/E looks. On the company's own adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings it trades at 28.9 times, and on analysts' average estimate for fiscal 2027 it trades at 25.3 times. That is a full price, not a discount.
 
 
The better case for the stock is not in its earnings line but in its cash. Nutanix turned $841 million into free cash flow last year, a yield of about 4.9% on its approximate market value, and it holds about $843 million more cash than debt. That is what an owner is really paying for.
 
 
Where The Low P/E Comes From
 
 
A reader of 
our look at whether the supply chain explains Nutanix's slower growth
 asked us for more like it. Since that piece ran on Wednesday, Nov 11, the stock has slipped another 2.0% and now sits 22.4% below its 52-week high of $75.98. A stock that has fallen and shows a low P/E is exactly what an owner checks before buying more, so the next question is whether that P/E is real.
 
 
 
 
 
It mostly is not. In its fiscal fourth quarter, which ended in July, Nutanix released a $1.2 billion valuation allowance on its US deferred tax assets. In plain words: tax assets the company had kept off its books were put back on, and that one entry flowed through profit. The CFO said that without it, fiscal 2026 GAAP net income would have been $299 million, or $1.04 a share, instead of $1.507 billion and $5.17.
 
 
The same entry turned shareholders' equity from negative $726 million at the end of April to positive $703 million at the end of July. The balance sheet looks healthier on paper, but no new cash came in.

The Cash Tells A Better Story
 
 
Strip out the accounting and the business itself is growing. Revenue rose 12.4% in fiscal 2026. GAAP earnings excluding the tax benefit were up 60.0% from $0.65 a share in fiscal 2025. Analysts' fiscal 2027 average is 14.2% above last year's non-GAAP earnings.
 
 
 
 
 
Story Continues
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Cash is where Nutanix looks reasonable. The $841 million of free cash flow was a 29% margin on revenue, and the market value is only about 20.5 times that figure. Management's fiscal 2027 guidance calls for $850 million to $950 million of free cash flow; at the midpoint that is a 5.2% yield on the approximate market value at Monday's close. The company ended July with $2,361 million of cash and short-term investments against $1,519 million of debt.
 
 
So the honest framing, in our view, is this: Nutanix is not a cheap stock on earnings, but it is a fairly priced one on cash. Those are different things, and the 11.4x figure blurs them.
 
 
What Has To Hold For The Cash Case
 
 
A forward multiple of 25.3 times needs growth to keep coming. Three things would test it.
 
 
 
Recurring revenue.
 Annual recurring revenue (ARR) was $2.549 billion at the end of July, up 16%, with net dollar-based retention of 106%. If that growth rate slows sharply, the multiple has less support.
 
 
 
Servers and start dates.
 Management's fiscal 2027 guidance already assumes server supply constraints continue and that more orders come with future start dates. That is the supply-chain question from our earlier piece, now built into the plan.
 
 
 
A heavier first quarter for cash.
 The guidance includes $33 million to $43 million of restructuring charges, affecting about 5% of the global workforce, with $30 million to $35 million paid in cash in Q1. The Q1 revenue guide is $755 million to $765 million.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Nutanix fell 2.4% on Monday. It was a down day for the whole market: the S&P 500 lost 1.9% and QQQ dropped 2.7%.

What An Owner Can Do
 
 
If you own Nutanix because the P/E looked low, it is worth resetting the yardstick. Judge the stock on free cash flow and ARR growth, not on the 11.4x headline. If you are thinking of buying more, we think the cash yield and net cash give some cushion, but you are paying about 25 times next year's expected earnings, which leaves little room if growth slows.
 
 
The idea underneath is simple: a single accounting entry can make a stock look cheap, so check what the earnings are made of before you trust the multiple.
 
 
That is how we try to read a stock: start from the cash, then test the story. We are happy with that approach, but if you size up a stock like Nutanix a better way, we would genuinely like to hear it.

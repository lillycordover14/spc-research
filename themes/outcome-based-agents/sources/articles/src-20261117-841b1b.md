Siemens Combines Industrial AI and Additive Manufacturing for Adaptive Production&nbsp;&nbsp;ARC Advisory Group

[page text]
November 17, 2026

By
Marianne D’Aquila

Category:

Company and Product News

The approach connects AI-assisted engineering, virtual validation, additive manufacturing, and automated post-processing to support more flexible product development and production.
Siemens
 is developing an end-to-end digital thread that combines industrial AI with additive manufacturing to support more adaptive product development and production. The approach connects agentic, prompt-based engineering with an adaptive manufacturing environment so that product requirements can move from design and virtual validation through manufacturing preparation and production.
The company is demonstrating the concept using an additively manufactured robotic gripper for a humanoid robot, while also developing an adaptive production cell that brings together additive manufacturing, automation, post-processing, and software.
Moving Toward Prompt-Based Engineering
Working with AGIBOT, Siemens is demonstrating how an agentic AI workflow can automate parts of the engineering process for task-specific robotic grippers. Instead of manually redesigning a gripper for each application, a user describes the object to be handled and the required task.
The AI workflow then selects an appropriate gripper type, adapts its geometry in Siemens Designcenter, simulates the design, validates the robot interface and handling requirements, and prepares the data for additive manufacturing. Through Model Context Protocol (MCP) integration, the AI agent can access engineering software functions and orchestrate the individual workflow steps.
The objective is not to remove engineering validation, but to reduce the amount of manual interaction required across individual engineering tools. The resulting component can be virtually validated and prepared for manufacturing before physical production begins.
Extending Adaptive Engineering into Production
Generating more individualized designs also creates challenges downstream. Production systems must be able to accommodate greater variation in part geometry, processing requirements, and production sequences without sacrificing repeatability.
Siemens is therefore working with EOS and AM Solutions on an adaptive production cell at its Electronics Factory in Erlangen. The planned environment integrates additive manufacturing, automation, software, and automated post-processing into a connected process chain designed to handle different components and manufacturing requirements.
Post-processing is being included as part of the production architecture rather than treated as a separate downstream activity. This is important for moving additive manufacturing beyond individual printing steps toward repeatable industrial production workflows.
Building an End-to-End Digital Thread
Integrated engineering and production data provide the foundation for the approach. Design and simulation information must remain connected with manufacturing preparation, production, and operations so industrial AI systems can work across the broader process rather than within isolated applications.
Siemens also emphasizes that quality, traceability, security, and human responsibility remain important requirements as AI becomes more deeply integrated into industrial engineering and production workflows.
This broader digital-thread approach reflects an industry shift toward connecting engineering intent more directly with manufacturing execution. ARC has also highlighted how digital threads can improve traceability and create stronger data foundations for AI-enabled manufacturing.

Helping Manufacturers Identify Industrial AI Applications
Siemens has also expanded its 
AM I Navigator
 to incorporate industrial AI. The tool is intended to help manufacturers identify and prioritize AI applications across additive manufacturing, including design, engineering, and production.
Companies can use the assessment to identify potential pilot projects and establish a roadmap for broader deployment rather than introducing industrial AI without a defined manufacturing use case.
The agentic AI workflow for the humanoid robotic gripper is being presented at Formnext 2026 in Frankfurt.

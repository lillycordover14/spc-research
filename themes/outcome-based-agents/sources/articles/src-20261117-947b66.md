Crew Rescued From Sinking LPG Tanker Off Bulgaria as Pace of Attacks Grows
                        

Gas Husky was at least the 17th ship struck in the western Black Sea since mid-July (Bulgarian Defense Ministry)

                                By
                                
The Maritime Executive

                                2026-11-17 12:24:34
                            

Bulgaria’s Air Force and Maritime Administration coordinated the rescue of the crew from a Greek-owned LPG carrier that went down off the coast on Tuesday, November 17. Bulgarian officials did not comment on the circumstances of the loss, but Romania’s maritime authority quickly reported that it was another suspected drone strike after it had warned ships about the dangers over the weekend.

The Liberian-flagged LPG carrier 
Gas Husky
 (7,218 dwt) was inbound for Romania when Bulgaria’s Maritime Administration Agency reports the vessel “disappeared from radar” shortly after midnight local time. It was approximately 50 nautical miles east of Byala on the central Bulgarian coast. 

The rescue agency reported it detected an emergency position independent radio beacon (EPIRB) signal around 0800 this morning and requested that the Air Force conduct an aerial survey. The aircraft team detected an overturned vessel, which was believed to be the 
Gas Husky
. About 300 meters from the capsized vessel, a life raft was spotted. The raft fired a flare as the plane approached, signaling there were people aboard.

A rescue helicopter reached the raft at mid-morning and hoisted 19 Filipino crewmembers from the LPG carrier aboard. They were taken to a local hospital and reported to be in good condition. A cutter from the Border Police was also dispatched to survey the area.

Built in 2012, the LPG tanker was operated by Stealth Gas of Greece. Pictures released by the Bulgarian Air Force showed the vessel lying on its side. It was reported to be sinking.

The 
Gas Husky
 was in close proximity to where a small cargo ship was sunk yesterday in another suspected drone strike. Bulgaria also successfully 
rescued
 the crew from the cargo ship 
Betul 8
. It followed three ships struck to the north in the Romanian zone on Saturday night and one more struck on Friday.

Bulgarian authorities are reporting this was the seventh commercial vessel struck in its EEZ since the beginning of October. The security firm Diaplous reported that at least 17 vessels have been attacked in the western area of the Black Sea since mid-July. 

Russian officials have not acknowledged the attacks on vessels off Bulgaria and Romania, while the local authorities have only said they were investigating the attacks and analyzing drone fragments found on the ships. Romania, however, cautioned vessels to avoid the anchorage off the Danube Delta, reporting that the risk remains high in the region.

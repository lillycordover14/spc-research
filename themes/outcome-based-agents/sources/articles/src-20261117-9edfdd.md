WKU stuns Jax State, Bears notch OT pick-six: Recapping a wild Week 11 in CUSA
WKU scores two touchdowns in the final two seconds to upset Jax State. Missouri State completes a rare OT feat.
by
 
 
Steve Helwick
Nov 17, 2026, 6:23 AM UTC
No conference delivered more entertaining finishes across the board in Week 11 than Conference USA.
All 10 CUSA teams took the field Saturday, and four of the five games were decided by one score. From two touchdowns in the final two seconds to an 88-yard overtime pick-six, the conference delivered a memorable afternoon. Here’s a recap of a wild Week 11 in Conference USA:
WKU scores two touchdowns in final two seconds, hands Jax State first CUSA loss
How often do you see two touchdowns scored in the final two seconds of a game?
That improbable outcome transpired Saturday in Bowling Green, KY. WKU trailed Jacksonville State 24-21 with the clock winding down. The Hilltoppers were set from the 10-yard line as early as 19 seconds remaining but needed to allow time for Gamecock substitutions. Despite having a timeout in its backpocket, WKU let the clock run down to seven seconds before snapping the ball.
Quarterback Brock Glenn rolled to his right, faced an unblocked blitzer and backpedaled all the way to the 23-yard line. He launched a pass toward the back of the end zone, where Jyziah Rockwell made a spectacular diving catch to give WKU a 28-24 lead with two seconds remaining.
Even after the game-deciding touchdown, the Hilltoppers weren’t finished. On Jax State’s last-ditch lateral attempt, WKU recovered a fumble just before the end zone for its second touchdown in a two-second span, sealing a 34-24 upset. The Hilltoppers snapped the Gamecocks’ six-game win streak and handed them their first conference loss of the season, as Jax State fell to 25-5 all-time in CUSA play.
Perhaps the most stunning element of the upset was WKU more than doubling Jax State’s rushing output. The Hilltoppers recorded 125 rushing yards and three touchdowns, led by Glenn’s 71 yards and two scores. Meanwhile, a Jax State ground attack that had found its footing in recent weeks was limited to 48 yards on 24 attempts. WKU’s run defense was among the nation’s worst at the beginning of the season, but the vastly improved unit delivered when it mattered most, allowing the Hilltoppers to pull off a signature upset and keep their bowl hopes alive.
Boamah’s OT pick-six lifts Missouri State to OT win
The unusual finishes weren’t limited to the WKU-Jax State showdown. How about an overtime game where only one team possesses the ball?
Missouri State walked off New Mexico State in overtime on an 88-yard interception return by cornerback Joel Boamah, who won the CUSA Defensive Player of the Week honors for his highlight. The Bears became the first team since 2018 to win an overtime contest without sending its offense onto the field since, when Cincinnati defeated SMU on an 86-yard pick-six by James Wiggins.
The Bears’ offense already played a crucial role in overtime. Trailing 21-14, Missouri State strung together an 8-play, 80-yard drive in the final minutes. Quarterback Henry Belin IV found star wide receiver Isaiah Myers for a 29-yard gain to move the team into scoring position, setting up a 5-yard Connor Lair touchdown to tie the game at 21-21.
The victory kept Missouri State’s bowl eligibility hopes intact at 4-6, while eliminating New Mexico State from postseason contention at 3-7. It was a strong performance by a Bears defense which secured three takeaways, including two pick-sixes. Boamah wasn’t the only defender to reach the end zone, as defensive end Dylan Brooks also took an interception the distance in the second quarter. Thanks to their efforts, Missouri State is now 4-1 at home in year one of the Casey Woods era, winning three consecutive conference matchups in Springfield.
Liberty survives closest call in conference play vs. Middle Tennessee
Liberty continues to roll. The Flames secured their ninth consecutive victory, tying Boise State for the third-longest streak in the country behind Utah and UCLA.
However, Saturday’s home victory over Middle Tennessee pushed the Flames in unfamiliar territory. After producing seven-straight double-digit wins, the 28-23 win marked the team’s first one-score finish since Week 2. Middle Tennessee’s 23 points also matched the most allowed by a stingy Liberty defense all season.
The Flames largely contained a Middle Tennessee rushing attack which had performed well in recent weeks, but Roman Gagliano provided Liberty a challenge with 202 passing yards and two touchdowns. Meanwhile, the Flames leaned heavily on their ground game with 370 rushing yards and three touchdowns, led by 126 from running back Kam Davis.
The win propelled Liberty to No. 22 in the rankings, becoming the first CUSA team represented in the AP Poll since the program finished No. 25 in 2023. Additionally, the Flames secured their second conference championship appearance under Jamey Chadwell, punching their ticket to the Dec. 4 title game.
Minicucci Magic boosts the 302 over the 305
With two 4-5 teams squaring off in Newark, DE, Saturday’s result carried major bowl eligibility implications. It was an elongated game of call-and-response. Every time Delaware scored, FIU matched. Delaware’s 7-0 start turned into a 7-7 ballgame. 10-7 turned into 10-10. And 17-10 was about to turn into 17-17 with 5:42 remaining in the contest.
But Delaware blocked FIU’s game-tying extra point, preserving a 1-point cushion heading into the final stretch. On the the Fightin’ Blue Hens’ ensuing possession, quarterback Nick Minicucci delivered the game-changing play. Facing a 4th-and-1, the veteran quarterback shattered his career-high with a 62-yard scamper that was a originally ruled a touchdown until officials ruled that he was down at the 1-yard line. The ruling only delayed the inevitable as Minicucci punched in a rushing touchdown on the next play to give Delaware a 24-16 victory.
While Minicucci’s legs stole the show in crunch time, the quarterback also dominated with his arm. He set the program single-game completion percentage record by connecting on 27-of-30 attempts, delivering a pair of touchdowns in the home victory. The Fightin’ Blue Hens are 3-1 since Minicucci returned from injury, and they can clinch bowl eligibility with a win over New Mexico State on Nov. 21 or Jax State on Nov. 28.
Kennesaw State’s win streak reaches three in Huntsville
The reigning CUSA champions didn’t have the ideal start to the season, stumbling to a 1-6 record after a loss to Liberty on Oct. 22. Three weeks later, Kennesaw State is still alive for a postseason berth, producing three consecutive victories behind an inspiring resurgence from the offense.
Kennesaw State averaged 15.5 points per game during its six-game losing skid but has responded with 37.3 points per game during its last three wins, using its newfound offensive firepower to take down Middle Tennessee, Delaware, and Sam Houston. On Saturday, the Owls seamlessly dismantled the Bearkats on the road, 26-7, behind 181 passing yards from Landon Varnes and 101 rushing yards from Boogie Woods III. To reach that coveted six-win threshold and become bowl eligible, Kennesaw State must defeat FIU at home and WKU on the road.
Meanwhile, Sam Houston remains winless against FBS competition this season. The Bearkats are 0-2 since firing Phil Longo and turning to co-interim head coaches Freddie Aughtry-Lindsay and Eric Raisbeck. Sam Houston closes its disappointing season with matchups against Middle Tennessee and FIU, still searching for its first FBS victory since Nov. 15, 2025.

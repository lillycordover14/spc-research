Korea Investment Management Boosts Samsung Electronics Sixfold, Cuts SK Hynix to One-Fifth in Semiconductor ETF
Add preferred source
Korea Investment Management, in the regular rebalancing of its "ACE Global Semiconductor TOP4 Plus" ETF, raised its weighting in South Korea's Samsung Electronics roughly sixfold to 19.75% from 3.43% and slashed its stake in South Korea's SK Hynix to 3.29% from 17.58%. As a result, Samsung Electronics replaced SK Hynix as the fund's largest holding. The adjustment reflects third-quarter earnings and global market share data, while weightings for Nvidia, TSMC and ASML were left unchanged, with no new additions or removals.

Korea Investment Management adjusted the weightings of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix in opposite directions during the regular rebalancing of its global semiconductor exchange-traded fund (ETF), redrawing the portfolio so that Samsung Electronics, not SK Hynix, now holds the spot as the representative memory chip stock.

According to the asset manager on the 17th, the "ACE Global Semiconductor TOP4 Plus" ETF raised its weighting in Samsung Electronics by 16.32 percentage points to 19.75% from 3.43% through a recent regular rebalancing. Over the same period, its weighting in SK Hynix fell 14.29 percentage points to 3.29% from 17.58%. The adjustment took effect on the 5th of this month, and as of the previous day's (the 16th) closing price, Samsung Electronics stood at 19.81% and SK Hynix at 3.03%.

The adjustment changed the ETF's largest holding. Before the rebalancing, SK Hynix accounted for the biggest single-stock weighting, but after the rebalancing Samsung Electronics took that position. Samsung Electronics' weighting grew about sixfold from the prior level, while SK Hynix's shrank to about one-fifth.

The asset manager said it swapped the representative memory stock after factoring in third-quarter earnings and global market share data. Weightings for Nvidia, TSMC and ASML — the representative names in non-memory (system semiconductor), foundry and semiconductor equipment, respectively — were kept unchanged. No stocks were newly added or removed.

The product is structured to divide the global semiconductor industry into four segments — memory, non-memory (system semiconductors), foundry and semiconductor equipment — and concentrate investments in the leading company of each. About 80% of total assets are allocated to the four representative stocks, with the remainder spread across semiconductor companies with the largest global market capitalizations.

Nam Yong-soo, head of Korea Investment Management's ETF division, said, "This product invests in the representative stocks of each segment within the global semiconductor industry, and rebalancing is carried out based on data such as market share and revenue. It will serve as an option for investors seeking to benefit from industry growth while diversifying across the leading companies in each segment."

The rebalancing is seen as a case of ETF management reflecting a shift in the memory semiconductor landscape. Behind the diverging fortunes of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are third-quarter earnings and market share trends. The holdings and weightings, however, may change again in future management.

Weighting adjustments within an ETF are separate from the flow of funds from pension funds or retail investors and are in part carried out mechanically under rules set by the asset manager. Investors holding this ETF should note that, after the rebalancing, exposure to Samsung Electronics has increased and exposure to SK Hynix has decreased, so the impact of the two stocks' price moves on the product's returns has changed accordingly.

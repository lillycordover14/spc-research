Kestra’s Scott Wilson on Why the Breakaway Trend Still Has Room to Run


                      by                      AdvisorHub Digital                    

November 17, 2026

 

Kestra Private Wealth Services was founded by advisors who had lived inside the wirehouse world and understood why some of its best people eventually want out. Since then, the firm has built its business around a simple premise: independence only works if advisors have the right support—and the right people behind them. Sitting with us at the Advisors to Watch Summit, co-founder Scott Wilson talks about trust, authenticity, the evolution of the breakaway market and why it believes the flow of top advisors toward independence still has plenty of runway.

It seems like one of the things that’s important in this business is a sense of authenticity. How do you maintain that while still keeping it corporate?

Yeah, I mean, look, Rob Bartonstein, my partner, and I—at the heart of it, we’re financial advisors and we love financial advisors. We believe they are a unique group of people, and we try to be as authentic about it as possible. I think people who want to leave the wirehouse or a bank situation want to follow people they can trust. In this business today, it’s all about who you trust, being authentic and letting people know what you believe in. They want somebody who, when the chips are down a little bit, they know is going to be there for them.

And if you tell them something, you better deliver on it, because the minute you don’t deliver on certain promises you make to people, it’s over. People—especially financial advisors—talk. If you don’t deliver on what you tell people you’re going to do, it’s going to be a very short-lived relationship.

With the growth of the independent space over the past decade, there are so many options. How do you differentiate yourselves to breakaway advisors

There’s a lot of great firms here at this conference, and we at Kestra Private Wealth try to differentiate ourselves really through a fully supported model. We believe that if you’re exiting a wirehouse or a W-2 model, having somebody by your side to help with real estate, compliance and technology gives you the best opportunity to set up your firm successfully. We think that fully supported model is a great way to differentiate ourselves in the marketplace.

How has the independent space changed over the past 10 to 15 years?

Yeah, it’s been rewarding. We were a little early back in the day when we started, but the quality of advisors today, along with the quality of the resources and models available for advisors to provide sophisticated solutions to clients, has been exciting. The trend of breaking away is strong, and we continue to see amazing firms want to set up their own firms and really build brands for themselves and their families.

What are you noticing in terms of trends regarding wirehouse breakaways? Are you seeing that slowing down or picking up?

I think the trend continues to be strong. The wind is at our backs. The financial institutions, especially the big banks, have struggled to find a way to gain market share, and generally that’s been through headcount reductions and compensation changes. I think as long as that continues, the future is bright, and the quality of advisors continues to be tremendous. So we’re excited about the future and think the trend is definitely strong.

[page text]
November 17, 2026
By 
Mandy Dalugdug
Photo credit: ElevenLabs
Kotoba co-founder and CEO Noriyuki Kojima, ElevenLabs co-founder and CEO Mati Staniszewski, and Kotoba co-founder and CTO Jungo Kasai
ElevenLabs
 has agreed to acquire Japan-focused voice AI developer 
Kotoba Technologies
.
The 
London
 and 
New York
-headquartered company, which operates AI music platform 
ElevenMusic
, 
announced the deal in a blog post
 on Monday (
November 16
).
ElevenLabs
 did not disclose financial terms in its blog post, but 
Nikkei Asia
 
reports
 that the deal is worth more than 
USD $100 million
.
Business Insider Japan
 
reports
 that 
ElevenLabs
 CEO 
Mati Staniszewski
 put the price at around 
$100 million
 at a press conference on Tuesday (
November 17
, Japan time).
According to 
Nikkei Asia
, 
ElevenLabs
 will buy all of Kotoba’s shares, and Kotoba will become a wholly owned subsidiary.
ElevenLabs
 says it expects the acquisition to close later in 
Q4 2026
, subject to customary regulatory approvals and closing conditions.
Kotoba
 was founded in 
2023
 by AI researchers 
Noriyuki Kojima
, its CEO, and 
Jungo Kasai
, its CTO.
The company is headquartered in 
San Francisco
, with its research and development base in 
Tokyo
.
ElevenLabs
 calls Kotoba “the leading Japanese voice AI lab.”
Kotoba’s voice model, 
Koto
, covers Japanese text-to-speech, speech-to-text, and speech-to-speech for simultaneous interpretation, according to 
ElevenLabs
.
Salesforce
 uses Koto to handle Japanese-language conversations in its 
Agentforce Voice
 product.
“To bring the value of AI to every person, it needs to be natural and intuitive to interact with,” said 
Mati Staniszewski
, co-founder and CEO of 
ElevenLabs
. “The language people speak and the social norms they follow need to be reflected in the technology’s architecture from the very start.
“
Kotoba
 has done exactly that for Japan, building state-of-the-art voice models that have already been adopted by some of the world’s leading enterprises. Together, we can take that even further together and build the full interaction layer for AI in Japan.”
“When we founded Kotoba, our goal was to build a frontier AI lab from Japan,” said 
Kojima
, co-founder and CEO of 
Kotoba Technologies, Inc.
 “Along the way, we’ve seen the enormous potential of voice AI, and three years later, we’re more determined than ever to build technology that benefits society.
“I’m incredibly excited for 
Kotoba
 to join 
ElevenLabs
. Together, we can accelerate the development and adoption of voice AI, bringing tangible benefits to people and businesses in Japan and beyond.”
“I’m incredibly excited for 
Kotoba
 to join 
ElevenLabs
. Together, we can accelerate the development and adoption of voice AI, bringing tangible benefits to people and businesses in Japan and beyond.”

, 
Kotoba Technologies
Kojima
 holds a PhD in computer science from 
Cornell
 and previously worked as a research scientist at 
Hugging Face
, according to ElevenLabs.
He also co-built 
Fugaku-LLM
, an open-source Japanese model trained from scratch on 
RIKEN
‘s Fugaku supercomputer.
“Kotoba’s team has done exceptional research on the Japanese language,” said 
Piotr DÄbkowski
, co-founder and CTO of 
ElevenLabs
. “It is an incredibly intricate language that poses a whole range of technical challenges when building voice models. Bringing their research together with ours will improve Japanese across the entire stack ElevenLabs builds.”
Kasai
 holds a PhD in computer science and engineering from the 
University of Washington
, and was previously a research assistant professor at the 
Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago
.
ElevenLabs
 describes him as “one of Japan’s most-cited researchers.”
“I’m incredibly excited for Kotoba and ElevenLabs to join forces and lead the next wave of voice AI in Japan and East Asia,” said 
Kasai
. “Since I began my AI research career, I’ve dreamed of building a frontier AI research lab in Tokyo and creating products that transform people’s lives, from voice AI agents to simultaneous translation. Our journey is just beginning.”
ElevenLabs
 says the acquisition is “just the first step” in a long-term plan for 
Japan
.
Over the coming months, 
ElevenLabs
 intends to expand its local sales leadership in Japan and continue investing in infrastructure there.
ElevenLabs
 already has a subsidiary in 
Japan
.
It 
launched ElevenLabs G.K. in Tokyo
 in 
April 2025
, describing it at the time as its first international subsidiary.
Salesforce Ventures
 has invested in both companies.
“Voice is emerging as a powerful new interface for AI, with potential to transform how people interact with technology,” said 
Nowi Kallen
, a partner at 
Salesforce Ventures
. “We’ve been proud to back both ElevenLabs and Kotoba, and together, they’re uniquely positioned to bring more natural, intuitive AI experiences to businesses and consumers worldwide.”
Salesforce Ventures
 took part in Kotoba’s 
$10 million
 seed round in June, which was led by 
Kindred Ventures
 and also included 
Sony
 Innovation Fund
.
That round brought Kotoba’s total funding to date to 
$23 million
, the company said at the time.
Developers at 
Salesforce
 also use ElevenLabs’ voice infrastructure, according to ElevenLabs.
ElevenLabs
 was 
valued at $22 billion
 in a 
$300 million
 employee tender offer completed in September.
That was double the 
$11 billion
 valuation it reached in its 
$500 million
 Series D round, announced in February.
At the time of the tender, 
ElevenLabs
 said its 
ElevenAgents
 platform was fielding more than 
15 million
 conversations per week, and that enterprise customers accounted for 
55%
 of its revenues.
The company entered AI music generation in 
August 2025
, 
launching Eleven Music
 (now 
ElevenMusic
) alongside licensing agreements with 
Merlin
 and 
Kobalt
.
In September, 
ElevenLabs
 signed 
a multi-year licensing agreement with Universal Music Group
, which the two companies described as its first with a major music company.
Under that deal, 
ElevenLabs
 is building a separate AI-powered platform for fans, covering remixes, mashups, and “new track interpretations.”

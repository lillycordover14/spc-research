fileAI Opens in Tokyo and Launches fileForensics as AI-Generated Document Fraud Rises
 
 
 
 
  PR Newswire 
 
 
Mon, November 16, 2026 at 7:00 PM CST
 
4 min read
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
TOKYO, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- fileAI, the enterprise intelligence company behind fileForge, has opened its Japan office, marked by a launch event at The Peninsula Tokyo on 10 November 2026. The opening coincides with the wider release of fileForensics, which screens business documents for signs of editing, AI generation, reuse and hidden content before they are used to approve a payment, a claim or a new customer.
 
 
 
 
Backed by SMBC Asia Rising Fund and JRE Ventures, the Singapore-based enterprise AI company brings its governed AI platform and new document fraud detection to Japan
 
 
 
The Japan office builds on fileAI's established base in Singapore, where its platform runs in production at enterprises across regulated industries such as banking, financial services, insurance and healthcare. The company serves more than 500 customers worldwide and has processed over 1 billion files, and its outcome-driven AI agents cut processing time by more than 90% compared with manual handling.
 
 
 
 
 
A governed platform for running AI in real business workflows
 
 
Japanese enterprises are adopting generative AI quickly, but many are still building the rules needed to run it safely at scale. A May 2026 Teikoku Databank survey found that 34.5% of Japanese companies now use generative AI in their operations, with 86.7% of users reporting positive results, yet accuracy and internal operating rules remain key challenges [
1
]. Japan's national framework, set by the AI Promotion Act and the AI Basic Plan, commits ¥1 trillion in public investment over five years and places "trustworthy AI" at the centre of national policy [
2
][
3
].
 
 
fileAI acts as a trusted data intelligence partner, turning enterprises' documents into decision-ready AI workflows on a single, auditable platform. It unifies data capture, preparation, governance and orchestration into SOP-driven workflows, so every result can be traced, checked and reused. For regulated industries such as banking, insurance and financial services, this means AI that meets the same standards of control and accountability as the rest of the business.
 
 
 
 
 
fileForensics: knowing whether to trust a document
 
 
Extraction tells you what a document says. fileForensics tells you whether to believe it.
 
 
Fake photos, invoices and statements are now easy to make with a phone app or a single AI prompt. An accurate extraction of an edited invoice is still an edited invoice. For most businesses, these problems come to light only after payment, approval or onboarding, when the money has already gone.
 
 
fileForensics screens the original file at intake, before its data is extracted or acted on. In a single run it examines eight areas, from file structure, metadata and edit history to image tampering and AI-generated content, and reports on each one separately with the evidence behind it. A finding is a reason to review, not a verdict. The decision stays with the customer's own team, who can act before any money moves.

Arkansas' Ian Geffrard (95) and Texas' Arch Manning (16) are shown, Saturday, Nov. 22, 2025, during the second quarter at Darrel K Royal-Texas Memorial Stadium in Austin, Texas. (Hank Layton/WholeHogSports)

Steve Sarkisian got a memorable introduction to the Arkansas-Texas rivalry in September 2021.
Sarkisian's first loss as the Longhorns' head coach came in Week 2, when Arkansas thumped No. 15 Texas 40-21 at Reynolds Razorback Stadium. The game came 43 days after Texas and Oklahoma formally agreed to join the SEC, offering Sarkisian an early taste of the challenges that awaited in the conference.
It also gave him a glimpse of the rivalry's significance to Arkansas fans, especially those who grew up during the Southwest Conference era.
“It’s a longstanding rivalry,” Sarkisian said Monday. “I think it goes back about 143 years or something like that. I’m well aware of the rivalry. I don’t forget 2021, going there as a ranked team and losing, and then having the field stormed and all that entailed.”
Five years later, much has changed for both programs. 
Texas has since been to multiple College Football Playoffs, including a semifinal appearance two years ago. The Longhorns have established themselves as an SEC contender, making their adjustment to the conference in 2024 appear seamless.
 
Texas coach Steve Sarkisian leads his team onto the field to play Arkansas during an NCAA college football game Saturday, Sept. 11, 2021, in Fayetteville, Ark. (AP Photo/Michael Woods)
On the flip side, the most success Arkansas has experienced since going 9-4 in the 2021 season is a pair of seven-win campaigns in 2022 and 2024, both of which ended with Liberty Bowl victories. The Razorbacks are now trying to find their footing under first-year coach Ryan Silverfield, who is tasked with getting the program back to competing consistently in the SEC.
When No. 6 Texas (8-2, 5-2 SEC) hosts Arkansas on Saturday at 2:30 p.m., neither roster will have any players remaining from that 2021 matchup.
“I think from a staff perspective, we know,” Sarkisian said. “A lot of us were there in ’21 when we went there and all that it entailed. I think the good thing for us right now is we have Brad Spence and Ian Geffrard in our locker room now, and they know the importance of this game in Arkansas’ locker room. [They’re] able to convey that message so our players can understand the magnitude of the game clearly from a fan base perspective.”
Spence was a linebacker at Arkansas from 2023-24 and recorded 7 tackles and 1 sack in a 20-10 loss to Texas in November 2024. Geffrard played defensive tackle for the Razorbacks from 2023-25, and he recorded 1 tackle against the Longhorns in a 52-37 loss last November in Austin. 
“This is a very big game, for both schools,” Sarkisian said. “It’s a very big game for us for obvious reasons, and they are an improving football team. They continue to get better … especially on the defensive side of the ball. These guys on offense can run the ball. I think they’ve rushed for 150 yards on four separate occasions against SEC opponents.”
Jokers on you
Arkansas cornerback Joker Johnson did not win SEC defensive player of the week honors from the conference office, but the fourth-year transfer from Tulane had to be a strong candidate.
Johnson intercepted two passes from South Carolina’s Cutter Woods and he returned one of them for a 63-yard touchdown that drastically changed the tone of the game by giving Arkansas a 24-6 lead. Johnson also had an interception and forced a fumble, both inside the red zone, and had eight tackles.
Johnson became the third Arkansas player since 1956 to record multiple interception returns for a touchdown in the same season, joining Orlando Watters (1993) and David Dudley (1985). He had a 25-yard pick 6 in the Razorbacks’ 31-21 win at Vanderbilt on Oct. 17.
The SEC defensive player of the week was awarded to Texas safety Jelani McDonald, who had 3 interceptions, 11 tackles and a pass breakup in a 22-21 win at LSU.
Arkansas defensive coordinator Ron Roberts has talked about Johnson all season.
“Obviously he’s just got tremendous savvy, skill, knowledge,” Roberts said. “The kid’s just done an amazing job getting better all the time. The play on the pick 6 was just a really nice break and knowledge of what’s getting ready to happen, being patient and then makes a nice catch. 
“The one on the goal line was probably more impressive. He doesn’t get the pick 6 for it, but the one on the goal line, that was a really good read play and he was in great position and just does an incredible job.”
 
Arkansas defensive back Joker Johnson returns an interception for a touchdown, Saturday, Nov. 14, 2026, during the third quarter of the Razorbacks' 34-6 win over South Carolina at Reynolds Razorback Stadium in Fayetteville. (Hank Layton/WholeHogSports)
Double play
Arkansas had top receivers Chris Marshall and Antonio Jordan on the field together for numerous plays against South Carolina and both players delivered.
Marshall had a 23-yard back shoulder touchdown catch from KJ Jackson among his 5 receptions for 63 yards. Jordan had a 28-yard over-the-defender’s shoulder touchdown catch among his 5 receptions for 62 yards.
Their tandem work should give Texas and LSU something to plan for the next two weeks.
“It forces defensive coordinators to make a decision,” Arkansas offensive coordinator Tim Cramsey said. “They cause some mismatches outside, big bodies out there that have shown the ability to catch those redline balls and gets guys to think twice about playing man coverage, gets guys to think about putting the extra hat in the box. And that also helps the run game out when you get Chris and AJ healthy.”
Familiar kicker
Texas kicker Gianni Spetic, one of 20 semifinalists for the Lou Groza Award, was the starter at Memphis under Silverfield in 2025.
Spetic is 17 for 18 (94%) on field goal attempts with a long of 52 yards. He made a key 50-yard field goal in the fourth quarter of the Longhorns’ 22-21 victory at LSU last week.
“The protection, snap, hold and all those things play a part in it, as does how hard we rush him in practice,” Sarkisian said. “Credit to him. He’s off to a heck of a start this season, and hopefully he’ll continue to make the big kicks when we need him.”
In his lone season at Memphis, Spetic was 15 for 20 (75%) on field goals with a 55-yard long. He was 2 for 3 during a 32-31 victory over Arkansas on Sept. 20, 2025.

Quarter of runs
Arkansas did not attempt a pass in the fourth quarter of its 34-6 victory over South Carolina. With a big lead and wanting to milk clock, the Razorbacks ran the ball 17 times for 53 yards in the period.
“It feels good, man,” Arkansas offensive coordinator Tim Cramsey said Tuesday. “When you can run the ball, and when we've had success running the ball, that opens everything else up, and it allows you then to hit a screen here or there. It allows you then to hit the RPO here or there. It allows you to take the shot and the play-action ball because you feel really good about getting yourself [open].”
It was the first chance for Arkansas to be conservative with its play calling for extended time in SEC play. The Razorbacks finished with 168 sack-adjusted rushing yards and did not commit a turnover for the second time this season. They were also without a turnover in a 31-21 win at Vanderbilt.
“The smart play when you're up three possessions at home, first time for a long time in Arkansas football, is to keep that clock rolling,” Cramsey said. “Specifically with the way our defense was handling them. In my mind and the decision made from everybody was the only way things go bad there, and the only way you lose that game is if we do something dumb with the ball.”

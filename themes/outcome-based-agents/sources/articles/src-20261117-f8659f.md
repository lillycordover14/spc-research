ElevenLabs announced
 on November 16, 2026, that it is acquiring Kotoba Technologies, the Japanese voice AI lab behind the Koto model. The transaction is expected to close later in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to customary regulatory approvals and closing conditions.
The planned acquisition would combine ElevenLabs’ research resources and global business infrastructure with a team already developing and deploying Japanese speech systems. Kotoba’s work spans voice agents, simultaneous interpretation technology and its own interpretation app, which would give 
ElevenLabs
 specialized research expertise alongside existing routes into enterprise use.
Koto Adds Speech Models and Enterprise Deployments
ElevenLabs describes Koto as supporting Japanese text-to-speech, speech-to-text and speech-to-speech conversion for simultaneous interpretation. The planned combination is intended to help more enterprises build agents that handle complex Japanese conversations naturally.
Koto already powers Japanese-language conversations in Salesforce’s Agentforce Voice. 
Kotoba’s announcement
 also describes voice AI powering products at companies in Japan and the United States, interpretation technology supporting artists such as YOASOBI, and a simultaneous interpretation app called Kotoba.
Japanese Conversation Shapes the Technical Requirements
The technical rationale extends beyond speech recognition. ElevenLabs says Japanese sentences can omit subjects and objects, vocabulary changes with the relationship between speakers, and pauses can signal thought rather than awkwardness. Direct refusals are often avoided, while the concept of 
kūki o yomu
, or “reading the air,” describes the expectation that people will understand what remains unsaid.
Kotoba draws a product requirement from those features: natural voice AI must understand context, follow the flow of a conversation and decide when to respond, in addition to recognizing and generating speech accurately. That helps explain why Kotoba’s post-closing role spans research, product development and deployment support alongside model work.
Kotoba’s Research Team and Post-Closing Plans
Kotoba was founded in July 2023 by AI researchers Noriyuki Kojima and Jungo Kasai. Its 
corporate profile
 lists a San Francisco headquarters and a Tokyo operation, Kotoba Technologies Japan K.K., which was incorporated in October 2023.
Kojima holds a computer science PhD from Cornell, worked as a research scientist at Hugging Face and co-built Fugaku-LLM, an open-source model trained from scratch on the RIKEN Fugaku supercomputer. Kasai holds a computer science and engineering PhD from the University of Washington and was an assistant professor at Toyota Technological Institute at the University of Chicago.
After closing, the Kotoba team is expected to continue across research, product development and deployment support. Kotoba plans to combine its East Asian language expertise with ElevenLabs’ research capabilities and computing resources, improving voice AI first in Japanese and then in Korean and other East Asian languages. Insights from real-world Japanese deployments are intended to contribute to models and products ElevenLabs delivers worldwide.
ElevenLabs also plans to build out local sales leadership and continue investing in infrastructure in Japan, while pursuing closer work with Japanese enterprises and multinationals operating or expanding in the country. The planned arrangement gives Japan a dual role in that strategy: a source of deployment-driven research for worldwide products and a market where ElevenLabs intends to expand its commercial and technical presence.

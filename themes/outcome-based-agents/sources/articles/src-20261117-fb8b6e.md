[page text]
Sullivan’s campaign says Republican incumbent will ‘let that process run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified’
This combination of photos shows Alaska Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, in Anchorage, Ala., left, and Sen. Dan Sullivan, R-Ala., on Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, in Soldotna, Ala. (AP Photo/Lindsey Wasson)
By Mark Sabbatini
Juneau Independent
Democrat Mary Peltola is inching ever closer to an outright majority of first-choice ballots in Alaska’s U.S. Senate race, with an updated tally on Monday night giving her 49.98% of votes counted so far. Republican incumbent Sen. Dan S. Sullivan has 45.93%, 
according to
 the Alaska Division of Elections.
The Associated Press estimates 98% of ballots cast have been tallied. Peltola led by 0.3% when about 75% of ballots were counted as of Nov. 4 — the day after Election Day — and has seen her lead consistently expand since with votes mostly coming from rural areas where she is strongly supported. 
The Democratic challenger had 49.94% of the vote as of Saturday, when AP estimated 97% of votes were counted. 
Peltola’s lead of 4.05% exceeds the total of the two other candidates on the ballot — Gerald Heikes at 2.12% and retired Petersburg teacher Dan J. Sullivan at 1.65%.
An AP analysis of projected voted counted so far in Alaska’s 40 House districts shows the near-certainty of a Peltola first-ballot win: In a district with 85% of the votes counted she leads Sen. Sullivan by 9.8% of ballots counted so far; in another 90% of votes counted she leads by 23.8% and in a third with 95% counted she leads by 30.3%.
In contrast, Sen. Sullivan has a 3.4% lead in a district where 91% of votes have been counted and 1.3% in another district where 95% of ballots have been counted. Nearly all other districts have 98% to 99% of ballots counted.
Monday’s tally also includes 874 write-in votes, which would be immediately excluded at the beginning of the ranked choice process if it becomes necessary on Wednesday. That alone would be enough to give Petola a majority of first-choice votes before looking at the second-choice votes for any of the candidates.
The tally Monday is for 285,439 ballots, representing 47.15% of registered voters. NBC News estimates about 6,500 ballots remain to be counted.
Nate Adams, a spokesperson for Sullivan, issued a statement Monday night that "
Alaskans deserve a complete and accurate count, and we will let that process run its course until every legal vote is counted 
and certified" 
— with the bold emphasis as part of the statement. The Division of Elections is scheduled to certify the election Nov. 25 — a week after the ranked choice tally for any undecided races, and those results can be legally challenged in court until Dec. 5. 
"
 
The Division of Elections has work left to do, and we respect the Alaskans doing it," Adams said. "We will not be commenting on individual ballot updates or outside projections in the meantime. Senator Sullivan is grateful to the Alaskans who made their voices heard, and he remains focused on his work for our state. The campaign will have more to say by the end of this week.”

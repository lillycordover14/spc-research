Cardano's branded debit card has stopped working after payments partner Wirex pulled out of the custodial business that held and settled customer funds, leaving holders of the token without a confirmed replacement.

The product, launched in late 2025, let users spend ADA, Bitcoin, Ether and stablecoins anywhere Visa was accepted. Emurgo, Cardano's commercial arm, handled the ecosystem side while Wirex supplied the payment rails. Wirex has since decided to wind down that custodial infrastructure and shift to a non-custodial operating model, which brought the partnership behind the card to an end.

The Cardano Card team has said more details about the program will follow but has not confirmed whether another payment product will take the card's place. Former users therefore have no announced substitute to plan around.

A Difficult Stretch for ADA

The shutdown lands in the middle of a prolonged slump for the network's native token. ADA is trading near $0.15, roughly 95% below its record high of $3.09, and Cardano has slipped to 18th place in CoinGecko's rankings of digital assets by market capitalization.

The loss of the card also removes one of the few everyday spending channels tied to the ecosystem. Users had been able to draw on several digital assets through a single payment product connected to a global card network; that link to retail payments is now gone.

The episode also illustrates how much of Cardano's consumer-facing activity rests on outside providers. Because the payment product depended on infrastructure run by another company, a strategic shift at that partner was enough to take the service offline.

Founder Promises a Better 2027

Against that backdrop, Cardano founder Charles Hoskinson sought to calm frustrated holders. Responding on X to an investor's complaint, he wrote: "It's all going to be ok. Everyone will be happy next year."

He offered no price target, delivery date or list of specific developments to back the confidence, and the remarks do not establish when market conditions might improve. The message points to 2027 as the year conditions improve.

The comments follow Hoskinson's earlier clarification that his work on Cardano was finished. He explained that the network's original roadmap had been completed after more than 11 years of development by Input Output, covering the Ouroboros protocol, smart contract functionality and decentralized governance tools. He said his role would increasingly involve building applications on top of the network rather than extending its base layer, and rejected readings of his remarks as a signal that he was walking away from the project or writing it off.

Community reaction to the reassurance has been measured. One widely followed account replied that words alone cannot move markets, adding that holders still hope 2027 proves to be the year they have been waiting for.

What It Means for Holders

For investors, the two threads leave little hard information to work with. The card's future is unresolved, and the founder's outlook carries no measurable milestones against which progress can be judged. Attention is likely to center on whether ecosystem activity picks up enough to revive demand for ADA, and on whether Emurgo or another partner lines up a replacement payments product.

Cardano's payment ambitions also now hinge on finding infrastructure partners willing to hold customer funds, a business model Wirex has chosen to leave. Until a substitute is announced, the network's consumer payments story remains on hold while the token trades near multi-year lows.

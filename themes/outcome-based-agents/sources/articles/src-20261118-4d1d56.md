Cardano

However, the funds in its decentralized finance (DeFi) applications have dwindled, and Grayscale has retracted its ETF filings related to Cardano.

ADA trades around 15 cents, 95.1% below its all-time high of $3.09 in September 2021. The cryptocurrency has fallen 31.2% in the last 30 days, 15.1% over the previous week, and 68.4% over the past year. So, should investors buy Cardano before 2027, or is it wiser to wait?

3 Reasons to Consider Buying Cardano Before 2027

Cardano’s Dijkstra Upgrade Starts in the Fourth Quarter

Cardano is preparing for its Dijkstra upgrade, targeting phase 1 for the fourth quarter of 2026 and phase 2, known as Peras, for the second quarter of 2027. The Peras phase aims to speed up transaction finalization, meaning payments and trades can settle faster. With both phases scheduled to complete by mid-2027, buying now lets investors hold ADA before these enhancements go live.

About 58% of ADA Is Staked

Approximately 57.9% of ADA is staked, or delegated to pools that secure the network for rewards, equating to about 21.8 billion ADA worth around $3.3 billion. Cardano offers a staking system that doesn’t lock coins, giving holders the freedom to sell whenever they wish. The high percentage of staked coins indicates that most ADA holders prefer to stay invested.

In October, the net staking yield was about 2.1%, translating to roughly $21 annually for every $1,000 invested in ADA.

ADA Trades About 9% Above Its One-Year Low

ADA closed at around 14 cents, its one-year low, on June 28. It now trades about 9% higher than that low, giving investors a nearby floor at 15 cents to gauge risk. Our look at

2 Reasons to Hold Off on Buying Cardano

Cardano’s DeFi Deposits Fell About 81% in a Year

Cardano’s total value locked (TVL) in DeFi has plummeted about 81% over the past year, from about $284 million to around $54 million. This drop in deposits signals fewer transactions and lower fees on the network, weakening the token’s fundamentals.

Grayscale Pulled Its Cardano ETF Filings

On August 7, Grayscale withdrew its ETF filings for Cardano, sidelining ADA, for now, from the kind of spot funds that hold cryptocurrencies like XRP and Chainlink. Furthermore, ADA has slipped below its 200-day moving average of 20 cents, a key indicator of long-term trends, and needs to rise about 31% to surpass this level.

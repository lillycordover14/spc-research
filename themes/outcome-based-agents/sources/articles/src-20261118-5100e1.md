There is an emerging consensus that community engagement is an essential component for building resilience. The National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine report 
Advancing Health and Resilience in the Gulf of Mexico Region
 identifies a range of strategies for incorporating community perspectives into health and resilience efforts. The Gulf Research Program’s 
Adaptive Capacities for Transformation (ACT) Initiative 
is taking these insights and putting them into action in three disaster-affected communities across the Gulf Coast — Houston, Texas; New Orleans, Louisiana; and Mobile, Alabama.  The ACT Initiative leverages science, collaboration, and strategic investments for communities to promote health and resilience in disaster adaptation.
The Process
The initiative began with a series of GRP-commissioned landscape assessments identifying groups, organizations, and institutions working at the intersection of health and resilience as well as the existing resources, needs, and challenges in each location. At initial orientation meetings in early 2025, findings were then vetted with individuals from the identified organizations to understand whether the findings rang true to them, ensuring the initiative stayed grounded in lived experience as well as scientific research. The meeting attendees also participated in a stakeholder mapping activity to identify other relevant organizations and make sure they were offered a seat at the table.

                    ACT Houston participants completing the stakeholder mapping activity.
                

As a follow-up, the GRP hosted a brainstorming activity that included both attendees from orientation and new participants identified in the stakeholder mapping activity. Participants were asked to provide as many responses as possible to the question: “From your perspective, what do the communities you regularly engage with need to deal with the effects of disasters?” Additional responses to the question were also gathered via an online brainstorming platform.
The outputs from the brainstorming sessions were supplemented with findings from the academic literature as well as local policies and plans. With help from teams of local volunteers, GRP staff narrowed the number of responses from about 300 responses to 60 unique ideas per location.
Once the list of priorities was finalized, participants took a survey to rate the ideas by their importance and to what extent they were already happening in the community. Participants also sorted ideas into the thematic categories of their choosing. Their responses were aggregated and analyzed using state-of-the-art techniques to help the GRP better understand local needs and priorities and their relationships to each other.

                    Participants from New Orleans learn about the sorting and rating activities.
                

Once the results of sorting and rating were synthesized, the GRP reconvened participants to review the results and provide feedback on them, particularly whether the results related to their real-life experiences. The response was encouraging.  As one participant remarked, “I can see myself — my words — in the data.”  
After confirming that the data indeed reflected the community perspective, the GRP drafted location-specific planning grants built around the priorities the community had rated as high in importance and low in happening. This iterative process of community engagement and data collection has enabled the GRP to better target its funding to address community-identified priorities. Participants particularly valued this feature of ACT, with one ACT New Orleans participant sharing that the ACT process “… is a great model for directing funding where it is most needed.”
ACT has not only been able to target funding effectively, but it has also changed the way in which community members work together by providing an opportunity to share information, experiences, and perspectives. A participant from Houston noted that ACT “gave [her] a meaningful space to connect with colleagues across Houston” and “reinforced how essential it is for academic work to stay deeply connected with community needs.”
Where are we going from here?
The selection of five planning grant proposals in each hub marks a transition from dialogue to design. Beginning April 1, 2026, project teams officially moved into the planning phase to develop place-based interventions rooted in community-identified needs. The 
planning grants
 aim to build on the early milestones of the previous meetings, turning budding connections into formalized cross-sectoral partnerships oriented around shared priorities. Building on the successes so far, the GRP has expanded the ACT Initiative to 
Baton Rouge, Louisiana
, which kicked off in July 2026.

                    Baton Rouge participants learn about the Initiative in the kickoff meeting.
                

The goal of ACT is to move beyond sets of isolated projects to durable systems of collaboration that can withstand future disasters. If the early meetings are any indication, that transformation is already underway.
ACT is funded by the Gulf Research Program and the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation.

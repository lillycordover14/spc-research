Aurzen Zip Pro Portable Projector Review: Upsized Cyber Foldable&nbsp;&nbsp;CNET

[page text]
Geoffrey Morrison/CNET		

 

 

						Aurzen Zip Pro					

	6.9

			$700 at Amazon					

				Pros & Cons			

 

						Good performance for the size					

 

						Cyberpunk-inspired looks					

 

						Touchscreen greatly improves usability					

 

						Performance is good for the size but only for the size					

 

						Price has also inched up (understandably)					

 

						No HDMI input					

It’s hard to make a portable projector stand out these days, as there are so many out there. But this one has a gimmick, and it’s not a 
gimbal
. The Zip Pro is a
 foldable
 
portable projector
. It’s a larger and more capable version of the 
Zip
, with greater brightness and higher resolution. Like its little brother, it lacks built-in streaming or an HDMI input, but with wireless connectivity and USB-C you should be able to get content from your phone/tablet/PC fairly easily. “Should” is the key word there. The Pro keeps the cyberpunk-inspired design from the Zip Cyber, which I think looks great.

Performance is decent for a small projector. There isn’t a ton of light but enough for a TV-sized image on any flat surface. The dual-hinge design lets the Zip Pro fold down to a small square for portability, or up and at various angles for convenient setup. You can get a claimed 2.5 hours of playtime with the internal battery. Getting content to the Zip can still be a challenge for some, depending on your other gear, but otherwise the Zip Pro is easy to use and can go just about anywhere.

Portability is the main benefit for the Zip Pro. If this is something you intend to leave at home for the occasional movie night, there are 
larger projectors
 for similar money with better performance. Those can’t fit in a backpack or large-ish pocket like the Zip Pro, however.

More from CNET

Specs

Resolution: 1,920×1,080 

Lumens: 200 (claimed)

Zoom: No

Lens shift: No

Image tech and light source type: DLP, LED

The Zip Pro looks like Aurzen just stretched the Zip in every dimension. It’s about double the size, but that still means it’s one of the smallest projectors I’ve tested. Inside the larger case Aurzen has upped the resolution and claimed brightness. While the Zip’s 720p was fine for its class, 1080p is noticeably more detailed and often worth a price premium. Because of the lack of an HDMI input (more on this in the next section), I wasn’t able to do my full suite of measurements. These are a little more “ballpark” than I normally publish, but should be fairly close. Aurzen claims 200 lumens. 
I measured
 135 in the Normal mode. That’s reasonable for a small, portable projector like this. Compare that to the Zip Cyber which managed 88 or the 
Anker Capsule 3’s
 123. Those are meaningful differences, but none of these will look “bright” unless they’re creating a relatively small image in a dark room.

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

In the brighter, louder, Conference “Turbo” mode (it’s labeled as both in the UI), I got 162 lumens. Unlike most bright-boost modes, this one doesn’t mess with the color and only makes the color temperature slightly bluer. So that’s not a bad trade-off for a few more lumens, but if you’re sitting near the Zip Pro you’ll definitely notice the extra fan noise. While 162 is not the 200 that Aurzen claims, very few projectors ever match their specs. I usually expect some variation, and while ~20% is pushing that a bit, it’s still basically close enough.

Color isn’t great, again that’s typical for portables, with most colors looking a little off from natural. These are the kind of sacrifices made for small sizes. Contrast, though, just isn’t great regardless of size. I got an average of 155:1 — among the lowest I’ve measured. That said, every portable projector I’ve measured has been in the “few hundred:1” range and when you’re dealing with an effectively infinite scale for contrast ratio, there’s less difference between 150, 300, or even 600:1 than what it seems on paper. 

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

The small touchscreen on top makes setup pretty easy, including switching to a keypad to put in Wi-Fi passwords. I wish there were more, or any really, picture adjustments beyond brightness levels. While there’s no zoom or lens shift, the hinged design makes it easy to position and get angled onto whatever flat surface you’re using as a screen. 

Aurzen claims 2.5 hours of playtime, but that’s in the ECO mode, which is about 30% dimmer than the Normal mode. It’s easy enough to just move the projector a little closer to the wall/screen since a smaller image will be a brighter one. The top screen also shows you battery remaining in minutes, which is great. Because it’s powered via USB-C, you can connect a portable battery bank to recharge when away from an outlet.

Connections

HDMI inputs: 0

USB port: 1 USB-C

Audio: Bluetooth

Internet: Wi-Fi (2.4 and 5GHz)

Streaming interface: None (Screen mirroring/casting only) 

Unlike most projectors, unlike most displays of any kind really, there’s no HDMI input. This makes it a little more difficult to get content to the Zip Pro. You can connect some devices via USB-C and others wirelessly by screen mirroring, AirLink, Google Cast and so on. When I reviewed the original Zip my Pixel 9 Pro was one of the few that wasn’t compatible, necessitating the use of the 
CastPlay Pro
. I was able to mirror my screen to the Zip Pro, so non-copyrighted content like Instagram, games and YouTube played fine.  

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

Unfortunately, neither my Pixel 9 Pro nor TCL Nxtpaper 11 tablet successfully cast content wirelessly from apps like Netflix, Amazon Prime or Disney Plus. I had no problem with my Windows laptop, and I could even watch Netflix in a web browser without issue. Connecting via USB-C worked fine for all content. Casting wirelessly from YouTube was strange, as some videos would play fine while others had a weird bug where they played an alternate language track despite listing “English” in the video settings. I dusted off an old iPad Pro and that was able to connect, screen mirror and cast Netflix without an issue. 

There is one connection workaround, but it does require a special cable. If you search for “USB-C-to-HDMI” the majority of the dongles you’ll find are intended to go from a USB-C source to an HDMI display, like your phone to a TV, for example. There are some that go in the opposite direction, as in an HDMI source like a 
Roku
 to the USB-C of the Zip Pro. I used this 
$18 Benfei
 and it worked fine, but there are others that require external power. Depending on the cable you get, you can use the Zip’s other USB-C connection to power the cable, but that will reduce battery life. Not as elegant as just having an HDMI input, but it works.

Which is to say, you might still have some issues depending on your phone and what you want to watch on the Zip Pro. When I reviewed the Zip Cyber, Aurzen said that my phone was one of the few that wasn’t fully compatible, due to Google restricting some aspects of their mirroring feature. The Pro fixed this to an extent, but an HDMI input would make all this so much easier. I understand that adding one would increase the price beyond what Aurzen wants to charge, but maybe a future Super Pro version? 

Picture quality comparisons

Anker Soundcore Nebula Capsule 3

Aurzen Zip Cyber

The price of the Capsule 3 is a bit more than the Zip Pro, though it’s often on sale for around the same price. The Capsule adds an HDMI input and Google TV. It’s 6 times larger in volume compared to the collapsed Zip Pro, but the lack of adjustability means it’s not as easy to set up. The Zip Cyber, smaller and cheaper than both, is here to compare what you get for spending more on the Zip Pro. I found it was impossible to get the same source running on all three at the same time, but I did get the same movie running on different sources. Not ideal, but it is what it is. I viewed them all on the same 102-inch, 1.0-gain screen. 

Between the Zip Pro and the Capsule the former is a little brighter, but it’s probably only noticeable if you saw them side by side. Still, for small, low-powered projectors like these, every bit helps. The Capsule’s much higher contrast ratio was far more noticeable, with a punchier, more natural looking image. The Zip Pro looked far more washed out in comparison. 

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

The Zip Pro might be better if there were any picture settings, not least color temp but perhaps contrast and brightness, too. The Capsule looks better not just because of its better contrast, but also because you’re able to dial in a better image with its far more expensive picture settings. Though, to be fair, turning 
off
 most of the on-by-default image “enhancement” features does more for the image than any of the other settings. 

Color is more vibrant on the Capsule, though both are wrong. The Capsule’s colors are more pleasingly wrong, if that makes sense. The colors are deeper on the Capsule, which for a device that’s not intended for critical viewing, that’s fine. 

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

The Zip Cyber is far dimmer, no surprise there. Its colors are a little worse as well. The Pro is far more watchable between the Aurzens and can create a far larger image. As long as you don’t need something absolutely tiny like the Zip, the Zip Pro is still very small and worth the premium. Stepping up to the Capsule 3 gets you a more capable, flexible projector, but it’s larger still and if its not on sale it’s even closer to the price of a “real” projector that will be significantly brighter than any of these.

Zip up(grade)

OK, I’m of two minds on the Zip Pro. If what you need is a compact, highly portable projector, usually to stream content from your phone or computer, I think the Pro is a reasonable performance upgrade over the smaller, dimmer, lower resolution Zip. I think the foldable design can fit in places better, and be easier to setup on more surfaces, than the cylindrical Capsule 3. If you don’t mind something larger and usually little more expensive, the Capsule 3 also looks better overall and has built-in HDMI. Both are pricey, though, for the performance. You’re paying extra for the small size. 

Geoffrey Morrison/CNET

Beyond price and performance, I’m disappointed that Aurzen didn’t include a micro-HDMI connection. I’m sure this would have added even more to the price but it would make it, to me anyway, significantly more practical. Yes, you can get a fairly inexpensive cable and it will work, but that’s far less convenient and yet another thing you’ll need to remember to have with the Zip Pro at all times. Aurzen had mentioned a CastPlay Pro HDMI dongle when I reviewed the Zip Cyber, but it still doesn’t seem to be available. If you’re not bothered by the special cable requirement, or aren’t interested in connecting a game console, and you can stream directly to the Zip Pro from your devices, then all this hand-wringing about the lack of an HDMI input won’t be an issue for you and it’s just a fun, tiny projector.

Which is to say, I do like the Zip Pro. It’s a great design with performance that’s basically similar to other portable projectors, as long as you can connect.

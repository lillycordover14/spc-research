Ema Boateng, a key behind-the-scenes player for San Diego FC over the franchise’s first two seasons, is retiring.

The 32-year-old midfielder announced his decision on Wednesday, calling it quits after 13 professional seasons played across the United States and Europe. He spent 11 years in Major League Soccer, the final two with San Diego FC.

Boateng appeared in just 13 MLS matches with San Diego, recording one goal, but was seen as a key mentor to the team’s players. Boateng came up through the Right to Dream system, which now includes SDFC.

Boateng won SDFC’s Great Teammate Award in both his seasons with the club; in 2025, he won the We Through Me Award, given for his commitment to both his club and community.

“Ema has been an incredibly important part of our club, not only for what he brought on the field, but for the impact he made in our locker room every single day,” said SDFC sporting director Tyler Heaps.

“He has enjoyed a decorated career, representing some of the most successful teams in MLS and consistently demonstrating the qualities that make him such a respected player and teammate. But above all, Ema is an outstanding person and professional. He is someone who brings positive energy wherever he goes, makes people feel welcome, and is simply someone you want on your team. We are grateful for everything he has given to San Diego FC and wish him and his family nothing but the very best in this next chapter.”

A native of Ghana, Boateng moved to the United States in 2012 to attend UC Santa Barbara. After starring for the Gauchos, he signed with Ventura County of USL League Two. Boateng then played three seasons in Sweden before being signed by the LA Galaxy in 2016. Boateng and the Columbus Crew captured an MLS Cup title in 2020; a year later, he won a Supporters’ Shield with the New England Revolution.

“To the fans, thank you for your unwavering loyalty and passion,” Boateng said. “Professional sports wouldn’t exist without your support. I appreciate all the love you’ve shown me throughout the years, and I hope I was able to bring some joy into your lives as well.”
 

More SDFC awards

For the second consecutive season, forward Anders Dreyer was named SDFC’s Most Valuable Player by a club vote.

Manu Duah was named Defensive Player of the Year, while Anibal Godoy won the Top Competitor Award, Ian Pilcher won the Growth Mindset Award and Alejandro Alvarado Jr. won the We Through Me Award.

The 28-year-old Dreyer finished the 2026 season with 16 goals and 14 assists over all contests. He was named an MLS All-Star for the second consecutive season. Dreyer has 40 goals and 34 assists in his SDFC career, both club-bests.

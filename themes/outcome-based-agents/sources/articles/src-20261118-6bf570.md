The city of Republic aims to adopt a transition plan in March 2027 that will identify barriers to accessibility and projects that are needed to make city spaces more compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).

Inaccessible sidewalks and curb ramps are major concerns for members of the public, according to a self-evaluation from Republic. The city partnered with the firm Cole & Associates Inc. to study the severity of accessibility barriers on sidewalks using special technology mounted on a Segway.

Members of the public will have their first opportunity to see and discuss the results of the ADA study at a meeting on Wednesday, Nov. 18, at 5:30 p.m. at Republic City Hall (4221 S. Wilson’s Creek Boulevard). The city plans to hold one more public meeting before City Council takes up the matter.

Republic previously adopted an 
ADA transition plan
 in 2016, identifying numerous ADA compliance issues in aging city facilities such as parks and the police department and animal control buildings. Many of the accessibility issues identified in 2016 have since been fixed.

The city will create a new plan and list of priorities based on the severity of each accessibility obstacle and demand from the public. This project is funded by a Safe Streets for All grant award of $240,000, according to Republic Capital Planning Funding Officer Trent Sims.

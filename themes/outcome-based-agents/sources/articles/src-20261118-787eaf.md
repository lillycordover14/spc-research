Abstract
Fake news spreads faster than detection models can adapt, and models updated on new data tend to forget what they learned before, an effect severe enough to make naive deployments impractical for continuous, streaming fake-news detection (FND). Continual-learning methods address this either by storing real past examples for replay, which is accurate but carries a memory and privacy cost many streaming deployments cannot accept, or by staying exemplar-free, which avoids that cost but can degrade badly under severe task drift; a deployer is typically forced to commit to one property or the other before knowing which their data will actually require. We introduce EAKD-CFND, a class-incremental continual-learning framework built around one mechanism, per-instance uncertainty-adaptive knowledge distillation with external fact-checking verification, offered in two configurations that share that mechanism: an exemplar-free default (EAKD-CFND) that never stores a past instance, and a rehearsal-augmented configuration (EAKD-CFND-R) that adds a small reservoir buffer for settings where storage is available and drift risk is high. We evaluate both configurations against five baselines (fine-tuning, EWC, LwF, the rehearsal-based DER, and the category-level adaptive-KD method LUD) under one identical protocol on three real class-incremental splits built from PHEME and FakeNewsNet, and measure calibration, hyperparameter sensitivity, and verification cost end to end. The exemplar-free configuration is statistically indistinguishable from every baseline tested, including rehearsal-based DER, on both FakeNewsNet splits, at zero storage cost. On PHEME’s more demanding five-task sequence, the exemplar-free configuration collapses to near-random accuracy alongside every other exemplar-free method tested, while DER’s stored exemplars carry it to 84.8%; switching to the rehearsal-augmented configuration closes that gap (85.5% on PHEME-Event, matching DER within noise), giving a working option for exactly the regime where the exemplar-free default falls short. The rehearsal-augmented configuration is not a single-benchmark fix: on FNN-Poli-Time it adds a further, measured gain over both the exemplar-free default and DER, while on FNN-Gossip-Time it adds nothing, a benchmark-specific split traced directly to how much genuine task drift each benchmark actually contains. The uncertainty signal itself is poorly calibrated in absolute terms (Expected Calibration Error 0.80–0.84 across four estimators), but the framework’s accuracy is robust to its two governing hyperparameters across a wide sweep, and its API cost is quantified rather than assumed. Together these results give practitioners a working choice between the two configurations, made on measured grounds rather than a qualitative argument: exemplar-free by default, rehearsal-augmented when storage is available and drift risk is severe.

Introduction
Fabricated information, commonly called “fake news,” spreads rapidly across online platforms. Its effects are real: election coverage, public health guidance, and political discourse have all been measurably shaped by coordinated misinformation
1
. The volume of social media content makes manual fact-checking impractical, and automated FND systems have stepped in to fill that gap
2
.
Most FND models are trained on static datasets
3
. They work well for the data they were trained on, but online information does not stay still. News topics, narrative styles, and misinformation tactics all shift over time. When a static model is updated with new data, it typically suffers from 
catastrophic forgetting
4
: performance on previously learned tasks degrades sharply as the model adjusts to new ones. This makes such systems poorly suited to real-world streaming environments.
Figure

 illustrates the problem. Panel (a) shows a model initially trained to 90% accuracy on detecting political fake news (Task 1). When subsequently updated with health misinformation data (Task 2), it learns the new task, reaching around 85% accuracy, but its performance on Task 1 drops to roughly 40%. That degradation is catastrophic forgetting, and it makes the model unreliable for deployment. Panel (b) shows the continual learning target: after training on Task 2, the model achieves high accuracy on both tasks, maintaining approximately 90% on each.

Continual Learning (CL) provides a framework for training sequentially on data from different tasks while retaining past knowledge. Knowledge Distillation (KD) has become a practical CL strategy, particularly where storing old data is infeasible for memory or privacy reasons
5
. In KD-based CL, a frozen “teacher” model (trained on previous tasks) transfers knowledge to a “student” model (learning the current task) by aligning their output predictions
6
.
This paper introduces EAKD-CFND (Adaptive Knowledge Distillation with External Verification for Continual Fake News Detection). The motivation behind it is twofold, and both halves center on one shared mechanism rather than a single storage commitment. First, when the teacher is genuinely uncertain about an instance, EAKD-CFND treats that uncertainty as a trigger to consult an external, verified source of knowledge instead of forcing the student to imitate a guess. Second, this same uncertainty-adaptive mechanism is deployable in two configurations depending on what the deployment can afford: an exemplar-free configuration that never stores a past instance, for settings where retaining raw historical data carries storage or data-retention costs, and a rehearsal-augmented configuration, EAKD-CFND-R, that adds a small reservoir buffer on top of the same mechanism for settings where a storage budget is available and task drift is severe enough that exemplar-free methods alone are not sufficient. “
Methodology
” section defines both configurations precisely; “
Results
” section evaluates both across all three benchmarks and states which configuration each benchmark’s drift pattern favors. The core logic is simple: a teacher model’s confidence in its own prediction is a direct signal of how much its guidance should be trusted. EAKD-CFND uses that signal to scale the distillation loss per instance, in the same spirit as adaptive per-instance loss weighting in prior KD work
7
. A confident teacher exerts strong distillation pressure, anchoring the student to past-task knowledge. A hedging teacher gets less weight, freeing the student to learn from current data. Past a certain uncertainty threshold the system skips the teacher entirely, queries an external fact-checking API (e.g., the Google Fact Check API) for a verified label, and trains against that instead.
When the teacher is genuinely uncertain, this design lets the student decline to imitate it rather than absorbing whatever the teacher happens to output; over time this is intended to let the student accumulate knowledge the teacher never had access to, through the external-verification channel specifically, what we call the “student surpasses teacher” dynamic. “
Ablation studies
” section tests this mechanism directly against a fixed-weight and a randomly-weighted alternative and reports the measured outcome, which is not uniformly favorable to the instance-level design; we do not restate an unconditional advantage here that the results do not support.
The main contributions are:

EAKD-CFND itself: a framework that weights the KD loss per instance using a teacher-uncertainty signal, evaluated against both a fixed-weight and a randomly-weighted control to test whether the per-instance signal carries exploitable information (“
Ablation studies
” section reports this test did not separate the trained signal from noise on FNN-Poli-Time).

An external verification mechanism triggered by high teacher uncertainty, which substitutes a fact-checked label into the student’s training signal; “
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section reports a direct test of this mechanism’s marginal contribution once a rehearsal buffer is present, and states the (null) result directly.

EAKD-CFND-R: a rehearsal-augmented configuration of the same framework, documented in “
Methodology
” section as a first-class deployment option (not only a diagnostic), for settings where a storage budget is available and task drift is severe enough that the exemplar-free configuration alone is insufficient; evaluated across all three benchmarks in “
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section.

A worked demonstration that an uncertainty signal can be wired directly into the CL training loop as a training-time control signal rather than only a post-hoc diagnostic, together with a direct empirical test (“
Calibration validation
”, “
Ablation studies
” sections) of whether that signal is calibrated and whether it is exploited by the mechanism built around it.

Empirical evaluation of both configurations on three class-incremental FND benchmarks, five seeds each, with seed-matched significance testing against all five baselines (fine-tuning, EWC, LwF, DER, LUD), showing the exemplar-free configuration matches rehearsal-based and category-level adaptive-KD baselines at zero storage cost under moderate task drift, and that the rehearsal-augmented configuration recovers accuracy under the severe drift where the exemplar-free configuration alone falls short.

Ablation studies that isolate the contribution of each component, with qualitative examples showing how the framework handles individual instances.

What the student ends up with is a model whose behavior reflects the limits of its own teacher, which, practically speaking, is also the limits of standard KD.
Literature survey
Three bodies of work bear directly on what EAKD-CFND is trying to do: fake news detection, continual learning, and the use of model uncertainty as a training signal.
Fake news detection
Automated fake news detection has gone through several phases. Early approaches built hand-crafted features from text (word choice, emotional tone, syntactic patterns) and fed them into classifiers like SVMs, Naive Bayes, and Random Forests
1
. These handled narrow problem settings reasonably well but struggled with the nuance and scale of online text.
Deep learning models, RNNs and CNNs in particular, improved on this by learning feature representations directly from text. Transformer-based models like BERT
8
 and its variants (RoBERTa, XLNet) pushed further still; pre-trained contextual embeddings from large corpora gave these models a much stronger baseline for detecting the subtle semantic patterns that distinguish misinformation from credible reporting
3
. Lighter distilled variants keep most of this benefit at a fraction of the cost: DistilBERT embeddings combined with PCA-based dimensionality reduction and genetic-algorithm feature selection have been shown to reach competitive accuracy with a much smaller feature space
9
.
Since fake news rarely involves text alone, researchers have also explored multi-modal approaches that incorporate images and video alongside written content
10
. Image-text mismatches, for instance, are strong detection signals. Social context (propagation patterns, user credibility, comment stance) provides another useful layer of evidence
11
. Other systems build explainability directly into the content model itself: dEFEND jointly attends over news content and user comments, using their co-attention to both classify and explain a prediction without querying any external source
12
.
Prior continual/incremental FND work:
 We searched explicitly for prior work applying continual or class-incremental learning directly to fake news or rumor detection, since the title of this paper implies that intersection is itself a contribution. We did not locate an established prior system that poses fake news detection as a class-incremental learning problem in the sense used here (disjoint per-task label sets, a frozen-teacher/trainable-student protocol, sequential task boundaries); the closest related work is on concept drift in text stream mining generally
13
, which studies distributional shift in streaming text classification without the CL-specific machinery (rehearsal buffers, regularization penalties, or KD-based teacher-student transfer) this paper and its baselines use. This is a gap-check, not a claim of certainty: the absence of a located prior system does not rule one out, only that our search did not surface one.
Automated fact-checking as a knowledge source:
 External verification (“
Uncertainty-driven external verification
” section) queries a fact-checking API rather than treating it as a neutral, always-correct oracle, and the design choices in Algorithm 1 (query construction, similarity filtering, majority-vote conflict resolution) sit inside a broader automated fact-checking and claim-verification literature that studies exactly this pipeline. FEVER
14
 established a standard large-scale benchmark for evidence-based claim verification, and Guo, Schlichtkrull & Vlachos’s survey
15
 documents that automated fact-checking systems’ coverage is uneven across claim type, language, and topic salience, rather than a uniform sample of all checkable claims. “
Efficiency analysis’s
” section finding that 90% of triggered queries in this paper’s pipeline return no usable match is consistent with that documented coverage variability; we return to what this means for label provenance, not only latency, in “
Efficiency analysis
” section.
Gap:
 The majority of FND research relies on static datasets, but the actual problem is not static. News topics, narrative styles, and misinformation tactics shift continuously: this is concept drift, a recognized problem in text stream mining
13
. Static models updated with new data forget what they learned before, which makes them unsuitable for continuous deployment.
Continual learning
Continual Learning addresses the problem of training on a sequence of tasks without forgetting earlier ones
16
. The underlying tension is well-known: preserving past knowledge and acquiring new knowledge pull against each other, and getting the balance wrong in either direction hurts. Tighten too hard on old knowledge and the model stops learning; loosen too far and it overwrites what it already knew. When the forgetting side wins, catastrophic forgetting results
4
, a problem that persists even in large language models
17
. CL methods fall into three broad categories:

Regularization-based methods:
 These add penalty terms to the loss function to protect parameters that matter for previous tasks. Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)
4
 and Synaptic Intelligence (SI)
18
 are common examples. They can become overly restrictive as the number of tasks grows.

Rehearsal-based methods:
 These store a subset of old data and replay it during new-task training. Often effective
19
, but they require extra memory and raise privacy concerns. Gradient Episodic Memory (GEM)
20
 constrains gradient updates using stored exemplars; Dark Experience Replay (DER)
21
 combines rehearsal with KD.

Architecture-based methods:
 These grow the network or allocate task-specific parameters to avoid interference. Adapter-based approaches train small plug-in modules per task while leaving the base model largely frozen.

Exemplar-free CIL as its own studied trade-off:
 The storage/privacy-vs-accuracy trade-off between exemplar-free and rehearsal-based methods is not new to this paper; it is the CL field’s own organizing axis for the taxonomy above, and vision-CIL work has directly measured where exemplar-free methods hold and where they break, using prototype-based or feature-translation techniques to substitute for stored exemplars
22
. That literature’s finding, that exemplar-free methods are competitive under moderate drift and degrade under severe drift or long task sequences, is broadly consistent with what “
Quantitative performance
” section reports for EAKD-CFND on PHEME-Event versus FakeNewsNet; we position this paper’s contribution as measuring where that known boundary falls for an uncertainty-adaptive KD and external-verification design specifically, not as introducing the exemplar-free/rehearsal trade-off itself.
Knowledge Distillation in CL:
 KD is widely used in exemplar-free CL
5
. A model trained on previous tasks (teacher) guides the current model (student) by encouraging it to match the teacher’s output distribution on new inputs. Past-task knowledge is retained without storing old data
23
. Learning without Forgetting (LwF)
24
 is an early instance of this, and is one of the five baselines evaluated in “
Quantitative performance
” section.
Gap:
 Standard KD applies a fixed distillation weight across all instances. This uniform pressure hinders learning when the teacher’s predictions are uncertain. Recent work has moved toward adaptive KD: weighting by task similarity, sample difficulty
7
,
25
, or category-level uncertainty as in Logits Uncertainty Distillation (LUD)
26
. None of these, however, use instance-level uncertainty to also trigger external verification for knowledge augmentation in open-world settings. That direction is identified as a frontier in recent CL surveys
27
.
Uncertainty as an explainable signal
A model’s confidence in its own prediction is one of the most direct forms of explanation available: it answers “how reliable is this output?” without requiring post-hoc interpretation
28
. Uncertainty can be estimated through Monte Carlo Dropout
29
, ensemble variance, or simpler softmax-based measures such as entropy or maximum probability
30
. These estimates can flag samples for human review and improve decision transparency
31
. In FND, explainability has mostly been used after training to highlight predictive features
32
,
33
, not as an active component during training. Uncertainty has also been used more directly inside a multimodal FND architecture itself: a dual-uncertainty framework separately estimates fact-level and sentiment-level uncertainty and fuses them within a single-shot multimodal detector
34
. That use of uncertainty is architectural (it shapes how one prediction is fused across modalities) rather than temporal: it does not address what happens when the model is updated on a new task, which is the continual-learning setting this paper targets.
Gap:
 While post-hoc explainability is well-studied
35
,
36
, integrating uncertainty signals directly into the learning process, to guide knowledge transfer and trigger external verification, has not been explored in continual learning contexts. That is the direction this work pursues.
Novelty of EAKD-CFND
The closest prior work is LUD
26
, and the distinction is worth stating precisely, since both use teacher uncertainty to modulate distillation. LUD’s uncertainty signal is 
category-level
: one confidence estimate per class, applied uniformly to every instance of that class. EAKD-CFND’s signal is 
instance-level
: 
\(\omega (x)\)
 is computed per input, so two instances of the same class receive different distillation weights if the teacher is more confident about one than the other. That distinction is structural, not merely notational, and Table 
4
’s “EAKD w/Random 
\(\alpha (x)\)
” row tests it directly by replacing the instance-level signal with uniform noise. We report the outcome as measured (“
Quantitative performance
” section): across our three benchmark splits, this granularity distinction does not resolve into a consistent accuracy advantage over LUD: the two methods are statistically indistinguishable on both FakeNewsNet splits and collapse together on PHEME-Event. The instance-level design is a more expressive mechanism by construction, but expressiveness alone was not sufficient to beat category-level weighting in this evaluation; we return to why in “
Interpretation of results
” section. Second, and orthogonal to that first distinction, EAKD-CFND is the only method in Table 
7
 that acts on high uncertainty rather than just discounting it: LUD (and every other uncertainty-aware KD method we are aware of) reduces distillation pressure and stops there, leaving the student to learn from whatever the current task’s data provides. EAKD-CFND instead queries an external source for a verified label at exactly the instances where the teacher’s guidance would otherwise be least reliable. “
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section reports a direct test of whether that mechanism moves accuracy. Each of the three literature areas above has a gap that EAKD-CFND is designed to fill:

Catastrophic forgetting in dynamic FND:
 The framework targets streaming data directly. Single-model systems updated on new data forget: that is the problem EAKD-CFND exists to solve
17
.

Instance-level adaptive KD:
 Fixed-weight KD and category-level methods like LUD
26
 apply the same distillation pressure regardless of whether the teacher actually knows what it is talking about. EAKD-CFND adjusts per instance.

Uncertainty as a training signal, not just a diagnostic:
 Teacher uncertainty is wired into the learning loop as a control signal, not examined after the fact.

Open-world knowledge augmentation:
 When the teacher is uncertain, EAKD-CFND queries external fact-checking APIs rather than either blindly trusting the teacher or simply reducing the distillation weight and leaving a gap. This is what no closed-world CL system does.

Methodology
EAKD-CFND has six interacting components, described below in order from the problem setup through the combined loss.
Problem formulation
We address FND in a Class-Incremental Learning (CIL) setting, not a domain-incremental or purely temporal continual-learning setting: what changes from task to task is the set of classes the model must recognize, and task boundaries are defined by disjoint label sets rather than by a shift in input domain alone. The model receives a sequence of tasks 
\(\mathcal {T} = \{T_1, T_2,..., T_N\}\)
, where each task 
\(T_i\)
 introduces a new set of fake news classes with dataset 
\(D_i = \{(x_{ij}, y_{ij})\}\)
. When training on 
\(T_i\)
, only 
\(D_i\)
 is available. The goal is a single model 
\(f^S\)
 that performs well on 
\(T_i\)
 while retaining performance on 
\(\{T_1,..., T_{i-1}\}\)
. Tasks are ordered chronologically (“
Datasets and task construction
” section), so the sequence also reflects the real-world temporal drift the streaming scenario is meant to capture, but the learning protocol itself (what the model is evaluated on and what triggers a new task) is class-incremental. This setup mirrors real streaming environments where new data arrives continuously and re-training on the full history is not feasible.
Framework overview
EAKD-CFND uses a teacher-student architecture. Let 
\(f^T\)
 be the model trained through task 
\(T_{i-1}\)
, held frozen. Let 
\(f^S\)
 be the student initialized from 
\(f^T\)
 and trained on the current task 
\(T_i\)
.

 Transfers knowledge from

 Extracts an instance-specific confidence signal

 Converts

Knowledge distillation (
\(L_{KD}\)
)
The distillation loss aligns the student’s predictions with the teacher’s over previously learned classes. Given logits 
\(z^{T}(x)\)
 and 
\(z^{S}(x)\)
, we use the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between softened distributions:
$$\begin{aligned} L_{KD}(x)=\tau ^{2}\cdot KL(\sigma (z^{T}(x)/\tau )||\sigma (z^{S}(x)/\tau )) \end{aligned}$$

                    (1)
                
where 
\(\sigma\)

\(\tau\)

Uncertainty signal generation (
\(\omega (x)\)
)
The uncertainty signal 
\(\omega (x)\)
 is extracted from the teacher’s output distribution 
\(P(x) = \{p_1, p_2,..., p_C\}\)
 over 
C
 classes. We use two measures:

Prediction Entropy:
 
\(H(P(x)) = - \sum _{i=1}^{C} p_i \log p_i\)
. Higher entropy means higher uncertainty.

Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP):
 A low maximum probability indicates an uncertain prediction.

Adaptive weighting function (
\(\alpha (\omega (x))\)
)
The weight 
\(\alpha (\omega (x))\)

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha (\omega (x))=max(0,1-\beta \cdot normalize(\omega (x))) \end{aligned}$$

                    (2)
                
where 
\(normalize(\omega (x))\)

\(\beta\)

Uncertainty-driven external verification
Failure and fallback design.
 The module is best-effort by construction: an API timeout, an empty result set, a result that fails the similarity filter, or a tied conflict all resolve to the same fallback: keep the original dataset label 
y
 rather than blocking training or injecting an unfiltered verdict. This means external verification can only ever 
replace
 a label with a filtered, mapped, majority-supported one; it never introduces an unvetted label into training.
Label leakage and temporal consistency.
 External verification is invoked only on instances drawn from the current task’s 
training
 split 
\(D_i\)
; it is never invoked at evaluation time, so no fact-check result ever substitutes for or informs a test-set label. This keeps the verification step a training-signal-quality mechanism rather than a channel through which test-set information could reach the model. Because tasks are processed in the same chronological order used to construct the task sequence (“
Datasets and task construction
” section), each query is issued against the fact-checking corpus at the point in time corresponding to that task, so the external knowledge a task can draw on is never drawn from a point later than the task itself, avoiding retrospective fact-checks (verdicts issued after the fact, with the benefit of hindsight the original news cycle did not have) leaking into earlier tasks.

\(\mathcal {B}\)

$$\begin{aligned} L_{total}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal {B}|}\sum _{x\in \mathcal {B}}(L_{task}(f^{S}(x),y_{effective})+\alpha (\omega (x))\cdot L_{KD}(x)) \end{aligned}$$

                    (3)
                
where 
\(L_{task}\)
 is the cross-entropy classification loss, and 
\(y_{effective}\)
 is the original label 
y
 unless external verification was triggered, in which case 
\(y_{effective} = y_{verified}\)
.
EAKD-CFND-R: a rehearsal-augmented configuration
EAKD-CFND as defined above is exemplar-free: at no point does Eq. (
3
) reference a stored past instance. This is the right default for deployments where retaining raw historical data carries a storage or data-retention cost. It is not the right choice for every deployment: “
Quantitative performance
” section shows that under severe task drift, no exemplar-free method, including EAKD-CFND, keeps pace with a rehearsal-based baseline. For deployments where a storage budget is available and drift risk is high enough to justify spending it, we define a second configuration of the same framework, 
EAKD-CFND-R
, that adds a small reservoir buffer of past 
\((x, z^S(x))\)

Baselines
” section) replayed via an MSE loss against the stored logits, on top of, not instead of, EAKD-CFND’s uncertainty-adaptive KD and verification terms:
$$\begin{aligned} L_{total}^{R} = L_{total} + \gamma \cdot \mathbb {E}_{(x', z') \sim \text {Buffer}}\left[ \Vert z^{S}(x') - z' \Vert _2^2\right] \end{aligned}$$

                    (4)
                
with 
\(\gamma =0.5\)
 (matching DER’s own replay weight) and 
\(L_{total}\)
 as in Eq. (

EAKD-CFND trains 3.5x slower than fine-tuning (198.2s vs. 56.5s) and 1.8x slower than DER despite DER’s much larger memory footprint from its replay buffer (6679MB vs. 4318MB). The API call rate of 0.106 (400 calls across 3770 training instances) means only about one instance in ten crosses the uncertainty threshold and triggers a query, yet those calls account for 128.4 of the 198.2 total training seconds: roughly two-thirds of EAKD-CFND’s entire wall-clock overhead over fine-tuning is literal network wait time on external API calls (mean latency 0.32s, p95 0.66s per call), not the uncertainty computation itself, which is negligible (softmax entropy over a small logit vector). Of the 400 calls issued, 360 (90%) fell back to the original label rather than substituting a verified one; this is not a 90% API failure rate in the sense of the service being broken: it blends genuine transport/timeout errors with the much more common legitimate outcome of a query finding no matching fact-check entry in the source database, which Algorithm 1 treats identically (both resolve to the original-label fallback). We did not instrument the two outcomes separately; a future cost table should report them as distinct rows, since a 90% “no relevant fact-check found” rate says something different about deployment value than a 90% transport-error rate would.

\(\sim\)

do

Discussion
Interpretation of results
The results are best read through the exemplar-free trade-off the framework was designed to test, not through a single accuracy ranking. On both FakeNewsNet splits, that trade holds cleanly: EAKD-CFND is statistically indistinguishable from DER and LUD while storing zero past instances (Table 
3
), against DER’s persistent buffer of up to 200 real exemplars per task (Table 
10
 shows the memory cost directly: 6679MB peak for DER versus 4318MB for EAKD-CFND). This parity has a specific scope: Table 
3
’s full 15-comparison sweep shows EAKD-CFND is equally indistinguishable from fine-tuning, the paper’s own stated lower bound, on both FakeNewsNet splits. “Matches the strongest baselines” is therefore evidence that the exemplar-free trade costs nothing on these two benchmarks, not evidence that any of EAKD-CFND’s specific mechanisms are separating it from a naive baseline; those are different claims, and only the first is supported here. On PHEME-Event, the trade breaks: a five-task sequence of small, sharply disjoint classes is severe enough forgetting pressure that every exemplar-free method we test (not just EAKD-CFND, but fine-tuning, EWC, LwF, and the category-level adaptive-KD method LUD as well) collapses together, while DER’s stored exemplars alone survive. That four-way collapse is the more informative result: it says the limitation is structural to exemplar-free continual learning as a class under this severity of drift, not a defect specific to instance-level uncertainty weighting or to EAKD-CFND’s particular design choices. We attribute this to task count and class disjointness together, but Table 
1
 shows PHEME-Event’s smallest task has only 376 training examples, well below FNN-Gossip-Time’s smallest (789) and an order of magnitude below its largest (8586); we cannot fully separate “five tasks of severely limited per-task data” from “five tasks of severe forgetting pressure” as the operative cause with the experiments run here, and flag task-size as a candidate confound alongside task count.
The FakeNewsNet splits do not behave identically once EAKD-CFND-R is added, and the difference is informative. On FNN-Poli-Time, every EAKD-CFND-R variant measurably beats both EAKD-CFND and DER (“
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section); on FNN-Gossip-Time, no method, R-variants included, separates from fine-tuning at all (Table 
2
, “
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section). This is not the same flatness for the same reason on both splits, and we tested why directly rather than assuming a shared explanation. Using the exact task construction the models train on (“

” section), we computed TF-IDF task-centroid cosine similarity and top-30-word Jaccard overlap between each dataset’s own tasks (Table

FNN-Gossip-Time’s tasks are topically near-duplicates of each other: mean task-pair similarity of 0.816, four times PHEME-Event’s 0.199, with consecutive-task vocabulary overlap reaching 62% (Task 1 vs. Task 2). The words shared across all three Gossip-Time tasks are not generic filler; they are specific recurring entities (the Kardashian-Jenner family, Justin Bieber) and tabloid headline templates (“X’s baby,” “new wedding”) that appear in every year-bin. PHEME-Event’s five tasks share nothing but Twitter boilerplate (“amp,” “http”), consistent with five genuinely disjoint real-world events. FNN-Poli-Time sits in between (0.406), with recurring political figures (Clinton, Obama, Trump) but real event-specific vocabulary changing year to year, which is consistent with EAKD-CFND-R showing a measurable benefit there while showing none on Gossip-Time.
The task-similarity measurement above is a correlation; we tested it against a specific alternative explanation directly. FNN-Gossip-Time’s roughly 29% accuracy ceiling could reflect either an absence of real forgetting pressure (the topic-similarity story) or simply a difficult, low-signal classification problem on headline-only text, in which case the flat BWT numbers would reflect a low starting ceiling rather than successfully avoided degradation. We distinguished these by training a fresh, non-continual FT model on each task in isolation: no prior task, no teacher, no sequence, so no forgetting is possible by construction, and the resulting accuracy measures each task’s own achievable ceiling independent of any continual-learning mechanism. FNN-Gossip-Time’s three tasks reach 83.8%, 86.9%, and 86.8% in isolation (mean 85.8%, 3 seeds each), far above the 
\(\sim\)
29% every method achieves in the full three-task sequence. The tasks are individually easy; the low-ceiling explanation is ruled out. The same check on PHEME-Event (83.1–89.9% per task in isolation, versus 16.8% for EAKD-CFND in the full sequence) and FNN-Poli-Time (78.5–89.2% in isolation, versus 29.8% in sequence) confirms the opposite pattern on those benchmarks: real forgetting is happening, and rehearsal has real degradation to recover from, which is exactly why it helps there and does nothing on Gossip-Time.
The result in “
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section sharpens this further rather than complicating it. On PHEME-Event, adding a rehearsal buffer to EAKD-CFND closes the gap almost completely, confirming that rehearsal specifically, not some other property DER happens to have, is what severe forgetting requires. On FNN-Poli-Time, rehearsal adds a measurable benefit over both EAKD-CFND and DER, with the label-replay variants (+PP, +PP+UW) the strongest. On FNN-Gossip-Time, rehearsal adds nothing, for the same reason nothing else does: there is no real forgetting there to recover from. Across all three benchmarks, one further pattern holds: once a rehearsal buffer is present, neither weighting the replayed instances by uncertainty nor consulting external verification changes the outcome by more than the variant-to-variant noise (Table 
11
). We do not read this as evidence that instance-level uncertainty or external verification are useless in general: the ablation in Table 
4
 shows they at least do not hurt, and the calibration and sensitivity results (“
Calibration validation
”–“
Hyperparameter sensitivity
” sections) describe real, separate properties of the signal itself. But on the specific evidence we have, their measurable contribution to accuracy on top of a rehearsal buffer remains within the noise floor of the variants tested.
This also matters for a practical streaming data problem that often goes unaddressed: out-of-distribution or noisy samples from past tasks. When such samples produce high teacher uncertainty, the adaptive weight reduces their distillation contribution. At the threshold, an external label takes over entirely, so the student does not train on whatever unreliable signal the teacher would have produced. This mechanism itself is unchanged by the results above: what the results narrow is how much of the observed accuracy on our three benchmarks should be attributed to it.
Significance and implications
The practical case for EAKD-CFND is the exemplar-free property itself, not an unconditional accuracy edge: it requires no stored exemplars and no full retraining, so it fits streaming deployments where retaining raw historical data is undesirable or disallowed: data-retention policy, storage cost at scale, or simply not wanting a growing archive of past user content sitting in a training buffer. Table 
2
 shows that trade is free on two of our three benchmarks (FNN-Poli-Time, FNN-Gossip-Time) and costly on the third (PHEME-Event); a deployment choosing between EAKD-CFND and a rehearsal-based method now has a measured basis for that choice rather than a qualitative argument. The transparency point is worth naming too, though perhaps not for the usual reasons. A system that explicitly defers to an external source when uncertain is, at minimum, inspectable: you can see which instances triggered verification and why. Whether that translates into user trust in practice is an open question, but it beats a model that outputs confident predictions indiscriminately, even though “
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section did not find this specific mechanism moving accuracy once rehearsal was present. Inspectability and accuracy are different properties, and we make a claim about the former, not the latter, here. We also flag the claim’s own limit rather than assume it: the human-factors literature on trust in automation distinguishes appropriate reliance from inflated reliance produced by the mere appearance of principled behavior
44
, and a formal treatment of what would count as warranted trust in an AI system’s output is itself an active research question
45
. Given “
Calibration validation’s
” section finding that the trigger condition is gated by a poorly-calibrated score, an inspectable trigger could just as plausibly produce miscalibrated user trust, over-trusting instances flagged as “verified” because the flagging looks principled, as it could produce appropriately calibrated trust; no user study distinguishes these here, so inspectability is a property the mechanism has, not a demonstrated trust benefit. The pattern also generalizes beyond FND: any CL problem with a queryable external ground truth (legal databases, medical registries, scientific literature APIs) is a reasonable candidate. The underlying bet is that an exemplar-free student should not be permanently capped at what its teacher already knew and what fits in its own weights; Table 
11
 shows that betting on external verification alone is not sufficient once forgetting pressure is severe, and that rehearsal, when the storage budget allows it, remains the more reliable fix. That table also gives a concrete answer to when rehearsal is worth its storage cost: on PHEME-Event, where the exemplar-free default collapses, plain replay alone recovers almost all of DER’s accuracy; on FNN-Poli-Time, where the exemplar-free default already ties DER, adding a buffer with the label-replay term still buys a further, measurable gain (+PP or +PP+UW); on FNN-Gossip-Time, no rehearsal variant buys anything over the exemplar-free default, so the storage cost is not worth paying there. The deciding factor is not the benchmark’s domain but its task-to-task drift: “
Interpretation of results’s
” section task-similarity measurement, and the isolated-per-task ceiling check that confirms it causally, both point to drift severity, not dataset identity, as what determines whether rehearsal is worth its cost.
Limitations
Not everything works perfectly here:

Implementation complexity:
 EAKD-CFND has more moving parts than standard KD or regularization methods, which increases engineering overhead.

Uncertainty calibration is measured and is poor:
 “
Calibration validation
” section reports Expected Calibration Error of 0.80–0.84 across four estimators (entropy, MSP, MC Dropout, deep ensemble) on FNN-Poli-Time: all four are far from well-calibrated in absolute terms, and the two cheap signals EAKD-CFND actually deploys (entropy, MSP) are the worst-calibrated pair. 
\(\omega (x)\)
’s numeric value should not be read as a probability that the teacher is wrong; it is a usable relative ranking, not a calibrated one.

Exemplar-free performance has a measured boundary, not just a described one:
 “
EAKD-CFND-R: evaluation
” section shows this boundary precisely: EAKD-CFND (exemplar-free) matches rehearsal-based DER on two of three benchmarks and collapses on the third (PHEME-Event, 16.8% vs. DER’s 84.8%), where a five-task sequence of small, sharply disjoint classes produces forgetting severe enough that the exemplar-free approach cannot keep up without adopting some form of rehearsal.

Instance-level weighting and external verification’s accuracy contribution is smaller than claimed once a rehearsal buffer is present:
 Table 
4
’s ablation on FNN-Poli-Time shows the individual design choices (instance-level vs. random weighting, entropy/MSP vs. loss-based uncertainty, with vs. without verification) inside a 4-point band that overlaps the per-row standard deviations. Table 
11
 tests this again with a buffer added, across all three benchmarks: on PHEME-Event, disabling verification changes accuracy by under 0.2 points; on FNN-Poli-Time and FNN-Gossip-Time, uncertainty-weighted replay does not consistently beat plain or label-replay variants either. We do not yet have a setting in which either mechanism shows a rehearsal-buffer accuracy contribution outside the noise floor.

Forward Transfer (FWT) is not reported:
 computing it requires recording a random-initialization baseline accuracy per task before training on that task, which the training loop used for these experiments did not do. Every table in this manuscript that would previously have included an FWT column omits it rather than reporting a number we did not actually measure.

Cost accounting is single-seed:
 Table 
10
’s training-time, inference-latency, memory, and API-cost figures come from one seed per method on one dataset (FNN-Poli-Time), not the 5-seed protocol used for the accuracy tables; we report point estimates, not the mean/SD format used elsewhere in this manuscript, and have not verified how much these figures vary across seeds or datasets.

FNN-Poli-Time’s per-task test splits are small enough that sampling noise rivals the effects being discussed:
 Table 
1
 shows FNN-Poli-Time test sizes of 31, 65, and 28 examples per task. The binomial standard error of an accuracy estimate near this dataset’s 
\(\sim\)
30% operating point is 
\(\sqrt{0.3 \times 0.7 / n}\)
: roughly 8.7 percentage points at 
\(n{=}28\)
, 8.2 at 
\(n{=}31\)
, and 5.7 at 
\(n{=}65\)
, each larger than every cross-seed SD reported for FNN-Poli-Time in Table 
2
 (1.0–3.4pp) and larger than the 1.4pp ablation gap discussed in “
Ablation studies
”. This is a separate, additional source of uncertainty from seed-to-seed training variance, and the reported ±SD figures throughout this manuscript’s FNN-Poli-Time tables do not capture it; a bootstrap or exact-binomial CI over the held-out test examples themselves would, but computing one requires per-example test predictions that were not retained.

API dependency:
 External verification introduces a dependency on a third-party service, with associated latency, availability, and coverage constraints. “
Efficiency analysis
” section quantifies part of this (call rate, latency) but does not separate genuine transport failures from legitimate no-coverage outcomes within the 90% fallback rate; see the corresponding paragraph for why that distinction matters and was not measured. Nor does it characterize whether the claims that do find a match are a representative sample of the flagged instances or a systematically narrower slice, a coverage-bias question the automated fact-checking literature documents as real
15
 and which this paper does not audit (“
Efficiency analysis
” section).

Evaluation scope:
 Experiments cover two FND benchmarks (PHEME, FakeNewsNet) under the class-incremental scenario, giving three task sequences across two distinct domains (social-media rumors and long-form news). The core comparison (Table 
2
), the EAKD-CFND-R evaluation (Table 
11
), and the isolated-task-ceiling causal check (“
Interpretation of results
” section) all run on all three benchmarks; the calibration, sensitivity, and cost measurements (“
Calibration validation
”–“
Efficiency analysis
 ”sections) still run on a single dataset (FNN-Poli-Time), so whether those specific findings generalize across datasets remains untested. Other CIL formulations (domain-incremental, task-free), other forms of concept drift, and live/real-world streaming deployment also remain untested.

Text-only:
 The framework does not incorporate images or other modalities, which are often relevant for real-world fake news detection.

Future research directions
The limitations above each point toward something worth investigating:

Larger buffer capacities:
 Table 
11
’s rehearsal-design comparison (plain replay, +PP, +UW, +PP+UW) now runs on all three benchmarks at a fixed buffer size (matching DER’s, “
Baselines
” section). Whether the FNN-Poli-Time variant ranking (label-replay strongest) or the FNN-Gossip-Time null result hold at larger buffer capacities is untested.

An artificial similarity-manipulation test:
 “
Interpretation of results’s
” section isolated-per-task-ceiling check confirms, causally, that FNN-Gossip-Time’s flatness reflects low forgetting pressure rather than a low intrinsic ceiling. A complementary test would construct an artificial high-similarity versus low-similarity task split from a single underlying corpus (e.g., FNN-Poli-Time re-split by topic cluster instead of by year), holding domain and text length fixed, to test whether manipulated task similarity alone predicts the degree of method separation within one dataset.

Splitting verification’s fallback outcomes:
 Instrumenting the External Verification module to log “no relevant fact-check found” separately from genuine API/transport errors would turn Table 
10
’s blended 90% fallback rate into two numbers with different practical implications: low fact-check coverage for this claim population versus an unreliable service.

Recording FWT baselines:
 A training loop that records random-initialization accuracy per task before training on it would let a rerun of Tables 
2
 and 
11
 report Forward Transfer alongside BWT, closing the one metric that could not be computed retroactively here.

Calibration-aware or learned thresholding:
 Given “
Calibration validation’s
” section finding that entropy/MSP are poorly calibrated, replacing the fixed 
\(\theta _{uncertainty}\)
 with a threshold that adapts to the current calibration state of the teacher, rather than a single global cutoff on an uncalibrated score, may recover some of the accuracy headroom “
Hyperparameter sensitivity
” section shows is not currently being captured by threshold tuning alone.

Multi-modal extension:
 Incorporating images and video alongside text
10
 would make the framework more applicable to real-world FND.

Broader and live evaluation:
 Testing on additional datasets, under different types of concept drift
13
 and adversarial manipulation, and ultimately against a live/streaming fact-checking deployment rather than a fixed benchmark, would clarify the framework’s real-world practical limits, and would test whether PHEME-Event’s severe-forgetting pattern is representative of a broader class of streaming FND scenarios or an outlier of this particular task construction.

Other CL settings:
 Task-free and domain-incremental learning scenarios present different challenges and deserve separate evaluation.

Efficient architectures:
 Replacing the BERT backbone with lighter Transformers like FastFormer

,

,

 would improve scalability for high-throughput streaming.

Conclusion
Standard deep learning models forget when updated. That is a well-documented failure mode, and it makes these systems impractical for fake news detection once misinformation patterns shift past whatever the training data covered. Rehearsal-based continual learning methods counter this reliably, but only by storing real past examples, a cost many streaming deployments cannot pay. This paper introduces EAKD-CFND as one framework offered in two configurations: an exemplar-free default that never stores a past instance, and EAKD-CFND-R, a rehearsal-augmented configuration for deployments where a storage budget is available. The experiments reported here evaluate both, across all three benchmarks, to say which configuration fits which deployment.
The exemplar-free default matches rehearsal-based DER and the strongest prior adaptive-KD baseline (LUD) on two of our three benchmarks while storing zero past instances. On the third, PHEME-Event’s five-task sequence, it collapses alongside every other exemplar-free method we test, not a defect specific to EAKD-CFND but the measured boundary of exemplar-free continual learning under severe forgetting pressure. EAKD-CFND-R closes that gap almost completely on PHEME-Event, and adds a further, measurable benefit over both the exemplar-free default and DER on FNN-Poli-Time, where the label-replay variants (+PP, +PP+UW) are strongest. On FNN-Gossip-Time, EAKD-CFND-R buys nothing: an isolated-per-task-ceiling check confirms that this benchmark’s tasks carry little real forgetting pressure to recover from in the first place, so no rehearsal variant separates from the exemplar-free default there. Across all three benchmarks, once a rehearsal buffer is present, neither its own instance-level weighting nor external verification adds a measurable further edge. The uncertainty signal driving the framework is poorly calibrated even though the framework’s accuracy proves robust to how its two hyperparameters are set, and its computational and API cost is now quantified rather than argued qualitatively.
Neither configuration is an unconditional win; together they give a practitioner a measured basis for choosing. Exemplar-free by default: it is free on two of three benchmarks tested and the right choice whenever storing raw historical data is undesirable or disallowed. Rehearsal-augmented when the drift is severe enough and the storage budget allows it: PHEME-Event and FNN-Poli-Time both show a measured benefit from the switch, while FNN-Gossip-Time shows none, and the isolated-per-task-ceiling check gives a direct way to tell which regime a new deployment is in before committing to either configuration. Whether this specific pattern (collapse under small-class, high-drift task sequences; no benefit from rehearsal under low-drift ones) holds on benchmarks beyond the three tested here is the most direct next question, and any continual-learning problem with a queryable external source of ground truth remains a reasonable place to extend this line of work.

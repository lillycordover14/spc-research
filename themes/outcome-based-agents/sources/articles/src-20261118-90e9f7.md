Investing.com -- Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller called for more aggressive action to contain inflation on Wednesday, warning that price pressures are proving more persistent than he expected and are spreading across the economy.

Speaking in a live CNBC interview, Waller said the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) should consider raising its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points at its December 8-9 meeting, rather than the conventional quarter-point increase.
 
 
"I'm very, very concerned about inflation. It's kind of headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out," Waller told CNBC's Steve Liesman. "If you really want to get it down, you've got to start taking more aggressive actions."
 
 
Waller said the Fed needs to reverse the three quarter-point rate cuts it approved in 2025 and potentially go further. He argued that simply returning interest rates to their levels last summer would not be enough to bring inflation down, since policy at that time was not sufficiently restrictive to achieve the Fed's 2% inflation target.
 
 
"You might need to, like, shock people," Waller said.

His preference for a larger December move comes with a caveat. Waller said a 25-basis-point increase could be sufficient if the Fed clearly signaled another hike in January. However, if policymakers are unwilling to provide that forward guidance, he believes they should deliver a 50-basis-point increase in December instead.
 
 
The comments highlight a growing divide among Fed officials over how aggressively to respond to persistent inflation. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem suggested Tuesday that as many as four rate increases could be necessary by spring 2027, while Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan have favored a more gradual approach.
 
 
New York Fed President John Williams offered a notably different assessment Tuesday, saying he did not believe the situation called for dramatic action and that the Fed's recent approach of gathering data and making decisions meeting by meeting was working well.

Markets have been pricing in a more gradual tightening path, with futures reflecting expectations for a quarter-point increase in December, another move in January and at least one additional hike before the end of 2027, according to CME FedWatch.
 
 
Waller also warned that businesses and consumers may be becoming accustomed to inflation around 3%, raising concerns that expectations could become entrenched above the Fed's target.
 
 
The FOMC is scheduled to release minutes from its October meeting later Wednesday, offering further insight into the debate among policymakers over the appropriate path for interest rates.

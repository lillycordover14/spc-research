ElevenLabs Plans Kotoba Acquisition to Expand Japanese Voice AI&nbsp;&nbsp;www.tokenpost.com

[page text]
A microphone stands before acoustic panels in a quiet lab / TokenPost.ai
ElevenLabs plans to acquire Kotoba, a Japanese voice AI lab, to broaden AI-agent and voice-interface adoption in Japan while adding technology built around the country’s language and social conventions.
The deal is expected to close later in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to regulatory approval. ElevenLabs also plans to expand its sales presence in Japan and invest in infrastructure there.
Japanese sentences can leave subjects and objects unstated, vocabulary can shift with social context, and speakers often communicate indirectly. These patterns can complicate how voice systems interpret conversations and respond appropriately.
Kotoba’s Koto model handles three tasks: turning written text into speech, transcribing speech as text and providing simultaneous interpretation. Salesforce uses Koto in its Agentforce Voice platform. ElevenLabs aims to help more companies build AI agents that can handle complex Japanese conversations.
AI researchers Noriyuki Kojima and Jungo Kasai founded Kotoba in 2023. Kojima, the company’s CEO, previously worked as a research scientist at Hugging Face and co-developed Japan’s Fugaku-LLM on the RIKEN Fugaku supercomputer. Kasai, Kotoba’s CTO, has experience researching East Asian languages.
The proposed acquisition would add to ElevenLabs’ business AI work. At its Nov. 11 New York summit, the company previewed an 
AI interaction engine
 for managing conversations and outlined model-customization tools for businesses. Its plans for Japan extend that work into a market where Kotoba has developed speech technology for local language and social norms.
“Kotoba has built state-of-the-art voice models that reflect Japan’s linguistic and social norms,” said Mati Staniszewski, ElevenLabs’ co-founder and CEO. “Together, we can further develop the interaction layer for AI in Japan.”
The acquisition’s next step is regulatory review, with closing expected later in the fourth quarter of 2026.

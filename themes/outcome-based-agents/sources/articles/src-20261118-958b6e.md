Registered voters in both Clay and Charles Mix counties on Tuesday elected not to approve ordinances that would impose a local option sales tax to offset county property taxes on owner-occupied homes.
According to unofficial results from the Clay County Auditor’s Office, 1,332 voters cast ballots against the ordinance during a special referendum election, while 975 voters cast ballots in favor of the ordinance. With 8,406 current active registered voters, that is a turnout of nearly 27.5%. There were also four votes that were not counted in the final overall vote totals, meaning that they likely were provisional ballots.
The Clay County Commission is scheduled to canvass the special election results during its meeting on Thursday at the courthouse in Vermillion.

The unofficial results from a special referendum election in Charles Mix County were much closer. According to unofficial results from the auditor’s office, 654 voters cast ballots against the ordinance, while 648 voters cast ballots in favor of the ordinance. With 5,791 current active registered voters, that is a turnout of nearly 22.5%.
There were also seven provisional ballots not counted in the final overall vote totals for Charles Mix County’s special election. A provisional ballot is issued when there is a question about a voter’s eligibility at a polling place. For example, one reason a voter could be asked to fill out a provisional ballot is if his or her identification is challenged at a polling location.
Provisional ballots are not counted on Election Night. These ballots are set aside until voters’ eligibility can be verified. If a county auditor determines provisional ballots should be counted, the counting of the provisional ballots is conducted one hour prior to the county canvass.
Depending on what happens, the final overall vote totals could change for Charles Mix County’s special election, and a recount could also become a possibility. The County Commission is scheduled to canvass the election results during its meeting on Thursday at the courthouse in Lake Andes.
If each ordinance had been approved by a simple majority of voters, a half-percent county gross receipts tax and use tax would have become effective on July 1 next year in both Clay and Charles Mix counties. However, because each ordinance was not approved by voters, these taxes will not take effect, though that remains to be seen in Charles Mix County.
The special elections in Clay and Charles Mix counties both originated from petitions by registered voters referring each county’s proposed ordinance to a public vote.

The three-member Charles Mix County Commission unanimously approved the second reading of its proposed sales tax ordinance on Sept. 1 and then adopted it.
Despite public opposition two weeks in a row, the five-member Clay County Commission voted 3-2 on Aug. 25 in favor of the second reading of its proposed sales tax ordinance and then adopted it.
With both County Commissions approving their proposed ordinances, residents of each county who are registered voters had the choice to petition to refer each decision to a public vote — and a group of people collected signatures throughout each county to do so.
If implemented, these ordinances in Clay and Charles Mix counties would have each imposed a half-percent county option gross receipts tax — or sales tax — and a use tax, authorized by the passage of Senate Bill 96 during the 2026 session of the South Dakota Legislature. Passage of these ordinances would have affected owner-occupied homeowners, who would have received a reduction in the county government portion of their property taxes.
Opponents of the local option sales tax policy have said it takes money from renters and non-homeowners to provide relief to homeowners. However, proponents have said it allows for a wider tax base — including out-of-state spenders — to help ease property tax pressures on homeowners.
The South Dakota Legislature has been working on property tax relief for years. Senate Bill 96 became a new state law — South Dakota Codified Law (SDCL) 10-52B — and took effect on July 1, giving counties across the state their first opportunity to enact a half-cent local option sales tax. The state and cities already have the authority to do that.
Custer, Fall River, Lawrence, Lincoln, Meade and Pennington counties have all approved a local option sales tax that takes effect on Jan. 1. Most of these counties are in the tourism-friendly Black Hills region of western South Dakota, while Lincoln County in the eastern part of the state includes part of Sioux Falls.
Earlier this month, by a 3-2 vote, the Yankton County Commission tabled indefinitely any action on a proposed county sales and use tax. The commissioners, who have received public opposition to the sales tax ordinance, expect the South Dakota Legislature to revisit the issue when the 2027 session convenes in January.

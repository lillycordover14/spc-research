Fed Minutes Show Some Officials Weighed December Hike Above 25 Basis Points&nbsp;&nbsp;Bitcoin Foundation

[page text]
Fed minutes released Nov. 18 showed broad support for more rate hikes if the economic outlook called for them. A couple of officials said the committee could consider an increase of more than 25 basis points at its next meeting if incoming inflation data turned unfavorable.
The October minutes put the possible size of a December increase under scrutiny after stronger inflation and hiring figures. Further tightening had broad conditional support, while only a much smaller group discussed a larger move.

What the October Fed Minutes Revealed on Nov. 18
What Did the Fed Decide on Oct. 28?
The committee voted 10-2 to keep its benchmark interest rate at 3.75% to 4%. Both dissenting voters wanted a quarter-point increase, making the October disagreement a question of whether to tighten immediately.

How Federal Reserve Policy in 2026 Is Shaping Bitcoin’s Next Move
The split extended beyond the two dissenting votes. Some officials favored an increase, while a few who supported holding rates said the decision had been a close call.
What Did the Minutes Add to the October Decision?
The minutes showed conditional support for further tightening, alongside a narrower discussion about moving faster. As 
BeInCrypto quoted the minutes
: “A couple of participants noted that an increase of more than 25 basis points could warrant consideration at the next meeting if incoming inflation data were unfavorable.”
The officials tied a larger increase to later inflation figures rather than committing the committee to a specific move.
The Officials Behind the Quarter-Point and Half-Point Cases
What Is the Case for Steady Quarter-Point Increases?
Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack has backed steady quarter-point increases, according to BeInCrypto.
The October dissenters also supported a quarter-point increase. Favoring an immediate hike and favoring a larger move in December were separate policy choices.
What Did Christopher Waller Call for on Nov. 18?
Fed Governor Christopher Waller called for a half-point increase on Nov. 18, according to BeInCrypto. His position gave the larger-move argument a named advocate after the October meeting.
The minutes said a couple of unnamed participants had earlier discussed an increase exceeding 25 basis points.
From July’s Divided Hold to September’s Hike and October’s Pause
How Did the Tightening Debate Develop in July?
At the July 28–29 meeting, the Fed kept its benchmark range at 3.50% to 3.75%. Three voting members dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. Many participants also saw a possible need for more tightening if inflation failed to move convincingly toward the Fed’s 2% target.
Officials who favored earlier action said price pressures had spread across the economy and that acting sooner could reduce the risk of a more aggressive series of increases later. Softer hiring, they said, could justify a cautious approach.
Most participants still expected inflation to decline over time, but many warned that it could stay above target for an extended period. They also pointed to elevated underlying price pressures after excluding some effects linked to energy and tariffs. July CPI had cooled to 3.4%, leaving policymakers to judge whether the improvement would last as energy prices and broader supply pressures remained elevated.
The market backdrop was different when the July minutes came out. Treasury yields fell sharply after the U.S. Treasury announced plans to increase purchases of longer-dated government debt. Earlier in that session, the move supported stocks and Bitcoin while pressuring the dollar; the S&P 500 and Dow also rebounded ahead of the minutes.
What Changed at the September Meeting?
The Fed raised rates by a quarter point at its Sept. 15–16 meeting, taking the range to 3.75% to 4%. Officials also penciled in another increase by year-end, according to Reuters coverage published by SRN News.
October’s hold left that September range unchanged. The sequence moved from a July debate over an increase to a September hike, then to a pause that kept further tightening on the table.
Why Were Officials Already Watching Treasury Market Stress?
A few participants said in September that Treasury markets were functioning smoothly but urged the Fed to prepare for possible stress. They proposed strengthening its strategies, communications and tools for responding to dysfunction while limiting its market footprint.
Reuters said officials attributed higher yields to a solid economic outlook, strong technology investment competing for capital and geopolitical concerns. The rise had also increased mortgage borrowing costs.
The discussion raised a balance-sheet question as well. During the early stages of the COVID-19 pandemic, the Fed bought large amounts of Treasury and mortgage debt to address market stress before those purchases became a form of stimulus. New Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh opposed large Fed holdings and had named a task force to examine ways to shrink the balance sheet.
Derek Tang, an analyst at Monetary Policy Analytics, read the September discussion as a signal that the Fed would intervene “only if policy transmission is threatened, not simply because yields are rising rapidly.” He expected liquidity-providing standing repo operations and the discount window to be the first line of defense if markets became unsettled.
Gennadiy Goldberg, head of 
US
 rates strategy at TD Securities, offered a more technical interpretation. He said the discussion could involve Treasury bill purchases to bolster reserve levels. In the Oct. 7 report, he said the Fed might resume Reserve Management Purchases “at a modest pace before year-end” to keep markets functioning smoothly. That was an analyst’s expectation, not an announced purchase plan.
Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari had shown little appetite for broad intervention in an interview the preceding week. “I see every indication that the Treasury market is functioning fine,” he said, adding that he saw neither a financial stability risk nor a reason for the Fed to intervene under those conditions.
Why the Rate Debate Matters for Bitcoin and Borrowers
How Did Crypto Trade Immediately After the Release?
Bitcoin and ether 
slipped on Coinbase in the 10 minutes after the minutes came out
, according to The Defiant. BeInCrypto said gold also slipped after the release.
Bitcoin traded at $62,492, down 2.22% over 24 hours, as of 21:00 UTC on Nov. 18, according to CoinGecko market data. Traders focused on the December policy decision and how far officials might go if inflation stayed unfavorable.
Who Would Face Higher Financing Costs From a Larger Increase?
BeInCrypto said a half-point increase would raise borrowing costs for homebuyers, companies and AI firms behind that month’s stock sell-off.
The outlet also said officials were weighing AI investment risks as they considered the rate outlook.
The Economic Numbers Behind the December Rate Debate
What Did October Inflation Add to the Discussion?
Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts, according to BeInCrypto. The monthly increase came after the October policy meeting.
The minutes made unfavorable incoming inflation data the condition for considering a larger move. BeInCrypto reported that October’s inflation data had arrived.
How Strong Was Hiring Compared With Expectations?
Employers added 165,000 jobs, compared with expectations of 65,000, according to BeInCrypto.
What Were Traders Pricing Before the Minutes?
CME FedWatch figures cited by BeInCrypto shortly before the release put the chance of a December rate increase at 95%. The probability of a half-point move stood at 5.3%, up from 3.1% a day earlier.
A quarter-point increase remained the favored outcome, according to BeInCrypto.
What Was Happening in Bonds and Stocks?
BeInCrypto said the 10-year Treasury yield was above 5% before the release and that technology stocks had led a $2.2 trillion global sell-off on Nov. 16.
AI Financing and Hedge Fund Leverage Are Risks to Watch
Why Did AI Borrowing Draw Officials’ Attention?
Almost all officials discussed what could happen if AI investments disappointed, according to BeInCrypto. Most said debt tied to the sector had expanded through “complex and opaque financing structures.”
Could Closer Scrutiny of AI Spending Help Cool Inflation?
A number of officials said closer scrutiny of AI spending could cool inflation on its own, according to BeInCrypto.
What Risk Did Staff Identify in Hedge Fund Leverage?
Fed staff warned that hedge fund leverage could spread a sell-off, according to BeInCrypto.
Waller Also Put Fed Payment Rails at the Center of Tokenization
A day before calling for a half-point increase, Waller addressed another issue for digital asset markets: how tokenized assets could settle through central bank payment infrastructure. In a Nov. 17 speech in New York, covered by 
CoinDesk
, he said the Fed could help with “cross-system connectivity and trust.”
Waller pointed to the resilience of traditional large-value and instant-payment rails during market events and to their links with central bank money. He called those systems “a reliable backbone for final funds settlement” on which services including tokenized markets could develop. He had also advocated limited-access “skinny” Fed master accounts for firms seeking access to central bank payment systems without the full compliance burden of a standard account.
Waller also connected tokenization with AI. “While agentic commerce is still in the early stages, tokenization and AI could be catalysts for one another,” he said. He identified shared questions about trust, standards and whether networks should be open or closed.
Fellow Fed Governor Michael Barr raised a separate concern that week, warning that AI agents could trigger high-speed bank runs and threaten U.S. financial stability. His warning focused on the speed of withdrawals, adding a specific AI risk alongside the minutes’ discussion of investment financing.
What to Watch at the Dec. 8–9 Fed Meeting
What Will Shape the Choice Between Smaller and Larger Steps?
Officials are scheduled to meet Dec. 8–9, according to BeInCrypto. The participants who raised the possibility of a larger increase made it conditional on unfavorable incoming inflation data.
BeInCrypto reported October’s consumer-price increase and strong hiring. The outlet also said some officials thought closer scrutiny of AI investment could cool inflation on its own.
Where Does the Committee’s Debate Leave Crypto Investors?
Hammack’s preference for steady quarter-point increases and Waller’s half-point call show the competing approaches ahead of December, according to BeInCrypto.
Bitcoin and ether slipped on Coinbase in the 10 minutes after the release, according to The Defiant. BeInCrypto said officials had discussed risks tied to AI financing and hedge fund leverage.

Google upgrades Gemini’s agentic Task mode with new model, e-commerce features

																																	by								
Maria Deutscher

Google LLC is releasing a new version of its Gemini artificial intelligence assistant that is better at performing multistep tasks.

Josh Woodward, the vice president responsible for the Gemini app, detailed the update in a Tuesday 
blog post
.

The AI assistant has two modes that are accessible through separate interface tabs. The first, Chat, generates quick, relatively simple responses to prompts. The second mode, which is called Task, enables Gemini to perform complex tasks such as booking appointments and comparing online product listings. Task is the focus of this week’s update.

Google is switching the feature to Gemini 4 Hydrogen, its newest large language model. It’s somewhat less capable than Gemini 4 Carbon, the search giant’s most advanced LLM, but significantly more cost-efficient. Hydrogen is also fast: Google says it can process some tasks 2.8 times faster than its previous entry-level model.

One way Hydrogen reduces wait times is by parallelizing project steps that are usually performed one after another. According to Woodward, users of Gemini’s Task mode can give the model feedback after each step to guide its work. The model proactively asks users for instructions before performing a sensitive task such as making an online purchase.

Today, Gemini’s Task mode uses Google Wallet to process transactions. Woodward said that the search giant is working with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa to add support for a technology called payment tokenization. It replaces the user’s card number with a placeholder value called a token. That token is shared with merchants instead of the card number to reduce the risk of data leaks.

Google also plans to add support for a second technology called the Universal Commerce Protocol, or UCP. It makes it easier for AI agents such as Gemini to interact with online stores and thereby lowers the risk of errors.

Gemini’s Task mode can use data in other Google services such as Gmail to inform its decisions. For example, the agent might take the user’s past purchases into account when generating shopping suggestions. One of Gemini’s main selling points is that it can use such data without moving it off Google infrastructure, which is often not the case with competing agents.

“When you assign Gemini a task, it uses Google Cloud’s Agent Compute Engine to work in a hardware-isolated virtual machine that’s dedicated to your account and never shared with other users,” Woodward wrote. “Each virtual machine is sandboxed, so Gemini works in a separate, isolated environment that’s regularly tested by our security red teams.”

Google has made Gemini’s Task mode free in the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India as part of the update. That’s notable because Anthropic PBC and OpenAI Group PBC charge for the comparable features they offer as part of their chatbot services. Google’s move may prompt them to revise their pricing.

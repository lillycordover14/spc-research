Commanders Film Review: Ben Sinnott vs Giants
It’s fair to say Commanders tight end Ben Sinnott has yet to live up to the lofty expectations of being a second round pick back in 2024. Sinnott has quietly developed into a strong blocker that can line up as a tight end or move around into fullback positions, so he still provides some value to the team, but his receiving numbers have been almost nonexistent. Prior to the Giants game on Thursday night, Sinnott had a total of 17 catches for 148 yards and two touchdowns through the first two and a half years of his career. Obviously, much more production is expected from a second round pick.
But against the Giants, Sinnott had the best game of his career to date. When starting tight end Chig Okonkwo left the game with an injury, Sinnott stepped up and caught four passes for 42 yards and a touchdown. Again, those aren’t staggering numbers, but they were career high numbers for a player that has lacked opportunities and production in the passing game. So let’s dive into Sinnott’s career day and see what might be sustainable going forward.

Can the Commanders Keep Pace With Joe Burrow Without Terry McLaurin?
A month after tying the Giants, the 
Washington Commanders
 
beat them 24–21 Thursday night
 and kept their season alive.
Monday brings a familiar quarterback in Joe Burrow. Jayden Daniels beat him and the Bengals 38–33 in 2024. Now Washington faces the prospect of keeping pace with Burrow without Terry McLaurin.
What Changed Since the 2024 Shootout
Daniels, in just his third career game, began his iconic rookie-year scoring reel in Cincinnati, at a time when the Bengals were still respected as one of the NFL’s feared contenders. His 
27-yard pass to lead wideout Terry McLaurin
 was one of the most electrifying instances of the stars’ immediate chemistry, as well as one of the first indicators of the potentially special underdog roster that Washington’s executives had compiled.
McLaurin caught four passes for 100 yards against Cincinnati in 2024. He’s out for the season after tearing his patellar tendon. Stefon Diggs caught the winning 32-yard touchdown against the Giants last week, and 
Antonio Williams
 scored from 31 yards out. If Monday requires the same kind of scoring from Daniels, those are plays Washington needs from its current receivers.
Cincinnati’s defensive front looks different from the one Daniels faced in 2024. After Trey Hendrickson’s departure, Boye Mafe became a primary edge rusher, while Dexter Lawrence and former Commander Jonathan Allen added strength inside.
Cincinnati’s defense gave up 36 points to Tennessee on November 1, so Daniels should have chances. The Bengals have since beaten Atlanta and Pittsburgh, holding the Steelers to 18 points Sunday while Burrow threw for 
409 yards and four touchdowns
. Cincinnati is 7–2 and has won five straight. Washington may need far more than it scored against the Giants to keep up Monday.

’Baby Kobe’: Jayden Daniels was clutch when Commanders needed him most
Jayden Daniels, who was the only starting quarterback not to throw an interception heading into the Washington Commanders’ second matchup with the New York Giants, opened his postgame press conference with a joke.
“Yes, I threw two picks,” Daniels said. “Yeah, got that out of my system. So, we can move forward about that.”
Daniels delivered a clutch performance -- his first fourth-quarter comeback in almost two seasons -- against the Giants, finding Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard touchdown to give the Commanders a 24-21 victory. However, what he did on the previous two drives played a role in making the moment necessary, as he threw two interceptions in a span of five plays. Each moment felt like a death knell for the Commanders, who needed a win in Week 10 to keep their playoff hopes -- and their season -- alive.
But if the interceptions were proof that Daniels is still a young quarterback with plenty to learn, the plays he made with the game on the line were the latest examples of why he’s viewed as the future of the franchise.
“I was proud of him [Daniels] for coming back to respond and ripping it,” head coach Dan Quinn said after the game.
Daniels had put together a solid game through the first three quarters. He looked comfortable in the pocket as he led the offense to 17 points, throwing his ninth and 10th touchdowns of the season to Ben Sinnott and Antonio Williams, respectively. There were some bumps along the way, like taking a sack for a 14-yard loss and failing to capitalize on a forced fumble from the defense, but Daniels looked mostly like himself, completing 16 of his 21 pass attempts for 180 yards in the first 45 minutes.
The Commanders’ next three drives ended in frustration at best and disaster at worst. They stalled on fourth-and-1 at the Giants’ 30-yard line, setting up a 66-yard touchdown from Isaiah Likely that gave New York a four-point lead three plays later. The Commanders got into Giants’ territory again, but Daniels’s pass to Chig Okonkwo was underthrown and thrown inside, which made it easy for safety Javon Holland to intercept it at the Giants’ 2-yard line.

Laremy Tunsil’s next step leaves Commanders facing more than a medical call
Head coach Dan Quinn confirmed that left tackle Laremy Tunsil has moved on to the next stage of his 
rehabilitation from a torn triceps
 after a recent doctor’s visit. It’s the strength part of the process, but the Washington Commanders still don’t know if he will feature again in 2026.
It’s a precarious situation, but the Commanders shouldn’t rush things.
And Tunsil’s return may even be predicated on how the Commanders fare heading into their stretch run.
Laremy Tunsil’s recovery takes the next step, but Commanders should be cautious
Losing the five-time Pro Bowl blindside enforcer 
during training camp was a body blow. Tunsil is one of the few elite players Washington has, arguably the team’s best overall player. And his presence has been sorely missed.
Brandon Coleman is trying to fill the void
, but his skills look best suited to the interior. There isn’t really anybody else who can man the left tackle spot, aside from Esa Pole on the practice squad. That speaks to a lack of depth and the wrong offseason recruitment approach, but it shouldn’t impact Washington’s plan with Tunsil.
Tunsil’s been out for months. He may be making good progress, but until his strength returns, the Commanders’ hands are tied.
And if they are out of contention in the coming weeks, one has to wonder what the point of Tunsil’s return would be.
Obviously, Tunsil is a supreme competitor. Even in his absence, the Ole Miss product has stayed engaged, helping the likes of 
Coleman and right tackle Josh Conerly Jr. 
with their technique, overall approach, and week-to-week preparation.
That’s a sign of his 
exceptional leadership
, but he will no doubt be itching to get back onto the field.

Inside the Cowboys historic defensive collapse against the 49ers
The Dallas Cowboys lost a game to the San Francisco 49ers. That’s not a shocker to anyone, as this Cowboys team has struggled all season, and the 49ers are the best team they’ve played so far, and by a good margin. Looking at their opponents, San Francisco is the only offense the Cowboys have faced this year that is ranked in the top eight in scoring, now averaging over 31 points a game. In fact, the Cowboys haven’t fared well against offenses that average over 22 points a game.
They’ve played five teams that average more than 22 points/game. Their record against those teams is 1-4.
They’ve played five teams that average fewer than 22 points/game. Their record against those teams is 3-2.
Clearly, playing decent offenses is not ideal for this Cowboys defense, so when they faced the NFC’s highest-scoring offense, it was a huge problem for a unit that has struggled immensely all season. Even so, it shouldn’t have been that bad. The defense gave up 584 total yards on the day. It was the most yards they have ever given up at home during their 67-year existence. The most ever is never good.
The frustrating part about this game is that when you take a closer look, it’s easy to tell that the Cowboys never had a shot. The defense was just outmatched. It was like playing one of those games where you play against an older sibling, where they might toy around with you for a bit, only to flatten you when it’s time to get serious.
The 49ers did that to the Cowboys’ defense. That doesn’t mean they were playing opossum at any point during the game. It’s just that whenever the 49ers found themselves in a crunch, they always had a go-to play where they knew they could take advantage of the Cowboys’ vulnerabilities.

When the two teams line up on Sunday, it will mark the first time this season the Giants will look across the field at a team with a winning record. The 6-4 Los Angeles Rams are currently the only winning team the Giants have faced, but the Rams were 0-1 when the teams met.

Commanders next game is on Monday Night Football at home in Landover vs the Cincinnati Bengals
3 reasons why the Bengals will beat the Commanders on MNF, and 3 reasons they won’t
The 
Cincinnati Bengals
 did it! They not only got revenge on their hated division rival for their Week 3 defeat, but they absolutely dominated the Pittsburgh Steelers from the first snap to the last. They won the game 38-18, and 15 of those 18 points were from garbage time scores.
So even though the Bengals won by 20 points, the game wasn’t even 
that
 close.
Now they travel to Landover, Maryland, to face the Washington Commanders on Monday Night Football. The Bengals last played the Commanders in 2024, when Jayden Daniels and the Washington offense beat the Bengals in a shootout, 38-33.
What will happen on Monday night?
Here are three reasons the Bengals will win on Monday Night Football, followed by three reasons they won’t.
The Bengals will win because….
They’re a better team from top to bottom.
This is a fact. The Bengals have a more talented roster, and that more talented roster is playing at a higher level than the Commanders are likely even capable of. The Commanders’ offense has scored over 30 points just once this season, and that was in Week 3. The Bengals have scored at least 30 points in five consecutive games, and Joe Burrow is leading the pack for NFL MVP at this point in the season.
And then there’s Ja’Marr Chase and Tee Higgins, who have time to do their thing because the offensive line is doing a good job at protecting the quarterback. The Bengals scored seemingly at will on Sunday Night Football against a defense that has been playing good football over the prior month.
The Commanders are talented—there isn’t a team in the league that doesn’t have talent all over their roster. But they’re not nearly as loaded as the Bengals. Nobody is.

No-Huddle Notes

 

Welcome to the Week 11 edition of 
Snaps & Pace
 — back-to-back winner of the FSWA’s Best Football Series — where we examine trends in play volume and game pace. It is meant to be a 30,000-foot view of upcoming contests, while identifying main-slate matchups that will — and will not — be played on fertile fantasy soil. For a primer on why this is important,
 
click here
.

If the NFL was hoping to build anticipation for its Thanksgiving quintet, things could not have broken much better in Week 11. The operative word being “broken.”

If 
Geno Smith
 and 
Baker Mayfield
 cannot suit up, eight of the 10 main-slate matchups will feature backup quarterbacks. Four of those games have double-digit spreads, with two hitting 16.5 points.

Three of the top four offenses in EPA per play are not on the main slate, and the late-afternoon window looks like something 
Roger Goodell
 scraped off his shoe. While the Europe games have mercifully ended, we have six teams on bye for reasons known only to the NFL’s accountants and schedule-making AI.

Sadly, this main slate won’t lend itself to the usual format of three up-pace games and three slogs — so we will avoid shoehorning games under inappropriate labels. Inappropriate analogies, of course, are another story. Fortunately, we do have one fast-paced matchup to rule them all. It will not, however, be a secret to anyone with a Twitter account.

As we eagerly await next week’s Thanksgiving football festivities — as well as Vikings-49ers this Sunday night — Week 11 stands lopsided before us, and we must dive in.

Up In Pace

 

Tennessee Titans at Dallas Cowboys

The Cowboys spent Sunday afternoon piling up 40 points on nearly 450 yards, and they still lost while getting out-gained by almost 150 yards. Yet the biggest crime Dallas’ defense commits against its offense is not being on their schedule. Luckily, like 
Jerry Jones
’ favorite Fleshlight, the Titans make a perfect proxy. They’ve been pumped for 83 points and 969 yards in two games since jettisoning 
Jeffery Simmons
, and no defense has allowed a higher success rate over the last three weeks. The truly exciting thing about Tennessee — for us geeks, anyway — is an offense finally making good on preseason pace predictions. With, among other things, half a season under OC 
Brian Daboll
’s belt, the Titans have fully deployed his preferred no-huddle approach. They’ve barreled into first in hurry-up rate on the year (23%) with outings of 29%, 41%, and 49% over their last three games. 
Cam Ward
 has been a clear tempo advocate, and 
Robert Saleh
 noted they’re doing all they can to evaluate him as a franchise quarterback. The approach isn’t a game plan-specific detour.

The elevated pace has significantly increased play volume. Over the last month, Titans games are second in combined plays (132.3), as well as second in total points (64.3). Before that, they were 30th (118.8) and 31st (35.5), respectively. Cowboys contests, of course, rank first in both plays (136.3) and points (64.5) during that four-week stretch. On the season, Dallas operates at the fifth-fastest neutral pace over expected and ranks second in neutral play-clock seconds. Not to be outdone, Tennessee is up to first in pace and third in play-clock seconds during the past month. The only thing the Cowboys have done lately that depresses play volume is deploy the fifth-lowest PROE of the last four weeks, versus ranking fifth highest before that. It hasn’t made a dent. When a fast-paced, efficient passing game mixes with the stinking carcass of a defense that’s allowed the fifth-most yards per game of any team since the turn of the century, a few extra handoffs aren’t slowing the pace train. With the Titans also speeding into Jerry World, Sunday afternoon will feel like we’re all riding that train.

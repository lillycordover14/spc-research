[page text]
Why Should Delaware Care? 
Thomas McKean High School is the second largest high school in the Red Clay Consolidated School District, but it’s among the smallest high schools in the state by enrollment. A new proposal could see the Pike Creek-area school converted into a career and technical and early college program, but opponents have put forward a counter plan.

On Wednesday night, the Red Clay Consolidated Board of Education will discuss alternative plans for the future of McKean High School.  

The contents of that conversation were not immediately clear, as a relevant line on the agenda only stated “Reimagining Secondary Education update.” Another line under the section for items submitted by the board stated “Discussion of Plan B, C, and D – J. Howard, D. Hynson.”

On Tuesday afternoon, Spotlight Delaware received a copy of an alternative plan to the controversial McKean Innovation Center, which would transition Thomas McKean High School into a drop-in building focused on career and technical education programs and early college credit opportunities.

The proposal, which is being brought forward by board members Jenny Howard, Devon Hynson, and Jose Matthews, includes three different plans. 

None of the plans includes closing one of the district’s traditional high schools. 

The first option proposes using existing schools, facilities, and programs to distribute career and technical education (CTE) pathways across the district, rather than creating a centralized hub with the McKean Innovation Center. It would also have Alexis I. duPont High School serve as the “foundational campus and first implementation site” by strengthening the school’s existing CTE programs. 

The second option would convert A.I. High School into a vocational technical high school. According to the proposal, the district could either transfer or establish the campus as an additional New Castle County Vocational Technical School District campus. It could also be established as a standalone technical high school and operated by the Red Clay Consolidated School District.

The last proposes that Red Clay “develop a five-school magnet system” where every school has a “specialized identity,” and students have equal access to the district’s three traditional high schools – A.I DuPont, McKean and Dickinson – along with the Cab Calloway School of the Arts and Conrad Schools of Science. 

Under the five-school magnet system, traditional high school attendance zones would be eliminated, and students entering ninth grade would rank all five schools in order of their preference. 

Innovation Center sparks controversy

The alternative options come seven months after 
board members voted 6-1
 to indefinitely pause the McKean Innovation Center plans following community pushback last spring. 

Multiple parents voiced concerns during public meetings leading up to the pause, with some expressing concerns about the lack of certainty surrounding the future Meadwood program for students with intellectual and developmental disabilities. 

At the time, parents questioned the stability of the Meadwood program if it was moved to A.I. High under the innovation center plan.

Board members also produced 11 conditions they said must be addressed if the district were to move forward with the plan. Those conditions included calls for an equity analysis to be completed and expanded communication plans, among others.

During an October board meeting, Red Clay’s Director of Secondary Schools Mark Pruitt unveiled a 
75-page report
 that he said answered many community members’ concerns and addressed the board’s conditions.  

If implemented, the McKean Innovation Center 
would open in August 2028
 and reduce the number of traditional high schools in the district from three to two while increasing enrollment numbers at A.I. High and Dickinson. 

The school district has held town hall meetings in the weeks since Pruitt’s report to the school board, but community members have continued to push back against the plan, arguing that the district must publish reports such as financial impacts and transportation logistics before any decisions are made. 

Friends of A.I. members
, an alumni organization for A.I. High School that advocates for current students, have also pushed against the plan. 

Two members of Friends of A.I., Brenda Steffon and Jared Obstfeld, expressed support for the alternative option that would disperse CTE programs throughout the district. 

“Plan B gives me a little hope that the board is finally listening… the people who are behind it know the potential of A.I.,” Steffon said.

Still, the two said feasibility studies should be completed for any of the options before the district moves forward. 

They also expressed concerns about how the McKean Innovation Center plan would impact A.I. students if it were implemented as is. If the innovation center plan is implemented 
in accordance with the October report
, students would use a shuttle to attend McKean during the morning or afternoon of a school day. 

Steffon said that when she has asked what happens if a student is unable to make the shuttle or misses it, and said the response is that a parent would take their student to the school.  

“We’re dealing with primarily probably working-class families who may not have that luxury to leave their jobs [and take their child to school,]” Steffon said.

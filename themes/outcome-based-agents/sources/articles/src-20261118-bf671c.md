Denglin Technology (09917.HK) announced unaudited operating data for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, with cumulative revenue topping CNY 500 million (approximately $75.0 million), up about 592% from a year earlier — nearly sixfold. The company showed simultaneous improvement on both customer structure and hardware delivery, the two highlights of this report card.

According to the announcement, Denglin Technology had 123 cumulative customers in the first nine months, with its top three customers accounting for 50.7% of revenue. Notably, customer concentration shifted: the top five customers' revenue share fell to 68.8% in the first nine months from 90.4% in the four months ended April 30, 2026 — a drop of more than 21 percentage points, indicating that revenue sources are gradually diversifying and no longer heavily dependent on a few large orders.

On hardware delivery, cumulative board shipments exceeded 27,990 units as of the end of September, with GPGPU hardware product deliveries clearly accelerating. The company positions itself as a Chinese supplier of domestic inference GPUs, with main commercial scenarios covering industry large models, enterprise private clouds, smart cities and industrial quality inspection.

Denglin Technology said that compared with the data for the four months ended April 30, 2026, disclosed in the prospectus published on October 30, 2026, multiple operating indicators showed significant improvement. The board is optimistic about the progress of product commercialization and emphasized that it will rely on chip innovation and architecture evolution to steadily advance expansion of product business scenarios, in line with the development trend toward localization of AI computing power.

From an industry perspective, mainland China continues to advance its self-reliant supply chain in high-performance computing chips, and inference GPUs — with their relatively lower barriers to adoption and fragmented application scenarios — have become the main breakthrough for local chipmakers entering the market in recent years. Denglin Technology's rapid revenue expansion reflects that its products are moving from the validation stage to volume shipments; the increase in customer count and the decline in concentration also mean its business model is gradually moving away from reliance on a few flagship customers.

For investors, the key takeaway from this data is not the single revenue figure but the quality of growth. Simultaneous improvement in shipment volumes and customer structure is usually more informative than a mere revenue jump; however, the company disclosed unaudited figures this time and did not publish gross margin, expenses or cash flow items, so actual profitability still awaits verification in full financial reports. In addition, competition in the GPGPU market is intense, with international giants and local peers all expanding capacity. Although customer concentration has improved, the top three customers still account for more than half of revenue, and the stability of future orders will be a key focus for tracking.

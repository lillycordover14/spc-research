UNC Charlotte’s 2026 Three Minute Thesis winners expertly convey complex research for everyday understanding

    November 18, 2026

Categories:

General News
 

                                        Winners of the 2026 Three Minute Thesis competition with Katherine Hall-Hertel, associate dean for graduate academic and student affairs, left, and Bill Tolone, associate provost and dean of the Graduate School, right: Kayla Lenz '22, Bobby Luker, Seamus Finerghty '26 and Samiha Jainab.
                                    

The three winners of UNC Charlotte’s 2026 Three Minute Thesis competition are master’s and doctoral students from differing disciplines, but with key attributes in common. First, as they represent the breadth of research and scholarship taking place across the University, they share a remarkable ability to translate complex ideas into compelling stories that resonate with audiences beyond their fields. And through the work of the labs they support across campus, each is participating in the development of new disease-detecting methods that potentially will result in quicker diagnoses and earlier treatments.

Held at more than 900 universities worldwide, 3MTⓇ  is an academic research communication competition developed in 2008 at the University of Queensland, Australia. Its goal is to challenge graduate students to summarize their research — free of jargon — for a general audience in under three minutes. UNC Charlotte has hosted a Three Minute Thesis competition since 2016, consistently spotlighting the innovation, creativity and impact of graduate student research. 

This year, 11 finalists emerged from the preliminary round of 74 entrants to UNC Charlotte’s annual competition. The winners, who made their culminating presentations in Fretwell Hall on Friday, Nov. 13, are: 

First place/$1,500: 
Kayla Lenz ’22
, Ph.D. candidate, mechanical engineering, for outlining how “the early bird catches the worm” related to possible new ways to apply the use of polymer nanomaterials — similar in idea to the materials used for rapid COVID-19 tests — to diagnose quickly, early and affordably medical conditions such as cancer, heart disease and Alzheimer’s disease. As the winner, Lenz will represent UNC Charlotte at the regional 3MTⓇ competition at the 2027 annual meeting of the Conference of Southern Graduate Schools in Arlington, Virginia.

Second place/$1,000: 
Samiha Jainab
, Ph.D. candidate, electrical engineering, for her descriptive presentation of the “second pair of eyes” that a trained AI model provides beyond a microscope for diagnosing breast cancer more accurately and efficiently, particularly in the case of remote populations where pathology expertise is limited.

Third place/$750: 
Bobby Luker
, master’s student, bioinformatics, for his explanation of the “genetic tug of war,” specifically identifying the genetic variants responsible when diagnosing oral cancers, and the use of computational tools to detect and treat the disease in early stages. 

The People’s Choice award went to 
Seamus Finerghty ’26
, a master’s student in kinesiology, for his presentation expressing whether gender and relative strength affect jump strategy based on the “dip, drop and leap” associated with strength training’s accentuated eccentric loading.

“It takes courage to do research, and these students have boldly stepped into a space where answers are unknown but where finding them is possible,” said Bill Tolone, associate provost and dean of UNC Charlotte’s 
Graduate School
. “Communicating clearly about research and its outcomes is essential, now and for the work these students will undertake in the future. As an R1 research university, UNC Charlotte takes seriously its responsibility to provide the opportunity for research students to effectively express the importance and impact of their processes and findings.”

The 3MTⓇ finalists included 
Weston Boudurant ’24

, master’s student, applied physics; 
Mille Elsborg

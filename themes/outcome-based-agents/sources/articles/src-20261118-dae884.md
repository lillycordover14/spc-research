Bitcoin
 traded lower on Wednesday despite a rally in the Nasdaq, where 
Nvidia
’s latest earnings results helped ease concerns about the technology sector.

The largest crypto asset was down 1.5% over the past day, trading near $64,000 at press time. 
Ethereum
 also declined, falling below $1,800, while 
Solana
 and 
XRP
 gained approximately 2% each and 
BNB
 was unchanged.

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures edged higher in early trading, while the VIX fell more than 4.5% from the prior session’s spike.

Nvidia shares rose about 2% after reporting better-than-expected 
quarterly results
 and stronger guidance, although uncertainty over its leadership succession, regulatory scrutiny, and tighter US chip-export restrictions kept gains in check.

The market outlook has faced pressure from renewed calls for higher interest rates. Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller 
told CNBC
 Wednesday that the central bank should consider a 50-basis-point rate increase at its December 8-9 meeting, arguing that inflation was proving more persistent than anticipated.

He said inflation was becoming more widespread, warning that policymakers might need to “shock people” to prevent price growth around 3% from becoming entrenched.

Waller said the central bank should first undo the three quarter-point cuts made in 2025, but indicated that a return to the previous rate level might not adequately address inflation. He said a quarter-point increase in December could be sufficient if the Fed also signals another hike in January. If officials are unwilling to provide that signal, he favors a larger half-point increase immediately.

Interest rate futures continue to reflect expectations of smaller increases. The most likely outcome, with a 95% probability, is a quarter-point increase to a 400-425 basis point range, according to the CME FedWatch tool.

The Fed delivered three rate cuts in 2025 as concerns about employment and expectations of easing inflation supported a less restrictive policy.

However, inflation has since accelerated, with higher energy prices following US and Israeli strikes against Iran in late February and the continued impact of tariffs keeping most inflation readings above the central bank’s 2% target.

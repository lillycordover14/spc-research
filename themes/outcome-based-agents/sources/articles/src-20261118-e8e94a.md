Breathing shouldn't depend on your zip code&nbsp;&nbsp;Turlock Journal

[page text]
Every breath matters. Yet for thousands of Californians living in rural communities, access to specialized lung care remains frustratingly out of reach.
Whether it is a farmworker exposed to years of dust and agricultural pollutants, a senior struggling with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), a child battling severe asthma, or a cancer patient experiencing respiratory complications, the need for pulmonology services in rural California has never been greater. Unfortunately, many communities have little or no access to a pulmonologist, forcing patients to travel hours for care or, worse, go without it entirely.
This is more than an inconvenience. It is a public health crisis.

Pulmonologists specialize in diagnosing and treating diseases of the lungs and respiratory system, including asthma, COPD, pulmonary fibrosis, sleep apnea, lung cancer, pneumonia, and long COVID. They are often the difference between a patient managing a chronic condition successfully and a patient experiencing repeated hospitalizations, declining health, and premature death.
In rural California, the need for these specialists is especially acute.
Many rural regions face higher rates of poverty, lower rates of health insurance coverage, increased environmental exposures, and older populations with complex medical needs. Agricultural communities are particularly vulnerable. Workers are routinely exposed to dust, pesticides, diesel emissions, and other airborne irritants that can contribute to chronic respiratory disease. Wildfire smoke, which has become a near-annual reality throughout California, only compounds these challenges.
Yet while the burden of respiratory disease continues to grow, access to specialty care has not kept pace.
For many rural residents, obtaining an appointment with a pulmonologist requires a trip to Sacramento, Fresno, San Francisco, or another major metropolitan area. That often means missing work, arranging transportation, securing childcare, and spending hours on the road. For individuals already struggling financially or coping with chronic illness, these barriers can be overwhelming.

The result is predictable. Patients delay care. Symptoms worsen. Emergency department visits increase. Hospital admissions rise. Costs escalate for families, healthcare systems, and taxpayers alike.
Bringing a pulmonologist to rural communities is not simply about convenience. It is about creating a healthcare system that focuses on prevention, early intervention, and disease management rather than crisis response.
Imagine a patient with COPD who receives specialized care before a severe exacerbation results in hospitalization. Imagine a patient with newly diagnosed lung cancer receiving prompt evaluation and treatment rather than waiting months for specialty care. Imagine agricultural workers obtaining screenings for occupational lung disease before symptoms become debilitating. These interventions save lives while reducing healthcare expenditures.
The economic case is compelling, but the human case is even stronger.
Behind every statistic is a person struggling to breathe. A grandfather who can no longer walk across the room without becoming short of breath. A mother who worries every time wildfire smoke fills the air because her child has severe asthma. A retired farmworker whose years of hard labor have left lasting damage to the lungs that once sustained a lifetime of work.

This is why the arrival of Dr. Anitha Channabasavaiah, MD, a nationally recognized and highly respected pulmonologist practicing at the new Community Health Centers of America clinic in Turlock, represents such a monumental step forward for our region. Legacy Health Endowment is deeply proud to support Dr. Anith’s vital work across Stanislaus and Merced Counties.
Rural residents deserve access to the same level of specialty care available in California's urban centers.
Most importantly, we must recognize that access to pulmonology is not a luxury. It is a necessity because every Californian deserves the chance to take a healthy breath, regardless of where they call home.
When you meet Dr. Anitha Channabasavaiah, you will quickly realize she is a gift to our communities. She has blessed my life and hopefully yours.
— Jeffrey Lewis is the President and CEO of Legacy Health Endowment and the EMC Health Foundation. The words expressed are his own.

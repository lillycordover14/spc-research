Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller again stressed the need for a so-called big step, or a 0.5-percentage-point increase in the benchmark interest rate.

BlockBeats reported on November 18 that Waller said the Fed should raise rates by 50 basis points in December if it does not signal a rate increase for January next year.

“Inflation is moving in the wrong direction, and the rise in prices is becoming more broad-based,” Waller said. He added that price increases exceeded 3% in about 70% of the items in the core services inflation basket. “This cannot be viewed simply as an oil-price issue,” he said.

Waller said he is concerned that companies could pass along price increases of more than 3% because they view consumers' spending power as resilient. He added that inflation is heading toward an annualized 3.5%.

The Fed needs to deliver a “shock” so markets do not come to accept 3% inflation as tolerable, he said, adding that policymakers should take a more aggressive approach to rate increases.

What I think you should know about Zip
Zip is one of the only BNPL apps I review that offers three biweekly payment options, giving you more flexibility in your payment amount and timeline without having to opt into monthly financing.
But fees keep Zip from being a highly rated lender. While plenty of apps I review charge fees, most are avoidable, either by repaying your loan on time or by shopping only at partner retailers.
Zip is different, because it may charge an automatic origination fee, and there aren’t any steps you can take to avoid it, which makes its BNPL loans costlier than other popular options on the market. For that reason, I’d recommend shopping with other BNPL apps before Zip.

What is Zip?
Zip is a “buy now, pay later” provider. It offers three payment plans to break up the cost of your purchase, so you pay over a series of installments, instead of all at once.
You can download the Zip mobile app to get started or check out with Zip when shopping at a partner store.

How does Zip work?
Zip works by dividing the total cost of your purchase into smaller installments when you check out with Zip online, in stores or in the Zip mobile app. There may be three payment plans to choose from: Zip Pay in 2, Pay in 4 and Pay in 8.
Payments are automatically billed to the debit or credit card you used to make the original payment. Though you can make payments manually or early, you can’t turn off automatic payments, so it’s important to pay attention to your due date and ensure you have enough funds available.
Zip will likely charge an automatic origination fee that can vary widely based on the details of your purchase. Other fees include a late fee if you miss a payment and a payment rescheduling fee if you reschedule more than once per calendar month. You can read more about these fees below.

Is Zip legit?
Zip is a reputable provider of BNPL loans. But with any BNPL app, there’s a risk of overspending, since the smaller installments can make the purchase seem more affordable than it is. Plus, if you fall behind on payments, you’ll accumulate fees.
Here are the pros and cons of Zip.
Multiple biweekly payment options: 
If you prefer a biweekly payment structure, meaning payments due every two weeks, Zip is hard to beat. It’s one of only two providers I review (Sezzle being the other) that offers three biweekly options, and no other provider offers a Pay in 8. Eight installments may be just enough time to pay off a larger purchase without needing to apply for a traditional credit product, like a loan.
Free payment rescheduling: 
While some BNPL lenders won’t let you change your payment due date, Zip lets you reschedule one payment for free each calendar month. Additional payment changes made within the same month include a $2 fee, which is a lower rescheduling fee compared to other providers. This flexibility can help users avoid a fee altogether or pay a smaller fee overall — for example, a $2 rescheduling versus a $7 late fee.
Pauses account after missed payment: 
If you miss a payment, Zip automatically pauses your account and won't approve you for additional orders until your missed payment is complete. This can prevent you from incurring more debt. Once you catch up, Zip reactivates your account.
Cons of using Zip
Charges origination fee: 
Zip’s origination fee is essentially interest, since it’s added automatically to the total loan amount. According to Zip's terms of service, this fee ranges from $0 to $140 and will be included in your first installment. Almost none of the other BNPL providers I review charge this type of fee — with the exception of Sezzle, which may charge an automatic service fee — and it makes your purchase more expensive than if you hadn’t used BNPL.
Charges late fee: 
In addition to automatically pausing your account, Zip charges a late fee of $7 if you miss your payment’s due date. Some BNPL providers don’t charge any fees, even when you fall behind on payments.
No monthly financing: 
Many BNPL providers offer long-term plans to choose from at checkout, with some terms stretching as long as five years. Monthly plans can help finance a particularly large purchase via smaller installments and a longer repayment period. Monthly plans usually come with interest, though, and aren’t the right fit for everyone.
How to get approved for Zip
To be eligible for Zip, you need to be at least 18 years old, live in the U.S. and provide a valid mobile phone number, debit card and mailing address.
Zip says it takes into account numerous factors when making an approval decision, including your credit report and any previous Zip repayment history.
Zip isn’t available in New Mexico and Vermont.
🤓
 Nerdy Tip
One of the best ways to get approved for a BNPL loan is to show a history of on-time payments with that provider. Consider using BNPL to make a small purchase first, then pay off your loan on time or early. This may help you get approved for a slightly larger purchase in the future.
Does Zip check credit?
Zip checks your credit with a soft credit pull when you sign up for its service. This won’t hurt your credit score.
Though there’s no minimum requirement, Zip considers your credit score as part of your application.
Zip vs. Affirm vs. Klarna
Zip and Affirm both offer a classic Pay in 4 plan, but Affirm promises no interest and no fees, making it the more affordable option. Affirm also offers the longest terms of any BNPL lender I review, stretching up to five years at some stores.
Klarna also offers a no-interest Pay in 4 and no fees if you pay on time and shop at a partner retailer. It even offers the ability to earn cashback rewards by shopping exclusively in the Klarna mobile app.

Alternatives to Zip
0% APR credit cards
If you have good or excellent credit (a 690 credit score or higher), you may consider applying for a 0% APR credit card. These cards offer introductory periods up to 21 months with no interest, meaning you can buy something with credit and then pay it off at no additional cost.
Other perks include a potential sign-up bonus or access to a rewards program. And since your payment history is always reported to the credit bureaus, it’s a more reliable way to build credit than BNPL.
Just make sure you pay off the purchase before the introductory period ends or you’ll owe interest on the remaining balance.

Small personal loans
If you’re looking to fund a larger purchase, you can apply for a personal loan. Small personal loans start at $1,000 or less, and there are options for borrowers with fair or bad credit (a 689 credit score or lower).

Frequently asked questions about Zip
What are the disadvantages of Zip?
Like all BNPL providers, Zip may cause you to overspend, since you’re not paying the total cost of your purchase at checkout. Zip also charges fees, including an origination fee, which increases the cost of your purchase.
How long does Zip take to approve?
Zip’s approval decisions are instantaneous, meaning you’ll know immediately whether you’ve been approved or denied. Zip doesn’t approve everyone, and each loan you apply for is an individual approval decision. This means you may be approved for one purchase but not another.
What is the credit limit for Zip?
Though there’s no exact credit limit for Zip, you’ll receive an initial “spending power” limit when you sign up. If you pay off five consecutive orders on time or early, your limit should increase by $25 to $100. 
Does Zip hurt your credit?
Zip doesn’t conduct a hard credit check, so applying for Zip won’t hurt your score. Zip also doesn’t report payment history to the credit bureaus, so you won’t see Zip loans reflected in your credit score. But if you become significantly delinquent and your account is sent to collections, that could hurt your score.
How do I contact Zip customer service?
You can reach out to Zip via live chat in the mobile app, by submitting an online request form or by calling 888-274-3159. Phone support is available seven days a week, from 9 AM to 9 PM EST.

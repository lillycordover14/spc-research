Monthly contributions to the National Social Security Fund (NSSF) have increased sixfold since 2023, rising from Ksh.1.4 billion to more than Ksh.8.5 billion, President William Ruto has announced.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Speaking during his State of the Nation Address to Parliament on Wednesday, Ruto said the growth in contributions had pushed the Fund's value from Ksh.288 billion in 2022 to Ksh.850 billion as of November 2026.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The President said the Fund was now on course to reach the Ksh.1 trillion mark by June 2027, attributing the growth to increased contributions from employees and their employers.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
"Since 2023, monthly NSSF contributions have increased sixfold, from Ksh.1.4 billion to over Ksh.8.5 billion. For the 2024/2025 financial year, members earned interest of 17 per cent on their savings," Ruto told Parliament.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Ruto said the government's efforts to strengthen the Fund were aimed at securing dignified retirement for workers while building a savings culture that could support national development.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
He also highlighted improvements in the processing of NSSF benefits, saying digitisation had reduced the time taken to process claims from 82 days to seven days.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
He added that the government was targeting a further reduction, with claims expected to be processed within 24 hours.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
"Over the past three years, the Fund has paid Ksh.40.1 billion to approximately 400,000 beneficiaries," he said.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The President linked the growth of domestic savings to the government's broader ambition of transforming Kenya into a first-world economy within a generation, arguing that the scale of investment required could not be financed through ordinary government expenditure alone.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
“Our transformation programme is estimated to require Ksh.5 trillion over the next 10 years. We must therefore mobilise longterm capital in ways that protect debt sustainability, safeguard public resources and avoid placing an unsustainable burden on taxpayers,” he said.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
“An important foundation for this ambition is the growth of our national savings. Article 43 of our Constitution guarantees every Kenyan the right to social security.”
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The Head of State explained that growing national savings would provide an important source of domestic financing for infrastructure development and productive enterprises.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
"Our ambition is a nation that saves more, invests more and creates more opportunities for its people," he said.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
“By investing workers’ savings prudently and productively, we can secure their future while financing the infrastructure and enterprises that build a more productive country.”

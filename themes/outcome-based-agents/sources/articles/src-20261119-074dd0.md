The Tennessee Vols host former head coach Lane Kiffin and his LSU Tigers on Saturday afternoon at Neyland Stadium.  It’s certainly a game that has far different meanings for both programs. 

Tennessee, considered by many fans and pundits to be a target of disrespect by the CFP selection committee, can make one last big statement with a win before their regular season finale at Vanderbilt.  LSU is basically playing out the string after losing to Texas 22-21 at the last second to pick up loss number three and fall out of the playoff picture. 

Will Tennessee get that win and continue to pressure the committee?  Or will Kiffin get one small victory in what’s been a lost season in his first year in Baton Rouge?  Our college football writers weighed in with their game predictions. 

Tennessee 33, LSU 27

I think it was common thought before the season that this game could be for a playoff spot between two of the better teams in the SEC. But only part of that remains true. LSU’s playoff dreams went up in smoke last week after the Tigers lost to Texas. As for Tennessee, they are playing every game as if it’s a playoff game. The margin for error is razor-thin, and losing to an unranked, 6-4 Tigers would likely seal their fate in the 2026 season.

That’s why I’m taking the Vols. Lane Kiffin continues to stir the pot when it comes to the Vols, and Neyland Stadium will be rocking come Saturday afternoon. Tennessee is the better team head to toe, and I expect that to show. The Vols have one of the best defenses in the country, and Sam Leavitt is prone to interceptions. Give me Tennessee.

Rob Gregson
, 
A to Z Sports Alabama

Tennessee 26, LSU 23

Tennessee comes in needing one last big home win to put themselves on the doorstep of a second CFP playoff appearance in three years.  LSU comes in with basically nothing to play for except playing out the string and for the love of the game (if that’s even still a thing in today’s NIL/transfer portal world).

And still, I think LSU has the athletes to give Tennessee all they can handle in this game.  LSU’s top WR Jackson Harris is listed as questionable, as is number two wideout Winston Watkins, Jr.  If both play, I think LSU can challenge a good Tennessee defense that’s only allowed four passing touchdowns all season.  And if I had to guess, I think they do play. 

But Tennessee’s ground game, even without starting OL Nic Moore, will continue to find its way in the second half as it’s done all year, and a crucial late game touchdown for heavily used DeSean Bishop will be enough to come away with an enormous win to keep the Vols’ playoff hopes alive.

Craig Smith
, 
A to Z Sports Tennessee

Tennessee 27, LSU 23

This should be a playoff-type environment in Knoxville between LSU and the Vols. It’s a win-and-in type of situation for Tennessee when it comes to the College Football Playoff, while a loss would likely knock them out. LSU already has four losses and is reeling after back-to-back defeats to Alabama and Texas. 

These are two teams that are strong defensively, with LSU having the better offense, especially with Mike Matthews’ injury for Tennessee. Still, I think the Vols are going to protect home field and come out on top. Tennessee runs the ball well, and Sam Leavitt turns the ball over twice. That’ll be the difference.

Brandon Little
, 
A to Z Sports Ohio State

Tennessee 24, LSU 20

I was leaning toward LSU coming into this matchup, but the uncertainty surrounding Sam Leavitt has changed my thinking. I’m going with Tennessee to pull out a tight win. The Vols have a tremendous defense, and I think that unit is capable of making life difficult for the LSU offense throughout the afternoon. Tennessee should be able to create pressure, disrupt the rhythm of the opposing passing game and force LSU into some uncomfortable situations. In a game where points could be difficult to come by, those defensive stops will be especially important.

Ultimately, I think the difference comes down to turnovers. Tennessee’s defense forces multiple takeaways, giving the Vols extra possessions and opportunities to capitalize on LSU mistakes. I don’t necessarily expect Tennessee to dominate offensively, but I trust its defense to set the tone and make enough game-changing plays to swing a close contest. The questions surrounding Leavitt make it difficult for me to pick LSU with confidence, especially against a defense this talented. Give me the Vols to make the most of those opportunities and escape with a hard-fought road victory.

Ryan Roberts
, 
A to Z Sports Notre Dame

Tennessee 45, LSU 21

The College Football Playoff committee has been disrespecting the Volunteers since the initial rankings came out. There’s already enough motivation with the most hated man in Knoxville coming to town in Lane Kiffin, but not being ranked high enough will be the extra step to show the committee how bad they are at rankings.

Tyler Forness
, 
A to Z Sports Florida

LSU 21, Tennessee 17

The Tennessee Vols have to figure out their passing game. They do have an elite rushing attack, but that won’t always work, especially in the SEC. LSU has the more balanced offense and is the team to beat Tennessee, given what the game plan should be for both teams. For LSU, keep the out-of-phase; make them pass the ball in third-and-long situations. For Tennessee, they’ve got to put pressure on the pocket and make LSU make tough decisions. 

Justin Churchill, A to Z Sports Oklahoma

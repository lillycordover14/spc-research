Bluespring Surpasses Last Year's Acquisition Haul With $1.6B Kestra Affiliate
With its latest deal, Kestra’s Bluespring Wealth has added about $7.2 billion in assets so far this year and further proves the popularity of IBD to RIA moves.

Summarize this article
Bluespring Wealth, the Austin, Texas-based hybrid registered investment advisor arm of independent broker/dealer Kestra Holdings, has acquired the wealth management business of Kestra affiliate Dynasty Advisors, a Freehold, N.J.-based firm overseeing about $1.6 billion in assets.
The deal pushes Bluespring’s total acquired assets, albeit some from affiliate Kestra firms, to $7.2 billion year to date. That pushes it above 
last year’s $6 billion in added assets for the acquisitive RIA
. The deal is Bluespring’s ninth so far this year, the same figure of add-ons it had in 2025, while showing the continued popularity of the RIA channel for established broker/dealer affiliate teams.
“As we look to the future, Bluespring gives us the opportunity to continue advancing while staying true to the culture and personal approach that shaped our practice,” Ronny Lomangino, managing director and co-founder of Dynasty, said in a statement.

Dynasty has offices in Freehold and Clark, N.J., and in Montgomery, Bucks, and Chester counties, Pa., with practice areas including wealth management, employer-sponsored retirement plans, and group benefits.
The 20-person firm was founded and owned by Lomangino, William “Bill” Grundig, and William “Bud” James. The team had been with LPL Financial’s Commonwealth Financial Network for eight years until 2025, when they 
moved to Kestra in August
 after LPL’s acquisition was announced.
Dynasty Advisors also has what it calls a “Gen2” initiative, which looks to prepare its next generation of advisors to step into leadership roles with the founding partners. That type of forward succession planning often helps raise sale valuations for teams, as M&A dealmakers and analysts 
often remind the marketplace
.
Bluespring is among the handful of active, private equity-controlled wealth managers competing both for acquisitions and new client engines to drive organic growth. Private equity firm Stone Point Capital is the majority owner of the holding company Kestra, and Oak Hill Capital also owns a minority stake in the firm.
According to Marshberry's tracking of publicly announced wealth management deals released Thursday, there have been 326 transactions through October, a 3.5% increase over the same period in 2025.
The top three acquirers so far this year have been Wealth Enhancement, Hightower Advisors and Carson Group, according to Marshberry.

Wolverine Construction Highlights Key Risks in Adaptive Reuse Through Grand Rapids Childcare Project
Construction Owners Editorial Team
Case Studies
Michigan
https://construction-owner-club.webflow.io/news/
wolverine-construction-highlights-key-risks-in-adaptive-reuse-through-grand-rapids-childcare-project
Highlights
Wolverine Construction details the challenges of converting existing buildings for new uses through the YMCA Early Childhood Center project in Grand Rapids, Michigan.
The 26,500-square-foot facility occupies three floors of Rapid Central Station, an operating public transit hub.
The conversion required extensive fireproofing, safety upgrades and coordination with ongoing bus operations.
Early physical investigations helped identify structural, utility and fire-safety requirements before construction advanced.
The project includes a rooftop playground, an elevator addition and preservation of elements associated with the building's LEED certification.
Converting existing buildings for new uses can introduce substantial cost, code compliance and construction coordination challenges. Wolverine Construction’s work on the YMCA Early Childhood Center at Rapid Central Station in Grand Rapids demonstrates how early investigation and contractor involvement during design can help manage those risks.
Existing Building Conditions Shape Project Costs
The 26,500-square-foot childcare facility spans three floors of an existing bus station. The change from transit-related use to childcare triggered building code reviews and broader fire-safety requirements. These included fireproofing across the entire building rather than only the floors occupied by the new centre, expanding the scope beyond the initial fire alarm work.
Wolverine also identified the importance of inspecting existing conditions before finalising construction budgets. Reviewing historical drawings alone can leave uncertainty about structural connections, utility routes and fire separation assemblies. Selective openings and material testing provided physical evidence to help engineers define the required work before interior framing began.
Construction Within an Operating Transit Facility
The project required construction activities to be coordinated around Rapid Central Station’s daily bus operations, which run from 6 a.m. to midnight.
Fireproofing work above active transit areas required air purification measures to protect occupants and operations. The project also involved removing and replacing structural glass above the main public entrance while maintaining protection for server rooms, maintenance areas and existing equipment.
Wolverine coordinated with transit station staff on access restrictions, shutdowns and construction activities to limit disruption to the operating facility.
Design Solutions and Building Preservation
The new childcare centre incorporates features shaped by the existing station’s physical constraints. A rooftop playground provides outdoor space where ground-level green space is unavailable. Existing rooftop plantings associated with the building’s LEED certification were preserved and relocated rather than removed.
Other project elements include an elevator addition and enhanced safety features. The work was delivered through construction management, with coordination among the owner, design team and construction professionals throughout planning and execution.
Implications for Adaptive Reuse Projects
The project illustrates the value of involving contractors during preconstruction, particularly when a building remains occupied or operational. Early investigation can help identify code triggers, hidden conditions and construction constraints before they affect budgets and schedules.
For owners and developers considering adaptive reuse, the Grand Rapids project highlights the importance of selective demolition, early code reviews and communication with building users. These measures can help turn existing structures into functional new spaces while managing the risks associated with renovation and change of use.

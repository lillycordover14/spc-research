Abstract
Despite these developments, image enhancement remains a challenging task, because it requires effective noise removal while preserving edges, textures, and fine structural details. Existing diffusion-based techniques may cause excessive smoothing, which can result in the loss of important image information. To address this issue, the present study proposes an image enhancement framework that integrates the Caputo fractional derivative, Hausdorff Fractal Derivative (HFD), and Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) within the Perona–Malik anisotropic diffusion model. A fidelity term is incorporated to maintain structural consistency and improve the reconstruction accuracy during the enhancement process. The mathematical properties of the proposed model are established through convergence and stability analyses. The proposed framework is evaluated using two-dimensional and three-dimensional benchmark datasets, as well as medical images corrupted by Gaussian noise. A parameter sensitivity analysis is performed to identify suitable fractional and fractal orders. The results show that parameter values within the range (0, 1) enhance the image while effectively preserving important structural details. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves better quantitative and visual performance than the existing image enhancement methods, with higher Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) values and reasonable computational time. Under Gaussian noise, the proposed method demonstrates improved performance compared with deep learning-based methods, including DnCNN, SU-Net, and RED-CNN. To further assess its generalization capability, the proposed framework is evaluated under Gaussian, salt-and-pepper, speckle, Poisson, and mixed noise conditions. The results show that the proposed method provides effective enhancement under different noise conditions, although its performance varies with the type of noise. Multiple realizations of Gaussian noise, together with statistical analysis based on the mean and standard deviation, further confirm the consistency and reliability of the proposed method. In addition, ROI-based comparative analysis demonstrates improved preservation of fine structures and important image details. Overall, the integration of fractional-order diffusion, fractal-based feature preservation, and multiscale wavelet analysis provides an effective and versatile framework for image enhancement.

Introduction
Image enhancement is a fundamental operation in image processing and plays an important role in computer vision and image analysis. It is widely used in applications such as low-light image processing, remote sensing, and medical imaging. The main objective of image enhancement is to improve image quality by reducing unwanted noise while preserving important structural and textural details. It also helps improve contrast and make relevant features more visible for better interpretation and analysis. However, some enhancement techniques may unintentionally amplify noise or distort important image details. Therefore, choosing an appropriate enhancement method is important to improve image quality while preserving the structural information and relevant features of the image.

Research motivation
Fractional-order differential equations have attracted considerable attention in image processing in recent years because of their ability to model complex diffusion processes. Fractional-order image processing has been developed using several definitions of fractional derivatives, including the Riemann–Liouville (RL), Grünwald–Letnikov (GL), Riesz, and Caputo derivatives, each of which has distinct mathematical and computational characteristics. The Riemann–Liouville (RL) fractional derivative has been applied to image enhancement and edge sharpening through fractional-order operators

In the proposed work, a time-dependent anisotropic diffusion model is considered, for which the Caputo fractional derivative is particularly suitable. The Caputo derivative naturally incorporates the memory effects of time while allowing the use of classical initial conditions expressed in terms of integer-order derivatives. These properties make it suitable for the formulation and numerical solution of the proposed model. Recent studies on Caputo-based image denoising models have also demonstrated their ability to improve denoising performance while preserving fine image structures

Therefore, the Caputo fractional derivative is adopted in the proposed framework to provide a suitable balance between temporal memory modeling, mathematical formulation, and numerical implementation. It is further integrated with DWT and HFD to improve edge preservation, texture representation, and medical image denoising performance.

Proposed methodology
In this work, a new Wavelet–Hausdorff Anisotropic Diffusion (WHAD) model is presented. It is designed to enhance the denoising process in medical images, while preserving the fine anatomical details. The method considers the irregular and non-local features of images by using DWT’s multiscale localization and HFD’s to capture the image features. These two mechanisms are combined in the WHAD model in an anisotropic diffusion space. Therefore, the method preserves the edges better, enhances the texture more clearly, and reduces the noise more effectively than traditional diffusion-based methods.

Parameter sensitivity analysis
The experimental results indicate that the denoising performance is affected by the interaction between the two fractional-order parameters. In conclusion, an improvement in the PSNR and SSIM is observed for almost all values of α as γ increases from γ = 0.1 to γ = 0.5. Specifically, the PSNR improved from 24 to 40 dB, and the SSIM enhanced from 0.9792 to 0.9991, thus demonstrating the capability of the Caputo fractional derivative to suppress noise while maintaining the important structures of the image, owing to its memory property. The parameter combination (γ, α) = (0.5, 0.5) has the best restoration effect, and the obtained PSNR is 39.9927 dB, and the SSIM is 0.9991.
As the fractional order of the Caputo derivative increases from γ > 0.5, the restoration quality decreases slowly for all values of α investigated. This behavior suggests that a strong fractional memory makes the diffusion more effective and results in over-smoothing of fine image details, thus degrading the objective quality measures. The Caputo fractional order provides a balance between noise suppression and preservation of structural details.
The PSNR and SSIM values changed slightly with different values of α for the same value of γ. The difference is mostly small over the space of the parameters investigated, which means that the proposed framework is stable with respect to the choice of α. This behavior indicates that the spatial properties of the diffusion process are mostly controlled by the HFD through the provision of local regularization and maintenance of the structural consistency, whereas the strength of the denoising is mostly controlled by the Caputo fractional order.
In addition to γ and α, the remaining model parameters were specified and fixed throughout the experiments. The time-step size was set to Δt=0.1, as preliminary experiments showed comparatively lower restoration performance for smaller time steps. The fidelity parameter, diffusion threshold, and number of iterations were fixed at λ=0.02, ρ=0.02, and N=10, respectively. These settings were kept unchanged so that the sensitivity analysis specifically focused on the influence of fractional-order parameters γ and α.
The parameter sensitivity analysis confirmed the robustness of the proposed framework. The PSNR and SSIM heat maps in Fig.

Based on the overall restoration performance and numerical stability, γ = 0.5 and α = 0.5 were selected for all subsequent experiments, as shown in Fig.

Conclusion
This study developed an image enhancement framework by integrating the Caputo fractional derivative, HFD, and DWT within an anisotropic diffusion model. The framework effectively suppresses noise while preserving edges, textures, and fine structural details. The fidelity term improves structural consistency and reconstruction accuracy, while the convergence and stability analyses support the mathematical validity of the model. Parameter sensitivity analysis showed satisfactory performance for fractional and fractal orders within (0, 1). Experimental results demonstrated improved PSNR and SSIM values, better visual quality, and a favourable balance between restoration performance and computational efficiency compared with the selected existing methods. Under Gaussian noise, the proposed method also outperformed deep learning-based methods, including DnCNN, SU-Net, and RED-CNN. The framework was further evaluated under Gaussian, Salt-and-Pepper, Speckle, Poisson, and mixed noise conditions, consistently demonstrating effective performance across different degradation models. Multiple Gaussian noise realizations and statistical analyses based on the mean and standard deviation confirmed the consistency of the enhancement process, while ROI-based comparative analysis showed improved preservation of fine structures and important image details. However, the higher computational time observed for the Brain Y-slice dataset indicates a limitation of the current framework. Future work will aim to reduce the computational cost through GPU acceleration, parallel processing, and optimization of the iteration strategy. Overall, the experimental and mathematical analyses demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework for medical image enhancement and image restoration, with potential for use as a preprocessing step in computer vision and deep learning applications.

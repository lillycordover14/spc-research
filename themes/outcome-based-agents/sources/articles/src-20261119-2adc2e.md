The Kyle City Council tabled a decision on whether to convey city-owned property to the local VFW post after being unable to reach a majority on two motions. (Michael Milliorn/Community Impact)
Following two hours of public comments and a lengthy discussion, the Kyle City Council tabled an item to convey the property currently occupied by the Veterans of Foreign Wars, or 
VFW, Post 12058
 to the VFW on Nov. 17. 
The council could not reach a majority decision to convey the property or provide the VFW with a long-term lease. The conveyance motion failed because two council members had concerns about the VFW’s ability to support itself financially, while the motion to approve a long-term lease failed because the council members who voted for conveyance wanted to honor the VFW representative's request.
The overview
The VFW currently occupies the property at 107 Veterans Drive, Kyle, which the city owns and leases to the organization. The lease limits the VFW’s ability to make permanent improvements without city consent and renews annually unless terminated, 
according to agenda documents
.
The VFW is not required to pay rent to the city for the use of the building. The organization maintains the property and repairs any damage caused by its use. The city covers utility costs and provides property insurance coverage for the building.
Under the Local Government Code, the city could convey the property to the VFW as long as the organization uses the facility for identified public purposes, such as the provision of food and housing services for the less fortunate, according to agenda documents.
The VFW currently uses the building to provide services including:
A cooling and warming center
Disaster assistance
Community food bank and closet
Red Cross partnership
Sponsorship of two Scouting America Troops and a Cub Scout Pack
The details
The VFW requested a property conveyance with a reversionary clause that would return the property to city ownership if it is no longer used for a qualified public purpose, according to agenda documents.
The organization currently faces difficulties making property improvements and applying for national grants because of the uncertain nature of the current lease. If the VFW were granted ownership, it would take on costs the city currently pays.
“Because the current lease is uncertain, the VFW will have difficulty making improvements and applying for grants,” said VFW Post 12058 representative Steven Cook. “A draft resolution already finds that our long-term occupancy is in the best interest of the residents of the city.”
The VFW currently holds supplemental property insurance for the building and has funded several repairs, including improvements to flooring, a wheelchair ramp, bathrooms and rear access to the building, according to agenda documents. 
“We are deeply grateful to the city of Kyle,” Cook said. “You brought us here, you stood with us and you have kept faith with veterans and with this community. We are not asking for something new. We are asking you to make the commitment permanent.” 
The discussion
The council failed to pass two motions, one to convey the property to the VFW and one to offer the VFW a long-term lease. Council members Melissa Medina and Michael Tobias were absent for the vote, so the council needed four votes to pass a motion.
Without four votes in favor of either motion, the council voted to table the item until the Dec. 1 meeting.
“We may not have received the outcome we hoped for, but we will never forget those who stood beside us,” said the VFW in a 
social media post
. “Our mission continues, our doors remain open and our commitment to serving this community will never waver.”

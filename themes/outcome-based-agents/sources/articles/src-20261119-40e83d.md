For as long as Sidney Crosby has been in the league, the topic of him returning to Montreal to play for the Canadiens, a team who drafted his father back in 1984, has seemingly been nothing more than a dream. However, based on recent comments by the Penguins' general manager it might be the closest to a reality it has ever been.
The Penguins right now are struggling with only 16 points in 20 games and the chances they can return to the playoffs are quickly diminishing and even if they do make it, they are very unlikely to win a Stanley Cup. It puts a franchise in a tough situation with such an old roster that moving some of these players may be the best thing not only for the team but also players towards the end of their career.
On Wednesday, general manager 
Kyle Dubas met with the media
 and made it clear that the Penguins were going to follow the lead of players like Sidney Crosby, Evgeni Malkin, and Kris Letang. If they ask to be traded, the Penguins will do the best they can to accommodate that.
"I think with the three of them, it's such a unique circumstance in terms of all that they've done for the organization and for the team. I think that because of that, they have to be handled differently than you would handle any other veteran that you came into contact with. I don't know there'll ever be a circumstance like that in the league again," Dubas said.
He went on to speak about Crosby specifically and said, "I think with Sid's case, he's as unique as it gets, and I would just go back to my view on it from the opening of the season is that we want to get into this as far as we can, have an indication about where we're at, where we're going and make sure everyone's comfortable with that. He's owed all the respect in the world. If he wants to be here, that's great and obviously what we would love as well but at the same time there at an age where it's not given that they're always going to continue to play either."
There are not many players in the NHL who have a career as long and as productive as Sidney Crosby. He was the first overall pick in the 2005 NHL Draft, is in his 22nd season and has won two Stanley Cups and two Hart Trophies. However, what might be even more impressive is that he has done it all with one franchise.
Montreal Canadiens might give Sidney Crosby the best chance at one more Stanley Cup
The question for Crosby will be whether he wants to finish his career playing for only the Pittsburgh Penguins or if he wants 
one more chance at a Stanley Cup.
 While the Canadiens can't guarantee that will happen, there is no question that Montreal is much closer than Pittsburgh right now.
It also would give Crosby a chance to be surrounded by a young core that is still improving, if he wanted to continue his career past this season.
While Dubas made it clear that the decision will ultimately be up to the veterans, general manager 
Kent Hughes and the Canadiens
 can certainly help their case. This would be a perfect chance for Hughes to make an offer that is enticing enough to Dubas that he lets Crosby know that Montreal wants to add him.
If Crosby ultimately decides that he wants to play for only one franchise for his entire NHL career, then he has certainly earned that right. However,  the childhood connections and the chance at a third Stanley Cup could be just enough motivation for the Canadiens to finally bring in Sidney Crosby before he calls it a career.

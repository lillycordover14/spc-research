Zendesk CX Trends 2027: Ready to Manage an AI Workforce?
 

 

Enterprise Software & Digital Workflows
 

 

Analyst(s): Keith Kirkpatrick

Publication Date: November 19, 2026

Zendesk’s CX Trends 2027 report finds that 74% of business leaders surveyed expect AI agents to outnumber people in service interactions within two years. The same report shows that few companies have the management, pricing, and identity controls needed to run that many AI agents.

What Is Covered in This Article:

Key findings from Zendesk CX Trends 2027, including the 74% of leaders who expect AI agents to outnumber people in service interactions within two years.

Futurum data showing that enterprises are deploying AI agents in internal functions before customer-facing ones.

The gap between buyer demand for outcome-based AI pricing and what vendors offer today.

The lack of a way to verify AI agents that act on behalf of customers.

Why companies need to redesign service processes to manage AI agents well.

The News:
 On November 17, 2026, Zendesk released Zendesk CX Trends 2027, its annual customer experience report. Zendesk says companies are entering “the era of AI Management,” in which they manage AI as a workforce instead of deploying it as a tool. According to the report, 74% of business leaders surveyed expect AI agents to outnumber people in service interactions within two years, 81% already consider AI agents part of their workforce, and 87% say managing an AI workforce will be as important as managing human teams. Also, 90% say success depends on orchestration that connects agents, data, and systems, and 85% say AI vendors should price on verified outcomes instead of interactions processed. Zendesk ran two surveys in July and August 2026 across 10 countries. They covered more than 4,400 business respondents, from small businesses to enterprises, and more than 3,100 consumers.

Zendesk CX Trends 2027: Ready to Manage an AI Workforce?

Analyst Take:
 The 74% figure from Zendesk CX Trends 2027 will get most of the attention, but it tells buyers little. It is a forecast from a vendor-run survey of leaders who already use AI in service, and it counts interactions instead of measuring whether customers got their issues fixed. Other findings in the report are more useful because they show what companies still lack. Only 11% match AI capability to task complexity today. Also, 78% say coordinating several specialized agents on one customer issue is their biggest operational challenge, and 78% say they have no way to verify the identity of AI agents acting for customers.

Companies are adding AI agents faster than they are building the systems to manage them. Zendesk CX Trends 2027 shows gaps in orchestration, measurement, pricing, and trust, and I expect those gaps to slow adoption more than any limit in the AI models.

Enterprises Are Deploying AI Agents Internally First

Zendesk surveyed people who run service operations, so it makes sense that they expect AI agents to take over service work quickly. Enterprise buyers as a whole are putting agents somewhere else first. In Futurum’s 2H 2026 Enterprise Applications Decision Maker Survey (N=833, published August 2026), IT operations and observability (21.6%) and cybersecurity (16.4%) were the top first-ranked areas for agentic AI deployment. Sales, marketing, and service (12.8%) and customer engagement (10.4%) ranked lower. Enterprises are testing agents in internal functions, where mistakes are easier to catch and contain.

Zendesk’s forecast may still hold for digital-first brands and for the small and midsize businesses in its sample. These companies handle many simple, repetitive requests that AI agents can resolve today. The two-year timeline is less likely for complex issues that span several systems, and those issues cost the most to handle. Zendesk itself has forecast that AI agents will handle more interactions than humans in 2026. The 74% figure confirms a trend among early adopters; it does not show that the broader market has reached that point.

Zendesk CX Trends 2027 Shows Strong Demand for Outcome Pricing

In the report, 85% of leaders say AI vendors should price on verified outcomes. Zendesk introduced outcome-based pricing for AI agents in 2024 and expanded it with its Resolution Platform at Relate 2026, so the finding supports its own strategy. Buyers say different things when they have to choose a model. In Futurum’s 2H 2026 survey, among buyers who pay for generative AI as an add-on (n=645), 21.1% prefer outcome-based pricing and 36.6% prefer consumption-based pricing. Only 11.2% of those buyers say their preferred vendor offers outcome-based pricing today, and outcome-based pricing shows up in 8.6% of vendor pricing models overall.

Buyers want pricing tied to results, and few vendors offer it. That helps Zendesk and other vendors that already charge per resolution. Verification is the obstacle. Buyers and vendors have not agreed on what counts as a resolution, how to treat a resolution that leads to a repeat contact, or who checks the numbers. The report also finds that 86% of leaders want real-time visibility into cost per resolution. Buyers want to see the numbers first. Outcome pricing will spread only when both sides trust how resolutions are counted.

Companies Cannot Yet Verify AI Agents That Act for Customers

In Zendesk CX Trends 2027, 78% of business leaders say they have no solution for verifying the identity of AI agents acting for customers. I expect this to cause the most problems over the next 24 months. Meanwhile, 48% of consumers say they want to resolve service issues through a personal AI assistant without contacting the company directly, and 77% of leaders worry that AI systems will skip their brand if those systems cannot access the brand’s content.

Zendesk
 addresses part of this problem with its 
Open Service Directory
, which helps AI assistants find a brand’s verified support channel. That helps the assistant find the right company. It does not tell the company whether an incoming agent has the customer’s permission to change an account, issue a refund, or access personal data. Consumers also give mixed answers: 71% would accept more friction for stronger fraud protection, but 75% are uncomfortable with behavioral analysis used for verification without their consent. Until standards for agent identity and permissions mature, I expect enterprises to limit what customer-side agents can do. Those limits will slow the growth in AI-handled interactions that the 74% forecast assumes.

Managing AI Agents Requires Process Redesign

Zendesk CX Trends 2027 is right that managing AI agents will become a core part of running service operations. In the report, 84% of leaders say AI workflow design is becoming a core skill for CX operations roles, and 64% expect to match AI capability to task complexity within 12 to 24 months, compared with 11% today. Companies will not close that gap by adding more agents to existing processes. As I wrote in Futurum’s research on why 
agentic AI’s real test is process redesign
, companies get results when they rebuild workflows around the tasks agents handle well and the tasks people handle well.

I expect AI workforce management to become its own software category by the end of 2027, in the same way that workforce engagement management grew up around human agents. It will cover agent orchestration, quality monitoring, performance scoring, cost-per-resolution reporting, and governance. Zendesk can compete with its Resolution Platform and the agent technology it gained from Forethought. Salesforce, ServiceNow, Microsoft, Genesys, Sierra, and Decagon will compete too. Buyers should favor the vendors that make it easiest to measure and control AI agents.

The consumer data in Zendesk CX Trends 2027 also shows that 88% of consumers want a human available if needed. In the announcement, Shashi Upadhyay, Zendesk’s President of Product, Engineering and AI, says leaders need to decide where human judgment is needed. Using the 74% figure to justify staff cuts is likely to add AI agents without improving service. The better use of the finding is to redesign service operations around clear roles for AI agents and people.

What to Watch:

Whether Zendesk and its competitors publish audited autonomous resolution rates and cost-per-resolution figures, so buyers can test the forecast against actual results.

Whether buyers and vendors agree on what a “verified outcome” is, including how repeat contacts, escalations, and partial resolutions count in outcome-based contracts.

Adoption of standards for agent identity, permissions, and discovery, including whether other CX platforms and AI assistant makers support Zendesk’s Open Service Directory.

How Salesforce, ServiceNow, Microsoft, Genesys, Sierra, and Decagon respond as tools to supervise, orchestrate, and check the quality of AI agents become buying criteria.

Consumer and regulatory pushback on AI-based identity verification, since most consumers surveyed are uncomfortable with behavioral analysis used without their consent.

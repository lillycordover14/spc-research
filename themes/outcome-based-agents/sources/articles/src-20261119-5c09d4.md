Riders of CTA, Metra and Pace can now report crimes and concerns through a centralized smartphone app.
The Northern Illinois Transit Authority released the app, called NITA Ride Safe, on Thursday.
The app allows users to report safety incidents or concerns without calling a hotline or drawing attention to themselves. Users can send reports by text and photos. They have the option to remain anonymous.
The idea behind the app is to allow riders to discreetly report incidents that are not life-threatening, according to NITA. Those incidents can include harassment, threatening or disruptive behavior, like smoking, a suspicious or unattended item, drug use, vandalism or a person in crisis.
NITA still wants riders to call 911 for more serious incidents, like if someone is hurt or in immediate danger, a weapon is being used or a fire is spreading.

Screenshots of the new NITA Safe Ride app

The app is an updated version of Metra’s COPS app, which has allowed its riders to report safety concerns since 2019. Reports sent through NITA Ride Safe will be answered by Metra staff who had been handling the COPS app, officials said in a previous NITA board meeting. Those employees will determine if a response requires police, CTA’s new 
Safe Ride Specialists
, other first responders or a customer service agent. Officials said the employees would be able to handle the expected increase in reports.
Riders without the app can report safety incidents by texting 312-313-9015.
NITA released the app days before the agency was required by law to have one. The app is one of several public safety requirements baked into the NITA Act, which requires the agency centralize coordination around public safety.
NITA is in the process of hiring a chief transit safety officer to lead a new Office of Transit Safety and Experience.
By next month, a NITA law enforcement task force, led by the Cook County Sheriff’s Office, must share a report on whether to recommend the agency create its own police department. The NITA board must vote by next June on that recommendation.
Chicago-area transit officials have prioritized public safety over the last year, following a series of 
high profile attacks

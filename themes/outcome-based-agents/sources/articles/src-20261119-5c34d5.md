PRINCETON — Gibson County Emergency Management Agency Director Stephanie McKinney Tuesday recognized volunteers who work with EMA in bringing Project Lifesaver to the community.
Project Lifesaver is a nationwide program that helps quickly locate people at risk of wandering to due to Alzheimer's disease, dementia, autism and other special needs.
Participants enrolled in the program wear a small transmitter on their wrist, and if they wander, trained teams use a receiver to locate them quickly.
The program is available at no cost to qualified Gibson County residents.
Agencies involved in the program include the Princeton Fire Department, Gibson County Ambulance Service, Gibson County Sheriff's Department, Gibson Southern High School school resource officer and the Owensville Montgomery Township Fire Protection District.
Tuesday, McKinney presented certificates of appreciation to volunteers Zach Lienemann, Mike Pflug, Jeromy Wade, Jane and Herb Gray, Randy Anthis, A.J. Alldredge and Levi Sims.
New Gibson County Community Paramedic Shelby Riordan updated commissioners on the new program launching in early 2027 through a five-year grant.
She said the community paramedic program offers preventative medicine rather than emergency medicine, with the goal of seeing patients in the community who are frequent users of emergency medical services, those who are readmitted for chronic diseases or other conditions.
Program goals also include more community health education, fall prevention, access to mental health services.
"The goal is to see these patients and set them up for success long-term," she said. Riordan said the program works solely off referrals and community outreach. Referrals can come by phone, email or a referral system.
Preparing to go live soon, Riordan has focused on outreach with Deaconess Gibson Hospital to coordinate services, networking with other programs and completing protocols, including a prenatal and newborn program.
A vehicle has been ordered and should be ready for use when the program launches. Riordan said. 
In other reports to commissioners this week:
• Gibson County Emergency Medical Services spokesman Arica Cole reported 442 runs from 911 calls and 81 transfers. EMS crews participated in trunk-or-treat events and educational programs in the schools. Princeton Fire Department helped install Christmas lights at the new Princeton ambulance base while they were installing lights at the courthouse and Princeton fire station.
• Gibson County Health Department Administrator Juli Ellsworth reported her office closed on the purchase of a new headquarters building in October and is getting quotes on flooring in preparation to move into the building in December. An open house will likely be planned in January.

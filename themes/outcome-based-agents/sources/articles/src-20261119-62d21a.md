MOULTON, Ala. (
WAFF
) - Lawrence County emergency leaders are working to bring back a local planning committee that would bring businesses, first responders and community leaders together to prepare for emergencies.
An introductory meeting is scheduled from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. Thursday, Nov. 19, at First Solar to gauge interest in the Local Emergency Planning Committee, according to Lawrence County Emergency Management Agency Director Steven Carlton.
Carlton said he hopes to have the committee up and running smoothly by spring or summer. 
He wants representatives from schools, health care, local government, utilities, industry and emergency services to be involved.
“One thing I’ve learned since becoming an emergency management director is that emergency management is a team effort,” Carlton said. “No single department, agency, organization can handle every situation by themselves.”
Carlton said former EMA Director Johnny Cantrell had been working to get the committee going, but it was inactive afterward. 
He said local partners, including First Solar, Lockheed Martin and West Lawrence Water, have shown interest in taking part.
The committee could help emergency responders connect with businesses that have expertise or supplies needed during a hazardous materials spill, Carlton said. 
Industries could also provide additional training to first responders.
While such committees traditionally focus on hazardous materials, Carlton said the relationships could also help the county prepare for winter weather. 
By working with utilities and road crews ahead of time, he said, partners could identify resources that might help restore power or reopen roads more quickly.
“I believe it’s a way for everybody to be prepared quicker and not trying to prepare once it’s already hit,” Carlton said.
He said businesses that see a problem developing could also reach out to emergency management early, giving his team more time to prepare and request resources.
“At the end of the day, this isn’t about the emergency management office, it’s about the safety of the people of Lawrence County,” Carlton said.

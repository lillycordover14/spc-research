Koch announced on November 19, 2026, that it is 
testing HappyRobot’s AI agents
 for candidate prescreening and employee HR support, expanding work that began with a two-day workshop in Wichita and produced two working prototypes.
The partnership addresses recurring demands on HR teams: employees regularly need answers about payroll, benefits and policies, while recruiters spend substantial time on preliminary candidate conversations. Koch says the goals are faster answers, a better candidate experience and more consistent support, allowing HR professionals to devote more time to work requiring judgment and expertise.
From Workshop to Working Prototypes
The collaboration began a few months before the announcement, when Koch HR professionals and business leaders joined HappyRobot’s team in Wichita. Participants started by examining where employees and candidates encountered delays, which tasks HR staff repeatedly performed and where more consistent responses could be useful.
By the end of the second day, the group had identified several promising applications and built two prototypes for testing. The process gave Koch’s HR staff a direct role in shaping the technology around their work rather than evaluating a finished product without that operational input.
Jeff Wendling, Koch’s recruiting innovation lead, described one result as already approaching the team’s objective: “Within a couple of days, we had a working solution that was already 75% to 80% of the way toward what we were trying to accomplish.”
Two HR Applications Under Test
In recruiting, an agent conducts initial prescreening calls, confirms key qualifications and captures information for recruiters. Koch says the agent is operating 24/7, enabling candidate screening around the clock. Faster candidate follow-up and additional time for recruiters to build relationships with talent are the intended benefits.
The employee-support application remains exploratory. Under the HR Service Center concept, an agent would help employees navigate common questions, including payroll and tax inquiries, and connect them with existing resources containing the information they need.
HappyRobot says its agents can interact through phone calls, email, documents and the web, as well as connect with existing business systems. Those channels cover several of the ways employees and candidates already seek information or communicate with HR.
Both applications are designed around the same division of labor: 
agents
 handle repeatable requests, while HR professionals remain available for conversations where their judgment and expertise matter most.
A Wider Test of Enterprise Agents
Joint teams have continued testing ideas, learning from the results and identifying additional use cases since the workshop. Koch gains an opportunity to assess emerging technology against operational needs, while 
HappyRobot
 receives direct feedback from people using its technology in a complex business environment.
The companies are also exploring possible applications in customer support, logistics, sales and other service functions across Koch. Those remain areas for investigation rather than announced deployments.
That makes the handoff between automated requests and human expertise central to Koch’s experiment. The practical value of the approach rests on whether agents can make routine interactions faster and more consistent while preserving HR professionals’ time for the conversations that depend on their experience and judgment.

First German MGA Runs Live AI Underwriting with Sixfold&nbsp;&nbsp;FinanceX Magazine

[page text]
exxis, the underwriting agency of Germany's 
Ecclesia Group
, has become the first German MGA to run AI underwriting in live production, deploying 
Sixfold
's platform across cyber, property and accident lines written for multiple carriers. The rollout reached live production within two months, and exxis underwriters now work in the system every day.
For finance professionals, the signal is less about the technology than about where it is spreading: AI underwriting is moving out of large carriers and into the delegated-authority channel, inside a European market governed by the EU AI Act and DORA.
What has exxis put live, and across which lines?
exxis is running cyber, property and accident on Sixfold across several carriers, having reached live production within two months. Underwriters open a case that is already assembled: the relevant carrier's appetite, the source documents, any gaps flagged, and a first-draft narrative, so the work begins at risk assessment rather than file-building. D&O, also part of the exxis book, is not among the lines named as live.
Each line posed a different problem. Cyber carried a high volume of questionnaires, property required loss ratios calculated by hand, and accident generated documentation out of proportion to the premium. Sven Grimm, head of underwriting at exxis, said some underwriters were sceptical at the start and changed their minds after checking the output against what they already knew and finding it held up. Philipp Bresch, a senior property underwriter, said he now reaches the risk assessment itself sooner than before because he can see the whole case at once.
Why does appetite separation matter when an MGA binds for several carriers?
Because exxis writes on behalf of multiple insurers under delegated authority, each submission has to be judged against the specific carrier whose capacity it will use. When a carrier grants an MGA binding authority, it is trusting the agency to underwrite as the carrier itself would.
The deployment keeps each carrier's guidelines and appetite separate by line, so the guidance an underwriter sees reflects the carrier the risk is being written for. That consistency is the commercial point, because it is what lets an agency hold capacity from several insurers at once and retain their confidence. Alex Schmelkin, Sixfold's founder and chief executive, described the platform as evaluating every submission in the context of each carrier's appetite.
How do the EU AI Act and DORA shape AI underwriting in Europe?
The regulatory bar is why a first deployment like this reads as a deliberate decision rather than a quick pilot. Integrating AI meant meeting 
EU AI Act
 readiness, 
DORA
, single-tenant hosting with data kept in Europe, SOC 2 Type 2 attestation, and German-language support alongside English.
The detail worth drawing out is where these lines sit in the Act. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 designates AI used for risk assessment and pricing of natural persons in life and health insurance as high-risk under Annex III, with a quality management system, bias testing, documentation and human oversight expected of in-scope systems by August 2026. Commercial lines such as cyber, property and accident sit largely outside that high-risk category, so exxis's live lines fall under the Act's lighter obligations rather than its strictest tier, though its wider transparency and governance expectations still shape how insurers and their agents adopt the technology.
DORA, in force across the EU since 17 January 2025, is the more directly binding constraint here. It makes insurers and intermediaries responsible for managing third-party ICT providers, which is what single-tenant European hosting and SOC 2 Type 2 attestation are built to satisfy. Tim Brozio, managing director at exxis, said bringing AI into underwriting for the first time is a decision a firm makes once and has to be able to defend, and that the technology had to reflect how each line and each carrier actually underwrites.
Where does this sit in Europe's MGA growth story?
Europe is the fastest-growing part of the global MGA market, which is what makes a German first worth noting. European MGA gross written premium reached about $23bn in 2025, up roughly 15% year on year and outpacing the United States at 7%, according to 
Howden Re
. The broker notes that Europe's MGA market is around five times smaller than the US while the underlying property and casualty market is only about 40% smaller, which it frames as substantial runway. Germany has no dedicated MGA licence category, so agencies such as exxis operate as specialist underwriters within the intermediary regime.
Founded in 2023 and based in New York, Sixfold has raised about $50m, including a $15m Series A led by Salesforce Ventures in 2024 and a $30m Series B led by Brewer Lane announced in January 2026. The company says its platform has processed more than one million submissions across over 40 lines of business, and counts carriers including Zurich North America, 
Guardian
, 
New York Life
, Generali GC&C, AXIS and Skyward Specialty among its users. The exxis rollout is its first disclosed German MGA client, and extends the European expansion the 2024 Series A was raised to fund.
Sixfold cites customer results of a 50% reduction in underwriter review time, new underwriters reaching full caseload 45% faster, and a 30% increase in gross written premium per underwriter. Those are company-reported figures and are not independently audited.
Why this matters to FinanceX readers
The investable signal is that AI underwriting is crossing from carriers into the delegated-authority channel, and doing so in a regulated European market rather than a lighter-touch pilot. For MGAs, the constraint has shifted from whether the models work to whether an agency can run them across several carriers without blurring one insurer's appetite into another's, and whether the governance stack holds up under the EU AI Act and DORA. exxis has answered both in live production on commercial lines, which is a harder test than a single-carrier demo.
For investors tracking listed carriers, private insurtech and the European MGA channel, the metric to watch is the pace at which delegated-authority platforms put AI into production across multiple carriers and deeper lines, rather than another self-reported efficiency gain. Whether exxis extends Sixfold to D&O, and whether more of Europe's $23bn MGA market follows, will say more about adoption than any headline percentage.

Insider Drops Update on Dylan Harper’s Injury After Spurs Star’s Scary Fall Made Victor Wembanyama Emotional
By
Deblina Roy
Nov 19, 2026, 2:25 AM CUT
Imago
IMAGO / ZUMA Press Wire
As Tuesday night ended for the San Antonio Spurs with a 105-93 loss to the Detroit Pistons, an end to their 12-game winning streak, and Dylan Harper’s potential long-term injury that was enough to shake up Spurs star Victor Wembanyama, NBA insider and ClutchPoints reporter Brett Siegel finally had an update.
“The Spurs are labelling Dylan Harper’s injury as left hip soreness after he left last night’s game with an apparent groin injury,” he announced on X on November 18, 2026. “He will miss tomorrow’s game against the Clippers.”
But the injury that happened with 53.6 seconds remaining in the fourth quarter looked worse than what the Spurs have labeled it as. Harper slipped on an apparent wet spot while trying to receive a pass from Spurs guard Stephon Castle and landed awkwardly on the hardwood as his legs went in different directions. The 20-year-old stayed down for some time and needed assistance to get to the locker room.
Initially, NBC reporter, NBA insider and senior writer Chris Mannix reported it to be an “undisclosed injury.”  But it showed clear signs of a groin injury as Harper continued clutching the area while limping out of the paint. The Spurs didn’t make any official announcement immediately, and HC Mitch Johnson also refused to elaborate when asked about it postgame.
“Looked like he slipped, obviously, so hoping for the best, but don’t know anything yet,” Johnson said during the media availability.
Usually, the recovery timeline for mild to moderate hip and groin injuries ranges from 1 to 6 weeks, while it can stretch to 3-6 months in extreme cases. Though the team has labeled it, per Siegel, they have yet to give any detailed elaboration on its nature and Harper’s exact recovery timeline. But despite everything, it was “terrible” for Wemby to see his teammate going down like that.
“It's a terrible feeling, terrible sight," the 7-foot-4 forward said, as reported by the San Antonio Express-News’ 
Tom Orsborn
. "I know as much as you guys, so I hope he's fine. I'll check on him today. But, yeah, I just hate this. I don't know how else I can say this."
Harper has averaged 18.4 points, 5.4 rebounds, and 4.5 assists across 15 games this season, with 50.5/31.5/86.5 from the field, the deep and the charity stripe. He also showed great improvement by moving from sixth man into the starting lineup by replacing Julian Champagnie early in the season. 
Last Sunday, Harper’s impact reflected on the scoreboard as he racked up 31 points, 9 rebounds, 4 assists, and 1 steal in the Spurs’ 110-102 victory over the Utah Jazz.
Not having him for one game shouldn’t be as bad as playing without him for weeks or months. But if it comes to the worst-case scenario, the Spurs will have to figure out a way around playing with a numbers disadvantage.

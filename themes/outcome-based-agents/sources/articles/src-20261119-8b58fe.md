Grand Junction city officials and project partners cut a ribbon to celebrate the reopening of Crosby Avenue and completion of an adjacent pedestrian walkway Tuesday, Nov. 17.

Kalen McCain/Daily Sentinel

A ribbon-cutting ceremony on Tuesday, Nov. 17, saw city officials and stakeholders gathered off the beaten path, on a sidewalk that runs beneath Broadway and parallel to the busy Riverside Parkway, celebrating the completion of a long-running construction project.
Plans to overhaul the pavement and build a pedestrian walkway along Crosby Avenue — a road connecting Rimrock Avenue’s myriad shopping centers and apartments with Grand Junction’s West Main Street and downtown area — have been in the works since 2006, according to city Engineering and Transportation Director Trent Prall.
He said the improvements would facilitate transit between the neighborhoods by car, as well as for pedestrians traveling by bike and on foot. He later added that a Grand Valley Transit bus stop may also be in the road’s future, although that decision wouldn’t fall to the city.
“The corridor was just 22 feet of asphalt, originally,” he said. “It was definitely not conducive to any modes of transportation if you weren’t in a truck or in a car. And the asphalt had been failing for quite some time ... Where we’re standing was actually an irrigation ditch.”

Photos by Kalen McCain/Daily Sentinel
Grand Junction Engineering and Transportation Director Trent Prall speaks ahead of a ribbon-cutting ceremony for the completion of construction of Crosby Avenue, a $3-million project he says made pedestrian access far easier for the corridor connecting Main Street to Rimrock Avenue.

Kalen McCain/Daily Sentinel

The $3-million project broke ground at last in 2025 with utility preparations, and wrapped up in the past few weeks, according to Prall. It was funded by a mix of sources, including a $1 million Main Street revitalization grant from the Colorado Department of Transportation.
Prall added that he was pleased with the outcome, saying it was “fortunate” that another project ran over budget in the early 2000s, since it delayed Crosby’s improvements enough to allow a redesign of plans for the corridor, now open for transit.
In a brief speech before the ribbon was cut, City Manager Mike Bennett also praised the work as well as the municipal staff and partners involved in the road’s facelift.
“It’s an investment in economic vitality and it supports future growth near downtown and in the Rimrock area,” he said.

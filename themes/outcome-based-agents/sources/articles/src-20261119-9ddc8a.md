Philip Rivers tabs longtime NFL assistant Jason Michael as NC State offensive coordinator

Longtime NFL assistant Jason Michael will be NC State's next offensive coordinator, reuniting with incoming head coach Philip Rivers after the two spent time together with the San Diego Chargers and Indianapolis Colts.

Incoming NC State football coach Philip Rivers has named his offensive coordinator.

Jason Michael, a longtime NFL assistant and former offensive coordinator with 23 years of coaching experience, is the pick. Michael, 48, served as the Philadelphia Eagles' tight ends coach from 2021 to 2025, working with Dallas Goedert and helping the team win Super Bowl LIX.

Other WRAL Top Stories

Michael worked with Rivers during his time with the San Diego Chargers, where Rivers spent the majority of his NFL career. Michael was also on the Indianapolis Colts' coaching staff in 2020, during Rivers' only full season with the team.

Michael previously served as the Tennessee Titans' offensive coordinator from 2014 to 2015 before spending two seasons as the team's quarterbacks coach.

“I’m really excited to add Coach Michael to our staff,” Rivers said in a release from NC State athletics. “I have history with him from my time with the Chargers and with the Colts and we are aligned in our vision for building this program. His expertise and experience in the offensive system that we are going to implement here at NC State is second to none, and I can’t wait for him to get started.”

Michael, like Rivers, is the son of a former high school football coach.

“I'm excited and grateful for the opportunity for me and my family to join the Wolfpack family!” Michael said. “The awesome game of football is what connected Philip and me 15 years ago in San Diego, and although it’s taken us both many places since then, I'm thankful for the chance to reunite at the place it all started for him.”

During his introductory press conference Monday, Rivers did not rule out calling offensive plays himself.

"Calling or not calling plays is not a must, one way or the other," Rivers said. "I think some of the ins and outs of our offensive and defensive philosophy, the play caller, and all those things will be known and worked out."

Through 10 games this season, the Wolfpack (4-6, 2-5 ACC) ranks 59th nationally in total offense and 61st in scoring offense, ranking 10th in the ACC in both categories.

Corn pressured by global futures selloff despite solid cash basis
CMB
CMB News Editorial
Editorial Desk
November 19, 2026
Corn futures on CBOT and Euronext weaken amid strong dollar and Brazil exports, while EU and Black Sea cash prices remain relatively firm. Concise market outlook.
Corn prices are under mild but broad pressure, with CBOT and Euronext futures retreating to recent lows, while cash corn in Europe and the Black Sea remains relatively firm thanks to strong basis levels and constrained farmer selling.

The global corn complex is digesting a renewed futures selloff triggered by a stronger U.S. dollar, weaker crude oil and improving South American weather, even as logistics risks in the Black Sea and solid ethanol demand lend some support. Nearby Euronext March 2027 corn around EUR 259/t marks the top of a downward-sloping forward curve, with deferred 2028–2029 maturities closer to EUR 205–227/t. In cash markets, German and Ukrainian feed-grade corn prices have softened only marginally, pointing to resilient regional demand and limited grower willingness to sell at current levels.

 Prices

Euronext corn futures closed on November 18 with March 2027 at EUR 259.00/t, June 2027 at EUR 255.25/t, August 2027 at EUR 254.25/t and November 2027 at EUR 223.50/t, with further declines to EUR 205.75/t for November 2028 and June 2029, underscoring a relatively steep carry structure into the outer years. 

On CBOT, December 2026 corn last traded near 435.50 USc/bu and March 2027 at 448.50 USc/bu on November 18–19, after the front month fell to its lowest level in roughly 4½ months as the firmer dollar and weaker crude weighed on grains. 

Physical quotations show only modest declines compared with futures: German feed-grade corn EXW Drentwede was indicated at EUR 0.28/kg on November 18, only fractionally below recent peaks, while Ukrainian feed-grade corn CPT Odesa held steady at EUR 0.141/kg. Ukrainian corn FOB Odesa eased to EUR 0.119/kg and French yellow corn FOB Paris traded around EUR 0.26/kg, both slightly below levels seen at the end of October. These prices highlight that futures weakness has not yet fully translated into sharp cash discounts.

 Supply & Demand

USDA’s November balance-sheet revisions trimmed U.S. 2026/27 corn production, domestic use, exports and ending stocks, leaving the market fundamentally tighter than earlier in the season but still comfortably supplied. 
 Harvest progress has been somewhat slower than the five-year average in key western Corn Belt states, which has helped support strong basis levels as commercials compete for nearby supplies. 

Internationally, Brazil remains a dominant bearish factor. Exporters’ association ANEC now projects November corn shipments near 8.1 million tonnes, potentially the highest since September 2023, signaling a heavy export program into early 2027. 
 At the same time, Brazilian ethanol capacity is expanding, slightly reducing export pressure but still leaving a substantial exportable surplus. 
 China’s corn imports have more than doubled year-on-year so far in 2026, with recent monthly arrivals up sharply, but this demand has yet to offset the combined weight of large U.S. and Brazilian supplies. 

In Europe, the Euronext forward curve shows only a modest discount from nearby March 2027 to June and August 2027, then a sharper step down into November 2027 and 2028–2029 contracts. This structure reflects expectations of adequate medium-term supply and the influence of competitive Black Sea exports, while still pricing in some risk premium for logistics and security in the region.

 Weather & Regional Highlights

In the United States, short-term forecasts point to only light precipitation across most of the Corn Belt over the coming days, allowing harvest to continue and easing any remaining fieldwork delays. NOAA’s 8–14-day outlook hints at warmer-than-normal temperatures and some wetter risks emerging in parts of the Central Plains toward late November, but without major immediate yield implications now that most of the crop is mature. 

In Brazil, early-season weather for the 2026/27 cycle has been mixed. High rainfall in southern states has at times slowed soybean fieldwork and raised concerns about potential delays to subsequent safrinha corn planting, while corn conditions in Rio Grande do Sul remain broadly favorable. 
 A strong El Niño pattern is expected to keep rainfall above normal in parts of southern Brazil into the planting window, which could shift some risk from yield quantity to planting timing; however, improved recent rain forecasts have eased immediate stress on Brazilian corn prospects. 

 Fundamentals & Sentiment

Fund positioning has turned increasingly bearish again after a brief correction, with managed money net-short in CBOT corn and adding to sales as prices broke to fresh lows for the move this week. 
 The latest price action shows futures reacting more to macro drivers—a stronger dollar and weaker energy markets—than to any sharp deterioration in demand, even as ethanol output remains historically high despite slipping slightly last week. 

Meanwhile, physical markets in Europe and the Black Sea tell a somewhat firmer story. German EXW and Ukrainian CPT corn prices have fluctuated within a relatively narrow band since late October, despite futures volatility, highlighting ongoing feed and industrial demand and limited farmer selling at current levels. At the same time, security and logistics risks in the Black Sea continue to inject risk premium into regional grain values, particularly for wheat and corn, even if this has not fully offset the weight of large global supplies. 

 4–6 Week Outlook & Trading Implications

Looking into December, the combination of ample global supplies, aggressive Brazilian exports and a still-bearish fund position suggests rallies in corn futures are likely to meet producer selling and hedging interest. Weather risks are shifting from Northern Hemisphere harvest to Southern Hemisphere planting and safrinha prospects; any sustained planting delays or yield concerns in Brazil could quickly slow the downside momentum or trigger short-covering rallies.

In Europe, the Euronext curve and regional cash prices imply a gradual normalization rather than an outright shortage scenario. The discount on deferred 2028–2029 contracts versus March 2027 also indicates that, absent a major weather shock, the market expects longer-term balance to remain comfortable.

 Trading Outlook (concise)

Producers (EU & Black Sea):
 Consider scaling in additional hedges on March–June 2027 futures on short-lived rallies, given the recent break lower on CBOT, the carry into outer maturities, and relatively resilient local cash prices.

Feed buyers:
 Maintain a hand-to-mouth approach for nearby needs but look to extend coverage modestly into mid-2027 if Euronext or regional FOB prices retest recent lows, as fundamentals remain broadly comfortable but geopolitical and weather risks persist.

Traders / Funds:
 The trend bias remains lower while macro headwinds (strong dollar, weaker crude) persist, but an already sizeable net short and strong basis argue for tactical caution and readiness to take profits or partially cover ahead of key South American weather windows and further policy news from China.

 3-Day Directional Price View

Euronext corn (March 2027):
 Slightly bearish to sideways; likely to consolidate around EUR 259/t with limited downside unless CBOT extends losses sharply. 

CBOT corn (Dec 2026 / Mar 2027):
 Bias remains cautiously lower after breaking to multi-month lows, though short-term oversold conditions may limit follow-through selling. 

European cash corn (DE, FR, UA):
 Mostly stable with a mild downward drift; basis expected to stay firm near term amid still-active demand and restrained farmer marketing.

If you live in Bureau County, you don't have to worry about the planned munitions blast in southeast Iowa.
Bureau County Director of Emergency Management Adam Lind says while his office is monitoring the planned detonation Friday in Iowa of munitions containing lead azide, it does not appear that Bureau County will see a direct impact from the detonation. Lind his opinion is based on information provided by the U.S. Army and the National Weather Service.
The army issued a public advisory for Iowa residents, warning that the two detonations are expected to result in airborne lead dust. The advisory said modeling efforts predict that the potential level of lead particles in the air may be elevated but well below the level at which EPA would require protective actions be taken. The army says maps for airborne exposure to lead dust and one for exposure from lead dust on surfaces are available 
Public Health Munition Site
, but they are subject to change due to weather.

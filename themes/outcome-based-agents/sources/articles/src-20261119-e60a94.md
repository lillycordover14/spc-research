The Thompson School District Board of Education voted 6-1 Wednesday to approve a plan addressing noncompliance issues with the Americans With Disabilities Act, a $7.5 million effort that board members said was long overdue.

The plan will rectify needs throughout the district ranging from issues as small as braille signs in school buildings to accessible parking and entryways at some of the district’s oldest buildings. It will also reform the district’s policies to become more inclusive, expanding accessibility in areas like job descriptions and applications and streamlining district management of accessibility, including hiring a new district ADA coordinator.

“I do think the plan is very much needed,” board member Nancy Rumfelt said before voting to approve the measure. “I think it will go a long way to show that this district is taking seriously the Americans With Disabilities Act. To have a community where we welcome everybody, everybody needs to be able to access what goes on here, and we should make it as easy and simple as possible.”

Thompson School District develops ADA compliance plan

The new coordinator appeared to be the sticking point for the plan's sole “no” vote, board member Lori Goebel, who worried over adding a new position to the district. The salary for the new coordinator, according to Assistant Superintendent of Operations Todd Piccone, would initially come from bond funds approved in 2025, but would eventually be paid for from the district’s general fund, the operating fund that pays most salaries in the district. The salary for the position, according to a job posting on the district's website, ranges from $74,666 to $108,122.

“I totally support the plan, regarding the work being done, but the position, that’s where my challenge lies,” Goebel said.

Superintendent Bret Heller said that the position was an important part of the district’s plan to address ADA compliance, an area that, according to a recent analysis by a consulting firm presented to the board at a study session, was often fragmented and lacking direction.
 

“There’s a strong belief, I have a strong belief, the folks who did the audit, even some of the folks I’ve met with who have concerns around how ADA has been handled in our district, have suggested something along the lines of an expert in ADA work to coordinate as we move into this transition plan,” he said. “If that means we need to make decisions with other allocations or expenditures we’ll have that conversation when the time comes, but to me, this report makes it very clear that we have a lot of work to do when it comes to ADA.”

Goebel’s no vote sparked a reaction from fellow board member Dawn Kirk, who levied criticism during a later portion of the meeting set aside for board member comments.

“I’m just incredibly disappointed to see that we didn’t have a unanimous vote for investing and making sure that our facilities are accessible and our work is accessible, given how important it is to make sure that everyone can access their government,” Kirk said. “I have no words, I’ll leave it at that.”

Goebel used the same time to criticize a perceived lack of traditional family values in the school district.
 

"I truly wanted to support, and I do support, the work going forward with the Americans With Disabilities Act, as outlined in tonight's presentation," she said. "However, the steady removal of family values leads to one unavoidable conclusion: policies and cultures are purposely pushing out families with strong traditional values."

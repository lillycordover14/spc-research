Clean Shipping Alliance Questions Basis for Greenland Scrubber Discharge Ban&nbsp;&nbsp;Ship & Bunker

[page text]
Andreas Chrysostomou is the executive director of CSA. Image Credit: CSA

The 
Clean Shipping Alliance (CSA)
, a coalition of
shipping companies that support the use of scrubber systems, has questioned the
basis for 
Greenland
's phased restrictions on discharges of washwater from
scrubber systems.

Greenland's Parliament has recommended the adoption of a proposal to prohibit discharges from open-loop
scrubbers in the country's port areas, inland waters and territorial waters
from 
July 1, 2027
.

The restrictions would be extended to all other scrubber
systems, including closed-loop systems, from 
July 1, 2029
.

“We are disappointed to see Greenland announce restrictions
on exhaust gas cleaning systems (EGCS, or ‘scrubbers’), starting in 2027,” 
Andreas
Chrysostomou
, executive director of CSA, said in an emailed statement on 
Wednesday
.

He questioned which specific risks Greenland had identified
from scrubber discharges and how it had assessed them.

Chrysostomou added that the 
IMO's MEPC.1/Circ.899

provides an agreed method for evaluating local risks, including receiving
environments, discharge composition, dilution and ecotoxicity.

He added that “a precautionary approach cannot replace the
assessment that the IMO framework calls for”.

Chrysostomou also said that scrubbers reduce sulphur and
other substances released into the air, as well as particulate matter, PAHs and
metals.

Citing lifecycle studies, the alliance questioned whether
switching to MGO would necessarily deliver better environmental outcomes.

“Assessments should compare complete fuel pathways, not a
visible discharge against an alternative assumed to have no impact,”
Chrysostomou said.

The proposed restrictions come amid a wider move to curb discharge
of washwater from scrubbers.

Denmark
, 
Sweden
 and 
Finland
 introduced
bans on open-loop scrubber discharges in their territorial waters last year,
while a separate restriction covering open-loop and hybrid scrubber washwater
discharges in ports and inland waters across the 
North-East Atlantic

region is due to take effect in 
mid-2027
.

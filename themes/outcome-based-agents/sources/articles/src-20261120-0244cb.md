Bitcoin plunges below $59,000, on pace for its worst month since June 2022
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
David Hollerith   
 
·
 Senior Reporter
 
 
Fri, November 20, 2026 at 1:44 PM MST
 
2 min read
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
BTC-USD
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
ANT
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Bitcoin (
BTC-USD
) plunged below $59,000 on Friday, deepening a November sell-off that is shaping up to be the cryptocurrency's worst monthly decline in more than four years.
 
 
Bitcoin fell as low as $57,957 in late Friday morning trading before recovering some of those losses to trade at $58,500 by the afternoon. At its current price, the digital coin is down more than 20% 
so far in November
, putting it on pace for its worst monthly performance since June 2022, according to Yahoo Finance data. That would be bitcoin's eighth-worst month since Yahoo Finance's daily records began tracking the digital asset in September 2014.
 
 
The sell-off comes as chips and other tech stocks are tumbling on the heels of 
Anthropic's public debut
, which investors largely viewed as the AI trade's "Super Bowl moment" for 2026.
 
 
 
US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds have also added to the selling pressure. So far this month, net investors have withdrawn roughly $3.5 billion, according to asset manager Farside Investors, which tracks the product flows. That includes $582 million in outflows on Thursday alone.
 
 
Thursday's monster Anthropic listing has also ushered some soul-searching among crypto's longtime believers.
 
 
"I said back in May that most of the crypto industry was dead and it was never coming back. I believe that even more today," investor Anthony Pompliano said in a Friday 
post
 on X, where he remarked on the "enormous benefits" and " impressive revenue growth" of AI companies.
 
 
 
 
(Reuters/Dado Ruvic/Illustration)
 
·
 
Reuters / REUTERS
 
 
Pompliano added that he views bitcoin, stablecoins, and certain infrastructure projects as exceptions that will inevitably stick around.
 
 
"It is a sign of intelligence to change your mind, or evolve your thinking, when the facts change," Pompliano wrote. "I remain bullish on bitcoin, but there are a lot of ways up the mountain too."
 
 
 
 
 
Along with intensifying competition from this year's biggest IPOs and the broader AI trade, which has prompted some bitcoin mining firms to 
sell their holdings at a faster pace
, analysts have pointed to the toll that higher interest rates are taking on investor appetite for riskier assets.
 
 
Fundstrat digital asset strategist Sean Farrell said Thursday evening that crypto exchange Coinbase Global (COIN) continued to lead bitcoin selling. He added that while signs of a bottom were beginning to emerge, the selling pressure hasn't yet reached past levels for a compelling buying opportunity.

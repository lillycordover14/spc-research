Sidney Crosby’s agent: “He isn’t in any rush to make a decision”
by
 
 
Jim Rixner
Nov 20, 2026, 3:46 PM UTC

Pat Brisson issued a statement yesterday, on the heels of Penguins general manager Kyle Dubas holding a
 press conference Wednesday w
here he indicated trades could soon be made with a “future-oriented” tilt for the team to get young player and prospects in the door while sending veterans out. Names like Rickard Rakell and Erik Karlsson are swarming fast in the rumor mill. Had Bryan Rust not been injured, he’d be right there with them.
All three of those players are usually on the ice with 39-year old Sidney Crosby. When uninjured, all three are usually in the top-5 of scoring on the team. Dubas’ message was clear that the next phase of team-building is on the way and it’s going to be to stock the deck with youth. In the modern era, one has to wonder how camp Crosby would respond to that, especially since it wasn’t 
that long ago
 that Brisson made no secret that, “we want Sidney to hopefully be in the playoffs every year. We want him to hopefully win another Cup or two”.
Pens GM Kyle Dubas told media Wednesday that it will be up to Sidney Crosby what he wants to do as far as his future, a respectful stance he has maintained on the iconic captain.
Here’s a statement from Crosby’s agent Pat Brisson today on the matter:
“Sidney has been in Pittsburgh for over 20 years and he isn’t in any rush to make a decision. Kyle will do what he feels is best for the organization in the meantime.”
If this was a game of ‘big deal, little deal, no deal’, in a sense the whole turn of events for the week is a very big deal. In a sense, the Penguins have been in a form of a reset/rebuild (whatever form you want to use) since the Jake Guentzel trade. However, they have not steered hard into that direction, keeping around several veterans and still trying to see out the Crosby/Evgeni Malkin era for its natural time.
Those decisions, plus a bunch of fortunate outcomes from other moves, worked out well and enabled the Penguins to be in a spot to make the playoffs last year. The recipe didn’t come together as cleanly for this season, and the team is ending the era on their own by dropping to the bottom of the division, conference and NHL at-large through the first 21 games of the season.
That leaves Dubas with little choice, especially in terms of Karlsson - who has an expiring contract. Removing the top defenseman, and only player around with his skillset and ability to competently play at the top of the lineup in that position will cascade an even greater downfall for the team.
That brings the question around to Crosby, who finds himself with individual and broader concepts to consider. Crosby has shown his age this season, easily his least effective play in the NHL. Crosby is not just ‘getting old’ at this point, he’s flat out old. He is, after all, the sixth oldest skater currently in the whole league.
As Brisson stated this week, Crosby has a decision to make for the future and he has been making it on his own timeline. Does he even want to come back in 2027-28 when he’ll be 40-years old? That’s a question that not even he may know the answer to at this point. If he does comeback, is he content to follow his previously stated wishes of remaining a Penguin - a team with a clear direction now of not being in position to be even a playoff contender in the near future?
These are the questions he will wrestle with internally and have to come to an answer on its own. He doesn’t have to find an answer today, tomorrow or next week, and probably won’t anyways.
Dubas and the Penguins appear comfortable with taking a deferntial role, at least in Crosby’s case.
“I think in Sid’s case, he’s as unique as it gets,” 
Dubas said
 earlier in the week. “I would just go back to my view on it from the opening of the season, is that we want to get into this as far as we can, have an indication about where we’re at and where we’re going, and make sure everyone’s comfortable with that. And then, he’s owed all the respect in the world. If he wants to be here, that’s great, and that’s obviously what we would love as well.”
As for the present, that leaves all parties in an awkward type of limbo. Everyone knows changes will come sooner than later. Crosby will have to react to that - and his own age and individual performance - to come to a decision about how, when and where he wants to go from there. So much has been so certain for so long for the franchise and their franchise player but the future at this point looks as cloudy and unpredictable for both of them as it has in a long, long time.

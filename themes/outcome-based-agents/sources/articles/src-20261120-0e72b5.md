The Kitchen Master owner takes his cooking up a notch with a new restaurant called Wabi Sabi&nbsp;&nbsp;Atlanta Magazine

[page text]
A rendering of the dining room
Courtesy of Y Studio
If you’ve eaten soup dumplings at 
Kitchen Master
 in Suwanee, you already know owner-chef Ian Peng’s cooking. Now the restaurateur is aiming higher. Wabi Sabi, an upscale evolution of Kitchen Master, will open in Midtown Union in August or September 2027, near 
Fleming’s Prime Steakhouse
 and 
White Windmill Bakery & Café
.

Peng hails from Taiwan. He moved to Suwanee at age 9 and later worked his way up from dishwasher to sushi chef. He launched Kitchen Master in Frisco, Texas, in 2020, with family-style, shareable Asian dishes, and has since debuted locations in Suwanee and Taiwan. Now, he’s ready to branch out.

“I want to take build on what I have. We’ll get more creative with the flavors and ingredients like morels, abalone, and quail,” he says.
Strawberry hamachi carpaccio with yuzu strawberry vinaigrette
Courtesy of Wabi Sabi
Peng says roughly 40 percent of the menu will be unique to Wabi Sabi with twists on elevated dishes like tea-smoked Peking duck. Expect premium ingredients like Berkshire pork, Japanese A5 Wagyu, Australian MS9 Wagyu, and truffles. Other offerings will feel familiar, like bao, dumplings, noodles, stir-fry and grilled dishes. Soup dumplings get a playful treatment, with flavors drawn from regional Taiwanese and Chinese cooking, including vine peppers known for their numbing effect. For lunch, look for teishoku sets served with rice, soup, and an entrée.

The bar is another place Wabi Sabi will show its creative side, utilizing smoke and clarification techniques. Peng plans to have a dozen craft cocktails made with Asian ingredients and named for zodiac signs. Dessert cocktails may feature Thai tea, Vietnamese coffee, cinnamon bark, or Sichuan pepper.
Wagyu Sando (slightly torched A5 Kagoshima Wagyu with miso butter onion and a Japanese tare sauce over toasted Japanese milk bread)
Courtesy of Wabi Sabi
Bottled beers will be primarily Japanese and Taiwanese, plus a few local picks. The sake list will feature 15 to 20 selections, from sweeter to fresh and filtered styles. Wines span New and Old World, both by the bottle and glass.

The 11,000-square-foot, dual-floor restaurant takes its name and look from the Japanese philosophy of wabi-sabi, which advises finding simplicity in imperfection, especially in nature. Peng, who studied art in high school, says it was the art form he loved most.
A rendering of the mezzanine
Courtesy of Y Studio
That appreciation for natural simplicity carries into the design. The ground floor houses the dining room, which boasts floor-to-ceiling windows and a 20-foot tree. Upstairs, a mezzanine overlooks the dining room. It’s loungey, lively, and dim with a small menu of finger food and drinks to finish a night. An invite-only speakeasy offers a third place to hang out, with red accents, a mysterious aura, and a chef-driven, tapas-style menu that rotates seasonally.
A rendering of the speakeasy
Courtesy of Y Studio
Peng also has his sights for Wabi Sabi set beyond Atlanta—he’s already looking for a space in Dallas.

Wabi Sabi is located in Midtown Union at 1331 Spring Street NW.

Penguins first-quarter report card: Low marks for Sidney Crosby and Erik Karlsson amid a terrible start 
King Jemison
11/20/2026 10:00 a.m. EST

The dejection was clear in Sidney Crosby’s face and words Thursday night.
Another terrible second period that erased a solid start. Another flurry of unanswered goals. 
Another lopsided loss
.
“It’s definitely deflating when we feel like we’re doing some good things and all of a sudden, one mistake is in the back of your net,” Crosby said. “That’s discouraging.”
It must also be deflating to again face questions about rampant trade rumors and his own future with the team. But those conversations are unavoidable after the Penguins’ 7-12-2 start.

So are some rough grades for a disappointing first quarter of the season.
We’ll evaluate every player who’s appeared in at least five games this season. They’ll be graded relative to their expectations.

Forwards
Sidney Crosby (6 goals, 8 assists, 21 games): D
This is on pace to be the worst season of Crosby’s 22-year career, and it’s not particularly close. His point-per-game streak is in jeopardy. And he just hasn’t had the all-around impact we’re used to seeing. 
Evgeni Malkin (6 goals, 12 assists, 19 games): B+
Advertise with us
Malkin earned another year in Pittsburgh with his 2025-26 season. This year, he’s showing that resurgence wasn’t a fluke. He leads Penguins forwards in scoring.
Rickard Rakell (8 goals, 5 assists, 21 games): C
He was headed for an F after not scoring for the first nine games. But he’s flipped the switch since and continues to contribute on both special teams.
Bryan Rust (3 goals, 2 assists, 7 games): B-
He barely meets the games-played threshold and is set to be sidelined until at least early next month. But Rust was his usual impactful self in his short stint. He was also one of the most vocal veterans amid the early struggles. They’ve sorely missed him since.
Advertise with us
Egor Chinakhov (3 goals, 3 assists, 17 games): D+
Based on production, he might deserve an F. He’s nowhere close to the 35-goal pace at which he played in Pittsburgh last season. But he has been one of the Penguins’ best forwards for several games now. The scoring just hasn’t followed.
Tommy Novak (8 goals, 7 assists, 21 games): A-
Dubas signed Novak to an extension this summer that would keep him in Pittsburgh until 2030. Novak is making that look wise. He’s tied for the team high in goals and ranks third in points while playing a smart 200-foot game.
Ben Kindel (4 goals, 5 assists, 18 games): C
Advertise with us
It’s been a strange sophomore season for Kindel, including a preseason injury and a scary medical episode last month. So he gets a pass for the first quarter. The flashes have been there. But it would be slightly concerning if he doesn’t ramp up soon.
Nick Robertson (7 goals, 0 assists, 20 games): B
The speedy winger soared out of the gate. His scoring has since cooled. But he’s held his own defensively and on the penalty kill while primarily playing a fourth-line role. Robertson has been one of the Penguins’ best newcomers.
Connor Dewar (2 goals, 3 assists, 21 games): C+
The Penguins prevented Dewar from reaching free agency last summer after he proved to be an ideal fourth-liner and ferocious penalty killer. That’s still true this season, but he’s been less noticeable.
Advertise with us
Blake Lizotte (0 goals, 5 assists, 21 games): C
Lizotte was quietly one of the Penguins’ most impactful players last season, both as a fourth-line center and penalty killer. He has not been as effective this season, the first of a three-year extension he signed in January.
Ville Koivunen (2 goals, 4 assists, 17 games): D+
He gets graded on a $32 million rubric, so it doesn’t look pretty. But Koivunen has been fine this season. He’s had a couple of highlights, and the production has ticked up slightly. He still hasn’t translated his American Hockey League dominance and feistiness to the NHL.
Hendrix Lapierre (2 goals, 5 assists, 16 games): C-
Advertise with us
Despite his speed and skill, the offseason pick-up has often been invisible — with a few strong games mixed in. His inconsistency mirrors the team as a whole.
Rutger McGroarty (0 goals, 0 assists, 5 games): C+
The 22-year-old does a lot of good things on NHL ice. Scoring is not yet one of them. McGroarty should get lots of opportunities this season as he learns to translate his hockey sense and strength to the highest level. Despite zero points, he seems to be slowly putting it together.
Justin Brazeau (1 goal, 0 assists, 7 games): C-
Brazeau scored in his second game back after missing the first month with an injury but has been silent otherwise. He has size the Penguins desperately need, but it hasn’t helped much yet.
Advertise with us
Elmer Soderblom (0 goals, 1 assist, 6 games): B-
The 6-foot-8 winger has only played six games, as he was a surprising healthy scratch early and then got injured. He wasn’t as productive as last season, but his size and physicality bring a dynamic the Penguins otherwise lack. They need him back.
Andrei Kuzmenko (0 goals, 1 assist, 12 games): D
The 30-year-old free-agent signee stood out in training camp and the Penguins’ first preseason game. But he and his family then suffered the tragic loss of his father in Russia. That understandably derailed his season. He’s still working to get back on track.
Defensemen
Erik Karlsson (3 goals, 16 assists, 21 games): D
Advertise with us
It’s a harsh grade for the team leader in points. But Karlsson is also last in plus-minus, reflecting his defensive drop-off from last season. The turnovers are back in a big way. He hasn’t looked particularly invested in his own zone recently.
Kris Letang (1 goal, 10 assists, 21 games): B
He’s one of the few returners playing better than last season. Letang has been more responsible with the puck. He’s still shutting down plays with his skating. He’s still doling out hits and playing incredibly hard.
Kaedan Korczak (0 goals, 10 assists, 21 games): B+
Korczak looks like a valuable long-term piece on the blue line. He’s got size, he can skate and he plays a simple game with the puck. Crushing St. Louis Blues forward Jimmy Snuggerud into the Penguins’ bench Thursday earns some extra credit. They need more of that.
Advertise with us
Sam Girard (2 goals, 6 assists, 18 games): C
Girard was headed for a higher grade, then his production tapered off. He has definitely been better than last season and fits well with Korczak. Girard’s lack of size can create problems in the defensive zone. 
Declan Carlile (3 goals, 3 assists, 20 games): D+
Carlile’s effort is never in question. He’s one of the Penguins’ most physical players. He was looking like another free-agent steal early in the season. Then the loud defensive mistakes crept in and he started to look like an undrafted player with little NHL experience. Perhaps some time in the press box will stabilize him.
Spencer Stastney (0 goals, 3 assists, 16 games): B
Advertise with us
Stastney has been steady since arriving from Edmonton, a rare trait on the Penguins’ blue line. Like Korczak, he’s willing to make the simple play. He doesn’t bring much physicality but makes up for it with skating.
Trevor van Riemsdyk (0 goals, 1 assist, 6 games): C
Giving the 35-year-old a two-year contract over the summer still doesn’t make a lot of sense. But after missing 15 games with injury, van Riemsdyk showed Thursday why he’s built a long NHL career. Perhaps he can play well enough for Dubas to deal him.
Goalies
Arturs Silovs (5-7-1 record, .873 save %): C
It’s tough to evaluate the Penguins’ goalies because they’re facing so many high-danger chances. Silovs has been more consistent than last season. He really hasn’t allowed many bad goals. But the raw numbers aren’t good, and the advanced stats show he’s underwater in expected goals. So this is the highest grade he can get.
Advertise with us
Sergei Murashov (2-5-1 record, .894 save %): B-
The 22-year-old started hot, went through a bad stretch and is now back to playing well. Murashov will 
probably remain on the roller coaster
 — that’s just life for a rookie goalie, particularly behind this team. But he continues to flash his talent.

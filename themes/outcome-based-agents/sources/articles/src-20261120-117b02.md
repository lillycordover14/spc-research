One Grant Changed Her Life. Now Another Is Taking an Adaptive Mountain Bike Athlete Even Further&nbsp;&nbsp;SnowBrains

[page text]
Share this article 

 

A life-changing accident didn’t stop Utah athlete Erica Smith from finding a passion for the outdoors. Through adaptive mountain biking and support from the High Fives Foundation, she discovered a new community, completed major adventures, and found a career helping others. Now, a second grant is helping her take on even bigger challenges. | Photo: Erica Smith

In 2008, then-Kansas resident Erica Smith was in a motor vehicle accident that left her with a complete T6 spinal cord injury at the age of 17.
 The injury resulted in permanent paralysis below her mid-chest.

Rather than accepting a sedentary lifestyle, she initially turned to adaptive mountain biking to explore more of the outdoors and reach areas that weren’t accessible in her wheelchair.
 However, living in Kansas at the time and the high cost of specialized equipment made it difficult to participate regularly, and she only rode about once a year while on vacation.

That changed after she moved to Colorado in 2022 and attended a women’s adaptive mountain biking camp in Crested Butte.
 There, she discovered not only a new passion for riding but also a community of people who shared her interests.

“I think the first time I ever went downhill mountain biking I was mostly thinking, ‘wow I can’t believe I’m really doing this,'” Smith said in an interview with
 SnowBrains
. 
“When I first had the injury, social media wasn’t a thing like it is today. 
I couldn’t just open up an app on my phone and see someone like me mountain biking or skiing.  
When I began having those experiences myself, it shifted my perspective on life in general. I started saying yes to more things that scared me, and it just gave me confidence in so many ways I never expected.”

In 2022, Smith also received a $5,000 grant from the High Fives Foundation, a nonprofit that focuses on preventing life-changing injuries and providing resources, grants, and support to help injured athletes return to outdoor sports and recreation.
 Smith had learned about the organization through connections from the camp she had attended.

The grant helped Smith purchase a $15,000 
Bowhead Rogue
 adaptive mountain bike.
 In the years that followed, that bike helped her build strength, develop new skills, and discover a community that shared her love of the outdoors.

With her new adaptive mountain bike, Smith started exploring more.
 She rode trails in Colorado and Utah and participated in gravel races. She even rode the 
White Rim Trail
, a legendary 100-mile unpaved backcountry route in Canyonlands National Park near Moab, Utah. “I rode the entire trail in April of 2025,” Smith explained. “We did it over four days and three nights, and funding and support from the High Fives Foundation made the trip possible.

The Second Grant

Smith has now been awarded another $5,000 grant from the High Fives Foundation’s 2026 Empowerment Fund toward a new 
Bowhead RX
 adaptive mountain bike.
 The RX will let her tackle more technical terrain and keep progressing as an athlete. It will serve as the next stage of her adaptive recreation journey. The RX is a serious upgrade and costs $25,000.

“The RX has a one-of-a-kind articulating front-end suspension system, which allows the bike to lean more naturally through turns and navigate uneven, off-camber, technical terrain with greater agility,” Smith explained.
 “My current bike is Front Wheel Drive and struggles on steep, loose terrain. The RX will handle this kind of riding much better. It’s also a few inches narrower than my current bike, which opens up more single track options.”

Smith took a spill on this ride with the National Ability Center and instructor Seth Horowitz. Erica expects fewer crashes with her new, more capable bike. | Photo: Erica Smith

Smith plans to put the new bike to good use.
 “
I plan to compete in the Utah gravel series for the first time in 2027,” Smith said. “I’m in talks with friends about another White Rim trip as well. The old bike I used the first time definitely wasn’t meant for that kind of terrain, and I needed help in quite a few sections because I lost traction. I’m also a much more confident rider now, and I’d love to do it again on my new bike, independently.

Smith also plans to return to Rebecca’s Private Idaho (RPI), now called 
The Big Lost
, which is a world-renowned gravel cycling race and multi-day festival in Sun Valley, Idaho.
 “I plan to compete and do a longer course than I did last year,” Smith said.

The grant is one of 235 Empowerment Fund grants awarded by High Fives Foundation in 2026.
 The grants totaled $925,450 across two funding cycles, including $650,750 for adaptive equipment. The funding helps athletes with spinal cord injuries, traumatic brain injuries, amputations, and other mobility-limiting injuries access specialized equipment that allows them to return to outdoor recreation, gain independence, and pursue new adventures.

Turning a Passion Into a Career Helping Others

Smith’s passion for adaptive recreation has profoundly shaped her career.
 She now serves as Marketing Manager at the 
National Ability Center
 in Park City, Utah, where she uses her own experiences to help others discover the opportunities, independence, and sense of community that adaptive sports can provide.

“I can’t imagine my life without an adaptive mountain bike,” Smith said.
 “
Mountain biking changed the entire trajectory of my life. Not only did it bring me a new hobby, new friends, and a new way to access the outdoors, but it’s now my career. I get to help other people discover what is possible for them.”

For Smith, the new Bowhead RX is about much more than getting a new bike.
 It’s about having the freedom to explore new trails, take on bigger challenges, and keep pushing herself. What started as a way to get outdoors has grown into a passion that has introduced her to new friends, incredible adventures, and a career helping others discover the possibilities of adaptive recreation.

With new races and adventures planned, Smith has plenty to look forward to.
 Her journey also reminds us how access to the right equipment and support can open doors that might otherwise remain closed. Thanks to the support of High Fives Foundation, Smith has another opportunity to keep exploring, inspiring others, and seeing just how far she can go.

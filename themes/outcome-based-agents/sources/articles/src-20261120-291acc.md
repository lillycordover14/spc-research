Key Moments
WTI trades below $83.00 with a negative bias after failing to build on a modest recovery from near three-month lows.

Technical structure remains bearish below the 200-day EMA at $84.74, with key Fibonacci levels defining downside and upside triggers.
WTI Pullback Extends as Recovery Momentum Fades
West Texas Intermediate (WTI) – the benchmark US crude oil contract – is unable to extend the prior session’s modest rebound from its lowest level since late August and is trading with a downside bias below the $83.00 level during the Asian session on Friday. The move leaves the contract on course to post a third consecutive weekly decline, as market participants monitor developments related to the Middle East crisis before committing to the next directional move.
Geopolitics and Supply Outlook Send Mixed Signals
The New York Times reported that US President Donald Trump was considering another round of extensive military strikes on Iran. In addition, Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia’s capital, Riyadh, along with reports of tanker disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, have revived fears of supply interruptions, providing some support to crude prices.
At the same time, the International Energy Agency (IEA) projected that oil supply would outpace demand into the fourth quarter of 2026 and 2027. This outlook for a future surplus acts as a counterweight to geopolitical risk and serves to limit the upside in WTI.
Technical Landscape: Bearish Bias Below the 200-Day EMA
From a technical standpoint, WTI continues to exhibit a bearish short-term profile while it trades beneath the 200-day Exponential Moving Average (EMA), currently located at $84.74. Momentum indicators reinforce this negative tone: the Relative Strength Index (RSI) hovers near 35, and the Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) histogram remains in negative territory, signaling persistent downside forces even as conditions approach oversold levels.
However, confirmation of further weakness would require a decisive move below the 61.8% Fibonacci retracement at $81.25, which is seen as a critical level for validating additional depreciation.

Key Levels: Supports and Resistance Zones to Watch
Should selling pressure intensify, the next notable downside levels are located at deeper Fibonacci supports at $75.09 and $67.24. These areas are likely to attract attention as potential demand zones if prices extend their slide.
On the upside, the initial hurdle is the 200-day EMA at $84.74. A break above this moving average would then put the 50.0% Fibonacci retracement at $85.58 in focus. A daily close beyond both of these resistance levels would be required to meaningfully relieve the current bearish tone and potentially open a path toward higher objectives at $89.90 and $95.26.

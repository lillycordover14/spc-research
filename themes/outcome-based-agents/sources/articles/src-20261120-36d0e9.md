FRESNO, Calif. (FOX26) — 
The Fresno Discovery Center is hoping to dig up some community support to bring a new dinosaur fossil digging experience to the Central Valley, one where children of all abilities can play, explore and learn together.
The nonprofit science center is looking to raise $60,000 to build a new ADA-accessible Dino Dig, an outdoor classroom where children can uncover fossils, explore rocks and minerals and learn about science through hands-on activities.
But the project is about much more than dinosaurs.
The Discovery Center already has a popular Dino Dig where younger children can get down in the sand and search for fossils. However, the current experience isn't accessible to everyone, particularly children with disabilities and parents who may have difficulty getting down on the ground.
"Accessibility is one of the main reasons why we're looking to invest in this project," said Elizabeth Johnson, executive director of the Fresno Discovery Center.
Johnson explained that the new exhibit would allow children of different ages and abilities to participate in the same activities rather than creating separate spaces for children with disabilities.
The goal is to create an inclusive environment where parents can also join their children in exploring and learning.
"It'll be like an outdoor classroom where kids of all abilities all ages can go in and dig for fossils," Johnson said.
The Fresno Discovery Center has served local families since 1954, providing affordable educational opportunities through interactive science, technology, engineering, arts and mathematics activities.
For more than 70 years, the center has welcomed children for school field trips, educational camps, after-school programs and family outings.
Johnson said experiences like the Dino Dig can help introduce children to careers they may have never considered, including paleontology and geology.
"We know through research that having kids explore through hands-on discovery really ignites their curiosity for future learning," Johnson said.
Brandi Snow, vice president of the Fresno Discovery Center's board of directors, said providing new experiences is especially important in a community with limited children's science education attractions.
"It is so crucial for the kids in this community to see themselves reflected in these professions, in science and technology professions in these fields," Snow said.
The center recently added a new playground designed to be more accessible to children with different abilities. Snow hopes the Dino Dig will continue that progress.
She said the new exhibit would allow older children, younger children, parents and children with disabilities to participate together.
While the Discovery Center has been a part of Fresno for decades, Snow said securing funding for new projects can be challenging.
She explained that many residents remember visiting the center during childhood field trips but may not realize the organization continues to serve families throughout the Central Valley.
The $60,000 fundraising goal would help cover permits and infrastructure improvements necessary to bring the new Dino Dig to life.
Johnson said the center has relied on community generosity for decades, including volunteers, donated materials and financial contributions.
"The community support is crucial for us to be able to invest in projects like this," Johnson said.
For families, the project would mean another opportunity to spend time together while encouraging children to get outdoors, ask questions and discover something new.
And for the Discovery Center, it's an investment in the next generation of scientists, engineers and curious young minds.
The Fresno Discovery Center is seeking donations and sponsorships to help raise the $60,000 needed for the new Dino Dig.
Community members and businesses interested in supporting the project can visit FresnoDiscoveryCenter.org for more information.
The center also offers educational programs, camps and events throughout the year, including an upcoming Thanksgiving break camp.

'We probably were not the fastest' – Russell admits Mercedes lacking pace despite topping FP2 in Las Vegas
George Russell and Mercedes finished Thursday quickest in Las Vegas during a day of wet running.
Nov 20, 2026 6:50am UTC
George Russell admits that "realistically we probably were not the fastest", despite finishing top of the times in a wet Free Practice 2 ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

The 
Mercedes
 driver finished less than one-tenth clear of Pierre Gasly's Alpine during 
Thursday's second, one-hour session
 on the streets of Las Vegas, which was held in wet but drying conditions.

Russell
's time, a 1m 47.272s, came in the best of the conditions and just as the chequered flag was shown to end proceedings, with the likes of Ferrari's Charles Leclerc and McLaren's Lando Norris having been towards the top of the times all afternoon.
"We know in the wet that it's different every single lap, every single session you do," said Russell reflecting on his session.

"I think realistically we probably were not the fastest today. McLaren looked exceptionally fast, Charles looked very fast at points, we just got the lap in when the track was at its driest and Norris didn't run.

"We've got some things to understand because we were very competitive in the wet last week [in Brazil] and both Kimi [Antonelli] and I were off the pace [today]."
Russell finished fastest in FP2 but only after posting a lap in the best of the conditions
Thursday's second session was the only meaningful running drivers and teams got during the day and, with Friday set to be dry, Russell stated Free Practice 3 would be crucial in order to get a better understanding of how the tyres will perform in the cold conditions.

"I think nobody knows how the graining is going to be for Sunday," he said. "Last year was an easy one-stop but the year before there was lots of graining and it was a two-stop.

"So they're questions that we want answered but we probably won't get an answer until race day."

Ahead of the weekend, 
Russell opted against using a new turbo
 that Mercedes have introduced, but it will be used by team mate and World Champion, 
Kimi Antonelli
.
Antonelli will serve a five-place grid penalty in Las Vegas
As well as the new turbo, Antonelli’s Mercedes – which finished FP2 in P8 – has been fitted with a new internal combustion engine (ICE), his sixth of the season and surpassing the permitted number of four, meaning he will serve a five-place grid penalty.

"It's a very small performance gain in the sort of tens of milliseconds," said Mercedes Deputy Team Principal, Bradley Lord, when speaking about the new turbo.

"But we'll need to wait to get running in the dry to really see the full benefit and the full understanding of performance it brings and how it might improve some of the drivability of the PU."

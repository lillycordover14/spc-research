Tennr and OpenAI Partner to Test AI Agents for Prior Authorization Automation&nbsp;&nbsp;HLTH

[page text]
Tennr has partnered with OpenAI to test AI agents capable of completing prior authorization tasks, aiming to reduce administrative workloads and accelerate patient access to care. The collaboration will evaluate how accurately and consistently AI agents can handle insurance authorization processes compared with human staff, helping determine whether autonomous technology can reliably support complex healthcare workflows.
Tennr specializes in automating patient referral management using large language models to analyze information received through fax, email and online portals. The company's platform helps healthcare organizations process referral documentation and coordinate patient access to services. Through the new pilot, Tennr will extend these capabilities into prior authorization, a process that often requires staff to navigate multiple payer systems, submit clinical documentation and track insurance decisions.
The AI agents will use patient and insurance information provided by Tennr to complete authorization requests directly within payer portals. Their responsibilities will include entering required information, submitting supporting medical records and communicating insurers' decisions to healthcare staff. The pilot will compare these activities with traditional staff-led workflows to assess the agents' accuracy, consistency and ability to complete the required steps.
The collaboration also emphasizes operational safeguards and human oversight. AI agents will operate with defined access restrictions, detailed activity logs and mandatory staff review requirements. These controls are intended to help healthcare organizations evaluate autonomous AI while maintaining accountability and visibility over sensitive administrative processes.
The initiative follows Tennr's continued investment in healthcare automation. In 2025, the company raised $101 million in Series C funding at a $605 million valuation, supporting the expansion of its referral management technology. The partnership with OpenAI represents another step toward applying AI agents to more complex administrative tasks across healthcare organizations.
OpenAI has also established partnerships with other healthcare companies, including Abridge, Oscar Health and Color Health, to explore applications of AI in clinical workflows, insurance operations and cancer care. The Tennr collaboration extends this work into prior authorization, where reliable automation could help reduce manual processing and improve the speed of patient access to treatment.
The pilot remains an evaluation, and the companies have not yet reported measurable improvements in authorization turnaround times or administrative costs.

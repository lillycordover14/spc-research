Cardano
 (
CRYPTO:ADA
) has seen a 95.5% decline in value since reaching its all-time high of $3.09 in September 2021. ADA is trading at around 14 cents after a 38.7% drop in the past month. This price is just 1.4% above the June low of 13.8 cents, leading many holders to question whether the coin is finished.

In discussions about ADA’s price, three key benchmarks continually emerge: 25 cents, 50 cents, and the former $1 mark. Each target would require a different influx of new investment. So, is Cardano truly dead, or is there a path to recovery by 2028?

Is Cardano Dead? Still Active but Struggling

Despite its challenges, Cardano still holds a market value of approximately $5.3 billion, ranking 18th among all cryptocurrencies. In the past 24 hours, about $300 million worth of ADA traded, indicating the token still has an active market.

However, ADA’s price trend has been significantly weaker than the overall market. In the past year alone, ADA has plummeted 
64.8%, while 
Bitcoin
 (
CRYPTO:BTC
) has dropped only 28.1% and 
Ethereum
 (
CRYPTO:ETH
) has declined
 37.8%. Cardano was already one of the weaker large-cap coins when it lost over 20% in October, as our look at 
Cardano, Hedera and Avalanche
 covered.

Price alone isn’t enough to determine if Cardano is finished. Network metrics such as active addresses, daily transactions, total value locked (TVL), and developer activity are essential to understanding its ongoing viability. While ADA’s price and trading volume suggest it is struggling, it remains actively traded.

With approximately 37.6 billion ADA in circulation and a hard cap of 45 billion tokens, each target becomes harder to reach. As more tokens enter circulation, the same $1 price that implies a market value of $37.6 billion today could require $45 billion at full supply.

ADA has achieved these levels before in bullish markets, but in a weak market, they seem far out of reach. The last time ADA traded above $1 was August 2025, when it peaked at $1.02. It last cleared the 50-cent mark in November 2025 and traded as high as 27 cents on October 5, making 25 cents a recent and achievable goal.

Three Key Events That Could Decide Cardano’s Future

CME Futures Now Have a Market Maker

CME Group
 (
NASDAQ:CME
 | 
CME Price Prediction
) lists Cardano and Micro Cardano futures, regulated contracts allowing institutions to gain exposure to ADA’s price without directly holding the token. A crypto trading firm, GSR, is providing continuous buy and sell quotes in both contracts, according to the Cardano Foundation. While this aims to improve liquidity, it hasn’t stopped the recent sell-off.

DJED Stablecoin Shuts Down on December 31

DJED is a Cardano stablecoin intended to maintain a value of $1 and relies on a reserve token called SHEN. Both will wind down at 11:59 p.m. UTC on December 31, according to the DJED team’s post on X, and holders need to redeem their tokens before then. Losing a native stablecoin will limit a key tool for trading and lending in Cardano’s ecosystem, which could hinder a rebound.

Bitcoin Needs to Lead the Recovery

Trading at around $59,025, Bitcoin has held up better than Cardano throughout the past year. A significant rally can substantially benefit ADA, as seen in November 2024, when ADA surged from 34 cents at the start of the month to $1.08 by its end. 
For ADA to recover, Bitcoin’s price will likely need to lead the charge, as our look at 
whether Cardano could recover in 2027
 and 
Cardano versus XRP under $1
 explains.

Can Cardano Bounce Back by 2028?

While Cardano isn’t dead, a return to $1 by 2028 seems unlikely, and a more realistic target is 25 cents. The first step is ADA reclaiming its 20-day moving average near 17 cents, a rise of about 21%, then its 200-day and 50-day averages at 20 and 21 cents. 
Closing a month above 25 cents could bring 50 cents into play, as our look at 
a return to $1
 and 
$1,000 in ADA
 shows.

Conversely, if Bitcoin remains weak and ADA closes a month below its June low of 13.8 cents, even 25 cents could become a distant goal. However, if ADA stays above that low through DJED’s shutdown and reclaims 21 cents in early 2027, the climb toward 25 cents by 2028 becomes more plausible.

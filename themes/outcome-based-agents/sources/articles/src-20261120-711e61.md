A year ago, I wrote a 
cover story
 for 
D Magazine
 about the Mushroom House, aka the Workman House, at 4200 Armstrong Pkwy. The house had been purchased by Lisa and Clay Cooley, the car salesman. The couple had also bought an adjacent lot. As expected, they 
demolished
 the Mushroom House after our story published, in preparation for what is to come. Well, the couple has shopped for a builder first. They’ve shopped for a builder last. And either way, I came to see Clay’s building permits by filing an open records request with the town of Highland Park. For part of my request, the town has appealed to the AG’s office for a decision on whether they have to cough up the info. That will take another 45 business days. So let’s dive in to what we’ve got so far.
The builder is 
Tommy Ford Construction
, and the owner’s rep is Jimmy Rupple of 
Pritchard Associates
. Name check!
The main house includes 
19,308 square feet
 of conditioned space, occupying two floors. That’s according to the energy-code report the builder filed in July. But wait. There’s also a three-car conditioned garage that has a bedroom, suggesting an ADU. That adds another 1,143 square feet.
Yes, there is a 
basement
 in the main house, but it won’t be conditioned, so the square footage there doesn’t count toward the total. Curious. Me? I’d finish out that basement and put a ping-pong table in there. Maybe a dart board.
Let’s talk juice. The Cooley house will have a 
1,200-amp electrical service
 (where a typical house might draw 200). It’ll have 568 circuits (215 power and 353 lighting) and 864 light fixtures using Lutron Ketra tunable LED lights on a Lutron HomeWorks control system. I don’t know what that is, but it sounds baller.
Putting the “cool” in Cooley, the house will have a geothermal HVAC system with nine ground-source heat pumps, accounting for about 
35 tons of capacity
. My own personal unit, if we’re talking HVAC, is 5 tons.
Stairs require too much stepping. The Cooley house will have an 
elevator
. The documents I got don’t indicate whether that elevator will have a full-time operator, but I assume it will. “First floor: Hyundais and Kias! Second floor: 
Chase Cooley’s aggressive facial hair
!”
Fun fact: the house will be engineered on 
drilled piers
, the sort normally used in a commercial building. They require special inspections. Drill, baby, drill!
The Mushroom House was odd, but it wasn’t really all that big. So this place needed 
variances
. On April 8, 2026, the Board of Adjustment voted 5-0 to allow an 8-foot wall along the Bordeaux Avenue frontage and part of Armstrong Parkway. It also let the house extend 12 feet into the required 43-foot side setback. Both approvals depend on the property being replatted, because the Cooleys are combining two lots.
If you drive down Preston on the regular, you’ll have plenty of time to watch this project round into shape. The permit plans for 24 months, but as anyone who has ever built or remodeled can tell you, things always take longer than they’re supposed to. I am hereby establishing the official over-under at 34 months. That puts us at Wednesday, 
September 19, 2029
. Place your bets.
Speaking of money, how much is all this going to cost? The documents I got don’t reveal a total construction budget, which is a shame. But we can make an educated guess. First, I did get the electrical contract, whose permitted value is $637,311. Typically, electrical might run you 6 percent of your total construction, according to a 2024 cost survey by the National Association of Home Builders. But on a project like this, where the fancy millwork, stonework, and other finishes cost more than usual, your electrical might run only about 3 percent of your total. That puts us at about $21 million. That figure also checks out if you assume construction will cost about $1,000 per square foot, not unreasonable when you’re talking about a house with an elevator and drilled piers and that baller Ketra lighting. Throw in the cost of the land, and the Cooleys will blow past 
$40 million
 real quick.
I’ll wrap this up with a thank-you and a warning to the Cooleys. First, the thank-you: your dream home, depending on how things shake out and whether you homestead this place as your primary residence, will throw off about $500,000 per year in property taxes. We all appreciate that.
Now the warning: Americans’ auto debt and defaults are at 
historic highs
. I know the privately owned Cooley Auto Group doesn’t itself make loans. But, as you must know, you’re still exposed: lost commissions on loans that go bad, refunds on add-on products, fewer subprime buyers able to afford cars, and lower used-car prices when repossessed rides flood the market. So, please, for everyone’s sake, I hope you’re putting all this construction on a credit card. At least then, if things do go pear shaped and you’re forced to sell the property to Andy Beal for pennies on the dollar, you’ll still have the miles.

Arizona celebrates the Wildcats’ 62-55 comeback win over New Mexico State (Josiah Lopez/AllSportsTucson) 

Coming off of an overtime win against Oregon State on Sunday night, Arizona was determined to get the win over New Mexico State in regulation. 

That wasn’t an easy task as New Mexico State gave the ‘Cats all they could handle on both sides of the ball. A late rally by Arizona secured a 62-55 win in McKale Center at ALKEME Arena on Thursday night.

“It’s about just finding a way to get it done, and we did that today,” Arizona head coach 
Becky Burke
 said. 

“Not pretty by any means. An ugly, grind-it-out game, but I think this was really good for us to to be down, to have faced adversity, for it to be low scoring and to still grind it out and win. Every game’s had a different identity. Every game’s kind of had a different story, and this one’s no different. But that’s a really good basketball team. I think he came in and built an entire new roster from overseas, and they’re big and they’re physical and they play well together.”

All but one player on the New Mexico State roster is from overseas. Twelve from France, one from Serbia, one from Latvia and one local player from Las Cruces, N.M. Their coach 
Adeniyi Amadou
 is originally from Paris, France.

“We probably won’t be scheduling them next year,” Burke jokingly said.

Arizona held a lead of only 15-14 at the end of the first quarter.

New Mexico State pulled away in the second quarter to a go ahead by 28-19 before Arizona went on a 9-0 run to tie the game with 3:15 left in the half. 

The Wildcats went into the locker room at half time trailing by three.

On her message to the team at halftime, Burke said, “A character check and a competitiveness check.”

“Like, you need to come out for the next 20 minutes and decide how competitive you want to be, how spicy you want to be, how tough you want to be,” she added. “I mean, they beat us in every toughness category the entire first half, and I thought we beat them in the toughness categories in the second half.”

To start the second half, the two teams traded baskets both ending with 13 points in the third quarter. 

Arizona switched up the lineup to start the fourth quarter going with two bigs, 
Gerda Raulusaityte
 and 
Breanna Williams
. That combo wasn’t giving the Wildcats the outcome they wanted so when Williams was assessed her fourth foul Burke subbed in 
Priyanka Ponnam
 for Williams. 

Ponnam’s presence wasn’t felt in points scored, but the freshman added size that caused chaos for the Aggies offense. She added one steal and one rebound in the seven minutes she was on the court. 

“I thought Pri came in, and you have to guard Pri on the 3-point line, and I think that’s something that was a little bit different, and that was our reasoning in bringing her in because they had a great game plan.” Burke Said. 

“They tried to take us out of transition, they tried to pack the paint, and they did that to an extent, but obviously we were able to execute when it mattered.”

With Arizona down one point and three minutes left in the game 
Puff Morris
 threw up a long three as the shot clock was running out. The shot banked in to give the ‘Cats a two-point lead. 

Arizona guard Puff Morris banks a long three as time runs out on the shot clock
 
(Josiah Lopez/AllSportsTucson)

On New Mexico State’s next offensive possession, Morris stole the ball and took it to the hoop for a layup, putting Arizona up 57-53 with 2:54 left in the game.

“Gerda was hedging really well so distracting the ball handler and I was able to come behind her a few times,” Morris said on her opportunity to get the steal in waning minutes of the fourth quarter.

Morris led the team in scoring with 17 points on 6-of-10 shooting. Ten of those points came in the fourth quarter. She also had three rebounds, three steals and one assist.

With the game clock at under 40 seconds the Aggies started fouling. Once again, Arizona hit crucial free throws down the stretch including two by 
Makayla Presser-Palmer
 with 31.2 seconds remaining that gave the Wildcats a 61-55 advantage and essentially secured the win for Arizona.

Arizona shot 88% going 21 for 24 from the charity stripe with ten of those free throws coming in the fourth quarter.

“We’ve been shooting a lot of free throws, like a lot of reps a day,” Morris said. “But just today before the game, that was one of our goals and something that we wanted to pay attention to going into the game. So, I think that’s something that was mindful and we executed.”

McKale effect:

“I feel like it gets me turned up and gets my teammates turned up,” Presser-Palmer said on the McKale crowd’s effect on the team.

“I think it’s like, honestly crazy like I get more excited, I’m not tired anymore apparently.” Morris said. “It’s like something different at McKale.” 

Stats roundup:
 

Arizona’s defense created 19 turnovers resulting in 21 points.

New Mexico State outrebounded Arizona 40-22.

Presser-Palmer was the only other Wildcat in double figures. The freshman ended with 11 points, all in the second half. 
She added four rebounds and a steal in 30 minutes of play.

Raulusaityte led the team in rebounds with five and had nine points and two steals. 

Arizona (4-1) next heads to Jacksonville, Fla., to face No. 11 Vanderbilt in the Scout Jacksonville Classic on Monday. Tip is set for 3 p.m., Tucson time.

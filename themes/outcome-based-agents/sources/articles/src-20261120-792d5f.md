SAVANNAH, Ga. (WTOC) - Hospice Savannah said they received $295,000 from Congressional directed spending to help fund accessible transportation for participants in Georgia’s new PACE program.
RELATED COVERAGE: 
Hospice Savannah opens first of its kind PACE program in Georgia
PACE is Georgia’s first program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly, according to Hospice Savannah. The model is designed to help older adults stay at home instead of a nursing home for as long as possible. 
Officials said the funding will will help provide accessible transportation for PACE participants between their homes, the Kole Center for PACE Georgia, and healthcare and wellness appointments included in their individualized plans of care. 
“For our PACE participants, these vehicles will provide much more than a ride. They will be a vital connection between home, healthcare, wellness and the community, helping older adults remain where they want to be: at home,” said Dr. Katheen Benton, CEO of Hospice Savannah. 
Pace will use Ride-Well Mobility to help transport their patients, according to Officials. Savannah Hospice thanked Senators Reverend Raphael Warnock and Jon Ossoff for their help in passing the funding.

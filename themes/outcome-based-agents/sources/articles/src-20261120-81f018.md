Sidney Crosby's agent issues vague statement on future plans amid Penguins rebuild

            By 
Adam Gretz

           | 
        Last updated Nov 19, 2026 7:28 PM ET
  

    Add us as a preferred source on Google
  

Pittsburgh Penguins
 general manager Kyle Dubas addressed his team's slow start on Wednesday and very 
strongly indicated
 that if the current roster does not turn things around, moves are going to have to be made and that they will be future-oriented. It is the next natural step in a rebuild that has already started, but it also brought up some questions regarding the future of captain Sidney Crosby.
Crosby is in the final year of his contract and has yet to re-sign beyond this season. With the playoffs looking like an increasingly long shot, it's created speculation on whether or not Crosby would want to play elsewhere for one more shot at winning,

Dubas said that as long as Crosby wants to play for the Penguins, they are happy to have him do that. But he also strongly suggested that if Crosby wanted to retire after the season, or play somewhere else with a better chance to win, they would respect it.
On Thursday, Crosby's agent, Pat Brisson, issued a vague statement on Crosby's future.

Said Brisson, via NHL insider Pierre LeBrun: “Sidney has been in Pittsburgh for over 20 years and he isn’t in any rush to make a decision. Kyle will do what he feels is best for the organization in the meantime.”

Pens GM Kyle Dubas told media Wednesday that it will be up to Sidney Crosby what he wants to do as far as his future, a respectful stance he has maintained on the iconic captain.

The idea of Crosby playing for another franchise has been almost unimaginable given what he has meant to the Penguins, how important it has been for him to play for only one team, and how much success both he and the team have had together.
From 2006-07 to 2021-22 the Penguins were the most successful organization in the NHL, reaching four Stanley Cup Finals and winning three of them with Crosby as their captain. Individually, he has been one of the best players ever and more than exceeded the hype that accompanied him into the NHL at the start of the 2005-06 season. 
The past few years, however, have been a little tougher as it relates to team success as the Penguins' core, including Crosby, has become older. The Penguins have made the playoffs just one time over the past four years and are likely to miss again this season. 

Given the lack of an extension, and the lack of winning, it has certainly sparked some trade speculation. 
But until Crosby actually asks for a trade, or says he wants to play elsewhere, there is no way the Penguins are going to move him. 
All of this does, however, create another possibility — what if he just retires after this season?
Crosby is 39 years old, has accomplished everything there is to accomplish in the sport, on both a team and individual level, as well as an NHL and international level, and has nothing left to prove. He has also, very clearly, 
started to slow down and is on pace for the worst offensive seasons of his career.
Crosby is a lot of greats things as a player and ambassador for the league, but two things he does not seem to be are: 1) A player who will keep playing just for the sake of playing if he cannot reach the level he expects of himself, and 2) the type of person who would want a season-long farewell tour. He seems like the type of player who, when it comes to retiring, he is just going to do it without much build-up or fanfare.
That is, of course, just speculative at this point, and maybe a strong second half that gets the Penguins back into the playoff race changes that, but it certainly could be on the table. If nothing else, it still seems more likely than him putting on another team's uniform.

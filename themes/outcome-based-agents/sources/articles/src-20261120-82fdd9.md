ADA-backed legislation would expand FSA, HSA eligibility for dental service arrangements

by 
Olivia Anderson

November 20, 2026

 
 

 

The American Dental Association is supporting new bipartisan legislation in the U.S. House of Representatives that would allow certain noninsurance dental service arrangements to qualify for tax-advantaged health savings accounts and flexible spending accounts.

Introduced Nov. 20, the Dental Freedom and Tax Savings Act of 2026 is sponsored by Rep. Blake Moore, R-Utah, and Rep. Mike Thompson, D-Calif. The legislation is intended to clarify the tax treatment of dental service arrangements that operate outside traditional dental insurance.

Under the proposal, qualifying dental service arrangements would be eligible for FSA and HSA funds. These arrangements generally involve patients paying a fixed periodic fee for access to dental services and do not involve insurance underwriting or risk pooling, according to the ADA.

This legislation follows congressional action clarifying FSA and HSA eligibility for direct primary care arrangements, including certain in-office medical plans. The ADA emphasized that dental service arrangements operate similarly but are not afforded the same tax benefits.

The proposal also would allow employers to reimburse employees for qualifying dental service arrangements on a tax-free basis without purchasing group dental insurance or assuming insurance risk, benefiting patients and small businesses around the country. 

The ADA expressed support for the legislation as a way to expand the use of tax-advantaged funds for dental care and provide additional flexibility for patients, dental practices and employers.

The organization believes that this expansion will help patients more easily afford care while incentivizing long-lasting relationships with dentists in their communities. The bill is also likely to increase the number of dental offices offering these direct arrangements as they become more attractive to patients. 

The ADA emphasized that in-office dental plans offer a promising alternative that can help patients access and pay for care in a way that may better meet their needs. This bill would expand the ability to use tax-advantaged accounts to pay for these direct dental care arrangements, potentially making them an even more attractive option for patients who might lack employer dental benefits and are looking for an affordable option with a true benefit. Increased premiums and reduced reimbursement rates have led to a greater cost-shift for dental care to the patient. In-office dental plan arrangements offer a sound financial alternative, according to the Association. 

“As dentists, we’re seeing patients look for more affordable ways to get the care they need as insurance premiums rise and benefits become more limited. In-office dental plans can help bridge that gap by making care more accessible,” said ADA President Thomas Paumier, D.D.S. “The Dental Freedom and Tax Savings Act would give patients greater flexibility to use their own healthcare dollars to pay for these plans and invest in their oral health without facing interference by an insurance company.”

Interest in these plans is growing, according to the ADA Health Policy Institute. Nearly one-third, or 31.5%, of dental practices already offer in-office dental plans, and another 16.9% are considering doing so. Of the practices that offer them, nearly three-quarters, or 74.6%, administer the plans themselves, giving practices greater control over how they structure and deliver these savings opportunities to patients.

The bill would not establish a new federal spending program but instead clarify the eligibility of certain dental service arrangements under existing tax-advantaged account rules.

The team from the Free-Space Optical Communications Laboratory (FSOC), integrated into the CELESTE project, presented in Sorrento the results of its adaptive optics tests and atmospheric turbulence characterisation work within the Spanish GEO-QKD project.
The 
Instituto de Astrofísica de Canarias (IAC)
 participated in the 26th edition of the 
International Conference on Space Optics (ICSO)
, held from 12 to 16 October 2026 in Sorrento, Italy, one of the leading international meetings dedicated to optical, optoelectronic and photonic technologies for space applications.
The IAC was represented by 
Nauzet Bethencourt de León, Raúl Key Sánchez and Elena Reyes Rodríguez
, members of the 
Laboratorio de Comunicaciones Ópticas en Espacio Libre (FSOC)
, which forms part of the 
CELESTE
 project and works on the development of new technologies for transmitting information through laser links between terrestrial and space-based platforms.
During the conference, Elena Reyes presented the group’s latest advances in the talk 
“The Crucial Role of Adaptive Optics in Spanish GEO-QKD Free-Space Optical Links”
, focused on the role of adaptive optics in the development of future quantum communication systems between Earth and satellites in geostationary orbit.
The presentation covered the results of adaptive optics tests carried out in the laboratory and atmospheric turbulence measurements obtained during campaigns conducted over the optical link between La Palma and Tenerife within the framework of the 
Spanish GEO-QKD
 project, led by 
Thales Alenia Space and Hispasat
.
“The atmosphere introduces distortions that degrade the quality of optical links, just as it does in astronomical observations, and this effect is especially critical when working with quantum communications, where maximising the received signal is essential. Adaptive optics allows us to measure and compensate for these disturbances, contributing to the development of systems capable of operating under real-world conditions,” explains 
Elena Reyes Rodríguez, researcher at the Laboratorio de Comunicaciones Ópticas en Espacio Libre (FSOC) of the IAC
.
Correcting the effects of the atmosphere
Free-space optical communications use beams of light to transmit information between distant points. When these beams travel through the Earth’s atmosphere, variations in air temperature and density cause turbulence that can distort the signal and make it more difficult to receive.
Adaptive optics
 makes it possible to measure wavefront distortions and compensate for them almost in real time, a capability that is particularly important when working with extremely weak signals, such as those used in quantum communication systems.
In this type of link, 
quantum key distribution (QKD)
 makes it possible to generate and share cryptographic keys by taking advantage of the properties of quantum physics. The 
Spanish GEO-QKD
 project is developing a system capable of carrying out this process using a platform located in geostationary orbit.
To move towards this goal, it is necessary to understand precisely how the atmosphere affects the propagation of light and to verify that correction systems can operate under conditions representative of a real link.
The Canary Islands as a validation environment
The tests carried out between the observatories in 
La Palma and Tenerife
 make it possible to study these technologies over a long-distance optical link of 
144 km
 under real atmospheric conditions.
The results presented at 
ICSO 2026
 provide information on the turbulence encountered along this link and make it possible to evaluate the performance of the adaptive optics systems responsible for compensating for its effects.
This work connects laboratory testing with the validation of technologies under real-world conditions, a necessary step towards their future use in optical and quantum communication links with satellites.
Optical communications from the Canary Islands
The 
Laboratorio de Comunicaciones Ópticas en Espacio Libre (FSOC)
 forms part of 
CELESTE (Cutting Edge Leap to Excellence in Space and Optics Technologies)
, an initiative led by the Instituto de Astrofísica de Canarias to strengthen the Canary Islands’ capabilities in advanced optical technologies and space.
Within this framework, FSOC develops technologies related to optical and quantum communications, adaptive optics, and Earth-to-space and space-to-Earth links, combining laboratory work with the validation of systems under real atmospheric conditions.
“Participating in ICSO allows us to share the results we are obtaining in the Canary Islands with the international community and, at the same time, to learn first-hand how optical technologies for space are evolving. It is also an opportunity to compare our work with that of other groups and open up new avenues for collaboration,” says 
Elena Reyes Rodríguez
.
The group’s participation in ICSO 2026 provided an opportunity to present these advances to the international scientific and technological community, exchange knowledge with specialists in the field and learn about new developments in an area that will play an increasingly important role in future space communication infrastructures.
The IAC’s presence at this event also helps to strengthen, within the framework of 
CELESTE
, the capabilities developed in the Canary Islands for the testing and validation of optical and quantum communication technologies, as well as the Institute’s participation in national and international projects focused on future space communications.

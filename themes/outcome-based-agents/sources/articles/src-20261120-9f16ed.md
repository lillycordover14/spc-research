With news that Dylan Harper will be out for roughly two to three weeks, the San Antonio Spurs potentially caught a break. Speculation surrounding a potential groin injury had him missing up to six weeks.

Dylan Harper's injury is a left hip external rotator strain. Much better diagnosis than a groin. Should miss two to three weeks but could return the first week of December.

Fortunately, the Spurs may have dodged a bullet in terms of severity, but there may be another big silver lining. If Harper were to miss two weeks, he may be back in enough time for the NBA Cup knockout rounds, which begin on December 4th.

Assuming the Spurs make it to the knockout rounds, they will need him, and there is at least a chance he plays. In the worst-case scenario, he misses the first two games of the knockout rounds but could be available for the NBA Cup Finals.

The Spurs dodged a bullet with Dylan Harper injury news

The Spurs' schedule gets a bit tougher heading into December, though Harper's absence could give other players a chance to find their rhythm. De'Aaron Fox and Devin Vassell, I'm looking at you two. 

Fox hasn't lived up to expectations as a scorer thus far, averaging just 12.8 points per game. Here's hoping that he can average closer to 20 points in Harper's absence. Vassell has at least been aggressive offensively, though his shot hasn't been falling. 

He at least had a strong game, scoring 15 points against the L.A. Clippers, playing without Harper. Get those two players going, have 
Tobias Harris make his Spurs debut
, and have Harper return prior to the NBA Cup knockout rounds, and we should be in a great position.

Spurs' path to the NBA Cup Finals depends on Harper's return

Before the Spurs get to that point, they first needed to take care of business against the Portland Trail Blazers on November 25th and the Los Angeles Lakers on November 27th. 

They are currently 2-0 in group play with a 53-point differential. That puts them in a great position to make it past the NBA Cup qualifiers. Realistically, they need to at least split their remaining NBA Cup qualifying games.

The Spurs will also need the Minnesota Timberwolves and/or the Dallas Mavericks to lose at least one of their remaining qualifying games. That would help the Silver and Black secure a spot in the NBA Cup knockout rounds.

Having Harper back is crucial to those plans, with him being San Antonio's second-leading scorer this season. Not having Harper could dramatically impact their chances. Luckily, the latest injury update suggests he'll hopefully be back at full strength if and when the Spurs get that far.

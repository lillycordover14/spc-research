VMI Wins Keydet Invite, Clay Posts 51.93 100 Breaststroke&nbsp;&nbsp;SwimSwam

[page text]
The Keydet Invitational was a battle between two programs who know each other very well. Virginia Military Institute (VMI) won the combined meet over Le Moyne College, where 
Kangaroo’s head coach Adam Zaczkowski
 led the program for four seasons. Alongside the friendly competition, Le Moyne Junior 
Lucas Clay 
made headlines with strong national performances in the breaststroke races.

Men’s Meet 

Le Moyne’s 
Lucas Clay
 starred at this meet for the Dolphins, part of six event wins while setting best times across the board. Notably, Clay swam 23.88 in the 50 breaststroke and 51.93 in the 100 to dominate both races. The 50 ranks 4th nationally and the 100 slots in 9th. It took a 
51.51 to qualify for the 2025 NCAA Championships
, so he is in the hunt to swim in Austin next March. His previous 100 breast PB was the
 53.58 he swam to set the conference record
 and win the Northeast title last February. His 1:56.03 in the 200 breaststroke was also a best time by two and a half seconds. Across the weekend, his best performances arguably came on relays, where he set best times in the 50 freestyle (20.69) and 100 freestyle (45.15), while adding a 23.08 breaststroke split on the medley relay.

This is a season of change for the Le Moyne program. They recently named 
Abbey Poore the program’s new head coach in September
 after she served two seasons in an assistant role. Le Moyne also 
announced in October
 of this year that it would be leaving the Northeast Conference (NEC) for the Metro Conference (MAAC) effective July 1, 2027. As previously reported, Le Moyne began its transition to Division I in July 2023 and completed its accreditation in June of this year. The Northeast Athletic Conference awarded its first men’s team title in 2021.

VMI sophomore 
Ian Lutz 
helped his team win the men’s meet and the overall tally with wins in the 50 free (20.23) 100 free (45.17) and the 50 and 100 butterfly (21.56/48.38). His 20.68 butterfly split on the 200 medley relay was significantly faster than his flat start 50. Depth carried VMI to victory alongside strong individual performances, placing four in the top five of the 200 butterfly and the 400 IM. Freshman 
Elijah Tong
 led the charge in both of those races, posting 1:48.72 and 4:03.26 to win comfortably. Junior 
Jack Mills
 also made an impact, winning the 200 free (1:39.55), 500 free (4:34.10), and 200 back (1:48.10). 
Aubrey Struder 
emerged victorious in the 1650, opening up nearly a ten second lead over Le Moyne’s 
Jack Mummery
 at the 500 mark and maintaining an eight second lead at the 1,000. Mummery started adding in sub-30 second splits late to close the gap, but Struder prevailed 16.23.53 to 16:25.74.

Division III University of Mary Washington was also in attendance, getting event wins from sophomore 
Tucker Burkey 
in the 200 IM (1:52.52) and junior 
Sam Roundtree
 in the 100 IM (50.61).

Division II Converse University’s best showing came from 
Jay Ownens
, placing second in the 200 and 400 IMs. (1:54.47/4:07.41).

Of note, Le Moyne was without long course Junior National Champion 
Neil Adamski
, who won the 50 breast (27.89) and placed 2nd in the 100 (1:01.81) in Irvine this summer.

Women’s Meet

Senior sprinter 
Kayleigh Bigwarfe 
was key to Le Moyne’s victory in the women’s meet by sweeping the 50 (23.63), 100 (51.63), and 200 freestyle (1:54.61). 
The 50 and 100 marked new school records
 which had stood for a decade. Her improvement has been rapid this year, as her prior quickest swims in the sprints (24.27/53.29) were done in February at the Northeast Conference Championships. In the 200 free, her final 50 split of 29.76 was enough to pass Mary Washington’s 
Spera Moy-Jacobs 
and win by a tenth. The next three swimmers were from Mary Washington.  Le Moyne also won the 800 freestyle and 400 medley relays. Bigwarfe’s anchor in the 200 freestyle relay (22.94) was the fastest split in the entire field by half a second.

Le Moyne teammate 
Madeleine O’Dea 
captured the 50 and 200 breaststrokes (30.05/2:23.89) in new season best times while placing second in the 100. She was runner-up in the 200 and placed third in the 100 at last year’s conference championships. O’Dea was in a tight battle with Mary Washington’s 
Soleil Clifford 
in the 100. Clifford used an excellent closing 50 to win in 1:05.08.

The meet saw one other individual triple event winner. VMI junior 
Mackenzie Byrne
 won the 50 fly (25.48), 100 fly (56.53), and 100 IM (1:00.63). The 100 marked her first time under the 57-second barrier. The VMI butterfly group showed up at this meet, taking three of the top four spots in the 100 and 200 fly. 
Carissa Witte 
captured the 500 (5:12.10) and 1650 (17:50.18) for VMI as well.

Sophomore 
Christina Korkos
 emerged victorious in the 50 and 100 backstrokes for Mary Washington and her time from the prelims (56.62) should put her on the bubble to qualify for the Division III NCAA Championships. It took a 56.34 to 
earn an invite last year
.

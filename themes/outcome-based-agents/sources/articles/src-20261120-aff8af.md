Citing concerns over commercial activities at the beach, the La Jolla Shores Association nixed a proposal to host an artisan market at Kellogg Park next fall, suggesting the fair be held elsewhere in the area.

At the LJSA’s Nov. 18 meeting,
 
Jackalope
 founders Melissa Kohout and Sara Diedrich explained they hold 12 years of experience running independent artisan fairs in cities such as Burbank, Pasadena, Santa Barbara and San Diego. The intent is to showcase small businesses and local artists that offer handmade goods. 

And after a recent visit to Kellogg Park, Kohout said the group “fell in love” with the area and thought it would make for a great fit for an event. Jackalope events are family-friendly and free to the public, but come with a $375 booth fee for each vendor.

That became a sticking point for the board.

“We have surf days for kids and things that are (from) volunteer organizations,” board member John Shannon said. “This is something which is much more commercial, and this is where we’ve had to draw the line in the past. It’s because once we allow something like this into the park, then it ends up giving other people the green light to come in. This isn’t just a thing we see at Kellogg Park. You see it at Scripps Park (near La Jolla Cove). And these beach-facing parks are so small, so limited, and there’s so many groups that want to use them for a wide variety of things.”

Board member Mary Munk contrasted the proposal to
 
Walter Munk Day
, which she runs at Kellogg Park every year in honor of her late husband with the Walter Munk Foundation for the Oceans.

“I think what you’re trying to do sounds really nice, but (for) ours we don’t charge exhibitors anything — we provide everything, and everyone who attends doesn’t pay anything,” Munk said. 

A projected map of an artisan fair at Kellogg Park is shown by co-presenters Melissa Kohout and Sara Diedrich at the Nov. 18 La Jolla Shores Association meeting. (Noah Lyons)

Acknowledging the for-profit nature of their business, Kohout said, “All artists that work with us are all small businesses. Yes, for Sara and myself this is our company, this is our baby. But it’s just Sara, myself and our families. … So yes, I see that we are for-profit in that aspect, but we’re not out to get people’s money. We just want these makers to get their handmade items out and sell some work and make a living and bring out the family.”
 

Thus, Shannon suggested they relocate the fair to Avenida de la Playa, portions of which are closed to vehicle traffic to accommodate outdoor dining, where a street closure may be “more suitable” than hosting the event at the park.

“Maybe the right venue is Avenida de la Playa, where you … bring in people that patronize the restaurants as well, rather than taking up the real estate of the park and displacing people there,” he said.

Munk and fellow board member Kathleen Neil agreed.

“I think it would be a win-win for you,” Neil said. “You may not be able to make it as large as what you’d like … but October would be a perfect time to go for a street closure there.” She added that Laureate Park on that street could be “an ideal grass area for your headquarters.”
 

Kohout said they did previously evaluate Avenida de la Playa, but the two-block stretch in question may “not be big enough for what we’re trying to do,” she said. 

Still, Kohout agreed to look into Avenida de la Playa further as an option for hosting their event.

Rather than rejecting the original proposal to host the event at Kellogg Park, the LJSA board voted on a modified motion to support the event should it take place on Avenida de la Playa.

The final vote passed, 6-1, with Avenida de la Playa business owner and board member Terry Kraszewski opposed. Kraszewski, who was attending the meeting via Zoom, told
 
La Jolla Light after the meeting that their profit-driven event model and competition with local vendors “doesn’t feel right to me unless it’s an event that is a nonprofit or is for the community.”

Next

Tencent Stocks Slip 1.26% as Sixfold Capex Jump Pushes Cash Flow Negative
Less than 1 min read
700
Tencent Holdings (TCEHY) slipped 1.26% to HK$344 at the November 20 regular-session close in Hong Kong, extending a slide that took the shares to their lowest level since May 2024 the day before. The trigger was a third-quarter report that missed on revenue and showed AI spending racing ahead of returns.
Revenue rose 8% to RMB207.3 billion, short of the roughly RMB210.8 billion LSEG consensus. Marketing services grew 19% and cloud revenue climbed 35%. But non-IFRS operating profit fell 6% to RMB68.1 billion, and the margin shrank to 33% from 38%. Capital expenditure jumped to RMB81.4 billion from RMB13.0 billion a year earlier, and free cash flow was negative RMB69.1 billion. Excluding compute prepayments, free cash flow would have been a positive RMB16.9 billion. Tencent still holds RMB525.7 billion in cash and bought back about HK$7.0 billion of shares in the quarter.
Capex now equals about 39% of quarterly revenue. The GuruFocus chart shows a GF Score of 93/100, built on strong profitability and decent momentum, with the balance sheet the softer spot as spending ramps. GF Value is HK$560.37, a 38.6% discount to the close, which GuruFocus rates significantly undervalued. The discount is real, but it rests on margins Tencent has just chosen to sacrifice. The next test is whether cloud and WorkBuddy revenue grows fast enough to turn compute prepayments back into positive cash flow.

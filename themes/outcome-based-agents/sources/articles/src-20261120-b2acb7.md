Dimitri Masin, Founder and CEO of Gradient Labs, says AI moves so fast that fintechs and banks must ask if their product is obsolete every three months now
The next wave of AI in financial services will not be defined by chatbots or copilots, argued Dimitri Masin, Founder and CEO of Gradient Labs.

Instead, he believes it will be defined by customer-facing agents that can resolve complex issues, coordinate back-office work and remove the friction that has long made banking feel slow.

Speaking to 
FinTech Magazine
 at Money20/20 USA, Dimitri explains that Gradient Labs works with banks and leading fintechs to automate customer operations. 
Its focus, he says, is helping firms turn AI from an experimental tool into a dependable layer of the customer experience.

“We work with some of the most forward-thinking fintech companies to power their vision to create a customer-facing super agent in their apps,” he says.

“We enable them to automate most of their customer operations by creating a platform which enables them to do so very quickly.”

Automating operations

Dimitri says the core challenge facing financial firms is not new – with the challenge being the sheer volume of manual, repetitive work that sits behind everyday customer interactions.
How it impacts the end customer is ultimately the main motivation
Dimitri Masin, Founder and CEO of Gradient Labs
“All the companies that we work with have very heavy customer ops areas,” he explains. “For most banks, more than 50% of their staff are just doing manual, repetitive work over and over again, every day.

“As you scale, the company becomes even more complex, because there are more regulations that you need to comply with and more interactions between different teams – it just becomes a case of operations always being on fire.”

However, Dimitri is clear that operational strain is not simply an internal efficiency problem – it eventually reaches the customer.

“How it impacts the end customer is ultimately the main motivation,” he continues. “We want to enable them and help them – but ultimately we want to create much better customer experiences.”

Why AI ROI stalls

Despite many financial institutions having invested in AI, Dimitri believes too few are seeing clear returns. 

The reason, he argues, is that AI behaves differently from the software firms are used to building.

“Companies are used to building software that is deterministic: you know exactly how it works, you can test it to death and know that it’s going to work out,” he says. “With AI, you now have something which nobody really knows exactly how to deploy.”

That uncertainty is particularly acute in customer-facing use cases, where an AI system must avoid offensive language, identify vulnerable customers, detect complaints and meet regulatory expectations.

“Figuring out how to get those new types of use cases through the governance and risk functions, and defining a process and a way to reason about those things, is the first thing – and it’s just taking quite a bit of time,” Dimitri says.

This has led many firms to begin with copilots: AI that suggests an action but leaves the final decision to a human. 

While that can feel safer, Dimitri says it often fails to deliver measurable value.
Dimitri Masin, Founder and CEO of Gradient Labs, speaking with FinTech Magazine at Money20/20 USA
“Copilot use cases are actually very difficult to measure ROI on because they’re not obviously making you faster,” he says. “Sometimes you create additional mistakes because you didn’t catch something wrong – you start essentially being negligent about what the AI is suggesting to you.
“If it’s correct 90% of the time, then you just start clicking ‘yes, accept, accept, accept’. As a result, the quality degrades, but also – because the human is still in the loop – all the bottlenecks are still there so you don’t see clear time savings.”
Market noise compounds the challenge, Dimitri adds. With dozens of vendors competing in every category, he says choosing the wrong platform can cost firms valuable time.
“There are probably 20 or 30 products for everything, but for every area you would probably have two or three products that work really, really well,” he says. “If you pick the wrong one, you’re essentially putting yourself on the wrong path for at least half a year.”
Successful AI in financial services
So what does successful AI look like in financial services? Dimitri says it is an agent that can do more than answer questions.
“The vision we’re currently working towards with some of our frontier customers is having a customer-facing super agent in their app that can not only answer simple questions, but can also resolve more complex issues around disputes and account reviews on the spot, without waiting for weeks,” he says.
To achieve that, agents must be able to work across the business, following back-office operations and coordinating with other teams. They must also be proactive rather than reactive.
Dimitri explains: “Imagine you start a loan application and you don’t complete it. The agent within the app can proactively help you to get through the rest of the process.
“That’s the vision that many financial services companies have: a super assistant that helps you with your financial life and everything around it that can resolve things instantly.”
He acknowledges that although many firms are still some distance from that outcome, the direction of travel is clear.
Keeping pace with change
The speed of AI development is itself a challenge for companies building in the space. 
Dimitri says Gradient Labs continually questions whether its products are still fit for purpose.
That’s the vision that many financial services companies have: a super assistant that helps you with your financial life and everything around it that can resolve things instantly
Dimitri Masin, Founder and CEO of Gradient Labs
“Every three months you need to ask yourself if your product is obsolete or if something fundamentally better is now possible,” he says.

“Luckily, it hasn’t happened to us yet, but you need to ask yourself that question every three months, which is intense.”

That intensity, however, reflects the scale of the opportunity. 

For Dimitri, the future of AI-powered banking operations converges on one idea: a customer-facing super agent embedded in the app.

“All banks are looking for the customer-facing super agent experience in the app that helps you with your whole financial life, in a nutshell,” he says. “Whenever I talk to any CEO of a bank, it always boils down to that vision. 

“It may be far away and most companies will still struggle to get there, but that’s what everyone’s aiming for.”

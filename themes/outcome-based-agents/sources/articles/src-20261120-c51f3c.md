CLAY COUNTY, Mo. (KCTV) - A motorcyclist who survived a serious crash on Missouri Highway 210 met some of those who helped save his life during a 
Clay County Sheriff’s Office
 awards ceremony. 
Steven Matney was riding with a group on June 13 when his motorcycle began to wobble, the sheriff’s office said. 
He lost control and was struck by an oncoming pickup truck, suffering a severe leg injury and life-threatening blood loss, according to first responders. 
Brandon Smith, who was riding with Matney, pulled over and made a tourniquet from a belt and stick, applying it above the injury. The sheriff’s office said his actions helped slow the bleeding.

Dr. Austin Lehr, a trauma surgeon who was traveling with family to a wedding, saw the crash and stopped. He assessed Matney’s injuries, monitored his vital signs and directed others on how to help, according to the sheriff’s office. 
Deputy Taylor Nave was among the first responders to arrive. He replaced the makeshift tourniquet with a department-issued one to stop the bleeding, the sheriff’s office said. 
Authorities indicated that Lehr also helped emergency medical personnel move Matney onto a gurney and into an ambulance. 
Matney’s leg was amputated at North Kansas City Hospital, but he survived and is getting around well with a prosthetic, according to the sheriff’s office. 
From left to right: Brandon Smith, Steven Matney, Dr. Austin Lehr, Deputy Taylor Nave.
(Aaron Bridge | Clay County, Missouri, Sheriff's Office)
The agency said Matney was eager to attend its recent awards ceremony and meet the people who helped him. 
Sheriff Will Akin presented Nave with a Life-Saving Award and gave Smith and Lehr certificates of appreciation.

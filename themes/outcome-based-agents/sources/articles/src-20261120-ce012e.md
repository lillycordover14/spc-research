S&P 500 Logs Six Straight Losses and Breaks EMA 250: A 30-Year Backtest
The S&P 500 fell 
2.02% to 7,175.83 on Thursday, Nov. 19
, its sixth straight loss, and broke below its 250-day exponential moving average. The Nasdaq swung from 
up 2.1% to down 2.25%
 even as Anthropic closed its IPO debut up 17%. 
All 11 S&P 500 sectors fell
, with 464 stocks down and only 38 up.
What Does the 30-Year Backtest Show?
A 
30-year historical backtest
 found just 
10 prior cases
 where the S&P 500 closed below its 250-day exponential moving average after six straight losses. 
The index was higher three trading days later in 
eight of the 10 cases
. 
By day 10, only 
five
 were above the signal-day close. 
After 60 trading days, 
six
 were up, with a median gain of 
7.37%
. By day 250, 
nine
 were up. 
The 2008 case
 shows how rough the ride could get. The index fell another 
31%
 from the signal-day close before recovering.
Stagflation or Recession?
The market looks closer to pricing a growth scare with persistently high real rates.
 The clearest clue came from Treasuries. The nominal 10-year yield fell 
3 basis points to 5.04%
, while the inflation-adjusted yield held at 
2.88%
. The entire move came from lower inflation compensation, which fell from 
2.19% to 2.16%
. Crude settled roughly flat. That combination offers little evidence of a fresh inflation shock.
How Did Options Make It Worse?
The VIX jumped 
15.8% to 28.44
, above the three-month implied-volatility reading of 
27.03
. Investors were paying up for near-term protection, and estimates suggested dealers were 
short gamma
. 
As the index falls toward that strike, dealers who sold downside puts may need to sell more futures to stay hedged. Rising implied volatility can add to that selling. 
If bids thin out, each round of futures selling can push the index lower and trigger another round of hedging
, spreading pressure across sectors.
What Would Make a Bounce Stick?
A close above the 
250-day EMA near 7,258
 would carry more weight if more stocks joined the rally and short-term volatility eased. 
The bigger test is whether credit stress starts showing up in jobs and earnings.
 Watch 
Nov. 25 PCE
 alongside high-yield spreads. Sticky inflation and wider spreads would raise the stagflation risk. Cooling inflation alongside wider spreads and rising jobless claims would make the recession case stronger.
Keep near-term positions sized so you can withstand another leg down.
 If credit stabilizes and earnings expectations hold, the longer-term record still offers room for optimism.

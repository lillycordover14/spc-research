rabbit has been slowly been rolling out more footwear options. The rabbit Pace Chaser is a lightweight training shoe aimed at performance. The Pace Chaser is intended to perform at a variety of paces from daily efforts to even race day. This will sit more in the performance trainer category and will compete with shoes like the Saucony Endorphin Speed, New Balance SC Rebel, and Nike Zoom Fly.

Performance Summary
David: 
The rabbit Pace Chaser is a shoe that has a really high amount of potential. There are some pretty high highs and some low lows on this model. The highs are mostly situated around the platform, with the lows revolving around the upper.
The ATPU-based midsole provides an excellent blend of cushion and responsiveness. The foam falls right in the middle of soft and firm (with this being firmer than most of the other ATPU foams out there). The bounce is noticeable and gives you more pop with more load that you put into it. This is the type of shoe that I feel comfortable taking it to the track/ road at 5k pace and above. It feels best at uptempo paces, but feels decent enough at easy paces too. This falls right in line with other plated workout shoes on the market. It can go fast, but can also fall into rhythm for longer efforts too.
The Carbon Trax plate is stiff, but the rocker is dialed in pretty well. This is a rocker profile that you will notice, but not one that makes you feel like you are falling off of the front of the shoe. The heel bevel is moderate and does its job pretty well. The forefoot is an early stage rocker that curves up gradually. The platform width is pretty normal throughout. I would like to see the midfoot landing area to be a touch wider though. I can feel my foot pronating over it a small amount. Thankfully the stiffness of the platform saves it there. This is a gradual rocker that’s got some decent pop to it when you push off of the forefoot.
The upper needs some work around the heel and instep. The forefoot and midfoot aren’t too bad. The width throughout the length of the platform is normal. The forefoot has just enough volume for foot swelling. Those needing a lot of space will need to look elsewhere, but the dimensions will work well enough for those that like a more snug offering. The counter has mild flexibility and weaves together with some thicker material. Although it has some flex, it curves forward and can put some pressure into your achilles tendon. I was a little nervous about this on my first run. Although I did feel it some, it did not irritate anything. This has started to break in with more use. I know that some people on our team did have some pain resulting from that heel design though. If you have a Haglund’s or achilles sensitivity this won’t be the shoe for you. The instep around the front of the ankle is also pretty tight. The hold from the one piece upper on the foot is great, but it chokes up on the anterior ankle more than I would like it too. I find that I barely tighten my laces to avoid it pinching at the front. When I can get the fit right though the ride of the shoe is quite nice.
The $200 dollar price tag feels a touch steep, but it is decently competitive with what is out there. I think it would be a direct competitor with something like the Saucony Endorphin Speed 6 at $180. The foam and platform is very promising. If the upper has some tweaks this could be a highly competitive shoe on the market.

OVERALL FINAL THOUGHTS
David
Fit: C 
(Good lockdown, but heel counter and collar can put pressure on the achilles. Instep tight on the anterior ankle.)
Performance: A- 
(Very promising foam and geometry. Responsive and versatile. Midfoot platform a little narrow.)
Stability: B 
(Midfoot platform a little narrow, but the stiff platform, traction, and geometry all help.)
Value: B 
($200 feels close… but a touch high. I think $180 would line up with the other shoes in this category better.) 
Personal: B 
(I really like the foam and geometry. The upper needs some work though.)
Overall Design: B
The rabbit Pace Chaser is a lightweight training shoe that can handle some daily miles, but really loves to pick up the pace. This is a shoe that work well as a training companion for hard workouts, speed days, and tempo runs. There is enough cushioning to handle longer efforts as well and it could reasonably tackle long distance races if needed. The ATPU foam has an excellent blend of cushioning and responsiveness for a variety of paces. I feel comfortable running on the track and the road in these. The rocker geometry is gradual and the platform is very stiff. The upper has a snug performance fit throughout. The heel counter is curved forwards and can put some pressure on the Achilles. I did not have irritation, but if you have a Haglund’s or sensitive tendons, it is something to watch for. There is a lot of potential in the rabbit Pace Chaser to be a top shelf performance trainer with some slight tweaks.

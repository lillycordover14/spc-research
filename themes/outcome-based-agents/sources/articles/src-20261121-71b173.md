Two New Jersey women were indicted after being accused of falsely reporting that one of them, a former staffer for U.S. Rep. Jeff Van Drew, R-N.J., 
was the target of a violent political attack in 2025.
On Friday, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of New Jersey announced that Natalie Greene, 27, of Ocean City, was indicted on charges of conspiracy to convey false statements and hoaxes, conveying false statements and hoaxes, and making false statements to federal law enforcement.
She had previously been charged with the same offenses in a 
federal criminal complaint
.
Officials said Anamaria Moe, 25, of Atlantic City, was newly charged in the indictment with conspiracy to convey false statements and hoaxes and conveying false statements and hoaxes.
Moe made her initial appearance in federal court in Camden on Thursday.
On July 23, 2025, prosecutors said Moe called 911 and claimed she and Greene had been attacked by three men while walking on a trail at a nature preserve in Egg Harbor Township, New Jersey.
Officials said Moe told authorities that the men called Greene by name and referenced her employment with a federal official, later identified as Republican Rep. Jeff Van Drew.
When police arrived at the scene, they found Greene on the ground in a wooded area near the trail with her hands and feet bound with black zip ties. Greene’s shirt was pulled over her head and secured with a zip tie, investigators said.
Greene also had several lacerations on her face, neck, upper chest and shoulder, according to officials.
NBC10 Philadelphia
Police said the words “TRUMP WHORE” were written on her stomach, while a message claiming that Van Drew “IS RACIST” was written on her back.
Greene told police that at least one of the men who allegedly attacked her said he had a gun and threatened to shoot her, investigators said. She also claimed that at least one of the alleged attackers held her down, restrained her, cut her and wrote on her body.
Greene received medical treatment for her lacerations and repeated her account of the supposed attack to law enforcement officers, including an FBI agent, a few days later.

During the investigation, however, officials determined Greene had lied about the attack.
Instead, Greene paid a body modification/scarification artist to cut the lacerations on her face, neck, chest and shoulder based on a design that she provided, according to investigators. Police also recovered black zip ties from Greene’s car on the night of the alleged attack which were similar to the zip ties that had been used to bind her arms and feet, officials said.
Investigators also said that two days before the alleged attack, Moe’s cellphone had been used to search “zip ties near me.”
Van Drew spoke about the allegations back in 2025.
“I’m just praying for her,” he said. “Wish her the very best. I don’t know anything more than you all do and we never would interfere with an investigation.”
In a 2025 statement, Greene’s lawyer, Louis Barbone, said in part that she served her community with “loyalty and fidelity” while working for Van Drew and that under the law, “she is presumed innocent.”
Each of the charged counts has a maximum penalty of five years’ imprisonment, a $250,000 fine and a term of three years of supervised release.

Brent Tomberlin

It happened last week. A teacher died suddenly. It is not the way it is supposed to be. Students are supposed to have their teachers for a long time; yet it did not happen that way.
Students lost a beloved instructor. Friends lost a friend. Family members are missing an important, meaningful, and funny member of the family.

Jessica Clay Farlee became all of these things: teacher, friend, mom, sister, aunt, etc. in her brief time on this earth. She is truly an exceptional individual who left us way too soon.
Like any of us, she played multiple roles. She dedicated herself to service in and out of the classroom.
When her daughter was swimming in high school, she committed her time to helping the team by volunteering to work meets and brought snacks for swimmers and coaches.
Moreover, there was not a time when she was not encouraging and supporting her daughter and the other team members. As with her students, she was present — supporting and encouraging.
Recently, she posted pictures of her daughter swimming on a club team at her university. Like any parent, she was proud.
Many of us are stunned by the sudden news of her passing and suspect to be so for some time. When a teacher dies, in a way, it feels as if a collective breath leaves the body and it becomes hard to breathe.
Teachers are not supposed to leave us, but they do. In a flash, students begin to miss the love and individual intention they provide.
Ms. Farlee was good, kind, a caregiver, a supporter; someone who would come alongside anyone and give them a bit of hope.
It can be easy to put someone on a pedestal when they are no longer present to ask us to be more humble.

If she was here right now, she would be saying so; telling us she was only doing her job. She taught a lot of people a lot about character and service.
Of course, right now, we are thinking about her extended family who lost someone in a hurried and tragic way. But we are also thinking about the entire student body at Hickory Day School and her colleagues and friends.
When someone passes, the rest of us are left contemplating the loss and asking a lot of questions.
Mrs. Farlee was doing what she loved. Each of us should be so fortunate. Separation is never easy, but she exemplified a standard her daughter’s high school swim team tried to follow: that we should leave places better than we found them.
She certainly did — in every way.
From one teacher to another, the passing of an instructor hurts. We miss their commitment to students and to do the job well. We miss their individual encouragement, laughter, and dedication to the job.
Ms. Farlee was so proud of her education, recently completing a secondary degree. She cared for the job, and especially, for the students.
What great lessons for the rest of us. All of us. She showed us how to live with amazing potential and amazing grace.
Each school administration and students do things differently to honor a special individual. There is no question Hickory Day School will construct an appropriate and loving memorial to Mrs. Farlee.
Perhaps, in some way, it will remind her students and colleagues of the great grin she displayed and how hard she worked.
That would be good and right.

Brent Tomberlin is a social studies instructor for Caldwell County Schools and an adjunct instructor for various other institutions. He can be reached at

GTA 6's Adaptive Speed Limiter turns the PS5 DualSense trigger into cruise control as Xbox players miss out&nbsp;&nbsp;Notebookcheck

[page text]
4

Grand Theft Auto VI
 began its rolling midnight launch on November 19, starting in New Zealand, and one of its more unusual driving features is exclusive to the PlayStation 5. As a car nears the speed limit, the DualSense controller's R2 trigger stiffens under the player's finger, offering a physical cue to ease off the throttle. Players who would rather keep speeding can simply press through it.

How the Adaptive Speed Limiter works on PS5

According to 
GameSpot
, 
GTA VI
 on PS5 uses the DualSense's adaptive triggers to add resistance as a vehicle approaches the legal limit, making it easier to match the flow of traffic. The resistance acts as a nudge rather than a lock, so a firmer press still pushes the car past it.

Adaptive triggers use small motors inside the L2 and R2 buttons to vary how strongly each trigger pushes back, under the game's control. In 
GTA VI
, that behavior appears as the Adaptive Speed Limiter in the Controller settings menu. It sits beside separate trigger resistance and vibration toggles in the DualSense section and is on by default.

The menu describes the feature as keeping the vehicle at "a reasonable speed for the road" while R2 is held at the resistance point. That makes it closer to a cruise-control aid than a speeding deterrent, and the menu text never mentions posted speed limits.

No confirmed link to the police or the Criminal Profile

GameSpot frames the feature as a way to avoid drawing the attention of law enforcement, and it is tempting to connect it to 
GTA VI
's new Criminal Profile, which grades how violently Jason and Lucia carry out their crimes. Rockstar, however, has not tied speeding to either system. Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson told IGN that a crime must be witnessed or set off an alarm before it is reported to the police. Some high-end stolen cars even carry trackers that alert the police when they are driven too slowly, so obeying the limiter in the wrong car could backfire.

Sony's promotional material says even less about the feature. A June 24 
PlayStation Blog post
, published with Rockstar, says only that the controller's haptics deliver responsive vibrations while its adaptive triggers offer "dynamic resistance." Sony's 
GTA VI
 product page uses similar language, and neither mentions driving, throttle control, or speed limits.

From 
Gran Turismo 7
's brakes to real-world speed assistance

Racing games have used the DualSense's triggers for driving feedback before. Sony says 
Gran Turismo 7
 recreates the pulsing feel of anti-lock brakes when players stamp on the brake trigger, and Polyphony Digital's Kazunori Yamauchi has explained that the trigger's resistance drops away when the brakes lock up. That effect reports what the brakes are doing, however, while Rockstar's limiter guides the throttle.

The closer parallel sits in real cars. Under the EU's 
Intelligent Speed Assistance rules
, mandatory on every new car sold since July 2024, one permitted warning gently pushes the accelerator pedal back against the driver's foot, and drivers can override it by pressing harder. 
GTA VI
 applies the same idea to a game controller.

Xbox Series X|S players miss out

Xbox Series X|S owners will not get the same resistance. The standard Xbox Wireless Controller and the Xbox Elite Wireless Controller Series 2 have Impulse Triggers, which use small rumble motors to vibrate the triggers but cannot push back against a finger. The Adaptive Speed Limiter is listed among the PS5 version's DualSense options, and Rockstar has not described an Xbox equivalent.

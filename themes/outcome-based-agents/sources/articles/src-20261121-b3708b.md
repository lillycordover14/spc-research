The most expensive ZIP code for homes in the state of Nevada is in Crystal Bay (89042) on the north shore of Lake Tahoe, according to a new report.

The median home sale price in the ZIP code hit a record $4.7 million, according to the Property Shark report, following a 74 percent year over year jump.

The Property Shark report ranked the top 100 most expensive ZIP codes in the country, with Crystal Bay landing at No. 13, its highest ever ranking. The ZIP code was shy $177,000 from cracking the top 10.

Glenbrook’s 89413 ZIP code, which is also on Lake Tahoe, came in at No. 28 in the rankings, losing the top Nevada position after nine years. The median home sale price was a record $3.69 million, an 8 percent jump year over year, according to the report.

Jesse Schue, the broker and owner for Remax Gold in South Lake Tahoe, said what makes Crystal Bay such a draw is its history and character, notably the Cal Neva Hotel, which is being redeveloped into the Lake Tahoe Proper Resort and Casino. The hotel was owned by Frank Sinatra for a period in the 1960s.

“It has always had a little bit of glamour associated with it, going back to the Cal Neva and its connection to Frank Sinatra and the entertainment industry,” she said. “What makes it special from a real estate standpoint is the limited inventory, beautiful lakefront properties and the fact that you’re on the Nevada side of the lake. You have incredible views, access to the water and proximity to Incline Village.”

She said the planned redevelopment of the Cal Neva property, which just secured $298 million in financing and is scheduled to reopen sometime next year, is something he will be watching.

“The planned Lake Tahoe Proper Resort & Casino redevelopment could bring a whole new level of energy and investment to Crystal Bay,” she said. “It’s exciting to see renewed interest in an area with so much history.”

Schue said she thinks investments in the historic properties around Tahoe is a positive thing.

“The Crystal Bay Club and the former Cal Neva property are both important parts of the area’s identity. The Cal Neva redevelopment, in particular, has the potential to bring new hospitality, dining, entertainment and visitors to the North Shore.”

She added Glenbrook offers the same type of luxury as Crystal Bay, with lakefront living, private beaches, boating and golfing.

“I think buyers who are looking for something more exclusive and understated are really drawn to Glenbrook. It’s not necessarily about having the biggest house, it’s about the lifestyle and the location.”

Why is Lake Tahoe such a luxury hotspot?

Schue said there are several things that have attracted wealthy buyers to the Nevada side of Lake Tahoe over the years, the big one being no personal income tax and an overall low rate of taxes compared to California.

“The Nevada shoreline has some very special properties, and because inventory is limited, those properties can be particularly desirable,” she said.

She said the fact that there is a finite amount of shoreline at Lake Tahoe definitely makes prices fall into the luxury category.

“That scarcity is important. Properties with lakefront access, beautiful views, privacy, and unique amenities are always going to stand out,” she said. “Of course, real estate is cyclical, and buyers need to look at individual properties, pricing and carrying costs. But I think Tahoe has a long-standing appeal that goes well beyond market trends.”

A number of wealthy California residents recently took up residence on Lake Tahoe in Nevada, in part to potentially avoid new taxes such as the proposed one-time billionaire’s tax that failed to pass in the recent election.

In January, 
Google’s co-founder Sergey Brin
 was linked to the $42 million sale of a mansion on the Nevada side of Lake Tahoe. According to the listing, the Crystal Bay residence has eight bedrooms and nine-and-a-half bathrooms over 16,232 square feet, sitting on a 5.1 acre lot.

The Property Shark report noted that the most expensive ZIP codes are located on the coast.

“Of the 122 ZIPs making up the top 100 (the result of multiple price ties), 72 are located within five miles of the coast and 39 percent are less than one mile away. In fact, only five locations are more than 25 miles from the ocean.”

Nationally, Atherton, California, (94027) is the most expensive ZIP code at $10.73 million, which is the first eight-figure median sale price ever. Second was $7.35 million in Sagaponack, N.Y., (11962). Nine ZIP codes in the country surpassed $5 million median sale price, 52 ZIPs stand north of $3 million. Newport Beach, California remains most expensive city with all six of its ZIP codes in the top 100.

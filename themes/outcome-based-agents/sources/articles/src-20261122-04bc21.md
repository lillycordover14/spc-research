Attendees listen to remarks during a ribbon-cutting ceremony held on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, for Five Rivers MetroParks ADA-accessible trail at Possum Creek MetroPark's Argonne Lake.

JOSEPH COOKE / STAFF

The afternoon of Friday, Nov. 20, Possum Creek MetroPark held a ribbon cutting ceremony for a new trail, designed to be accessible to all guests. The project, which took just eight months to complete, was an investment of approximately $750,000.
“For many of us, a walk around a beautiful lake is something we take for granted,” said Five Rivers MetroParks CEO Karen Hesser in a speech at the opening. “We enjoy the wildlife, stop to fish or simply to spend time with family and friends. But for someone who uses a wheelchair, walker or other mobility device, or is even just unsteady on his feet, an unpaved trail means those experiences are simply out of reach.”

Circumnavigating Argonne Lake, this new trail is 3/4-mile long and stretches nine feet wide. At several points throughout the lake, there are outcroppings to allow for fishing.
“Accessible fishing outcrops make it possible for even more people to enjoy one of our park’s favorite activities,” said Board of Park Commissioners President Jessica Salem.
To celebrate this aspect of the trail, the ceremonial ribbon was adorned with fishing bobbers, which guests could take home.

Officials prepare to cut the ribbon during a ceremony held on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, for Five Rivers MetroParks ADA-accessible trail at Possum Creek MetroPark's Argonne Lake.

JOSEPH COOKE / STAFF

While much of the funds for this project came from a grant given out by the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, the trail could only cover 3/4 of the lake without additional fundraising. The task of raising the remaining funds went to Five Rivers Chief of Philanthropy Alexis Larsen, who happens to be a strong advocate for accessibility.
“ADA’s really driven me because I’ve seen the need for it in my own personal life,” said Larsen. “My dad could not do this unless he was in a wheelchair or an adaptive device. And so he’s been really excited about this.”
Larsen’s father attended the ribbon cutting, riding along the trail in an adaptive bike.
This project is part of a larger push toward making the Five Rivers MetroParks more accessible for all. The aforementioned ODNR funds were used to purchase two accessible docks, one of which is located on Argonne Lake.

Officials socialize during a ribbon-cutting ceremony held on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, for Five Rivers MetroParks ADA-accessible trail at Possum Creek MetroPark's Argonne Lake.

JOSEPH COOKE / STAFF

“It is one of my personal and professional promises to the people of Montgomery County,” Hesser later said. “We don’t have lakes at every park, but every aspect like this should be available in some manner so that all of our parks should be able to be accessed and welcome for all.”
Possum Creek MetroPark is located at 4970 Frytown Road in Dayton. This new trail can be reached by using the Argonne Lake parking lot.

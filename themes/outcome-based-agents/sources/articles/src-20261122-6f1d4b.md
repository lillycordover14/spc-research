The European Medicines Agency and the Heads of Medicines Agencies announced that the Accelerating Clinical Trials in the EU pre-submission advice pilot will transition into a permanent initiative starting in January 2027, establishing a unified channel across the European Union for clinical trial sponsors to obtain consolidated regulatory and technical feedback before filing multi-country applications in the Clinical Trials Information System.

Consolidation of ACT EU Clinical Trial Advice

According to an 
official agency announcement
 published by the European Medicines Agency on 8 October 2026, the pre-CTA advice service will become a permanent feature of European clinical research governance in January 2027. The programme operates under the Accelerating Clinical Trials in the EU initiative, commonly known as ACT EU, which was co-founded by the European Medicines Agency, the Heads of Medicines Agencies, and the European Commission to overhaul and strengthen the clinical research ecosystem across member states.

The advice procedure is operated and coordinated by the Heads of Medicines Agencies through its Clinical Trials Coordination Group. Under this framework, trial sponsors preparing to conduct multi-state clinical investigations in Europe receive joint regulatory and technical guidance from the relevant national competent authorities before lodging a formal Clinical Trial Application in the Clinical Trials Information System. This consolidated review allows sponsors to identify and resolve dossier gaps, trial design ambiguities, and procedural discrepancies simultaneously across all intended European jurisdictions.

Prior to the establishment of the pilot in 2024, sponsors preparing multi-country trials had no mechanism for joint pre-submission engagement. Investigators and sponsors were required to request separate national scientific or regulatory advice meetings with each individual national competent authority. This fragmented process frequently generated contradictory instructions on protocol elements, trial endpoints, and monitoring strategies, causing substantial validation delays when dossiers finally entered formal assessment.

Key Procedural Details and Operating Framework

The transition from a temporary pilot to a permanent regulatory pathway formalises a streamlined pre-submission route for commercial pharmaceutical companies, small biotechnology developers, and non-commercial academic sponsors. The service is coordinated through the 
Clinical Trials Coordination Group working group
, which convenes experts from national competent authorities to deliver coordinated feedback on key pre-submission topics in a single procedural step.

Throughout its pilot phase between 2024 and 2026, the initiative managed approximately 30 pilot applications spanning complex multi-state trials, low-intervention studies, and trials involving combination therapies. The operational scope covers critical technical and regulatory areas that sit outside traditional single-drug scientific advice, including trial classification, transition mechanics from the repealed Clinical Trials Directive, master protocols, decentralized trial elements, and compliance with harmonised 
ICH M11 protocol specifications
.

Regulatory Context Under Regulation EU No 536/2014

The permanent advice service represents a cornerstone of the broader modernization underway across the European clinical trials landscape. The European Union fully transitioned to Regulation (EU) No 536/2014, the Clinical Trials Regulation, which replaced the fragmented 2001 Clinical Trials Directive and mandated the exclusive use of the Clinical Trials Information System for all new clinical trial applications and legacy trial transitions. As documented in industry reporting by 
Clinical Trials Arena
, creating a unified regulatory portal was a crucial step, but operational friction persisted when sponsors encountered divergent national interpretations during formal Part I scientific evaluation and Part II national ethical review.

ACT EU was established to dismantle these remaining barriers through ten targeted priority actions. Priority Action 7 explicitly tasked the Clinical Trials Coordination Group with establishing consolidated advice pathways, bridging the gap between the Scientific Advice Working Party at the European Medicines Agency, which primarily addresses drug development endpoints and marketing authorization strategy, and national competent authorities responsible for trial authorization and site safety. The permanent advice framework ensures that early trial design aligns seamlessly with rigorous standards for 
clinical trial protocol design
 before applications are formally registered.

Stakeholder Reception and Strategic Policy Alignment

The decision to make pre-submission advice permanent has been welcomed by regulatory professionals and industry associations seeking greater predictability in European drug development. An analysis published by the 
Regulatory Affairs Professionals Society
 emphasized that establishing a permanent advice procedure directly reinforces the European Commission strategic objectives for the European Biotech Act. The European Biotech Act is designed to streamline administrative pathways, accelerate clinical development timelines, and reverse the gradual decline of early-phase trial starts across the continent relative to the United States and the Asia-Pacific region.

Regulatory experts have highlighted that the permanent mechanism provides Europe with a collaborative equivalent to the Pre-IND meeting framework administered by the United States Food and Drug Administration. While the FDA system relies on a single national agency, the ACT EU framework achieves multi-jurisdictional alignment among sovereign member states before dossier validation. This pre-submission consensus substantially reduces the risk of protracted Requests for Information during assessment, which have historically caused unexpected trial delays and budgetary overruns for biopharmaceutical developers.

Implications for University Sponsors and Research Offices

For university sponsors, academic medical centers, and clinical trial offices, the permanent ACT EU advice framework addresses a long-standing operational vulnerability. Non-commercial sponsors frequently design multinational academic trials to evaluate rare diseases, pediatric indications, or repurposed therapies, but often lack dedicated regulatory affairs departments in multiple European capitals. A detailed clinical research report by 
MedPath Trial
 noted that managing divergent competent authority expectations across six to twelve member states historically created severe operational burdens that disproportionately discouraged academic research consortia.

Academic research administrators must adapt their pre-award and protocol finalization timelines to leverage the permanent pathway effectively. When developing a multi-country 
clinical trial protocol
, trial management teams should schedule pre-CTA advice requests at least four to six months prior to their planned submission target in CTIS. Research offices must ensure that trial documentation accounts for both technical trial design and downstream compliance obligations, including prospective trial registration and mandatory results disclosure governed by 
clinical trial registration and reporting compliance
 mandates.

University grant offices should also factor the procedural timelines into grant milestones and sponsor-investigator budgets. Early engagement through the Clinical Trials Coordination Group minimizes subsequent dossier revisions, prevents costly protocol amendments after authorization, and facilitates uniform patient safety standards across all academic medical centers participating in the consortium.

Implementation Roadmap and Next Milestones

The transition to the permanent framework follows a structured regulatory roadmap outlined by European authorities. The pilot initiative will remain active through the end of 2026, continuing to process applications currently under review and accepting qualifying requests within its initial scope. Officials confirmed that insights gathered across the approximately 30 pilot procedures will be compiled into a comprehensive evaluation report scheduled for public release before the end of 2026.

Following the publication of the evaluation report, updated guidance documents, briefing templates, and revised submission instructions will be officially released on the Clinical Trials Coordination Group website in January 2027. Sponsoring organizations planning multi-site European investigations for 2027 and 2028 are advised to review the forthcoming templates to ensure their pre-submission dossiers align with the finalized procedural standards.

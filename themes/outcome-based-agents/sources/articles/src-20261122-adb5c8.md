Why Ada Brook From The Gilded Age Looks So Familiar

By
 
Michael Boyle

Nov. 22, 2026 11:12 am EST

Karolina Wojtasik/HBO

If the kind and gentle Ada Brook (now named Ada Forte) on the riveting HBO drama "The Gilded Age" seems familiar to you, it might be because you're a fan of "Sex and the City." Brook is played by Cynthia Nixon, best known for her major role on the '90s HBO dramedy as Miranda Hobbes. Miranda is a career-minded, independent woman who stays in main character Carrie's life throughout the whole series. She played Miranda in the "Sex and the City" movies, and 
Nixon even returned for the 2021 sequel series "And Just Like That,"
 exploring how Miranda has evolved in middle age.

Away from the "Sex and the City" franchise, you may remember Nixon from an early role in the HBO historical drama movie "Warm Springs," in which she played first lady Eleanor Roosevelt. Other notable small screen roles include Senator Carly Armiston in the Prime Video political satire series "Alpha House," investigator Kade Prurnell in the NBC horror/thriller series "Hannibal," and press secretary Gwendolyn Briggs in 
Netflix's disappointing "One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest" prequel series "Ratched.
" She's also made guest appearances in the likes of "ER," "House," "30 Rock," and "Broad City."

You may also recognize Nixon from the stage. Not only has she starred in many critically acclaimed plays, but in 1984 (when she was still a freshman in college) she became the first Broadway performer to be in two plays at the same time. She would perform the first act of the dark comedy "Hurlyburly," then rush to a different theater for the second act of the drama "The Real Thing," and then return to "Hurlyburly" for the ending. "I only did it for about three months, then gave my notice because I was panicked I would flunk my geology final," she told 
Playbill
.

You may also recognize Nixon from her foray into politics

Drew Angerer/Getty Images

Back in 2018, Cynthia Nixon made a bid to become the Democratic nominee for New York governor, running against the 
not-yet-disgraced Andrew Cuomo
 in the gubernatorial primary. She didn't come out on top, but she was still able to get her messages about things like criminal justice reform across during her campaign, and she saw that as a success. "I knew the most important thing about my running was to shed light on these issues," she told 
Time
 afterward. She believed her run was at least partially successful in pushing Cuomo to the left on certain issues.

Nixon leaned into her "Sex and the City" image during her primary run, selling merch labeled "I'm a Miranda and I'm voting for Cynthia." She told Time, "It was a really useful fundraising tool. People know me as Miranda, and I share a lot in common with her. I think it was kind of a shorthand for saying, 'Women like me are stepping up and are outspoken and are warriors and crusaders in the way that Miranda was.'" When asked if she would ever run again, she said, "I don't think I will, but you never know. Life is long and strange."

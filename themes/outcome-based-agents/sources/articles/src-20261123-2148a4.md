Wayne County EMA urges residents to sign up for free emergency weather alerts

        WENS system provides weather and public safety notifications

As part of Winter Safety Awareness Week, the Wayne County Emergency Management Agency encourages residents to sign up for its free Wireless Emergency Notification System to receive weather alerts and emergency notifications.

As Ohio observes Winter Safety Awareness Week, Nov. 15-21, the Wayne County Emergency Management Agency is encouraging residents to register for its free Wireless Emergency Notification System to receive important weather warnings and emergency information.
The notification system allows Wayne County EMA to send emergency messages directly to residents through text messages, telephone calls and email, helping them stay informed about severe weather and other situations that could threaten public safety.

AD

Residents can register by visiting wcemaohio.org and selecting the Emergency Notifications button.
Wayne County EMA Director Jerry Winkler said the county’s strong sense of community is an important part of responding to emergencies, but staying informed is equally essential.
“One thing about Wayne County is the people of Wayne County are extremely resourceful," Winkler said. "They're very good about helping each other. And they will do what needs to be done to take care of their people and their neighbors."
That willingness to help others, he said, is most effective when residents know about potential emergencies and have time to prepare.
“A lot has to do with awareness, which is why one of the most important things you can do is sign up on our WENS system,” he said.
The service is free and allows users to choose which types of notifications they receive. Alerts can also be targeted geographically, allowing residents to receive information relevant to their communities rather than notifications affecting other parts of the county.

AD

The system can distribute emergency weather warnings, public safety notifications and other important information to registered users. It also allows emergency management officials to target specific geographic areas when an incident affects only part of the county.
Registration requires users to provide their name, address and contact information, including the telephone numbers or email addresses where they want to receive notifications. Users then select their preferred notification categories and submit their registration.
Residents may register multiple telephone numbers and email addresses, allowing members of the same household to receive notifications through their preferred communication methods.
Although registration is free, standard messaging or telephone charges from individual service providers may apply.
Wayne County EMA officials said residents who need assistance registering can call the agency at 330-262-9817. Staff members can help complete the registration process, including assisting people without internet access.
The emphasis on emergency notifications coincides with Ohio's Winter Safety Awareness Week, recognized by Gov. Mike DeWine and the Ohio Committee for Severe Weather Awareness.
The Ohio Emergency Management Agency is encouraging households to review emergency plans, assemble preparedness kits and establish reliable ways to receive weather information before hazardous conditions develop.
Winter storms can bring heavy snowfall, freezing rain, ice accumulation and prolonged power outages. Wayne County EMA officials said preparedness should extend beyond receiving alerts to ensuring households have the supplies necessary to remain safe if utilities are interrupted or travel becomes difficult.
Recommended emergency supplies include several days of food and water, necessary medications, flashlights, spare batteries and items needed by pets. Portable phone chargers can also help residents maintain communications during power outages.
Wayne County EMA specialist Tom Brennan emphasized the importance of preparing for winter storms before severe weather disrupts everyday routines.
“Loss of power is always a big concern with winter weather," Brennan said. "You get the heavy snow; you get the ice storms. We recommend an emergency kit for everybody with some basic supplies. Everything from food and water for a couple of days to medications.”
Residents should consider the needs of older adults, children, individuals who depend on medical equipment or medications, pets and livestock when developing their emergency plans.
Officials also stressed the importance of safely operating equipment used during power outages.
Portable generators should never be operated inside homes or garages, even with garage doors open, because of the danger of carbon monoxide poisoning. Electric space heaters should be plugged directly into appropriate electrical outlets rather than extension cords, and residents should follow manufacturers' operating instructions.
Wayne County EMA also recommends that residents know how to report electrical outages to their utility providers and establish plans for alternative accommodations if extended outages make their homes unsafe.
Checking on elderly neighbors and others who may be particularly vulnerable during extreme cold is another important part of winter preparedness.
While WENS provides an additional way to receive emergency information, Wayne County EMA cautions that no notification system is infallible. Residents should maintain multiple ways of obtaining emergency information, including weather radios, local television and radio broadcasts and other established warning systems.
The Ohio EMA recommends that families discuss their emergency plans and practice what they will do when severe weather threatens.
Dan Starcher is the communications coordinator for Wayne County.

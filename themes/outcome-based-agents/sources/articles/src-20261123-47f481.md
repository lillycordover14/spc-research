Tomb festival in Osaka holds qualifying tyrannosaurus race and ancient clay sculpture chase

Ready to unpack all that?

Monday 23 November is a public holiday in Japan, creating a long weekend this year, which is always a good time to hold a major event. In Osaka, that meant it was time once again for 
Kofun Fest Hanicotto, a regional festival celebrating ancient Japanese graves at Imajozuka Kofun Park
.

A 
kofun
 is an ancient tomb typically reserved for royalty. Since Osaka was an ancient capital of Japan and adjacent to other historical capitals like Nara and Kyoto, the city is dotted with quite a few of them, easily spotted in aerial photos due to their large sizes and distinctive, keyhole-like shapes.

▼ Here are a few of them.

As for “Hanicotto,” that is a combination of the words “
haniwa
” and the Japanese pronunciation of “mascot.” Players of 
Animal Crossing
 ought to be somewhat familiar with the ancient clay sculptures known as haniwa that resemble scared or confused cactuses. These figures were buried with the deceased in kofun to help protect them in the afterlife.

▼ Kofun Fest Hanicotto was also the venue for HaniCon 2026.

With all this talk of fifth-century Japanese culture, you’re probably wondering, “This is all well and good, but why aren’t there any dinosaurs here, too?” Well, I was just getting to that.

For a few years now, 
Kofun Fest Hanicotto has also been the host of the West Japan qualifying round of the Tyranno Fest national Tyrannosaurus rex race
. This is the only qualifying race of its kind on this side of Japan and promises to bring the very best in men, women, and children who run around while wearing a big T-Rex costume.

The first and second-place male and female racers, as well as one wildcard entrant who wins the MIP (Most Impressive Participant) award, advance to 
Tyranno Fest Vol. 3 on 20 March, 2027 at Funabashi Racecourse in Chiba Prefecture
.

The Kofun Fest Hanicotto races are prestigious events in this dignified spot. At Tyranno Fest Vol. 1, the women’s champion came from Kofun Fest Hanicotto, as did the men’s champ in Tyranno Fest Vol. 2. This grand prix of T-Rex racing is a gate-style sprint, in which the dinos start behind closed gates like racehorses, presumably because it looks really funny.

These qualifying rounds, however, are standard time trial races, combined with Kofun Fest Hanicotto’s signature 
Haniwa Capture Race
, where the Tyrannosaurus Rexes have to also pick up haniwa statues on the ground, and probably fall down a lot in the process.

The 
main sponsor of this event is none other than Zenpo-Koenfu
, a grave operator that has made headlines for their award-winning modern 
Takeda-style kofun tombs
. The centerpiece of these is a stylish reimagining of the ancient burial grounds that lies atop Mt. Ikoma, overlooking nearly all of Osaka Prefecture in a picturesque park designed by the firm of noted architect Tadao Ando. It won the Kansai Region Grave Grand Prix for both its layout and as a convenient, cost-effective alternative to traditional family graves.

All in all, it’s a great time to learn about Japanese burial methods old and new, while also watching people dressed as dinosaurs 
wobble around for a bit
. Although this year’s event is behind us now, it’s sure to return next year, bigger and more ancient than ever.

Keeping tabs on a late-week storm system

Aidan Cera KAALTV

Through the week ahead, we’ll be keeping tabs on a more potent weather system tracking east of the viewing area Thanksgiving day into Friday. While most of the precipitation is 
likely
 to remain south and east of us, the western edge of this system 
*could* 
graze us with a few light snow showers.
This technically isn’t our first chance of a few flakes this week. The first chance arrives early Wednesday morning as an upper-level low tracks in from the west. There isn’t going to be much moisture with this feature and the forcing that comes with it. With that said, any snow will be light in nature as well as scattered, with the best chance of such an event mainly west of I-35. Any snow accumulations are not anticipated to add up to more than a very fine dusting at this time. 
Now for the system later this week…
Low pressure will scoop up copious amounts of moisture from the Gulf Wednesday into Thursday. This moisture will be met with a pair of upper-level lows interacting across the Central United States, and will kick off widespread rain, possibly mixed with snow, across portions of the Mid-Mississippi River Valley up into the Great Lakes Region. 
The catch with this system is the high degree of uncertainty with where it will track, even at a shorter lead time. This is because two or more upper-level lows interacting with each other are notoriously hard to predict, throwing wrenches in the going forecasts. With that said, confidence is highest that most, if not all, of the precipitation will be off to our south and/or east through late-week. Out of an abundance of caution, if we do see any snow locally, accumulations of only a tenth or two are currently expected by most model guidance.

Bitcoin faces resistance as short-term holder cost basis fails
2 min read
BTCUSD
Bitcoin has run into a wall, and the wall used to be a floor.
On-chain analytics from Glassnode show that the cost basis cluster for short-term holders, sitting between $62,000 and $65,000, failed to hold as support in mid-to-late November. After price broke below that band, recent buyers started selling at a loss. The zone that once caught falling prices now caps rebounds.
As of November 21–22, Bitcoin was trading around $60,452. In Glassnode's framing, a close above $65,000 would be the first real sign that buyers can absorb the selling coming from underwater holders.
When price drops below a cost basis cluster, those same buyers are suddenly losing money, and many of them want out at breakeven. So every bounce back toward their entry price meets a line of sellers looking for the exit. That is how support turns into resistance, and it is exactly what Glassnode describes happening in the $62,000 to $65,000 range.
The scale of buying in that zone helps explain why it carries weight. During the November 15 sell-off, on-chain data shows around 66,000 BTC changed hands in the $62,000 to $66,000 band.
That figure represents about 20% of short-term holder coins. A fifth of the recent-buyer cohort now has a cost basis sitting directly above the current price.
At the time of the report, Bitcoin sat below key exponential moving averages while still trading within its Bollinger Bands.
Earlier in the year, clusters in the $69,000 to $75,700 range played similar roles. They served as resistance or support before declines that followed.
Even earlier in November, the broader short-term holder cost basis stood near $75,600 to $75,700 before the pullback.
Long-term holders are in a very different spot. Their cost basis sits much lower, around $48,000 to $53,000. Long-term holders remain comfortably in profit at current prices, while the newer cohort is the group feeling the squeeze.
The level that matters is $65,000. A close above it, per the analysis, would suggest demand is strong enough to soak up the selling from holders trying to break even.
Until then, the $62,000 to $65,000 band acts as a ceiling on any rebound. Every rally into that zone runs into a crowd of sellers who just want their money back.
A clean reclaim of $65,000 would mean the market absorbed a large block of anxious supply, roughly the 66,000 BTC that traded in that zone during the November 15 sell-off.
The long-term holder cost basis, around $48,000 to $53,000, sits well below current prices, a reminder that the current stress is concentrated among recent buyers rather than the whole holder base.
For now, Bitcoin near $60,452 sits below a crowded band of recent buyers, and the next move depends on whether new money shows up to absorb their exits.

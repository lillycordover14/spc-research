The Cochran-Bleckley Recreation Department has agreed to make a series of improvements to its athletic fields, parking areas, restrooms, and pedestrian pathways after a federal investigation into allegations that its facilities were not accessible to individuals with disabilities.

The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Middle District of Georgia announced the agreement last week, bringing to a close the investigation spawned by a complaint from a family with multiple children with disabilities.  

According to federal officials, the family regularly visited the recreation department’s 28-acre facility at 250 West Dykes Street in Cochran but encountered several accessibility barriers. The complaint alleged that the facility lacked adequate accessible parking, accessible restrooms, and stable walking surfaces leading to restroom facilities. The family also reported that several athletic fields, including all five soccer fields, were inaccessible to individuals using wheelchairs.

Federal officials investigated the allegations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), which prohibits disability-based discrimination and establishes accessibility requirements for public facilities and services.

The recreation department signed a Letter of Resolution on November 18, agreeing to complete improvements in two phases over the next two years.

Improvements Required by February 2027

Under the agreement, the Cochran-Bleckley Recreation Department must complete several accessibility upgrades by February 15, 2027, including:

Establishing the required number and type of designated accessible parking spaces.

Constructing an accessible restroom inside the main concession building.

Installing directional signs throughout the facility identifying the accessible restroom.

Establishing accessible pathways connecting the soccer complex to the restroom.

Additional Improvements Required by July 2028

A second phase of improvements must be completed by July 1, 2028.

Those improvements include creating an accessible route from the facility entrance through the soccer complex, with connections to other athletic fields and restroom facilities.

The department must also construct, improve, or repair accessible pathways connecting entrances to athletic fields, playground areas, restrooms, and picnic shelters.

The improvements are intended to provide individuals with disabilities, including those who use wheelchairs or other mobility devices, access to the recreation department’s facilities and programs.

Federal Officials Respond

U.S. Attorney William Keyes acknowledged the recreation department’s cooperation in resolving the accessibility concerns and also pointed to the role members of the community play in identifying noncompliance with federal disability protections.

“We appreciate the cooperation shown by the Cochran-Bleckley Recreation Department in addressing these concerns and taking the necessary steps to improve accessibility,” Keyes said.

The Justice Department’s announcement did not specify the estimated cost of the improvements or identify a funding source.

The investigation was conducted by the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Middle District of Georgia in coordination with the Disability Rights Section of the Department of Justice’s Civil Rights Division.

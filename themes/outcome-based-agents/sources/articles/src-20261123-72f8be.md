Artisan Market Nights help breathe life into new venue
Monday, November 23rd, 2026
 
Share

WhatsApp
 
The artisan market in the Embarcadero area hosted another lively market night on November 20, bringing together local vendors and shoppers and showcasing homegrown products. The recurring event helps spread the word that the new marketplace is open and encourages residents and visitors to support local businesses.
 Shoppers browsed the booths, enjoyed live music, and met local artists. Vendors offered wooden handicrafts, cultural clothing, original artwork, and refreshments. Several vendors said the market needs more promotion to draw customers. The San Pedro Town Council (SPTC) says it will continue hosting market nights to raise awareness of the new location.
 One vendor said the market nights have helped businesses at the new location. “Most customers still do not know about this new area; that is why these events need to take place more often,” the vendor said. “We are approaching the high season, and we ask the different hotels and even restaurants to let their customers know of the location of the new artisan market.” The vendor added that the area sees little foot traffic most days and said they hope the SPTC will continue working with vendors to promote the market.
 Councilor Marina Kay, one of the organizers, attended the event and said it is bringing more business to the new market. She will continue looking for ways to promote the site, which is in an area of the island that sees relatively few tourists.
 When the venue opened in June, only a handful of artisans moved to the new market. Several vendors chose to stay at the old market on Angel Coral Street, across from the Honorable Louis Sylvestre Sporting Complex. They say they are reluctant to relocate because the new market has limited space, fewer customers, and higher rent.
 The sporting complex is expected to undergo major renovations in 2027. Island authorities have indicated that once the work is complete, the old artisan market site will be converted into a parking facility. Artisans still operating there will eventually have to relocate.

        Two Bloomington businessmen breathe new life into old Clay Dooley building

CEO of Premium Loaves Marc Largent explains how he and his business partner, President Jerome Hranka, took their real estate plan from vision to reality. 

Marc Largent took his vehicle to Clay Dooley at the corner of Grove and Gridley to have a flat tire replaced in 2014. Little did he know in 12 years he would help purchase the building as CEO of his company, 
Premium Loaves
.
Premium Loaves, a Jimmy John's franchisee based in Bloomington, was founded by Largent in 2018. Jerome Hranka came on as president in 2021. Now, the duo operates 130 Jimmy John's location across eight different states, including all five in Bloomington-Normal.
After eyeing the building for many years, the pair bought the historic Clay Dooley building 
earlier this year
 to serve as corporate headquarters for Premium Loaves. Largent said the building made an immediate impression on him when he moved to town in 2010. In 2021, the building went up for sale for the first time, and Largent was tempted.
"I wanted to look at it bad, but it just didn't make sense at the time," Largent said.
Back then, Hranka was less tempted. "When Mark first mentioned this building to me a couple years ago, I was like, 'No way! It's way too big. It's too big of a project.'"

At that time, "we weren't big enough," Largent said, "and the size of this building is massive." But Premium Loaves kept growing, and it was having a hard time finding the right property to expand into. When the building went back up for sale this year, Hranka had a change of heart.
"I fell in love with it. I saw the vision that Mark had for it," he said. The two acted quickly. Largent said they knew the property would be popular, so they had their offer ready 36 hours after it hit the market.
Largent and Hranka 
bought the building
 from David Bentley, who said it was originally built in 1945. Bentley purchased it in 2014 from Clay Dooley. Clay Dooley continued to operate from the lower level while Bentley operated his specialty painting company from the top floor.

The top floor: 'Fresh and open'
Starting first quarter of 2027, the top floor is where Premium Loaves' back-office employees will work. They hold roles in accounting, operations support, and administration. The company has grown from three employees when they started at their current location, off Fort Jesse Road, to approximately 20 expecting to move into the new headquarters. There is room to grow, with capacity for up to 45 employees in the new space.
The duo has made a point to contract with Bloomington-Normal companies and labor as they renovate. Hranka said it was important to both of them "to lift up the community as much as we can." Former building owner Bentley is providing painting services through his company. Hranka and Largent have partnered with Axis 360 for real estate needs, Catalyst for construction, Hewn Architectural Studio for building design, RoomReady for audiovisual conferencing needs, and Nord Custom Containers for a unique shipping container conference room configuration in the middle of the top floor.
Hranka said they knew they'd be removing a big ramp previously used to drive large vehicles into the building. Before they did that, "We wanted to bring something in that was like, 'How did you get it in here?'" he said. The container will be a signature design piece, painted and redecorated to serve as a meeting space — one of four the Premium Loaves staff can use.
Largent said getting the container into the building was a tight fit.
"I mean, we're talking inches from the top, there, of the main door. It was a forklift that went under it and then brought it in, and it was — it was something to see," he said.
Hranka said other interior renovations they've made include adding walls, offices, cubicle spaces, break spaces, restrooms, a lounge, and an ADA-compliant lift for access to the upper level. Exterior renovations were focused around "all things that needed to be done to make sure the space lasts another 100 years," he said. This included tuck pointing, roof touch-ups, parking lot resurfacing, and all new doors and windows.

Hranka said the whole team is most excited about the beautiful natural light coming in, with a view of surrounding trees, providing a "fresh and open" space for work and collaboration.
The lower level - 'something fun for the neighborhood'
Entering from Grove Street, the Premium Loaves entrance is at street level. But back around the corner on the Gridley entrance, there's another striking rotunda which opens into the lower level — the former tire shop.
Hranka said this 10,200-square-foot floor of the building will also be renovated and divided into up to three smaller suites, to be leased in phase two of development. The suites can be used for retail, office or flex-use space.
Largent hopes the lower level attracts something fun for the neighborhood. He's hoping for a fitness studio, coffee shop, restaurant, or something else that could bring in some foot traffic and extend the downtown revitalization the City of Bloomington has been working on.
"There's so much stuff going on further downtown, but we believe in that pushing further out with support from the City Council," Largent said.

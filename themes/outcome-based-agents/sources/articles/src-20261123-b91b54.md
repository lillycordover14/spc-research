SoundCloud cracks down on ‘AI slop, spam, and excessive uploads’ – weeks after striking AI deals with Moises and ElevenLabs&nbsp;&nbsp;Music Business Worldwide

[page text]
November 23, 2026
By 
Mandy Dalugdug
Photo credit: SoundCloud
Eliah Seton
SoundCloud
 has updated its Terms of Use and Community Guidelines to target what it calls “AI slop, spam, and excessive uploads.”
Continue to article...
CEO 
Eliah Seton
 set out the changes in 
an open letter
 published on Tuesday (
November 17
).
“AI slop has no place on SoundCloud,” wrote 
Seton
.
The same letter also addresses 
SoundCloud
‘s partnerships with AI companies 
Moises
 and 
ElevenLabs
, both struck in the past month.
“Today, we are announcing updates to our Community Guidelines and Terms of Use to combat spam, fraud and deceptive activity on 
SoundCloud
,” the letter states. “These updates focus on two areas designed to protect the integrity of SoundCloud and create a more trustworthy experience for artists and fans.”
The first area, under the heading “Addressing Slop and Spam,” reads: “AI slop has no place on 
SoundCloud
. We are drawing a clear line against AI slop, spam, and excessive uploads that overwhelm the platform, interfere with music discovery, or undermine the experience for artists and fans.
“We are drawing a clear line against AI slop, spam, and excessive uploads that overwhelm the platform, interfere with music discovery, or undermine the experience for artists and fans.”
Eliah Seton
, 
SoundCloud
“
SoundCloud
 has always been a place for artists to freely share their work, experiment, and push creative boundaries. But creative freedom does not extend to abusive practices that compromise platform integrity or make it harder for authentic artists and their music to be discovered.”
The second, headed “Preventing AI-Enabled Fraud and Manipulation,” states: “
SoundCloud
 will not tolerate the deceptive use of AI to manipulate platform systems, exploit creator opportunities, or mislead artists and fans. This includes misrepresenting the origin of content or the identity of a creator, such as falsely presenting AI-generated music as entirely human-made or portraying an AI-generated persona as a real human artist.
“AI should be a tool for creativity, not a means to deceive, exploit, or game the system.”
The updated 
Terms of Use
 define “AI Abuse” as using AI “to deceive, whether to game our systems, manipulate monetization, earnings, platform features, or users’ perception of authenticity.”
Under the terms, 
SoundCloud
 says it “may withhold, reverse, or recover payouts” in response to such activity.
The crackdown follows two deals between 
SoundCloud
 and AI companies.
On 
October 21
, SoundCloud and 
Moises
 
announced an integration
 that lets artists import their SoundCloud tracks into Moises, then publish finished music back to SoundCloud.
Earlier this month, 
ElevenLabs
 
rolled out a feature
 allowing users of its 
ElevenMusic
 AI music platform to publish tracks directly to SoundCloud.
In his letter, 
Seton
 wrote: “Our recently announced partnerships with Moises and ElevenLabs expand access to innovative tools for music creation and collaboration. Both partnerships reflect our commitment to working with companies that use ethically trained AI, prioritize transparency, and respect creator rights.
“Today, 
SoundCloud
 works with more than 
10
 approved AI partners that share these commitments, with more to come. As AI changes how music is made, direct publishing integrations also give us greater visibility into AI-generated content entering SoundCloud and a better understanding of where that music originates.”
“Too often, AI is framed as a binary choice: all good or all bad, embrace it or reject it,” wrote 
Seton
 elsewhere in the letter. “It is time that we address this transformation with the nuance that artists deserve.
“The reality is that AI presents existential risks if managed wrong and game-changing opportunities if managed right. It is our responsibility to artists to both minimize those risks and maximize those opportunities.”
Seton
 added: “Simply wishing AI away won’t make it disappear. It will only leave the future of this technology to those who may not share our commitment to artists, putting their work at greater risk of exploitation or being crowded out.
“Simply wishing AI away won’t make it disappear. It will only leave the future of this technology to those who may not share our commitment to artists, putting their work at greater risk of exploitation or being crowded out.”
Eliah Seton
, 
SoundCloud
“We have a responsibility to help shape what comes next, ensuring AI expands opportunities for artists rather than undermining them.”
On labeling, 
Seton
 wrote: “Transparency is an important part of that commitment, but it’s not as simple as applying a label. Distinguishing fully AI-generated music from music created with AI-assisted tools is complex, and industry standards for labeling and detection are still evolving.
“Through our approved AI partner integrations, we display identification information provided by partners, giving listeners greater visibility into where music comes from and how it was made.”
Seton
 previously addressed SoundCloud’s AI policies in an open letter in 
May 2025
.
That letter 
followed a backlash
 over a 
February 2024
 update to SoundCloud’s Terms of Use, which stated that users’ content “may be used to inform, train, develop or serve as input to artificial intelligence or machine intelligence technologies or services.”
SoundCloud
 then revised its terms to state that it would not use artists’ content to train generative AI models that “aim to replicate or synthesize your voice, music, or likeness without your explicit consent.”
SoundCloud
‘s latest update arrives as streaming services respond to a rise in AI-generated uploads.
Deezer
 said in July that it had been 
receiving nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks a day
 in June, accounting for more than 
50%
 of new music delivered to the platform on peak days.
The company also said that up to 
85%
 of streams generated by fully AI-generated tracks in 2025 were fraudulent.
On the same day as SoundCloud’s announcement, 
Spotify
 
rolled out its Artist Profile Protection tool to all artists
, allowing them to remove their names from releases that aren’t theirs.
Spotify
 said on 
October 20
 that it had taken more than 
50 million
 “slop and spam” tracks out of its recommendations so far in 2026.
SoundCloud
 says it hosts more than 
400 million
 tracks from over 
40 million
 artists across 
193
 countries.

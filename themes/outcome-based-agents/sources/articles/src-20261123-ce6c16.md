“
Every Strategic Bomber Russia Loses Is Gone for Good — and Ukraine Has Figured That Out
.”  This was a stunning statement to read … until I dug a little deeper:

Russia has the world’s third-largest strategic-bomber fleet — and can’t build most of it. The Tu-95 and Tu-22M haven’t been produced since 1993, the expertise gone with the USSR. Only the Tu-160, the largest warplane ever built, is still made — six in six years. So every bomber Ukraine destroys is gone for good.

Every Strategic Bomber Russia Loses Is Gone for Good — and Ukraine Has Figured That Out

Russia can no longer manufacture most of its major strategic bombers, specifically the 
Tu-95
 and 
Tu-22M3
, as production lines were dismantled after the collapse of the Soviet Union in the 1990s and 
the expertise is considered lost
[emphasis mine].
 ⁠

I grew up in the good old days of the Cold War regularly reading about the enormous threat the Soviet strategic bomber fleet, armed with nuclear weapons, posed to our lives. To find out that these decades old hobgoblins of the sky are slowly dying away is cheerful news. They are not all gone but thanks to ADAPTIVE INDOMITABLE UKRAINE, these menaces, still being used to terrorize Ukrainians with missile strikes, are steadily being lit up, frazzled from over-use and dragged into the bone yard of aviation ignominy. To wit:

The propeller-driven 
Tu-95MS
 strategic bomber has not been produced since 1992 so Russia relies on its limited existing stock, with estimates suggesting only 
12–38
 combat-ready aircraft remain after significant losses to Ukrainian strikes and crashes. 
Yessiree Bob!

The 
Tu-22M3
 supersonic long-range bomber is also out of production. Experts estimate Russia has lost 
22
 of these aircraft since the full-scale invasion began, with fewer than 
55
 remaining in total and only a fraction flight-worthy. 
Yes, again!

Russia still makes the 
Tu-160
 but output is reportedly extremely slow, with only 
six
 new aircraft delivered in six years, often using incomplete Soviet-era airframes. But even with the Tu-160 the old Russian bugbears of incompetence, bribery and corruption, etc, etc., rear their ugly heads:

⁠Critically, Russia cannot manufacture a Tu-160 airframe from scratch. ⁠ The “new” Tu-160M2s are actually assembled from 
mothballed Soviet-era airframes
 that sat at the Kazan, Russia plant for decades. ⁠ The production line was dismantled in the 1990s, and the tooling, jigs, and specialized manufacturing skills were lost.  Having personally spent a career in the aerospace industry, companies carefully curate and husband these things.  Only a know-nothing pin-headed bureaucrat or party 
apparatchik
 in a top-down government knows best environment would get rid of such things … which takes years, tons of money, know-how and effort to develop. But such was the Soviet Union and now Russia.

Western sanctions, however porous they may seem to us casual obsevers, are still taking their toll … cutting off access to critical imported components (electronics, precision instruments, materials). Additionally, the Soviet-era subcontracting network included 
Ukrainian plants
 that supplied key parts — those relationships are now severed, and the documentation for their equipment was “almost completely lost.”   Case in point, the story of the late 
Oleksandr Ivchenko, the Ukrainian scientist who built the engines at the heart of Soviet aircraft:

born on 23 November 1903 in Velykyi Tokmak, in Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia Oblast, a city now occupied by Russian forces. Under his direction, the ASh-82FN engine entered mass production, powering the La-5 fighter and the Tu-2 bomber, and his teams built more than 27,000 aircraft engines for military aircraft.

He also headed the Zaporizhzhia machine-building design bureau Progress, leading it until his death.

Its engines flew the Antonov transports, the An-8, An-12, and An-22 “Antei,” and the An-10 and An-24 passenger planes, as well as helicopters from both main Soviet design houses, Mil and Kamov.

Beyond aviation, Ivchenko’s team designed engines for civilian needs, including hydrofoils, hovercraft, aerosleds, and even chainsaws

https://www.facebook.com/photo/?fbid=1636503248492359&set=a.623290673146960

⁠Additional issues related to building the complex aircraft include difficulty getting sufficient engines, qualified aerospace workers are increasingly in short supply, lack of modernization of the industrial base and of course the evergreen corruption and mismanagement.  It is reported that actual production rates have been roughly ten times lower than planned, yet production costs and targets have not been meaningfully revised and the manufacturers have had to absorb associated cost increases: ⁠

Russia is unable to establish mass production of the Tu-160

https://ua.news/en/war-vs-rf/rosiia-ne-mozhe-nalagoditi-seriine-virobnitstvo-tu-160

More here
 … an extensive and in-depth exposé:

From Tu-95MS bombers to Kh-22 missiles: Seven Russian weapons Moscow can no longer manufacture

After consulting military analysts and weapons manufacturers, NV identified seven types of Russian weapons that Moscow continues to use against Ukraine but can no longer produce because of lost technologies, manufacturing expertise and Ukrainian missile strikes.

Russia’s military arsenal includes hundreds of types of weapons and equipment, ranging from Soviet-era systems to modern developments — from artillery and armored vehicles to jet-powered drones.

However, the loss of technological expertise means Russia’s current defense industry cannot manufacture every type of weapon the country uses in its war against Ukraine. Analysts believe that as stocks of weapons that cannot be replaced steadily dwindle, Russia’s military capabilities will eventually weaken.

NV[
New Voice of Ukraine
] consulted military analysts and weapons experts to identify the systems Russia’s armed forces continue to use but no longer manufacture. Each time an expert identified a particular weapon system, it received one point. The review includes Russian weapons and military equipment that received at least two expert points. Although the full range of military equipment Russia can no longer replace is broader, NV focused on key systems.

https://english.nv.ua/nation/experts-identify-seven-russian-weapons-moscow-can-no-longer-produce-nv-analysis-50661491.html

Russia’s losses in strategic bombers are irreplaceable, every 
Tu-95
 and 
Tu-22M3
 
destroyed by Ukraine represents a permanent reduction in Russia’s strategic air power. ⁠

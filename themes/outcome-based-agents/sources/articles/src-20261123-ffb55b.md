Cardano
 (
CRYPTO: ADA
) is trading about 95% below its all-time high of $3.10, reached in September 2021. Cardano’s price history shows the coin has hit five earlier lows, each down 90% or more from a previous high. Investors have seen almost the entire 16,332% increase that ADA achieved from its low of 1.88 cents in March 2020 to that peak wiped out. ADA now trades around 15.4 cents.

In 2026, sellers have been particularly aggressive. Since December 31, 2025, ADA has plummeted by 56.9%, and it has fallen 30.9% over the past month, reaching an intraday low of 13.32 cents on November 21, 2026. Over the last five years, it has lost 91.4%.

Cardano isn’t alone in drops this deep. We’ve run the same analysis on what 
Dogecoin

after every 90% drop
. So what does Cardano’s price history show about its performance six, 12, and 24 months after each major decline?

All five earlier lows led to gains after 12 months, even though ADA saw only a modest 16.0% gain following the December 2018 low. However, ADA broke two of these lows below, once in March 2020 after the December 2018 floor, and again in September 2023 after the December 2022 low.

The worst result came after the August 2024 low, with ADA trading 29.4% below that level by August 2026. That drop shapes how we assess 
Cardano’s potential recovery
 in 2027.

Staking, Smart Contracts and Politics Drove Each Cardano Rebound

The 2019 rebound had little behind it. ADA peaked at 10.71 cents in June 2019 but fell to a monthly close of 6.01 cents by July, continuing to decline until the pandemic sell-off in March 2020.

The price recovery in 2020, however, was driven by upgrades to the Cardano network. The Shelley upgrade allowed holders to stake ADA, securing the network while earning rewards. ADA then set its all-time high after Cardano announced the Alonzo hard fork, which brought smart contracts to the platform.

Later recoveries leaned on broader crypto rallies. In December 2023, ADA hit 67.7 cents, followed by a peak of $1.15 in November 2024 as 
Bitcoin
 (
CRYPTO: BTC
) reached an all-time high after Trump’s election victory. In March 2025, ADA reached $1.19 after Trump named Cardano among the coins for a U.S. strategic crypto reserve. It last traded above $1 in August 2025, and our comparison of 
Cardano and XRP under $1
 favors a return to that level.

While upgrades have boosted prices before, newer upgrades haven’t had the same effect as Alonzo. After Cardano completed its Van Rossum hard fork on July 18, 2026, ADA peaked at 26.52 cents in September 2026 but has since lost 42% of that value.

Cardano’s History Points to a Bounce, Not a Full Recovery

Based on Cardano’s history, a bounce from these deep lows looks likely, but a full recovery isn’t guaranteed. After each past floor, ADA gained within a year. However, its highest price after the 2021 peak was $1.32 in December 2024, which is still 757% above the current price. The long-term gain of 456.2% mainly reflects early investors and says little about those who bought near $3.10.

The next test will be Cardano’s Dijkstra upgrade, whose first phase targets code completion in the fourth quarter of 2026, according to Cardano.org. If ADA holds above its 13.32-cent low into 2027, history suggests a bounce like those in 2019 and 2023 might follow, as our look at 
$1,000 in Cardano
 by 2028 explores. But if ADA drops further, the 2018 and 2022 floors show the first deep low isn’t always the final bottom.

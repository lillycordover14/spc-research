The New York Rangers have had to deal with their fair share of some of the top stars in their division. Since 2005, the Rangers had to deal with Sidney Crosby, who was drafted the year prior by the Pittsburgh Penguins. Crosby quickly solidified himself as the league's top star, Not to mention, they watched him win three Stanley Cups during his tenure. But now, the Penguins are getting ready to prepare for life without Crosby.
After shocking the league by making the playoffs last season, the Penguins have fallen back to Earth, and are near the bottom of the Eastern Conference standings. Team president Kyle Dubas said recently that the plan moving forward is to make moves that are more focused on the future. That means, they are going to get ready to build a team for when the likes of Crosby, Evgeni Malkin, and Kris Letang 
are no longer in Pittsburgh
.
So, could this be the last season the Rangers see Crosby donning a Penguins uniform after over 20 years with the team?

Rangers may not have to deal with Sidney Crosby on Penguins based on uncertain future
While Crosby wants to be with the Penguins forever, that doesn't necessarily mean that it is set in stone. As Brisson mentions, there are still a couple of months to go until the trade deadline. Considering each team has only played over 20 games each, it is too early to tell if teams are true playoff contenders just yet. There is a chance the Penguins can turn things around. But they could also wave the white flag sooner rather than later.
The Rangers are familiar with that approach. Let's not forget that last season, the Rangers opted to send out a letter in January that they were going to undergo a retool. That decision coincided with their slow start to the season, and stars Igor Shesterkin and Adam Fox being sidelined long-term due to injuries.The Penguins could very well take that same approach in a couple of months time.
It truly all depends on what Crosby wants to do. He has been adamant about remaining in Pittsburgh for the rest of his career. But could Dubas' plans change his mind? Could he instead decide he wants to go for one more Stanley Cup win before retiring? Those are the questions that must be answered.

Ada County Democrats back Van Hollen over Schumer as US Senate leader&nbsp;&nbsp;Idaho Capital Sun

[page text]
14:40

Brief

U.S. Sen. Chris Van Hollen, a Maryland Democrat, is among four senators who say a stopgap spending bill must cut off funding for the Iran war. (Photo by Bryan P. Sears/Maryland Matters)

Ada County Democrats are calling for a change in U.S. Senate party leadership. 
The county party committee passed a resolution Monday to call on U.S. Senate Democrats to back U.S. Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland over New York’s U.S. Sen. Chuck Schumer as majority leader. 

A spokesperson for the statewide Idaho Democratic Party could not immediately be reached. 

Schumer has been in the Senate since 1998 and was first selected as majority leader in 2021. Van Hollen was first elected in 2016, and announced on Nov. 12 he was challenging Schumer for the leadership position. 

“This is the first contested race for a Democratic leader in decades, and it’s an opportunity to 
show the country a new direction,” Ada County Democratic Party Chair Jared DeLoof said in a press release. “The leadership of the past has not been adequate to meet the challenges working families are facing. We are in a crisis. The middle class is disappearing, the wealthy hoard money at the top while the rest of us scrape by. Artificial intelligence threatens the jobs we all depend on, all while corruption goes unchecked in Washington. We need major action now on AI, health care, inflation and jobs, and Senator Van Hollen has shown he will fight for it.”

During the Nov. 3 general election, Democrats flipped nine seats in the U.S. Senate and will have a 56-44 majority when the new Congress begins in January. 

Members of the party that controls the chamber select their 
majority leader,
 who sets the schedule for floor business each day and serves as a spokesperson for their party’s interests. 

The Ada County Democrats’ resolution was sent to Schumer, Van Hollen and members of the U.S. Senate Democratic Caucus. Both of Idaho’s senators are Republican and will not be selecting the majority leader.

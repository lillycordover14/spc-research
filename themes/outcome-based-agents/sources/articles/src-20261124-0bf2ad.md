The 60th anniversary of "Star Trek" has been marked by both highs and lows for fans of the beloved sci-fi franchise. While this massive milestone is obviously a cause for jubilation, the 
deaths of franchise legends like Sir Patrick Stewart
 and Walter Koenig have left Trekkies in deep mourning. However, it's the sad moments that make it all the more important to celebrate the happy times when they come. So, please join us in wishing "Star Trek: The Next Generation" alum Denise Crosby a happy birthday. Crosby turns 69 years old today. 

The actor was born on November 24, 1957, meaning she was just a few months out from her ninth birthday when 
the original "Star Trek" TV series
 premiered on NBC in September 1966. Little did Crosby know that she would be a founding cast member on sequel series "The Next Generation," which debuted in late September 1987, when the actor was 29 going on 30. Early in the hit sci-fi show's life, Crosby portrayed Lieutenant Natasha "Tasha" Yar, the chief security officer aboard the U.S.S. Enterprise-D. Nearly 40 years later, Crosby is not only still a total stunner, but she proves that a truly great hairdo never goes out of style.

Joy Malone/Getty

On "TNG," one of Tasha Yar's defining visual traits was Crosby's signature shaggy blonde pixie cut, which instantly made her stand out among the show's other, long-haired leading ladies like Marina Sirtis and Gates McFadden. And while Crosby may have famously left early in its run, she kept that iconic look in the proverbial divorce. The above photo, for instance, was taken just last month at a "Star Trek" 60th anniversary event in New York.

Denise Crosby has made peace with 'Star Trek' in the years since her abrupt 'TNG' exit

George Rose/Getty Images

As previously mentioned, the newly-69-year-old Denise Crosby, granddaughter of the 
legendary (and rather wealthy) jazz singer Bing Crosby
, portrayed Tasha Yar on "Star Trek: The Next Generation." However, while she was originally billed as a main cast member alongside the likes of Sir Patrick Stewart and LeVar Burton, Crosby's time on the show was surprisingly brief. Citing frustrations with the show's writers and the belief that her character was being underutilized, Crosby requested to be let go before the first season of "The Next Generation" had even concluded. She soon got her wish, though Tasha's departure ended up being a lot more permanent than a simple reassignment. 

Instead, the character was abruptly killed off near the end of Season 1 in the 1988 episode "Skin of Evil" — a development that has notably never been undone. At the start of Season 2, Lieutenant Worf, played by 
former "CHiPs" star
 Michael Dorn, replaced her as the U.S.S. Enterprise-D's head of security. However, thanks to sci-fi time-altering shenanigans (and a change of leadership in the writers' room), Crosby reprised her role as Tasha Yar on two separate occasions — first in the 1990 Season 3 episode "Yesterday's Enterprise," and again in the 1994 series finale, "All Good Things..." 

Additionally, Crosby briefly re-joined the fold to play an entirely different character named Sela in a handful of installments of Seasons 4 and 5. All told, though, of the more than 170 episodes that make up "TNG," Crosby physically appeared in fewer than 30 of them. Still, she has expressed little regret over how things played out. "There was no turning back. I knew I had to go," Crosby told 
IGN
 in 2024.

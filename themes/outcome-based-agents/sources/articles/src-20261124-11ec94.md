Long-term borrowing costs just got cheaper in a hurry: the 10-year yield slid 12 basis points to 4.92%, back below the 5% line that has been squeezing mortgage rates and equity valuations all autumn.

The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 4.92% after falling 12 basis points in a single session, back beneath the 5% threshold that had pressured mortgage rates and equity valuations through the autumn. Above 5%, financing costs for home purchases and corporate debt rise enough to visibly slow economic activity.

A 12-basis-point single-session drop is a large move for the Treasury market, where daily changes are typically measured in single digits.

Sponsored

A lower 10-year yield reduces the discount rate used to value future earnings, supporting rate-sensitive sectors like real estate, utilities, and growth technology. Mortgage rates, which track the 10-year closely, should also ease, unlocking demand that had been sidelined by affordability concerns.

At 4.92%, the yield still sits toward the upper end of its recent 4.6% to 5.3% range, so the next few sessions decide whether buyers hold the long end or sellers reassert control.

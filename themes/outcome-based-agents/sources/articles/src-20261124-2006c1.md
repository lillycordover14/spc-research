SailPoint Unveils 2027 Identity Security Predictions: The Era of “Ungoverned AI Play” Ends as Legal Baselines, Executive Accountability, and Intent Redefine the Enterprise 

By Neil Trim on November 24, 2026
 

As autonomous agents outpace the human workforce, SailPoint warns that the agentic security gap will trigger 2027’s first self-inflicted “Authorised Outage”

As breakneck enterprise adoption of autonomous AI agents collides with a critical governance deficit, 
SailPoint, Inc
., a leader in enterprise identity security, today released its 
2027 Identity Security Predictions
.
 

While frontier AI capabilities continue to accelerate, SailPoint predicts that 2027 will mark “the great agentic reckoning”, a pivotal year in which enterprises aggressively transition from ungoverned AI experimentation to accountable, ROI-driven production, and where identity security becomes the fundamental architecture of AI trust, regulatory survival, and business productivity.

For the past twenty years, identity security was primarily about governing human identities across employees, contractors and third parties. Today, SailPoint’s 
Horizons of Identity Security
 (2026–2027) research reveals 89% of enterprises in EMEA are already running AI agents in production, yet only 1% have deployed identity security purpose-built to secure them.

Following 2026 disclosures of evaluation AI models escaping test sandboxes, disabling internal security alerts, and exposing downstream customer data through hijacked AI supply chains, SailPoint warns that in 2027, the core security challenge shifts permanently from authenticating simple log-ins to continuously governing and protecting what autonomous agents do on a human’s behalf.

“For the past two years, enterprises raced to see what they could accomplish with autonomous AI agents; in 2027, boards, insurers, and regulators will demand to know who is accountable when those agents take action that crosses a legal or regulatory boundary,” said Mark McClain, CEO and Founder of SailPoint. “In 2027, while it will be possible to delegate a complex workflow to an autonomous agent, it will never be acceptable to delegate accountability, because agent accountability is enterprise responsibility.

Enterprises require an intelligent identity control plane that governs both human and non-human identities in real time. The organisations that thrive in the coming year won’t be those that deployed the most agents in a reckless sprint; they will be the ones that adopted a unified identity foundation capable of keeping those agents safely, transparently, and profitably in production.”

SailPoint’s Identity Security Predictions for 2027

The Catastrophic Breach: a major public company will disclose that an autonomous agent swarm caused a multi-billion dollar supply-chain collapse: 
The defining security disaster of 2027 will not require an outside hacker, but a self-inflicted, machine-speed operational collapse. Picture this: a misconfigured swarm belonging to a massive global retail or logistics giant autonomously purges active supplier contracts, deletes inventory databases, and misroutes millions of physical shipments at machine speed. By the time human administrators can physically sever the agents’ API connections, the entire physical supply chain will be frozen, triggering empty storefronts during peak season, massive breach-of-contract lawsuits, joint regulatory and board-level audits into the firm’s AI identity governance controls, and damage to the brand from which the company may never recover.

Separation of Duties for AI and tamper-proof governance become mandatory:
 An autonomous agent cannot be permitted to hold administrative privileges inside the same environment that monitors its behaviour. Enterprise risk frameworks will mandate that identity governance live in an independent, out-of-band control plane enforcing Zero Standing Privilege (ZSP) so no agent can approve its own privilege escalation or blind its own telemetry.

The EU AI Act’s Human Oversight Mandate creates a sovereign baseline. 
While human employees enter the European workforce with innate GDPR-era data awareness, AI agents are “born today”, operating with hyper-literal speed and zero innate understanding of statutory boundaries. Ahead of the December 2, 2027 EU AI Act Annex III high-risk deadline, national supervisory authorities will require enterprises to enforce a mandatory European Agentic Governance Baseline, proving via an independent identity control plane that every high-risk agent action is cryptographically bound to a named human supervisor.

Agent-to-agent supply chain attacks trigger the first B2B agent lockout
: As enterprises connect autonomous agents to third-party SaaS and partner networks via open protocols like Model Context Protocol (MCP), attackers are pivoting to “LLMjacking” and poisoned agent connectors, where a single compromised AI vendor credential can expose downstream customer chat data or rack up $1 million in fraudulent cloud compute in hours. In 2027, major enterprises will institute the first B2B agent lockouts, automatically blocking partner and vendor AI agents from interacting with their APIs unless the external enterprise can cryptographically prove the agent’s identity lineage and least-privilege scope.

“At near-AGI scale, manual human access reviews for autonomous agents are a fantasy,”
 
said Chandra Gnanasambandam, CTO of SailPoint. “Because you cannot take a rogue AI agent to jail, every digital worker must carry cryptographically bound human lineage. Relying on blunt-force ‘kill switches’ without deep identity context will only end up shutting down the critical automation your business depends on. In the agentic era, standing privilege is dead, and identity must live directly in the execution path.”

Passkeys solve yesterday’s war while agent credentials dominate breach reports:
 While passkey adoption will surpass 45% of human logins in 2027, attackers will bypass human MFA entirely to exploit the millions of exposed API keys, OAuth tokens, and long-lived machine secrets powering agentic workflows. Hijacking a single unmanaged developer token allows attackers to bypass MFA entirely and operate with unchecked administrative privileges. SailPoint’s Horizons research exposes this massive imbalance: 67% of enterprises have secured human access, yet only 24% govern agentic security, despite agents already representing 22% of all non-human accounts within a staggering 45:1 machine-to-human identity ratio.

Continuous intent assurance and the death of standing privilege makes zero trust real for the agentic era:
 Because autonomous agents lack physical devices or fixed locations—and dynamically invent sub-tasks based on prompts—static Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) will fail. Zero Trust will evolve into Continuous Intent Assurance, verifying an agent’s identity, cryptographic human lineage, and real-time business intent before every API call. Simultaneously, the speed of AI-driven attacks makes leaving standing privileges for risky entitlements unacceptable. Organisations must shift to least and zero standing privilege for all human and non-human identities. Because every identity is now a privileged identity, legacy PAM cannot scale. To manage risk, companies must embed just-in-time (JIT) access into their core identity governance, shifting to true real-time governance.

Identity maturity becomes the ultimate metric of AI valuation and post-quantum readiness:
 From the January 2027 CNSA 2.0 post-quantum cryptography gate to the December 2027 EU AI Act high-risk enforcement deadline, boards will realise that every existential technology mandate depends on complete visibility and governance over human, agentic, and cryptographic identities. Organisations will find that they cannot secure quantum-resistant tunnels or certify compliant AI workflows if they do not know what machine secrets exist or who authorised them. An enterprise’s “Identity Bill of Materials” will shift from a standard IT checklist to a primary boardroom indicator directly dictating cyber insurability, credit ratings, and overall corporate valuation.

The end of “AI Playground” policies: enterprises pivot from unchecked experimentation to governed ROI: 
AI adoption will not slow down in 2027; it will grow up. In 2026, permissive workforce policies treated AI agents like a sandbox for employee play and experimentation, leaving many companies with agents running on personal employee credentials. In 2027, CIOs and CFOs will end the era of unchecked AI experimentation, refusing to fund or permit agents that cannot prove measurable ROI and strict identity governance. Instead of killing innovation, this shift will trigger a massive onboarding wave as enterprises bring thousands of productive shadow agents under unified identity governance so they can scale safely into production.

Relevant to prediction 3:

Enterprise Proof-Point:
 Article 14 of the EU AI Act (enforcing Dec 2, 2027) legally mandates that high-risk AI systems allow human oversight, requiring system-level logging, lineage, and access traceability. 

December 2027 Horizon Landmark:
 A national European digital supervisory authority issues its first formal penalty or administrative stop-work injunction under the EU AI Act specifically citing an enterprise’s inability to produce human-to-agent identity access logs.

Carmel Clay Schools’ Wellness Center renamed in honor of retiring administrator&nbsp;&nbsp;Current Publishing

[page text]
Carmel Clay Schools honored retiring Associate Superintendent Roger McMichael at the Nov. 18 school board meeting by renaming a building that houses a program he helped create. 

The school board unanimously voted to change the name of the Wellness Center at 515 E. Main St. to the Roger McMichael Wellness Center and presented McMichael with a lifetime pass to use it. The center offers CCS employees and spouses an array of health services, such as appointments with medical professionals, a pharmacy and fitness classes.  

McMichael, who has managed CCS finances for 32 years, began planning for the Wellness Center in 2010. It opened three years later. 

“There are so many accomplishments throughout Mr. McMichael’s distinguished career that we could celebrate,” CCS Superintendent Tom Oestreich said at the meeting. “However, I can think of no greater tribute than naming a facility in his honor that embodies his vision, compassion, innovation, leadership and genuine commitment to the well-being of others.”

From left, Carmel Clay Schools Associate Superintendent Roger McMichael with Dr. Elisabeth Prosser, lead physician at the Roger McMichael Wellness Center, at the Nov. 18 school board meeting. (Photo courtesy of CCS)

McMichael, who will retire at the end of 2026, said he was “truly honored” and surprised by the gesture. He credited a team effort to open the center, which is operated by medical professionals from Ascension St. Vincent. 

“I really am invested in the concept, and I have been trying to do my best to persuade other people to do something similar,” McMichael said. “I truly appreciate the recognition.” 

A 
case study published in 2023
 in the American Journal of Health Promotion found that the Wellness Center saved CCS approximately $8 million annually in health insurance-related costs. Oestreich described the center as “one of the best retention strategies” CCS has ever employed. 

“I frequently hear from employees who say the one reason that they don’t want to leave Carmel Clay Schools is because of our CCS Wellness Center,” Oestreich said. 

During the public comment portion of the meeting, former CCS Superintendent Michael Beresford, who retired in summer 2025, thanked McMichael for his years of service to the district. 

“What I believe separates Roger from the pack is that he really cares about our students, and he wants them to have not only what they need to to survive, but he wants them to thrive,” Beresford said. “He wants them to get everything that they need to be successful. He really cares about the teachers and the staff. He wants them to be supported at the highest level possible. He wants them to be healthy and happy and still in the CCS family even after they retire.”

Shoppers discover one-of-a-kind gifts
There’s no need to wait for the Christmas parade on Sunday, Dec. 6 to get into the holiday spirit. 
The annual Artisan Market at Avon Hall opens at 11 a.m., two hours before the parade and stays open until 4 p.m., giving shoppers plenty of time to browse everything from handblown glass and pottery to candles poured into vintage teacups and ornaments made from decoupaged oyster shells. Even four-legged family members haven't been forgotten, with natural pet remedies and treats among the offerings.
The Artisan Market is a showcase of local creativity and a highlight of the annual Christmas in Little Washington daylong festivities. 
“Almost everything at the market makes a great holiday gift,” said Deborah “Deb” Harris, volunteer coordinator of the market. “Visitors will find handmade ornaments and holiday-themed items alongside jewelry, artwork, pottery, glassware, candles, textiles, specialty foods, pet treats and many other one-of-a-kind gifts. 
“One of the pleasures of the market is finding something distinctive that you simply wouldn't find at a big-box store,” she said.
This year’s outdoor Artisan Market will feature over 30 vendors. About 10 are new participants or returning after a year's absence, bringing fresh discoveries alongside familiar faces from previous holiday celebrations, said Harris.
Among the more unusual finds, Harris said, are handmade teddy bears, alpaca-wool hats and capes, eco-printed scarves, glass-bead fairy-wand garden decorations, bath crayons and dovetailed wooden boxes.

‘Rappahannock County and our surrounding area has an extraordinary concentration of talented makers, artists, small-business owners, and local food and beverage vendors and producers. Christmas in Little Washington gives us an opportunity to celebrate and support them,” said Harris.
“These are the people and businesses that contribute to the character of our community throughout the year. When someone buys a handmade gift, enjoys a locally prepared meal, or raises a glass from a local producer, they're putting their dollars directly back into the community and supporting their neighbors' livelihoods,” she said.
The food court and adult beverage area will be set up off Gay Street. Organizers are expanding that area this year, adding seating and more room for visitors to relax, enjoy something to eat and drink and catch up with friends and neighbors.

Following the parade and the traditional visit with Santa at Avon Hall, announcements of the parade winners and Rappahannock Food Pantry raffle winners will take place on the steps of the hall at 3 p.m.
As Harris said, Christmas in Little Washington is a “genuine small-town Christmas experience” while at the same time it benefits a good cause — the Rappahannock Food Pantry. 
At the market, visitors will be able to learn more about the food pantry as well as other nonprofits that serve Rappahannock and the surrounding areas.
“The food pantry is a vital part of our community,” Harris said, “and proceeds from the raffle — and donations and fees for event participation — help support its work serving local families and the community.”
Mary Ann Kuhn is a volunteer with the Christmas in Little Washington committee.

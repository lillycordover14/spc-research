Sidney Crosby reiterates his desire to remain a Penguin while offering some clarity on his future

Speculation about Sidney Crosby’s future in Pittsburgh has swirled amid the Penguins’ plummet to 31st in the NHL standings.
But his agent, Pat Brisson, told Sportsnet 590 The FAN on Monday that his most famous client hoped to be “a Penguin forever.”
Crosby certainly didn’t disagree with the comment his agent made on Toronto radio.
“I don’t think I have to at this point explain myself,” Crosby said Tuesday. “It’s pretty clear how much this team and place means to me. So I hope that it works out that I can do that.”

The 39-year-old Penguins captain remains unsigned for next season, which would be his 23rd in the NHL.

“It’s a matter of, at my age, just trying to figure out how long I want to play, and it’s mostly to do with that,” Crosby told the Post-Gazette. “While you’re in it, it’s a little difficult sometimes to try to weigh those things.
“I’m trying to focus on playing as much as I can and not as much that. But I understand that there’s a lot of interest there.”
Crosby does not have a set timeline for when he’ll make a decision about his future. He may wait until the offseason.
Advertise with us
“It’s something that could be done in a month. It could be done at the end of the year,” Crosby said. “ ... I don’t have much to tell you as far as any updates. So when it’s time, it’s time. But we haven’t got to that point yet.”
Crosby’s future in Pittsburgh is up to him, something president of hockey operations Kyle Dubas made clear last week while signaling the Penguins would 
likely lean harder into a rebuild this season
.
“We want to get into this as far as we can, have an indication about where we’re at and where we’re going and make sure everyone’s comfortable with that,” Dubas said. “And then he’s owed all the respect in the world. If he wants to be here, that’s great and that’s obviously what we would love, as well.”
Crosby has 15 points in 23 games, putting him on pace for the lowest total of his career by far. The Penguins appear to have no chance to reach the playoffs this season, much less the Stanley Cup.
When asked about his motivations and goals, Crosby reiterated the same team-focused mindset that has fueled him throughout his Hall of Fame career.
Advertise with us
“Just to be consistent, to contribute, try to play at a high level. And ultimately, with all that, you’re going out there to win every night,” Crosby said. “As an athlete, you want to play at a high level to help your team win and you want to have success. ... That’s always the motivation — just to do your best to contribute and to help the team win.”
Crosby acknowledged recently that trade rumors come with the territory when losses pile up. 
Ottawa Senators general manager Steve Staios even attended the Penguins’ game Saturday in Nashville and is reportedly interested in veteran wingers Bryan Rust and Rickard Rakell.
The Penguins will probably deal some of their veterans this season. It remains to be seen how that will impact Crosby’s thinking, though he re-signed with Pittsburgh in the summer after they traded away Jake Guentzel in 2024.
It seems like Crosby hasn’t made up his mind about his future. In the meantime, he’s focused on helping the Penguins climb out of the massive hole they’ve dug from their 7-14-2 start.

“Everybody puts a lot into this — players, staff. A lot of hard work goes into it, and I’ve been on both sides of it,” Crosby said. “When you’re winning, it’s a lot more fun.”
Around the boards
• After missing both games over the weekend with an upper-body injury, Evgeni Malkin returned to practice Tuesday and was a full participant. He was back on the top power play and the first line with Crosby and Rakell.
• Bryan Rust also skated with the team. He started in a non-contact jersey but swapped it for a regular one and “got a little bit of contact,” coach Dan Muse said. Rust has been out for over a month with a lower-body injury and is set to be reevaluated early next month.

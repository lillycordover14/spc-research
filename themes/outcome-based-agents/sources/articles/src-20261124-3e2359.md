Sierra, the AI customer service company co-founded by OpenAI board chair Bret Taylor and former Google executive Clay Bavor, 
said Monday it is raising
 $1.75 billion at a $30 billion valuation, roughly 75 times its 
more than $400 million
 in annual recurring revenue (ARR). That multiple only holds if Sierra's outcome-based pricing, which charges customers when an AI agent resolves a conversation rather than for the compute it burns, produces revenue that lasts. Anyone weighing AI customer service contracts, or judging whether private AI valuations are tracking real revenue, needs to understand that bet.
Greenoaks and Lightspeed are leading
 the round, and Benchmark, Sequoia, GV and Tiger Global are investing more. Sierra's own announcement says it is "raising" the money, while 
TechCrunch reported the round
 as raised, so the funds may not all have closed.
Valuation Doubled in Six Months While Revenue Also Doubled
The $30 billion figure is about twice the "more than $15 billion" valuation Sierra carried when it 
raised $950 million in May
, led by Tiger Global and GV. Revenue grew about as fast: Sierra reported 
$200 million in ARR in May
 and more than $400 million by October 30, and it says the third quarter alone added more than $150 million.
That makes the multiple compress, not inflate. At the $10 billion valuation, TechCrunch cited a 
100x revenue multiple on $100 million
 in ARR. Using the 
$15.8 billion figure reported
 for the May round, Sierra was priced near 79 times ARR, and it sits near 75 times now.

How Per-Outcome Billing Moves Inference Cost Onto Sierra
Traditional software bills per seat. Many AI vendors bill for usage, meaning the tokens a model processes, which passes the cost of running the model to the customer. Sierra instead charges when its agent completes a task such as resolving a support request, and says an 
unresolved conversation usually means no charge
. The logical consequence is that Sierra, not the customer, absorbs the inference cost of every conversation the agent fails to resolve. That is an analysis of the pricing model, not a figure Sierra has disclosed, and Sierra has not published its gross margin.
Sierra's engineering answer is to route each conversation through a 
constellation of more than 15 models
, with its own fine-tuned models handling some steps, rather than one large model. Its tooling includes 
Agent OS 2.0
.
The cost pressure is industrywide. 
ICONIQ's State of AI snapshot
 tracks average gross margins for AI products rising from 41% in 2024 to 45% in 2025, with a projected 52% in 2026, against 
70% to 85% for classic software
. Sierra's per-outcome model makes reliability a margin issue: every failed attempt costs compute with no revenue attached.
Reliability is measurable. Sierra's own τ-bench benchmark scores agents on repeated attempts at the same task, using a metric called pass^k, the probability that all k attempts succeed. In the 
2024 τ-bench paper
, GPT-4o completed fewer than half of tasks and scored under 25% on pass^8 in the retail domain. Those results are from 2024 and from Sierra's own benchmark, not an independent audit, and Sierra has not published current figures for its production agents.
Attribution is the other hard problem. Sierra's own 
June 2026 Outcomemaxxing post
 says outcome pricing is more complex than seat or consumption pricing operationally, contractually and in accounting terms. That is why the largest unstated question in this deal is whether "ARR" means the same thing for an outcome-priced company as for a seat-based one. ARR is a company-reported run-rate, and 
neither GAAP nor IFRS defines it
, so a figure built on per-outcome fees can move with conversation volume and with how outcomes are defined in each contract.

Is 75 Times Revenue High Compared With Harvey, Fin and Decagon?
It is the highest multiple among the comparable deals below, though some rivals are close or cannot be compared cleanly. Harvey was valued at 
$15.6 billion on about $400 million
 in ARR, roughly 39 times; Harvey's own blog gives $15.5 billion. Decagon, valued at 
$4.5 billion in January
, said in August its 
annualized revenue passed $100 million
, about 45 times. Parloa, 
valued at $3 billion in January
, reported more than $50 million in ARR in December 2025, about 60 times. Wonderful raised $550 million at a $5 billion valuation in September, and Calcalist 
estimated its run rate
 at $70 million, about 71 times.
Salesforce's $3.6 billion deal for Intercom's Fin, which 
closed September 10
, looks cheap by comparison at about 9 times a reported $400 million in ARR. But 
roughly $300 million of that
 is reported to be legacy software revenue, and only about $100 million is the Fin AI agent itself, which implies a multiple of up to 36 times on the AI portion.
Analysts quoted so far say the price needs more than headline growth. One analysis says the May valuation 
should not rest on revenue alone
, and another names 
net revenue retention
 on Fortune 50 accounts as the diligence item that matters. Sierra says its customers include BNY, HSBC, Singtel, Wells Fargo, JPMorganChase, Pearson, Next and Cox Automotive, and it claims 
almost half of the Fortune 50
, a figure that appears in its social post and in TechCrunch but not in its blog text and that Sierra has not independently documented.
Bret Taylor Chairs OpenAI's Board While Sierra Serves Many Model Providers
Taylor, Sierra's co-founder and CEO, also 
chairs the OpenAI board
, including the OpenAI Foundation and OpenAI Group PBC. In 2024 he told 
Bloomberg that OpenAI and Sierra
 do not compete, and told 
Fortune he would recuse
 himself from OpenAI matters whenever potential overlap arose. OpenAI is also pushing business agents of its own, with 
enterprise revenue now topping consumer
. Sierra's constellation design lets it swap model suppliers, but a company run by the chair of a possible competitor's board is a governance fact enterprise buyers can weigh.
Taylor's technology policy record is narrow. In October 2024 he told CNBC that AI policy should 
empower "little tech"
 and warned that well-intended constraints can help incumbents. His résumé, per 
his Wikipedia biography
, includes Google Maps, Facebook's CTO role and Quip. Bavor spent 18 years at Google, and has called customer service 
"hands down the first killer app"
 of generative AI for businesses. No documented science fiction engagement or direct AI legislative sponsorship was found for either executive.
Customer Service Job Losses and Chatbot Liability Cap What Agents Can Replace
Bavor told a HumanX audience in Amsterdam that 
most customers reassign contact center workers
 to other roles rather than replace them, which is the company's view and not independent headcount data. The Bureau of Labor Statistics 
counts about 2.67 million
 customer service representative jobs in 2025 and projects a 5% decline, about 141,800 jobs, from 2025 to 2035.
Failure carries legal and reputational cost. A Canadian tribunal held Air Canada liable for wrong information its website chatbot gave a customer in 
Moffatt v. Air Canada
, citing negligent misrepresentation. Klarna, which cut human support for AI, 
rehired humans in 2025
 after acknowledging lower quality, though it says it remains AI-first. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau 
reported in 2023
 that all of the top 10 US commercial banks use chatbots and that chatbot failures can cause law violations, diminished service and other harms. In the EU, the AI Act's Article 50 chatbot disclosure duty 
has applied since August 2
, with a 
grace period to December 2
 only for marking pre-existing generative systems' content.

Buyers should treat this round as a verdict on a pricing model, not just a product. Sierra's $30 billion price assumes outcome-based fees stay profitable as inference costs fall, that customers keep paying as agents handle more, and that ARR built on per-outcome fees is as durable as seat-based revenue. The valuation multiple has barely moved since May while revenue doubled, but reliability data, gross margin and net revenue retention remain undisclosed. Those three numbers, more than the $30 billion headline, decide whether 75 times revenue was cheap or a peak.
Frequently Asked Questions
How much is Sierra worth after the $1.75 billion round?
Sierra is 
valuing itself at $30 billion
, about twice the more-than-$15 billion valuation from May, on more than $400 million in ARR.
What is outcome-based pricing for AI agents?
Instead of billing per seat or per token, the vendor charges when the agent completes a defined result, such as resolving a support case. The vendor then bears the compute cost of attempts that fail, which Sierra describes in its 
outcome-based pricing explanation
 explanation.
Does ARR mean the same thing for Sierra as for a software-subscription company?
Not necessarily. ARR is a company-reported run-rate, not an audited figure, and per-outcome revenue varies with conversation volume and with how each contract defines a successful outcome. Sierra itself calls outcome pricing 
more complex operationally
 than seat or consumption models.
Will AI customer service agents replace call center workers?
Sierra's Bavor says most customers reassign roles, but the Bureau of Labor Statistics 
projects customer service jobs
 to fall 5% by 2035.

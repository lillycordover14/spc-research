ILOILO CITY
 – Three communities in Iloilo City are expected to benefit from an additional source of potable water following the inauguration of a seawater reverse osmosis treatment facility in Barangay Nabitasan on Tuesday.
 
Developed by Metro Pacific Water, the PHP150-million facility can produce up to one million liters of treated water daily, enough to meet the estimated daily needs of about 1,000 households.
 
Metro Pacific Iloilo Water (MPIW) will distribute the additional supply through its existing pipeline network to households, businesses, and institutions in Barangay Nabitasan, Barangay Bantud, and Magsaysay Village.
 
MPIW Chief Operating Officer Angelo David Berba said the facility draws saline groundwater from a 52-meter-deep well and processes it through several filtration stages.
 
“It filters out dirt and other particles, then uses special filters called membranes to remove dissolved salts. The water undergoes further treatment and disinfection to meet Philippine drinking water standards before distribution,” he said.
 
In her message, Mayor Raisa Treñas said the city is preparing not only for the El Niño phenomenon but also for its water requirements over the next decade.
 
She said the city is pursuing additional and alternative water sources to ensure a reliable supply.
 
“As a mayor, I don't want our people to reach a point that they have to worry about whether there will be water coming out from their faucets tomorrow,” Treñas said.
 
Through the desalination plant and other water supply projects, the city is strengthening its water sources and improving its preparedness, particularly during droughts, she added.
 
Treñas also stressed that water infrastructure must keep pace with the city's growth, increasing investments, and expanding economic opportunities.
 
The newly inaugurated modular facility is the first of five planned water treatment plants in Iloilo City aimed at augmenting supply and meeting rising demand.

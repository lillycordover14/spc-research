President Donald Trump has nominated outgoing Forsyth County District Attorney Jim O'Neill to serve as a federal judge the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of North Carolina.

President Donald Trump announced Tuesday that he is nominating outgoing Forsyth County District Attorney Jim O'Neill to a federal judgeship in the U.S. District Court's Middle District of North Carolina.
O'Neill has served more than four terms as district attorney, but he was weeks away from being out of a job. Democrat Amara Hunter, an 
assistant public defender and former police officer
, defeated him in November's election. 
In a news release, North Carolina's U.S. senators announced that they had jointly put O'Neill's name forth for the lifelong appointment. Both Sen. Ted Budd and Sen. Thom Tillis are Republicans, like O'Neill, and they pointed to his prosecution efforts targeting sex offenders and repeat offenders.
"Jim O’Neill has built a strong record of public service and brings considerable legal experience to the federal bench. I am confident he will uphold the Constitution, respect the rule of law, and remain dedicated to serving the people of North Carolina," Tillis wrote in a statement.
Longtime Tillis political consultant Paul Shumaker also 
worked on O'Neill's
 recent re-election effort. O'Neill has typically easily won re-election, but Shumaker accurately warned that political headwinds against Republicans could stymie him this time around.
The judgeship became available when District Court Judge Thomas D. Schroeder, a George W. Bush appointee, announced his decision to 
seek senior status
 on July 31. Senior status is 
a form of semi-retirement
 for experienced federal judges in which they can be replaced by a full-time judge while keeping their regular salary on a reduced workload.
The current Republican-controlled Senate has just weeks to potentially confirm O'Neill and about 20 other federal judges before Democrats take control of the chamber in January. 
That list includes two other North Carolina appointees. Brian Meyers, a sitting U.S. Magistrate Judge, would replace Judge Terrence Boyle as a U.S. District Court Judge in the Eastern District of North Carolina if confirmed. And Phil Aubart, the top assistant to the U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of North Carolina, would serve as a U.S. District Court Judge in the state’s Middle District.
Aubart and Meyers testified before the Senate Judiciary Committee last week.
On O’Neill, Budd wrote in a statement, "I thank President Trump for nominating a strong constitutionalist to serve the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of North Carolina. I urge my Senate colleagues to confirm him swiftly.”
A simple majority vote is needed for the Senate to confirm an appointment, and Republicans hold 53 seats in the chamber. 
In 2026, a 
federal district court judge
 earned $249,900.
"The people of North Carolina are lucky to have a dedicated public servant like Jim who will be a fantastic Judge. Now, let's get him confirmed!" Trump, also a Republican, wrote in a Truth Social post announcing the appointment.
U.S. Senator Dick Durbin, D-Ill., called for Senate Republicans to not consider O’Neill’s nomination, calling it part of a Trump Administration effort to confirm as many judges as possible before Democrats take control of the Senate. Durbin is the ranking Democrat on the Senate Judiciary Committee, which will consider the nomination.
“There simply isn’t enough time to allow Senators to fulfill their constitutional responsibility to provide advice and consent. This is clearly an attempt to bypass Senator-elect Roy Cooper, who is entitled to be consulted on who will fill this seat,” Durbin wrote in a 
social media post
.
Former Gov. Bev Perdue appointed O'Neill as Forsyth County's district attorney in 2009, and he then won re-election four times.
Even while serving as district attorney, O'Neill has repeatedly sought higher elected office in North Carolina. 
In 2016, he ran for the Republican Party's nomination for attorney general, losing to State Senator Buck Newton in that year's 
primary election
.
O'Neill won a three-way Republican primary for attorney general in 2020, beating now-State Board of Elections Director Sam Hayes and criminal justice reform advocate Christine Mumma. That fall, then-incumbent Attorney General Josh Stein 
defeated 
O'Neill by about 13,600 votes.
Then, in 2024, O'Neill 
finished second
 in an 11-person primary for Republicans' lieutenant governor nomination.

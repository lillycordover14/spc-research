As the Workday Rising season winds down, there is a change: customers are now ready to share go-live lessons with Workday AI agents. Here are field views from Intermountain Health, Ochsner Health and CLEAResult. One that I've been tracking: Workday's Deployment Agent in action.

[page text]
Robyn Funcannon, Intermountain Health

At this year's enterprise events, we finally saw a notable shift: 
a critical mass of customers with live AI agent stories to tell
. Granted, many of these agentic stories were early stage, but they were still instructive. 

Given the amount of agentic hype we've heard from the keynote stages all year, digging into field stories was welcome.

Workday's Rising events are a good example; I already shared some vivid exchanges in 
Workday Rising - customers press customers on AI adoption and ROI, and get answers
. 

When customers start answering other customers' questions on AI pricing and ROI, you are getting somewhere. 

But there is more. As I write this, Workday Rising EMEA is winding down. It's a good time to share a few more highlights from my customer 1:1s in Las Vegas a few weeks ago, before tarmacs and takeout took over. 
Across agentic projects, what early lessons can we derive? 

Can AI agents reduce administrative friction? Tracking the agentic progress at Ochsner Health

Reduce administrative friction? That's a big goal for 
Ochsner Health
, which has embarked on the use of Sana agents in Workday for candidate engagement, employee and manager self-service, and case management. 

But as Stacy Bundrik, VP, Corporate Systems, Ochsner Health told me, a big win for Ochsner was getting live on Workday's Enterprise Management Cloud across HR and Financials (Ochsner Health went live on Workday Finance and Supply Chain Management in late July 2026; they are also running Adaptive Planning). I asked Bundrik: is there an AI advantage to being live across an end-to-end platform, versus launching Ai agents as point solutions? (It's a question a lot of enterprises are debating at this moment in time). Bundrik: 

If you look at the the schematics of what AI is supposed to look like, which is systems of record, and then data layer and orchestration layer, you'll be able to do that, but you would have to go outside in order for those different agents to orchestrate... You'll be able to do that, but if you're doing it inside, then you don't have to go to the orchestration layer or the data layer, it's just inside inherently in there... That's a whole lot easier to roll out than having it go and orchestrate across an orchestration layer.

We're back to the undeniable truth: 
adoption isn't magic, even with the best AI system you can conjure. 
User adoption and managing change are still inseparable. Bundrik says that as they started their Workday AI plans, users were well aware of interfaces like ChatGPT. She wanted users to connect the dots: can a modern ERP system bring a governed version of this to life? Bundrik: 

The world outside was changing, and we're building this giant ERP. The interesting part was having to reconcile with people who would come to me with problems, and I would say, 'Well, actuality, in about three months, that's not going to be a problem anymore.' And educating those people on the fact that the world was changing. 

Soon, says Bundrik, users will just type what they need from Workday into a text interface, rather than scour through reports. Things are moving fast: almost 5,000 employees have used Workday's Self-Service Agent with Sana for HR:

If you think about keeping those people inside their system - they're not having to go outside their system, right? Now you add that self-service, and it's like immediate return. I know exactly what my benefits are.

Intermountain Health - agentic results start with operational changes

Intermountain Health
 was front and center in Las Vegas, sharing their agentic story from the keynote stage. Intermountain Health's Mike Harmer (VP of Enterprise Business Platforms/ERP Digital Services) spoke during the Community Keynote about the impact of Workday agents in a healthcare setting.

So far, Intermountain Health has started deployed agents across operations—including a Self-Service Agent, Payroll Agent, and Developer Agent. On stage, Workday's Shannon McGovern asked Harmer about the live AI agents so far. As Harmer told attendees: 

The Self-Service Agent has been amazing because we've turned that on two or three weeks ago, and we've already got several thousand users now where our caregivers are going through and using that to solve problems. It's been really great - Payroll Agent and several different ones [are] coming.

You can turn the agents on quickly, but: it's the organizational changes that are key. Harmer:

We can turn that on, but it's the operational changes, right? You bring those new capabilities. You give them to the team of caregivers, and they need to rethink their workflows and how different teams work together. And for individuals, you're now empowering a caregiver and employee with new things that they can do, and they've got to step back, and rethink all of that. 

Another lesson from Harmer: pick the right agents for your environment; don't just load up on agents for the sake of it.

So I think getting that alignment, so that we're picking the agents together from the IT and the operational side, the ones that are going to really make a difference and match with our strategy. 

After the keynote, I sat down with Robyn Funcannon, Senior Director of Business Applications, Intermountain Health. Fucannon says the first challenge is getting out in front of the change: 

It's really trying to get ahead of the curve, rather than kind of playing catch up. Workday right now is releasing agents. I swear, left and right. We're so used to this six month cycle of new features, and now they're just dropping them every week, every two weeks, monthly....

So how is Intermountain Health managing the new pace of change? Funcannon says this next phase cannot be a tech-centric project:

We held a large workshop with our operational partners, because AI cannot just be tech-driven. You have to have operations tied into that mix, and operations needs to have a level of comfort with the AI. Traditionally, healthcare is pretty risk averse - so how do we start to surface that, and create the baby steps to where they're going to feel comfortable using it, thinking it's trustworthy? 

For Intermountain Health, part of managing AI change is keeping humans in the process - wherever it makes the most sense.

We do have kind of a big phrase that we use: still keeping human in the loop, just to manage and help support some of that risk. We're starting to take that venture where we are part of Workday Extend Pro. So we're coming up with custom agent use cases as well to start to build and develop out for that.

But we've already got potential roadmap agents for with each of the business groups, and then understanding what does that look like for them, because there could be a potential change management component that they'll need to own and support. 

CLEAResult shares early lessons from Workday's Deployment Agent

Of all the agentic demos I saw this year, Workday's Deployment Agent was the most compelling - and I wasn't alone.  We discussed the Deployment Agent at the Workday Innovation Summit for analysts in April 2026. Why was I bullish on this agent? Because it bears down on the brass tacks: 
can we deliver more successful projects? And why aren't we using agentic technologies to attack that problem? 

At Workday Rising, I got a live review from Stacy Cornelius, Senior IT Manager, 
CLEAResult
 (CLEAResult is billed as "
North America's largest provider of energy efficiency, energy transition and decarbonization solutions). 
But first, check out this description from
 Google Gemini
 on the core abilities of Deployment Agent: 

The Workday Deployment Agent is an AI-powered conversational assistant that gives Workday implementers, partners, and administrators configuration and troubleshooting guidance directly inside Workday environments (such as Implementation, Sandbox, Preview, and Customer Central). Built with LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), it draws from Workday Administrator and User Guides, Community articles, implementer-only documentation, and WSDLs—without using customer data to train its underlying models.

I like these capabilities because they tie to customer success - and should be linkable to customer success metrics. And: they should provide Workday's partners with tools to deliver better/higher value projects. A few use cases: 

Configuration Intelligence: Provides step-by-step setup instructions (such as creating calculated fields or linking compensation grades) and supports Configuration Catalog tasks.

Technical Troubleshooting: Helps diagnose SOAP/web service errors, condition rules that do not trigger, business process issues, and security configurations.

Knowledge Discovery: Explains complex platform concepts and surfaces links and citations from official guides and community sources.

I asked Cornelius: how is the Deployment Agent working out so far? As she told me: 

I decided I need to play with it first before I encourage my team. I had a use case... I needed to come up with some calculated fields for a complicated report...

So I thought, I'm going to throw this into Deployment Agent, and see what it comes up with. It literally spit out the order I needed to create the calculated fields and how I needed to connect them. It saved me, I would say 75% of the time. 

And what has the team been using it for so far? 

We typically get requests for projects from the stakeholders. Sometimes it's part of the new releases; they just want to see how much time it would take us to set up, or just anything new... Normally we would start out by researching the Workday Community. Now, most of the time they go to Deployment Agent first. So it's been a pretty big time saver for my team specifically.

Cornelius says she is looking forward to future iterations, where the agent will act on the project guidance and documentation it creates.

My take - customers speak to controlled autonomy, self-service, and the pace of change

Managing the pace of change is one big lesson that jumps out here, but there are more. Self-service/research use cases are clearly valuable for getting teams in agent adoption mode. Human approvals keep the balance between autonomy and control, allowing teams to define their comfort levels.

This goes back to a 
Workday customer theme from Elevate New York City:
 customers fare better when they play an active role defining their own governance and levels of autonomy, 
rather than trying to force premature autonomy for its own sake
.

Owning your data strategy also stands out. In the case of Intermountain Health, this includes a push into the Workday Data Cloud, and a shift into unified data and zero-copy architectures. (Looks like their approach is working; Intermountain Health was recognized at Workday Rising 2026 with a North America Customer Powering Innovation Award for its transformation). 

Vendors will fare better in the agentic economy if their ecosystems add choices and depth. As I've said, either 
disrupt yourself with your own partners, or be disrupted from the outside
. In the case of Ochsner Health, they have recently implemented Workday partner Auditoria for AP automation. And: they were already customers of Paradox, prior to 
Workday's completion of the Paradox acquisition in October 2025
, As Stacy Bundrik told me: 

We've actually had a really good response around Paradox. It makes the recruiting process smarter. It makes it easier for us. It makes it easier. More importantly, for the candidates.

Which gets us to a burning question: 
what kind of AI do you want, and how do you measure the results?
 I went 
deeper into Workday costs/results in prior pieces
. But one thing I'll point out here is: yes, let's use AI agents to improve processes, not just trend towards efficiency. Instead of brute force screening, let's make application easier for candidates.

Let's design systems that find us the best talent, rather than screen out in a brute force way to manage volume. I can't force customers down this path, but I do like hearing it in their own words.

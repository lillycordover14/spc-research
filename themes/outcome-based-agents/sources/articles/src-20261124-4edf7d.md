Clay Travis has long been one of ESPN’s loudest critics, but that won’t stop Stephen A. Smith from trying to get him on 
First Take
.

Smith dedicated a more than hour-long episode of 
Straight Shooter
 on his YouTube channel this week to pushing back on the criticism he’s received for 
claiming the Los Angeles Lakers won’t win a championship
 so long as their three best players are white. Travis called the 
argument racist
 and challenged Smith to a debate on whatever platform he wants. Smith rebuked the offer, believing there is no debate to be had on this issue. But that doesn’t mean Smith isn’t interested in debating other sports topics with Travis.

During the 
Jeff Pearlman portion of Smith’s response to his critics
, Smith noted Pearlman directed a lot of animosity toward him. And when questioned about where the animosity came from, Smith suggested Travis should target Pearlman, which led to his admission about a possible collaboration on 
First Take.

<span data-mce-type="bookmark" style="display: inline-block; width: 0px; overflow: hidden; line-height: 0;" class="mce_SELRES_start">﻿</span>

“I have no problem with Clay Travis,” Smith insisted. I actually wanted Clay Travis on 
First Take.
 That just wasn’t something I could pull off this year. I don’t mind debating Clay Travis any day of the week. Don’t bother me at all. I got no problems with Clay Travis. But those are companywide decisions from time to time. Particularly when you’re involved in the world of politics and beyond.”

Travis sold OutKick to Fox News in 2021, and he 
formally exited the site
 he built last June. He remains a Fox News contributor, however, and continues to host 
OutKick the Show
 on FS1 every Wednesday evening, which may have put a damper on any possible deal with ESPN or 
First Take
.

But Travis also built an entire sports media company by essentially branding it as the anti-ESPN. He spent years mocking the network and much of its talent, including past and 
current 
First Take contributors
. It’s fair to wonder whether some of those contributors would have been willing to share the stage with Travis on ESPN.

While Smith had the power to bump Max Kellerman from 
First Take
, he has made it clear that ESPN has the final say on who joins him on the show. Smith vouched for Shannon Sharpe and Ryan Clark in recent years to no avail. And apparently, they similarly said no to the idea of Travis being on 
First Take
.

If there’s still a bridge that could land Travis on ESPN, Smith would probably know about it. But it would have seemed more likely that Travis had probably burned that bridge long ago.

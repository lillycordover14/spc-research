A boycott of the American Diabetes Association (ADA) now includes 1,221 members and has erased the masthead of the group's flagship journal, Diabetes Care, 
STAT reported
 Nov. 23. The dispute comes weeks before the ADA's 2027 diabetes care guidelines are due. The ADA confirmed they will be published online in Diabetes Care in December.
The guidelines, known as the Standards of Care in Diabetes, are updated every year and shape how clinicians screen for and treat diabetes and prediabetes. Physicians familiar with their production told STAT the 2027 edition was nearly complete.
For patients and families who never read a medical journal, the question is simple: could a fight among scientists change the care they receive? For now, current treatment recommendations remain in place, and the new guidelines appear to be on schedule.
Empty Masthead, Interim Editors and a Resignation
Editor-in-chief Steven Kahn and the journal's deputy editors, including Elizabeth Selvin, are part of the boycott. The ADA has appointed an interim editorial board and says its members' names will be posted on the Diabetes Care website soon.
That decision prompted a resignation. Chris Kohler, the ADA's vice president for professional publications, resigned Nov. 6. He wrote that the association moved ahead with interim editors despite his repeated objections, and he raised concerns about the lack of an accountable editor-in-chief and of a formal process for vetting the interim editors, 
Retraction Watch reported
.
The ADA pushed back, saying Kohler's letter 
"does not accurately reflect the caliber or qualifications of the individuals involved."
 It said Kahn's term ends in December 2027, that it has opened applications for a new editor-in-chief and that it plans to meet with boycott leaders. The association also said it will review its event protocols and code of conduct.
The journal's status matters beyond academia. Kahn told Retraction Watch that about 400 manuscripts submitted since the boycott began had not been handled. Slower peer review can delay when new research reaches clinicians, though it does not change studies already published.
Since MedicalDaily's September Report
MedicalDaily reported on Sept. 16 that 
Diabetes Care editors had halted manuscript reviews
 as the boycott began. At that time, Kahn said hundreds had signed, and the ADA said the journal was still accepting and reviewing submissions.
Much has changed since then. In October, an internal ADA review acknowledged some missteps but defended the association's rebukes of members who objected to Trump administration policies. The boycott count has reached 1,221, the journal's top editors remain absent, and its publications chief has resigned.
The dispute began in June, when five members, including Kahn, were removed from the ADA's scientific meeting in New Orleans after handing out printouts of a journal editorial critical of NIH funding cuts.
Researchers are now shifting their work. John Buse, a former ADA president at the University of North Carolina, has stopped submitting papers to ADA journals and does not plan to attend the June 2027 meeting in Washington, D.C. David Nathan, founder of the Diabetes Center at Massachusetts General Hospital, called the conflict 
"a colossal blunder"
 and said NIH funding cuts and the ADA turmoil together have left diabetes researchers anxious.
The 2027 Standards and the Patients Who Feel Them
The standards are written by the ADA's 
Professional Practice Committee, an expert panel
 that updates them annually. They are published as a supplement to Diabetes Care, but they are not produced by the journal's editorial board.
The document covers nearly every part of diabetes care, from 
diagnosis and screening
 to 
blood pressure and cholesterol targets
, kidney and eye care, diabetes technology, and care for children, older adults and pregnant people. The ADA describes it as the gold standard in evidence-based guidelines for diagnosing and managing diabetes and prediabetes.
Recent editions have shaped everyday care. The 
2026 edition of the Standards of Care
, released Dec. 8, 2025, expanded recommendations for continuous glucose monitoring, added guidance on 
obesity medications
 and covered the heart, kidney and liver benefits of glucose-lowering drugs.
Concerns center on future editions more than on this December. Selvin told STAT that without the journal's usual governance, readers must simply trust that the ADA is not making decisions that benefit industry more than patients. Mark Atkinson, who resigned as chair of the planning committee for the 2026 scientific meeting, said journals are how research reaches patient care.
"Without having medical journals, you don't have a real way to impact how patients are treated in their care,"
 Atkinson told STAT.
There is no evidence so far that the content of the 2027 standards has been affected. Their recommendations will not be known until release.
Steps for Patients Before and After the December Release
People with diabetes should not change medicines, insulin doses or glucose targets because of the dispute. Treatment plans set with a clinician remain the guide, and any changes in the 2027 standards are best discussed at a regular visit.
Patients can ask at their next appointment whether new recommendations apply to them, such as guidance on glucose monitors, 
weight-management drugs
 or kidney and heart protection. Insurance coverage for devices and medicines depends on each plan, not on the guidelines alone.
Clinicians also draw on other sources. The ADA and the European Association for the Study of Diabetes published a 
joint consensus report on type 2 diabetes management
 on Oct. 2, and other endocrine societies issue their own guidance.
Very high blood sugar with vomiting, confusion, deep breathing, or fruity-smelling breath can signal diabetic ketoacidosis and needs emergency care. So does severe low blood sugar with confusion or loss of consciousness.

Key Questions Answered
Why are ADA members boycotting?
 The boycott began after five members were removed from the ADA's June meeting for handing out an editorial critical of NIH funding cuts.
How many people have joined?
 STAT reported 1,221 members as of Nov. 23.
Will the 2027 Standards of Care still come out?
 Yes. The ADA confirmed they will be published online in Diabetes Care in December.
Who writes the guidelines?
 The ADA's Professional Practice Committee, not the journal's editorial board.
Does this change my diabetes treatment now?
 No. Current recommendations remain in place. Do not change medicines without talking to a clinician.
What should patients ask their doctor?
 Whether any 2027 recommendations, such as those on glucose monitors or newer drugs, apply to their care.

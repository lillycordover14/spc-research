GALLUPVILLE — While the Holiday Artisan Market has run in various forms for a number of years, Kathy Catlin has been collaborating with the Gallupville House Association since 2024 to make the market a annual community tradition.
Each November, a juried group of crafters and vendors band together in the historic 1872 Gallupville House to sell everything from soaps and honey to tinctures and pottery.
Catlin has been a part of the program since 2005, helping to run it with friends and selling her pottery. Then, the market fell apart.
“COVID came, and the market just died,” Catlin said.
She couldn’t see an event like that go to waste, though, so she got it up and running again, and now, Catlin added, the market is running stronger than ever. It's a one-day event and it was held Nov. 14 this year.
“A lot of the people here selling this year are familiar faces," she said. "They’ve been with me for two or three years, and we have people who come every year to buy from us.”
Catlin likes to run a juried program, where there is only one vendor for each art type. And to get in, they have to make it through Catlin.

“We’re trying to keep the charm,” Catlin said. “We love hosting at the Gallupville house. The building lends itself to that holiday feel, but there’s only so much room.”
This year, the only new addition to the roster was Earthly Remedies, run by Erin Ethier. This herbalist sells alternatives to Western medicine, including syrups, teas, herbal salves and creams, flower essences and botanical skincare.
Ethier added to what Catlin likes to describe as the vendor’s “mini community.” Many of the sellers at the Gallupville House are tied into other markets, but always make it back for the Holiday Artisan Market, Catlin said.
Within the “mini community” are local vendors selling a wide variety of handmade goods. Besides Earthly Remedies’ botanical care, the Nov. 14 Holiday Artisan Market featured a jewelry and goldsmith table by Rise Designs fine jewelry, honey from Sun Mountain Apiary, seasonal brews from the Hardscrabble Coffee trailer, endless timberwork from Green Pine Woodworks in Catskill and prints, cards and framed shots from Andrea Lawyer Photography.
To top it all, the market keeps the generous holiday spirit by functioning as a fundraiser for the Gallupville House Association.
“Everything is done through donations here. The vendor’s tables, anytime we place an advertisement, stuff like that,” Catlin said. “Anything to keep the community going.”

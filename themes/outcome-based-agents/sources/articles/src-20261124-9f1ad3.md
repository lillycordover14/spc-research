The Clark County School District recommends closing six schools around the valley over the next three years as enrollment continues to decline. 

In an announcement Monday evening, the district said it could begin closing schools next year and redistributing students to nearby campuses. All are elementary schools: Helen Anderson Toland International Academy in the Historic Westside, Richard J. Rundle Elementary on the east side, Marc A. Kahre Elementary in the west valley, Quannah McCall Elementary and Marion Cahlan Elementary in North Las Vegas, and C.T. Sewell Elementary in Henderson. 

The district also uses the terms “consolidate” and “merge” when describing the proposed changes. The school board will discuss 
the proposal
 on Dec. 2. 

The mergers are part of a $3.7 billion bond investment that would also include 19 school renovations and eight school replacements. 

“While there will be fewer CCSD schools, the school buildings in use will be newer and more energy-efficient, directing additional financial resources to the classroom,” the district said in its announcement. 

The district said paring down the number of campuses and updating others would give students access to better facilities and programming, such as science, technology, engineering and math, career and technical education and fine and performing arts. 

“Every student in Clark County deserves to learn in a safe, modern school building equipped for today’s learners,” Superintendent Jhone Ebert said in a statement. “These recommendations allow us to invest responsibly in school renovations and replacements, expand high-demand magnet programs, and bring school communities together so we can offer more dedicated adults on campus and richer curricular opportunities for our students.” 

CCSD has been refining its latest facility master plan for about two years. That has included 
public outreach through forums
 and surveys, along with analyses on enrollment trends and facility conditions. 

The student count has been sliding for nearly a decade, which the district largely attributes to declining birth rates that are mirrored around the country.  

CCSD enrollment peaked in 2017 with around 327,000 students. Last year it had about 288,000; last month, about 278,000. It further anticipates serving 45,000 fewer students over the next five years. 

It wasn’t immediately clear what CCSD would do with all of the vacated buildings, although it did propose using Rundle for its Student Services Division and adult education, and Kahre for the new headquarters for the CCSD Police Department. The department is housed in an industrial park in Henderson. 

Kahre Elementary is named for Metro Police officer Marc Kahre, who was fatally shot in the line of duty in 1988.  

The district said it would continue to consult with the community, including local governments, “to make the best use of surplus properties and school buildings to provide services that support our CCSD students and their families, as well as the community.” 

Closure and reassignment details
 

Toland and Rundle could close next year, Kahre in 2028, and McCall, Cahlan and Sewell in 2029. 

Students from Toland, 1735 D St., would move to Matt Kelly Elementary, less than a mile away at 1900 N. J Street. Kelly is under reconstruction, and a new campus is expected to open next year with an attached community center and social services.

Toland’s magnet program would come with it to the new building, and CCSD suggests renaming the building The Helen Anderson Toland International Academy at the Matt Kelly Campus. Toland’s current building is about 70 years old and “faces major infrastructure failures,” CCSD said. Students can also choose to attend Wendell P. Williams Elementary School, a little more than a mile south of Toland. 

Rundle students would move from Rundle, 425 N. Christy Lane, into its “sister” school next door, Keller Elementary. Some students may be rezoned to nearby Edwards and Mendoza elementary schools to prevent them from crossing major streets on the way to school. Keller is scheduled for renovation by 2028. 

Students at Kahre, 7887 W. Gowan Road, would move next door to Eisenberg Elementary, which is planned for renovations soon. Families can also attend what is currently Molasky Junior High, about half a mile away. Molasky is being renovated for reconfiguration as a prekindergarten-8th magnet school beginning in 2028. 

McCall, 800 E. Carey Ave., and Cahlan, about three-quarters of a mile away at 2801 Fort Sumter Drive, would send most students to what is currently Bridger Middle School. Bridger is also set to be converted into a prekindergarten-8th magnet. Some Cahlan students may attend Lincoln or Herron elementaries. 

Students from Sewell, 700 E. Lake Mead Parkway, would attend what is now Brown Academy of International Studies, a junior high school about a mile away. Brown will also be converted into a prekindergarten-8th magnet. 

Recent school closures
 

CCSD has been approaching closure discussions for the last couple of years. 

The district considered permanently closing the small Lundy Elementary School atop Mount Charleston after a tropical storm in 2023 devastated Lundy’s grounds, utilities and foundation and required students to be bused to Indian Springs. The Mount Charleston 
community rallied
, and the district repaired and 
reopened the school
 last year. 

Less controversially, CCSD
 closed Goodsprings Elementary School
, a historic two-room schoolhouse in the rural former mining town southwest of Las Vegas, in May after enrollment was expected to drop from two students to one. 

In September, Ebert said CCSD would 
convert Rex Bell Elementary School
 in the central valley to a school for students with special needs. The Bell campus would become the new home for Helen J. Stewart Special School, which has been in an aging campus on the east side since the early 1970s, when the current Cashman Middle School, which is under reconstruction, reopens as prekindergarten-8th school in 2028. Bell students would be absorbed into Cashman, about three miles away, and nearby Wynn Elementary. 

Before, CCSD hadn’t closed a school since it shut down Ruth Fyfe Elementary at Bonanza Road and Valley View Boulevard in 2018. Since then, CCSD has often used the Fyfe campus to 
temporarily house elementary schools
 being rebuilt.  

The board meeting to discuss the facility recommendations begins at 4 p.m. on Dec. 2 at the Greer Education Center, 2832 E. Flamingo Road.

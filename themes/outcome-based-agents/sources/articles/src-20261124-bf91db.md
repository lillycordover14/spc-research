Input Output (IO), a major developer of the Cardano (ADA) blockchain, has published a comprehensive report outlining the network's technical progress. According to the company's November 23, 2026 announcement, 97 of 104 active engineering milestones under its 2025 treasury-funded contract have been completed. Meanwhile, the Ouroboros Leios scaling protocol achieved approximately six times the theoretical transaction data throughput of Cardano's current mainnet during public testnet experiments. Input Output also confirmed that efforts to transfer core development responsibilities to independent organizations are expected to continue into 2027.

Cardano Completes 97 of 104 Engineering Milestones
According to Input Output's 
official development report
, the company completed 97 of the 104 active engineering milestones established under its 2025 community treasury funding agreement.
The organization stated that each completed milestone underwent independent verification before the corresponding funding was released.
Independent assurance providers involved in the process included Dquadrant, Bcryptic, and QuviQ.
Another notable development involved the cancellation of projects that were no longer considered economically justified.
Input Output discontinued the Acropolis and Tiered Pricing initiatives and returned a combined 4.1 million ADA in unused funding to the Cardano treasury.
Of that amount, 1.4 million ADA came from the Acropolis initiative, while 2.7 million ADA was returned from Tiered Pricing.
Community governance also played a central role in the company's 2026 funding proposals.
The consensus development initiative, which includes financing for Ouroboros Leios, received approximately 84% approval and secured 27.7 million ADA in funding.
These developments illustrate how Cardano's engineering progress increasingly operates alongside treasury oversight and community approval mechanisms.
Leios Testnet Achieves Approximately Six Times Mainnet Capacity
One of the most significant technical developments highlighted in the report involves Ouroboros Leios, a protocol designed to expand Cardano's transaction processing capacity.
During testing on the Musashi Dojo network, which opened to public participation on June 23, 2026, Leios reached a peak throughput of 26.8 kilobytes of transaction data per second.
For comparison, the current Ouroboros Praos protocol has a theoretical maximum of approximately 4.51 kilobytes per second under the parameters used in the assessment.
This represents an approximately sixfold improvement under the reported testing conditions.
According to the 
Leios development team's technical reports
, the first phase of testing lasted 41 days.

During that period, participants produced more than 127,000 blocks, recorded nearly 8,000 on-chain certificates, and increased the number of participating stake pools from three to 63.
However, an important distinction applies to these results.
The sixfold capacity improvement has not yet been deployed to Cardano's production mainnet.
The figures were obtained through controlled testnet experiments using synthetic transaction loads.
Additionally, the performance comparison measures transaction data throughput rather than simply counting the number of transactions processed per second.
Leios is scheduled to be introduced to mainnet through the planned Dijkstra hard fork.
Nevertheless, successful testnet performance does not guarantee identical results under real-world production conditions.
Cardano Targets Independent Development Infrastructure by 2027
Input Output's report also highlighted significant organizational changes to Cardano's development model.
The company aims to establish at least three independently maintained node implementations by 2027, developed using Haskell, Rust, and Go.
The objective is to reduce reliance on a single organization for maintaining and upgrading Cardano's core infrastructure.
According to the report, responsibility for Plutus development has already been transferred to Midgard Labs.
Consensus engineering responsibilities have moved to Serokell, while developer experience initiatives have been transferred to AlwaysTrue.
Hydra development is scheduled to transition to BlockPQR on December 1, 2026.
Input Output also reported improvements in network infrastructure efficiency.
The company stated that the memory requirement for running a Cardano node has been reduced from 24 GB to 8 GB, while Plutus smart contract execution costs have declined by more than 10% on average.
These improvements could reduce infrastructure requirements for node operators while making application development more accessible.
Kriptofoni Assessment
The most important aspect of Input Output's latest report is that Cardano's development strategy extends beyond increasing transaction processing capacity.
Distributing engineering responsibilities among independent organizations represents another significant component of the network's long-term decentralization efforts.

The sixfold capacity improvement achieved during Leios testing is technically encouraging.
However, the results must still be validated under production conditions, with network security maintained and the corresponding upgrade successfully deployed.
Completing 97 engineering milestones also demonstrates substantial progress toward fulfilling previously funded development commitments.
Nevertheless, technical milestones should be evaluated separately from measures of real-world adoption, including active users, application revenue, and on-chain activity.
For Cardano, the next critical developments will involve deploying Leios to mainnet and demonstrating that its independent development model can operate sustainably.
Consequently, the reported achievements should not be interpreted as a guaranteed bullish signal for ADA.
Instead, they provide 
measurable evidence of progress in Cardano's technical capabilities and decentralized development structure
.

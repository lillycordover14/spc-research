As Jordan Walsh's minutes go down, it's time for Ron Harper Jr.'s to go up. 
Harper was a bright spot in the first half of the Boston 
Celtics' 88-87 loss
 to the Golden State Warriors. Early in the second frame, he broke baseline for a dunk off a dime from Neemias Queta. Later came a sequence where he picked off a pass from Brandin Podziemski. After Derrick White missed a 3 at the other end, the Rutgers product grabbed the offensive rebound. That second-chance opportunity led to a layup for Payton Pritchard.
The six-foot-five wing also buried a shot from beyond the arc and was disruptive in the passing lanes. However, he did not play after the intermission.
That speaks to 
how crowded the Celtics' rotation is
. Depth is a necessity. A team can also have so much of it that it becomes a luxury. What happened to Harper against Golden State reflects how Boston's young wings are suppressing each other's minutes. 
Ron Harper Jr. is ready to move up the Celtics' rotation
He's a dynamic talent
 at both ends of the floor. Offensively, the 26-year-old from New Jersey is a connector and a reliable three-point shooter off the catch. He's a savvy cutter and generates second-chance opportunities.
Defensively, the fifth-year wing can capably guard 1 through 4. His wingspan stretches over seven feet. He has a high IQ to win with anticipation, and he moves well to stay attached on drives. He comes up with steals and deflections, and he contributes on the defensive glass.
A minor shoulder ailment slowed his start to the season. However, what Harper showed in that opportunity against the Warriors is that he's ready for more playing time.
Joe Mazzulla and the Celtics must manage a delicate balance
This isn't a video game. Teams must be mindful of how these decisions impact the people involved.
Walsh has played fewer than 10 minutes in each of the last three games. Signing a three-year contract extension over the summer makes that a bit easier to handle. However, he's still a 22-year-old looking to build on a breakout season.
For Harper to get more minutes, someone has to get less. Playing time fluctuates for most. Fortunately, the Celtics have a mature group that understands that. They do a good job focusing on maximizing their opportunities. With Walsh's time on the court dropping, Harper should be among the beneficiaries.

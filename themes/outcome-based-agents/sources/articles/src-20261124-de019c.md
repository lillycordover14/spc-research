AI agents as business interfaces

Sierra builds AI agents for almost half of the Fortune 50 companies, and it positions these agents as primary customer touchpoints rather than supplementary support tools.

Despite only being founded in 2023, according to Sierra, annual recurring revenue exceeds US$400m.

The platform targets enterprises seeking to replace traditional customer service channels with AI systems, which aim to deepen customer relationships while creating new revenue opportunities – shifting AI from back-office automation to front-line customer interaction.

“We founded Sierra in the belief that 
AI agents
 would become the digital front door for every company, deepening their relationships with customers and opening up new growth opportunities,” Bret says.

Outcome-based pricing aligns AI incentives with business results

One of Sierra's core differentiators is its approach to pricing AI: chiefly, tying fees to outcomes rather than usage or token consumption.

Rather than charging customers for every conversation or query an agent handles, Sierra ties its fee to successful resolution of a task.

This model has significant implications for how Sierra's agents are architected. It requires agents capable of orchestrating multi-step, multi-system workflows autonomously, integrating directly with a company's backend systems, pulling relevant data, navigating edge cases, and guiding a customer all the way through to completion.

Sierra's agents are built to go beyond answering questions: instead, they resolve problems and completing complex tasks end-to-end.

The company argues this pricing structure keeps its incentives aligned with customers. Sierra only succeeds commercially when its agents actually solve the problem in front of them, not merely when they generate engagement.
Sierra's AI in action. Credit: Sierra
Ghostwriter and a constellation of models
Underpinning Sierra's platform is Ghostwriter, a tool that lets enterprise teams define goals and guardrails, build AI agents, test them securely and deploy them into production environments.
As agents are handed more autonomous responsibility, guardrails matter increasingly. Any enterprise deploying AI at the complexity level Sierra's customers are pushing for needs confidence that agents will operate reliably and predictably at scale.
Ghostwriter also incorporates a continuous improvement loop, reviewing agent conversations, identifying opportunities and suggesting new experiments to refine performance over time reflecting the broader industry move toward self-improving AI systems over static, one-time deployments.
Notably, Sierra's platform is built on what the company describes as a “constellation of models,” rather than a single foundation model.
Such architecture allows enterprises to stay at the frontier of AI capability without needing to choose a specific model or manage token spend directly.
Sierra says agents built on its platform can go live within weeks, a timeline that stands in contrast to the months or years often required for traditional enterprise software implementations. 
As customer ambition continues to scale, the speed of deployment is becoming as important a differentiator as the agents' capability itself.

rounds out Sierra's multi-model approach, with Gemini models providing additional capabilities in reasoning and long-context processing for agents handling complex, multi-step workflows.

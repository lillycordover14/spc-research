On Nov. 20, Lawrence County Emergency Management Agency (EMA) brought together representatives from across the county for the first meeting of the reestablished Lawrence County Local Emergency Planning Committee (LEPC).
“This is an important step toward strengthening hazardous materials preparedness, improving emergency response coordination, and building stronger relationships between the industries, agencies and organizations that help protect our communities,” Lawrence County EMA Director Steven Carlton said in a Facebook post.
During the meeting, participants discussed the purpose and responsibilities of the LEPC, membership and leadership, the importance of identifying potential risks in Lawrence County, and hazardous materials reporting requirements under EPCRA.
The LEPC meeting brought together representatives from 17 organizations and agencies. They included: Alabama Department of Public Health, Alabama EMA, First Solar, Caddo-Midway Fire & Rescue District, Hillsboro Fire & Rescue, Chalybeate Fire & Rescue, West Morgan East Lawrence Water Authority, Courtland, Tennessee Valley Cooperative, Joe Wheeler EMC, North Central Alabama Regional Council of Governments, Lawrence County Commission, Lawrence County Road Department, Lawrence County Maintenance, Lawrence County Central 9-1-1, and the Lawrence County Industrial Development Board.
Carlton said that level of collaboration is exactly what the LEPC is designed to accomplish. By bringing those partners together, they can share information, strengthen response capabilities, identify potential hazards, and improve coordination before an emergency occurs.
“One of our biggest responsibilities as emergency managers is identifying potential risks before they become emergencies,” Carlton said. “Reestablishing the Lawrence County LEPC gives us an opportunity to bring our industries, first responders, local government, and community partners together to have those conversations, share information, and plan for the challenges we may face.”
No one can do this alone, Carlton said.
“Emergency preparedness is a shared responsibility, and building these relationships now will make us stronger when our communities need us most,” he said.
Carlton said that moving forward, Lawrence County EMA will continue to work with their partners to identify planning priorities, expand participation, and establish a framework for continued collaboration through the new LEPC.
Carlton thanked every organization and agency that contributed and participated in this important first meeting. He also thanked First Solar for hosting the inaugural meeting at their facility, and to NARCOG for providing lunch for the attendees.
“Together, we’re building a stronger and more prepared Lawrence County,” Carlton said.

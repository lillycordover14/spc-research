Coinbase Prime is letting institutional clients run 
Bitcoin
 basis trades with up to 10x leverage. The mechanism is a feature called Cross-Margin, which treats offsetting spot and futures positions as one combined portfolio instead of two separate bets.

For the hedge funds and trading desks that live on these spreads, that is a meaningful change. Basis trading has always been a capital-hungry way to make thin, steady returns, and anything that frees up collateral tends to get noticed fast.

How Prime Cross-Margin works

Start with the trade itself. A classic basis trade pairs a long position in spot Bitcoin with a short position in Bitcoin futures. The goal is not to bet on direction but to capture the gap between the two prices.

The problem is that many venues margin each leg separately. The spot side ties up capital. The futures side ties up more capital. The risk engine acts as if the two positions have never met, even though they are designed to cancel each other out.

Prime Cross-Margin, which 
Coinbase
 abbreviates as XM, folds both legs into a single portfolio view. It applies basis credits and portfolio margin offsets so the hedged nature of the position actually lowers the margin required. Those risk calculations are run daily.

The result, for qualifying clients, is effective leverage of up to 10x on BTC. Coinbase says the unified approach improved capital efficiency for basis trades by 10-20%. That figure sounds modest until you remember that basis desks measure returns in small percentages, so a double-digit efficiency gain on collateral can change the math of a whole strategy.

Who gets access, and on what terms

This is not a retail product. Access is limited to US institutions with more than $10 million in investments, and only for business purposes. Availability is also restricted to specific states and designated commercial uses.

Coinbase is also leaning on predictability as a selling point. The feature uses what the company describes as deterministic risk modeling, meaning clients should be able to calculate their margin outcomes in advance rather than guessing how an opaque engine will react.

It also removes auto-liquidation during non-trading hours.

Part of a larger margin push

Cross-Margin did not arrive alone. It sits inside a broader unified margining strategy at Coinbase that began in March 2026, when the XM rollout started.

That effort was extended with the launch of US spot margin trading on November 18, 2026. The spot margin product lets institutions borrow directly against spot positions in major assets, also with up to 10x leverage.

Taken together, the two products push Coinbase Prime toward a one-stop model. A client can hold spot, borrow against it, short futures against it, and have the whole stack margined as a single book, all inside a regulated US venue.

What this means for institutions and the market

The most direct effect is on the economics of the basis trade. When the same strategy requires less posted collateral, a desk can either run a larger position with the same capital or run the same position and keep more cash idle as a buffer.

The risks deserve equal billing. Leverage cuts both ways, and basis trades are not riskless just because they are hedged. Spreads can widen unexpectedly, funding conditions can shift, and a 10x position magnifies any mismatch between the two legs. The protections Coinbase highlights, such as daily risk calculations and no auto-liquidation outside trading hours, are designed to make those risks more manageable, not to make them disappear.

For now, the restrictions keep the circle small: US institutions only, more than $10 million in investments, business use, select states.

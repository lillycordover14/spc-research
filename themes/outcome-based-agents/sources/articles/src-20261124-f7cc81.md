Former Director of Political Party Monitoring and Liaison at the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Regina Omo-Agege, has appealed to President Bola Tinubu to guarantee the safety of her brother, former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege, following an alleged attack on his campaign team in Delta State that reportedly killed six people.

In a statement issued in Abuja on Tuesday, Regina described the reported attack during Omo-Agege’s visit to Uduere in Ughelli North Local Government Area on Monday as disturbing and an assault on citizens’ right to participate in the democratic process without fear of violence.

The former INEC director, who is the sister of the former Deputy Senate President, said political disagreements and electoral ambitions could never justify the killing of citizens, insisting that campaigns should provide opportunities for the exchange of ideas rather than become theatres of bloodshed.

“Politics must never become a death sentence, and political ambition must never be pursued at the expense of human life,” she said.

She expressed particular concern over reports that firearms were present and used during the political gathering, arguing that the incident, beyond being a manifestation of political rivalry, constituted alleged criminal conduct requiring urgent and comprehensive investigation.

Regina disclosed that her brother had previously raised concerns about threats to his life but admitted that she initially underestimated the seriousness of his fears.

She said the latest development had, however, compelled her to speak out, stressing that the safety of Omo-Agege, his supporters and other innocent citizens could no longer be taken for granted.

According to her, no political ambition or decision should expose anyone to intimidation, violence or assassination, adding that the right to seek elective office must be protected irrespective of political affiliation.

Making a direct appeal to Tinubu, she urged the President to use his influence to ensure that adequate security measures were provided for her brother.

She said the appeal was not intended to secure political advantage but to prevent further loss of lives.

“Mr President, I believe that the personal relationship we share makes this appeal particularly important.
 

“I have not wronged you in any way, and I respectfully ask that my brother, like every other Nigerian, be protected,” she said.

The former INEC director also called on the Inspector-General of Police, the Department of State Services (DSS) and other relevant security agencies to launch a thorough and transparent investigation into the reported attack.

She demanded that those responsible be identified and prosecuted, including individuals suspected of sponsoring, financing or facilitating the violence, irrespective of their political status or connections.

She further urged the authorities to ensure that victims and their families received adequate assistance, insisting that the families of those reportedly killed deserved justice, while injured persons should be provided with appropriate medical attention and other necessary support.

Regina, who drew on her experience in Nigeria’s electoral administration, said she had witnessed intense political competition and disagreements over the years, including a period when the country had more than 100 registered political parties.

She said such political diversity and competition should never degenerate into violence, adding that the country’s democratic space must remain open to all citizens without threats to their lives.

“Delta State is not a war zone. Nigeria is not a battlefield. Campaigns should be platforms for ideas, not grounds for bloodshed,” she said.

The reported incident has raised fresh concerns about political security and the protection of political actors and their supporters as preparations gradually gather momentum for the 2027 general elections.

The central issue is not merely the alleged attack on one political campaign team, but the wider implications of political violence for citizens’ freedom to associate, campaign and contest elections without fear of deadly reprisals.

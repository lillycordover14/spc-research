Cardano (ADA) Drops 7% Amid Broad Crypto Risk-Off Sentiment
By CMC AI
November 24, 2026 at 4:25 PM UTC
Cardano's (ADA) Recent Drop Explained by Broad Crypto Risk-Off, Not Specific Issues

Cardano (ADA)'s approximately 7% decline over the past day is primarily due to a widespread risk-off sentiment in the crypto market, rather than any specific issues with Cardano.

Broad Crypto Risk-Off Move

ADA's drop over the past 20-24 hours aligns closely with a general market selloff.

The total crypto market cap decreased by about 4.1%, from roughly $2.14 trillion to $2.05 trillion.
Bitcoin (BTC) fell by about 5.1%, while Cardano (ADA) dropped by about 7.2% with a 24-hour trading volume of around $304 million.
ADA's price fell from approximately $0.15285 at 23 Nov 17:00 UTC to about $0.14501 at 24 Nov 15:00 UTC, a decline of about -5.13%. This mirrors BTC's 24-hour drop and indicates a correlated market move rather than an isolated ADA event.

Recent market weakness is attributed to:

Expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike in December, which typically pressures speculative assets.
Concerns about a tougher US regulatory outlook for crypto as political control shifts, increasing perceived risk for the sector.
Significant net outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs, pulling liquidity out of crypto and weighing on prices. These factors are discussed in reports such as 
this analysis on Bitcoin's recent lows due to ETF outflows and rate worries
.

ADA's move is consistent with market-wide selling pressure linked to macro and ETF flows, rather than ADA-specific bad news.

Altcoin De-Risking and Negative Relative Narrative

Within the general selloff, large altcoins like ADA have underperformed, adding extra pressure on their prices.

An altcoin market cap analysis notes that the non-Bitcoin crypto market (TOTAL2) has fallen about 34% from its September peak, with altcoins losing roughly $408 billion in market value. Only about 19% of Binance-listed alts are still trading above their 200-day moving average. Cardano is mentioned among altcoins that have recently dropped in this environment in 
this altcoin drawdown report
.
Another piece highlights that Cardano is down about 95% from its all-time high and describes ADA as having "effectively fallen to zero," favoring Solana instead. This commentary, appearing in mainstream investor outlets such as 
this top crypto performance article
, reinforces a rotation narrative away from ADA toward other L1s.
Bitmine Executive Chairman Tom Lee contrasted ADA’s annualized supply increase of about 2.9% with Bitcoin’s roughly 0.8% and Ethereum’s about 0.9%, framing ETH and BTC as “hard money” and ADA as more inflationary. This discussion, found in 
this supply growth comparison
, contributes to a weaker store-of-value narrative for ADA relative to BTC and ETH.

Social channels echo this relative underperformance, with posts noting ADA's consecutive red weekly candles and significant declines since early October, encouraging traders to treat bounces as opportunities to sell or short rather than accumulate.

This narrative makes ADA more vulnerable to downside when the whole market is under pressure.

No Major ADA-Specific Negative Catalyst; Mostly Flow and Sentiment

Recent Cardano news is neutral to constructive, with no clear protocol shock explaining a sudden 3–7 percentage point move.

Governance and community tooling improvements, such as the Cardano Foundation's CIP-179 and the Tessera app, are highlighted in 
this article
.
Scaling and performance upgrades, including the public testnet for Leios and the Hydra fast-payments layer reaching version 1.0, are technically positive for Cardano’s long-term scalability.
Network health metrics show continued uptime, billions of ADA staked, thousands of active stake pools, and tens of thousands of daily transactions, indicating a functioning network.

On the trading and sentiment side:

Chartists frame ADA in a large falling wedge near $0.14–$0.15, watching for a possible breakout.
At least one visible public short trade call around $0.1466, with take profit targets at $0.1442 and $0.1426, amplifies downside in already weak conditions but is usually a response to trend and liquidity, not a root cause.
No prominent reports of an ADA-specific security incident, critical bug, CEX delisting, or regulatory action in the last day.

Given the neutral or mildly positive fundamental news flow and the lack of a unique "Cardano shock" event, ADA's move appears to be part of a continuous trend driven by market-wide flows and existing negative narratives about ADA’s relative performance, rather than a fresh, discrete Cardano news catalyst.

Conclusion

Cardano’s recent drop is primarily due to:

A broad crypto selloff tied to Bitcoin ETF outflows, rate hike expectations, and regulatory worries.
Stronger de-risking from altcoins, where ADA has been an underperformer and is increasingly framed as less attractive than BTC, ETH, or SOL.
Ordinary trend following and short-term trading flows acting on a still-weak chart, rather than any clear new, negative Cardano-specific fundamental event.

Confidence: Medium, as macro and sector-wide drivers are clear, but order flow and positioning data at the exchange level is not fully observable.

Lio Man Cheong captures  the ‘Soul of Macao’ at IIM exhibition

2026-11-25 02:27

BY admin

Comment:0

Review by Rui Pastorin 
        Yesterday, I visited the new gallery at the International Institute of Macau’s (IIM) headquarters on Rua de Berlim (
栢주쌍
) to view the exhibition of watercolours by veteran artist Lio Man Cheong. Upon exploring the artworks on display, I left the venue with a newfound appreciation of watercolour painting and how the medium can capture the beauty of a destination.
As I walked up to the institute’s headquarters, I wondered what kind of artworks I could expect, with the title, “The Soul of Macao”, having particularly piqued my interest.
The entrance leads directly to both the gallery and IIM’s newly renovated library, Biblioteca Comendador Joaquim Morais Alves, which officially opened earlier this month. And upon entering, I was immediately greeted by Lio’s artworks and their unmistakable style lining both sides of the gallery’s narrow halls.
From the vibrant to the subtle and muted, Lio - who was born in Zhuhai and settled in Macau at an early age - presents it all across 40 new paintings. Each piece captures the essence of the exhibition’s title, offering exhibitiongoers snapshots of the city as he sees it through his brush.
The artist depicts a wide range of places around the city, including Macau’s historic landmarks and bustling city centre. He presents scenes such as the Ruins of St. Paul’s in the rain, the solemn energy at the Chapel of Our Lady of Penha, and the pastel colours of the Macau Government House, the Dom Pedro V Theatre, and St. Lawrence’s Church, alongside pieces that offer sweeping views of places like the old Taipa Houses and Calçada do Gamboa.
The exhibition also goes beyond the well-known spots as Lio highlights everyday scenes one might spot while simply walking through Macau’s districts, or the transitional corners just steps away from major tourist attractions. These include pieces that depict a small cluster of shops and residential buildings on Rua Central, a distinct arch on Rua de Gamboa, and a small passage along Calçada do Carmo. 
Not missing from the showcase is also a glimpse into the past as Lio depicts areas like the Barra district in the piece “Barra Antigo” (literally “old harbour entrance”), and a piece that still features the façade of the erstwhile Hotel Estoril.
It is through all these places depicted, along with what makes them distinct and alive, that I think Lio conveys the very soul of the city. 
Going further, IIM highlights in a recent statement on the exhibition that each piece conveys the deep sentiments Lio has developed for Macau over the years, weaving the city’s unique urban landscape, daily life, customs, traditions, diverse culture, and historical heritage into his work.
The statement added that the exhibition “shares the painter’s singular vision with art enthusiasts and the general public, especially younger generations, inviting them to discover the soul of this city from a cultural perspective and to appreciate each of Macau’s special places through the brushstrokes of Lio Man Cheong”.
Lio has held numerous solo exhibitions since 1998, and his pieces have been featured in a number of publications, the statement pointed out. Now a watercolour and figure-drawing instructor, Lio is a member of the China Artists Association and an active participant in art events and joint exhibitions, the statement pointed out.
Exhibitiongoers can admire Lio’s watercolours at the IIM-organised exhibition until 
December 29. The gallery is open from Tuesday to Saturday, from 11:00 a.m. to 8:00 
p.m.
– Photos taken at the International Institute of Macau (IIM) headquarters yesterday afternoon by Rui Pastorin.

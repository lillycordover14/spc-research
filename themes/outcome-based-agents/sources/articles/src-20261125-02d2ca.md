            SailPoint warns 2027 will bring tighter AI agent controls
          

            Thu, 26th Nov 2026 (Today)
          

                JOSEPH GABRIEL LAGONSIN
              

News Editor

SailPoint has published its identity security predictions for 2027, focusing on tighter control of autonomous AI agents in large organisations.

The forecasts suggest businesses are moving beyond early AI experimentation towards formal governance as autonomous software agents spread across operations. Recent SailPoint research found that 79% of enterprises already run AI agents in production, while only 2% have deployed identity security designed specifically for those systems.

That gap underpins the warning that identity management is becoming as much a business risk issue as a technical one. The report says organisations are shifting from managing employee and contractor access to supervising what software agents can do on behalf of staff and departments.

One of the main predictions is that a large listed company will suffer a self-inflicted operational crisis caused by a misconfigured group of autonomous agents. Such an event could involve supplier contracts being deleted, inventory databases removed and shipments redirected before human administrators can intervene.

It also predicts stricter separation of duties for AI systems by 2027. Under that scenario, an autonomous agent would not be allowed to hold administrative rights in the same environment that oversees its behaviour, with governance moved to an independent control layer.

Another theme is business-to-business access. As companies connect AI agents to software vendors and partners through open protocols, SailPoint predicts some enterprises will begin blocking external agents from their application programming interfaces unless those third parties can prove an agent's identity history and limits of access.

Mark McClain, Chief Executive Officer and Founder of SailPoint, said the growing use of autonomous agents would draw greater scrutiny from boards, insurers and regulators.

"For the past two years, enterprises raced to see what they could accomplish with autonomous AI agents; in 2027, boards, insurers, and regulators will demand to know who is accountable when those agents take action that crosses a legal or regulatory boundary," said Mark McClain, Chief Executive Officer and Founder, SailPoint.

"In 2027, while it will be possible to delegate a complex workflow to an autonomous agent, it will never be acceptable to delegate accountability, because agent accountability is enterprise responsibility. Enterprises require an intelligent identity control plane that governs both human and non-human identities in real time. The organisations that thrive in the coming year won't be those that deployed the most agents in a reckless sprint; they will be the ones that adopted a unified identity foundation capable of keeping those agents safely, transparently, and profitably in production."

Credential shift

The report says traditional user authentication will become less central to breach prevention as attackers target machine credentials tied to automated workflows. While passkeys are expected to spread across employee logins, exposed API keys, OAuth tokens and long-lived machine secrets could become a more common route for unauthorised access.

SailPoint's research suggests 67% of enterprises have secured human access, compared with 24% that govern agentic security. The same data indicates that AI agents account for 22% of all non-human accounts, within what SailPoint described as a 45:1 machine-to-human identity ratio.

The company argues that established access control models are under strain because software agents do not operate from fixed devices or locations and can generate new subtasks from prompts. In response, it predicts a move towards what it calls continuous intent assurance, in which systems check an agent's identity, human lineage and business purpose before each API call.

That approach is tied to a broader push against standing privileges. Companies will need to rely more heavily on least-privilege and just-in-time access for both human and non-human identities, as older privileged access management models struggle to keep pace with the volume and speed of automated activity.

Chandra Gnanasambandam, Chief Technology Officer and Executive Vice President, Product at SailPoint, said manual review processes would not match the scale of autonomous systems.

"At near-AGI scale, manual human access reviews for autonomous agents are a fantasy," said Chandra Gnanasambandam, Chief Technology Officer and Executive Vice President, Product, SailPoint.

"Because you cannot take a rogue AI agent to jail, every digital worker must carry cryptographically bound human lineage. Relying on blunt-force 'kill switches' without deep identity context will only end up shutting down the critical automation your business depends on. In the agentic era, standing privilege is dead, and identity must live directly in the execution path."

Board pressure

SailPoint also linked identity governance to broader corporate oversight. It said requirements tied to post-quantum cryptography and AI regulation would push boards to treat visibility over human, machine and cryptographic identities as a core control issue.

In that view, organisations would need a clear record of machine secrets and authorisations not only for cyber risk management, but also for insurance, compliance and valuation discussions. The report describes this as an "Identity Bill of Materials" becoming a board-level measure.

The final prediction is that companies will move away from permissive internal AI policies that allowed staff to experiment with agents using personal credentials. SailPoint said Chief Information Officers and Chief Financial Officers would increasingly require measurable returns and formal identity governance before approving wider use, leading businesses to bring shadow agents under central oversight.

Key Takeaways
 
 
 
A hypothetical $400,000 portfolio split evenly across Hercules Capital, Artisan Partners Asset Management and Mechanics Bancorp pays about $3,820 a month in dividends at today's prices, an 11.5% yield.
 
 
 
Hercules Capital's net investment income matched its $0.47 quarterly payout in the third quarter and covered its base distribution 118%.
 
 
 
Artisan Partners pays out about 80% of the cash it generates each quarter, so its dividend rises and falls with a business that had $10.6 billion of net client outflows in the third quarter.

Hercules is paying out what it earns
 
 
Hercules is writing loans at a record pace. On Nov. 17, it said that it and its private funds had passed $4.0 billion of debt and equity commitments this year, a first in its history. Net investment income came in at $0.47 a share in the third quarter, exactly what it paid in base and supplemental distributions.
 
 
CEO Scott Bluestein said in the results release: "We continue to comfortably cover our base dividend operating at these levels with 118% coverage for Q3, despite a nearly 75% reduction in early repayment volume during the quarter."
 
 
On Oct. 27, Hercules held its payout at a $0.40 base plus a $0.07 supplemental. Over a full year, it has paid out between 91% and 108% of normalized EPS every year since fiscal 2016:
 
 
 
 
Hercules Capital (HTGC): dividends per share vs. normalized EPS, $ per share, fiscal 2016-2025 (TIKR)
 
 
 
That was a thin 98% in fiscal 2025. Analysts' 2026 estimates bring it to 88%, though they assume $1.69 a share of dividends against today's $1.88 rate. If income slips, I'd expect the supplemental to go before the base.

Artisan pays what it makes
 
 
Where Hercules pays a set base, Artisan's dividend changes every quarter by design. The company says it expects "to pay a quarterly dividend of approximately 80% of the cash the Company generates each quarter from operations," and its board weighs a special dividend after year-end from the 20% it holds back. On Oct. 27, that formula produced a 10% raise to $0.88 a share from $0.80.
 
 
 
 
 
The business behind it is under pressure. Clients pulled a net $10.6 billion in the third quarter, including $5.9 billion when a large client rebalanced. Assets under management fell to $168.7 billion from $183.4 billion in June.
 
 
Free cash flow has covered the dividend in every year but one since fiscal 2016:
 
 
 
 
Artisan Partners Asset Management (APAM): free cash flow vs. common dividends paid, $ millions, fiscal 2016-2025 (TIKR)
 
 
 
In fiscal 2025, dividends took 129% of free cash flow as it fell to $171 million. Over the twelve months to Sept. 30, they took a well-covered 75%, in line with the policy. So Artisan's check won't outrun its business, but it'll shrink if outflows keep eating into fees.
 
 
Mechanics is paying out more than it earns
 
 
Mechanics Bancorp is a different story. Its third-quarter net income fell to $46.0 million, or $0.20 a diluted share, from $57.7 million in the second quarter. That included $34.6 million of one-time losses from restructuring its securities portfolio and exiting auto lending. Without those losses, core net income was $60.6 million.
 
 
 
 
 
It paid $0.25 a Class A share in the third quarter, and on Nov. 20 it declared $0.43 for the fourth. On its third-quarter call, management said the fourth quarter's payout ratio would top 100%, given a planned $100 million dividend. CEO C.J. Johnson said the bank is focused on "returning capital to shareholders via a market-leading dividend yield," and its CET1 ratio rose to 14.73% at Sept. 30 from 14.39% in June, so it has the capital for now.
 
 
HomeStreet's record before the merger shows how fast a bank's earnings can fall out from under its dividend:
 
 
 
 
HomeStreet (MCHB): dividends per share vs. normalized EPS, $ per share, fiscal 2019-2023 (TIKR)
 
 
 
Normalized EPS fell from $5.46 a share in fiscal 2021 to $0.44 in fiscal 2023. That year, the dividend took 148% of it, even after falling to $0.65 from $1.40 in fiscal 2022.
 
 
If one cuts, it's probably Mechanics
 
 
Mechanics is the least covered of the three, so it's the one to test. A cut isn't a given, since it has plenty of capital and its core earnings ran well ahead of its reported earnings. If Mechanics halved its dividend, this portfolio's income would fall to about $38,600 a year. If it stopped paying, the income would fall to about $31,500.
 
 
 
 
 
Frankly, an 11.5% yield should make any retiree a little nervous. Of the three, Hercules looks like the steadiest check to me: it earned every cent of what it paid last quarter. Artisan's check is only ever 80% of what it brings in, and its assets are still shrinking. Mechanics' is the one I'd keep the closest eye on.
 
 
Hercules needs fourth-quarter prepayments back in its $200 million to $300 million range to deliver the higher net investment income it has guided for. Artisan's board decides on a special after the year closes. And Mechanics' fourth-quarter results, due early next year, will show how far over 100% that payout ran.

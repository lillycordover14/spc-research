Chennai banks on December rain as agencies step up efforts to augment water sources

The recent rain has offered Chennai’s reservoirs a marginal reprieve, but the inflows have only matched the daily drawal and have been inadequate to boost storage for drinking water supply.
Though water rationing is not yet on the cards, Chennai Metrowater is preparing contingency plans in case of water shortage next year. The waterbodies feeding drinking water to Chennai, including Veeranam tank, now have a storage of nearly 39.4% of total capacity of 13,222 million cubic feet.
Officials of the Water Resources Department (WRD) said the rainfall over the past two days brought an inflow of nearly 700 cubic feet per second to Red Hills reservoir and about 300 cusecs to Chembarambakkam. Cholavaram reservoir also received marginal inflow. However, there was no significant change in storage.
With little more than a month to go for the official end of the Northeast monsoon, the water agencies are hoping for subsequent rain spells to augment available water sources. Implementing some of the projects proposed under the WRD’s water security plan would help strengthen drought resilience, sources said. Metrowater has already started using some sources, including abandoned quarries in Sikkarayapuram and Erumaiyur, for supply. It recently began tapping a small volume of water from Perungudi lake that receives tertiary treated water from Perungudi tertiary treatment ultrafiltration (TTUF) plant established to promote indirect use of recycled water.
While the water agency would take a call on water rationing post the monsoon, it has accelerated various measures to augment resources and implement decentralised water management, said a senior official. Besides scouting for new abandoned quarries for use as alternative sources, works are in final stages to draw water from Ayanambakkam lake and Perumbakkam lake. Water tapped from the lakes would be treated at Mogappair and Perumbakkam water treatment plants respectively before linked to the existing supply network.
These projects, which would add another 20 million litres a day to daily distribution, would be functional by early next year once work resumes after monsoon, officials said.
Water Security plan
It may be recalled that the WRD had proposed a comprehensive water security plan for the city and the Chennai Metropolitan Area last year to combat climate change induced floods and droughts.
Pointing out that a minimum of 20,000 mcft of flood water drained into the sea in Chennai region, sources said at least 3-4 mcft could be conserved in implementing some of the short-term projects recommended in the plan. Additional storage could be carried over to the following year to tide over a drought.
For instance, check dams across Palar river, including Vayalur, could be converted into barrages to store additional water. Water bodies around the city such as Sriperumbudur and Thiruninravur, could be improved into reservoirs, sources added.

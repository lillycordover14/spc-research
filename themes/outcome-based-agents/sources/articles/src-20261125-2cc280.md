It’s a question nobody wants to ask.

If you’re younger than Sidney Crosby, you’ve always wanted to be him. If you’re around Crosby’s age like I am, the day his star fades will be a reminder that everyone has an expiry date. If you’re older than Crosby, you’ve long respected the athlete and the man. At 39, he’s a hockey icon in 
every sense of the word
.

Through 23 games this season, Crosby has six goals and 15 points. The 
Pittsburgh Penguins
, whom he has carried on and off the ice for 22 seasons, sit 11 points out of a playoff spot in late November. At best, the situation is unsettling. At worst, it’s the end of an era.

As we often do in this space, we’ll take a thoughtful approach to answer a difficult question. With a player as universally revered as Crosby, discussing the possible end of his reign feels heavy. We’ll alternate between reasons that No. 87 still has gas in the tank (⛽) and the corresponding warning signs (⚠️). Here it goes:
 are we finally seeing Sidney Crosby’s decline?

⛽ OPTIMIST: He’s Sidney Crosby

Crosby’s longtime rival Alex Ovechkin has changed over time — his role, hit count, physique, visor, goal celebrations. Last week, Matt Larkin explored the 41-year-old 
Ovechkin’s final gasp
. Crosby is different, though. Except for the salt and pepper hair, the Penguins’ captain appears more or less identical in style, deployment and demeanor as his rookie year. Crosby is as unwavering as arena coffee on a winter morning.

For 21 consecutive seasons, he has scored at least one point-per-game, an NHL record. Crosby’s game has evolved through subtle adjustments spanning multiple generations. The man’s Jofa shoulder pads are older than some of his teammates.

Crosby’s 82-game era adjusted pace by season from age-18 through age-38 is perhaps his magnum opus:

It’s an unparalleled visual. A career without off years or identifiable dips. While ‘Sid the Kid’ reached a different level as an offensive force in his mid-20s, the last dozen years or so have been stunningly consistent. A player this steady and relentless simply won’t fade into the night, right?

⚠️ PESSIMIST: He’s Sidney Crosby… at 39

If the above chart were extended to Crosby’s current age-39 season, however, it would plummet to 54 points per 82 games. Uncharted territory. A 35-point drop in pace. But hey, it’s still early.

What’s the biggest culprit physically, you might ask? Speed…

One thing is clear: 
Crosby does not play at the same pace he did as recently as last season
. While his top speed (X-axis) is down a little, it’s not concerning given Crosby’s peak speed in 2021-22 was narrowly faster.

But it’s the swift dip in 18+ MPH bursts per 60 minutes (Y-axis) that is more concerning. Crosby averaged 29.5 such bursts per 60 over the last five seasons. This year? It’s down to 21.4. You have to wonder if the MCL sprain that Crosby suffered at the 2026 Winter Olympics is to blame. Loss of foot speed can be a problem. But a cerebral player with generational vision, strong board play, and silky mitts is a great candidate to adapt to a slower pace. How quickly Crosby can adjust to his new reality feels critical to his future.

It’s a short list. Two of the players Crosby has been most associated with — Ovechkin and Malkin — thrived in their age-39 seasons. You may recall that Ovechkin at 38 had eight goals through 43 games, temporarily derailing his pursuit of the all-time goal scoring record. But he recovered that season, then scored another 44 goals in 65 games at 39. Seeing Ovechkin and Malkin recently author point-per-game seasons at Crosby’s current age is a positive sign.

⚠️ PESSIMIST: Other stars Sid’s age have faded fast

What you may notice in the list above, however, is that none of those players was a No. 1 center on a bad team. All had some form of protection from the big minutes, brutal matchups and heavy faceoff usage that Crosby faces nightly.

There are many cautionary tales among G.O.A.T forwards in the last few decades:

Wayne Gretzky
 scored nine goals as a 38-year-old and retired.

Mario Lemieux
 played just 26 games at 40 and retired for good.

Joe Sakic
 had 100 points at 37, yet his age-39 season was his last after only 15 games.

Steve Yzerman
 played just 12:47 per game at 40 and retired.

Jarome Iginla
 said goodbye after he produced just 27 points at 39.

Now, Crosby is in better physical condition at 39 than all of these legends were — a product of exceptional habits and modern sport science. But the NHL is also faster too. Can Sid continue to sip from the Fountain of Youth like Selanne and Jagr? Or will his star burn out with little notice like many of his heroes?

⛽ OPTIMIST: The underlying numbers say it’s bad luck

There are two key data points that point to sunnier days ahead for Crosby.

From the 2020-21 through 2025-26 seasons, Crosby earned a point on 72% of on-ice goals per 
Natural Stat Trick
. Each year, his IPP (Individual Point Percentage) was between 70% and 73%. When Pittsburgh scores, their captain is part of the action and earns points. Like clockwork. This season? It’s at 52%. 
If Crosby earned a point at his usual rate on the 29 goals he’s been on the ice for, he’d have 21 points in 23 games.
 If that were the case, this article would not be written.

Crosby has also been on the ice for 31.8 expected goals in all situations per 
HockeyStats.com
. But the Penguins have only 29 goals, a difference of about three goals. Add three more on-ice goals at Crosby’s usual rate of participation and he’s got two more points to add to the 21 above. 
That’s your 23 points in 23 games
, another vintage point-per-game season.

This might seem like creative accounting to some. But a player’s IPP and expected on-ice goals are not fringe stats. Both are likely to regress in Crosby’s favor over a long season. The absence of a handful of secondary assists, a deflection or two, and a few empty net points that have reliably occurred for years are enough to bury a stat line. It can be that fine of a line on whether touches result in goals early in a season.

⚠️ PESSIMIST: The underlying numbers say it’s not just bad luck

While Crosby’s puck luck should reverse over time, there are also two troubling trends.

The Penguins power play has been terrible this year.
 Much of its flow runs through Crosby and Erik Karlsson. With No. 87 on the ice, Pittsburgh’s power play creates 5.9 expected goals per 60 minutes this season. That means over a full hour with the man-advantage, the unit should score about six goals. Last season, it was 9.0. That’s a massive 51% drop year over year.

It’s by far the worst mark of Crosby’s career and part of the reason he has three measly power play points in 23 games. Incredibly, he has taken only two (!) shots on goal on the power play through 23 games this season. Yes, really. Courtesy of HockeyStats.com, Crosby’s on-ice power play work is below:

Dating back to Jan. 25, including the 2026 postseason, Crosby has just nine goals in 47 games. The power play is a big reason: he’s scored just two PPGs in nearly a calendar year. No, the Pittsburgh’s current setup isn’t designed for Crosby to be a volume shooter; however, he has 195 career power-play goals yet has simply stopped shooting the puck with the man advantage.

Crosby’s defensive play has fallen off.
 It’s been about six seasons since Crosby was a reliable upper-tier defensive forward. He remains excellent at faceoffs and plays against the opposition’s best, something players his age never contend with. But per HockeyStats.com, his 5-on-5 defensive impact is in the league’s bottom 10%. Crosby’s lines are treading water from a play driving perspective, but it’s now become a risky game of trading chances. It’s a battle they are now losing.

⛽ OPTIMIST: It’s not Sid, it’s his team

Crosby has been so good for so long that Pittsburgh has never truly abandoned hope. It’s the enigma of the last decade of Penguins hockey. They haven’t won a playoff round since 2018. But Crosby’s unparalleled consistency into his late 30s has also kept Pittsburgh from drafting high or assembling a prospect pool that could meaningfully help. Where other franchises rebuilt and retooled aggressively, Crosby’s presence has kept a decade of Penguins front offices from taking major steps back to move forward.

But time has run out. The team card below shows their current rank among the NHL’s 32 teams.

It begs the question what Crosby might do in a new uniform.
 We got a preview at the Olympics where Canada’s captain had six points in three-and-a-half games as a middle-six forward that took important draws and thrived on a potent No. 1 power play unit. Dylan Larkin has just landed with the 
Montreal Canadiens
 and will reap the benefits of playing behind Nick Suzuki in a loaded top six. What could Crosby do in such a role? It remains an irresistible thought, though 
his agent Pat Brisson reiterated earlier this week
 that Crosby “wants to be a Penguin forever.”

⚠️ PESSIMIST: Sid’s worn out from carrying his team

Crosby is the sixth oldest skater in the NHL, and two of the players older than him — Malkin (40) and Kris Letang (39) — still eat significant minutes on the Penguins’ geriatric roster. If you’ve watched Pittsburgh hockey this season, the team often looks exhausted and has issues holding leads.

Crosby has taken more faceoffs (31,497) than anyone since the NHL started tracking in 1997. He’s sixth all-time in points (1,776) despite losing part of his prime to concussions and peaking in a low-scoring era. He’s spent more seasons as a captain (20) than anyone in NHL history. He’s played a punishing game off the cycle, below the hashmarks and in front of the net — areas past generations of superstars typically left for lesser talents. 
Crosby has been both the game’s biggest star and its consummate grinder.

Whereas Jagr, Selanne or Ovechkin were wingers that got to dial back their minutes and matchups as they aged, the next wave of talent to ease Crosby’s burden in Pittsburgh has never arrived. His usage at his age is effectively unprecedented. There is no relief in sight.

💭 Closing Thoughts

Sidney Crosby is not done.
 Offensively, the scuffling Penguins are generating more offense than either of the last two seasons with Crosby on the ice at 5-on-5. Their power play, frankly, cannot get any worse. So, there is enough bad puck luck in the data through 23 games that suggest reports of Crosby’s demise are greatly exaggerated. 
But there is a limit to what we can reasonably expect him to do.
 A 39-year-old with a ton of mileage playing unsheltered minutes on an old, fading team is a recipe for pain.

Crosby has nothing left to prove. He’s 
given more to hockey
 than perhaps anyone ever. He’s won everything. And then he won everything a second or third time. Crosby wants to end his career in Pittsburgh and have a chance to win again. The harsh reality? Unless he plays for another five years, contending in a Penguins sweater is extremely unlikely. Staying put may also prevent him from remaining an elite producer given his team’s expected talent exodus in the months ahead.

Is this the end of an era? Maybe. If so, it will be because of Crosby’s loyalty to Pittsburgh, not because his game collapsed overnight. Sidney Crosby owes us nothing. But I know I’m not alone in wanting to see him get another opportunity to win on a big stage.

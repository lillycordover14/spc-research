S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia's credit rating on CreditWatch Negative on Wednesday, pointing to mounting management and governance risks at the chipmaker and warning that a downgrade could follow within 90 days.

The rating agency also cut its management and governance modifier on the company to neutral from positive, saying recent events had laid bare weaknesses in the board's structure, composition and effectiveness. The current AA rating, one step below the top AAA tier, reflects a very strong capacity to meet financial obligations.

At the center of the review is the departure and subsequent return of Chief Executive Jensen Huang. According to S&P, the episode did not fully resolve questions about how well the board oversees executive conduct. The agency also cited the long tenure of some directors as a possible drag on their independence, and said Nvidia's governance practices may not have kept pace with its rapid expansion into one of the world's most important technology suppliers.

Exposure to China added a second layer of concern, raising doubts about how the company manages geopolitical risk and internal controls. S&P said tighter export restrictions could crimp Nvidia's growth prospects, and that evidence of export-control violations or disclosures that mislead investors about China exposure would weigh on its overall view of the company's credit quality.

Weak executive oversight and the absence of a clear succession plan were flagged as key-person risks. S&P said it intends to meet with Nvidia's board and management to discuss any plans to address shortcomings in board independence, management oversight and leadership succession.

The agency left room for the company to avoid a cut, stating that unless investigations or further disclosures damage Nvidia's reputation or its business outlook, any downgrade would likely be limited to a single notch. It also said the company's competitive position, technology leadership and financial profile remain unchanged for now.

For bondholders and lenders, the move is a reminder that reputational and governance factors can move credit assessments even when balance-sheet metrics look solid. A one-notch reduction from AA would still leave Nvidia in investment-grade territory, but the negative watch could influence borrowing costs and the terms of any new debt. Equity investors, meanwhile, may read the action as a signal that scrutiny of board oversight and China-related compliance is intensifying.

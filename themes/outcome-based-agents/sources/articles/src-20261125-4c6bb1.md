The developments come as Bitcoin trades near $58,900 on November 25, following a prolonged correction from its October 2025 peak above $126,000.

Although the declining valuation could create opportunities for longer-term investors, continued selling pressure raises questions about whether Bitcoin has completed its correction.

Bitcoin AVIV Ratio Falls to 0.8 as Valuation Resets

Bitcoin’s AVIV Ratio measures the relationship between its market price and True Market Mean, a model estimating the acquisition cost of economically active Bitcoin investors.

The indicator divides Bitcoin’s market price by that modeled cost basis. A reading above 1 indicates 
$BTC
 trades above the active-investor cost basis, while a reading below 1 suggests aggregate unrealized losses within the modeled investor cohort.

With AVIV now near 0.8, Bitcoin trades approximately 20% below the benchmark. This represents a substantial deterioration in investor profitability compared with earlier in 2026, when the ratio briefly recovered above 1.

Bitcoin’s AVIV Ratio returns to approximately 0.8 as the 
$BTC
 Risk Index rises above 2, highlighting renewed stress and a significant valuation discount

The historical comparison is particularly important.

During the 2022 bear market, AVIV declined toward 0.55 as Bitcoin approached its November bottom near $15,500. The ratio subsequently recovered through the current region in early 2023 as a new market recovery developed.

However, the present situation differs. Bitcoin is approaching 0.8 from above, with the latest observations still showing downward momentum.

This means the lower valuation has not yet been accompanied by a sustained recovery in investor profitability.

Bitcoin Risk Index Climbs Above 2

A second development concerns Bitcoin’s Risk Index, which has returned above 2 and is approaching its previous 2026 peak.

The model compares Delta Cap with Bitcoin’s market capitalization to assess changing market-risk conditions.

Bitcoin’s Risk Index has climbed above 2, approaching its previous 2026 peak.

Higher readings have historically accompanied periods of substantial market stress, particularly when Bitcoin experiences sharp declines.

The historical series identifies pronounced risk increases around the 2018 and 2022 bear markets. Those earlier spikes were larger than the current reading.

Interestingly, a longer-term descending trendline also connects progressively smaller risk peaks across successive cycles.

However, the latest increase demonstrates that market stress has intensified again despite Bitcoin trading at a significantly lower valuation.

The index’s 90-day moving average helps distinguish persistent changes in risk from temporary daily fluctuations.

For a more convincing stabilization, Bitcoin would need to show sustained moderation in the Risk Index alongside improving price action.

Bitcoin ETF Investors Withdraw $801 Million in Two Days

The latest valuation reset has occurred alongside substantial institutional selling.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately $801.3 million in combined net outflows on November 23 and 24.

November 23 accounted for $569.7 million in withdrawals, followed by another $231.6 million on November 24. These outflows indicate that demand through regulated investment products remains weak.

Additional on-chain data reinforces the pressure. As The Crypto Basic reported on November 24, Bitcoin’s long-term holders realized average losses of approximately 33% during the November 23 rebound.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin’s broader demand indicators have also reflected persistent weakness across spot and derivatives markets.

Together, these developments indicate that lower valuations have not yet attracted sufficient buying demand to establish a sustained reversal.

Has Bitcoin Finally Reached Its Bottom?

The combination of a depressed AVIV Ratio and elevated Risk Index places Bitcoin in a historically important market environment.

However, the two indicators measure different conditions. AVIV identifies how far Bitcoin trades below the active-investor cost basis, while the Risk Index measures stress within its own valuation framework.

Both can deteriorate further during the same selloff.

The immediate price reference remains the June daily closing low near $58,500, followed by the November 21 intraday low around $55,150. Meanwhile, Bitcoin must reclaim the $62,000–$65,000 region to demonstrate stronger recovery momentum.

An improving AVIV Ratio, declining risk readings, and easing ETF withdrawals would strengthen the case that buyers are absorbing available supply.

Conversely, fresh lows accompanied by persistent selling could push Bitcoin into an even deeper valuation discount.

The current data establishes a significant valuation reset, but not yet the end of the selling cycle.

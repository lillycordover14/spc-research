Yongin Mayor Lee Sangil: "I'll Convey Kindergarten Requests to Superintendent and Find Solutions"

by 
Lee jonggu
 

Published 25 Nov.2026 19:16(KST) 

 Share 

 Language 

Text Size 

 Print 

Yongin City holds meeting with around 30 public and private kindergarten directors
Seeks solutions to 28 concerns, including shuttle bus support and parking shortages
Mayor Lee: “Resolve kindergarten issues to make Yongin a good place to raise children”

"I plan to meet with the Gyeonggi Provincial Superintendent of Education soon to discuss education issues in Yongin. I will convey the various suggestions raised at the kindergarten meeting and work to find solutions."

On the 25th, Lee Sangil, mayor of Yongin Special City in Gyeonggi Province, rolled up his sleeves to address long-standing concerns in early childhood education. That afternoon, Lee held a meeting with kindergarten representatives in a conference room at City Hall's First Annex to discuss education issues. About 30 people attended, including Kim Kiwoong, chairman of the Yongin Private Kindergarten Association, directors of local public and private kindergartens, and officials from the Yongin Office of Education.
"Until now, we have held meetings mainly with elementary, middle, and high schools. It is meaningful to sit down with kindergarten directors who teach the children who are the future of Korea and Yongin," Lee said. "I hope this will be a practical opportunity to address the difficulties they face."
Challenges facing private kindergartens, including operating difficulties and convenience for parents, were among the issues discussed. One private kindergarten director called for operating cost subsidies, saying, "Unlike kindergartens attached to elementary schools or stand-alone public kindergartens, private kindergartens have to cover the high cost of shuttle buses themselves, which creates significant difficulties."
An official from the Yongin Office of Education responded, "We understand that there are practical difficulties, as current guidelines require private kindergartens to handle certain matters on their own. We understand that the Gyeonggi Provincial Office of Education is aware of the difficulties faced by private kindergartens and is reviewing various options."
Kindergarten directors also repeatedly raised concerns about insufficient space and parking shortages during events. They asked the city to "open nearby elementary school playgrounds and auditoriums for events such as sports days," and called for temporary permission to stop and park, saying, "It is common for parents to receive parking fines because there are no spaces available during admissions briefings or parent-observation classes."
 

Yongin Special City Mayor Lee Sang-il speaks at a meeting with directors of public and private kindergartens held at the City Hall's First Annex on the 25th. Provided by Yongin City.

View original image

Mayor Lee promised to seek immediate solutions. "These events take place two or three times a year and are for the children's education, so I will consult with the police and others to find a solution," he said. "If we submit an official request for cooperation in advance, I will discuss with the police and the three district offices the possibility of suspending parking restrictions during event hours."
The risk of declining enrollment due to the low birth rate was also a key topic. Kim Kiwoong called on the city to provide support to help make it a "city where it is good to raise children," saying, "More private kindergartens are considering closing as the number of children enrolled falls."
Mayor Lee emphasized, "This year, Yongin Special City's education budget is 98.9 billion won, the largest in Gyeonggi Province. If the massive semiconductor projects planned by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix proceed as scheduled and tax revenues increase, we will invest even more boldly in education and welfare for future generations."

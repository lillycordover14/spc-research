CLARK COUNTY, Mo. (WGEM) - The Clark County Emergency Management Agency (EMA) released a statement assuring residents of their progress monitoring the environment following the lead azide detonation in Iowa.
The EMA stated according to the EPA, the real-time air monitoring following the controlled detonation reported zero detection of airborne lead throughout the monitored area.
The EMA also reported the EPA maintained continuous air monitoring throughout the event and will continue collecting samples from the air, soil and water over the coming days.
The EMA explained additional precautionary measures have been taken locally to ensure the health, well-being and safety of local residents and children, even with the encouraging results.
At 2 p.m., Wednesday, the Clark County Fire Corporation and Kahoka Fire Department conducted precautionary washing operations throughout the community, according to EMA.
While the EPA monitoring results are encouraging, the EMA advises residents to remain informed and follow any additional recommendations issued by local, state and federal authorities.
The Clark County EMA stated it extends its appreciation to the Clark County Fire Corporation and the Kahoka Fire Department for the proactive measures and continued dedication to the safety of the community.
The Clark County EMA thanks local residents for their patience, cooperation and understanding throughout this event.

I 
joined Clay in July 2026
 as a go-to-market engineer for customer experience. This means supporting all of the teams that support our customers—support, customer success (which we call growth strategy), and applied AI. I spend most of my time enabling our enterprise growth strategy team to make sure customers get the full value of Clay and continue to expand. Some of that is traditional RevOps: making sure that people are assigned to accounts, that we’re forecasting correctly, and that renewals are getting closed. Other parts of my role include quintessential 
go-to-market engineering
, like building agents to score account health or creating real-time expansion signals. At the end of the day, the job is making sure the CX teams operate smoothly, and our systems unblock and enable them to do their best work.
The company gifts new Claymates a clay-like version of themselves.
Clay coined the title GTM Engineer in 2023; it’s a relatively new career lane, so I’m occasionally asked what kind of background you need. The answer: it depends. My teammates and I on the GTM Ops team have varied professional experience. I majored in economics, interned in DC, and realized I just wasn’t quite ready for the world of politics and policy. So I did what you do when you don’t know what you want to do with your life: I went into consulting. For three years at RSM, I did CRM consulting—deep in Microsoft Dynamics, building workflows and integrations. 
At a certain point, I realized I wanted to experience the startup space in New York City, including the faster pace and greater ownership. I joined Mutiny and ended up in a founding CX role, where I wore all the hats needed to both support customers and build out the function. That experience cemented my love for operations. That’s how I landed in an analogous role here at Clay. 
Beyond my background, people also ask me what my day-to-day looks like. Every week looks different, but here’s one from November.

A work week in my life as a GTME at Clay
There's always a lot happening at once on the GTM Ops team, and this week was no exception. We made some internal changes in our sales team org structure, so we were getting all the parties together to align on these process updates. We’re also 
hiring on the GTM Ops team
, so I had a few interviews on my calendar. 
But my big focus for the week was an internal tooling enablement session for our growth strategists (more on that later).
A week working at Clay in our GTM Ops org
Monday: Kicking off the week (and each day) with focus 
My brain is best from 9 a.m. to 11 a.m., so I try not to schedule meetings during that time. I come in and usually already have a backlog of things people are asking me for. After that, I try to make progress on whatever I’m working towards during this sprint. 
This sprint cycle is enablement-focused: I’m training our growth strategists on Terra. Terra is our internal rep tool. It’s long been our account context for our sales reps. But our growth strategists haven’t used it very much because it’s been very pre-sales focused. We’re extending it into a GS-specific customer management platform, where growth strategists can quickly see and act upon all key data about their accounts. My big project over the last couple of months has been working with my teammate, Ashwin Desai, and our Terra product team to build the GS version of the app.
We just finished that build, so this week I’m prepping for the enablement session. I’m using my focus blocks to close out the remaining engineering items and create guides and resources so the team has what they need. I am also meeting with CX leaders to define the roadmap for the next feature builds in the app. On Thursday, we’re doing a live demo training session to make sure that they really understand how to use it.

Wednesday: Checking in on our sprint
Today’s big meeting was our mid-sprint sync. My sprint items this week were the Terra enablement, system updates to accommodate our sales process changes, and documentation on the renewal process. We work in two-week sprints, and we meet at the beginning and ask ourselves the following:
What are we going to get done?
How are we going to know it’s done?
Then, like today, we have a mid-sprint sync with the whole GTM Ops org, where we ask these questions:
Are we still on track to get the things done that we said we would? 
Are there any blockers that we need to talk about as a team?
We move quickly and stay flexible to the most immediate needs of the team. Having a strong pulse on long-term roadmaps while being able to adjust priorities often is key.

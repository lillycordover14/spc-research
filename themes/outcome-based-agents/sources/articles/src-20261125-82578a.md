Juneau Public Market returns with dedication to local artisan

Published 4:30 pm Tuesday, November 24, 2026

By 
Arlo Jukes
 Juneau Empire Staff

 

Photo courtesy of Peter Metcalfe

Tom Meyer during the 1986 Juneau Public Market.

The annual Juneau Public Market will return this year with a dedication to a local artisan who died earlier this year.

The Juneau Public Market has been running annually since 1983 as an opportunity for small vendors to test their products before opening a successful business.

Event organizer Peter Metcalfe says vendors who test out products in the Public Market can cater to 1.5 to 2 million people during the tourist season.

One prominent local artisan and vendor was Tom Meyer, an early vendor and potter who sold his products through retailers after finding success in Juneau.

“Tom was a personal family friend,” said Metcalfe who decided to dedicate 2026’s Public Market to Meyer after his death in May of this year.

The market attracts crafters from all over Alaska and the lower 48 states, such as Todd and Annie Fritze from Dillingham.

“Todd is a fur trapper and Annie is a Yup’ik artisan who fabricates the furs into gloves and hats and so on” said Metcalfe, adding that the couple returns to Juneau every year for the Public Market and to visit family.

“I’m 75 now, I feel like a greeter at the door,” he said, “and now a new generation is telling me ‘I’ve been coming to the public market since I was a kid.’”

The public market will be open over Thanksgiving weekend on Friday, Saturday and Sunday in the Juneau Centennial Hall & Convention Center and the Juneau Arts & Culture Center.

More details can be found at www.juneaupublicmarket.com.

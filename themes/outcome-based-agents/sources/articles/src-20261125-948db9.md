OpenAI
 has started folding ChatGPT’s Work tab into its main Chat experience. The first users to see the change are a small group of Pro subscribers.

The merger also comes with a new shared usage allowance. That second part is what has some paying customers reaching for the comment box.

What’s changing inside ChatGPT

Product lead Thibault “Tibo” Sottiaux confirmed the limited rollout in a post on X on Tuesday. OpenAI had already told users about the merger in an earlier email.

The rollout began on November 24, 2026, and initially covers Pro subscribers only. OpenAI says it wants a simpler user experience by January 2027.

Until now, ChatGPT has effectively run two lanes. Chat handled everyday back-and-forth, and Work handled longer, multi-step jobs.

The bigger operational shift is how usage gets counted. Starting in December 2026, Plus, Pro, and Business plans will draw from a single token usage allowance.

That shared pool covers tasks run in both ChatGPT and Codex, OpenAI’s coding product. Tokens are the small chunks of text that AI models read and write, and AI companies use them as the basic unit for metering consumption.

There is one exception. Instant Chat, the fast-response mode, will not count against the shared pool.

That carve-out matters for users who mostly fire off quick questions. Their routine usage should be unaffected by the new accounting.

OpenAI expects most users to see roughly the same reset frequencies they had before. Users running heavier workloads, however, might hit limits sooner.

How we got here

ChatGPT Work launched in July 2026. OpenAI pitched it as an agent for complex tasks, powered by models such as GPT-5.6.

Agents differ from standard chatbots because they carry out sequences of actions instead of giving single replies. They also tend to use far more compute per request.

Usage has been a sore spot for months. OpenAI had planned to reduce usage limits for Pro subscribers, then reversed course after user feedback.

On October 29-30, 2026, the company went further and doubled usage limits for Pro and Business Premium users. That was just weeks before this merger began rolling out.

OpenAI is not the only company moving this way. 
Anthropic
 has also combined its offerings, which suggests AI companies are moving toward unified interfaces instead of separate products.

What this means for users and OpenAI

For power users, the picture is less clear. Anyone relying heavily on both agent tasks in ChatGPT and coding work in Codex now has to budget across a single allowance.

Previously, heavy use in one area did not necessarily eat into the other. Under the new structure, a long Codex session could leave less room for Work-style tasks later in the cycle.

OpenAI’s defense rests on its own projection that most people will notice little difference. Whether that holds will depend on how usage actually plays out once the shared allowance takes effect in December.

The recent doubling of limits gives OpenAI some cushion. Pro and Business Premium users are entering the shared-pool era with more headroom than they had a month earlier.

The phased rollout is telling too. Starting with a small group of Pro subscribers lets OpenAI watch how heavy users behave before the change reaches more people.

Vincent Harper says he relished the chance to play for Kenya during the international break.
The Exeter City wing-back, 
on loan from League Two rivals Walsall,
 came on in his country's 2-1 Africa Cup of Nations qualifier loss to South Africa in Nairobi last week. 
It was his second cap this season, having played 90 minutes against Rwanda in a friendly last month. 
"It's a massive honour," Harper told BBC Radio Devon.
"My parents were both born there, it just feels almost like you're repaying back, not to them but also to the whole country.
"It's a lot of pressure at the same time, but overall it's just a big honour."
Harper has played 13 times since returning to Exeter this season - he had a two-year spell at St James Park before he joined Walsall in the summer of 2025. 
The former Bristol City youth player made his English Football League debut when he moved to Exeter from Eastleigh in 2023 and went on to play 65 times in all competitions before moving to the Midlands.
"I think it just shows you the next level or the level you need to be at to play consistently at an international level," he added.
"It's very intense and very quick and you're playing with the best of the best of the country so you've got to definitely be at your 'A' game.
"If you make one mistake then you'll definitely be punished."

Harper started all seven of Exeter's games after arriving back at the club.
But while he was away on international duty in October, Exeter hit a rich vein of form and he has not been able to get back into the starting line-up.
He is hopeful he can force his way back into a side that has been one of League Two's in-form teams over the past two months - Exeter have risen from close to the relegation zone to the brink of the play-off places after just one loss in their past 10 games in all competitions.
"It's a bit of a frustrating one," he admitted. "Before the first internationals I was heavily involved and then the boys started doing well while I was away.
"It's nice to see the boys doing well, but I want to be more part of it and spend more time on the pitch and show them what I can do.
"But overall it's good to see the team winning and although I might not get as many minutes as I want, it's nice to come on here and there and play my part as well."

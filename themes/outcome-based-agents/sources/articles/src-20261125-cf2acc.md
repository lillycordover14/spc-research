Crafting Through the Holidays: Handmade Gifts From Clay to Lasers&nbsp;&nbsp;GeekDad

[page text]
My love language is gift-giving, and with everyone I give to during the holidays, it can add up. The past few years, I’ve given my family something handmade. One year, I skipped the handmade and just bought glass ornaments for everyone. That year, I had two people ask, “Does this mean you’re doing glassblowing now?” That’s when it hit me…they’d noticed that the craft changed every year. Unintentionally, the gifts had become a reflection of what I had been learning or hyper-focusing on that year.

This year, I’ve been blessed with so many wonderful craft tools that I decided to do a test run of a few possible gift ideas. I’m going to be covering projects using a wide range of tools and budgets, from polymer clay cutters to laser machines and UV printers. I want to explore what you can create with equipment you already own or might be considering adding to your crafting space.

I’ll be giving you the cost, time spent, and materials for each project. Project costs reflect the materials used, not the cost of the machines or reusable tools. I’ve included prices for specialty tools separately when applicable.

Now, in the words of Darkwing Duck…LET’S GET CRAFTING!!

Clay Impress — A Little Holiday Charm

Let’s kick this off with something that doesn’t take fancy equipment or a lot of money to get started. Polymer Clay!

Earlier this year, I talked about my new love of polymer clay projects after discovering Clay Impress. They were kind enough to send me some items to make holiday gifts with, including a lovely birdhouse cutter and cardinal earring cutter.

Building a Winter Birdhouse

Difficulty:
 Beginner

Project Cost:
 Estimated under $5 in clay and adhesive used per birdhouse

Time:
 Approximately 30 minutes, plus baking time

Materials:
 Sculpey Premo polymer clay and bakeable adhesive

Reusable Tools:
 Clay Impress 3D Birdhouse Kit (starting at $19.25), clay roller, and oven. Tool costs are not included in the project estimate.

I didn’t realize you could make a tiny birdhouse with clay until I saw this as an option on their website. It looked fun and something that could make for both a gift for my grandmother and a possible trinket to make for my 
baby dragon hatchery
.

The cool thing about the birdhouse is that you can get it in three different sizes. The smallest is good for earrings or miniature work. The medium and larger sizes are fun for making trinkets and small decor for around the house.

Both the birdhouse and the cardinal use relatively little clay and can be completed in about 30 minutes (not including baking time).

One thing I love about Clay Impress is how they have tutorials on their website to show you how to use and build with their cutters. The birdhouse tutorial goes a little fast, but you can slow it down in your browser if you need to.

The birdhouse is baked in stages. The first is baking the walls and base. Once that is done, you will use either a small amount of super glue or liquid clay adhesive to assemble the pieces and attach the roof. After everything is together, you bake it again to finish the curing process (fun fact: polymer clay is cured in an oven).

My birdhouse isn’t perfect because I put the sides on wrong (whoops), but I think that is part of its charm.

If you are looking to save some money down the road, check out the 
 Clay Impress’s Sculpey Premo Color Recipe Book.
 You only need Cobalt Blue, Fuchsia, Zinc Yellow, and White to mix over 40 colors.

Cardinal Cutouts: Earrings or Holiday Decorations

Difficulty:
 Beginner

Project Cost:
 Estimated under $5 per pair or set of decorations

Time:
 Approximately 15 minutes, plus baking time

Materials:
 
Sculpey Premo polymer clay
, optional acrylic paint, and 
earring posts and backs
 if making earrings

Reusable Tools:
 
Clay Impress Cardinal Mirrored Cutter Set
 ($9.80), oven, and optional 
Earring Post Embedder Kit
 (from $19.95). Tool costs are not included.

During the holidays, cardinals take center stage since their bright red feathers are easy to spot on white snowy days. There’s a saying that goes, “When cardinals appear, angels are near,” and some people believe it’s a sign that a loved one who has passed on is visiting.

As far as projects go, you don’t get much easier on this list than rolling out your clay, pressing into it with the cardinal cutter, baking, and finishing with earring posts. You can also use them as small trinkets for the birdhouse or dollhouse decor. I appreciate the mirrored design because I can choose which direction the design faces.

If you have some acrylic paint on hand, you can add the beak and eye detail after baking. Turn these into earrings with the Earring Post Embedder Kit and earring posts or use them as an addition to another project (I put mine on the birdhouse).

Clay Impress has a ton of amazing cutters and tools for polymer clay crafters. Make sure you check out my 
full review of their lineup here on GeekDad
. 

xTool X1 — A Dice Box for Tabletop Adventurers

Difficulty:
 Beginner

Project Cost:
 Less than $5 in materials per box

Time:
 Approximately 30 minutes, plus stain drying time

Materials:
 
(1) sheet of 12×12 size 3 mm basswood
, 
wood glue
, optional wood stain, and cover artwork

Reusable Tools:
 
xTool X1 laser
 (not included in cost)

xTool has its own creative community with 
Atomm
. It’s a project-sharing platform for you to create and share projects that you can import directly into xTool Studio.

For the X1 project, I wanted to keep it simple and utilize the 55W CO₂ laser to make a fun dice box. I went with the 
Card Box Imitating a Bound Book
 by user nini86. Special note: This project is licensed for personal, noncommercial use.

You can make this with basswood, and with the 55W CO₂, the X1 cuts it in less than 5 minutes. Grab some wood stain (I love 
Varathane Premium Fast Dry American Walnut Wood Stain Spray
 on Amazon, retail $10). Total time from cutting to staining to assembly was about 30 minutes (give or take drying time on the stain).

If you’d like to personalize the design, open your creative app of choice (mine is Canva) and create a PNG file. Drop it in xTool Studio, and now you have a cool personalized gift.

The only challenging part of this project was gluing a few of the pieces. You need to be careful not to let the glue spread and lock pieces that are supposed to move (please don’t ask how I know this…).

Stay tuned to GeekDad for my full review of all that this machine can do (and it’s a lot!). 

xTool Omni O1 — Creating a Stained-Glass Effect

Difficulty:
 Beginner

Project Cost:
 Estimated under $5 in physical materials per ornament; UV ink and Atomm credits may add to the cost

Time:
 Approximately 30 minutes

Materials:
 
Clear acrylic ornament
, ribbon, UV ink for the Omni, and a stained-glass design generated with 15 Atomm credits, xTool Select acrylic prep wipes

Reusable Tools:
 
xTool Omni O1
 (not included in cost)

The xTool Omni is a wonderful UV printer that comes in three varieties: UV, dual UV, and UV+DT. I have the UV and DT (supports direct-to-garment and direct-to-film) model.

For this project, I took a photo of my grandfather who passed away in 1997 and asked Atomm’s AI-assisted stained glass generator to make it into a stained glass file. It cost me about 15 credits to get the file, and the result was worth it.

You get three layers: color, varnish texture, and the top lead lines. With the file, you also get the settings (you can adjust them to your liking, like I did, if you want to save on materials or don’t care about the varnish layer). You just need to layer them together and tell the Omni to print.

I used clear acrylic ornaments I found on sale and chose his favorite color for the ribbon. When the holidays are over, you can keep this out as a suncatcher and enjoy it year-round.

xTool M2 — Print or Engrave, You Choose!

The xTool M2 is more of a beginner machine than the xTool X1 and is a great intro into lasers. For the holidays, I leaned into the unique features that the M2 has: the printhead and engraving capabilities.

Custom Notebooks for Readers, Writers, and Gamers

Difficulty:
 Beginner

Project Cost:
 Approximately $2 per notebook, including estimated ink usage

Time:
 Approximately 15 minutes plus printing time

Materials:
 
Blank kraft notebook
 and CMYK printing ink

Reusable Tools:
 
xTool M2 with CMYK printhead
 (not included in cost)

I’m a lover of notebooks, and with the xTool M2 printhead, I can print directly onto kraft notebooks to personalize them for friends and family. I also make them for when I need a dedicated notebook for a book I’m reading or a project I’m working on.

If you’re not sure about a design, make sure to look at Atomm for ideas. My suggestion is to keep it simple but personal. One year, I created logos for each of my family members (their first and last initial) and put that on the cover. They all had the same design, but personal to them.

If you feel adventurous, you can also grab a pack of 
#2
 pencils and engrave their name or a favorite geeky saying on them. You can create a jig to hold the pencils in xTool Studio. Just make sure you have a few extra to get your settings right before final production. For the “dammit donut” pencil, I did the engraving and then ran a Sharpie marker over it to get the details black. Once it dried, I wiped off the excess marker with isopropyl alcohol. 

And if you’re feeling really crafty, look up a video on how to turn a small alligator clip into a cute pen or pencil holder!

Engraved Metal Necklace Tags 

Difficulty:
 Beginner

Project Cost:
 Approximately $10 each, depending on the blank and accessories

Time:
 Approximately 20 minutes, including assembly

Materials:
 
Stainless-steel bar pendant
, leather cord, eyelet, and optional die or charm

Reusable Tools:
 xTool M2 laser and small drill (not included in cost)

With the 10W or 20W diode laser heads, you can engrave on stainless steel if you get the settings just right. Confession time. I got mildly addicted to engraving on the stainless steel bars that xTool sent me when reviewing the M2 machine.

I went with a personal design for this one and used my Fanttik to drill into a die from my dragon trinket case. Paired with a leather cord, I think it not only came out giftable, but also sellable.

It took about 15 minutes to engrave, and another 5 minutes to add the dice eyelet and leather cord. Not bad for something that looks like it cost me $20.

For more project ideas, check out my 
full review of the xTool M2
 here on GeekDad!

Final Thoughts — It’s the Thought (and Maybe a Little Glue) That Counts

Looking back at these projects, I’ve shown that handmade gifts don’t need to be complicated or perfect to mean something. Whether it’s a personalized notebook, a tiny clay birdhouse that didn’t quite go according to plan, or a special ornament made from a favorite photo, each project has a little story behind it.

May your holidays be merry, your gifts be handmade, and your glue stay away from anything that’s supposed to move.

Adaptive Eyeglasses that Automatically Adjust for Close or Distant Viewing 

Are the VIVIFoCAL S1's too good to be true?


                        By 
Rain Noe

                        - 
November 25

in 

Object Culture

This seems too good to be true: A Chinese startup called VIVIFoCAL claims to have invented adaptive eyeglasses, which automatically adjust between close and distant viewing. A laser sensor on their 
S1 glasses
 supposedly measures the distance of what you're looking at, and a tiny motor then slides a pair of interior lenses sideways to change their focusing strength.
 
If true, this would do away with the need for people to switch between reading and distance glasses, which would be amazing. My skepticism comes from the company's skimpy description; for instance, they don't say how the laser sensor can tell precisely what your eyes are looking it, nor is there any eye-tracking. If you were to look down the street in Manhattan, or at a deep stand of trees in the woods, what does the laser decide to measure to?
 
As you'd expect, the lack of detail hasn't deterred Kickstarter backers, who have successfully 
crowdfunded the S1
 to the tune of $200,000 with 37 days left to pledge at press time. The glasses are going for $340 and are due to ship in January. I'll eagerly be awaiting independent reviews.

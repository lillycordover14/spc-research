                How Programmable Networks Will Unlock Outcome-Based Connectivity Revenue

	For most of the past decade, operators have sold connectivity by the gigabyte and by the megabit. AI is about to change who uses the network, how connectivity is bought and what customers are prepared to pay for. The next wave of growth will not come from carrying more traffic, but from guaranteeing what that traffic achieves: a factory robot that never loses its control link, a video analytics workload that always meets its latency budget, or an AI agent that can ask the network for what it needs and receive it.


	This is the core of the new network economy. Turning AI infrastructure and programmable networks into revenue depends on operators being able to promise outcomes, deliver them automatically and prove that they did. Five shifts will define the year ahead.

1. AI Agents Become the Network's Fastest-Growing Customer


	The first wave of generative AI produced bursty, human-driven traffic: a question, an answer and a pause. AI agents behave differently. They run continuously, call other agents and services, retrieve data from multiple sources and act on what they find. As enterprises deploy them across operations, logistics and customer service, network demand will shift towards sustained machine-to-machine flows, increased traffic between edge, cloud and data centre environments, and tighter expectations around latency and reliability.


	In 2027, operators will begin treating agent traffic as a distinct customer segment, with its own measurement, forecasting and pricing models. Those able to identify this traffic clearly within their networks will be better positioned to plan capacity and develop targeted service offerings. Those that cannot will continue carrying it as undifferentiated network load.


	Network planning will also need to evolve. Agent workloads are frequently tied to specific sites, cloud environments and operating schedules, while their value to customers depends more on consistent connectivity than peak speeds. Understanding these requirements will become increasingly important as AI agents take on a larger role in enterprise operations and generate sustained demand for reliable network services.

2. Connectivity Is Sold by Outcome Rather Than by Gigabyte


	Enterprises purchasing connectivity for AI workloads are not necessarily looking for more bandwidth. Their priority is ensuring that applications perform as expected. In 2027, more operators are expected to package network capabilities around measurable outcomes, including application performance backed by service-level commitments, network slices dimensioned for specific workloads, and private networks designed to keep production processes operating within defined performance tolerances.


	Programmable networks, network slicing and network APIs are making these offerings technically possible. However, the ability to deliver them consistently at scale will determine which operators successfully monetise these capabilities and which remain at the announcement stage. Delivering service commitments without extensive manual intervention will be particularly important, as operational overhead can quickly erode the margins associated with premium connectivity services.


	This shift will also influence pricing models. Instead of conventional service tiers defined primarily by connection speed and data volume, operators will increasingly introduce offers based on the outcomes they guarantee and the remedies available when those commitments are not met. Pricing will reflect the value of the guaranteed outcome as well as the operator's confidence in its ability to deliver it.

3. Outcome-Based Connectivity Makes Verifiable Service Assurance Essential


	As connectivity offerings become increasingly outcome-driven, operators will need to demonstrate that the performance commitments made to customers are being fulfilled. A promise that cannot be verified cannot be priced effectively. Service assurance will therefore evolve from an internal network operations function into an integral part of the connectivity product itself.


	Customers will increasingly expect evidence, at the individual service and reporting-period level, that the connectivity they purchased has delivered the agreed outcomes. This evidence must be derived from actual network traffic, provide explanations when performance deteriorates and identify the corrective actions taken to address service degradation.


	Premium service tiers and automated service credits will both depend on the availability of reliable performance evidence. Operators capable of generating this evidence from governed, multi-vendor network data will be better positioned to introduce and defend outcome-based pricing models. Those relying on aggregated key performance indicators (KPIs) and manually generated reports may find it more difficult to substantiate their service guarantees.


	Independent verification across multiple network domains will also become increasingly important. Most outcome-based services extend across radio access, transport, core and cloud environments involving equipment from different vendors. Customers will therefore expect performance evidence that covers the complete service rather than stopping at individual network-domain boundaries.

4. Networks Open Their Capabilities to External AI Agents Under Governance


	Network APIs are currently used largely by developers building applications and services. However, their role is expected to expand as enterprises increasingly deploy their own AI agents to interact directly with network infrastructure. These agents could check service health, request temporary quality improvements or determine whether specific performance commitments can be met over a defined period.


	This development will place network infrastructure directly within customers' automated decision-making processes. As a result, operators will need to expose network capabilities through standardised interfaces that can be accessed by external agents. Each agent will need to be identified, its permissions governed by defined policies and its requests recorded to maintain accountability and operational control.


	Governance will also be essential to protecting the commercial value of these services. An agent requesting premium network quality must have the appropriate authorisation and its usage must be accounted for and billed accordingly. Similarly, abnormal agent behaviour must be identified and contained as quickly as other network faults. Without effective controls over access, permissions and usage, exposing network capabilities to external agents could introduce operational and financial risks that undermine the business opportunity.

5. AI-Native Telcos Move Towards Governed Autonomous Network Operations


	Delivering guaranteed network outcomes will require operators to move beyond processes that depend on human-speed intervention. Networks must increasingly be capable of detecting, investigating and correcting service degradation before customers experience an impact. While most operators are now targeting Level 4 autonomous operations, progress remains uneven, advancing one network domain and use case at a time. Persistent data silos continue to be a major obstacle to broader autonomy.


	In 2027, operators making the fastest progress will be those establishing a governed data foundation that can be shared across their AI agents. They will also need to introduce autonomy incrementally, granting agents greater authority as they demonstrate reliable performance. This means allowing agents to make recommendations when underlying data is insufficient, execute actions when confidence levels are high and maintain an auditable record of decisions and interventions in both cases.


	This transition will also reshape the role of network engineers. Rather than responding to every alarm and managing routine operational tasks, engineering teams will increasingly focus on establishing policies, reviewing performance evidence and correcting agents when their decisions are inaccurate. Each correction can then become part of the operational knowledge available to support subsequent investigations, enabling continuous improvement across the network.


	The convergence of AI agents, programmable connectivity, outcome-based service models, verifiable assurance and governed autonomous operations will reshape how telecom operators create and capture value in 2027. As enterprises increasingly depend on AI-driven workloads, the competitive advantage will shift towards operators capable of guaranteeing application outcomes rather than simply providing connectivity. Those that can translate network intelligence into measurable customer value, deliver commitments automatically and demonstrate performance consistently will be better positioned to unlock new revenue opportunities in the emerging network economy.

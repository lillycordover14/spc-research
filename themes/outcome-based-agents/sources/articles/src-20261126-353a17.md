John and Sheri Puppo were winners in the Father (80 and Over) and Daughter division.

From Nov. 6 to Nov. 8, more than 160 players from 20 states traveled to Wellington for the 2026 National Family Clay Court Championships at the Wellington Tennis Center. It was part of four national family events on multiple surfaces — grass, hard, indoor and clay — held across the U.S.

The tournament was a USTA Level One Gold Ball Championship event, and players were competing for national ranking points, as well as the prestigious gold, silver and bronze ball awards that go to the winning teams. Play was hosted in Mother/Son, Father/Daughter and Mother/Daughter divisions in various age groups.

Competition was at an extremely high level, as many of the players were past and current NCAA Division I college players, national champions, ATP tour players and current ITF competitors.

“This is the sixth year that we have hosted the USTA Family Clay Court Championships,” said Chuck Gill, tournament director and director of the Wellington Tennis Center. “The level of tennis gets better each year. Many matches were decided by the super tiebreaker, and upsets were seen each day.”

According to Gill, the event lived up to its pre-tournament billing.

“The tournament continues to showcase everything that is good about the sport of tennis,” he said. “We had players of all ages competing with family members at a great venue. This year was again a true national championship with gold, silver and bronze ball awards going to players from 23 states outside of Florida. This event attracts the best teams from all over the United States.”

Noteworthy winners from Palm Beach County were the team of local Wellington tennis player John Puppo and his daughter Sheri Puppo of Boynton Beach. As a team, they won the Gold Ball for finishing first in the Ultra Senior Division. Mayacoo Lakes Country Club teaching pro Bjorn Munroe and his daughter Addyson finished third in the Father-Daughter Open Division.

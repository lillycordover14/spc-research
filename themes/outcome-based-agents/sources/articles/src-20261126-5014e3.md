ElevenLabs has held preliminary talks about a funding round that would value the London-based voice AI company at about $33 billion, 
Bloomberg reported Wednesday
, citing people familiar with the matter. Measured against the $700 million in annual recurring revenue (ARR) that one unnamed source cited, that price equals about 47 times revenue, up from about 37 times at the 
$22 billion employee share sale
 ElevenLabs announced Sept. 30.
Management plans to formally launch the raise in the first quarter of 2027, according to The Next Web's relay of the report. ElevenLabs declined to comment, Bloomberg's article is paywalled, and TechTimes has not independently confirmed the talks.
For anyone weighing private AI valuations, the check is straightforward. The reported price rose 50% since September, while the one revenue figure behind it rose about 17%, and that figure comes from a single person.
Bloomberg Sources Describe Preliminary Talks, Not Signed Round
The report 
names no investors
 and no round size. The talks are early, and the timeline described is a formal launch in the first quarter of 2027, not a close.
How Much Revenue Does ElevenLabs Make? Only CEO Interview Figure Reaches $600 Million
ElevenLabs said it ended 2025 with $350 million in ARR and 
passed $500 million in 2026
, months after its February Series D post cited "over $330 million" for the same year-end. In a 
TechCrunch interview published Sept. 24
, CEO Mati Staniszewski put ARR at about $600 million. The Sept. 30 tender announcement itself disclosed no ARR figure.
That makes $700 million the first November figure, and it rests on one unnamed person. If it holds, ARR grew about 17%, roughly $100 million, in about two months. ARR is a company-reported run rate, not audited revenue, and the sources TechTimes reviewed do not explain how ElevenLabs calculates it.
Underneath the headline number, ElevenLabs said enterprise customers account for 55% of revenue, that five of the ten largest tech companies use its technology daily, and that its agents handle more than 15 million conversations a week. The company counts more than 800 employees, according to its 
tender announcement post
.

The Next Web report
TechTimes' July report calculated about 44 times for the $22 billion tender using the $500 million ARR then available, before the CEO's $600 million figure surfaced. On the newer figure, the September multiple falls to about 37 times, which makes the reported November target a sharper step than it first appeared. The February multiple uses the $350 million figure from ElevenLabs' later post; on the "over $330 million" cited at the time it would be slightly higher.
Peer marks offer a comparison. Synthesia was valued at $4 billion in January on about $140 million in annual revenue, roughly 29 times by TechTimes' arithmetic.
Two of Three Marks Since September 2025 Came From Employee Share Sales
In an employee tender, staff sell existing shares to outside buyers, so the proceeds go to the sellers and the company raises no new capital. Wellington and T. Rowe Price 
led the September sale
, with new buyers including EQT, Goldman Sachs, Singapore's GIC and Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan.
The Series D did not end in February. A third close in May brought the round past $550 million and ran alongside a 
$100 million tender
, the second in under a year.
Bloomberg's report does not say whether the 2027 round would raise new money, sell existing shares, or do both. The distinction matters because a share-sale price reflects what buyers will pay for existing stakes. A priced round with company-supplied financials would test the 47 times figure; two more tenders would not.
CEO Declined Margin Detail While Agent Pricing Bills Model Costs Separately
Staniszewski declined to discuss gross margins in detail in the TechCrunch interview and said he is comfortable with margins shrinking if that wins share and builds value over several years. None of the company statements TechTimes reviewed says what gross margin sits underneath the $700 million figure.
Staniszewski also named a competitive wrinkle: Decagon, a customer that trained on ElevenLabs' voice technology, now competes with it.
Strategic Backers Nvidia, Santander and Deutsche Telekom Joined Series D
ElevenLabs said in May that many of the largest companies building with its technology are now investing too, and its post named 
NVIDIA, Santander and Deutsche Telekom
 among new investors. The reports TechTimes reviewed do not say what share of ARR comes from companies that are also investors, and none alleges that any customer contract is tied to an investment. The overlap still widens the group with a stake in the next price.
Staniszewski Backs AI-Call Disclosure as Regulators Question Fraud Safeguards
Staniszewski said businesses should tell callers when they are talking to an AI agent, though he expects that norm to fade within about five years as people use their own agents. The policy view sits against a harder record. Sen. Maggie Hassan (D-N.H.) 
wrote to voice cloning companies
 on April 16, pressing them on how they prevent exploitation by scammers.
Separately, 
Consumer Reports published an assessment
 of AI voice cloning products, including ElevenLabs, in March 2025.
Illinois Voiceprint Class Action Carries Per-Violation Statutory Damages
In May, Illinois journalists, podcasters, narrators and voice actors 
filed class actions in Chicago
 against ElevenLabs and other technology companies, alleging their voices were used to train AI models without consent. The Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act sets damages of $1,000 per violation, or $5,000 if the violation is intentional or reckless. The allegations are unproven and the cases are pending; the Chicago Sun-Times reported that none of the companies responded, and TechTimes found no ruling or damages estimate.

Confirmed ARR, Round Type and Margins Would Settle Whether Multiple Holds
Three disclosures would turn a reported ask into a defensible price. A company-confirmed ARR figure would show whether the 17% rise since September is real. A statement on primary versus secondary would say whether new money is involved. Gross margin would show how much of each revenue dollar remains after costs. The investor list matters too: whether September's buyers, including Wellington and T. Rowe Price, return, and who joins them.
An IPO would add a fourth test. Staniszewski has said ElevenLabs 
could start preparing for a listing
 in 2028, though in the Sept. 24 interview he did not confirm a 2028 date. A $33 billion round in early 2027 would set the reference price ahead of any listing. Until ElevenLabs confirms its revenue and round type, the 47 times multiple is a reported ask that rests on one unnamed source, a CEO-quoted run rate and undisclosed margins, and readers should weigh it that way.
Frequently Asked Questions
Is ElevenLabs raising new money or selling existing shares?
Bloomberg's report does not say. The September $22 billion mark came from a $300 million 
employee tender announcement
, in which staff sold existing shares, while the February Series D was new capital and grew past $550 million after its May close.
Is ElevenLabs going public?
ElevenLabs has not announced an IPO date. Staniszewski has described groundwork for a listing in the coming years, and 
earlier reports pointed to 2028
 as the start of preparation.
What is annual recurring revenue, and is it audited?
ARR annualizes the recurring revenue a company is collecting at a point in time. It is a company-reported business metric, not an audited accounting figure, and ElevenLabs has not published audited financials in the sources TechTimes reviewed.
Can retail investors buy ElevenLabs shares?
ElevenLabs said retail investors can invest through Robinhood Ventures, and 
Robinhood's fund RVI
 is among its holders. The company has not detailed how a retail offering would work.

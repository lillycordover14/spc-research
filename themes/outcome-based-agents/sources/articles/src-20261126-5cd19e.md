SailPoint sets out 2027 identity security predictions focused on AI agent governance&nbsp;&nbsp;Australian Cyber Security Magazine

[page text]
SailPoint has released a set of “2027 Identity Security Predictions” arguing that enterprise use of autonomous AI agents is outpacing identity and governance controls, increasing the risk of operational disruption and security incidents.

The company cited its “Horizons of Identity Security (2026–2027)” research, which it said found 79% of enterprises are running AI agents in production while 2% have deployed identity security designed to secure them. SailPoint described this as a “40:1 Agent Security Gap”.

SailPoint also pointed to “2026 disclosures” involving evaluation AI models escaping test environments, disabling internal security alerts and exposing downstream customer data through compromised AI supply chains. The company said this shifts the challenge from authenticating log-ins to governing what autonomous agents do on a human’s behalf.

“For the past two years, enterprises raced to see what they could accomplish with autonomous AI agents; in 2027, boards, insurers, and regulators will demand to know who is accountable when those agents take action that crosses a legal or regulatory boundary,” said Mark McClain, CEO and Founder of SailPoint.

Among SailPoint’s predictions is that a major public company will disclose an incident where an autonomous “agent swarm” triggers a large-scale supply chain failure. The company said such an event could occur without an external attacker, describing a scenario in which misconfigured agents purge supplier contracts, delete inventory databases and misroute shipments before humans can intervene.

SailPoint also predicts separation-of-duties controls for AI and tamper-resistant governance will become mandatory, arguing that an autonomous agent should not hold administrative privileges in the same environment that monitors its behaviour. It said this would drive demand for independent identity governance and enforcement of “Zero Standing Privilege”.

Another forecast is the emergence of “B2B agent lockouts” as autonomous agents connect to third-party SaaS and partner networks through protocols such as Model Context Protocol (MCP). SailPoint said organisations will increasingly require cryptographic proof of an agent’s identity lineage and least-privilege scope before allowing API interaction.

“At near-AGI scale, manual human access reviews for autonomous agents are a fantasy,” said Chandra Gnanasambandam, CTO and EVP, Product of SailPoint. “Because you cannot take a rogue AI agent to jail, every digital worker must carry cryptographically bound human lineage.”

SailPoint’s predictions also include increased exposure of API keys, OAuth tokens and other machine secrets supporting agentic workflows. The company said that while passkey adoption is expected to grow, attackers may bypass human MFA by exploiting unmanaged developer tokens and long-lived credentials tied to automated processes.

It further argues that zero trust will evolve toward what it calls “Continuous Intent Assurance”, where an agent’s identity, human lineage and real-time business intent are verified before each API call. SailPoint also suggested identity governance maturity will become a board-level metric tied to regulatory deadlines, cyber insurance and valuation, including post-quantum cryptography requirements and enforcement of the EU AI Act for high-risk systems.

The company said enterprises will also move away from permissive “AI Playground” policies, shifting from broad experimentation—such as agents operating under personal employee credentials—to governed deployments with measurable ROI and stronger identity controls.

Serval Resources Plc (AIM: SRVL)
 has commenced preparations for a fully funded 20,000-metre copper-silver drilling programme in Namibia’s Kaoko Basin, with two diamond drilling rigs being mobilised and geophysical surveys identifying priority exploration targets.

The company also confirmed that it has 
repaid £1 million of deferred consideration relating to its acquisition of Kalahari Copper Limited (KCL)
, reducing the outstanding acquisition liability by half and lowering future interest payments.

Serval is advancing exploration across its 
789-square-kilometre licence portfolio in north-western Namibia
, where previous drilling has identified high-grade copper and silver mineralisation at several prospects.

The first phase of drilling will comprise approximately 6,000 metres during the first quarter of 2027
, followed by a further 14,000 metres scheduled for completion in the third quarter.

Two diamond core drilling rigs and supporting equipment are being mobilised by drilling partner Capital Drilling, with the establishment of a drilling camp and local operational team planned for December 2026.

The company expects drilling to commence following completion of mobilisation and site preparations.

Geophysical surveys at the Otjozongombe West and Omatapati prospects are helping Serval identify priority copper-silver drilling targets
, building on geological mapping and historical exploration results.

An Induced Polarisation (IP) survey at Otjozongombe West, which began on 29 October, has now been completed.

The survey identified 
resistivity and chargeability anomalies in areas where previous drilling confirmed copper-silver mineralisation
, alongside additional targets that will be investigated during the forthcoming programme.

Serval plans to extend the IP surveys along strike to improve its understanding of the distribution of potential mineralisation before drilling.

Complementary ground magnetic surveys are also planned to refine the company’s geological interpretation and assist with target selection.

The Otjozongombe West survey covered three profiles of approximately 950 metres each
, using a high-resolution pole-dipole configuration with receiver electrodes spaced 10 metres apart.

The technique provided an estimated investigation depth of approximately 250 to 300 metres.

The results identified comparable geophysical patterns across the surveyed profiles, including conductive zones near the surface and deeper resistivity and chargeability anomalies associated with geological contacts.

Some anomalies may indicate disseminated sulphide mineralisation, although their copper content and economic significance will require confirmation through drilling and laboratory analysis.

Serval’s exploration programme builds on more than 9,000 metres of historical drilling undertaken by Kalahari Copper Limited
, which identified several copper-silver intersections across the Kaoko Basin licences.

Among the previously reported results, drill hole 
OGW005 intersected 13 metres grading 5.03% copper equivalent
, comprising 4.57% copper and 34 parts per million silver.

Another hole, 
OPR002, returned 13 metres at 3.32% copper equivalent
, including 1.82% copper and 109.8 parts per million silver.

The results were reported in October 2026 and form part of the geological information being used to guide the new drilling programme.

The company’s priority exploration licence, EPL 7081, hosts the Omatapati, Otjozongombe West, Otjozongombe East and Horseshoe prospects
, providing several targets for the planned campaign.

The Kaoko Basin forms part of the Damara Orogenic Belt and is interpreted as a geological extension of the Central African Copperbelt, a major sediment-hosted copper-silver province.

Copper mineralisation at Serval’s prospects is associated primarily with sulphide and oxide occurrences within dolostones and calcareous siltstones of the Ombombo Formation.

The company is using geological mapping, historical drilling, geophysical surveys and planned magnetic data to investigate the most prospective sections of these formations.

Chief executive Robin Birchall said the company had maintained a rapid pace of fieldwork to prepare for drilling as soon as Capital Drilling completes its mobilisation.

Birchall highlighted Serval’s systematic exploration approach, with each stage designed to improve the geological understanding established through earlier work.

He said combining historical drilling results with mapping, IP surveys and ground magnetics would help identify the strongest targets and focus drilling expenditure.

Alongside the operational preparations, 
Serval has reduced its outstanding deferred acquisition consideration from £2 million to £1 million
 following a repayment to KCL Investments Limited.

The liability relates to Serval’s acquisition of Kalahari Copper Limited, which held the company’s exploration licences in Namibia and Botswana.

The board reviewed the company’s cash position before making the repayment, seeking to reduce financing costs while preserving sufficient capital for its planned drilling activities.

                        Iran's Appliance Makers Say Shipping Costs Have Jumped Sixfold Since the War
                    

November 26, 2026

                                                IranWire
                                            

                                            1 min read
                                        

The secretary of Iran's home appliance manufacturers' association said Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026, that the cost of shipping raw materials and parts by sea has risen to more than six times its level before the 40-day war. Nasrin Ojaghi said production costs continue to rise. The deputy head of the home appliance union had earlier said prices for these goods had risen by about 80% from the start of the war to October.

Ojaghi said the cost of shipping a 20-foot container has reached $6,200 to $9,300 and that some shipments have been billed at as much as $21,000. She said insurance costs for shipments have risen by 90% to 100% and that difficulty obtaining parts and raw materials has forced factories to operate below capacity.

She said raw materials account for 70% to 80% of home appliance production costs. According to Ojaghi, the prices of some domestically produced polymers on the open market rose by as much as 370% between February and November 2026. Output fell from about 20 million units in 2024 to 18 million in 2025, she said. Production dropped by half in April, and by the end of the summer the decline had narrowed to 8%.

In October, Hossein Shahrestani, the deputy head of the home appliance union, blamed rising prices of raw materials and upstream products. He said that as prices climbed, household purchasing power and demand fell, and installment sales became harder for sellers.

On Wednesday, Nov. 25, First Vice President Mohammadreza Aref told a meeting of the government's market regulation headquarters that the government was not concerned about the naval blockade. He pointed to an increase in the unloading and clearance of basic goods at Iran's non-southern borders.

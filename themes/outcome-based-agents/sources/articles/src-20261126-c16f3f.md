China is building its own AI chip industry, and UBS thinks it is about to get much bigger. A recent research note from the bank projects domestic AI accelerator shipments rising from 1.7 million units in 2025 to more than 10 million units by 2030.

That is a nearly sixfold increase in five years.

What UBS is projecting

The bank also sees domestic suppliers taking a growing slice of the Chinese market. UBS expects homegrown chipmakers to hold 83% market share by 2027, rising to 88% by 2028.

UBS named Cambricon Technologies and Biren Technology as frontrunners. If the projections hold, both are in a position to capture a meaningful share of that demand.

Wall Street largely agrees on the direction

UBS is not alone in this view. Analysts at 
Morgan Stanley
 and Goldman Sachs have also forecast a sharp climb in China’s AI chip self-sufficiency.

Morgan Stanley expects China’s self-sufficiency in AI GPUs to rise from approximately 20% in 2023 to more than 40% by 2026. Its projections point to as much as 85% by 2030.

Estimates for 2030 land somewhere between 70% and 85% or higher, depending on the bank and the metric used.

There is also a nearer-term marker to watch. Domestic high-end AI chip shipments are forecast to reach 5 million units in 2026.

Market share data shows where things stand now. In the first half of 2026, 
Nvidia
’s share of China’s data center AI chip market reportedly fell to 34%, with Huawei capturing 28%.

The manufacturing bottleneck

China plans to expand advanced-node wafer capacity from under 20,000 wafers per month to 100,000 within one to two years. The longer-term goal is 500,000 wafers per month by 2030.

SMIC, China’s largest contract chipmaker, is targeting substantial increases in wafer production along with yield improvements.

Why Beijing is pushing so hard

The motivation is no mystery. US export restrictions have targeted the supply of advanced chips to China, cutting off access to some of the most powerful AI hardware on the market.

Beijing has responded by backing local production, particularly in advanced-node manufacturing.

What this means for the chip industry

For Chinese chipmakers, Cambricon, Biren, Huawei and SMIC are at the center of a government-backed push with a large and growing domestic customer base.

There are real risks, though. Forecasts that run to 2030 depend heavily on manufacturing execution, and the jump from under 20,000 to 500,000 advanced wafers per month is enormous.

The near-term checkpoints are clear enough: the forecast 5 million high-end domestic chip shipments in 2026, Morgan Stanley’s 40%-plus self-sufficiency target for the same year, and whether wafer capacity reaches 100,000 per month on time.

Ryan Strome capped a four-goal second period with his fifth of the season, while four other Flames players tallied two points each as Calgary’s offence erupted for a 6-3 win over the Pittsburgh Penguins.

PITTSBURGH — 
Jonathan Castagna
 had just spent the previous 20 seconds standing face-to-face with his childhood idol.

A scrum around the Pittsburgh Penguins’ net had just started settling down and there was Sidney Crosby, giving the 21-year-old Flames rookie a shove, followed by an awkward stanza in which the two just stood facing one another.

“I probably shouldn’t say this, it’s maybe too much information, but I was like, ‘I can’t touch this guy,’” he said of the staredown of sorts with the man whose photo hung on the wall of Catagna’s childhood bedroom.

“I gave him a crosscheck when the goalie covered the puck because I thought the play was still alive, and when I saw it was him and he was coming at me, I was like, ‘crap.’

“Fair enough, he gave me a whack back, and I was like, ‘I really hope he doesn’t start scrumming with me because I don’t want to touch this guy.’ It kind of pulled me away from the game a little — it was kind of a surreal moment. 

“I was going through a little bit of a moral dilemma there.”

So they stood in silence: a legend frustrated by what would be another loss, and a wide-eyed youngster trying to process a moment he’ll talk about for the rest of his life.

Oh, but it gets better.

Half a minute later, after Crosby lost a draw to Mikael Backlund, Castagna calmly collected a rebound in the high slot and ripped it into the open net for his fourth of the year – putting the Flames up 5-3 with ten minutes left in a 6-3 win at PPG Paints Arena.

“I mean, that’s about as good a Christmas present as you’ll get,” he beamed. 

“It was definitely really cool to score, especially growing up with this being my favourite team. He was definitely my favourite player growing up. I feel like I did a really good job shutting that out and not letting that emotion get to me, and just played the game, and left it for afterward to reflect on how cool that was.

“Really full circle there.”

These are the type of wide-eyed moments Flames fans can delight in watching over the next few years, as young prospects play out their dreams.

It’s been a remarkable run for Castagna since he was called up from the minors one week into his first season as a pro.

Establishing early on that his doggedness, defensive responsibility and face-off prowess will prevent him from ever returning to the minors, he’s since started finding the net, in bunches, no less.

That’s four goals in his last five games.

The Flames’ biggest revelation of the season has given them a middle-six pillar up the middle to build around — a piece they didn’t know they had.

Before puck drop Wednesday, there was plenty of excitement surrounding the formation of a new Kid Line the Flames felt ready to debut, putting Castagna between Matvei Gridin and Aydar Suniev.

They didn’t disappoint, as Castagna scored the insurance-marker midway through the third, and the trio did its part in allowing the second and fourth lines to score two goals apiece.

“I thought Jonathan was really good,” singled out coach Ryan Huska.

“I thought he had another game where he had an impact every time he was on the ice, and I think that’s important.”

He finished one for two on draws against Crosby and played a big role in killing off three of the Penguins’ five power plays.

Game 2 of a five-game eastern swing was a total team effort that saw the Flames bounce back from a 1-0 first-period deficit to score four in the second period, including goals from Joel Farabee, Brennan Othmann, Max Tsyplakov and a beauty from Ryan Strome.

Twelve different Flames had at least a point, with four lads chipping in multi-point efforts. 

And for those keeping track, after going his first 18 NHL games without a goal, Castagna now sits poised to catch his idol, whose six goals this season are just two more than he has.

“I wouldn’t say that just yet,” he laughed.

“But I’ll keep trying.”

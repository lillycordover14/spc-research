(Bloomberg) -- The Bank of England has laid out fresh measures that would cut UK lenders' capital requirements by another 30 basis points, but warned reductions beyond that point could lead to "financial instability."

In its twice-yearly financial stability update, the BOE announced the final details of its bank capital framework review, weeks after Chancellor John Healey called on officials to go further after a hike in the government's bank tax angered lenders.
 
 
Lobby group UK Finance had proposed radical reform of the domestic lending charges, arguing for changes that would release £250 billion even if half the capital freed up was returned to shareholders, with Lloyds, NatWest, HSBC and Barclays benefiting most.
 
 
Instead, the BOE is opting for more modest changes that will free up around £100 billion of lending capacity by changing add-ons known as Pillar 2A. The benefits will vary by bank, with domestically focused lenders enjoying a 60 basis point reduction in Tier 1 requirements.
 
 
The BOE will consult on the detail early next year, when it has also promised to consult on changes already announced to the leverage ratio, a cap on banks' total borrowings.
 
 
 
 
 
Officials said that, together, those changes will bring system wide capital requirements to a level "consistent with" the 100 basis points cut to benchmark capital announced by the BOE almost a year ago.
 
 
Banks have grown impatient for reductions to their actual requirements to the new 13% Tier 1 level, as deregulation gathered pace across the Atlantic, benefiting Wall Street rivals.
 
 
Still, the BOE moved to rein in hopes of further reprieves. Reductions in capital requirements beyond those set out by the BOE on Thursday "would put long-term economic growth at risk by undermining the banking system's ability to absorb rather than amplify shocks," according to the minutes of the FPC meeting.
 
 
"Materially lower UK capital requirements would also risk undermining international standards intended to promote financial stability and a level playing field."
 
 
Banks have argued that the easiest way to lower actual capital ratios is by reducing the neutral level of the countercyclical buffer from its current setting of 2%. Regulators rejected this.

Officials suggested easing the rules even as the BOE warned of escalating risks from "increasingly complex, circular and opaque" AI financing. "Alongside this, the higher interest rate and cost environment is eroding financial headroom for some households and adding to sovereign debt sustainability pressures. Taken together, these developments heighten the risk that multiple vulnerabilities crystalise simultaneously." Sovereign bond yields remain elevated and markets continue to price in further monetary policy uncertainty.

Last time the Chicago Blackhawks went on a three-game road trip, they were outscored 18-6 combined in three lopsided losses to the Florida Panthers, Tampa Bay Lightning, and Carolina Hurricanes. At the time, the Hawks were 4-13-1 through their first 18 games in the 2026-27 regular season.

Once the team returned home, the situation was so dire that GM Kyle Davidson held a press conference to discuss his sinking rebuild and the Blackhawks biggest issues. Considering the internal preseason expectations for Year 5 of Davidson’s rebuild, the opening month couldn’t have gone much worse.

But since Davidson’s presser on Nov. 11, the Blackhawks are now 5-1-1 in their last seven games, thanks to a perfect 3-0 Pacific Northwest road trip, capped by a 
3-1 win over the Seattle Kraken
 at Climate Pledge Arena on Wednesday.

Sure, the competition was much less strenuous than the Southeastern road trip, but the Blackhawks still deserve credit for the road turnaround and taking care of business against the Kraken, Vancouver Canucks, and Calgary Flames. They outscored those three opponents 13-7 during the road trip.

After failing to win away from the United Center in their first nine attempts (0-8-1), the Blackhawks earned their first three road wins in five days. They pushed through in overtime against Calgary, took down the Canucks in a track meet, and earned a hard-fought, scrappy victory over Seattle.

Wednesday’s win wasn’t the prettiest, with Seattle leading 30-19 in shots on goal and 32-25 in scoring chances, but the Blackhawks defended well overall and didn’t allow many high-danger scoring opportunities. The Kraken’s only odd-man rush, a 2-on-1 from center ice in, was negated with a perfect sliding play from 
Artyom Levshunov
.

Head coach Jeff Blashill didn’t like his team’s puck play for most of the game, but acknowledged they gave themselves a chance to win by playing responsible defense and checking hard for 60 minutes.

“I think our guys are getting rewarded for good, solid play,” Blashill said. “It hasn’t always been perfect, tonight I didn’t think we were our best with the puck, especially through the first two periods. But we defended really hard, and we checked hard.”

Go Ad-Free

Goaltender 
Spencer Knight
, who stopped 29 of 30 shots faced and was credited with 1.98 goals saved above expected, agreed with Blashill’s sentiment. Knight credited the players in front of him for blocking shots time and time again and making the correct defensive reads.

“I thought there was a lot of buy-in today,” Knight said. “A lot of commitment to play a good road game and commitment to play the right way. We stuck with it throughout the game. It was fun for me to see.”

After a 9-2 drubbing to the Carolina Hurricanes earlier this month, Knight, usually a soft-spoken, mild-mannered member of the locker room, called out his teammates for poor defensive effort early in the season, which left the Blackhawks goaltenders in horrific situations too often.

Knight’s frustration was justified, and the rest of the group got the message. The Blackhawks have been much improved defensively over their last seven games, including Wednesday in Seattle. Knight’s comments and tone have changed drastically, as has the spirit around the team.

1. Kantserov 50-goal Pace

With two more tallies on Wednesday, 
Roman Kantserov
 now leads the Blackhawks and all NHL rookies with 15 goals and 20 points in 25 games this season. The 22-year-old winger is currently on pace for 50 goals, a feat that’s only been achieved four times by a first-year player in league history.

Go Ad-Free

Kantserov’s three-game road trip was simply extraordinary, racking up six goals and eight points in the Pacific Northwest. The closer to the Motherland, the better he plays, it seems. He scored in all three games and is tied for sixth in the NHL in goals, ahead of notables like 
Connor McDavid
 and 
Auston Matthews
.

Fittingly, Kantserov has become just the third player in the past 30 years to record 15 goals in their first 25 games, joining fellow Russians Evgeni Malkin and 
Alex Ovechkin
. If Kantserov can impact the Blackhawks rebuild as Malkin and Ovechkin did for their respective teams two decades ago, it’s going to be a fun ride.

Furthermore, Kantserov has scored the most goals by a Blackhawks skater through 25 career games in the NHL’s modern era, with only Bill Mosienko scoring more way back in 1944. He’s also recorded a point in eight straight road games. Kantserov’s nine road goals in November are tied for the third-most by an NHL rookie in a single month.

Go Ad-Free

Most of Kantserov’s goals this season have come from the net front, but he scored in two different ways on Wednesday. First, he ripped a one-timer from a sharp angle into the top corner of the net, scoring the eventual game-winner on the power play. Then, with the Blackhawks clinging to a late 2-1 lead, Kantserov stripped Kraken forward Matthew Wood in the defensive zone and impressively hit the empty net from nearly 200 feet away.

Kantserov’s bulldog mentality and elite goal-scoring traits were on full display during the three-game road trip, and it’s put him in some special conversations. Kantserov hasn’t just been the NHL’s best rookie this season; he’s been the Blackhawks best player.

2. Glendening’s Debut

Go Ad-Free

After joining the team for practice on Tuesday, Luke Glendening’s veteran experience and familiarity with Blashill immediately thrust him into a fourth-line center role in his Blackhawks debut just one day later.

Despite little time to get acclimated, Glendening fit in seamlessly on the Blackhawks penalty kill and in defensive situations against the Kraken. He’s well accustomed to Blashill’s pressure-styled penalty-kill system from their time together in Tampa Bay, and it showed.

Glendening did his job well on Wednesday, winning 11 of 15 faceoffs and helping the Blackhawks go 4-for-4 while shorthanded. The 37-year-old won most of his faceoffs on the penalty kill and selflessly stepped in front of Seattle shooters, recording three blocked shots.

Go Ad-Free

The Blackhawks have been the NHL’s worst faceoff team all season, having won only 42.2 percent of their draws as a team entering Wednesday. Glendening was brought in to help in that department, and he did his part in Seattle. Chicago won 32 of 62 faceoffs against the Kraken, marking one of the few times they’ve been at or above 50 percent in a single game this year.

Glendening also helped the Blackhawks outhit Seattle 29-23, tallying a team-high six hits in 13:11 of ice time. Blashill wants his team to become harder to play against in all three zones, and Glendening’s willingness to be physical is another positive impact he can provide.

So far, so good for Glendening with his new team.

3. Kane’s 
Injured
 Again…

Patrick Kane
 extended his point streak to six games with an assist on Kantserov’s power-play tally, but unfortunately, his night ended shortly after. 

With the Blackhawks ahead 2-0 late in the third period, Kane blocked a shot in the defensive zone and immediately dropped to the ice. The 38-year-old couldn’t put much pressure on his right skate and headed down the tunnel into the locker room. Kane did not return for the final 3:01.

Go Ad-Free

Blashill didn’t have much of an update on Kane during his postgame media session, but it would be crushing for the Blackhawks to lose him for any stretch of time. Since returning from a three-game absence earlier this month, Kane has played his best hockey of the season and been a huge reason for the recent turnaround.

Kane’s playmaking and creativity have been terrific in the last seven games, but he’s also been more involved defensively than usual. That was apparent again in Seattle, as Kane, an unlikely candidate, recorded three blocked shots. Sadly, his final one might have some lasting effects, but that’s the self-sacrificing style Blashill wants from his players.

“It doesn’t come without some risk, and I certainly don’t want a guy like Patrick to miss time or anything like that,” Blashill said. “But it is part of the game now, and it’s part of the commitment to winning that you have to have.”

4. Willing Shot Blockers

Knight referenced the Blackhawks buy-in during his postgame media session, and it was obvious in the final minutes. With the game on the line, the forwards and defensemen jumped in front of every shot attempt and did everything possible to hold onto the one-goal advantage. It didn’t go unnoticed.

“It’s something the coaching staff has emphasized,” Knight said about the players in front of him blocking shots. “I think over the course of [the season], guys have started getting comfortable with just doing that and buying into doing that. For me, it’s pretty motivating to see, just because when that happens, it shows sacrifice, and it makes you want to play more for the guys.”

The Kraken led 68-55 in shot attempts on Wednesday, but only 30 found their way through to Knight. The Blackhawks led 19-7 in blocked shots in Seattle.

Stepping in front of opposing shots is another way Blashill wanted the Blackhawks to become harder to play against, with Knight often seeing too much rubber earlier this season. He’s been pleased with the progression in that aspect.

“Early in the season, we weren’t committed enough [to block shots], and I think we’ve gotten much better at it,” Blashill said. “We’re in lanes, and we’re way more committed to blocking shots. It’s one of those things that’s a little bit contagious.

5. Fourth Line Chips In

Mentioned not too long ago in this postgame column was something along the lines of the Blackhawks, especially now without Bedard, needing more offensive contributions from their bottom-six forward group. Well, they’ve gotten it over the last couple of weeks.

Fourth-line winger 
Cole Smith
 opened the scoring early in the third period with his third goal of the season, and he’s now recorded at least one point in four of the last five games. Glendening also provided a tremendous net-front screen on 
Ethan Del Mastro
‘s point shot, which Smith deflected into Seattle’s net.

Smith, Glendening, and 
Jordan Greenway
 served as the fourth line against the Kraken, and they were an effective trio in their first look together. The Blackhawks led 8-6 in shots on goal, 5-2 in scoring chances, and 1-0 in goals for at even strength with the fourth line on the ice.

They nearly added a second goal later in the third period, as Glendening beat Seattle netminder Joey Daccord, but the puck glanced off the post.

On both sides of the ice, the fourth line was crisp, and that’s a much-welcomed development for the Blackhawks. Glendening’s style seems to be a perfect fit, along with Smith’s pace and Greenway’s heavy 200-foot game.

Zip’s co-founders Rujul Zaparde and Lu Cheng explain why governance makes AI autonomy possible and how procurement can find its flow state
Procurement teams are being asked to do more with AI while keeping tighter control of risk, data and spend.

The challenge is not a shortage of technology. It is knowing how to apply automation without losing the governance that enterprise buying demands.

That tension ran through 
Zip
 Forward 2026 New York, themed “Find Your Flow”.

The company used the week to announce a run of products, and co-founders, 
Rujul Zaparde
 (CEO) and 
Lu Cheng
 (CTO), explained how they fit together.

Zip began by creating the intake and procurement orchestration category. It has since expanded to cover the process end-to-end, from intake all the way to pay.
Rujul Zaparde, Co-Founder & CEO at Zip. Credit: Zip
A week of launches built around orchestration

The announcements included AI Risk Orchestration, new AI data partnerships, an app marketplace, a sourcing Superagent, a PO Superagent and accrual automation.

Rujul says the data partnerships matter because AI is only as strong as what it can draw on. Zip can now enrich data natively for the first time, with more partners to follow.

“We announced new AI data partnerships because ultimately the AI that’s helping you is only as good as the model, and the data, that the AI has access to,” he says.
AI is incredibly powerful, but it has to live in a governed framework
Rujul Zaparde, Co-Founder & CEO at Zip
Marketplace addresses a core part of orchestration, which is connecting to more of the applications in an enterprise stack. The Superagents and accrual automation take on execution.

The aim, Rujul explains, is to free procurement and sourcing teams from the small tasks that interrupt their work: “We’re at a point where we can allow our incredible procurement and sourcing champions to actually find flow state and let AI do a lot of the end-to-end execution for all these smaller tasks that cause so much day-to-day friction.”
Rujul says AI can help procurement and sourcing teams find flow state. Credit: Zip
Scale that reflects the pain

Rujul says Zip’s customers have now orchestrated more than US$1.2tn in spend on the platform. For him, that figure shows how much difficulty existed before.

“To date, our customers have processed – orchestrated, rather – over US$1.2tn on Zip. It just speaks to how much pain there really was in this space before Zip. It’s maybe the most complex process inside of any enterprise, and for the first time we can automate this work with AI.”

Fragmentation, not a lack of tools

From the CTO’s seat, Lu sees the biggest obstacle to flow as fragmentation rather than tooling.

Organisations already own systems for security, compliance and governance, but those systems rarely connect: “The obstacle we see is not a lack of tools, because everyone has their own tools for security, compliance and governance. The challenge is really fragmentation. There’s no single thread through the entire end-to-end purchasing process.”

Zip’s answer is to bring that context together. AI Risk Orchestration uses the information in a purchasing decision to trigger the right risk assessments, then applies them consistently across the process.
Lu Cheng, Co-Founder & CTO at Zip. Credit: Zip
“With AI risk orchestration, we’re able to take all of the context in the purchasing decision and use it to kick off the right types of risk assessments,” Lu adds. 

“Ultimately, we apply that consistently through the entire end-to-end process, such that the entire context is all inside Zip.”

Lu believes the same thinking applies to contract orchestration: “With products like risk orchestration and contract orchestration, it’s truly about being one front door, and being able to orchestrate the entire process and help from directly inside it.”
Lu says procurement’s biggest obstacle is fragmentation, not a lack of tools. Credit: Zip
Governance makes autonomy possible

Both founders return to governance as the condition for automation. Rujul argues that procurement, finance and accounting carry more downside risk than most functions, which sets a higher bar for AI: “An LLM can’t be 
probably
 correct, right? For example, it’s got to be 
certainly
 correct.”

Precision matters most where transactions are involved. A purchase request that touches money cannot tolerate even a small error.

“AI is incredibly powerful, but it has to live in a governed framework,” Rujul says.
The obstacle we see is not a lack of tools, because everyone has their own tools for security, compliance and governance. The challenge is really fragmentation
Lu Cheng, Co-Founder & CTO at Zip
Lu breaks governance into three parts. The first is a single record of every decision made on the platform. The second is clear auditability. The third is the right controls.

“With governance, what we find to be critical is, number one, having a single through-line of every decision being made inside the platform, bringing all of the context into one place. The second is really clear auditability throughout the process. And the last piece is making sure that the right controls are in place as well.”
Rujul says AI is powerful but must live inside a governed framework. Credit: Zip
A function elevated rather than replaced

As AI takes on more execution, Rujul expects procurement’s standing to rise, as time freed from routine work can go into more strategic areas of focus.

“Teams can spend more time doing much more strategic work. Procurement and sourcing functions that adopt AI, that adopt orchestration like Zip, will be able to be much more strategic and much more proactive.”

Lu points to the scale of influence procurement already has. The function manages a large share of company spend, which makes the return on better decisions substantial.
No one ever feels ready to make a change, and the reality is that’s true for really every enterprise
Rujul Zaparde, Co-Founder & CEO at Zip
“Procurement is one of the few areas in the business where there’s a tremendous amount of ROI around the strategic spend that you’re doing. In many industries, procurement helps manage and optimise 30% to 40% of spend, and in some industries upwards of 60% of the spend goes through procurement.”

He sees AI’s next contribution in making sense of that data.

“The second part is leveraging AI to help provide better guidance and make sense of the data, the spend data that’s happening throughout your transactions.”
Lu highlights in some industries, 60% of spend goes through procurement. Credit: Zip
Starting before you feel ready

Asked what leaders should take from Forward, Rujul says waiting for the perfect moment is a trap. Few enterprises ever feel prepared for change.

“No one ever feels ready to make a change, and the reality is that’s true for really every enterprise.”
AI, he adds, lowers the barrier to entry. Teams no longer need tidy data or fully mapped processes before they begin.

“If you start now, and especially with AI, the bar is lower in terms of the level of structure and organisation you have to have around your data and even your processes. You can be much more nimble today than you could even just two or three years ago. So there’s no better time to get started than now.”

Kaoko Basin Geology and Background
 
 
The Kaoko Basin, in north-western Namibia, forms part of the Neoproterozoic Damara Orogenic Belt and is interpreted as an extension of the Central African Copperbelt, one of the world's foremost sediment-hosted copper-silver provinces. Copper mineralisation is typically controlled by redox boundaries at the contact between reduced, carbonaceous sedimentary units and oxidised red-bed sequences. At the Company's Omatapati and Otjozongombe prospects, it occurs primarily as disseminated sulphides and oxides, broadly stratabound within dolostones and calcareous siltstones of the Ombombo Formation, with local remobilisation into cross-cutting quartz veins.

Serval holds a four-licence, 789 km² package within this prospective trend. EPL 7081 is Serval's priority licence within the Kaoko Basin, hosting some of the Company's key targets, being the Omatapati, Otjozongombe West and East, and Horseshoe prospects. IP Survey at Otjozongombe West - Completed
 
 
The IP survey at the Otjozongombe West prospect, which commenced on 29 October 2026, has been completed. The survey was designed as an orientation study, to establish the geophysical response (chargeability and resistivity) of the known Cu-Ag mineralisation. This IP survey identified high-priority targets, with resistivity and chargeability anomalies in areas where mineralisation was confirmed by previous drilling. The survey will be extended along strike in a systematic manner to delineate the mineralisation ahead of drilling.

IP Survey Technical Parameters
 
 
Three profiles of approximately 950 m each were surveyed using a modified high-resolution pole-dipole array with a 10 m receiver electrode spacing, providing a depth of investigation of approximately 250 - 300 m.
 
 
The three IP depth profiles display comparable resistivity and chargeability anomaly patterns with a sub horizontal, weak resistivity layer near surfaces underlain by a high resistive and coincident chargeability anomaly at depth. The near surface low resistivity could be attributed to weathered schist, sediments or shale while the highly resistive and chargeable anomaly at depth occurs within dolomite, proximal to the dolomite-quartzite contact.
 
 
The low resistivity anomaly east of the dolomite-quartzite contact is interpreted as a fault zone and correlates with a chargeable anomaly. This zone could also represent a silt/shale unit with disseminated sulphides, potentially hosting copper mineralisation.
 
 
 
 
 
Corporate - Reduction of Company Debt
 
 
The Board has reviewed the Company's cash position to minimise dilution whilst maintaining a fully funded drill programme and Serval has subsequently repaid £1 million of the total £2 million deferred consideration owed to KCLI, with regards to the purchase of KCL (the private company acquired by Serval that held 100% ownership of the licences in Namibia and Botswana). This serves to halve the outstanding deferred consideration and will thereby significantly reduce interest repayments.

There was so much public acrimony in the course of this past summer’s Federov-Syrskyi imbroglio which in the end cost both men their high profile positions. It was all rather unfortunate. But both men, like their extraordinary adaptive country, have moved on and are already doing their best to be of service to their people and their country. Former Defense Minister Federov decided to go into the private sector and help his country from that vantage. General Syrskyi, the former Commander in Chief of Ukraine’s Armed Forces for his part made the unusual decision to stay on in the army in an advisory role and help in anyway he would be called upon to contribute. Good on both gentlemen. And good for Ukraine. No talent should be allowed to go to waste at this very critical moment in Ukraine’s history. All hands on deck.

Fast forward a few months. Mr. Federov initially had a bit of a rocky public relations with some of his first moves out of government but he has now seemingly stepped out of the harsh glare of the public spotlight and is reportedly helming several Ukrainian private sector cooperative efforts with international partners to develop Ukraine’s next generation of military automation.

He has launched several defense technology ventures primarily focusing on 
artificial intelligence
, 
robotics
, and 
drone warfare
. ⁠Principal among these ventures is 
Mission Defense Partners
, a newly established defense tech company he co-founded with 
Pavlo Yelizarov
 of Lasar’s Group. Some here may remember that the former Ukrainian Army colonel (and drone maestro) exited the army in protest of Federov’s removal as Defense Minister. ⁠ The venture, registered in October 2026 as a United States entity (under Delaware state law) aims to develop 
low-cost anti-drone missiles
, 
interceptor drones
, and 
AI-enabled guidance systems
. 
Alex Karp
, CEO of Palantir, serves as the company’s first major investor, with the goal of commercializing Ukraine’s wartime technological experience for allied nations:

Mission Defense: an early look at Mykhailo Fedorov’s defence tech enterprise

Mission Defense: an early look at Mykhailo Fedorov’s defence tech enterprise

We all had some inkling of what Mr. Federov’s plans were. And as the foregoing shows, he has been assiduously all about that. But what of the general? After having reached the pinnacle of his life-long career as the Commander in Chief of all the Armed Forces of Ukraine, what else is there for a soldier to do in the army? Apparently quite a lot. Oleksandr Syrskyi doesn’t have the easy-going wise-cracking avuncular “teddy bear” disposition which earned his predecessor, General Valerii Zaluzhnyi, the call sign of “
Yoda
” and appears to be serving him quite well in his new career as a diplomat. Syrskyi’s personality is all about the warrior ethos as attested by his call sign of “
Bars
“(Leopard in both Russian and Ukrainian). As he once said of himself … “the frontline is my life”.

Syrskyi’s 
bona fides
 as a warrior was never in question. But perhaps it is this admitted penchant for being constantly at the front line which also earned him the reputation of being a “micro-manager” who was seen at times as getting in the way of things in a fast moving war which required subordinate commanders in the field to make adjustments and decisions on the fly. I am sure we will learn more about this when the post-war recounting begins and personal memoirs come pouring out from the principals now engaged on the line of fire. It is always thus with every consequential war.

Word came recently that Syrskyi, too is involved in the development of what some are calling Ukraine’s emerging “Robot Army”, thereby continuing the process that he had championed earlier on as CinC … as far back as 2024:

Introduction of Innovative UAVs, Robotic Platforms into Ukrainian Forces Is Top Priority – CinC Syrskii

https://en.defence-ua.com/news/introduction_of_innovative_uavs_robotic_platforms_into_ukrainian_forces_is_top_priority_cinc_syrskii-11159.html

And just last month he offered these insights from his front row seat on current ongoing robotization of the Ukrainian armed forces:

Alexander Syrsky Says Humanoid Combat Robots Are Already Being Tested

Former Ukrainian commander Alexander Syrsky says humanoid combat robots are already being tested and could change battlefield tactics as military AI advances.

Humanoid robots could soon become active participants in military combat, according to former commander-in-chief of the Ukrainian Armed Forces Alexander Syrsky. 
He revealed that the technology has already moved beyond research, with testing now underway.
[emphasis mine]

https://voennoedelo.com/en/posts/id20939-humanoid-combat-robots-could-reshape-war-syrsky-says

But that’s not all. Just like Mr. Federov, General Syrskyi’s new work within the army has taken on an internationalist aspect … helping other frontline European countries like Norway improve their defenses against Russia:

Ukraine’s former Commander-in-Chief helps Norway improve air defences along the border with Russia

General Oleksandr Syrskyi has this week joined Norway’s Chief of Defence, Eirik Kristoffersen, on a covert mission to study the terrain along the 198-kilometre land border with Russia, with the aim of strengthening air defences against incoming drones and missiles.

https://www.thebarentsobserver.com/security/ukraines-former-commanderinchief-helps-norway-improve-air-defences-along-the-border-with-russia/462650

And of course the matter of one of Ukraine’s most spectacular exploits that occurred on his watch came up … 
“Operation Spiderweb”
:

Under Syrskyi’s leadership, Ukraine’s special forces have attacked Olenya Air Base on several occasions, with the first strikes taking place in 
July
 and 
August
 2024. The most successful and devastating attack came on 1 June 2025, when 
a swarm of drones
 launched from a civilian lorry destroyed four Tu-95MS bombers and one An-12 military transport aircraft.

Can we expect Ukraine to attack Olenya Air Base again?

“Yes, of course,” General Syrskyi said. “We will continue to do our utmost to maximally degrade Russia’s capabilities to deliver firepower against Ukrainian territory, people and infrastructure, using every means at our disposal.”

Sirskyi, as I have alluded to above does not seem to not have the easy affabile ways of his predecessor or quite possibly the same interlocutory savoir-faire often attributed to Federov. But it would appear he has that special skill which enables him to easily interact and cooperate with other chiefs. As an avid viewer of his official Facebook page while he was the CinC, it was clear to me that he was quite at home with fellow army chiefs from other countries. I dare say he had at least one or two of those meetings every month … in person or virtual meetings/video conferences …  demonstrating his diplomatic chops of a different sort, as it now appears.

It is to President Zelenskyy’s credit that he saw in both Federov and Sirskyi talents that could continue to accrue to the benefit of Ukraine and thereby sought to have both men stay on board in other capacities. It seems, at least to this point, that both men have landed on fertile soil … Federov rolling on as the young visionary tech entrepreneur and General Syrskyi in a new role where he feels quite at home and able to meaningfully contribute while still wearing the uniform of the country where he was born and raised[in Kharkiv] and dedicated himself to, thereby enduring the painful severance of his ties to his parents, siblings and his ancestral Russia.

Some warriors are so fated. My personal favorite general from the American Civil War … without a doubt the best field commander on both sides, 
Major General George Henry Thomas, Commander of the Union Army of the Cumberland
, beloved by his men who affectionately called him “Pap” even though he was younger than some of them. At the start of the war, his family in Virginia disowned him for keeping his sworn oath to the United States … unlike many of his southern-born contemporaries. Acknowledged for his skills on 
active defense
(hello Syrskyi !), he won renown as “
The Rock of Chicamauga
” for stepping in to rally the Union line and saving it from the disastrous command errors of General 
William S. Rosecrans
 at the Battle of Chicamauga.

Thomas would go on to also earn the sobriquet of “
The Hammer of Nashville
” and become the only commander on both sides to completely shatter two opposing armies in the field … completely destroying confederate 
General John Bell Hood’s Army of the Tennessee
 at the Battle of Nashville. In the preceding Battle at Franklin, Tennessee which Thomas also won, a record 6 high ranking confederate generals died. Thomas never lost a battle in which he commanded the field. Earlier on in 1862 he did the same to the confederates at the 
Battle of Mill Springs 
in which 
Brigadier General Felix K. Zollicoffer
, 
was killed, making him the first confederate general killed in the western theater.

Thomas was that good. And incredibly effective. But he had no patience for “Army Politics” and didn’t get along with his boss and later POTUS, 
General Ulysses S. Grant
, who accused Thomas of having “the slows” … for his deliberative but highly successful tactical approach to battle planning in order to conserve the lives of his men. It is said that Grant was on his way to relieve Thomas of command when he got the news about the spectacular win at Nashville. Sadly, Thomas did not live long after the war to toot his own horn and write his memoirs like many of his contemporaries did. He was 53.

General Oleksandr Syrskyi is now 61 and likely has a few more years of active service in him. Former Minister Federov, to whom he is now inextricably linked in history, is a young 35. Both men happily seem to have found new ways to continue contributing to VICTORY. May providence grant both men more years and guide their efforts towards resounding success on behalf of their beloved ADAPTIVE INDOMITABLE UKRAINE.

SLAVA UKRAINI !  PEREMOHA(Victory) !!

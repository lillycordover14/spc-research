Coweta County 911/EMA will use the Coweta County Sheriff's Office's Flock cameras after approval from the Board of Commissioners last week.
The intergovernmental agreement between the two agencies allows 911/EMA to collect and access captured license plate data for law enforcement purposes, which include monitoring hits for missing persons, warrants, stolen vehicles and Amber Alerts.
According to county documents, the agreement is narrowly tailored for 911/EMA to assist CCSO. Both agencies have standard operating procedures for training, permission, access to and use of the license plate data. Access to the data is limited to those law enforcement purposes.
The agreement states 911 employees will access license plate data only as approved by CCSO and will comply with CCSO's policies regarding use of the data.
911/EMA will also comply with any CCSO audit or compliance request and provide documentation, according to the agreement. 911 employees will also complete training designated by CCSO for use of the license plate readers.
Cathy Wickey, communications manager for the county, said the audits will be performed monthly to ensure compliance.
911/EMA communications officers will use the system to notify law enforcement of alerts.
Communications officers who are assigned to work law enforcement radios on their shifts will have access to the license plate data, Wickey said.

If CCSO allows other law enforcement agencies to access its Flock cameras, those alerts will also be provided to 911/EMA, according to Wickey.
Earlier this year, three employees resigned from CCSO for inappropriate use of the agency's Flock system.
CCSO has the largest Flock system in the county, with 62 Flock cameras.
The license plate readers, or LPRs, in CCSO's system may be used only by officers who have been properly trained on the systems. In a previous Newnan Times-Herald article, Jacob Herbert, CCSO public information officer, said approved Flock users include sworn personnel and civilian employees whose use of an LPR is vital to their job.
According to CCSO's policy, LPRs are for official agency purposes only. Users are required to confirm that a vehicle's plate number matches the one from the Flock notification.
Law enforcement officials can share data from LPRs for legitimate law enforcement purposes. When data is shared outside of CCSO, it is documented in a secondary dissemination log.
According to its policy, CCSO retains data from LPRs for two years.

More than 1,000 leaders gathered in New York as Zip unveiled its biggest launches yet and showed how AI can run inside a governed enterprise
At the end of September, 
Zip
 Forward brought together more than 1,000 leaders in procurement, finance and more at The Glasshouse in New York.
The fourth outing of the flagship event for the leading agentic procurement orchestration platform was also the biggest gathering of the Zip community yet.
Members from Shopify, T-Mobile, Verizon, Block and Gusto, as well as hundreds of other companies, were in the room to connect on the next era of AI and procurement potential.
Between main sessions, attendees connected with Zip product leaders and partners inside the Innovation Hub and booked one-on-one consultations with the solutions team at the Genius Bar, all alongside the iconic Hudson River.
Water flowing through New York was central to the event’s theme – flow. Keynote speaker Michael Strahan shared insights on entering a flow state and the power of teamwork in driving peak performance.
Attendees explored the next era of AI and procurement at Zip Forward
Box’s Linda Chuan encouraged the audience to “be like water”, while a panel of CPOs dedicated 25 minutes to discussing flow and identifying which points of friction provide genuine value.
Above all, the gathering offered everyone a shared space to exchange insights, discover new opportunities and strengthen professional connections.
Why context gives Zip its edge
Zip co-founders 
Rujul Zaparde
 and 
Lu Cheng
 opened day one by looking back over the past year, in which Zip shipped more than 900 new features. Each one runs inside a governed AI platform, with the approvals and audit trails that auditors and regulators expect to see.
Lu then showed how Zip’s AI agents stack up against general-purpose AI. In head-to-head tests on procurement tasks such as invoice coding, Zip’s agents and subagents outperformed frontier LLMs on the key metrics that matter most to procurement work.
Lu put the result down to the context layer. A frontier model sees an invoice; a Zip agent sees the invoice alongside the PO, the receipt, the supplier’s history, the company’s coding rules and every other element of the purchase, because all of it already runs through Zip.
Zip’s AI agents outperformed frontier LLMs on key procurement tasks
The biggest launches yet
The announcements that followed put more of this context to work. Customers from CIBC, Visa and Unifi Aviation joined Zip on stage for its largest set of product announcements to date.
AI Risk Orchestration
 brings full third-party risk management into the same platform where purchasing decisions are made. It triages incoming requests, pre-fills questionnaires with supplier data already in Zip, and scores each supplier against the customer’s own risk framework.
 
Accrual Automation
 generates month-end accruals from the POs, receipts and contracts already in Zip, then prepares journal entries for the ERP. A person approves every entry, and every calculation carries a full audit trail.
 
The Sourcing Superagent
 handles the coordination and analysis behind a sourcing event, leaving buyers to make the decisions. 
The PO Superagent
 recommends the next step on orders that need attention, such as closing a PO or submitting a change order, and requesters act on it with one click.
 
Marketplace
 launched with more than 20 integration templates spanning ERP, P2P, CLM, GRC and more. Admins connect them through an AI-guided setup wizard and apply upgrades in one click.
 
New AI data partners
 NPI, SpendHound, Tropic, Beroe, Brightfield and TEEM give procurement teams and Superagents external context on every purchase and negotiation across the Zip experience.
Zip unveiled its largest set of product announcements to date
Governing AI where the stakes are highest
Across two days of sessions, attendees explored how to run AI inside a governed enterprise. The central argument was that AI delivers value when it sits within the controls, data and workflows a business already runs on.
Day one, on the Peak Stage, opened with regulation as a design principle. CIBC’s Edina Cavalli explained how the bank treats its regulatory requirements as a constraint that makes its AI programme stronger.
Shopify CFO Jeff Hoffmeister described how the CFO role changes once a company goes AI-first, and urged leaders to decide where their organisation will be in two or three years and get there first.
A CPO panel with Invesco, Databricks and Humana looked at which friction protects the business, such as governance and supplier reviews, and which can be removed with technology.
Zip Forward highlighted governance as central to enterprise AI value
IDC’s Patrick Reymann closed the strategy strand by arguing that the companies that move fast on AI will build the operating model that decides competitive advantage for the next decade.
Former NFL star Michael then led a conversation on flow state, and the day finished with the first-ever Zippy Awards for customers and community members.
From big ideas to proving the return
Day two turned to the practical side: how to budget for AI and prove a return. CJ Gustafson tackled the problem of AI spend spreading across the P&L rather than sitting in one line item.
A controllers’ panel with Duolingo, Onebrief and Unifi Aviation covered how finance teams build trust in AI output. Unifi’s Mark Hlavek said his team plans to double next year, and all three controllers named accruals as the area they most want AI to improve.
Zip’s 
State of AI in Spend 2026
 report, a survey of 1,050 leaders, found that 62% use AI multiple times a day. The 17% furthest into transformation report measurable ROI, and 49% of those “Builders” expect their teams to grow.
Other sessions covered career paths in procurement with Pinterest, Lennar and Zillow, customer stories from Block, Gusto and Box, and a build-versus-buy discussion with PwC.
Zip found 62% of leaders use AI multiple times a day

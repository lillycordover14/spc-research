Actress Jennifer Garner returned to her home state of West Virginia to help deliver food to families in need, joining volunteers with Mountaineer Food Bank and Save the Children for a Thanksgiving-season distribution in Clay County.

Jennifer Garner via Facebook 

CLAY, W.Va. — Actress Jennifer Garner returned to her home state of West Virginia to help deliver food to families in need, joining volunteers with Mountaineer Food Bank and Save the Children for a Thanksgiving-season distribution in Clay County.
Garner, who grew up in Charleston and graduated from George Washington High School, shared photos and a message about the volunteer effort on social media Wednesday, expressing gratitude to the organizations, volunteers and families involved.

"Mountaineer Food Bank works tirelessly every single day to fight hunger across all of West Virginia," Garner wrote. "Lucky me to get to see their work in action and volunteer with Save the Children US who is striving to do more together to help fight food insecurity in WV."
The distribution took place last weekend in the parking lot of Clay County High School, where organizers expected to assist approximately 350 families ahead of Thanksgiving.
In a video shared alongside her post, Garner described the challenges facing residents in the area, noting that Clay County lacks a grocery store and that many residents have limited access to fresh food.

The drive-through distribution included fresh produce such as apples, carrots, potatoes and blackberries, along with ham, spaghetti, sauce, canned corn and other groceries.
Garner also helped place food into vehicles as families moved through the distribution line.
The event was part of Mountaineer Food Bank's Mobile Pantry program, which brings food directly to communities where grocery stores, food pantries and other sources of fresh food may be difficult to access.

Mountaineer Food Bank, based in Gassaway, is West Virginia's largest emergency food provider and a member of the Feeding America network. The organization serves 48 of the state's 55 counties, distributing more than 19 million meals annually through a network of hundreds of community partners, including food pantries, churches and nonprofit organizations.
The need remains significant. Mountaineer Food Bank estimates that more than 200,000 West Virginians experience food insecurity, meaning they lack consistent access to enough nutritious food.
The organization also works with Save the Children on its Extra Mile program, which is designed to help food-insecure children and their families in rural communities. The initiative provides food deliveries to families who might otherwise face transportation difficulties or other barriers to accessing assistance. Mountaineer Food Bank and Save the Children have partnered on the program, which uses schools and community partners to help coordinate deliveries.

Garner has maintained a longstanding relationship with Save the Children, serving as an artist ambassador for the organization for more than 15 years. She joined its Board of Trustees in 2014 and has helped bring the organization's early childhood education and literacy programs to rural communities, including in West Virginia.
Her latest volunteer appearance follows a November 2025 visit to Clay and Fayette counties, when she helped package meals for Mountaineer Food Bank's Extra Mile program at Clay County High School, participated in a literacy lesson at Clay Elementary School and visited New River Primary School in Fayette County.
Garner, known for her roles in television's "Alias" and films including "13 Going on 30," "Juno" and "Dallas Buyers Club," was raised in Charleston and graduated from George Washington High School in 1990.

She closed her Thanksgiving message by thanking the food bank's staff, volunteers and the families who participated in the distribution.
"Happy Thanksgiving to the team working hard behind the scenes, the incredible volunteers and families I met that day, and to you and yours!" she wrote.

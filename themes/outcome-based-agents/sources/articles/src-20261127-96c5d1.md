New York-based insurtech Sixfold is expanding its AI underwriting business across European insurance markets, extending its operations beyond traditional insurance carriers into managing general agents (MGAs) and delegated authority underwriting.

Founded in 2023, the company has raised approximately $50 mn to develop its underwriting technology and expand internationally. Its disclosed insurance customers include Zurich North America, Guardian, New York Life, Generali Global Corporate & Commercial, AXIS and Skyward Specialty.

Sixfold has raised approximately $50 mn since its founding in 2023, including a $30 mn Series B led by Brewer Lane in January 2026.

The AI underwriting platform has processed more than 1 mn insurance submissions across over 40 lines of business.

European expansion includes a partnership with Switzerland’s Adnovum and the deployment of its technology at German MGA exxis.

The company’s European expansion includes commercial insurance applications, technology distribution partnerships and AI products designed specifically for life and health insurance underwriting.

Sixfold funding and estimated valuation

Sixfold announced a $30 mn Series B funding round in January 2026, led by Brewer Lane. The investment followed a $15 mn Series A in 2024 led by Salesforce Ventures, which supported the company’s European expansion.

Sixfold had raised $52 mn in total funding, with an estimated valuation of $200-375 mn, according to NewMarketPitch

The valuation range is based on the company’s latest funding round and carries a low confidence rating. Sixfold has not disclosed a confirmed valuation.

The AI underwriting software market includes several venture-backed competitors with higher estimated or disclosed valuations.

Kalepa
, which develops AI software for commercial insurance underwriting, risk selection and pricing, announced a $470 mn valuation alongside a $75 mn Series B in November 2026.

Federato
‘s valuation is estimated at between $667 mn and $1.2 bn following its $100 mn Series D funding round in November 2025.

These figures place Sixfold among the smaller venture-backed underwriting software providers by estimated valuation, although differences in financing dates and valuation methodologies limit direct comparisons.

European insurance and MGA expansion

In May 2026, Sixfold announced a partnership with Adnovum, a Swiss technology and consulting company specialising in secure digital transformation for the insurance sector. The agreement expands the distribution of Sixfold’s underwriting technology to European insurers.

In November 2026, exxis, the underwriting agency of Germany’s Ecclesia Group, implemented Sixfold’s platform across cyber, property and accident insurance for multiple capacity providers. The deployment was completed within two months.

exxis became Sixfold’s first publicly disclosed German MGA customer, extending the technology provider’s presence beyond conventional insurance carriers.

The MGA distribution channel presents distinct technical requirements. Underwriting software must distinguish between the risk appetites and underwriting guidelines of individual carriers providing delegated capacity across multiple insurance lines.

According to Howden Re, European MGAs generated approximately $23 bn in gross written premiums in 2025, an increase of around 15% compared with 7% growth in the US.

Europe’s MGA market remains approximately one-fifth the size of its US counterpart, despite its broader property and casualty insurance market being only 40% smaller. The difference provides scope for further expansion of delegated authority underwriting and associated technology services.

AI underwriting performance and productivity

According to company figures reported by FinanceX Magazine, Sixfold has processed more than 1 mn insurance submissions across over 40 lines of business.

The platform automates elements of submission preparation, document analysis and risk assessment, applying carrier-specific underwriting guidelines to individual insurance applications.

Sixfold reports the following operational improvements among its customers:

50% reduction in underwriter review time.

45% reduction in the time required for new underwriters to reach full caseload capacity.

30% increase in gross written premiums per underwriter.

These metrics are company-reported customer results that have not been independently audited. They should not be interpreted as guaranteed performance improvements across different insurers, distribution models or insurance portfolios.

The exxis implementation provides evidence of operational adoption within a multi-carrier MGA, although the agency has not disclosed quantitative improvements in underwriting profitability, operating costs or risk selection.

AI underwriting for life and health insurance

Sixfold launched its AI Underwriter for Life & Health in September 2026, extending its technology into insurance segments with more demanding regulatory requirements in Europe. The product uses a governed AI agent to evaluate medical and financial evidence against carrier-specific underwriting guidelines.

Under Annex III of the EU AI Act, artificial intelligence systems used for risk assessment and pricing involving individuals in life and health insurance are classified as high-risk applications.

Relevant regulatory requirements include quality management systems, technical documentation, bias assessment and human oversight.

Commercial insurance applications generally fall outside this specific high-risk classification, creating different compliance requirements for technology providers operating across both segments.

European insurers and MGAs within the scope of DORA must also maintain appropriate oversight of third-party technology providers, including their data hosting arrangements, operational resilience and governance procedures.

For Sixfold, supporting commercial, life and health insurance requires accommodating different regulatory obligations alongside the underwriting requirements of individual insurance carriers.

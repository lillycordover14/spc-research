Bitcoin
’s chart is close to forming one of technical analysis’ most ominous-sounding patterns. The 50-day simple moving average is drifting toward the 200-day, and a crossover would produce what traders call a death cross.

It hasn’t happened yet on the simple averages. But the gap is narrowing, Bitcoin trades far below both lines, and several related indicators have already flipped bearish.

What the moving averages are saying

A moving average smooths out daily noise by averaging prices over a fixed window. The 50-day captures the recent mood. The 200-day reflects the longer-run baseline.

When the short-term line falls under the long-term one, chartists read it as a sign the trend has turned lower, because recent prices have become weaker than the market’s longer-term norm. The reverse, where the 50-day climbs over the 200-day, is a golden cross, which analysts treat as confirmation of an uptrend.

Bitcoin’s most recent golden cross came on September 8, 2026, when the price was approximately $78,456. The 50-day SMA is still slightly above the 200-day SMA, so technically that bullish signal remains on the books.

On November 26, 2026, Bitcoin traded around $60,577, roughly 15.8% below its 200-day SMA of $71,977.

In other words, the price has already fallen well beneath both averages. The averages are catching up to what the market did weeks ago.

The faster signals already flipped

Not every version of the indicator is waiting around. The exponential moving average gives more weight to recent prices, so it reacts faster than the simple version.

As of late November 2026, the 50-day EMA stood at approximately $71,178. That’s below the 200-day EMA of about $74,447, which means the EMA-based death cross is already in place.

Other crossovers have joined in. The 21-day SMA slipped under the 200-day SMA in mid-November, and a death cross between the 50-day and 100-day averages was flagged as a risk by the end of the month.

How Bitcoin got here

Bitcoin hit an all-time high of approximately $126,200 in October 2025.

By late November 2026, it was trading in a range of $60,000 to $62,000, less than half of its peak. The previous death cross occurred on November 16, 2025, shortly after the October peak.

Institutional flows have added pressure. US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw outflows of roughly $5.39 billion in November 2026, at the same time as the sharp leg lower in price.

Does a death cross actually predict anything

The name sounds worse than the signal is. Moving averages are lagging indicators by design, so a crossover confirms weakness that has already occurred. It doesn’t forecast fresh damage.

Returns 90 days to a year after past crossovers have often been positive, especially when the cross lined up with a market cycle low.

What this means for traders and investors

The near-term setup leans cautious. Price is below every major average and the EMA cross is confirmed, while ETF money has been leaving in size.

The levels to watch are concrete. A support zone sits between $55,000 and $58,500, below the current trading range.

On the upside, Bitcoin would need to climb back toward the 200-day SMA near $71,977 before the long-term trend picture improves in any meaningful way.

ETF flows matter too. November’s roughly $5.39 billion in outflows was a major part of the decline, so a slowdown or reversal there would mean more to many investors than any line on a chart.

Which reading proves right will likely depend less on the crossover itself and more on whether institutional demand returns and whether the $55,000 to $58,500 zone holds.

                        Iran's Appliance Makers Say Sea Freight Costs Have Risen Sixfold
                    

November 27, 2026

                                                IranWire
                                            

                                            1 min read
                                        

The secretary of Iran's home appliance manufacturers' association said Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026, that the cost of shipping raw materials and parts by sea has risen to more than six times what it was before the start of the 40-day war. Nasrin Ojaghi told the ISNA news agency that the increase has disrupted the supply of raw materials and the operations of production units.

Ojaghi said the freight cost of a 20-foot container, which was between $1,000 and $2,200 before the war, has risen to between $6,200 and $9,300. She said freight costs for some shipments have reached $21,000.

She also reported a 90% to 100% increase in insurance costs for shipments and said the disruption in the supply of raw materials and parts has forced production units to operate below capacity.

The United States resumed its naval blockade of Iran's ports on July 14. First Vice President Mohammadreza Aref has said the government is not concerned about the blockade, citing an increase in the entry and clearance of goods through Iran's non-southern borders.

Ojaghi also said domestic polymer prices on the open market rose by as much as 370% between February and November 2026. According to the association's figures, Iran's home appliance production fell from 20 million units in 2024 to 18 million in 2025.

In October, Hossein Shahrestani, deputy head of the home appliance union, reported that prices for these goods had risen by about 80% since the start of the U.S. and Israeli war with Iran and said people could no longer afford them.

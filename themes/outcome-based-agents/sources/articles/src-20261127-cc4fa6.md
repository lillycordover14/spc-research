With the end of November coming in hot, it’s looking like some decent snow will be on the way to the heart of Colorado 
ski country
 over upcoming days, offering a change of pace in terms of what snowfall has taken place thus far this season.

With the brunt of the incoming snow expected to hit the high country on Saturday, the most likely snowfall scenario indicates that Northern Colorado will be favored to get the deepest accumulation, though the central mountains could get a few inches, as well. In the National Weather Service’s 80-percent-chance scenario, the Park Range near Steamboat Springs gets about 12 to 18 inches, with around eight to 12 on peaks north of Granby. The central Colorado portion of the I-70 corridor gets about four to eight with about seven inches expected in the area of the Eisenhower–Johnson Memorial Tunnel Complex.

The National Weather Service high-end snowfall scenario, however, would mean deeper totals and snow being more widespread, though Southwest Colorado still gets mostly missed. While this scenario only comes with a 10-percent chance, it could mean snow in the range of 18 to 24 inches across many Northern Colorado peaks with about eight to 18 inches being fairly widespread in the central mountains. In this scenario, a foot of snow falls relatively quickly on Saturday in the area of the Eisenhower–Johnson tunnels.

Per the 
OpenSnow resort-by-resort five-day snowfall forecast breakdown
, Steamboat Resort is expected to get the most snow over upcoming days at about 15 inches, with Arapahoe Basin and Winter Park close behind at 12. Meanwhile, other resorts in Summit County get about nine.

This forecast is likely a breath of fresh air for Colorado’s 
Front Range skiers and snowboarders
, with it looking like resorts closer to the most populated part of the state will finally get some decent powder. As of November 27, the state is at about 69 percent of its typical snowpack norm – which is concerning in itself, but also a bit deceiving. Southern and Southwest Colorado are respectively at about 151 percent and 148 percent of their regional norms, bolstering statewide snowpack numbers in a significant way at a glance. Meanwhile, Northern Colorado is at just 30 percent of what’s typical.

In terms of a snowpack turnaround, though, there’s more good news, too – 
OpenSnow’s
 Joel Gratz is calling for multiple rounds of snow during the first 10 days of December: “This is not a guarantee for big-time snow, but we’re in the mix, and that’s all we can hope for,” wrote Gratz in his November 27 daily update.

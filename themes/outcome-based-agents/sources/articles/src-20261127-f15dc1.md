Florida Bar Complaint Against Walton County Attorney Clay Adkinson
 

										Casey Drayer					

 

										November 27, 2026					

 

										2:13 pm					

 

Politics and Elections
, 
Walton County
 

The Complaint

A complaint filed with 
The Florida Bar
 seeks an investigation into Walton County Attorney Clay Adkinson’s handling of funds related to a multi-million-dollar county land purchase.

Adkinson
, hired in 2025 by Walton County and the managing partner of 
Adkinson Law Firm, LLC
, also represents the cities of DeFuniak Springs, Freeport, and Paxton.

The complaint was submitted by Samuel A. Osborne, a Walton County resident involved in litigation and public advocacy over Walton County land-use and development issues. It centers on the county’s 2021 purchase of property located at 195 San Roy Road.

According to documents attached to the complaint, the property was appraised at approximately 18 million. Under the agreement, Walton County paid 9.5 million in cash, while the seller treated the remaining 8.5 million as a charitable contribution to the county.

Osborne alleges that county funds associated with the transaction were wired to an account identified as “Adkinson Law Firm LLC” and claims that publicly available records do not clearly establish whether the money was deposited into a designated attorney trust account.

The complaint asks The Florida Bar to determine whether the handling of the funds complied with attorney trust-account requirements. Osborne argues that only bank records obtained through an official investigation would reveal the type of account that received the transfer.

In addition, the complaint raises concerns about potential conflicts of interest. Osborne claims that Adkinson serves as Walton County Attorney while representing multiple local municipalities through his private practice and providing legal services to other clients whose interests could intersect with county government matters.

The complaint asks The Florida Bar to review whether any professional conduct rules were violated and determine whether additional action is warranted.

Alleged Violations

1.
      
 
Rules Regulating The Florida Bar — Trust Accounting & Client Funds

Rule 5-1.1(a) (Nature of Account):
 Mandates that a lawyer must hold all funds of a client or third person in connection with a representation in a trust account separate from the lawyer’s own funds.

Rule 5-1.1(g) (IOTA Program Requirements):
 Requires lawyers holding client or third-party funds to maintain such funds in an Interest on Trust Accounts (IOTA) eligible account designated specifically as a trust account with an approved financial institution.

Rule 5-1.1(b) (Prohibition Against Commingling):
 Strictly prohibits depositing firm or personal funds into trust accounts or placing client/third-party real estate closing funds into general law firm operating accounts.

Rule 4-1.15 (Safekeeping Property):
 Requires attorneys to maintain complete records and strictly safeguard property of clients or third persons.

2.
      
 
Rules Regulating The Florida Bar — Conflicts of Interest & Government Representation

Rule 4-1.7 (Conflict of Interest; Current Clients):
 Prohibits representation if there is a substantial risk that representation will be materially limited by duties to another client, a former client, or a personal interest.

Rule 4-1.11 (Special Conflicts for Government Officers & Employees):
 Establishes ethical boundaries and screening rules for attorneys representing public bodies while maintaining private practice clients.

Rule 4-8.4(c) (Misconduct):
 Prohibits conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit, or misrepresentation.

Currently, The Florida Bar has made no finding of misconduct, and the allegations have not been proven. We have reached out to Clay Adkinson and Adkinson Law Firm LLC for comment and will update if additional information becomes available.

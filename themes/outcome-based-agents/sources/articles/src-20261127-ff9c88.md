exxis, the underwriting agency of Ecclesia Group, one of Germany’s largest insurance brokers, has deployed Sixfold’s AI underwriting platform across cyber, property and accident insurance within two months, integrating underwriting requirements from multiple insurance carriers.

exxis had become the first German managing general agent (MGA) to implement 
Sixfold’s technology
. The platform is now used daily by exxis underwriters, with separate configurations for individual carriers and insurance lines. 

exxis, part of Germany’s Ecclesia Group, has implemented AI underwriting across three commercial insurance lines for multiple capacity providers within two months.

The deployment incorporates carrier-specific underwriting guidelines, European data hosting and governance requirements under DORA and the EU AI Act.

The German MGA is evaluating additional underwriting applications, with D&O liability insurance representing a potential next stage of implementation.

The implementation required an infrastructure designed to address the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and the EU Artificial Intelligence Act.

exxis is a multi-line reinsurer within the Ecclesia Group. We develop, underwrite, and manage insurance solutions for complex risks – with the goal of ensuring that portfolios remain insurable in the long term, economically stable, and operationally manageable. As the Ecclesia Group’s reinsurer, we handle key functions throughout the value chain.

For MGAs operating under delegated underwriting authority from multiple insurers, each risk must be assessed against the underwriting guidelines and risk appetite of the carrier providing capacity.

AI automation across commercial insurance lines

The system prepares underwriting case files by consolidating source documents, identifying missing information, incorporating the relevant insurer’s risk appetite and generating an initial underwriting narrative. Underwriters can begin assessing individual risks without first assembling the supporting documentation manually.

The administrative requirements differ across the three insurance lines. Cyber underwriting involves reviewing extensive questionnaires, property insurance requires manual loss ratio calculations, and accident insurance generates substantial documentation relative to the premiums written.

These differences required separate configurations rather than a standardised underwriting model across the entire portfolio.

Philipp Bresch, senior property underwriter at exxis, said consolidated case information allows underwriters to reach the substantive risk assessment stage earlier in the process.

Sven Grimm, head of underwriting, acknowledged initial scepticism among employees. Underwriters gained confidence in the technology after comparing its output with previously assessed cases they understood in detail.

According to Tim Brozio, managing director of exxis, underwriters began using the platform from the first day of deployment. Employee confidence developed through operational experience and comparisons with established underwriting decisions.

Managing multiple carriers’ underwriting appetites

exxis underwrites cyber, property, accident and directors and officers (D&O) liability insurance on behalf of multiple insurers across the European Union under delegated authority arrangements.

Each submission must comply with the underwriting requirements of the specific insurer providing capacity, including risk selection criteria and individual underwriting guidelines.

Alex Schmelkin, founder and CEO of Sixfold, explained that delegated underwriting authority requires MGAs to assess risks in accordance with the standards that carriers would apply internally. The platform evaluates submissions against the appetite of the relevant insurer.

At exxis, underwriting guidelines and risk appetites are configured separately for each carrier and insurance line. When assessing a property insurance submission, for example, the underwriter receives guidance specific to the insurer whose capacity is being considered.

Analysts identified consistency in applying carrier-specific underwriting requirements as a central commercial consideration for MGAs managing multiple capacity agreements.

Successful performance against a single insurer’s guidelines does not necessarily demonstrate that an AI system can distinguish between several carriers with different risk tolerances.

Testing submissions that fall near the limits of one insurer’s underwriting appetite but remain acceptable to another provides a more relevant assessment of the technology’s suitability for multi-carrier delegated authority operations.

DORA and EU AI Act requirements for insurance underwriting

Regulatory compliance, data security and operational governance formed part of exxis’s initial technology selection criteria. Requirements included DORA compliance, readiness for the EU AI Act, single-tenant hosting within Europe, SOC 2 Type 2 attestation and support for German and English.

The selection process focused on finding a technology provider capable of accommodating the underwriting practices of individual carriers and insurance lines within an appropriate governance framework.

DORA has applied across the EU since 17 January 2025, establishing requirements for financial institutions and insurance intermediaries within its scope to manage risks associated with third-party information and communication technology (ICT) providers.

Insurance businesses must maintain appropriate oversight and documentation of external technology providers supporting their operations.

The EU AI Act introduces different regulatory requirements depending on the intended application of artificial intelligence in insurance.

Annex III of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 classifies AI systems used for risk assessment and pricing involving individuals in life and health insurance as high-risk applications.

The high-risk framework includes requirements for quality management systems, bias assessment, technical documentation and human oversight. August 2026 was the original implementation deadline for the relevant Annex III obligations.

Commercial insurance lines such as cyber, property and accident generally fall outside this specific high-risk classification, although applicable transparency and governance requirements remain relevant. The distinction limits the extent to which the exxis implementation can serve as a model for life and health insurance MGAs, where additional regulatory obligations affect technology selection, documentation and human oversight.

Underwriting productivity and expansion into specialty insurance

Evidence from the exxis implementation remains qualitative, including the two-month deployment period, daily operational use and employee reports of reduced case preparation workloads.

The agency has not disclosed quantitative improvements in underwriting productivity, risk selection, operating costs or premium volumes attributable to AI.

For MGAs assessing similar implementations, relevant performance tests include the accurate application of different carriers’ underwriting guidelines to identical submissions, alongside an assessment of hosting arrangements, technical documentation and third-party risk management controls.

exxis and Sixfold are evaluating additional applications of AI within the agency’s underwriting operations.

D&O liability insurance represents a potential next stage of implementation. The agency already underwrites this class, but it was not included among the three insurance lines initially brought into production.

An extension into D&O would broaden the operational application of AI across the agency’s commercial and specialty insurance portfolio. Neither company has disclosed an implementation timetable.

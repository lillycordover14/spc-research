Tim Spencer realized just how complicated manufacturing procurement can be while running an e-commerce startup in Asia during the pandemic. Spencer told TechCrunch recently, "We had thousands of suppliers, and we were distributing products into dozens of countries around the world." His staff was overwhelmed by the manual complexity of sourcing suppliers, negotiating pricing, tracking orders, and managing payments. He said, "I found myself running this big team that was not really set up for success." Soon after, in twenty twenty-three, he sold that company just as it was becoming clear that generative AI could streamline the most time-consuming procurement hurdles for manufacturers and distributors. Later that year, Spencer launched Diderot with Laurence Palhouver, a veteran of McKinsey's procurement practice, and Tom Pettitt, the former technical co-founder of Landis. Diderot, whose mission is to automate many of the complexities of global procurement, just raised a thirty million dollar Series A, co-led by Chemistry and headlined with participation from Microsoft's venture fund, M12. Spencer said global trade runs on natural language communication. It's emails, WeChat, phone calls, purchase orders, and packing lists. Until the advent of generative AI, these fragmented pieces had to be tracked by humans who spent their days chasing suppliers and manually updating systems of record. Diderot claims its platform can ingest that communication, putting a significant portion of the procurement workflow on autopilot. Diderot functions as an agentic AI layer that sits on top of a company's existing ERP, acting as a coordinator that reads incoming communications and automatically executes the necessary updates and tasks. The goal is to go from, "I need a good," to payment without having to lift a finger. Unlike LevelPath, or Zip, or Oro labs, which use AI to streamline corporate purchasing, Diderot focuses on the supply chain.
